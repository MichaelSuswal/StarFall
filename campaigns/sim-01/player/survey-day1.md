# Day 1 World Survey: the player's answers

Campaign: sim-01. Creation Day 1. Survey: `engine/creation/day1-world-survey.md` (19 questions, fixed).

The answers below are the player's own words as given in the chat, verbatim and unedited. All 19 questions were answered; none was skipped. Bent or rejected premises are noted separately in `rejected-premises.md`.

1. Quiet and old and a little lonely. An image: a long blue hour just after sunset, smoke rising straight up from a few chimneys, and a lot of dark country around them that doesn't care about the people. Beautiful, but not kind or unkind.

2. High country. Uplands, moors and broken hills with long valleys, thin soil and a lot of stone. Coast somewhere within a few weeks' walk, but I don't want to start there.

3. Four real seasons, with hard winters and short bright summers. I'd like to wake into late autumn, when the year is turning inward and people are gathering stores and telling stories.

4. Some of each, but weighted toward wild. I want old kingdoms far enough away to be rumors and trade goods, and lots of country nobody has walked. Exploration is what I'm here for.

5. Ruins under ruins. Several failed or vanished peoples before the current ones, each leaving something odd behind. Nobody should be sure who built what.

6. Rare but real. A rumor most people doubt, with a few valleys where everyone knows a woman or a man who "has the knack." Not a wise-woman in every valley. I want a mortal with real power to be an event.

7. It should feel like listening, or like being listened to. Small, specific, and tied to place. The cost should be personal and slow: you lose something ordinary, like sleep, the taste of food, or a name you used to answer to. Nothing that works as a clean power meter.

8. Closer to the one-eyed man, but nearsighted. A god should be noticeably more than a mortal and still be wrong about things. I don't want to be a sorcerer with a halo, and I don't want to be a cheat code.

9. A few legends in the deep places, and a lot of familiar animals behaving a bit off. Mostly the world is ordinary. Then somewhere high there's a thing nobody has a name for, and people tell it differently every time.

10. Slow things. Famine, a bad winter, a pass that closes, sickness moving along a trade road, and a rumor that turns a village against itself. I like fear that comes from not knowing what's coming, more than a dragon.

11. Salt, I think, or something equally dull and essential. High country with no salt means people go without or walk a very long way. I want the scarcity to shape roads, marriages and wars. Second choice would be iron.

12. Old roads and sealed doors. Roads that go somewhere they shouldn't, built by people with a different idea of distance. A door or two that nobody has managed to open or has dared to. Bones of something enormous is fine as a detail, but I don't want it to be the main thing.

13. Yes, but few and strange, and I don't want them to be elves or dwarves. I'd rather meet a people we can barely talk to, who are living in the old ruins and have their own reasons. Don't tell me what they are. Let me find out.

14. One or two, very rare. A single traveler who arrived with a tool nobody understands and a language only half of which works. I'd like that to be a mystery I can follow for a long time, not a shop.

15. Face to face only, and not instant. I'd like to see another god and slowly work out what they are, and to be unsure for a while whether a stranger is a god or just a strange mortal. Maybe a faint unease at close range, nothing at a distance.

16. A sworn word should bind, but not as a rule the game enforces. I want it to bind the way a debt binds, with consequences that show up later and sideways. A god who breaks an oath should find the world slightly less willing to listen to them.

17. A mapmaker, a surveyor, a trader, or a wandering healer. Someone curious who goes places and writes things down. Not a king and not a chosen one. I want to follow a mortal who ends up with more knowledge than power, and see what that does to the people around them.

18. A companion, or a place that turns out to be listening back. Something I can't predict yet that behaves like it has its own mind. I'd like to be surprised by what the world already contains.

19. That it stays indifferent to me. I want to earn whatever place I get, and I'd like the world to keep a few secrets I never solve. Also, quietly, that somebody out there will remember what I did, long after I stopped being able to do it.
