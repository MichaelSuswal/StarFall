# Follow-ups for Day 2

Campaign: sim-01. Written by the Keeper at the close of Night 1, for the DM. The player may read this file.

Three questions, from the places where the Day 1 answers left the world something only the player can say. Weave them into the day's talk in the voice of The Between, in any order, where they fit. Each has a plain (oog) form to fall back on. Write the player's answer under each question, in their words.

Nothing was asked about the peoples who aren't human. The player asked not to be told, and that is honored.

## 1. Where the feet come down

In the fiction: "You asked for high country. When you picture the first place you stand, is it the bare top itself, with the wind on it, or a valley under the tops, with smoke going up?"

(oog) fallback: Does your god want to live up on the high ground or down in the valleys beneath it? This helps choose where it lands.

Answer:

Valley under the tops, high enough that the smoke goes up thin. I want to land at the head of it, where the valley narrows toward a pass. The bare top is for later.

## 2. Being known

In the fiction: "Suppose one like you came down off the road at dusk and sat across your fire. Would you want it to know you for what you are? Or would you sooner pass for something smaller, for as long as you could?"

(oog) fallback: You said you would like to be unsure for a while whether a stranger is a god. Turned around: would your god want to be able to hide what it is from others like it? If yes, that is something to build and price today, like any other ability.

Answer:

"Pass for something smaller. I'd sit across the fire as a cairn, or as a stone-faced old drover who doesn't talk much, for as long as it holds. Another god will know me the moment we're near, and I can't help that, but nobody else needs to. I don't want mortals deciding what I am before I've had a chance to be useful to them. I won't lie about it if asked straight, though. I'd go quiet instead."

DM's note: the player did not ask for any means of hiding what it is from other gods, so nothing was built or priced for that. The drover shape was declined as a shape; holding still as a cairn is the god's form. See `power-card.md`, Form.

## 3. A word given

In the fiction: "You said a sworn word should bind the way a debt binds. When you give your word to one like yourself, what do you set behind it? And is there a thing you would never swear to?"

(oog) fallback: When your god makes a serious promise to another god, what does it put at stake, and is there anything it refuses to swear? This is about your god, not about a rule.

Answer:

"What I set behind my word is the line itself. Every stone I've laid, and the truth every traveler leans on when they trust one. If I break a sworn word, I'd have made every cairn of mine a little less believable, and I'd feel that. That's the only collateral I have, and it's worth more to me than anything I could name. I'd put it behind any oath I give.

Things I would never swear: anything that needs a lie to keep. Anything that closes a road for good, or leaves a traveler lost on purpose. Obedience, to a god or a mortal, with no end and no terms. And anything open-ended that I can't see the end of. If I swear, I want it narrow, with a named thing and a named time, because I'll be held to the words exactly."

DM's note: the player also took two weaknesses that bear on this (cannot say a false word; cannot knowingly act against a sworn word). See `power-card.md`, Weaknesses bought against powers.

## For the DM: three things a god's build must fit

These hold for every god in this world, and a god would know them of itself. Use them for the Form section of the power card and for the "other gods" edge cases. They are not open to negotiation today.

- Bodies. A god here has one body.
- Knowing another god. Your god knows that others like it came down when it did, and nothing more about them. It feels nothing of them from far away. When one stands near enough to speak to, your god feels plainly that this is one like itself. It learns nothing else that way: not what the other is, what it can do, what it is called, or what it wants. That it must work out.
- A word given. A word sworn between gods, freely and on purpose, is a real thing here. Nothing stops a god from breaking one. What comes of breaking one is not written anywhere a god can read.
