# Power card: the Cairn

Campaign: sim-01. Written at the close of creation Day 2 (2026-11-18), from the card as read back to the player and accepted ("Yes. That's my god."). The table talk is in `transcript-day2.md`. Descriptions and Drawbacks are frozen from this date; everything learned later goes under Rulings.

"The Cairn" is a filing name. The god's own name is lost. Mortals will call it what they call it.

## Form

One body. This campaign allows a god one body.

A figure built the way a cairn is built: flat grey slabs balanced on one another, with a lichen skin in orange and pale green. It stands a bit taller than a tall man and never looks finished. Wherever one stone sits on another there is a seam of dim light, like banked coals. Two hollows near the top serve as eyes. It has no mouth. It speaks by the sound of stones settling, and people hear it as words if they have been quiet for a while.

What the form does by itself:

- It needs no air, food or sleep.
- Pace: a steady walker's pace on the pace table. It does not run. It does not tire.
- Strength: it can carry or drag about what a strong ox could, slowly.
- The seam-light: when the Cairn is still and attending to nothing, the light banks down to nothing and it looks like an ordinary cairn. As soon as it attends to something, the light shows and moves a little. A cairn that is watching can be caught watching, most easily in the dark.
- It has no other shape. Passing for something else by cloak, dusk and distance is an ordinary attempt in play and works as well as it would for anyone.
- Senses: the two hollows see as eyes. Nothing else about its bodily senses was settled on Day 2.

God-sense, as set for this campaign: the Cairn knows that others like it came down when it did, and nothing more about them. It feels nothing of them from far away. When one stands near enough to speak to, it feels plainly that this is one like itself, and is known the same way. It learns nothing else that way: not what the other is, what it can do, what it is called, or what it wants. The Cairn has no means of hiding what it is from another god.

### Cairns, two grades

Every power below depends on these two terms.

- An honest cairn is any true marker raised in the Cairn's name, by anyone. It carries Not lost only.
- A hand-laid cairn is one the Cairn built, or set a stone on, with its own hands. This takes about an hour of its attention. It carries Knowing where it is laid, Not lost, and The voice in the stones. There is no limit on how many. The Cairn's people can raise honest cairns and cannot make one hand-laid.
- A hand-laid cairn that is thrown down or moved goes out of the Cairn, unfelt, until the Cairn sets it right by hand. It finds out when it reaches for it.

### Broken

Fallen from a height or pulled apart, the Cairn breaks into slabs and the light goes out of the seams. It does not die. Pieces lying within a few hundred paces gather in about a day. Wider scatter takes longer, up to a season if they are spread across a slope. A slab that is carried off is a piece missing: the Cairn is smaller and a little less sure of itself until it recovers the slab or takes another stone in its place. While it is in pieces it has no sense and no voice at its cairns, and it knows nothing of the time that passes. Honest cairns go on keeping travelers.

## Powers

### Knowing where it is laid
Status: main
Description: At any hand-laid cairn the Cairn can feel what a blind man leaning there would know: the weather, the ground, footfalls, how many, heavy or light, beast or person, about a stone's throw around. It is a sense, not sight, dim and slow, and it works only at marked places. No faces and no words. One cairn at a time. A cairn holds roughly a day of what it felt, fading.
Drawback: While the Cairn listens at a far stone, its body is only a cairn. It stands where it is and knows nothing of what is around it until it comes back, and coming back is not quick.
Limits settled at creation:
  - Hand-laid cairns only. Honest cairns raised by others carry no sense.
  - Distance: any hand-laid cairn can be reached however far. A far one takes longer to reach than a near one, and longer to come back from.
  - A cairn's memory is about a day, fading: a party that passed this morning can be known, one that passed last week cannot.
  - A hand-laid cairn that has gone out (thrown down, moved, or lost to the voice) gives nothing until set right or touched again by hand.
  - None while the Cairn is in pieces.
Rulings:
Trades: none
Growth: none open

### Not lost
Status: main
Description: People who pass an honest cairn raised in the Cairn's name and add a stone, even without understanding, are a little harder to lose. They find the way back, or the way on. It does nothing against storm, cold, hunger or a bad step. It stops being lost.
Drawback: It holds only while the cairn tells the truth. A cairn that is moved, or raised to mislead, does not go quiet. It leads people wrong, with the Cairn's weight behind it, and the Cairn does not feel that from afar.
Limits settled at creation:
  - Works at any honest cairn in the Cairn's name, raised by anyone, and at hand-laid cairns, including ones that have gone out of the Cairn.
  - Continues while the Cairn is in pieces.
Rulings:
Trades: none
Growth: none open

### Staying put
Status: main
Description: A stone figure that decides to stay put stays put. Once the Cairn settles, no mortal effort shifts it.
Drawback: It is as heavy as it looks. It sinks and does not swim. Thin ice, small boats, rope bridges and rotten floors will not hold it, and it rides nothing.
Limits settled at creation:
  - Enough people with levers and time can still take a settled Cairn apart slab by slab. See Broken, under Form.
  - Water: it can cross a firm riverbed or lake bed on foot. Deep mud, bog and silt hold it, and getting out can take days or help.
  - What another god can do to a settled Cairn was not settled on Day 2. It is ruled when it happens.
Rulings:
Trades: none
Growth: none open

### The voice in the stones
Status: main
Description: The Cairn can speak through any hand-laid cairn, anywhere in the land, one or many at the same moment. One breath of words, a sentence, said once. It goes one way: the Cairn hears no answer, beyond what Knowing where it is laid would give it later. Anyone near who has been quiet hears it. The Cairn cannot choose the ears.
Drawback: Each time the Cairn speaks through a stone, one hand-laid cairn somewhere on its line goes out of it, and it does not choose which. Speaking through many at once, one goes out for every three that spoke, rounded up. A cairn that has gone out still stands and still keeps travelers from being lost, but the Cairn can neither feel nor speak there until it walks back and lays its hand on it again. It does not learn which one went until it reaches for it and finds nothing.
Limits settled at creation:
  - Hand-laid cairns only.
  - Distance does not limit it.
  - Who hears: other gods, and anything else with ears that has been quiet, hear the voice at a cairn the same as a herdsman does.
  - None while the Cairn is in pieces.
  - The Cairn's word carries its weight through a cairn as it does at its body. See Weaknesses bought against powers.
  - How the cost came to be this: the player offered to forget "one stone's worth of memory" of the old road each time, not chosen. The DM turned the offer so the loss falls on the new line, because a price paid out of memory already lost could not be felt. The player accepted: "The voice price is better than mine, because I'll miss a stone I built, and I couldn't miss one I never knew."
Rulings:
Trades: none
Growth: none open

## Weaknesses bought against powers

### It cannot speak falsely
Weakness: The Cairn cannot say what it knows to be false, by its body or through a cairn. It cannot set, or leave standing by its own hand, a marker it knows to be false. Silence, a true word beside the point, and letting someone draw their own wrong conclusion from a true word are all open to it. Anyone who learns this can use it: a straight question, and its silence read as an answer.
Bought: Its word carries weight a hearer can feel. Whoever hears it, at its body or at a cairn, mortal or god, feels plainly that the speaker means it. Not that it is so; that the Cairn holds it to be so. The Cairn can be mistaken, and a hearer stays free to doubt, disagree or ignore it. Whether mortals and gods come to rely on its word is reputation, earned in the world, not granted. The weight cannot be counterfeited at its stones: a voice that is not the Cairn's, speaking at one of its cairns, does not carry it.
Not bought: belief itself. The player asked that hearers know "without proof that I wouldn't say it unless it were so." The DM declined that as a power over minds and gave the felt weight in its place. The player accepted.

### It cannot break a sworn word
Weakness: The Cairn cannot swear what it does not mean to keep. It cannot knowingly act against a word it has sworn, in the same way it cannot lie. A badly worded oath, or one someone tricks it into wording badly, holds it until the other party lets it go.
Bought: Only that this is true of the Cairn, and that it can truthfully say so. Nothing is added to what a sworn word is in this world, which is the same for the Cairn as for every god: a word sworn between gods, freely and on purpose, is a real thing; nothing stops a god from breaking one; what comes of breaking one is not written anywhere a god can read.
Precedence, ruled on Day 2 at the player's request: where a sworn word and the ban on falsehood collide, the ban on falsehood wins. The Cairn stays silent, or leaves the false marker unset, the oath goes unkept, and whatever follows from an unkept oath in this world follows for it. That is the only way the Cairn can fail a sworn word.
