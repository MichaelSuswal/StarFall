# Hierarchy: refused

Campaign: sim-01. Written by the DM at the close of creation Day 3.

This god rejected the game's default hierarchy. It has no advisors, no speaker by office, no court, and no one who carries out its wishes. Its people have no town and no lord. The game remembers this out loud: this god does not play the usual way.

In the player's words:

"I'll refuse the town. I'll refuse the advisors who carry out wishes, as I don't want servants who do what I say. If someone wants to help, I'd like them to choose it and keep choosing it. I'll accept a few mortals who attach themselves to me, because there'll be a few, and they'll be people, not instruments."

## What exists in its place

- Listeners, if any come. "Someone to listen: yes, one or two who choose it. Not a servant, and not an advisor. Someone who sits quiet by me on the long evenings, and who may hear nothing for weeks, and may come to carry what I say to the others when they've heard it. They can leave whenever they like. I'd let them find me. I won't ask."
- Tally-walkers, a calling the god may found. They choose themselves and the god says yes or no. See `civs/carrowfolk/record.md`.
- The Carrowfolk's own way of settling things, the reckoning, which has nothing to do with the god.

## What this means for every agent

- No text gives this god advisors, officers, a seat, a court or a chain of orders (failures checklist, check 4).
- A decision of the god's reaches its people only by what it says with its own voice to those quiet enough to hear, by what a listener chooses to carry, or by what people see it do.
- Unplayed time cannot be delegated to advisors or kings, because there are none. The god does what it last decided, by its conduct profile.
- People who help the god are individuals with their own will. They may stop.
