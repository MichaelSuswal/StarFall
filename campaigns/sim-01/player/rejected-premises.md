# Rejected and bent premises

Campaign: sim-01. For the Keeper's follow-ups. Written by the DM at each day's close; later days add their own section below.

## Day 1, the world survey

No premise was rejected outright. The survey's standing assumption, that the god will rule a land and a people, was not rejected: the player answered question 4 ("Past the borders of your own land") on its own terms.

Four premises were bent. Each is given with what the player said instead, in their words. Full answers are in `survey-day1.md`.

1. **Question 16, oaths between gods.** The question offered two ends: a sworn word that binds with real force, or one worth only as much as the god who gave it. The player took neither. Instead: "A sworn word should bind, but not as a rule the game enforces. I want it to bind the way a debt binds, with consequences that show up later and sideways. A god who breaks an oath should find the world slightly less willing to listen to them."
   Gap for the Keeper: the player asks for a real consequence that is not a stated rule, and names one form it could take. Whether and how that works is a campaign decision (DESIGN 7.7).

2. **Question 8, how much a god stands out.** The question offered two ends: the one-eyed man in the kingdom of the blind, or barely more than a great mortal sorcerer. The player placed the god between them and added a condition the question did not ask about, that gods can be wrong: "Closer to the one-eyed man, but nearsighted. A god should be noticeably more than a mortal and still be wrong about things. I don't want to be a sorcerer with a halo, and I don't want to be a cheat code."

3. **Question 13, peoples who aren't human.** The question asks "If so, what are they like?" The player said yes and then declined to specify, on purpose: "Yes, but few and strange, and I don't want them to be elves or dwarves. I'd rather meet a people we can barely talk to, who are living in the old ruins and have their own reasons. Don't tell me what they are. Let me find out."
   Gap for the Keeper: their nature is left wholly to the world. This is a request not to be asked, so a follow-up question on what they are would go against the answer.

4. **Question 15, how gods recognize each other.** The question offered a pull at any distance, a knowing only face to face, or something else. The player took face to face and removed the knowing: "Face to face only, and not instant. I'd like to see another god and slowly work out what they are, and to be unsure for a while whether a stranger is a god or just a strange mortal. Maybe a faint unease at close range, nothing at a distance."
   Gap for the Keeper: this is weaker than the design's default, in which every god senses that others exist and all gods in a campaign recognize each other's presence and language (DESIGN 1.5, 7.2, 7.3). God-sense is decided per campaign.

## Not rejections, noted so they are not read as one

These are the DM's observations, not the player's statements. Nothing here has been inferred into a decision; each is a thing to ask on Day 2 or Day 3, not to assume.

- Question 19: "I want to earn whatever place I get." This may bear on the Day 3 assumptions (about 5,000 followers, a town or city, advisors built in). The player has not rejected any of them. Ask on Day 3.
- Question 17: "Not a king and not a chosen one" is about the mortal the player hopes the land produces, not about the god or its people.
- Question 6: the player asked for magic that is rare overall, with "a few valleys" where it is commonly known. That is a wish about uneven spread, which the Keeper weighs against the rule that no wish creates a local exception to the rules (DESIGN 3.19).
- Question 7: "Nothing that works as a clean power meter" is a constraint on how magic's cost is designed, not an answer to what it costs.
- Question 11: two answers given in order of preference, salt first, iron second.

## Day 2, the god

No premise was rejected. The player took the day's assumptions as given: one body, the campaign's god-sense, the campaign's rule on sworn words, and a drawback on every power.

Two things were bent, one by the player and one by the DM.

1. **Backstory, "who the god was".** The guide asks who the god was and what it lost. The player answered that it was never a person: "Before this, I was a marker. I don't remember being a person." The outline in `backstory.md` is of a made thing, a stone on a line of stones, not of a someone.
   For the Keeper: the secret rest of the backstory is being written for a thing that other hands built and prayed at.

2. **Follow-up 2, being known.** The question offered hiding what it is from others like it, as something to build and price. The player set that aside and answered about mortals instead: "Another god will know me the moment we're near, and I can't help that, but nobody else needs to." No means of hiding from gods was asked for or built.

Bent by the DM, recorded so it is not mistaken for the player's wish:

- **The price of the voice.** The player offered to pay in memory of the old road. The DM moved the cost onto the new line (one hand-laid cairn goes out per speaking). The player accepted without argument. Both versions are on the power card.
- **What honesty buys.** The player asked to be believed without proof. The DM declined belief as a power over minds and gave a felt weight of sincerity. The player accepted.

## Not rejections, noted for Day 3

The DM's observations. Each is a thing to ask on Day 3, not to assume.

- "I don't want mortals deciding what I am before I've had a chance to be useful to them." Together with Day 1's "I want to earn whatever place I get", this may bear on how the god meets its people and on whether it wants a hierarchy under it. The player has rejected neither.
- The god wants roads and passes marked "so that people can cross without dying". It spoke of travelers throughout, and has not yet said anything about a people of its own.
