# Follow-ups for Day 3

Campaign: sim-01. Written by the Keeper at the close of Night 2, for the DM. The player may read this file.

Three questions, from places where the god as built on Day 2 leaves something about its people that only the player can say. Weave them into the day's talk where they fit, in any order. Each has a plain (oog) form to fall back on. Write the player's answer under each, in their words. None of them is a rejection to be argued with; whatever the player answers, the game takes it.

## 1. First sight

In the fiction: "There is smoke in the valley below you. When those people first learn you are here, how would you have it happen? Do they wake to find a cairn at the valley head that was not there the night before, and make of it what they will? Or do you walk down?"

(oog) fallback: Your god said it wants to be useful before mortals decide what it is. How does it want its own people to first meet it: found standing and left to wonder, or arriving and making itself known? This shapes how the first morning opens.

Why it is asked: the god passes for a cairn when still, cannot say a false word, and goes quiet under a straight question. How it is first met decides what its people believe about it on Day 1 of play.

Answer:

"How they learn I'm here: a cairn at the valley head that wasn't there the night before. I land, I settle, and I stay still. The first shepherd up in the morning finds a figure the size of a big boulder with lichen on it and decides what to make of it. I don't want to walk down in the first week. I'd rather be found, and watched, and quietly tested. They'll push on me and walk around me and leave an offering or a rock, and the first person who adds a stone and then somehow doesn't lose their way home will do more than any speech."


## 2. Who speaks for a god that is hard to hear

In the fiction: "Your words come as stones settling, and only the quiet hear them. A people is rarely quiet. Is there someone among them you would have listen for you and carry what you say, or would you sooner each of them had to come and be still?"

(oog) fallback: The game assumes a god has advisors who carry out its wishes. Your god's voice is only heard by people who have been quiet a while, and it cannot say anything false through anyone's mouth but its own. Do you want advisors or a speaker, some other arrangement, or none? Rejecting them is allowed and is remembered.

Why it is asked: the usual hierarchy rests on a god that can simply speak. This one cannot.

Answer:

"On the defaults: I'll refuse the town. I'll refuse the advisors who carry out wishes, as I don't want servants who do what I say. If someone wants to help, I'd like them to choose it and keep choosing it. I'll accept a few mortals who attach themselves to me, because there'll be a few, and they'll be people, not instruments."

"Someone to listen: yes, one or two who choose it. Not a servant, and not an advisor. Someone who sits quiet by me on the long evenings, and who may hear nothing for weeks, and may come to carry what I say to the others when they've heard it. They can leave whenever they like. I'd let them find me. I won't ask."


## 3. A people who stay, or a people who walk

In the fiction: "You spoke all yesterday of travelers. Whoever is under that smoke has built walls. Are the people you would lead the ones who keep a valley, or the ones who cross it? And the cairns: would you have them raise stones in your name as a thing every one of them does, or leave that to whoever chooses?"

(oog) fallback: Your god talked about travelers and roads and has not yet said anything about a settled people of its own. Do you picture your people as settled, as people of the road, or both? And should raising honest cairns be a custom or duty of your people, or something left to each person? Honest cairns carry your second power and its drawback, so how freely they are raised matters.

Why it is asked: Not lost spreads through cairns anyone raises, and a false or moved cairn misleads with the god's weight behind it. A people's custom about raising them is the largest single lever on that power.

Answer:

"People who keep a valley and cross it, both."

"Raising them: left to whoever chooses. Adding a stone is already the habit, and I'd leave it that. Keeping a cairn, which means checking it, rebuilding it after a storm, and never moving it or letting it lie, I'd leave to the tally-walkers and to anyone who wants the job. I don't want a custom where everyone feels obliged and nobody feels it. More hands means more chance of a cairn moved wrong, and a wrong cairn with my weight on it kills people. I'd rather have fewer cairns that are kept than many that aren't."
