# Day 2 transcript: the god

Campaign: sim-01. The table talk of creation Day 2, as relayed. Each post is kept as it was sent.

## DM:

Still falling. The night you fell through was long, and something below was listening the whole way down. It kept some of what you asked. You can feel that much, the way you feel weather turning: there is high country under you, stone and thin soil, and cold coming on it.

You are not the only thing it listened to.

A voice has been beside you for a while now. It belongs to this place between, and it will not follow you down.

"You asked for high country. When you picture the first place you stand, is it the bare top itself, with the wind on it, or a valley under the tops, with smoke going up?"

"And you. The world took your wishes for itself and asked nothing about the one wishing. So tell me what is falling. What shape does it have, when it has one? What were you, before this, and what did you lose on the way? Say as much as you can still see. The rest is going to fog, and that is ordinary here."

"Then tell me what you can do that the people below cannot. Take it in any order. I will ask as you go."

(oog) Today is the god itself: its form, its past in outline, what it wants, and what it can do. Describe it freely and at any length. I will ask questions, and I will put a price on anything that is a power, because every power here comes with a drawback. Step out with (oog) any time you want to talk plainly.

## Player:

Valley under the tops, high enough that the smoke goes up thin. I want to land at the head of it, where the valley narrows toward a pass. The bare top is for later.

What is falling is a heap of stones that thinks it is a person. When it has a shape, it is a figure built the way a cairn is built, flat grey slabs balanced on one another, with a lichen skin in orange and pale green. It stands a bit taller than a tall man and never looks finished. Wherever one stone sits on another there is a seam of dim light, like banked coals, and the light moves a little when I am paying attention to something. Two hollows near the top serve as eyes. I have no mouth. I speak by the sound of stones settling, and people hear it as words if they have been quiet for a while.

Before this, I was a marker. I don't remember being a person. I remember being the thing travelers touched on the way past, one stone on a long line of stones across some other country, and every hand that added a rock to me was a small prayer that said "someone has been here and got through." I knew where the line went. I knew every place it had been laid. The line is gone now, and so is the country, and I don't know how it ended. What I lost on the way down is my name, and the end of the road. I can still feel that it ran to a place where something was waiting for it. I don't remember what.

What I want is to put a line across this country. I want roads and passes marked so that people can cross without dying, and I want the marking to mean something, so that when someone stacks a stone they are telling the truth to whoever comes next. The marker's whole nature is honesty. A cairn that lies gets people killed. I want that to stay true of me. Past that, I want to find out where the old roads here go, because I suspect some of them were mine, or something like me.

What I can do that mortals can't:

1. I know where I have been laid. Anywhere a stone has been set in my name, I can feel the ground around it, the weather, and who passes. It's a sense, not sight. It's dim and slow, and it works only at marked places.

2. People who walk by my stones and add one, even without understanding, are a little harder to lose. They find the way back, or the way on. It doesn't stop a storm or a bad step. It stops being lost.

3. I can't be moved easily. A stone figure that decides to stay put stays put, and mortals will find that out if they try to push me.

For the edge, I'm going to ask for something too strong, and I'd like you to price it. I want to be able to speak through any stone I've been laid in, anywhere in the land, at the same moment. Not just sense, but a voice. Standing at a pass in the snow, a traveler hears me say "the left path, not the right." And all my stones can do that at once, if I choose.

I expect a cost. Here is what I would offer without being asked: each time I do it, I forget something, one stone's worth of memory, and I don't get to choose which. It should be the slow loss of the road I already can't remember. If you want the price to be harsher, tell me, but I'd like to know what the voice costs before I use it.

## DM:

"The head of a valley, under a pass. The world heard that."

The voice is quiet for a while, the way a person is quiet when counting.

"A marker, then. One stone on a line, and the line gone. You are not the first thing to fall through here that was made by other hands. Three things you would know of yourself, so I will say them. You have one body down there, and only one. You know that others like you are falling now, and nothing else about them. You will feel nothing of them from far off. But if one stands near enough to speak to, you will know it plainly for one like yourself, and it will know you. Not what you are, or what you can do, or what you want. Only that."

"So I ask you this. A heap of stones that holds still is a cairn to any herdsman. Suppose one like you came down off the road at dusk and sat across your fire. Would you want it to know you for what you are? Or would you sooner pass for something smaller, for as long as you could?"

"And you said a cairn that lies gets people killed. Is that a thing you prefer, or a thing you are? Could you say a false word if you chose to?"

(oog) Pricing. I will take your four in order and say what I propose for each. Argue any of it.

One question sits under all of them: what makes a stone yours? "Set in my name" could mean a cairn you built with your own hands, or any heap a shepherd raises and calls yours. The second kind multiplies without you, so it matters a great deal. My proposal is two grades. Any honest cairn raised in your name, by anyone, carries power 2, the not-being-lost. Only a cairn you have laid your own hand on carries your sense and your voice. That keeps the line something you walk, at the pace of walking.

1. The sense. As you wrote it, with these limits. At a hand-laid cairn you feel what a blind man leaning on it would know: weather, the ground, footfalls, how many, heavy or light, beast or person, about a stone's throw around. No faces and no words. One place at a time. A cairn holds roughly a day of what it felt, fading, so you can learn that a party passed this morning but not last week. Drawback: while you listen at a far stone, your body is only a cairn. It stands where it is and knows nothing of what is around it until you come back, and coming back is not quick.

2. Not lost. As you wrote it: it does nothing against storm, cold, hunger or a bad step, it only keeps the way. Drawback, and this one comes from your own words: it holds only while the cairn tells the truth. A cairn of yours that is moved, or raised to mislead, does not go quiet. It leads people wrong, with your weight behind it, and you do not feel that from afar.

3. Staying put. Granted: once you settle, nothing mortal shifts you. The drawback is the same stone. You are as heavy as you look. You do not swim, you sink. Thin ice, small boats, rope bridges and rotten floors are not for you, and you ride nothing. Tell me also what happens if you fall from a height or are pulled apart: do you scatter and gather again, and how long does that take?

4. The voice. It is strong mainly because nothing else in this world carries a word across a country faster than a walker. I will allow it, shaped like this. It speaks only through hand-laid cairns. It is one breath of words, a sentence, said once. It goes one way: you do not hear an answer, beyond what the sense would give you later. Anyone near who has been quiet hears it. You cannot choose the ears.

On the cost, I am going to turn your offer rather than take it as given. The old road is already fog to you. A price paid out of fog would cost nothing you could feel, and you told the world you did not want magic with a clean meter. So the forgetting takes from the new line. Each time you speak through a stone, one hand-laid cairn somewhere on your line goes out of you, and you do not choose which. It still stands. It still keeps travelers from being lost. But you can no longer feel there or speak there, until you walk back and lay your hand on it again, and you do not learn which one went until you reach for it and find nothing. If you speak through many at once, one goes out for every three that spoke. Speaking through all of them at once would cost a third of your line.

That keeps your idea, the slow loss of a road, one stone at a time, not chosen. It moves it to a road you will have built and would miss.

Two more things when you answer. If honesty is a thing you are and not only a thing you prefer, so that you cannot say a false word or set a false marker, that is a real weakness here, and it can buy you something. Tell me what you would want it to buy. And you lost your name on the way down. What do people call a thing like you when they do not know its name?

## Player:

"Pass for something smaller. I'd sit across the fire as a cairn, or as a stone-faced old drover who doesn't talk much, for as long as it holds. Another god will know me the moment we're near, and I can't help that, but nobody else needs to. I don't want mortals deciding what I am before I've had a chance to be useful to them. I won't lie about it if asked straight, though. I'd go quiet instead.

Honesty is a thing I am, not a thing I prefer. I can't say a false word and I can't set a false marker. I could stay silent, or say something true that doesn't answer the question, or let someone draw their own wrong conclusion from a true word, but I can't say what I know to be false. I'll take that as a real weakness.

What I'd want it to buy: a word of mine counts. When I speak through a hand-laid cairn, anyone who hears it knows without proof that I wouldn't say it unless it were so, and they can act on it in the snow without arguing. And when I swear something to another god, it binds me the way a debt binds, with force I can't talk my way out of. I'd like other gods to know that about me, because I think it makes me worth bargaining with, and a witness worth having. I'm not asking for a power over minds. I'm asking to be believed, and I accept that the same trait could be used against me.

On your pricing, I'll take it. Two grades of cairn is right, and I like that the line has to be walked. Hand-laid stones for the sense and voice, any honest cairn for the not-being-lost. The sense drawback is fair, and being only a cairn while I listen far away is a real risk, so I'll be careful where I leave my body. The voice price is better than mine, because I'll miss a stone I built, and I couldn't miss one I never knew. I won't argue it.

On the stone body: if I fall from a height or am pulled apart, I break into slabs and the light goes out of the seams. I don't die. The pieces want each other. If they lie within a few hundred paces I'd expect to gather in about a day, and the further apart they are, the slower it is, up to a season if they're scattered across a slope. A slab that's carried off is a piece I'm missing. I'd be smaller and a little less sure of myself until I got it back, or found another stone to take its place.

As for a name, people who don't know it would call me whatever they've got. Herdsmen would say Old Cairn or the Grey Heap. Children would say the Stacked Man. Traders would say something shorter, probably Mark. Any of those is fine with me, and I'd like to hear which one sticks."

## DM:

"Then you will know them and they will know you, and the herdsmen will see a heap of stones. That much is yours without asking."

"You said a sworn word should bind the way a debt binds, and now you say you cannot speak a false one. So: when you give your word to one like yourself, what do you set behind it? And is there a thing you would never swear to?"

(oog) Rulings on what you brought, then the last edge cases. After your next answer I will read the whole card back.

Passing for smaller. Holding still as a cairn is your form and costs nothing. I propose that the seam-light banks down to nothing when you are still and attending to nothing, and shows as soon as you attend. So a cairn that is watching can be caught watching, most easily in the dark. The drover I am declining as a shape. You are slabs, taller than a tall man, with no face. A cloak, dusk and distance are things you can try in play like anyone, and they will work as well as they would. Changing shape would be a separate power and you have not asked for one.

What honesty buys. The weakness, as I will write it: you cannot say what you know to be false, by body or by cairn, and you cannot set or leave standing by your own hand a marker you know to be false. Silence, a true word beside the point, and letting someone mislead themselves are all open to you. Anyone who learns this can use it: a straight question, and your silence read as an answer.

I cannot give you belief itself. Making a hearer know a thing is true is a power over minds, and it would also make you right, which you told the world a god should not always be. Here is what I will give. Your word carries weight a hearer can feel: whoever hears you, at your body or at a cairn, feels plainly that the speaker means it. Not that it is so. That you hold it to be so. A cold traveler at a pass will not take your voice for a trick of the wind or a liar's call. They can still judge you mistaken, or not for them, and you can be mistaken. Others like you feel the same weight and are as free to doubt. Whether mortals and gods come to rely on your word is reputation, and that is earned below, not granted here. One thing more comes with it: the weight cannot be counterfeited at your stones. A voice that is not yours, speaking at a cairn of yours, does not carry it.

Oaths. What a sworn word between gods is in this world is fixed for all of you, and it is not mine to strengthen: such a word is a real thing, nothing stops a god from breaking one, and what comes of breaking one is not written anywhere a god can read. What I can settle is you. Two things follow from what you are. You cannot swear what you do not mean to keep, because that would be a false word. Whether you are able to break a word afterwards is your choice to make now. If you want it, I will write that you cannot knowingly act against a word you have sworn, in the same way you cannot lie. That is a second, heavier weakness: a bad oath, or one someone tricks you into wording badly, holds you until the other party lets you go. I would count it as paid for by the part you asked for, being known as a witness worth having, only to this extent: it is true of you and you can truthfully say so. Tell me yes or no.

The body. Accepted as you described. Broken, you fall to slabs and the light goes out. Pieces within a few hundred paces gather in about a day, and wider scatter takes longer, up to a season across a slope. A slab carried off leaves you smaller and less sure until you recover it or take a new stone in its place. I add: while you are in pieces you have no sense and no voice at your cairns, and you know nothing of the time that passes. Honest cairns go on keeping travelers.

The remaining edges. Say if any of these is wrong.

- Pace and strength. You walk at a steady walker's pace and do not run. You do not tire. You can carry or drag about what a strong ox could, slowly.
- Water. You need no air. You can cross a firm riverbed or lake bed on foot. Deep mud, bog and silt hold you, and getting out can take days or help.
- Staying put. No mortal effort shifts you once settled. Enough people with levers and time can still take a settled cairn apart slab by slab. What another god can do to you is not settled today and will be ruled when it happens.
- Laying a cairn. A cairn is hand-laid when you build it or set a stone on it yourself, which takes about an hour of your attention. There is no limit on how many. If a hand-laid cairn is thrown down or moved, it goes out of you, like one lost to the voice, until you set it right by hand.
- Distance. The sense and the voice reach any hand-laid cairn however far. Reaching a far one takes longer than a near one, and so does coming back.
- Who hears. Other gods, and anything else with ears that has been quiet, hear the voice at a cairn the same as a herdsman does.
- Your people. They can raise honest cairns in your name. They cannot make one hand-laid.

Last, for the record I need something to file you under until the world settles on a name. I will write "the Cairn" unless you would rather something else.

## Player:

"What I set behind my word is the line itself. Every stone I've laid, and the truth every traveler leans on when they trust one. If I break a sworn word, I'd have made every cairn of mine a little less believable, and I'd feel that. That's the only collateral I have, and it's worth more to me than anything I could name. I'd put it behind any oath I give.

Things I would never swear: anything that needs a lie to keep. Anything that closes a road for good, or leaves a traveler lost on purpose. Obedience, to a god or a mortal, with no end and no terms. And anything open-ended that I can't see the end of. If I swear, I want it narrow, with a named thing and a named time, because I'll be held to the words exactly.

(oog) Rulings and edges.

Passing for smaller: accepted. Seam-light banking to nothing when I'm still and attending to nothing, showing when I attend, is a good tell and I like being catchable. Fine to drop the drover. I'll use cloak, dusk and distance in play, and take whatever those get me.

Honesty: accepted as written, including the weakness to a straight question and a silence read as an answer. The weight you give my word, the plain felt sense that I mean it without any guarantee that I'm right, is better than what I asked for. It keeps me fallible. Not being counterfeitable at my stones is a nice protection and I'll take it.

Oaths: yes, I'll take the heavier weakness. I cannot knowingly act against a word I've sworn, same as I can't lie. I accept that a badly worded oath binds me until the other party lets me go, and it means I'll swear rarely and narrowly. One clarification I'd ask for in the write-up, not a new power: if keeping an oath would require me to say a false word or set a false marker, the oath doesn't make me do it. I'd stay silent and the oath goes unkept, and I'd bear whatever follows from that. Without this clause two of my own weaknesses could collide, and I'd rather you decide now than in the middle of a play.

Body: accepted, including no sense or voice while I'm in pieces and no knowledge of time passing. Honest cairns still keeping travelers is the part I like.

Edges, all fine as written. Walker's pace, no running, no tiring, an ox's load slowly. No air needed, firm beds crossable, bog and silt are dangerous. Settled means mortals can only take me apart slab by slab with levers and time, and you rule on gods when it happens. An hour of attention to hand-lay a cairn with no cap on number. A thrown-down or moved hand-laid cairn goes out of me until I set it right, and I take it that I don't feel it happen. I find out when I reach for it, same as the voice price. Distance doesn't matter to reach, but far stones take longer to get to and longer to come back from. Other gods and anything with ears hear the voice. My people can raise honest cairns in my name but can't hand-lay one.

"The Cairn" is fine for the file."
