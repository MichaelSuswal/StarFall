# What the Carrowfolk know of the land

Campaign: sim-01. Written by the Keeper at the lock, for the civ agent and the DM. This is what people in Carrow would say if asked, in their own names for things. None of it is the god's knowledge until someone tells it or it goes and looks. When a Carrowfolk tells the god of a place, the DM enters it with `tools/map.py reveal` at the grade given, with that person as the source. The ids are the map's.

The Carrowfolk are not always right. Distances are in days as they walk them.

## Where they go themselves (tell as reported)

- The valley's hamlets, thirteen in all. Four the map names: Tophouses, the highest (`upper-hamlet`); Stonefold (`stonefold`); Birch End (`birch-end`); Flockmeet, the lowest and largest, where the autumn gathering is held (`flockmeet`).
- The lower valley, where the water goes down south-east in three falls (`carrowfoot`, `lower-carrow-water`).
- The pass at the valley head (`carrow-pass`) and the bare tops round it (`pass-tops`). Open about thirty weeks in a common year.
- The Old Men: three great stacked stones on the pass, there before anyone, built of slabs no team in the valley could shift (`old-men`). Travelers add a pebble at the foot and do not climb them.
- Windlow, the long bare ridge west of the valley, summer grazing (`windlow`).
- The Broken Hills to the south-west, where strays go (`broken-hills`).
- Over the pass: the Sallow, a high pale basin with a salt lake, five or six days from Tophouses (`sallow`, `brine-mere`), and Brinehall, the salt people's town (`brinehall`). About thirty of the young cross each summer. The Sallowmen sell salt and a little iron and take wool and cheese. They are close with money and fair with measures.
- The stream heads below the valley, rough pasture (`wend-heads`).
- A fold above Tophouses with its gate walled up (`walled-fold`). Everyone knows it. No one grazes it and no one will say why.

## What they have only heard (tell as rumor; places are rough)

- "The long water": the Carrow Water becomes a river that runs south for a month's walk (`wend`, near 285, 420).
- "A walled town at a ford, far down the water, where they make you swear to things" (`corrford-town`, near 300, 500).
- "The dark wood" south-west beyond the Broken Hills, where no one lives (`holt-wood`, near 170, 430).
- "The white wall": great snow mountains north and east, seen from the tops on a clear day (`skarn`, near 300, 90).
- "Mountain folk east along the wall, who buy the same salt we do" (`stair`, near 480, 200).
- "A water a long way south-east, too wide to see across." Sallow carriers' talk (`enner`, near 450, 430).
- "The sea is south-west, two months off, or west, or there isn't one." No Carrowfolk has seen it (`the-murk`, near 120, 640).
- "A stone on Windlow with a swept doorstep." Herders' talk (`shut-house`, near 200, 295).
