# The unnamed people: the people's record

Campaign: sim-03. Written by the DM at the close of creation Day 3, 2026-11-20, from the people read back to the player and agreed. Lines in quotation marks are the player's words, from `player/transcript-day3.md`. Stores, treasure, trade, strength and mood are the Keeper's to set on Night 3a. Anything about the land or its past is in the last section, as wish or belief, and in `player/people-meeting.md` for the Keeper to reconcile.

The player left the people and their town unnamed: "Mortals name things best themselves, and I'd rather learn their names than hand them one." This folder's name, `unnamed-people`, is a placeholder and not a name. Nothing here is to be named until play names it.

## Who they are

Human. "They are woodland keepers more than farmers. They coppice, cut charcoal, graze the clearings, forage and cultivate mushrooms, tend small gardens and orchards, and keep a few pigs on the autumn mast." "They are careful and slow to trust, and they know that a bad year can end a family. I want them practical, a little grim, and stubbornly loyal to their own valleys."

## How many, and how they live

"About five thousand people, living in one town and the farms and clearings around it." The game's default, taken as offered. The town is unnamed. "They have no wide fields, because the ground won't take them." "Their wealth is wood, charcoal, cured mushrooms, and seed that breeds true, which they guard."

## Their code

- "They bury their dead in the soil, not on pyres, because burning wastes what the ground gave. This is a custom and not a doctrine, and it should be tied to old habit."
- A few words are said at the grave, "spoken by the eldest of the family or by whoever the dead person trusted, and that is the whole ceremony."
- "They are wary of salt and fire being used against land, and they say so as a rule of manners." It is manners only. It changes nothing on the god's card and stops no one from laying a line at a threshold. The player: "I didn't plan that as protection for me. It's fair if the world makes it a secret that someone could learn and exploit."
- "They keep seed in twists of cloth, the way I carry mine. I'd like that to be an old custom of theirs that I only recognized, so it isn't a coincidence I engineered."

## Skills and culture

Coppicing, charcoal burning, grazing, foraging and growing mushrooms, curing them, gardens and orchards, pigs on the mast, keeping seed lines. Ordinary, and unpriced. They keep hearths and burn charcoal; fire is part of their days.

## Priced edges and weaknesses

None on the people.

The seed is not an edge, at the player's word: "On the seed, I don't want a sure edge. Leave it as their craft and their pride. If it turns out to be rarer or better than other peoples' seed, I'd like the world to decide that, not me." That they hold it to breed true and guard it is so. What it is worth beside any other people's seed is the world's.

The one edge the player asked for (that the steward could speak a question into tended ground) would have changed the god's own powers. It was refused as asked, priced in a smaller shape on the god, and is on `player/power-card.md` as a power block marked "added Day 3" (the steward's pressing).

## Their magic

None described by the player. The one knack agreed is the steward's pressing, which belongs to the god's card and sleeps until the steward knows who it is for. Her cost (a day of her own speech each time) was set by the DM subject to the Keeper's reconciliation. Any other knack among them is the world's to fit.

## What they believe

Left to the world by the player. "I'd like to leave that to the world, and I'll record only a wish: that they don't have a god of their own, and don't mind." Of the grave words: "I'd like those words to be old, plain, and a little odd, so that they sound like something a mushroom would say. I'd like to learn them, not replace them." Both are wishes.

They do not know a god is coming. No one among them holds a priest's office by the player's design; who says the grave words is as under Their code.

## How they are ordered, and how they settle disputes

"A council of the heads of the old houses, about a dozen, who really do rule and argue among themselves. I don't want to rule them. I want them to be able to make decisions I disagree with, and I'm willing to take the consequences of that. The council is theirs, not mine." No court. No priests. The houses are unnamed.

Disputes: "The council settles the large ones, but I'd like the small ones to go to whoever both sides already trust, usually an elder of a third house. That gives the town a habit of settling things before they reach the council, and it gives the council a reason to be slow and quarrelsome. I won't take any part in it."

## Helpers

Recorded 2026-11-20, creation Day 3.

The player turned down the default body of helpers (no court, no priests, no command over the council) and designed three people, with no chain of command. Each is in `people/`. All are unnamed.

- **The steward.** Gravekeeper and orchard keeper. The only one orders travel through. She carries them to the council "as advice, never command."
- **The seed keeper.** Holds the town's seed lines.
- **The track warden.** Keeps the paths and the watch.

Their standing instruction, once they serve: "to follow my conduct and my last decisions, and otherwise to tend the town's ground and keep the peace."

**At landing none of them serves.** "Nothing. None of them hears me, and none has seen me. The gravekeeper is a woman at her work who has never met me." "The seed keeper and the warden don't know I exist." So the god lands with no helpers in fact. Until the steward knows what the god is and takes the office, no one acts for the god, its unplayed time runs on standing conduct alone, and its wants do not reach the town. The DM said so plainly and the player accepted: "Landing with no helpers in fact is the price of the shape I chose, and I accept it."

Whether any of them comes to serve is theirs, in play. The player sets who they are, never what they will do. No agent may write any of them as already serving, knowing the god, or carrying its word before play has made it so.

How orders travel, once she serves: god to steward, steward to council as advice. The seed keeper and the warden hear nothing from the god except through her or in person. If she is sick, dead, unwilling or wrong, nothing travels. The player: "I know the one-woman route is my choice and my risk. If she dies, falls sick, or turns against me, I'll find new ways to reach the others, and I won't treat the loss as a defect in the rules."

## The god's place among them

"A burial ground and orchard at the edge of town, on deep soil, where I can tend ground and plant lines. I'd rather be unremarkable there than central. It should be a place where an odd, damp-handed person sitting on the ground is normal. Their word for it can be whatever they call it." Unnamed. The god lands with no tended ground.

## Wishes and beliefs about the land and its past

Recorded as the player's wishes or the people's beliefs. The DM confirmed none as fact.

- That they live in valleys ("loyal to their own valleys").
- That the ground will not take wide fields.
- That the burial ground and orchard stand on deep soil at the edge of town.
- That the burial custom, the seed in twists and the grave words are old.
- How long they have been in this country and what was there before them: left to the world. "I'd like their own telling to be a little vague and a little defensive about it, in the way of people who don't fully know."
- The player said of the brief: "I'll take the smoke and the track as the world's business and not mine."
