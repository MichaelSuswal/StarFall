Year 1. Autumn, and evening. The light is going. You are down, and you stand barefoot on the trodden earth of a worn way, cold and wet under your soles, with deep leaf litter over living soil at either edge. It has rained lately and it will again. The air smells of rot and rain, of leaf mold, wet stone and fungus, and there is woodsmoke on it, thin, from the south-east. Water drips from the canopy. Close by, water runs a short way and then goes down into the ground with a hollow note. There are no voices, and no beasts close by. Your body is whole, unhurt and unhurried. It is one body, and you feel each of its edges. The linen is on you and the cloth bag is with you, and in it the seed in its twists of cloth, the sealed crock of starter and the short bone-handled knife.

The trees around you are very large and very old. Fallen ones lie where they fell, with young growth coming up through them, and fungi are up everywhere, on the logs and through the litter. A few paces off, pale rock breaks the surface, slick with wet. There are hollows and sinkholes among the trees, some dry, some with water standing at the bottom, one or two too deep to see into. Through the gaps in the canopy the cloud is breaking up, and where it breaks, high overhead, there is one small pale blue light, steady.

The way you stand on is wide enough for two abreast. About a mile of it is in view, running from the north-west to the south-east. Beside it there are coppiced stools and cut stumps, and in the clearings the turf is grazed short. About two miles to the south-east, smoke rises, enough of it to be a town's. The town itself is not in view. There is no one on the way.

You have no tended ground and no tended line, so the ground-ear is silent, and there is nowhere to send a slow word from. The soil under the litter is alive; your hands would know it if you put them in. You know that others like you came down when you did. You feel nothing of any of them: no direction, no nearness, no weight. That comes only face to face, and no one is near. You remember what you have always been able to say of yourself. The rest is fog.

The map is attached, drawn in close. It shows the whole of the forest country you stand in, because the map clears a country at a time once you are standing in it; what your own eyes take in from here is only what the story above gives. The worn way and the smoke are marked as your own sight reports them, and may be wrong in the details until you have walked them. At this scale the two marks sit almost on one point. The name printed across the wood is the map's own label, and no one has said a name for it in your hearing. You can ask for the map at any time.

The year is yours from this moment until the world turns at 1:30. Nothing is waiting on you and no one has asked you anything.
