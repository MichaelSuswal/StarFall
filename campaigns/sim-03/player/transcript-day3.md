# Transcript: creation Day 3 (sim-03)

## DM:

You are still falling, and now there is something under you. The voice is still with you.

"Look down. This is where you land."

Old forest on broken ground. The trees are very large and very old. The fallen ones lie where they fell, with younger growth coming up through them. The leaf litter is deep and wet. Pale rock breaks through it in ribs and ledges, and the ground is holed: hollows and sinkholes, some dry, some with water standing at the bottom, some too deep to see into. Streams run for a while and then go into the ground. Where the land drops, the forest thins into wet ground and marsh. It is autumn, and fungi are up everywhere. The air that reaches you smells of rot and rain.

There is woodsmoke on that air. A worn track runs through the trees, wide enough for two to walk abreast, going somewhere. Near it are coppiced stools and cut stumps, and in the clearings the turf is grazed short.

"That is all the land shows from here, and it does not say who made the track. A god comes down to lead a people, and today is for yours. So I will begin where every one of you begins: about five thousand of them, with a town, a place in it that is yours, a council, and helpers who keep things running when you are somewhere else. Tell me who they are. What kind of people, how they live in country like this, what they do with their days. And tell me who runs things for you when you are not there."

"Begin wherever you like and say as much as you like. I will ask where I do not follow, and I will tell you what a thing costs before it is theirs."

(oog) Three plain notes before you start.

Today builds one people, your own. You said on Day 1 you would sooner tend several peoples from underneath than rule one from the front. That stands on record. The other peoples are the world's, and you find them in play; nothing in today's talk closes that off. What today settles is the people you begin with, and how you stand among them.

On helpers, you asked to hear the options before deciding, and said you lean toward few, chosen carefully, with wants of their own. The options are offices, not named persons: a court, a council of elders or heads of houses, priests, stewards, captains, or a single steward, in any mix and at any size. With each goes the question of how orders travel: who hears you, and who carries it on. Whoever holds an office is a mortal with their own wants; none is a puppet. What helpers do is act for you inside your standing conduct and your last decisions when you are away from the table or in the ground.

Anything you tell me about the land or its past (what the track is, what was here before, what lies beyond) I record as your people's belief or as your wish, not as fact. The world decides, and the god will see. The names of your people and of their home are yours to give, or to leave unnamed.

## Player:

Thank you for the land. That's close to what I asked for, and I'll say so plainly: the pale ribs of rock, the holes, and the streams that go underground are exactly the country I wanted. I'll take the smoke and the track as the world's business and not mine.

**Who they are.** About five thousand people, living in one town and the farms and clearings around it. They are woodland keepers more than farmers. They coppice, cut charcoal, graze the clearings, forage and cultivate mushrooms, tend small gardens and orchards, and keep a few pigs on the autumn mast. They have no wide fields, because the ground won't take them. Their wealth is wood, charcoal, cured mushrooms, and seed that breeds true, which they guard. They are careful and slow to trust, and they know that a bad year can end a family. I want them practical, a little grim, and stubbornly loyal to their own valleys.

I'd like three things said about how they live:
- They bury their dead in the soil, not on pyres, because burning wastes what the ground gave. This is a custom and not a doctrine, and it should be tied to old habit.
- They are wary of salt and fire being used against land, and they say so as a rule of manners. I didn't plan that as protection for me. It's fair if the world makes it a secret that someone could learn and exploit.
- They keep seed in twists of cloth, the way I carry mine. I'd like that to be an old custom of theirs that I only recognized, so it isn't a coincidence I engineered.

**The town and my place in it.** I want my place to be a burial ground and orchard at the edge of town, on deep soil, where I can tend ground and plant lines. I'd rather be unremarkable there than central. It should be a place where an odd, damp-handed person sitting on the ground is normal. Their word for it can be whatever they call it.

**How I stand among them.** I don't want to be worshipped or to arrive as a god. I'd like to come down as a stranger who turns out to be good with the soil, a grave-keeper or orchardist, and be known first by what I do. I'll tell anyone who asks me directly and sincerely, as I said, but I won't announce it. If this conflicts with "a god comes down to lead," then I'm leading from the edge, and I'll accept whatever that costs in speed.

**The council.** No priests. No court. I want a council of the heads of the old houses, about a dozen, who really do rule and argue among themselves. I don't want to rule them. I want them to be able to make decisions I disagree with, and I'm willing to take the consequences of that. The council is theirs, not mine.

**My helpers, few and chosen.** I decide on three. I don't want a chain of command.

1. **A steward**, the one who hears me. An older woman, a gravekeeper and orchard keeper by trade, who runs my ground when I'm in the earth. She is pragmatic, sharp, and tired of people. She wants her ground to outlast her, and she is not sure she wants to be a steward at all. She is the only person orders travel through, and she carries them to the council as advice, never command.
2. **A seed keeper**, a young man from a small, poor house. He holds the town's seed lines and knows more about breeding true than anyone. He wants his house to matter, and he will be tempted to trade or hoard seed. That is a real flaw and I want it in the game.
3. **A track warden**, a blunt former hunter who keeps the paths and the watch. He distrusts anything that doesn't walk on the ground, and so he'll distrust me. He wants the town safe more than he wants my approval. He's the one who will hear first that something is wrong at the edges.

Each has their own want that may cut against mine. None is a puppet. Their standing instruction when I'm away is to follow my conduct and my last decisions, and otherwise to tend the town's ground and keep the peace.

**Questions for the price.**
- Does a steward who listens to me count as a helper, with a cost, or is this just a mortal who happens to be loyal? I'd like to know what each of the three costs me.
- Can my helpers do any tending that counts for my powers, or is "worked with your own bare hands" strictly mine? I expect strictly mine, and want that confirmed.
- I'd like one edge today that is stronger than it should be, so I'll ask: that my steward can, once a season, speak to me through the ground I have tended, as a spoken question to me, not a reply I hear. It would be a one-way message from her to my ground, heard when I next listen. Tell me what you'd charge.

That's what I have. I'd like to hear the cost of the three helpers before I decide whether to keep all three.
