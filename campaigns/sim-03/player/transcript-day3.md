# Transcript: creation Day 3 (sim-03)

## DM:

You are still falling, and now there is something under you. The voice is still with you.

"Look down. This is where you land."

Old forest on broken ground. The trees are very large and very old. The fallen ones lie where they fell, with younger growth coming up through them. The leaf litter is deep and wet. Pale rock breaks through it in ribs and ledges, and the ground is holed: hollows and sinkholes, some dry, some with water standing at the bottom, some too deep to see into. Streams run for a while and then go into the ground. Where the land drops, the forest thins into wet ground and marsh. It is autumn, and fungi are up everywhere. The air that reaches you smells of rot and rain.

There is woodsmoke on that air. A worn track runs through the trees, wide enough for two to walk abreast, going somewhere. Near it are coppiced stools and cut stumps, and in the clearings the turf is grazed short.

"That is all the land shows from here, and it does not say who made the track. A god comes down to lead a people, and today is for yours. So I will begin where every one of you begins: about five thousand of them, with a town, a place in it that is yours, a council, and helpers who keep things running when you are somewhere else. Tell me who they are. What kind of people, how they live in country like this, what they do with their days. And tell me who runs things for you when you are not there."

"Begin wherever you like and say as much as you like. I will ask where I do not follow, and I will tell you what a thing costs before it is theirs."

(oog) Three plain notes before you start.

Today builds one people, your own. You said on Day 1 you would sooner tend several peoples from underneath than rule one from the front. That stands on record. The other peoples are the world's, and you find them in play; nothing in today's talk closes that off. What today settles is the people you begin with, and how you stand among them.

On helpers, you asked to hear the options before deciding, and said you lean toward few, chosen carefully, with wants of their own. The options are offices, not named persons: a court, a council of elders or heads of houses, priests, stewards, captains, or a single steward, in any mix and at any size. With each goes the question of how orders travel: who hears you, and who carries it on. Whoever holds an office is a mortal with their own wants; none is a puppet. What helpers do is act for you inside your standing conduct and your last decisions when you are away from the table or in the ground.

Anything you tell me about the land or its past (what the track is, what was here before, what lies beyond) I record as your people's belief or as your wish, not as fact. The world decides, and the god will see. The names of your people and of their home are yours to give, or to leave unnamed.
