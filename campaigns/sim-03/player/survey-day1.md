# Day 1 World Survey: the player's answers

Campaign: sim-03. Creation Day 1, 2026-11-20. Survey: `engine/creation/day1-world-survey.md` (19 questions, fixed).

The answers below are the player's own words as given in the chat, verbatim and unedited. All 19 questions were answered; none was skipped. Bent or rejected premises and requests not to be told something are noted separately in `rejected-premises.md`.

1. The feel is of a world that was here long before us and will be here long after. Think of a forest floor after rain, where everything is quietly eating everything else and nothing is wasted. It should be beautiful without being kind. It should be indifferent, and it should also feel layered, so that anything you dig into has something older under it.

2. Old forest on broken ground: deep leaf litter, fallen giants, limestone and sinkholes, with wet marsh at the low edges. I want a land with a lot going on underneath it. If a mountain range comes with it, I'd like it far off and rarely seen.

3. Four real seasons, with a long, wet, rotting autumn that is the richest time of year. Winter should be hard enough to kill people, and spring should come late and all at once. I want to wake into autumn when each year turns. That is when the ground is most alive, and I'd rather start the year in harvest and decay than in bloom.

4. Some of each, but weighted toward old and rooted. I'd like to find kingdoms that have forgotten why they were built the way they were. I'd also like a few stretches of country no one has walked, kept rare so that they matter.

5. Very old: ruins under ruins, with at least one buried age that mortals only half remember and tell wrong. Some peoples should be recent arrivals, so the old and the new rub against each other.

6. Low and uneven. Most people doubt it, a few valleys have a wise-woman, and some places are plainly stranger than others. I don't want it everywhere. I'd like it to be a rumor that turns out to be true when you go and look.

7. It should feel like tending and listening more than command: a knack for coaxing, brewing, and bargaining with things that grow and rot. The cost should be bodily and slow. It should cost them time, health, or something they'd only notice years later. I dislike clean spell costs. I'd like the price to be something a mortal can misjudge.

8. Barely more than a great sorcerer, and I'd like that to be true of every god, me included. If I'm obviously a god, I get worshipped, and I don't want to play that. I want to be able to pass as an odd, patient mortal and be underestimated. Gods who stand out should draw attention from the others, and that's their problem.

9. Strange, but with internal logic. A few legends, plus creatures that are really just ecology taken to an extreme, like a thing that is also a forest or a beast that migrates underground. I'd like nameless things to be rare enough to be an event.

10. Slow dangers that you don't notice until it's too late: blight, sickness, hunger, a bad harvest year after year. I also like danger that is wrong but not hostile, like something in the woods that is simply not paying attention to you. Monsters are fine, but I'd rather the real threat be a bad winter.

11. Salt, and clean seed, meaning grain that breeds true. Salt is something everyone needs and few places have. Clean seed is something that took generations to keep and can be lost in one bad year. Both make for trade, theft, and long grudges.

12. Old roads that go nowhere now, sealed doors in the limestone, and something very large and very dead that the roots have grown through. I'd like the bones to be too big to be sure they're bones. I'd also like at least one thing buried that wants to stay buried.

13. Yes, a few, and not elves. I'd like one people who are close to the land and live in a pace of years rather than days, and one that is truly unreadable. Mortals should be unsure whether either counts as a person. I'd like to meet them slowly and badly.

14. Few, and only one or two at first. I like the idea of a lone traveler with tools no one understands and knowledge that is half right. I'd like them to be a problem for the mortals and not a gift to me.

15. Face to face only, and imperfectly. If I can feel other gods at any distance, then the world is small and the politics are cheap. I'd like to be able to be fooled. A god's presence could show up as something small and wrong, like a place where the mushrooms grow out of season. Recognition should take a little detective work.

16. Yes, and this is the center of my god. I want to be many bodies, a network and not a single figure, with the pieces spread across the land. The cost should be that I'm worse at being any one of them. Each body should be a little less me, and if one is lost, something of me goes with it, a memory or a habit. A body far from the others should drift and start wanting things I didn't.

17. Someone who tends things: a healer, a gardener, a grave-keeper, a midwife, or a cook who ends up holding a town together. I'd like people who matter because of what they quietly keep alive, and not only kings and heroes. I'll follow a good one for a long time.

18. A companion, and something to do with the dead and what comes after them. I'd like to find out what is underneath all this, and why the old forest and the old ruins line up so closely. I'd like the answer to be something I can't predict now.

19. I wish that nothing in this world exists only for me. I want things to go on without me, and I want to come back after a long absence and find that they changed, and that I didn't cause it. Please don't build a plot around my god. Build a world, and let me find out what it does.
