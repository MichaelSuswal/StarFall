# Follow-up questions for Day 3

Campaign: sim-03. Written by the Keeper after Night 2, for the DM to weave into Day 3. Few. Each comes from something the god's card or conduct leaves open that bears on its people. The DM writes the player's answer under each before the close.

1. In the fiction: "There will be a season when you are in the ground and cannot be asked. Who do they go to then?"
   (oog) fallback: Your slow return says that while you mend or grow back you do nothing, "and no one acts for you unless helpers do." You lean toward few helpers, chosen carefully. When you are gone for a season, who, if anyone, speaks or decides in your place?

   Answer (Day 3): The steward, once she serves, and no one before that. "A steward, the one who hears me. An older woman, a gravekeeper and orchard keeper by trade, who runs my ground when I'm in the earth." "She is the only person orders travel through, and she carries them to the council as advice, never command." At landing she does not know the god, so until play makes her a steward no one speaks or decides in its place; the council rules the town on its own judgment either way. The question was answered from the player's own account of the helpers and was not put in the Keeper's words.

2. In the fiction: "They keep fires. Where do you sit in a house with a hearth?"
   (oog) fallback: Your weakness names fire, and a line of fire that stops you. Your people will cook and keep warm with it. How do you mean to live among hearths: is there anything you ask of them, or do you take it as it comes? Nothing on the card changes either way.

   Answer (Day 3): "At the edge, near the door, on the floor if I'm allowed, and as far from the fire as the room will let me, without making a point of it. Fire hurts me more than it hurts mortals, and I would rather be thought odd for sitting by the door than be thought strange for flinching. If a host presses me to sit closer, I'll say I'm cold-blooded and prefer the draft, which is close enough to true. I won't lie outright about why. If someone asks directly and sincerely whether fire troubles me, I'll say it does." It asks nothing of the people. Nothing on the card changes.

3. In the fiction: "Some of them will kneel before you have said a word. What do you do with the ones who taught them to?"
   (oog) fallback: You will ask a kneeling mortal to get up. If the people you come to already have someone whose place it is to keep rites, what do you want done about that office?

   Answer (Day 3): "I ask them to get up, plainly, and I tell them I'm a person working. If they keep kneeling, I stay on my feet and keep working, and I don't make a speech. I won't punish anyone for believing something about me, and I won't correct them by force. As for whoever taught them to kneel: I don't want to take that away from them. If an old woman says the grave words and it comforts people, I'll stand beside her, learn what she knows, and be a better listener than a theologian. If someone has taught them to kneel to power in general, I'd rather out-wait it than fight it." The player designed no priest's office; the grave words are said by the eldest of the family or whoever the dead trusted. Nothing is to be done about any rite-keeping office the world gives them.
