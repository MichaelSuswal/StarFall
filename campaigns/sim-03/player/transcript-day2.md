# Transcript: creation Day 2 (sim-03)

## DM:

You are still falling. There is no ground yet and nothing to count the time by. The voice that asked you nineteen things is still here, closer now, and it is done asking about the world.

"Your words went down ahead of you. Now it is you I want. What comes down is not settled yet, and it settles by being told. So tell me what you are. Begin wherever you like: the shape of you, a name, who you were and what you lost, what you can do, what you want from the ground when it comes. Say it in any order and at any length. I will ask where I do not follow, and I will tell you what a thing costs before it is yours."

"One thing first, because you called it the center of you. Where you are going, a god has one body and is in one place. That is so for every one of you who is falling. Tell me what you are, knowing it."

(oog) Plainly, so nothing is hidden in the voice: one body, one place, is a fact of being a god in this world, the same for every god, and it is not open to negotiation today. What that one body is, and what it can do, is exactly what today is for. Anything that would let you reach, sense or act beyond the one body is a power, and a power is described by you, priced by me with a drawback, tested against edge cases, and written on your card. So describe the god you want with that in hand, and we will see what it comes to.

(oog) You also asked on Day 1 what it costs to be a gardener and not a king, and to tend several peoples from underneath. The answer as far as the rules give it: nothing is charged for the premise itself. The game does not punish playing it your way, and your note is on record. The costs are practical ones. With one body you are in one place, so any people you are not standing among goes on without you. A people ordinarily comes with helpers who act for its god while the god is away. A god that keeps no helpers has no one acting for it, and when you are not at the table it runs on its standing conduct alone and its people drift. How many peoples you tend and what your own people is are tomorrow's conversation (Day 3). What you want and what you will not do are today's, and they matter, because your standing conduct is built from them.

## Player:

Understood, and I accept it. One body, one place, the same for everyone. My Day 1 answer 16 assumed otherwise, so I'll rebuild the god around the rule and not argue it.

**What I am.** I am a mycelial god, the slow body under the forest floor. My one body is a body I can walk around in. It looks like a thin, patient person of no particular age, with pale hands that are always a little damp. It smells of leaf mold and wet stone, and small fungi come up wherever I rest for long. I don't have a throne look. I look like someone you'd step around on a path.

**Name.** I don't have one yet. The mortals can give me one, and I expect it will be wrong. For now, call me the Understory.

**Who I was and what I lost.** Before the fall I was the quiet part of something large, the connective tissue between other lives. What I lost is the connection itself. I came from a place where I was never alone and never one thing, and I am now a single body that feels each of its edges. I want it back, and I know I can't have it the way it was. That is why I'll try to build it again slowly, out of mortals, trees, and old ground.

**What I want from the ground.**
1. To learn the land's old layers: what is buried, and why the forest and the ruins line up.
2. To keep things alive that would otherwise be lost: seed lines, graves, the half-remembered knowledge of a valley.
3. To be underestimated, and to be useful before I am known.

**What I will not do.** These are my standing conduct, so please write them down.
- I will not rule by fear or demand worship. If a mortal kneels, I'll ask them to get up.
- I will not burn, clear, or poison a forest for advantage, even a losing one.
- I will not break a promise made to a mortal, and I will think hard before I make one.
- I will not lie about what I am to someone who asks me directly and sincerely, though I may answer slowly and decline to volunteer.
- I will not pick a favorite child and sacrifice the rest of a people for it.
- I will not force a mortal's choice. I'll tend, nudge, and feed, but not puppet.

**Powers.** I'm going to ask for something stronger than it should be, and you can price it.

**Ask 1, the ground-ear.** While I stand with my bare hands or feet on living soil, I can listen through the roots and fungal threads, with no limit on range, and learn anything that touches the ground within my forest: footfalls, digging, fires, deaths, who has walked where and roughly when. It's a perceptive power, and it lets me stay in one place and still know the land. I know it is stronger than a god ought to have, because with it I could know almost everything that happens in my country. Tell me what it costs.

**Ask 2, the slow word.** I can seed a mortal's field, garden, or grave with something living and small that will carry a message or a mood to them over days: a flush of mushrooms in a pattern, a smell, a dream. It's slow and ambiguous, and it can be misread. This would be my way to act where I am not, without having a second body.

For both, I would like a drawback that is real. A few ideas I'd accept: it only works on ground I have personally touched and tended first; I am nearly blind and deaf to the ordinary world while I do it; it leaves me open to blight, so that a sickness in the soil I listen through reaches me; and the more I listen, the less of myself I keep for talking to people. Pick what's fair and tell me the exact edge cases.

**Weakness I'm volunteering.** Fire, salt, and a dead or sterile land hurt me more than they hurt mortals. I would rather you wrote that down than have me hide it.

**On helpers.** You said a god with no helpers is only a standing conduct when I'm away. I'd like to hear the options tomorrow before deciding anything. I'd like the helpers to be people with their own wants, and I won't have them as puppets. I'll say now that I lean toward few, and chosen carefully.

**One question back.** If I never leave a given stretch of ground, can that ground become, over time, a place that is more mine than other ground is? I'm not asking for a power. I'm asking whether staying put can build up something the rules would recognize.
