# Transcript: creation Day 2 (sim-03)

## DM:

You are still falling. There is no ground yet and nothing to count the time by. The voice that asked you nineteen things is still here, closer now, and it is done asking about the world.

"Your words went down ahead of you. Now it is you I want. What comes down is not settled yet, and it settles by being told. So tell me what you are. Begin wherever you like: the shape of you, a name, who you were and what you lost, what you can do, what you want from the ground when it comes. Say it in any order and at any length. I will ask where I do not follow, and I will tell you what a thing costs before it is yours."

"One thing first, because you called it the center of you. Where you are going, a god has one body and is in one place. That is so for every one of you who is falling. Tell me what you are, knowing it."

(oog) Plainly, so nothing is hidden in the voice: one body, one place, is a fact of being a god in this world, the same for every god, and it is not open to negotiation today. What that one body is, and what it can do, is exactly what today is for. Anything that would let you reach, sense or act beyond the one body is a power, and a power is described by you, priced by me with a drawback, tested against edge cases, and written on your card. So describe the god you want with that in hand, and we will see what it comes to.

(oog) You also asked on Day 1 what it costs to be a gardener and not a king, and to tend several peoples from underneath. The answer as far as the rules give it: nothing is charged for the premise itself. The game does not punish playing it your way, and your note is on record. The costs are practical ones. With one body you are in one place, so any people you are not standing among goes on without you. A people ordinarily comes with helpers who act for its god while the god is away. A god that keeps no helpers has no one acting for it, and when you are not at the table it runs on its standing conduct alone and its people drift. How many peoples you tend and what your own people is are tomorrow's conversation (Day 3). What you want and what you will not do are today's, and they matter, because your standing conduct is built from them.

## Player:

Understood, and I accept it. One body, one place, the same for everyone. My Day 1 answer 16 assumed otherwise, so I'll rebuild the god around the rule and not argue it.

**What I am.** I am a mycelial god, the slow body under the forest floor. My one body is a body I can walk around in. It looks like a thin, patient person of no particular age, with pale hands that are always a little damp. It smells of leaf mold and wet stone, and small fungi come up wherever I rest for long. I don't have a throne look. I look like someone you'd step around on a path.

**Name.** I don't have one yet. The mortals can give me one, and I expect it will be wrong. For now, call me the Understory.

**Who I was and what I lost.** Before the fall I was the quiet part of something large, the connective tissue between other lives. What I lost is the connection itself. I came from a place where I was never alone and never one thing, and I am now a single body that feels each of its edges. I want it back, and I know I can't have it the way it was. That is why I'll try to build it again slowly, out of mortals, trees, and old ground.

**What I want from the ground.**
1. To learn the land's old layers: what is buried, and why the forest and the ruins line up.
2. To keep things alive that would otherwise be lost: seed lines, graves, the half-remembered knowledge of a valley.
3. To be underestimated, and to be useful before I am known.

**What I will not do.** These are my standing conduct, so please write them down.
- I will not rule by fear or demand worship. If a mortal kneels, I'll ask them to get up.
- I will not burn, clear, or poison a forest for advantage, even a losing one.
- I will not break a promise made to a mortal, and I will think hard before I make one.
- I will not lie about what I am to someone who asks me directly and sincerely, though I may answer slowly and decline to volunteer.
- I will not pick a favorite child and sacrifice the rest of a people for it.
- I will not force a mortal's choice. I'll tend, nudge, and feed, but not puppet.

**Powers.** I'm going to ask for something stronger than it should be, and you can price it.

**Ask 1, the ground-ear.** While I stand with my bare hands or feet on living soil, I can listen through the roots and fungal threads, with no limit on range, and learn anything that touches the ground within my forest: footfalls, digging, fires, deaths, who has walked where and roughly when. It's a perceptive power, and it lets me stay in one place and still know the land. I know it is stronger than a god ought to have, because with it I could know almost everything that happens in my country. Tell me what it costs.

**Ask 2, the slow word.** I can seed a mortal's field, garden, or grave with something living and small that will carry a message or a mood to them over days: a flush of mushrooms in a pattern, a smell, a dream. It's slow and ambiguous, and it can be misread. This would be my way to act where I am not, without having a second body.

For both, I would like a drawback that is real. A few ideas I'd accept: it only works on ground I have personally touched and tended first; I am nearly blind and deaf to the ordinary world while I do it; it leaves me open to blight, so that a sickness in the soil I listen through reaches me; and the more I listen, the less of myself I keep for talking to people. Pick what's fair and tell me the exact edge cases.

**Weakness I'm volunteering.** Fire, salt, and a dead or sterile land hurt me more than they hurt mortals. I would rather you wrote that down than have me hide it.

**On helpers.** You said a god with no helpers is only a standing conduct when I'm away. I'd like to hear the options tomorrow before deciding anything. I'd like the helpers to be people with their own wants, and I won't have them as puppets. I'll say now that I lean toward few, and chosen carefully.

**One question back.** If I never leave a given stretch of ground, can that ground become, over time, a place that is more mine than other ground is? I'm not asking for a power. I'm asking whether staying put can build up something the rules would recognize.

## DM:

"The Understory. I will keep you under that until someone gives you a worse name. I have the shape, the damp hands, the smell, the small things that come up where you rest. I have what you were and what you lost, and the six things you will not do. Those are written."

"Two things I do not have. A god cannot die, but its body can be hurt, held, burned, cut apart, shut out. When yours is broken, what happens to you? And you told me what you want that you would say to anyone. What do you want that you would not say aloud?"

(oog) Now the pricing. I am trimming Ask 1 and saying so plainly: as written it reaches a whole country with no limit, includes the past, and would settle most of what there is to find out by standing still. Here is what I can sign. Argue any line.

**Definition: tended ground.** Living soil you have worked with your own bare hands until your threads have taken in it. An acre takes you about three days of work (five worker-days by hand, at a god's rate). A tended line is a strip a pace wide, laid by walking it barefoot and slowly, about a mile a day. Ground stays tended while its soil stays alive. Fire down to the mineral soil, salting, poison or stripping ends it, and you must tend it again. Ground frozen hard is silent until it thaws, and is still tended after. I cannot promise you a forest, so nothing here is written on "my forest". It is written on what you tend, wherever you land.

**Ask 1, the ground-ear, as priced.** With bare hands or feet in tended ground, you hear what touches the surface of all tended ground joined to you by an unbroken tended line, and about a bowshot past its edges. No limit on distance along the line. What you get is weight, number, gait, direction, digging, felling, fire, and a body going into the soil. You get no words, no faces, no names. You know a tread only if you have stood near that walker in the flesh before. You hear the living layer and nothing under it: not what is beneath stone, behind a sealed place, or in ground with no life in it. You hear what happens, never why. The past is cut to what the soil still holds: footfalls for a few days, digging, fire and burial until the next winter.

Edge cases. Light and dark make no difference. Running water wider than a stride breaks a line unless roots cross it; marsh carries if it is alive; open water is silent. Paving, bare rock and floors are silent. Shoes and gloves block it, and soil in a pot is not joined to anything. While you listen your body sits where it is, and anyone can see it, move it or hurt it. If your skin is lifted from the soil the listening ends. Another god's tread sounds like any tread. The ground-ear never tells you a walker is a god, because a god knows another god only face to face, close, as a weight or unease that grows surer with time and can be wrong. That is a fact of this world for every god, along with knowing that others came down when you did. Ground worked by more than ordinary craft, a mortal's or a god's, is unruled until it happens, and so is anything this does to another god. Your own people get no exemption. Each listening takes at least an hour to sink into and an hour to come out of, so it is not a glance.

Given, not chosen: while you listen you are blind, deaf and numb in the body. Then the drawback, and you pick one:
- **A. The ground comes back up.** Whatever sickness, poison, salt or burning is in ground you listen through reaches your body, and you cannot know it is there before you listen. A blighted field heard is a season of your own body sick.
- **B. The listening spends your speech.** For as long as you listened, and never less than a day, you cannot hold speech with people. Words come slow and wrong, and you cannot make or keep up a conversation that matters.

**Ask 2, the slow word, as priced.** From tended ground, you send one thing along the line to one plot that is itself tended ground: a field, a garden, a grave you have had your hands in. It carries one image or one mood, no words and no names. It comes up over three to ten days as a flush, a smell, or a dream for whoever sleeps nearest that plot. One slow word in the ground at a time. It does not rule a mind: a dream can be shrugged off, a mood passes, a pattern can be read wrong or kicked over, and the mortal's reading is theirs, not yours or mine. You do not learn whether it arrived or how it was taken unless you listen or go. Anyone who walks past can see or smell it too, and what they make of it is theirs. Frost, fire or digging before it comes up kills it. Sent to ground another god holds or to a god in person, it is unruled until it happens.

Drawback, pick one:
- **A. You send what you have.** The mood or image leaves you while it is in the ground. Send calm and you are without calm until it fruits or dies. Send a memory and you cannot recall it until then. If it is killed in the ground, it comes back a season late.
- **B. You are rooted to it.** Until it fruits or dies you cannot leave the tended ground you sent from. Step off and it dies unsent.

**The weakness, and the one offer of a trade.** A weakness taken on can buy an improvement elsewhere. You do not have to take one. As you gave it, fire, salt and dead land hurting you more than a mortal is mostly a thing your form already pays, so to buy anything it needs a price you do not already carry. A version that buys: wounds from fire or salt do not close until you have lain a full season in living soil; you weaken each day you spend on dead or sterile ground and by the third day can do nothing but leave; and a mortal who knows it can hold a threshold against you with a line of salt or of fire. That is a real handle for anyone who learns it. It would buy one of these: the ground-ear's memory of footfalls lengthened from days to a full season; or the slow word reaching a plot you have only touched once, not tended; or two slow words in the ground at once. If you would sooner keep the weakness as plain color and buy nothing, say so and it goes under Form at no price.

**Your question.** Yes, and the answer is the definition above. Staying put and tending makes tended ground, and both powers run only on it, so ground you have kept for years is more yours than other ground in a way the card recognizes. Tending is work, and work produces what the time spent on it produces. Beyond what the card defines I have nothing to tell you today.

Helpers stay for tomorrow, with your lean toward few and chosen noted.
