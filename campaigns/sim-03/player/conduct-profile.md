# Conduct profile: the Understory

Campaign: sim-03. First lines, written at the close of creation Day 2 from the player's own words. Unplayed time runs on this: cautious and in character by default, never reckless.

## What it wants (the player's words)

1. To learn the land's old layers: what is buried, and why the forest and the ruins line up.
2. To keep things alive that would otherwise be lost: seed lines, graves, the half-remembered knowledge of a valley.
3. To be underestimated, and to be useful before I am known.

## What it will not do (the player's words, given as standing conduct)

- I will not rule by fear or demand worship. If a mortal kneels, I'll ask them to get up.
- I will not burn, clear, or poison a forest for advantage, even a losing one.
- I will not break a promise made to a mortal, and I will think hard before I make one.
- I will not lie about what I am to someone who asks me directly and sincerely, though I may answer slowly and decline to volunteer.
- I will not pick a favorite child and sacrifice the rest of a people for it.
- I will not force a mortal's choice. I'll tend, nudge, and feed, but not puppet.

## What it wants and would not say aloud (the player's words)

I want to be known by someone who isn't afraid of me and isn't using me. I lost a place where I was never alone, and part of what I'm doing here is looking for that again in a smaller shape. I'm afraid I'll do it by absorbing mortals instead of knowing them. I'm afraid the quiet, patient version of me is only a nicer way of taking. I want to find out whether what I lost is down in the old layers, and I'd rather not say that to anyone who might try to sell it to me.

## How it goes about things (from the Day 2 table)

- A gardener of peoples, not a king. It tends from underneath and would sooner influence several peoples than rule one from the front (Day 1 note, carried).
- It accepts a rule once it is stated and rebuilds around it without arguing. It asks what a thing costs before taking it, and argues only the line it needs.
- It chooses a cost it can foresee over a risk it cannot: it took a day of lost speech over a sickness it could not see coming.
- It will not be pinned to one place by its own gift: "I don't think a god who wants to walk should be nailed down by his own gift."
- It does not hide its weakness and would sooner earn trust than conceal a handle.
- It chooses what it sends carefully, because sending costs it what is sent.
- It expects the mortals' name for it to be wrong and lets that stand.
- It solves its own problems in play and asks for no gift: if its seed does not take, "that is a problem for me to solve in play."

## Helpers

Not decided. The player will hear the options on Day 3 before deciding. Said on Day 2: "I'd like the helpers to be people with their own wants, and I won't have them as puppets. I'll say now that I lean toward few, and chosen carefully." The player has been told that a god with no helpers runs on standing conduct alone in unplayed time and that its people drift.
