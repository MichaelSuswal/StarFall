# Rejected and bent premises

Campaign: sim-03. For the Keeper's follow-ups. Written by the DM at each day's close; later days add their own section below. Full answers are in `survey-day1.md`.

## Day 1

No question's premise was rejected outright. One premise was bent, and it is the survey's standing one.

1. **The standing premise, that the god comes down to rule a land and a people.** The player accepted leading and bent the rest, in a note after answer 19: "I'm happy to lead a people, but I'd like to be a gardener of them and not their king. I'd rather influence several peoples from underneath than rule one from the front, and if that's a problem, tell me what it costs."
   Two things are bent: the manner (tending from underneath, not ruling from the front) and the number (several peoples, not one). The same wish shows in answer 8: "If I'm obviously a god, I get worshipped, and I don't want to play that. I want to be able to pass as an odd, patient mortal and be underestimated."
   The player asked what it costs. That question is open. The DM did not answer it on Day 1, whose close allows only the closing lines, and it is carried into Day 2 with the note, which is kept verbatim under `## Also said` in `survey-day1.md`. Nothing has been decided or priced.

Requests not to be told something: none.

## Not rejections

The DM's observations, not the player's statements. Nothing here has been inferred into a decision.

- Question 19: "Please don't build a plot around my god. Build a world, and let me find out what it does." A wish about how the world is built, that nothing in it exists only for this god. It names no particular thing the player asks not to be told, so it is not recorded as such a request and it closes off no follow-up question.
- Question 18: "I'd like the answer to be something I can't predict now." A wish about what there is to discover, not a request not to be told.
- Question 13: "I'd like to meet them slowly and badly." A wish about the pace and manner of meeting the peoples who aren't human, not a request not to be told what they are. "Not elves" is an answer inside the question.
- Question 8: the player chose the low end of the range offered ("Barely more than a great sorcerer") and asked that it hold for every god, "me included". Inside the range. The wish not to be worshipped is noted under the bent premise above.
- Question 15: "Face to face only, and imperfectly", with a god's presence showing as "something small and wrong, like a place where the mushrooms grow out of season." The question offers "or something else", so this is inside the range. The player also wants to be able to be fooled.
- Question 16: the player chose several bodies and named the cost, as the question asks. Inside the range. The player calls it "the center of my god", so it is a Day 1 wish that Day 2 will lean on. How many bodies a god may have is set through the surveys and given before Day 2 (DESIGN 3.13); this is the player's one voice on it.
- Question 11: two scarcities named, salt and clean seed ("grain that breeds true"), where the question asks for one thing. Answered inside the question.
- Questions 2, 12 and 18 describe the land and what lies under it (limestone, sinkholes, sealed doors, the forest and the ruins lining up). These are wishes. The DM confirmed none as fact.

## Day 2

Written by the DM at the close of creation Day 2. Full talk in `transcript-day2.md`.

### Rejected or bent by the player

No premise was rejected on Day 2.

- **The Day 1 wish for many bodies (answer 16) was set down by the player.** On hearing the rule of one body: "Understood, and I accept it. One body, one place, the same for everyone. My Day 1 answer 16 assumed otherwise, so I'll rebuild the god around the rule and not argue it." The god was rebuilt as one walking body that reaches through ground it has tended.
- **The standing premise stays bent as on Day 1** (a gardener, not a king; several peoples from underneath). The player asked on Day 1 what it costs. Answered on Day 2: nothing is charged for the premise; the costs are practical (one body in one place; a god with no helpers runs on standing conduct alone in unplayed time and its people drift). How many peoples, and what its own people is, were left for Day 3.
- **Helpers: not decided, leaning away from the default.** "I'd like to hear the options tomorrow before deciding anything. I'd like the helpers to be people with their own wants, and I won't have them as puppets. I'll say now that I lean toward few, and chosen carefully." Recorded as a lean, not a refusal.
- **Worship refused as conduct:** "I will not rule by fear or demand worship. If a mortal kneels, I'll ask them to get up."

Requests not to be told something:
- What is lost on a return from nothing: "I'd like that to stay unspecified, so I find out by noticing a gap."
- The hidden powers: "Fine. I'd rather not know."

### Bent by the DM

These are the DM's reframings and trims, not the player's wishes. Each was announced and accepted.

- **The ground-ear was trimmed.** Asked for: no limit on range, anything that touches the ground within the god's forest, including who has walked where and roughly when. Signed: tended ground joined by an unbroken tended line and a bowshot beyond; the surface of the living layer only; effects, never causes; no words, faces or names; a short memory. "My forest" was not written, because the DM cannot promise a forest; the power is written on what the god tends.
- **The slow word was tied to tended ground,** sent from it and to a tended plot, one at a time. The player then bought reach to touched plots with the weakness.
- **The weakness was given a price by the DM.** As volunteered (fire, salt and dead land hurt more than they hurt mortals) it bought nothing. The DM proposed the version with teeth: wounds that do not close without a season in living soil, three days on dead ground, a salt or fire line that holds both ways. The DM also made "dead or sterile ground" exact after the player had accepted, and put it back for a second yes.
- **The broken body was written as a power.** The player gave a wish and asked for the rule. The DM proposed the terms and the drawback, taken from the player's Day 1 answer 16, and the player agreed.
- **The player's four drawback ideas were sorted by the DM:** "only on ground I have touched and tended first" and "nearly blind and deaf while I do it" became given conditions; "open to blight" and "the less of myself I keep for talking to people" became the priced choices for the ground-ear. The player chose the second.
- **Rates were set by the DM** for tending an acre and laying a line.
- **The question about ground becoming more the god's own** ("can that ground become, over time, a place that is more mine than other ground is?") was answered only through the definition of tended ground. The DM said it had nothing more to tell.
- **The player's descriptions of the land** (limestone country, sinkholes, "my forest") were recorded as wishes. The DM confirmed none as fact.

## Day 3

Written by the DM at the close of creation Day 3, 2026-11-20. Full talk in `transcript-day3.md`.

### Rejected or bent by the player

No part of the people was refused outright. The default (about 5,000, a town, a seat, a council, helpers) was taken in size and bent in every part that touches rule.

- **Rule.** The council is the people's, not the god's. "I don't want to rule them. I want them to be able to make decisions I disagree with, and I'm willing to take the consequences of that. The council is theirs, not mine."
- **Court and priests refused.** "No priests. No court."
- **The seat.** Not a seat of rule: a burial ground and orchard at the town's edge. "I'd rather be unremarkable there than central."
- **Arriving as a god refused.** "I don't want to be worshipped or to arrive as a god. I'd like to come down as a stranger who turns out to be good with the soil." "If this conflicts with 'a god comes down to lead,' then I'm leading from the edge, and I'll accept whatever that costs in speed."
- **Helpers bent, not refused.** Three people, no chain of command, one channel through the steward, carried to the council as advice. None of them knows the god at landing, so the god lands with no helpers in fact. Priced plainly by the DM: until the steward serves, no one acts for the god, unplayed time runs on standing conduct alone, and its wants do not reach the town. Accepted: "Landing with no helpers in fact is the price of the shape I chose, and I accept it."
- **A sure edge in seed declined.** "I don't want a sure edge. Leave it as their craft and their pride."
- **Left to the world by the player:** what the people believe about gods; how long they have been there; what came before them.
- **Left unnamed by the player:** the people, the town, the burial ground, the houses, the steward, the seed keeper, the track warden.

Requests not to be told something: none on Day 3.

### Bent by the DM

The DM's rulings and reframings. Each was announced and accepted.

- **Several peoples.** The Day 1 wish to tend several peoples was not built on Day 3. The DM said Day 3 builds one people, the god's own, and that the others are the world's and found in play. The wish stands on record.
- **The spoken question was refused.** Asked for: that the steward could, once a season, speak a question into tended ground, heard at the next listening. Refused because the ground-ear hears "no words, faces or names", a limit agreed on Day 2. Priced instead as one image or mood, on the god's card. The player did not argue the limit.
- **Helpers were not given a price.** The player asked what each of the three costs. The DM answered that helpers are the default and unpriced, that the costs are practical, and that the defaults turned down counted in the player's favor when the edge was priced.
- **Leading from the edge was not charged.** Its practical cost was stated: a people that does not know it has a god does nothing in its god's name.
- **Tending by helpers:** confirmed as strictly the god's own, as the card says.
- **The player's descriptions of the land and its past** (valleys, ground that takes no wide fields, deep soil at the town's edge, old customs) were recorded as wishes or beliefs. The DM confirmed none as fact. The player said the brief was "close to what I asked for"; the DM made no reply to that.
