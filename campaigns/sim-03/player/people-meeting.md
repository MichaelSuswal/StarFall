# How the Understory meets its people

Campaign: sim-03. Written by the DM at the close of creation Day 3, 2026-11-20. Lines in quotation marks are the player's words.

## What was agreed

The people are already there, with their own past. They are waiting for nothing and do not know a god is coming. They did not arrive with the god.

"I don't want to be worshipped or to arrive as a god. I'd like to come down as a stranger who turns out to be good with the soil, a grave-keeper or orchardist, and be known first by what I do. I'll tell anyone who asks me directly and sincerely, as I said, but I won't announce it."

None of the three people the player designed as helpers knows the god at landing. See `civs/unnamed-people/record.md`, Helpers.

## Standing conduct for the first morning

Decided by the player: "I walk in barefoot at the edge of the burial ground in the early morning, kneel in the soil, and start working." Where the god comes down, and what stands between that place and the burial ground, are the world's. The DM told the player so. The intent stands as conduct: go to the burial ground at the town's edge, kneel, and work the soil, saying little.

Decided: answer anyone who asks directly and sincerely what it is; announce nothing; "make it hard to ask by being plain." Tell the steward "in my own time, and not before she has a reason to believe me."

## Heard, not promised

- "I would like her to see a stranger who is good with the ground and who doesn't talk much. She can be suspicious of me, and I'd expect her to be."
- That the seed keeper and the warden "will form their own opinions of me."
- That the warden distrusts the god, and is "the one who will hear first that something is wrong at the edges."
- That the seed keeper "will be tempted to trade or hoard seed."
- That the steward comes to trust the god and takes the office.
- That an odd person sitting on the ground in the burial ground is thought normal there.
- That whoever says the grave words lets the god stand beside them and learn.

## For the Keeper to reconcile

The DM cannot read the deep history. Each item is the player's wish or the people's belief, confirmed by the DM as neither true nor false.

1. How long the people have been in this country, by their own telling. Left to the world. Wish: "a little vague and a little defensive about it, in the way of people who don't fully know."
2. What they say came before them. Left to the world, with the same wish.
3. What lies past their borders. The player said nothing of it. Their wealth names things that could be traded (wood, charcoal, cured mushrooms, seed); with whom is the world's.
4. The burial custom, "tied to old habit": how old.
5. The seed kept in twists of cloth, "an old custom of theirs that I only recognized": how old, and whether it is theirs alone.
6. The grave words, wished "old, plain, and a little odd, so that they sound like something a mushroom would say": their age and what they are.
7. What they believe about gods. Left to the world. Wish: "that they don't have a god of their own, and don't mind."
8. The town: one town with farms and clearings round it, about a dozen old houses. Whether and where it stands is the world's. The brief shows woodsmoke, a worn track, coppice, cut stumps and grazed turf, and no town.
9. The burial ground and orchard "at the edge of town, on deep soil". Whether the soil is deep is the world's. The god's first tending depends on it.
10. "Valleys", and ground that "won't take" wide fields.
11. The manners about salt and fire used against land. An ordinary custom, unpriced. The player allows that the world may make the god's weakness something that could be learned through it.
12. The seed "that breeds true": not an edge. What it is worth beside other peoples' seed is left to the world at the player's word.
13. The steward's pressing: the steward's half of the price (a day of her own speech each time) was set by the DM by shape and is subject to the Keeper fitting it to the world's terms for mortal magic. For the Keeper's review and ledger.
14. The old houses and the council: the people's own order, set by the player; listed in case the deep history bears on how old the houses are.
