# Day 3 brief: the land you come down into

Campaign: sim-03. Written by the Keeper after Night 2, for the DM, in words the player may hear. It says what the land itself shows and nothing else. Who lives there, how many, and what they are called are for the Day 3 table and are not in this file.

## The season

Autumn. The year opens in it, as the god asked.

## The country

Old forest on broken ground. The trees are very large and very old, with fallen ones lying where they fell and younger growth coming up through them. The leaf litter is deep and wet. The rock under it is pale and breaks the surface in ribs and ledges, and the ground is holed: hollows and sinkholes, some dry, some with water standing at the bottom, some too deep to see into. Streams run for a while and then go into the ground. Where the land drops, the forest thins into wet ground and marsh.

It is a rich season. Fungi are up everywhere. The air smells of rot and rain.

## What the land shows of people

Woodsmoke on the air. A worn track, wide enough for two to walk abreast, going somewhere. Coppiced stools and cut stumps near it. Turf grazed short in the clearings.

That is all the land shows from where the god comes down. Nothing in this file says who made the track.

## What is not in this file

No distance, direction or line of sight: those come from the map, after it is traced. Nothing of the past. Nothing of anyone or anything else that came down.

## Mortal magic, for pricing a people

It is simple, and it costs the worker. The Keeper fits a people's magic to the world on Night 3.

## What the DM cannot settle at the table

Anything the Day 3 talk raises that depends on the world (where a place is, what an old thing was, whether something the player describes is so) is listed by the DM in `campaigns/sim-03/player/people-meeting.md` under `## For the Keeper to reconcile`. The Keeper answers each item on Night 3. The player's own names for its people and their home stand; anything the player leaves unnamed stays unnamed until play names it.
