# Day 1 World Survey: the player's answers

Campaign: sim-07. Date: 2026-11-25 (Pacific).

Mode: standard

1. A long afternoon in a working town. Smoke from a hundred small chimneys, the smell of wet clay and hot brick, and everything old enough to have been repaired more than once. It should feel lived in and slightly warm to the touch.

2. River country with clay banks, set against low hills with woodland for fuel. I want good clay, a hill I can quarry, and enough rain that people have to build well. I'd rather not have mountains or desert.

3. Four real seasons, with a long dry stretch in late summer that is good for making and firing. Winters should be hard enough that a bad roof or a thin wall costs people something. For waking, my first choice is September, so I arrive with the dry season's work still fresh and have time to fire before frost. My second is March, at the thaw, when the ground can be dug and the year's building is still ahead.

4. Some of each. I want old kingdoms rooted far enough off to trade with and learn from, and wild country near enough to claim. I'd like to find one or two ruined works I can read like a book, to see how someone else's walls held up.

5. Thousands of years, with ruins under ruins. A world that has been building and losing things for a long time is more interesting to build in.

6. Closer to the wise-woman end, but quiet. A few people in most valleys have a knack. It should be a craft, the kind a person gets better at, and not a gift handed out.

7. It should feel like skill that has gone slightly past what hands can do. The cost is time and wear on the body. A mage who works too much goes stiff and slow, the way a potter's hands do.

8. Barely more than a great mortal sorcerer. I don't want to be the one-eyed man. I want to be the one who stays when the others leave, and I'd rather people trust my work than fear me.

9. Familiar animals, a few legends, and one or two things no one has a name for, far from where I'm building. The wild should be strange enough to need walls and ordinary enough that walls work.

10. Slow dangers. Floods that take a bridge, a hard winter, a bad harvest, rot in the timbers, and a neighbor who covets a finished city. I like threats that arrive on a calendar and can be prepared for, with one or two that can't.

11. Good fuel and fired brick or tile, which makes dry roofs and tight walls scarce. Mortals want shelter that outlasts a generation, and most go without. I'd like that scarcity to drive trade and quarrels.

12. Old roads, with a few sealed doors, and some very old kiln sites under the soil. I want to dig into the past and find someone else's mistakes in it.

13. A few, and I'd keep them few. I want one people who build in a different way from humans, so I have something to learn from and argue with. I don't want a race of simple helpers.

14. A few travelers. I like one or two who bring a tool or a technique the locals have never seen, and who are tired and trying to settle. They shouldn't arrive in numbers.

15. Face to face, with a faint pull at close range. I'd like to read another god by what they've made and left behind, and know them by their works.

16. One body. I'd rather be in one place and do the work than be everywhere and do none of it. If helpers come, I'd want them to be people with their own wills, not extensions of me.

17. A builder or a keeper. Someone with a story worth following because they finish things, such as a mason, an engineer, a widow who runs the yard. I'd like her to be difficult and right more often than not.

18. A truth about why things fall. I'd like to find out what makes a work last and what makes it quietly fail, and I'd like that to turn out to matter in a way I can't see yet.

19. I want something I build to still be standing, and still in use, long after anyone remembers who made it. I'm not asking for my name on it. I'd like it to belong to the people who use it.
