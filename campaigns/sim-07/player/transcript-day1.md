# The Between: Day 1

Campaign: sim-07. Date: 2026-11-25 (Pacific).

## DM:

(oog) Would you like to play mirror-mode, or in standard mode? In mirror mode, a few of your god's smaller gifts are drawn from you as a person. In standard mode, nothing is.

You are falling. Not through air. Through something thinner. Behind you is wherever you were, already too far to see. Below is a world that doesn't know you yet. This is The Between: the long descent from the place you left to the place you will land. Gods come down here to lead a people: to find them or gather them, to build, to rule, and to spread. That is what the world below expects of you, and what the others falling beside you expect of themselves. Others fall with you. You can't see them, but you can feel them, each telling the world below what they wish it to be. The world is listening to all of you. It won't grant everything, and you'll learn later which wishes it kept. Answer what speaks to you. Skip what doesn't.

1. When you imagine this world at its truest, what does it feel like? Give me a mood or an image.
2. What land calls to you? Mountains, marsh, coast, forest, steppe, desert, ice, river country, or somewhere stranger.
3. What should the seasons be like in this world? And when each new year finds you, which month would you most like to wake into? Name two, your first choice and your second.
4. Past the borders of your own land, what do you hope to find? Wild country no one has walked, old kingdoms already rooted, or some of each?
5. How old is the human story here? Thousands of years, ruins under ruins, or people who arrived within living memory?
6. How much magic should mortals have? Never gone entirely and never everywhere. Somewhere between a rumor most people doubt and a wise-woman in every valley.
7. If mortals have magic, what should it feel like, and what should it cost them?
8. How much should a god stand out here? Like the one-eyed man in the kingdom of the blind, or barely more than a great mortal sorcerer?
9. How strange should the wild be? Familiar animals and a few legends, or creatures no one has a name for?
10. Beyond each other, what should mortals fear here? Name the kind of danger that makes a good story to you.
11. What should be scarce in this world, the thing mortals want most and have least, whether they trade for it, fight for it, or go without?
12. What would you like to find buried in this land? Ruins, bones of something enormous, old roads, sealed doors, or nothing at all?
13. Should there be peoples here who aren't human? If so, what are they like?
14. Should there be travelers who came from somewhere far away, few or many, and brought strange knowledge or tools with them?
15. You already sense that others like you have arrived. How do you hope gods will recognize each other: a pull felt at any distance, a knowing only face to face, or something else?
16. Can a god be in more than one place at once? One body, or several, and if several, what should it cost?
17. Sometimes a mortal rises with a story worth following. What kind of mortal do you hope this land produces?
18. What should a god be able to discover here that it can't yet imagine? A place, a power, a truth, a companion?
19. Last: one wish for this world you haven't dared say yet. The world only listens, but this is when it's listening.

## Player:

Standard mode.

Q1. A long afternoon in a working town. Smoke from a hundred small chimneys, the smell of wet clay and hot brick, and everything old enough to have been repaired more than once. It should feel lived in and slightly warm to the touch.

Q2. River country with clay banks, set against low hills with woodland for fuel. I want good clay, a hill I can quarry, and enough rain that people have to build well. I'd rather not have mountains or desert.

Q3. Four real seasons, with a long dry stretch in late summer that is good for making and firing. Winters should be hard enough that a bad roof or a thin wall costs people something. For waking, my first choice is September, so I arrive with the dry season's work still fresh and have time to fire before frost. My second is March, at the thaw, when the ground can be dug and the year's building is still ahead.

Q4. Some of each. I want old kingdoms rooted far enough off to trade with and learn from, and wild country near enough to claim. I'd like to find one or two ruined works I can read like a book, to see how someone else's walls held up.

Q5. Thousands of years, with ruins under ruins. A world that has been building and losing things for a long time is more interesting to build in.

Q6. Closer to the wise-woman end, but quiet. A few people in most valleys have a knack. It should be a craft, the kind a person gets better at, and not a gift handed out.

Q7. It should feel like skill that has gone slightly past what hands can do. The cost is time and wear on the body. A mage who works too much goes stiff and slow, the way a potter's hands do.

Q8. Barely more than a great mortal sorcerer. I don't want to be the one-eyed man. I want to be the one who stays when the others leave, and I'd rather people trust my work than fear me.

Q9. Familiar animals, a few legends, and one or two things no one has a name for, far from where I'm building. The wild should be strange enough to need walls and ordinary enough that walls work.

Q10. Slow dangers. Floods that take a bridge, a hard winter, a bad harvest, rot in the timbers, and a neighbor who covets a finished city. I like threats that arrive on a calendar and can be prepared for, with one or two that can't.

Q11. Good fuel and fired brick or tile, which makes dry roofs and tight walls scarce. Mortals want shelter that outlasts a generation, and most go without. I'd like that scarcity to drive trade and quarrels.

Q12. Old roads, with a few sealed doors, and some very old kiln sites under the soil. I want to dig into the past and find someone else's mistakes in it.

Q13. A few, and I'd keep them few. I want one people who build in a different way from humans, so I have something to learn from and argue with. I don't want a race of simple helpers.

Q14. A few travelers. I like one or two who bring a tool or a technique the locals have never seen, and who are tired and trying to settle. They shouldn't arrive in numbers.

Q15. Face to face, with a faint pull at close range. I'd like to read another god by what they've made and left behind, and know them by their works.

Q16. One body. I'd rather be in one place and do the work than be everywhere and do none of it. If helpers come, I'd want them to be people with their own wills, not extensions of me.

Q17. A builder or a keeper. Someone with a story worth following because they finish things, such as a mason, an engineer, a widow who runs the yard. I'd like her to be difficult and right more often than not.

Q18. A truth about why things fall. I'd like to find out what makes a work last and what makes it quietly fail, and I'd like that to turn out to matter in a way I can't see yet.

Q19. I want something I build to still be standing, and still in use, long after anyone remembers who made it. I'm not asking for my name on it. I'd like it to belong to the people who use it.

## DM:

The world below has taken your words in with the others, and it gives nothing back yet. The fall goes on, and the ground is a night nearer.
