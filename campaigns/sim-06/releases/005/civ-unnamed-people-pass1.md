## The year
One count of months. Month 1 is the first month of autumn in your country. Months are thirty days. The market falls every third day without a break across months and years: on the second, the fifth, the eighth, and so on to the twenty-ninth, in every month.
This turn does not cover Year 5 for your people. It covers one stretch: from the fourteenth day of the seventh month of Year 3, at the door of the potters' hall, through the rest of Year 3 and on into Year 4, as far as the fourteenth day of the tenth month of Year 4. The first part of it, to the fourth day of the eighth month of Year 3, was met by the Weightward in person and is fixed. From the evening of that day it is yours.
Write your people's stretch up to Year 4, month 10, day 14, at the opening of the gate, the first held moment, and no further in your public files. Anything past it goes in your sealed files, under the line "Past the first held moment: not yet what happened".

## What was played, from the fourteenth day of the seventh month to the fourth day of the eighth month of Year 3 (fixed; not yours to change)
The decisions file, items 1 to 13, has it, with every figure. In short, as those present saw and heard it:
- The fourteenth day of the seventh month, the potters' hall. The Weightward asked the master of the potters at her door for leave to weigh each piece before the fire and after, and for three spares of each size. She took both on her own terms. The Assay-master said the hall pays for clay and charcoal for a proving, on his word, and that he tells the ring after. The twenty-two were weighed that afternoon, each set in the pan by her, called by the Weightward and cut by the Tally-keeper on a long stick, each foot marked. The Assay-master set the sevens and the twelves pair against pair on the potters' beam and said the beam agrees.
- She asked that the figures of the unfired pieces stay on the stick until the kiln was opened. The Weightward said yes, and said it to the Stallreeve at the gate on the seventeenth. Nobody asked it for one.
- The twenty-fifth: the thirty-three spares weighed the same way, on a second stick. The twenty-sixth and twenty-seventh: one kiln, one firing.
- The first day of the eighth month, the kiln yard: all fifty-five came out whole. Each was weighed again and cut beside its first figure. The Weightward called only what the pan gave and said nothing of which shrank most; no loss was cut as a figure; the sticks carry both figures for every piece and anyone can read them. The master broke the fifth spare of each size with a mallet and looked first: a close grey body, no stone, no hollow. She kept the halves. Both sticks were read through. The Assay-master saw the sevens and the twelves again on the potters' beam.
- The breaking, completed here with its figures, which are settled. The Weightward weighed the halves of each broken piece together and then each alone, and said what the pans gave, and how much of each piece is clay and how much is not clay, sand and shell, as its reading gives it. The Tally-keeper cut what she heard. In its ones:

The other eleven and the spares went back to the potters' hall. The sticks are the hall's, with the Tally-keeper.

## What your people meet, by month
Year 3, the rest of it.
- Month 8, from the evening of the fourth day. Spring; mild; the river falling. The night of the sixth day: the star dims, seen from the landing. About the middle of the month the boat people are gone from the channels for some days, at the lowest tides of spring. The yard has the nest proved and the sticks to read. What the potters, the diggers, the kiln-masters and the yard say of sticks that give every piece before and after the fire is yours; so is whether anyone works the loss out aloud, since the Weightward will not. Asked for a figure, it gives it from the Tally-keeper's cuts (item 14).
- What the Assay-master tells the ring of the hall's paying for the spares, and what the ring says, is yours.
- What the Stallreeve does with the answer of the second day is yours. The Weightward said what it would have the carrier told, and only that: "that the town's word is what the stick says, and that the hall's pieces are the hall's and the old six the town's weight from next autumn's first market." Whether he tells the carrier so, whether three call a sitting, and whether the ring has a second stick cut of the six things it took as the town's word at the winter sitting, are your people's own to decide. The Weightward said that if the ring wants more cut before the tenth month it will say it in session; it has given no words beyond those. If the ring asks it for more words, write the asking in the ring's words: it meets silence in this stretch and stays pending, and the ring knows only that it was not answered before the coasters went.
- Months 9 to 12. Late spring and summer, ordinary. Upriver boats come down in summer as in most years. Settled: in the first days of the tenth month the ship owner of the open water takes his coasters out past the Horn, with his half of the first stick. He is back before the end of the twelfth month. What he says in the town when he is back, to the ring, at his landing and at the gate, is yours, from what the sealed side release gives him to know. Settled: no coaster from the southern harbors came up to the town in the summer of Year 3, and he brings no stick, token or answer from any house or council there.
- Settled: at some time in the stretch the netmaker, or another, asks the Weightward again whether it can tell the height of the water, and it answers in the words of item 16. When, and who, are yours. That the tide-reckoner's telling costs her the feeling in her hands for a season is common talk in your town, and the Weightward has heard it at the gate through four winters.
- A boy asked at the gate in the first month whether the star is the Weightward's, and asked the empty post in the second. It has no decided words for that. If he or anyone asks again, the asking meets silence and stays pending.
Year 4, to the held moment.
- The weather, all through. An ordinary autumn. Winter wet, with a hard spell in the fifth month, and the fever in the back houses that comes with it. The flood at the thaw ordinary. Summer warm.
- Month 1, the second day: the first market of the autumn, the day the ring set. From this market the old six are the town's weight, and the eleven new pieces that sat level with them are what the market weighs by. How the ring, the Stallreeve, the Assay-master and the yard mark the day, what becomes of the hall's ten fired pieces, and how bargains made before it are settled, are yours. If the Weightward is asked to weigh anything in its pans before the yard, it does, and says what it is; what the pans give for the new pieces is in the sealed side release, for you to use only if they are set in its pans. Any other asking of it that day that its decided words do not answer meets silence and stays pending. The boat people are gone from the channels for some days this month.
- Month 2: the hill carriers come down, as every autumn. Whether the old weigher of the hill kin comes with them is yours.
- Month 8: the boat people are gone again for some days, at the lowest tides of spring.
- The term. By your own state file the Weightward's seat runs to the day after the third anniversary of the gatepost, in the ninth month of Year 5. That day is not reached. What the ring and the yard say of it as it comes nearer is yours.
- Month 10, the twelfth day. Settled: a southern coaster comes up the channel and ties up at the town. It is the hull that lay three days at the town in the tenth month of Year 2, with the same master. Her crew trade at the landing in the ordinary way on the twelfth and thirteenth. She stays by her boat, asks the landing who sits in the Weightward's seat now and which is the town's weight, and tells nothing. On the fourteenth, a market day, she comes up to the gate before it opens and stands at the head of the line. Stop there. What she says and wants is not yours to write.
- No other stranger out of the ordinary came in the stretch. Upriver boats of Year 4's summer are not in before the fourteenth day of the tenth month.

## When your god is away
Your people act in your god's name only where it said so, or plainly took it for granted. Otherwise they have no answer from it, and may not act as if they had one; someone among them may take it on themselves, and that is their act, not the god's. A question put to your god while it is away meets silence and stays pending, unless its standing words or conduct profile answer it. Your people know only that they were not answered.
