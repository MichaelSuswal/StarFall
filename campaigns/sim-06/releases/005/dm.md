## Year 3, from the evening of the fourth day of the eighth month to its end
### Spring, the eighth and ninth months
- The yard had the Assay-master's word from the hall door. Fifty-five whole was a thing each trade accounted for in its own way.
- From the fifth day the Tally-keeper kept both sticks on her table and read them aloud to anyone who asked, figure by figure, and nothing more. About forty asked in the month.
- The night of the sixth day the star dimmed, seen from the landing.
- The eighth day, a market: a boy at the gate asked whether the star is the Weigher's own. No decided words answer it. The line told him to hush.
- The ninth day: the master of the smiths stood at the Tally-keeper's table, had the fifteens read, nodded once and asked nothing.
- The eleventh day, a market: a digger of the lower pit asked whether the stick says his clay is good. It answered in the player's words: it does not say which clay is better, or whether the kiln did well. A kiln-master of the east kilns, who said his name at the post, Orrin, asked for the figures of the two sixes and the two twelves, before the fire and after. It gave them from the Tally-keeper's cuts. He worked the loss out aloud to the line, not to the Weigher: a thirteenth off the whole, and not the same thirteenth twice. The Weigher read the next scale. Nothing of that was cut. The yard has "a thirteenth" by heart.
- About the middle of the month the boat people were gone from the channels for some days, as at every lowest tide of spring.
- The sixteenth day of the ninth month: the ring sat, the door shut. The Weigher was not sent for.
- The seventeenth, at the post, before the yard: the Stallreeve brought what the ring let him bring. The hall's paying for the spares was taken as the hall's cost, seven for, three against, one not voting. A motion that the ring itself cut a second stick for the carrier, of the six things it took in the winter, did not carry: five for, five against, one not voting. He gave one reason: a stick cut on a tied vote is a word the ring is not agreed on. He asked the Weigher nothing.
- The same morning the head of the hill goods asked it, as himself, whether it would cut the six things for the carrier now. It answered in the player's words of the second day: nothing more of its own; the second stick is the ring's to cut or leave.
- The twenty-ninth, a market: the Stallreeve told the ship owner of the open water, before the line and in the Weigher's hearing, what the Weigher would have the carrier told, in its words. The ship owner said that was on his stick and he would say it so; and that the pair of things he would not promise stands as he said it in the first month.

### Summer, the tenth to the twelfth month
- The third day of the tenth month, a dawn with no market: the Weigher read the loads of both coasters at the open-water landing. The coasters went out past the Horn on the tide of the fourth, the carrier with his half of the first stick.
- No southern coaster came up to the town that summer.
- The eighth day of the eleventh month, a market: an upriver trader, who said he was of the Aldermark, asked whether one of the hall's old weights is light. It answered in the player's words for a captain who asks about the fifth.
- The twelfth month: the grain came in. The potters' hall fired sets of pieces for the stalls.
- The nineteenth, evening: the coasters came back up the channel. The twentieth, a market: the ship owner of the open water laid his half-stick on the Tally-keeper's table. She fitted it to hers and said it matches. He gave his account before the yard, to the Stallreeve and the Weigher. He said the town's word at Corrith, on the quay, to the masters of the houses his coasters deal with, as cut and no more, and put no price on it. One asked what the Weightward is; he said the Weightward of the hall. One asked whether a southern scale may be read against the hall's; he said a scale is read at the gate, like any. He found no hull and no man of the house of the master who stood at this post two summers before: her house has another port, along that coast, where his coasters do not go. So the word was not put to her house by name. The council's people at Corrith did not ask him for it. Nobody gave him an answer, a stick or a token to carry back. The Weigher said nothing.
- The year turned.

## Year 4, to the fourteenth day of the tenth month
### Autumn, the first to the third month
- The second day of the first month, a market, and the ring's day. Before the yard the Assay-master opened the chest, read the ring's word, and asked the Weigher to call the eleven new pieces in its pans so the market has a figure for each. It called them: 12, 24, 36, 48, 72, 84, 96, 120, 144, 168 and 180, each to a hair. The Tally-keeper cut them. From that market the old six are the town's weight and the eleven are what the market weighs by. The hall's ten fired pieces stand on the hall's bench and serve only to reckon bargains made before that day.
- An old man of the flats asked whether it goes, now the weights are set. It gave its standing answer: until it has seen the town's weights set right; past that it does not know.
- The line was twice its length all that month. Most stallholders' pieces read about a twentieth heavy, being copies of the hall's old pieces; a few read level; a pair were worse and were filed down. Two bargains made before the day came to the gate and the Stallreeve ruled both by the ten, as made. The Weigher read the scales and said nothing of the bargains.
- About the ninth to the thirteenth the boat people were gone from the channels.
- The second month: the hill carriers came down. Their scale read level. The old weigher of the hill kin did not come; the head carrier said at the gate that Ruddfold has the ring's decision and that the old man had seen agreeing done and would not come down a third time. The night of the fifteenth the star dimmed. On the seventeenth a boy asked again whether the star is its own. Silence.
- The third month: rain. The kilns cracked loads as before. The star dimmed on the night of the twenty-second.

### Winter, the fourth to the sixth month
- The star dimmed on the night of the ninth day of the fourth month. On the twentieth two households of netmakers asked it to stand witness to the lease of a weir. It stood, and said what it saw and heard. No power was used.
- The fifth month: a hard spell, and fever in the back houses. On the eighth, a market, the Stallreeve gave out the tide-reckoner's word. A netmaker, the one who asked in the spring of Year 3, asked again whether it can tell the height of the water as she does. It answered in the player's words. The Alder crested on the twelfth at the height she gave. On the twenty-third the woman of the back houses who asked across the ring in Year 2 came to the gate, said who she was and that she had not come since, and asked nothing. The Weigher said what the player decided for her: no, it has no power for it, and it is sorry about her child.
- The sixth month: the ring sat on the fifteenth, the door shut. On the seventeenth the Stallreeve said at the post that it had set no word for the Weigher.

### Spring and summer, the seventh month to the fourteenth day of the tenth
- The thaw; an ordinary flood.
- The eighth month: the boat people were gone from the twelfth to the sixteenth. The star dimmed on the night of the nineteenth. On the twentieth a girl of about six asked, in the boy's words, whether the star is its own. Silence. The boy has been put to a boatwright at the far channels for the season.
- The ninth month: the second year of the seat came round. The line talked of a year to the term; the Stallreeve said the term is three years and a day. The ring sat on the fifteenth with the door shut. On the seventeenth he said at the post that it had spoken of the term, set no day to speak of it again but its first sitting after the grain, and asked nothing of the Weigher.
- The twelfth day of the tenth month, evening: a southern coaster came up the channel and tied up at the town's landing. The boatmen knew her: the hull that lay three days at the town in the tenth month of Year 2, with the same master.
- The thirteenth, a dawn with no market: at the fish landing Bren told the Weigher she was in, and that her master had asked the landing who sits in the Weightward's seat now and which is the town's weight, and had told the landing nothing. The Stallreeve said he had a pair of the watch on the gate as always and would put no more. The Weigher did not go to her boat. The ship owner of the open water's coasters lay loaded at his landing, waiting on a wind.
- The fourteenth, a market day, before light: she came up from the landing in the dark and stood at the head of the line. The story stops there.
