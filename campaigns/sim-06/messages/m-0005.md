# Message m-0005

Year sent: 3 (given and cut in the first month, the fifth day; it goes when the carrier's coasters go out)
Sender: the Weigher, Weightward of the hall, at the market town on the levee, by the eleven's asking
Recipient: in the sender's words: "The word is sent to the captains of the coasting houses of the southern harbors, in the coaster master's own words: to the master who stood at this post in the tenth month of last year, and through her to every house she will tell. I put it to her house by name if the open-water owner knows it. If he does not, it goes to the master of whatever coaster of Corrith's he meets first, to be carried to the council of Corrith that keeps the set for quarrels."
Language: mortal tongue
Carrier: the ship owner of the open water, one of the eleven, named by the eleven to carry it, with the cut half of the split stick
Route: from the town with his coasters when they go out past the Horn; as the carrier said it, to Corrith, to the houses his coasters trade with, and to the council's people there if they ask
Form: spoken, with a cut half-stick

## Text
This is the keeper's word of the hall of this town. It is not a price and not an offer. It says what the hall's pieces are, as read in the Weightward's brass, and what the eleven have said of them.

The hall keeps six old weights, three of bronze and three of stone. In the Weightward's ones, where one is the smallest cup of his nest, they read forty-eight, ninety-six and a hundred and ninety-two for the bronze. The stone read three hundred and eighty-four, seven hundred and sixty-six and fifteen hundred and thirty-six, weighed on the hall's beam with the Assay-master's hand on it.

The hall's fired pieces are not all alike. Eight of them agree with one another, and I called each at twelve and a little better than a half in my ones on the first day. The chipped piece I called at twelve less a hair, and the cracked piece at eleven and something under a half.

The hall's fired pieces are in use this morning, and the eleven have said they stay in use for bargains made before the first market of next autumn. From that market the old six are the town's weight. What was bargained before then is reckoned by the hall's fired pieces as it was made.

## Carried with it
The carrier's half of the split stick the Tally-keeper cut at the gatepost. She holds the kept half.

## Sender's instructions to the carrier
"Carry it as cut and nothing more, and if you are asked what the town's weight is, say what the stick says. If a captain asks what I am, tell him I am the Weightward of the hall and that the Weightward answers yes or no about a power of its own, or does not answer. Do not say more than I did. Do not sell it as a price. If a house wants to send a scale to be read against the hall's, say it may come here, and that I read it at the gate like any."

What the carrier answered, in the Stallreeve's hearing: he carries it as cut and nothing more; asked what the town's weight is, he says what is cut; he will not make it a price. He would not promise to say what the Weightward answers about itself, which is not cut on the stick, nor that a southern scale may be read at the hall, which is on the stick carried to the eleven and not yet answered.

## Fate, as the Weigher knows it
Given at the gatepost and in the carrier's hands. Nothing more is known.
