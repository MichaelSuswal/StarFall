# Follow-ups for Day 3

For the DM. Three questions, each from something the god said on Day 2 that bears on its people. Weave each into the talk in the voice of The Between where it fits; the (oog) form is the fallback. Write the player's answer under each before the day closes. An answer that asks not to be told something is honored and not pursued.

## 1. The factors
From: Seals by factors, and "I want the reach to come through my people."
In the fiction: "You will put your mark into other hands. Whose hands are they, among the people you lead, and what does one of them give up to carry it?"
(oog): Who among your people would you license as a factor, and what, if anything, do you ask of a factor in return for the licence? What holding one costs the mortal is fitted afterward, from your answer.
Answer (creation Day 3, 2026-11-24, asked in the fiction): Who: the Clerk of the Seals first, "the office I would license first", and "a handful of factors, two or three to start, who go out with sealed goods and sealed agreements to the hill country and to the open water." What one gives up and what is asked:

> Their right to a false word in anything they seal. A licensed factor binds themselves, in front of the Clerk, to the same rule I carry: no false measure in a sealed term. I ask a share of what they earn, a stake in the goods they carry, and an open ledger. In return, I owe them the standing of my house, a share in the market's protection, and cover for their risk if a seal is broken against them in good faith. Any other cost to the mortal is the Keeper's to fit.

Asked whether the rule is an oath or a binding: "An oath that can be broken. It is a promise a mortal makes in front of the Clerk, and the Clerk and I can see whether they keep it. I do not want a binding, and I do not want to take a priced edge on myself for a factor's honesty. A factor who breaks the oath is a factor who has lied under my mark, and that should cost them my house and their standing with it. An oath that cannot be broken is not an oath." No licence is held at landing.

## 2. The hall of weights
From: "a hall where weights are kept and tested, and where anyone can bring a scale to be checked against mine."
In the fiction: "A stranger comes to your hall with a scale under one arm. What happens to them there?"
(oog): Who may bring a scale to be checked, do they pay for it, and what do your people do with a scale that fails?
Answer (creation Day 3, 2026-11-24, asked in the fiction): Anyone may bring one: "The house is mine, but the hall is open to everyone, and I want the town to see that."

> Yes, a small fee, paid in measure, not in coin, such as a handful of grain, or a fired weight. If the scale fails, the Warden marks it openly, and the owner is offered a corrected one at the cost of the clay. The scale is not broken in front of anyone, and nobody is shamed for owning it. I want strangers to feel it is safe to be wrong in my hall.

The DM said at the table that a scale tested by the Warden alone is honestly tested and not a true scale as the card means it. The player: "I also accept that the Warden's test is an honest test and nothing more. I will check the town's standards against my scales myself."

## 3. What will not be weighed
From: "I will not weigh people."
In the fiction: "Among the people you lead there may be some who are owed labor, or who owe it. What do you make of them?"
(oog): If the people you lead hold anyone in bond or in debt-service, what do you want your house to do about it, if anything?
Answer (creation Day 3, 2026-11-24, asked in the fiction):

> Debt-bonded folk exist here, and I accept it. I would add three rules to the town's law. Debt labor has a fixed term of years and a day, and then ends. It does not pass to a child. And a bonded person may bring the debt to the hall and have it weighed, and if the sum is wrong, it is struck. I will not make the town free of debt, because debt is what a market runs on. I will make it honest.

The DM said at the table that the scales do not know whether a sum on a stick is the sum agreed; the hall reckons a debt the ordinary way. The player: "I only asked that the town's law get the debt reckoned honestly, and I did not ask for an edge." Whether the town holds bonded folk is for the Keeper to reconcile; that the town takes the rules is heard, not promised.
