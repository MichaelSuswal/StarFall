# Day 1 World Survey: the player's answers

Campaign: sim-06. Date: 2026-11-24 (Pacific).

Mode: standard

1. A crossroads market at dusk. Lamps lit down a long street, and the air smells of salt fish, resin, hot bread and wet rope. Everyone is haggling and nobody is quite cheating. Under the noise there is a sense that the whole world is one long conversation about price.

2. River country meeting coast. I want a wide delta or estuary where several rivers reach a sheltered sea, with salt flats and reed islands, and low hills behind that hold ore and clay. I want the water to carry goods and the land to be hard to cross, so that routes matter.

3. Four real seasons, with a wet season that floods the lowlands and a dry season that opens the roads. Storms should close the sea for a stretch each winter. I wake into early autumn, when the harvest is in, the ships are loaded and the fairs begin.

4. Some of each, leaning toward old kingdoms already rooted. A trader needs someone to trade with. I'd like a few wealthy, proud, well-defended neighbors, and beyond them wild country that holds things nobody has priced yet.

5. Thousands of years, but with gaps. I want old roads and old weights and measures that nobody fully understands, a few dead cities, and living cultures that remember only part of what came before.

6. Low and uneven. Most people doubt it. There are a handful of known practitioners in a region, and people pay well for their work and half distrust them. It should be a service people buy, not a given.

7. It should feel like an exchange. The worker gives up something real, such as memory, years, warmth, a sense, or a name, and gets the effect in return. It should always cost something, and the price should be something a person can count.

8. Closer to a great sorcerer than to the one-eyed man, but with one thing that is clearly beyond mortal reach. I do not want to be the only power in the room. I want rivals who can hurt me and bargains that are real because both sides can walk away.

9. Mostly familiar animals, a good number of legends, and a few things in the deep country and the deep sea that nobody has a name for. The strangeness should be at the edges, where trade routes end.

10. Other people with leverage, and famine, and debt. I'd also like the kind of danger that arrives by a trade route: plague, a ruinous fashion, a currency that fails, a rumor that closes a market. I also want something old and patient in the far places.

11. Salt is the obvious one, and I'd go past it. I want one particular good, such as a dye, a metal, a spice or a seed, that only grows or lies in a few places, that everyone wants, and that is worth fighting over. I also want honest weights, meaning a trusted standard of measure, to be scarce.

12. Old roads, and sealed vaults of the kind a counting-house would have. I want weights, tokens and ledgers from a vanished trading power, and a question about why it ended.

13. Yes, a few. One people who are long-lived and patient, and who trade slowly and remember every deal. One who live on the water and own no land. They should be proper trading partners with their own customs, not monsters or props.

14. A few. Travelers who arrive with a new crop, a better ship design, a new way of keeping accounts, or a coin nobody has seen. I want them rare enough that each one changes something, and I want to be able to buy from them and be cheated by them.

15. A knowing face to face, plus a faint pull when another god's goods or marks cross my markets. I want to learn who they are by what they sell, and by what they will not sell.

16. One body for me. If there is a way to act at a distance, it should work through agents, seals and contracts, and not through copies of me. Anything else should cost real time or real trust.

17. A factor, a ship's captain, a debtor who climbs out, or a clerk who finds the one number that breaks a monopoly. I want a mortal whose story runs on cleverness and nerve, and who can be loyal or can be bought.

18. A truth about why the old trading power fell, or a market that opens only at certain tides or in certain years. I'd like a companion who is a rival first.

19. I want a world in which a promise has weight. Where a bargain made in good faith holds, and a broken one costs the breaker something the whole world can see.
