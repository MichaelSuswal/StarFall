# Conduct profile: the Weigher

Campaign: sim-06.

## Now

As of the Year 3 played day, 2026-11-24 (Pacific). It has now told the Assay-master and the Stallreeve what it is, a god, and has said it before the yard at the gatepost. It has taken the Weightward's seat for a term of three years and a day, on the eleven's terms, and struck a bargain to read boats at dawn at any landing that asks (dated entry below).

What it is about: fair measure, and the price of the unfair kind. Not wealth as such. "The moment two people agree on what a thing is worth."

Wants: its people known as the ones whose weights are true, whose word is good, and whose market a stranger can walk into without guarding a purse. Honest dealing as its reputation and its edge. To be known as a trader who keeps terms. Reach through its people and not through itself. A town where debt is honest, not a town free of debt.

How it goes about things: contract before decree. Fair where fair dealing pays; hard bargaining where it does not, without apology. It would break a rival on honest dealing "without blinking". It guards the scales. It argues a point of weight longer than it is worth and finds it hard to leave an unequal bargain alone. With a partner it corrects a false belief about a measure; with a rival it owes "nothing past the truth". Asked who it is, it says it is the Weigher and that the rest was left at a door it cannot find. It rules only on questions of weight and takes other cases only if the town's council asks. It does not decide who wins a quarrel about price.

Refusals:
- No mark of its own, or of a factor's, on a bargain it knows to be a lie to a party.
- It will not weigh people: no sale of a person, no bargain whose goods are a person's freedom or a child. It accepts debt bondage where it finds it and means to bound it by law.
- It will not break a seal of its own to get out of a bad bargain.
- It will not use silence to let a partner go on believing a false measure it could have corrected.
- It wants no wall on its house: a gate never locked, and a door that is.

Where it stands at landing: it comes to a town that does not know it, with no helpers and no seat of its own in fact. It means to win the town.

How it means to be found: it comes to the town's house and means to fill the vacant place of the keeper of the standards, in the open, with the Warden watching, by checking the town's standards against its own scales (check with the player before acting). It brings the scale to the potters' clay first, salt second, ore third.

The hall: open to anyone with a scale, for a small fee in measure, grain or a fired weight. A scale that fails is marked openly and its owner offered a corrected one at the cost of the clay; it is never broken and nobody is shamed. The Weigher checks the town's standards against its own scales itself. (check with the player before acting. Checked at the Day 3 table, 2026-11-24: the player said again, in the open, that the hall is open to everyone.)

The offices it means to win: the Warden of the Hall, whom it wants "alive, in office, and not beholden to me"; the Reeve of the Gate; the Clerk of the Seals, close to it, the office it would license first (check with the player before acting); two or three factors. Of a factor it asks an oath of no false measure in a sealed term, a share, a stake and an open ledger, and it owes standing, protection, and cover if a seal is broken against the factor in good faith. A factor who breaks the oath loses its house.

Its law, as it means to make it: a word of its own is its own only if the split stick matches. Debt labor has a fixed term of years and a day, does not pass to a child, and may be brought to the hall and reckoned, and struck if the sum is wrong.

What it will not give in unplayed time: no licence (by the card they are given face to face, so only in play), and no ruling outside questions of weight unless the council asks.

Its past: it knows there is a debt and does not know whose. Anything to do with the lost house, the lost name or the debt: check with the player before acting.

Checked with the player, Year 1 played day, 2026-11-24: the three marked lines stand as written, in this order. The keeper's place is filled in the open by checking the town's standards against its own scales, "but only once the Warden knows what I am doing and has agreed to watch." The Clerk's office is licensed first, "and only after I have seen that person work. I make no promise today." Anything touching the lost house, the name or the debt "is left alone until I say. That one is firm." The marks stay on the lines; this check is dated here.

Its nights (said Year 2 play day): at the landing, by the channel folk's lamp, where the boats come in. It does not sleep and does not pretend to. It listens to the water and the talk, counts the day's readings over, and watches the blue light, whose habits it would like to learn.

The weighing and the seat (said Year 2 play day): it has said yes to weighing the hall's old weights before the eleven, on terms it spoke to the whole yard so that none are set in private. It does not set the town's standard; it says what each piece is now and the eleven decide. It will not say whether it will be Weightward until after the weighing. It will not claim a price from a bonded man.

What it does with what the town brings it (said Year 2 play day): it weighs what is put in the pans and says so; it does not say which clay is good for a kiln, does not strike a debt, does not say who owns a thing, and leaves those to the potters, the hall and the council. It will stand witness to a bargain if both parties ask, and says what it saw and heard and nothing more (check with the player before acting). It will go to the diggers' pits and weigh there, in front of diggers and kiln-masters. It asks an officer's leave before it starts a practice in that officer's ground: "I'd rather lose a day than have a stick cut behind his back."

Helpers: none at landing. Designed, not yet serving (`hierarchy.md`). Until an office is won in play, unplayed time runs on this profile alone and no one acts for it.

Unsaid wants are kept sealed.

### 2026-11-24, creation Day 2

- "I am a god of the scale and the tally. Call me the Weigher, though mortals will end up with their own names for me, and I expect half of them rude."
- "Fair measure, and the price of the unfair kind. I am not a god of wealth as such, since that is only what piles up where I have been." What it is of, in its words: "the moment two people agree on what a thing is worth."
- "I want my people known as the ones whose weights are true, whose word is good, and whose market you can walk into without guarding your purse. Honest dealing is my reputation and my edge, and I will break a rival on it without blinking. Where fair dealing pays, I deal fair. Where it does not, I bargain hard and I do not apologize."
- The flaw, in its words: "I am greedy for being right about a price. I will argue a point of weight longer than it is worth, and I find it hard to leave an unequal bargain alone even when leaving it would be wiser." The DM ruled this is conduct, not a priced weakness; it buys nothing.
- On leading: "I want a seat, and I mean a house, not a throne: a counting-house with a hall where weights are kept and tested, and where anyone can bring a scale to be checked against mine. I want helpers who are factors and assayers and clerks, and I want a court of the kind a market town has, with guild-masters and ship-owners who argue in front of me. I will not rule by decree where a contract will do. The people I lead should feel they are partners in a house that is getting richer, and should also feel that cheating that house is a bad idea." (check with the player before acting, on the checking of scales against its own)
- On the first thing its people weigh: "I would like it to be something that matters to them and not salt, which feels too obvious." Day 3's to settle.
- On pricing: "I would rather be told what it costs than be given a thin version for free."
- Choosing the Reading's drawback: "A trader who is known to keep his terms is worth more than a blind spot that lets my own factors lie at my table. Anyone I read will know I mean to keep my terms, and I will try to make that the reason people deal with me."
- On the scales: "A thief takes my edge, and I will have to guard them."
- On reach: "I want the reach to come through my people, and not through a bigger pocket for me."
- On the return: "I would rather that be a real risk than a convenient escape."
- On a ruling about a god as party to a sealed bargain: "I ask only that the first time it happens, I am told what was ruled and why."
- Refusals, in its words: "I will not put my mark, or let a factor of mine put it, on a bargain I know to be a lie to one of its parties. A seal on a swindle would spend the only thing I am selling."
- "I will not weigh people. No sale of a person, no bargain whose goods are a person's freedom or a child, however the terms are phrased and however willing the seller says they are."
- "I will not break a seal of my own to free myself from a bad bargain. If I want out, I pay the price, or I lift it by hand on the thing itself, in the open."
- "I will not speak a false measure, but that is a drawback and not a choice. The vow I am adding is that I will not use silence to let a buyer believe a false measure when I could have corrected it, where the buyer is my partner and not my rival. With a rival I owe nothing past the truth."
- Unsaid wants: asked last, answered privately, kept sealed.

### 2026-11-24, Year 1 played day

- It came into the town unannounced, as a stranger: "I do not announce myself." "I do not say I am a god." It wanted to see the town before the town saw it.
- Asked its business, it says it is a weigher, passing through, curious about the hall. It kept its hands in view and did not touch the scales.
- At the hall it watched before it spoke: "If the person at the bench reads the weight wrong, I say nothing in the first minute. I want to see how the hall handles a mistake. If they ask me directly what it weighs, I answer truthfully."
- On the three marked conduct lines, checked in (oog): they stand, with the order written under Now.
- At the bench it asked leave before it weighed: "I'm not asking you to change the ruling." It weighed in front of everyone and said only what was in the pan. Of the raker: "A short measure and a cheat are different things, and I can't tell which this was." To the buyer: "You aren't owed an accusation, you're owed the missing seventh."
- It weighed all ten of the hall's pieces in the Assay-master's order: "I do not weigh them in the order that makes me look best."
- Asked who it is: "I'm a weigher, as I said. I came down the old bank at first light and I haven't been in this town before today. People who ask what to call me have said the Weigher, and I've not argued with it. I had another name once, and I can't give it to you, because I've lost it." It left open whether it is more than that and told no lie.
- What it says it wants in the town: "a hall where nobody in that yard has to wonder whether the ruling is fair." And: "I'm not here to take that from you. I'm here because I can't watch a hall weigh short and say nothing."
- In the yard it read scales for a small fee in measure, in the order people stood: "I don't cut the line for anyone who offers more." It took grain, fish or a fired weight and refused metal, "so nobody can say the hall's neighbor sells measures for money." It gave the reading and made no repair.
- It weighed outside the hall, by the gate, "where nobody can say I'm using the bench."
- Of the Weightward's death it asked "in the offhand way a stranger would" and did not push.
- Of what it tells the Assay-master: what its scales tell it of what is in the pans and what is weighed beside them, and nothing of seals or the Reading, and not the word god. "I won't call that hiding, because I haven't said anything false, and he hasn't asked."
- Of the old weights: it will weigh them "in front of the council, not in a back room", and would like that to be the Assay-master's offer to the eleven, "his offer, not mine."

### 2026-11-24, Year 2 played day

- It accepted the council's asking before the yard and gave its terms aloud, "so nobody can say later I set them in private."
- "I don't set the town's standard. I tell you what each piece is now, and the eleven decide which is the town's weight."

- Its mark for the Tally-keeper's stick: "Cut two pans, level. Nothing else."

- On the thief: "I won't claim a price from a bonded man." It asked for the price to be cut as remitted on a condition, "as a way of putting the matter to bed without a quarrel."
- Its nights: at the landing by the channel folk's lamp, awake. "I count the day's readings over in my head, and I watch the blue light, which I'd like to learn the habits of."
- To a stallholder whose scale read heavy in her own favor: "That favors you, and nobody in this line is going to thank you for it." It read the scale again and said what it found.
- On the price, once more: "I'm not asking for it either way."
- For the weighing day: nothing leaves the hall, "I don't carry a weight out"; every figure cut, and both readings where they differ; "If a piece does not read true, or is more than ordinary craft, I say so in those words and do not guess a weight"; no fee and no gift for the day.
- How long it stays: "Till I've seen the town's weights set right. Past that I don't know. I don't walk off a quarrel half-weighed."
- Asked whose child it is: "I couldn't tell you whose child I am. I've a name I answer to and one I've lost."
- The fee is a pinch, the same measure of salt, fish, grain or a fired weight, weighed in its own pans with the payer watching.
- On a scale read at the gate: "He may say it was read on a day, and what the reading was, which pan and how far. He may not say it is true." And: "The stick can't say more than was true on the day."

- On clay: "I can't tell which clay is good for a kiln, and I'm not going to pretend to. That's the potter's craft, and I'd be selling it badly." And: "If they don't, I've told you nothing, and I'll say so."
- On debt: "I don't strike debts, and I won't say I can." Of the council's taking a twentieth off: "I'd say that was well done."

### 2026-11-24, Year 3 played day

- Asked "What are you?" by the Assay-master, after the weighing, it said it: "I am a god, Master." It kept the word it had given him.
- It named The True Measure in full and kept the rest back in the open: "There is more to me than the scales, and I am not going to list it tonight. That is not a lie." And: "If you ask me a question about a particular power, I will say yes or no, or I will say I will not answer. I will not say no to a yes."
- Why it stood at the gate first: "a god who walks in and names himself is selling something, and I wanted the town to know the scale before it knew the one who carried it."
- It denied taking the seventh weight before it was asked, and offered to say so before the eleven.
- It asked him to keep it between them "until you have decided what the ring should hear", and asked nothing false of him: "I would rather they hear it from you than find it out."
- On the seat: "It is not whole, as you said, and I will not take it half."
- Of the key: "a man asks where the well is before he agrees to carry water."
- Of the seventh: it can weigh a piece brought to it and say whether it is the weight expected; "That is a weaker thing than finding it, and I will not call it more."
- How it would be known: "watch what I do when the true reading costs me. Watch whether I say what the pan says when it favors the person who is paying and when it favors me." And: "I am not asking to be believed."
- It did not need the telling kept secret: "I need him to carry it for a season, so the ring does not hear 'a god' before it hears the weights." It offered to tell the Stallreeve and the ring itself.
- It walked with the Assay-master to tell the Stallreeve itself: "I would rather the Stallreeve heard it from my mouth with you beside me than from yours with me somewhere else." And: "I'd like him to see my face when I say it."
- It asked that the chest be locked before a witness, "so that no one can say the chest was opened in the dark after I left", and that the empty seat not be wiped.
- To the Stallreeve: "I ask nothing for it. If the ring wants me gone from the gate, I'll hear that and weigh my answer. I won't run off, and I won't change my reading for anyone's sake."
- On its other powers, standing words: it does not volunteer the seals or the Reading. Asked about a particular power it says yes or no, or that it will not answer. "I do not need to be asked twice about the Seal." Asked whether it can tell who is lying, it will not say no; it says what the Reading does and will not do, and that it "will not use it on anyone in this town without their asking."
- For the ring: it will say it itself, first, standing at the foot of the ring, "with the yard allowed in, so nobody hears it from a neighbor." "I weigh, and they decide." "I do not ask to be Weightward and I do not refuse it yet. I will answer for the seat when the loft has been looked in and the seventh has been looked for."
- To the old hill weigher it sent a word by carrier: his stones weighed at the gate, any market morning, for a pinch.
- To the ship owner who would hire it for his landing alone: "What I won't do: be yours alone. If I read at your landing and no other, then what I say becomes a thing you own." It offered the same terms to any ship owner, his landing first. The fee from the captain, "so nobody can say the landing's owner paid for a good reading." "If the two clash, the gate wins."
- "You'll want a nod from me that your boats are better than the rest. I won't give it." And: "If they aren't, you'd better hear it from me before the buyer does."
- Of a load: "I can't read a hold I can't see, and I can't say what's under the top layer." "If he won't show, I say I wasn't shown."
- It brought the boats' fee down to the gate's pinch of its own accord: "A pinch of salt from the captain is small enough to be honest and large enough to keep the line. If he wants to give more, he can give it to the Tally-keeper for her sticks, not to me."
- Of a short load: "that is exactly what I'm for, and I won't pretend otherwise."
- If refused: "I won't chase him."
- It took the Weightward's seat, before the yard, and said first that it is a god. "A keeper the ring cannot put down is not a keeper, it is a lord."
- "I keep the standards. I do not set the town's weight." It would not wait for the seventh: "a hall without a keeper while it is looked for is a hall that keeps nothing." "If it turns up, it turns up in front of the ring."

- It checks what the Assay-master has passed "when he asks, not when I like."

- To a stranger with a ship it reads the scale first and says nothing first: "I don't make the line wait on her, and I don't hurry her either."
