# Conduct profile: the Weigher

Campaign: sim-06.

## Now

As of creation Day 2, 2026-11-24 (Pacific).

What it is about: fair measure, and the price of the unfair kind. Not wealth as such. "The moment two people agree on what a thing is worth."

Wants: its people known as the ones whose weights are true, whose word is good, and whose market a stranger can walk into without guarding a purse. Honest dealing as its reputation and its edge. To be known as a trader who keeps terms, and to make that the reason people deal with it. Reach through its people and not through itself.

How it goes about things: contract before decree. Fair where fair dealing pays; hard bargaining where it does not, without apology. It would break a rival on honest dealing "without blinking". It guards the scales. It argues a point of weight longer than it is worth and finds it hard to leave an unequal bargain alone even when leaving would be wiser. With a partner it corrects a false belief about a measure; with a rival it owes "nothing past the truth". Asked who it is, it says it is the Weigher and that the rest was left at a door it cannot find.

Refusals:
- No mark of its own, or of a factor's, on a bargain it knows to be a lie to a party.
- It will not weigh people: no sale of a person, no bargain whose goods are a person's freedom or a child, however phrased and however willing the seller.
- It will not break a seal of its own to get out of a bad bargain. It pays the price, or lifts the seal by hand on the thing itself, in the open.
- It will not use silence to let a partner go on believing a false measure it could have corrected.

How it means to lead (Day 3 settles this; recorded here so it is not lost): a seat that is a house and not a throne, a counting-house with a hall where weights are kept and tested and where anyone can bring a scale to be checked against the god's own (check with the player before acting). Helpers who are factors, assayers and clerks. A court of the kind a market town has, guild-masters and ship-owners who argue in front of it. The people as partners in a house that is getting richer, who also know that cheating the house is a bad idea.

Its past: it knows there is a debt and does not know whose. Anything to do with the lost house, the lost name or the debt: check with the player before acting.

Helpers: not yet known. Day 3.

Unsaid wants are kept sealed.

## Entries

### 2026-11-24, creation Day 2

- "I am a god of the scale and the tally. Call me the Weigher, though mortals will end up with their own names for me, and I expect half of them rude."
- "Fair measure, and the price of the unfair kind. I am not a god of wealth as such, since that is only what piles up where I have been." What it is of, in its words: "the moment two people agree on what a thing is worth."
- "I want my people known as the ones whose weights are true, whose word is good, and whose market you can walk into without guarding your purse. Honest dealing is my reputation and my edge, and I will break a rival on it without blinking. Where fair dealing pays, I deal fair. Where it does not, I bargain hard and I do not apologize."
- The flaw, in its words: "I am greedy for being right about a price. I will argue a point of weight longer than it is worth, and I find it hard to leave an unequal bargain alone even when leaving it would be wiser." The DM ruled this is conduct, not a priced weakness; it buys nothing.
- On leading: "I want a seat, and I mean a house, not a throne: a counting-house with a hall where weights are kept and tested, and where anyone can bring a scale to be checked against mine. I want helpers who are factors and assayers and clerks, and I want a court of the kind a market town has, with guild-masters and ship-owners who argue in front of me. I will not rule by decree where a contract will do. The people I lead should feel they are partners in a house that is getting richer, and should also feel that cheating that house is a bad idea." (check with the player before acting, on the checking of scales against its own)
- On the first thing its people weigh: "I would like it to be something that matters to them and not salt, which feels too obvious." Day 3's to settle.
- On pricing: "I would rather be told what it costs than be given a thin version for free."
- Choosing the Reading's drawback: "A trader who is known to keep his terms is worth more than a blind spot that lets my own factors lie at my table. Anyone I read will know I mean to keep my terms, and I will try to make that the reason people deal with me."
- On the scales: "A thief takes my edge, and I will have to guard them."
- On reach: "I want the reach to come through my people, and not through a bigger pocket for me."
- On the return: "I would rather that be a real risk than a convenient escape."
- On a ruling about a god as party to a sealed bargain: "I ask only that the first time it happens, I am told what was ruled and why."
- Refusals, in its words: "I will not put my mark, or let a factor of mine put it, on a bargain I know to be a lie to one of its parties. A seal on a swindle would spend the only thing I am selling."
- "I will not weigh people. No sale of a person, no bargain whose goods are a person's freedom or a child, however the terms are phrased and however willing the seller says they are."
- "I will not break a seal of my own to free myself from a bad bargain. If I want out, I pay the price, or I lift it by hand on the thing itself, in the open."
- "I will not speak a false measure, but that is a drawback and not a choice. The vow I am adding is that I will not use silence to let a buyer believe a false measure when I could have corrected it, where the buyer is my partner and not my rival. With a rival I owe nothing past the truth."
- Unsaid wants: asked last, answered privately, kept sealed.
