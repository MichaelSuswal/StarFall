# Hierarchy: bent, and empty at landing

Campaign sim-06. Written by the DM at the close of creation Day 3, 2026-11-24, with the player's yes.

Designed, not yet serving. The player designed a counting-house, a council and offices, and then set them in a town that does not know its god: "The three offices exist under other names, held by the people I described, and they know they are in office. They are waiting for something, but not for me." The Weigher lands with no helpers in fact, and the house is the town's and not yet its seat. The player: "I accept all four rulings, including the cost to my seat and helpers. I landed without being asked to, and I will have to win the town."

## What the god has

At landing: no one. No office takes its orders, no mortal holds its licence, and no scale in the town is a true scale as the card means it.

## The offices

- The Warden of the Hall: held by the town's chief assayer, under the town's own law and the town's own name for it. Tests weights for the town. Designed to keep the standards when the Weigher is away. Won when its holder takes the Weigher's orders. The player wants this one "alive, in office, and not beholden to me".
- The Reeve of the Gate: stands under the town's own law. Runs the market, its fees, its disputes between buyers and sellers, the watch of sixty and the call-up, and the runners. Won when its holder takes the Weigher's orders.
- The Clerk of the Seals: an office of the town's under another name, to be converted in play into the keeper of the register of seals and factors. The office the Weigher "would license first".
- The keeper of the standards: vacant since last spring. Not the Warden's office. The Weigher means to take it itself, in the open. Whether the town gives it is the town's.
- Factors, two or three to start: none exists at landing. Created in play, each by a licence given face to face. With the Clerk, they would fill the four licences the card allows.
- The council of eleven: the town's own, "a loose council that argues and decides little". Designed as the court that argues before the Weigher. It is the town's until it chooses otherwise.

How orders travel: none at landing, with no helpers. As designed, once anyone carries them: split tally-sticks, one half kept in the hall and one carried, the ruling notched and spoken aloud at the gate by the Reeve's runners, boats to the far channels, and a drum on the highest bank.

## The people's own order

The town runs itself. Buyer and seller go to the Reeve. A death, a boundary or a marriage gone bad goes to the council in its ring, the hearing led by the head of a trade that is no party, and is settled by a price in measure. The standards have had no keeper since last spring and the market quarrels.

## What this means for every agent

No one acts for the Weigher in unplayed time. Its unplayed time runs on its standing conduct alone (`conduct-profile.md`), and the town goes its own way under its own council until each office is won in play. The form is a bend and was stated at the table with its cost. It is not a refused default, it bears on no edge, and it bought nothing.
