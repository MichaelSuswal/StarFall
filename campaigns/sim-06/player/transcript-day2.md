## DM:

You are still falling. The world below is nearer than it was, and it has stopped listening; whatever it took from all those voices, it has taken. The voice that speaks to you now belongs to this place, The Between, and it is not asking about the world any more. It is asking about you.

Yesterday you spoke of a market at dusk, of a world that is one long conversation about price, of a promise that has weight. You said you would act at a distance through agents, seals and contracts, and not through copies of yourself. Those were words about a world. Today is for the one who said them.

Some things are so of every god that lands there, and you know them the way you know you are falling. You will have one body. You will not feel another god from far off: only at close quarters, in the same room, across a doorway, within a few paces, will you feel one as a weight in the air, and over a moment the weight settles into knowing that this is one like you. It tells you nothing more, not who the other is and not what it can do. And where mortals work magic, it is simple, and it costs the worker.

(oog) Those three are given, the same for every god. Anything past them, a sense that reaches further than a few paces, say, would be a power of yours, and priced. Nothing was carried over from yesterday that needs an answer, and the Keeper had no follow-up questions for you. Today is a conversation. Describe the god in any order and at any length; I will ask, price and rule as we go, and at the end I will read the whole card back to you for a yes.

So. What is it that is falling?

## Player:

I am a god of the scale and the tally. Call me the Weigher, though mortals will end up with their own names for me, and I expect half of them rude.

**What I am.** A single body, shaped like a tall, lean figure in a long coat with many deep pockets. The coat is the first thing anyone remembers. I keep a pair of brass scales at my belt. They are small, plain and slightly dented. I would like the dent to have a story, and I will let the DM decide what it is. I do not fall like a star. I fall like a coin dropped down a well, turning over and catching the light on each side.

**What I am a god of.** Fair measure, and the price of the unfair kind. I am not a god of wealth as such, since that is only what piles up where I have been. I am a god of the moment two people agree on what a thing is worth. I want my people known as the ones whose weights are true, whose word is good, and whose market you can walk into without guarding your purse. Honest dealing is my reputation and my edge, and I will break a rival on it without blinking. Where fair dealing pays, I deal fair. Where it does not, I bargain hard and I do not apologize.

**The edge I want.** Anything weighed in my presence, or sealed under my mark, becomes known to me. I mean its true weight, its true count and its true purity, and also whether the bargain struck over it was struck in good faith or in lies. I can't be shorted at a scale or fooled by a plated coin. A seal I put on a contract also lets me feel when it is broken, wherever the breaker is.

I know that is a lot. The seal-feel reaches past a few paces, which is the thing you said would be priced, and the good-faith reading is close to reading minds. So price it. I would rather be told what it costs than be given a thin version for free. I am ready to pay in something real, such as a limit on how many seals I can hold at once, or a cost to me each time one is broken, or a blind spot where my own people are concerned.

**The flaw.** I am greedy for being right about a price. I will argue a point of weight longer than it is worth, and I find it hard to leave an unequal bargain alone even when leaving it would be wiser. I also cannot lie about a measure. If I say a thing weighs a pound, it does, and that binds me when a lie would be convenient.

**How I want to lead.** I want a seat, and I mean a house, not a throne: a counting-house with a hall where weights are kept and tested, and where anyone can bring a scale to be checked against mine. I want helpers who are factors and assayers and clerks, and I want a court of the kind a market town has, with guild-masters and ship-owners who argue in front of me. I will not rule by decree where a contract will do. The people I lead should feel they are partners in a house that is getting richer, and should also feel that cheating that house is a bad idea.

**What I want to ask you.** First, what does a god of measure look like to mortals who have never seen coins? Second, what is the first thing my people will weigh? I would like it to be something that matters to them and not salt, which feels too obvious. Third, I would like to know whether the dented scales can be a real thing in the world, a relic and not a costume, so that other gods or mortals might want them.
