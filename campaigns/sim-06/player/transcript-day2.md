# Transcript: creation Day 2

Campaign: sim-06. Date: 2026-11-24 (Pacific).

## DM:

You are still falling. The world below is nearer than it was, and it has stopped listening; whatever it took from all those voices, it has taken. The voice that speaks to you now belongs to this place, The Between, and it is not asking about the world any more. It is asking about you.

Yesterday you spoke of a market at dusk, of a world that is one long conversation about price, of a promise that has weight. You said you would act at a distance through agents, seals and contracts, and not through copies of yourself. Those were words about a world. Today is for the one who said them.

Some things are so of every god that lands there, and you know them the way you know you are falling. You will have one body. You will not feel another god from far off: only at close quarters, in the same room, across a doorway, within a few paces, will you feel one as a weight in the air, and over a moment the weight settles into knowing that this is one like you. It tells you nothing more, not who the other is and not what it can do. And where mortals work magic, it is simple, and it costs the worker.

(oog) Those three are given, the same for every god. Anything past them, a sense that reaches further than a few paces, say, would be a power of yours, and priced. Nothing was carried over from yesterday that needs an answer, and the Keeper had no follow-up questions for you. Today is a conversation. Describe the god in any order and at any length; I will ask, price and rule as we go, and at the end I will read the whole card back to you for a yes.

So. What is it that is falling?

## Player:

I am a god of the scale and the tally. Call me the Weigher, though mortals will end up with their own names for me, and I expect half of them rude.

**What I am.** A single body, shaped like a tall, lean figure in a long coat with many deep pockets. The coat is the first thing anyone remembers. I keep a pair of brass scales at my belt. They are small, plain and slightly dented. I would like the dent to have a story, and I will let the DM decide what it is. I do not fall like a star. I fall like a coin dropped down a well, turning over and catching the light on each side.

**What I am a god of.** Fair measure, and the price of the unfair kind. I am not a god of wealth as such, since that is only what piles up where I have been. I am a god of the moment two people agree on what a thing is worth. I want my people known as the ones whose weights are true, whose word is good, and whose market you can walk into without guarding your purse. Honest dealing is my reputation and my edge, and I will break a rival on it without blinking. Where fair dealing pays, I deal fair. Where it does not, I bargain hard and I do not apologize.

**The edge I want.** Anything weighed in my presence, or sealed under my mark, becomes known to me. I mean its true weight, its true count and its true purity, and also whether the bargain struck over it was struck in good faith or in lies. I can't be shorted at a scale or fooled by a plated coin. A seal I put on a contract also lets me feel when it is broken, wherever the breaker is.

I know that is a lot. The seal-feel reaches past a few paces, which is the thing you said would be priced, and the good-faith reading is close to reading minds. So price it. I would rather be told what it costs than be given a thin version for free. I am ready to pay in something real, such as a limit on how many seals I can hold at once, or a cost to me each time one is broken, or a blind spot where my own people are concerned.

**The flaw.** I am greedy for being right about a price. I will argue a point of weight longer than it is worth, and I find it hard to leave an unequal bargain alone even when leaving it would be wiser. I also cannot lie about a measure. If I say a thing weighs a pound, it does, and that binds me when a lie would be convenient.

**How I want to lead.** I want a seat, and I mean a house, not a throne: a counting-house with a hall where weights are kept and tested, and where anyone can bring a scale to be checked against mine. I want helpers who are factors and assayers and clerks, and I want a court of the kind a market town has, with guild-masters and ship-owners who argue in front of me. I will not rule by decree where a contract will do. The people I lead should feel they are partners in a house that is getting richer, and should also feel that cheating that house is a bad idea.

**What I want to ask you.** First, what does a god of measure look like to mortals who have never seen coins? Second, what is the first thing my people will weigh? I would like it to be something that matters to them and not salt, which feels too obvious. Third, I would like to know whether the dented scales can be a real thing in the world, a relic and not a costume, so that other gods or mortals might want them.

I have no SEALED section for this.

## DM:

A coin down a well, then, turning. The Between takes the Weigher as it is given: tall, lean, a long coat with many deep pockets, a pair of small dented brass scales at the belt.

(oog) Your three questions first, then the price you asked for.

What a god of measure looks like to mortals who have never seen coins: I cannot tell you whether the people below have coins, or what they would make of you. That is found in play. What you look like is yours to say, and I will ask about it below.

The first thing your people will weigh: that belongs to tomorrow. Day 3 is the people, and you will describe them yourself, including what they bring to a scale. The seat as a counting-house, the hall of tested weights, the factors, assayers and clerks, the court of guild-masters and ship-owners: I have all of it written down, and it is also Day 3's to settle, not today's.

The dent: I do not decide its story, and neither do you today. Your past is foggy to you as you fall. You give me the outline of who you were; the rest is filled in out of your sight tonight, and the dent goes with it. You will know only that it is there and that you did not put it there on the way down.

The scales as a real thing: yes, two ways, and you choose. As an ordinary possession they are true brass scales, at no price. They can be stolen, copied, wanted for whose they are, and they do nothing a good pair of scales does not. Or they are the instrument of your first power: the true measure comes only through them, on what is set in their pans or weighed beside them. Then they are a relic in fact. Whoever takes them takes your edge until you get them back, though in any other hand they are only brass. That second way is a weakness you would be taking on, and a weakness buys something. More on that under trades.

Now the price. I am reading what you asked for as three powers, not one, because they cost differently.

The True Measure. Anything weighed within a few paces of you and in your sight, or held in your hand, is known to you: its true weight, its true count, its true purity. You cannot be shorted at a scale or passed a plated coin. It tells you what a thing is now. It does not tell you who made it so, how, where it came from, or what it was before. A thing made or held by more than ordinary craft may not read true; that is unruled until it happens, as is anything in another god's keeping. Drawback, from your own words: you cannot give a false measure. Not spoken, not written, not signed, not sealed, not by a nod. You may keep silent or refuse to answer. I am taking that as the price in full, since it binds you in every bargain you will ever make. Say if silence should be closed to you too; that would be a heavier price than this needs, and I would write it as asked.

The Good-Faith Reading. This is the one near to minds, so I am trimming it, and saying so: it reads the bargain, never the mind. When a bargain is struck in your presence, you know of each party one thing, yes or no: at the moment of agreeing, did this one mean to keep the terms as spoken. Not why, not what the lie is, not what they mean to do instead. It does not catch someone who honestly believes a false thing, nor a go-between dealing honestly for a master who is not. On another god it is unruled until it happens. It compels no one. For its drawback I offer a choice. One: the blind spot you named. It never reads anyone of your own house, and who counts as your house is defined tomorrow. Your own factors could lie to your face at your own table. Two: both pans show. Whenever you read a bargain, every party to it knows as surely whether you mean to keep your terms. Pick one.

The Seal. Your mark, set by your own hand, in the presence of every party and with their consent, on a bargain whose terms are spoken or written, or on a closed vessel, bale or chest whose contents you have measured. From anywhere, you feel the moment it is broken: a party knowingly doing what the terms forbid or letting a due day pass, or the vessel opened. You know which seal and, on a bargain, which party. You do not know where, how or why. Burning the paper breaks nothing and frees nothing. A seal ends only when its terms are fulfilled, when it is broken, or when you lift it with your own hand on the thing itself. Both of the costs you offered apply. You hold at most twelve seals at once, bargains and vessels together. And each breaking costs you: it lands like a blow, and until the next dawn all three of these powers are dark, and you weigh and judge as a mortal does. Anyone who learns that can break a sealed bargain on purpose the morning of a great fair. A god as party to a sealed bargain is unruled until it happens.

Trades, offered once: a weakness taken on can buy one improvement elsewhere. Seals your factors can set in your name, more than twelve seals, a reach for the True Measure beyond a few paces, the far pull you spoke of yesterday when another god's goods cross your markets: each is the kind of thing a weakness could buy. The scales as the instrument is one such weakness. You need not take any. The love of being right about a price, and the unequal bargain you cannot leave alone, I am writing into how you conduct yourself, where unplayed time will run on it. It is who you are, so it buys nothing.

The voice of the place comes back, close, as the light turns over on you again.

Under the coat, what is there? A face like a mortal's, or something a mortal would look away from? And what is in the pockets?

Who were you, before the fall, and what did you lose? The outline is enough. The rest is fog.

And one thing that will matter below. You cannot die there. You can be hurt, held, scattered, shut out. When the body is broken, what do you wish to happen?
