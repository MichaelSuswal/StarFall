# The god's side of the chronicle: Year 1

The DM's working draft, from the played day (`player/rolling-save.md`, `player/transcript-year-001.md`), the decisions handed off (`player/decisions-001.md`) and the Keeper's release (`releases/001/dm.md`).

## The landing day, played

Landed in the hour before first light on an old raised clay bank, under a round blue light it has no name for. Walked the path about a mile to the town and watched it wake. Went in unannounced on a market day and stood in the gate of the town's house. Asked leave at the Assay-master's bench and weighed a disputed load of salt in its own pans: the hall's ten small pieces are not alike, eight agree, one chipped and one cracked are light, and the salt was short by about a seventh of one weight. Weighed all ten pieces at the Assay-master's demand. The bench changed its ruling and the raker made the salt good. Asked who it was, it said it is a weigher, called the Weigher, with another name lost. Read scales in the yard by the gate for a fee in measure, beginning with Bren's. Listened in the market, bought a clay cup, ate with Bren's people. Went to the hall at dusk.

## The meeting at dusk

Resolved on its handed-off words. It told the Assay-master that its scales tell it what is in the pans and what is weighed beside them while it is near, and showed it again on three things. It gave the offer: the Weightward's old weights set right, weighed in front of the council, as the Assay-master's offer. It kept silent on five questions. It gave no name but the Weigher. The Assay-master said, unasked, that he would stand with his hand on the beam if the eleven would have it. Nothing was promised.

## The year at the gate, unplayed

Stood at the gatepost every day and read scales in line order for grain, fish or a fired weight. Refused silver a second time. Kept silent on everything its decided words did not answer. Its hand was on the nest before a thief's in the sixth month; it did not strike and asked no price. It heard at the gate and the landing of the nights the blue light dimmed; whether it was awake and looking up on any of them is not set, and the player is to be asked how it keeps its nights. No weight in the air from anyone all year. Body whole; everything carried is on it. Nothing new felt of its powers. No seal, no licence, no promise.

## Held

The Stallreeve's carrying of the council's invitation to the gatepost, in the high summer.

## The held moment at the gatepost, played (Year 1, the eleventh month)

Read the scale in its hand: the selling pan heavy by about one part in forty; level on a second trial after the stallholder took up the cord. Took up the half-stick and said yes before the yard, with its terms spoken aloud. Named its mark for the Tally-keeper: two pans, level. Asked the Stallreeve where the old weights are kept and who holds the key. Said it would answer on the Weightward's seat after the weighing and not before. Would claim no price from a bonded man, and asked that the price be cut as remitted if the master's scale read level within the month. Gave its standing answers at the gate. At the next market asked the Stallreeve's and the Tally-keeper's leave for split reading-sticks, and had it, with his rule and her notch. Went up to the hall at dusk.

## The hall door at dusk, resolved on its handed-off words

The Assay-master kept it at the door. It said its five things in the player's words. He would carry the weighing as an afternoon's work. He heard "Yes" to whether it is more than a man with good brass, did not ask what, and holds it to the word "after". He marked "that you know of". He has nothing to tell yet of the old Weightward's death and will tell it when he knows what there is to tell. He would not be called a keeper. He asked three things its words did not answer: to say the same to the eleven on the day; where it learned to weigh; why it asked the Stallreeve about the key. It kept silent on each.

## The rest of Year 1

Told the Stallreeve his rule is a good one. Asked the master of the potters to bring the new nest to the hall. Asked Bren's household once about the low-tide trips and had the town's telling: none has followed and none knows. In the twelfth month the bonded man's master's scale read level and the price was cut as remitted. The eleven told it where the old weights are kept and who holds the keys, and took its terms whole, eight to three.

## The weighing, the first month of Year 2, resolved on its handed-off terms

In the hall before the eleven, the door open. The rack is cut for seven and held six; the largest seat empty and clean; the Assay-master had known since the old keeper's laying out and had told only the Stallreeve, the evening before. It called the six: 48, 96, 192 in its own pans; 384, 766, 1,536 on the hall's beam with its scales in its hand. The Assay-master read the fifth level, then light, and said so; both readings were cut. The True Measure gave it plain old work in bronze and stone and nothing that does not read true. Four of the hall's fired pieces go down against the first bronze: the hall's pieces run heavy by about a twentieth. It called the potters' nest of eleven: 13, 25, 37, 52, 75, 89, 98, 130, 150, 179, 181. The old weigher of the hill kin, of Ruddfold, set his stones against the six and they agreed. It kept silent to the master of the smiths (where is the seventh), to the old hill weigher (weigh my stones) and to a boy on the step (will you be our Weightward now). It took no fee and no gift, and said it does not say what the fifth should be. The ring carried the clay head's motion, nine to one: no weight named the town's until the seventh is found or given up and the nest fired again; the market on the hall's present pieces; nothing in the hall mended or carried out; the chest opened only before two of the eleven; back at the first sitting after the next grain. The loft to be looked in by three.

## Held, first: the evening of the weighing, in the hall

The door shut, one lamp. The Assay-master: "It is after. You told me at this door there was more than a man in you and that you would tell me what I asked after. I am asking. What are you?" This is as far as the Year 3 morning story tells.

## The first held moment, played (Year 3 play day; rolling save, Year 3, sections 1 to 3)

Told the Assay-master it is a god; that others came down when it did and it has felt none; what its scales give; that there is more it would not list, answered yes or no if asked; that it did not take the seventh and never touched the chest. Answered him on the key, the seventh and the beam. Asked him to keep it between them until he had decided what the ring should hear; he refused to hold it alone (r-0011). Had the chest locked before the Stallreeve and the seat left unwiped. Told the Stallreeve itself. He keeps the gate as it was, says nothing in the yard, and carried the asking for a sitting. He did not ask about its other powers (r-0012). Its words for the sitting and its word to the old weigher at Ruddfold are handed to the night.

## Year 2 again, from the evening of the weighing

- The first month. After the hall door the three went down the yard and parted at the gate. The Stallreeve carried the Assay-master's asking round that night and named no matter. No sitting was called on it; it went to the winter sitting.
- Autumn. As told: the loft looked in by three, the gate, the hill carriers, who took its word for the old weigher up the Old Rudd Way on a stick the Tally-keeper cut (message m-0002), the Stallreeve among the boatmen, the boat people at the lowest tides, the dim night of the second month, the day at the pits, the third month. New: the Assay-master at the gate on market mornings, a little way off, arms folded, watching its hands, its face, and what it did with a reading that went against someone who could hurt it. The Stallreeve's look each dawn. Neither spoke of the evening.
- The fourth month. The landing bargain as played, with the Stallreeve's additions. At the next market the Tally-keeper said yes to sticks for boats, with her own additions: cut at the landing's table at the end of the dawn; a captain who wants none gets none and is still called; the landing's mark on the back of every boat stick and none hung; one half to the captain and one to the buyer if wanted, for that day only, the landing's man holding neither; the pinch for the stick paid to her by captain and buyer, nothing of it the Weigher's. The ship owner's gift of a measure of salt a month she took as the hall's and cuts on her own stick. Dawn readings at his landing from then on, on days with no market. The fifth month: the ship owner of the open water asked for a dawn through his landing man and had it. The sixth month: a pair of smaller owners asked for a dawn a month, and Bren's household at the fish landing. Some captains would not show a load; it said it was not shown. "I wasn't shown" is a saying on the water.
- Winter. The fifth month as told. Early in the sixth month, at the ship owner's landing: one of his own boats from the salt flats, raked salt in baskets, about five parts salt and one part sand and grit under a clean top, basket after basket. It called each before the captain, the landing man and the landing. The ship owner came down and said, "Read the rest." Heard afterward: one raking household with a thin season; the captain bought by the basket unturned; the ship owner said before the water that he had not known; the Stallreeve ruled short measure and not theft; the household made good the sixth in clean salt; the captain docked half and the ship owner bore the rest in the open. The captains now turn out every fifth basket unasked. The ship owner, a week after, in the hearing of some of the ring: "It read my own boat against me in front of the water, and I would sooner it read mine first than the buyer's." The water says he paid for a show. The household stayed from the gate a month and came back with a sieve.
- The sixth month, the ring's winter sitting, in the middle of the month. The yard let in, nine for it, the head of salt and the master of the smiths against; the door open, the hall full. It said it first at the foot of the ring, in the player's words: what it is; that others came down when it did and it has felt none; that it does not know where it came from or whose child it is; what its scales give; that there is more, answered yes or no if asked; "I weigh, and they decide"; "I do not ask to be Weightward and I do not refuse it yet." At the Assay-master's asking it said before the ring that it did not take the seventh and never touched the chest. He said only that it was said to him in the hall and that he asked. An old salt-raker woman: "The Maun Stair." A child, through its mother: whether it is the one that drowned. The head of reed and fish: "What else are you?", and that its answer was a good broker's. The head of salt asked whether it can tell when a man is lying: it would not say no, said what the Reading does and does not, and that it has used it on no one in the town and will not without their asking (handled under L-0044: the Description and Limits, the Drawback not volunteered). He said no bargain in the town is safe from it, and that he and his people will not ask. Met with silence: the master of the smiths' "Can you be killed? What would it take to break you?" and a woman of the back houses' "Can you cure a fever?" The landing readings stand; the open-water owner had his dawn. The clay head, the master of the potters, the master of the weavers and the ship owner of the channels each said their piece. The hall was cleared and the ring sat on alone. Learned as the town did: the ring voted to have the seventh looked for in every way open to the town, and set its sitting on the seat for after the sowing.
- After the sitting. The line twice as long at the next market; no fish bought until noon; eight of the watch at the post and six sent home by midday. Bren said she had fed it first and would again. The old of the flats took bread toward the Maun Stair at the next low tide. The back houses went on bringing scales. The woman who asked about the fever stayed from the gate; the line said her child died in the last week of the month, with the cold. The boatwrights' sale witnessed in the last week, as told. The dim night of the sixth month, as told.
- The looking for the seventh. The last week of the sixth month: four of the hall's officers through the hall's rooms for three days. The seventh month: the Stallreeve and the Assay-master to the old keeper's house. Through the spring the Stallreeve at every landing. Nothing brought to the ring.
- Spring, as told. The ninth month at the gatepost, as played: the yard was hearing it a second time. A boy cried "Weightward" and the whole line took it up, channel folk and potters loudest; the salt-rakers silent; the longest line of the year; no sitting called to put the seat down. How the ring divided, heard in the line: for, the Assay-master, the clay head, the master of the potters, the head of reed and fish, the master of the boatwrights and the ship owner of the channels; against, the master of the weavers, the head of salt, the master of the smiths and the head of the hill goods.
- The tenth month. In its first days the key taken from its peg before the ring, and cut by the Tally-keeper. The ring's rule for the chest: any pair of the ring may stand for it, one of the pair to be of the four who voted against the seat. Nothing opened that day.
- The coaster's master at the gate, resolved on its handed-off words; her words were the Keeper's. Her balance read level; a pinch of barley taken. Her answers: each southern house reads against its own weights; the council in Corrith keeps a set for quarrels, and nobody swears it is what it was; what a southern captain wants to know is which is the town's weight, the old stones or the clay, and she would tell every house on the coast for it. It gave no answer for the town. Asked who it works for, it gave its settled words. Asked plainly whether it had said it was a god, it said yes. "Where I come from we say a god ashore is a landlord. You take barley." It does not leave the gate; the town's weights are the town's. No gift offered, no name given or asked. No weight in the air. Her coaster lay three more days and went down the channel.
- The same day at dusk, at the gatepost, before the Assay-master: asked whether the town wants a keeper's word sent down the coast, the Stallreeve said, "Not this summer. Ask us after the grain sitting."
- After the coaster had gone, at the hall's doorway at dusk: asked once what the three saw in the loft, the Assay-master told it. The old man's coat and boots hanging on a peg, stiff with dried river silt to above the knee, unmoved; nothing else out of its place; they touched nothing; the ring has had it since the winter; he does not know what it means.
- The eleventh month. A second day at the pits, wanted by the diggers: every lump much alike, about a twentieth part sand and shell, the cracked no worse than the sound. The quarrel keeps. In the last ten days, at the lowest water, the Stallreeve, twelve of the watch and the boatmen of a pair of households dragged the Alder below the hall's landing and the reaches either side. Seen from the landing and the gate. Nothing that was a weight came up; nothing was brought to its scales.
- The rest of the summer. Upriver traders had scales read and went home with the story that the hall had been found heavy and had said so. A salt-raker's pinch of salt taken as the fee. The grain. The ring did not sit in the twelfth month; its sitting on the weight was set for the first market of the new autumn. Dim nights in the eleventh and twelfth months, each seen from the landing.

# The god's side of the chronicle: Year 3, from the gatepost to the potters' door

The DM's working draft, from the Year 4 played day (`player/rolling-save.md`, Year 4, section 1; `player/transcript-year-004.md`), the decisions handed off (`player/decisions-004.md`) and the Keeper's release (`releases/004/dm.md`). The year line: the clock read Year 4 and the played day stood at the held moment of Year 3, the first month, the fifth day. The night resolved the Weigher's own thread from that day to the next held moment, the fourteenth day of the seventh month of Year 3, and stopped there. Nothing of the Weigher or its town past that moment is resolved, and nothing of it is drafted here. The clock goes on to Year 5; the Weigher's own Year 4 has not begun. Where this section and the set-aside lines above differ, this section is what happened.

## The held moment at the gatepost, played (Year 4 play day; rolling save, Year 4, section 1)

The first month, the fifth day, a market morning. It read the fish seller's hand balance first and called it: out, the buying pan heavy by about one part in sixty. It took up the half-stick of the ring's asking, which is its yes, and gave the word at the post before the yard, to be cut as said. It mended the word before the split, once after the Assay-master spoke up on the calls of the fired pieces. The Tally-keeper cut it on a pair of sticks, read it back and split both. The first stick is the town's word, taken by the Stallreeve for the eleven; its text is `messages/m-0005.md`, and the carrier's half went to the ship owner of the open water. The second stick the Stallreeve carried up to the eleven, each thing by itself, neither yes nor no, with its asking that the word go in the order said and with nothing added. It said it would not push the second stick, and that it wants to hear which if the ring says no to any of it. To the carrier, in the Stallreeve's hearing, it gave its instructions; he took the carrying as cut, the town's weight as the stick says it, and that it is no price, and would not promise the line on what the Weightward answers about itself or the line on a southern scale read at the hall. It told the Stallreeve where it stands on the master of the smiths' ask. On the same day the player answered the asks that had waited from the winter sitting of Year 2 and let the ruling on the Reading stand (`player/decisions-004.md`, items 10 and 11).

## Autumn, the first to the third month (released at the Year 4 night; told at the Year 5 morning)

- After the fifth day the line ran as before. The yard had heard the word given and mended and was proud and ashamed of it together.
- Every night at the landing it ran its thumbnail down the cups, smallest first, and then counted. The boatmen saw.
- From the sixth day it asked at its landing dawns for large stones. They were brought: anchors, ballast, querns, door-stones, hearth stones. It weighed each with the owner watching and said what it was. The Tally-keeper cut a stick for every one, unasked. The Stallreeve forbade wagers at the beam. The figures, as it called them, are in `civs/unnamed-people/years/004-report.md`.
- The eighth day: the fish seller back with a mended cord; his balance read level.
- The night of the twenty-second day: the blue light dimmed, seen from the landing. The next morning a boy at the gate: "Is the star yours?" Met with silence. The line told him to hush.
- The second month. The hill carriers in the town, and the old weigher of the hill kin with them. On the eighth day his stones were weighed at the gate before the head of the hill goods, the Assay-master, the master of the smiths and the Stallreeve, the three largest on the Assay-master's own beam: 48, 96, 193, 384, 768 and 1,536. Asked what his people say the seventh was, he gave a saying of his grandfather's, that the six weigh what is and the seventh weighs what is owed, and would not stand behind it. He went home with the figures on his own stick.
- The eleventh day: a young carrier asked whether it can be killed, and it answered in the player's words. He asked what would break it; it did not answer. The same day it asked the Stallreeve for the fourteenth, the lowest tide; he gave it on his own terms, the household told the night before and not come upon.
- The fourteenth day, a market day: it walked out to the Flats, 1.6 miles over the marsh, and was back before dusk. The post stood empty with a pair of the watch at it; nobody was read and no notch was cut. At the pans the raking household turned out every basket before it was asked. The basket it chose and weighed was clean salt, and nothing it was shown there was bulked. The head of the household put a pinch in its pan. Told to it after: a boy asked the empty post whether the star was its own, and the watch had no answer to give.
- About those days the boat people were gone from the channels, as at every lowest tide of autumn, and came back.
- The third month: rain. The potters' hall's own beam came to the gate and read level. The master of the smiths brought a hearth slab to the beam and asked it nothing, then or since. A young stallholder asked whether it can tell who is lying, and then whether a man it read would know; both were answered by its decided words.

## Winter, the fourth to the sixth month

- The eighteenth day of the fourth month, at the fish landing: an old net-maker's door-stone read 3,011. Its own reckoning for the seventh, which the landings have by heart, is 3,072. It said what the stone read and nothing more. She asked whether it was the seventh and whether it wanted her step for the hall; it said only what the stone is, and that it asks no one to give anything up. It told the Stallreeve and the Assay-master first, and the Stallreeve carried the figure to the eleven that dusk. The Assay-master went to look and said, in its hearing afterward, that the stone was never cut. Four people tried to buy it. The Stallreeve forbade buying a stone at a landing for its weight. The yard says "sixty-one short". No other stone of the thirty read within a twentieth of its figure.
- The twentieth day: the Assay-master asked it to check six scales he had passed; a pair read level and four a hair out.
- The twenty-fourth day: a pair of boatwright households asked it to stand witness to the lease of a boatyard. It stood, and said what it saw and heard. No power was used.
- The fifth month: a hard spell, and fever in the back houses, fewer than the year before. An old woman of the back houses asked at the gate whether it can cure a fever, for her daughter's boy; it answered in the player's words. The woman who asked at the winter sitting of Year 2 has not come to the gate. The blue light dimmed on a pair of nights this month, seen from the landing.
- The fifteenth day of the sixth month: the ring's winter sitting, the door shut. It was not sent for.
- The seventeenth day, at the post, before the yard: the Stallreeve brought what the eleven said to the second stick, thing by thing, with the counts, and the Tally-keeper cut it. Taken as the town's word: that the rack is cut for seven and the seventh is not in it, that the hall does not know what it weighed and has looked for it; the four fired pieces against the first bronze and the sum, about a twentieth heavy; the chipped and the cracked piece against the eight; that the ten have not been weighed against the six since, and that the Tally-keeper has the figures; that the hall keeps no weight that anyone may not see weighed; and that the word go in the order said, with nothing added. Not taken: that the fifth is light by a hair and the hall says so; and that any captain may bring a scale to the hall and have it read. One reason given, for the captain's scale: a scale is read at the gate, like any, and the hall is for the town's. For the fifth he was given no reason and would not make one up. The Assay-master said at the post that the fifth is light by a hair on his own beam and that he would read it so again.
- Then the Stallreeve asked it, as himself, what it would have cut for the carrier now, since the things the ring took are not on the stick the carrier holds. Met with silence. Nothing more has been cut. He has not asked again.
- The ship owner of the open water, asked in the open, said it changes nothing he carries.

## Spring, the seventh month to the fourteenth day

- The thaw. The Alder rising to an ordinary flood.
- The second day: a netmaker at the gate: "Can you tell the height of the water as the tide-reckoner does?" Met with silence. He said it was good at that too.
- The fifth day: the master of the potters said at the post that the third nest is shaped and drying on its boards.
- The night of the seventh day: the blue light dimmed, seen from the landing.
- The fourteenth day, a market day: it read the line through the morning. When the last scale was read it walked to the potters' hall with the Assay-master at its left hand and the Tally-keeper behind, carrying a long stick. The line watched them go.

## Held: the seventh month of Year 3, the fourteenth day, at the door of the potters' hall

Early afternoon, the gate done. It stands at the door with the Assay-master at its left hand and the Tally-keeper behind, her long stick under her arm. The master of the potters is in the doorway, clay to the elbows, and has not moved aside. Past her shoulder: boards along a wall with unfired pieces standing on them in pairs, pale and dry; a beam with brass pans; a bench under a window. She has said: "Weightward. Assay-master. You are early for a firing. What do you want of my boards?" Nothing answered. None of its askings has yet been put to anyone. This is as far as the Year 5 morning story tells. The second scene the player prepared, the kiln-opening, is held behind this one and is not reached.

## Senses, body, powers

No weight came into the air at any time in the stretch. Body whole; no sleep; nothing taken. On it: the scales at its belt, the nest, and in its coat the half-sticks and the old keeper's key. In the stretch it read no bargain and no person, set no seal and gave no licence. The pinch at the pans was a fee, as at the gate.

## The sky

Seen from the landing in the stretch: the night of the twenty-second day of the first month, a pair of nights in the fifth month, and the night of the seventh day of the seventh month. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the light stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. The year is not told to its end, and no count is given for it.
