## Rulings

- Year 1, 2026-11-24. Matter: whether a scale the god read and found level is a true scale. Ruling: a scale is a true scale when the god has tried it by its own hand, with its scales on it or in its hand so that The True Measure bears on the trial, and it read level under that trial, at once or on a new trial after it was adjusted. The nest is part of the god's scales for this. It is a true scale only for as long as it reads true. A scale the god only watched in use is not one, and a scale tested by anyone else is not one. Binds: the Definitions and The Scattering. Ledger: L-0031.
- Year 1, 2026-11-24, at the first morning. Holding a licence costs a factor nothing; each day a factor sets one or more seals costs that factor the night's sleep that follows; lifting costs nothing; a factor feels no breaking. Ledger: L-0025.
- Year 2, 2026-11-24. Matter: what The True Measure gives of a lump of clay beyond its weight. Ruling: of a thing offered as one stuff, purity is how much of it is that stuff and how much is not. Of a lump of clay the god knows its weight and what part of it is clay and what part is not clay: sand, grit, shell, root. It does not tell one clay from another clay, a grade within the kind, or anything of how a clay will work or fire; those are not purity. The water a worked clay holds is part of the clay as it is. The same reading holds for any stuff sold by kind (salt, grain, charcoal, ore): how much is the stuff and how much is not, never how good the stuff is. Binds: The True Measure. Ledger: L-0037.
- Year 2, 2026-11-24. For the record, not the card. A piece too heavy for the god's pans is weighed on another's beam in its presence, with its scales in its hand, as the card's Description already gives; so the three stone weights were weighed under the term "each piece in my pans". A scheduled act handed off with its terms is resolved in the night on those terms and stops where a decision is needed. A promise the god has given to answer a mortal later is never met with silence in unplayed time; the asking is held. Ledger: L-0038.

## The people's code

As found, Year 1. None of it was set by the god.

- A quarrel of weight between buyer and seller is the Stallreeve's to rule. He has the scales read at the gate and rules himself. (Seen, Year 1.)
- A death, a boundary or a theft goes to the council in its ring and is settled by a price in measure. (Seen in the matter of the hand at the nest, Year 1.)
- The town holds debt-bonded folk. (Seen, Year 1: a bonded net-mender, a bonded man of a stallholder.)
- The first of a meal is given to the channel. (Seen at the landing, Year 1.)
- The town's sticks are split, one half kept and one carried; the Tally-keeper keeps the kept halves. (Told, Year 1.)
- A dead keeper's key hangs in the ring until a new keeper takes it. The old keeper's loft is kept shut until a new keeper takes the seat. (Told, Years 1 and 2.)
- A scale read at the gate may have a split reading-stick, one half its owner's and one the Tally-keeper's, carrying the day and the reading and holding for that day and no longer. Her fee is a pinch in measure. The Stallreeve's rule: no stick on a stall or a scale. Her notch: one for each market a scale is not brought, and the end cut at a season. (Asked by the Weigher and granted by the Stallreeve and the Tally-keeper, Year 1, the eleventh month; rolling save, Year 2, section 3.)
- By the ring's vote on the day of the weighing, nine to one: no weight is named the town's until the seventh old weight is found or the ring has given it up, and the potters' nest has been fired again; the market stands on the hall's present pieces; every debt and bargain made before the day is reckoned by them as made; nothing in the hall is filed, mended, replaced or carried out; the chest is opened only before two of the eleven; the matter comes back at the first sitting after the next grain. (Seen, Year 2.)

## Settled facts

- The nest of brass cups it carries: seven of them, each twice the last, one to sixty-four. (Its own, stated Year 1.)
- The hall has ten small clay weights of one size. Eight agree, at about twelve and six tenths in the nest's ones. One, chipped at the foot, is a hair under twelve. One, cracked from its mark, is about eleven and four tenths. (Weighed in its own pans, Year 1.)
- The house on the high bank is the town's own. The Assay-master keeps the bench in its hall. The eleven meet there in a ring on the floor. A flood drum hangs in its yard. (Seen and told, Year 1.)
- The Weightward, keeper of the hall's old weights, died in the spring before the landing and the seat is empty. It is the council's to fill. (Told by the Assay-master, Year 1.) He is said to have taken a chill in the spring flood and died in his bed within a fortnight. (Market talk, Year 1.) He was the Assay-master's friend. (Told by the Assay-master, Year 1.)
- The old weights are said to be older than the town. The god has not seen them and has not been told how many there are or where they are kept. (Market talk, Year 1.)
- The town prices in weights of salt, and each trade has its own measure. (Heard in the market, Year 1.)
- The Assay-master is a mortal. No one who came within a few paces of the god in Year 1 was one like itself. (Its own sense.)
- The path is the Bank Way; the sunken road is the Old Rudd Way; the country is called the Reedmouths. (Told by Bren and in the market, Year 1.)
- The town has no name for itself. (Year 1.)
- The town says the blue light in the sky dims on some nights for about an hour, and that it has seen such nights in other years. The god has no name for the light. (Told at the gate and the landing, Year 1. Not recorded as seen by the god.)
- Bren's balance has been a true scale since its second trial on the landing day, and so is each scale the god found level at the gate in Year 1, each for as long as it reads true. (Ruling L-0031.)
- Added Year 2. The old weights, seen and weighed. They are kept in a chest in the hall, in the room under the old keeper's loft; the Assay-master holds the second key and the old keeper's key hangs in the ring. (Told by the Stallreeve for the eleven, Year 1, the twelfth month.) They lie in a wooden rack cut for seven and are six: the seat for the largest is empty, and clean where the others are dusty. The three smallest are old bronze and the three largest cut stone. In the nest's ones: 48, 96, 192, 384, 766, 1,536. The fifth has an old chip at its foot. Nothing in the six reads other than true: plain old work in bronze and stone. (Seen and weighed, Year 2; its own sense.) This adds to the Year 1 line above, which said it had not seen them.
- The Assay-master says he found the rack so when he first opened the chest after the old keeper's laying out, and twice since, and told only the Stallreeve, the evening before the weighing. (Told by him before the ring, Year 2.)
- Four of the hall's small fired pieces are meant to make the smallest old weight, by the old keeper's practice, and they go down against it: the hall's pieces run heavy against the old weights by about a twentieth, fifty against forty-eight. (Seen on the hall's beam; the practice told by the Assay-master; the sum done aloud by the head of the hill goods, Year 2.)
- The potters' first nest of eleven, unmarked, in the nest's ones: 13, 25, 37, 52, 75, 89, 98, 130, 150, 179, 181. It does not agree with itself and sits heavy against the old weights. It is left in the hall under the Assay-master. (Weighed, Year 2.)
- The stones of the old weigher of the hill kin, of Ruddfold, agree with the old six to the beam's tremble at most seats. The hill kin have long supposed the hall's pieces heavy. (Seen on the hall's beam and told by him, Year 2.)
- The dimming of the blue light, Year 1: seen by the god itself from the landing, two nights in the first month of autumn and one in the dead of winter. (Released as seen at the Year 2 night.) This adds to the Year 1 line above.
- The scale of the bonded man's master has been a true scale since the god tried it in the twelfth month of Year 1, for as long as it reads true. The hall's beam was not tried by the god and is not one. (Ruling L-0031.)
- The god has told no one it is a god. It has said "Yes" to the Assay-master's asking whether it is more than a man with good brass. (Year 1, the eleventh month.)
- Added on the Year 3 played day. The god has told two mortals it is a god: the Assay-master, at his asking, in the hall on the evening of the weighing, and the Stallreeve, the same evening at the hall door. It told them what its scales give and that there is more to it, unlisted. This adds to the line above.
- The chest of the old weights was locked that evening by the Assay-master with his own key, before the Stallreeve; the empty seat was left as it was, unwiped; the key on the peg was not touched. (Seen, Year 2.)
- Clay at the diggers' pits, in the nest's ones. From loads that fired well: 96 (91 clay, 5 not), 104 (99, 5), 88 (84, 4). From loads that cracked: 100 (96, 4), 92 (88, 4), 108 (102, 6). What was not clay was sand and shell. (Weighed, Year 2, the second month.)
- The blue light dimmed on one night in the second month of Year 2. (Seen from the landing.)
- The river the town stands on is the Alder. (Named by the Stallreeve at the gatepost, Year 2, the fifth month.)
- The blue light dimmed on one night in the sixth month of Year 2. (Seen from the landing.)
- The potters' second nest, eleven chosen from twenty-two by the gate's readings, agrees with itself to a hair. It is kept at the potters' hall. (Read at the gate, Year 2, the seventh month.)
- The people's code, added: by the god's bargain with the ship owner of the channels, set before the Stallreeve, a boat's load may be set out and called at dawn at any landing whose owner asks, for a pinch from the captain, on days the gate is not kept; no reading on a market morning anywhere but the gatepost. (Played, Year 2, the fourth month.)
