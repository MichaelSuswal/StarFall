## Rulings

- Year 1, 2026-11-24. Matter: whether a scale the god read and found level is a true scale. Ruling: a scale is a true scale when the god has tried it by its own hand, with its scales on it or in its hand so that The True Measure bears on the trial, and it read level under that trial, at once or on a new trial after it was adjusted. The nest is part of the god's scales for this. It is a true scale only for as long as it reads true. A scale the god only watched in use is not one, and a scale tested by anyone else is not one. Binds: the Definitions and The Scattering. Ledger: L-0031.
- Year 1, 2026-11-24, at the first morning. Holding a licence costs a factor nothing; each day a factor sets one or more seals costs that factor the night's sleep that follows; lifting costs nothing; a factor feels no breaking. Ledger: L-0025.
- Year 2, 2026-11-24. Matter: what The True Measure gives of a lump of clay beyond its weight. Ruling: of a thing offered as one stuff, purity is how much of it is that stuff and how much is not. Of a lump of clay the god knows its weight and what part of it is clay and what part is not clay: sand, grit, shell, root. It does not tell one clay from another clay, a grade within the kind, or anything of how a clay will work or fire; those are not purity. The water a worked clay holds is part of the clay as it is. The same reading holds for any stuff sold by kind (salt, grain, charcoal, ore): how much is the stuff and how much is not, never how good the stuff is. Binds: The True Measure. Ledger: L-0037.
- Year 2, 2026-11-24. For the record, not the card. A piece too heavy for the god's pans is weighed on another's beam in its presence, with its scales in its hand, as the card's Description already gives; so the three stone weights were weighed under the term "each piece in my pans". A scheduled act handed off with its terms is resolved in the night on those terms and stops where a decision is needed. A promise the god has given to answer a mortal later is never met with silence in unplayed time; the asking is held. Ledger: L-0038.
- Year 3, 2026-11-25. For the DM's handling, not the card; told to the player at the Year 4 morning. Matter: what the god says, in time the player did not play, when asked whether it can tell who is lying. Ruling: it says of The Good-Faith Reading what the card's Description and Limits give, and does not volunteer the Drawback; the player's standing words promise what it does and what it will not do. A follow-up about the power waits for the player. The player may say otherwise at any time. Ledger: L-0044.
- Year 3, 2026-11-25. For the record. Salt cut with sand reads under L-0037, as how much is the stuff and how much is not.
- Year 3, 2026-11-25. For the DM's handling; told to the player at the Year 4 morning. Matter: how a year told with held moments is resolved again after they are played. Ruling: what was played is fixed, with the boards it was played on and the months they stand in. What does not turn on what a mortal now knows stands as told. What the town's people said and did between the played moments is resolved again, on what the god said in them. Where there is doubt, the told line stands. Ledger: L-0046.

True scales (L-0031), as they stand: Bren's balance; each scale found level at the gate, each for as long as it holds; the bonded man's master's scale; the coaster master's ship's balance, wherever it now is. The Assay-master's beam and the hall's beam were not tried by the god and are not among them. On the card as a dated entry.

In the stretch from the gatepost to the door of the potters' hall it read no bargain and no person, set no seal and gave no licence. On the card as a dated entry. No ledger id was released with it.

## The people's code

As found, Year 1. None of it was set by the god.

- A quarrel of weight between buyer and seller is the Stallreeve's to rule. He has the scales read at the gate and rules himself. (Seen, Year 1.)
- A death, a boundary or a theft goes to the council in its ring and is settled by a price in measure. (Seen in the matter of the hand at the nest, Year 1.)
- The town holds debt-bonded folk. (Seen, Year 1: a bonded net-mender, a bonded man of a stallholder.)
- The first of a meal is given to the channel. (Seen at the landing, Year 1.)
- The town's sticks are split, one half kept and one carried; the Tally-keeper keeps the kept halves. (Told, Year 1.)
- A dead keeper's key hangs in the ring until a new keeper takes it. The old keeper's loft is kept shut until a new keeper takes the seat. (Told, Years 1 and 2.)
- A scale read at the gate may have a split reading-stick, one half its owner's and one the Tally-keeper's, carrying the day and the reading and holding for that day and no longer. Her fee is a pinch in measure. The Stallreeve's rule: no stick on a stall or a scale. Her notch: one for each market a scale is not brought, and the end cut at a season. (Asked by the Weigher and granted by the Stallreeve and the Tally-keeper, Year 1, the eleventh month; rolling save, Year 2, section 3.)
- By the ring's vote on the day of the weighing, nine to one: no weight is named the town's until the seventh old weight is found or the ring has given it up, and the potters' nest has been fired again; the market stands on the hall's present pieces; every debt and bargain made before the day is reckoned by them as made; nothing in the hall is filed, mended, replaced or carried out; the chest is opened only before two of the eleven; the matter comes back at the first sitting after the next grain. (Seen, Year 2.)
- Sticks for boats, on the Tally-keeper's yes and her own additions: a boat stick is cut at the landing's table at the end of the dawn and not during it; a captain who wants none gets none and is still called; the landing's mark goes on the back of every boat stick and none is hung; one half to the captain and one to the buyer if he wants one, holding for that day only, and the landing's man holds neither; the pinch for the stick is the captain's and the buyer's to pay her. (Asked by the Weigher and the ship owner of the channels at the gate, Year 2, the fourth month; told at the Year 4 morning.)
- Short measure in a load is the Stallreeve's to rule; in the matter of the salted baskets he ruled short measure and not theft, and the raking household made good the short part. Since then the captains at the landing turn out every fifth basket unasked. (Heard at the gate and the landing, Year 2, the sixth month.)
- The ring's rule for the chest of the old weights, set when the key was taken: any pair of the ring who come may stand for it, and one of the pair is to be of the four who voted against the seat. (Seen, Year 2, the tenth month.) This adds to the vote of the day of the weighing above.
- The town's weight. By the ring's vote at the first sitting after the grain, Year 3, the first month: the old six are the town's weight, from the first market of the next autumn; bargains made before then are reckoned by the hall's present pieces; the master of the potters is to fire a nest to the old six in spring; the seventh seat stays as it is. (Told to the yard from the step by the Stallreeve, Year 3.) This follows the vote of the day of the weighing above.
- The keeper's word. The words the Weigher gave at the gatepost, cut on the first stick and taken by the Stallreeve for the eleven, are the town's word (`messages/m-0005.md`). (Played, Year 3, the first month, the fifth day.)
- No wager at a landing's beam. (The Stallreeve's rule, Year 3, the first month, when stones were brought to be weighed.)
- No stone is bought at a landing for its weight. (The Stallreeve's rule, Year 3, the fourth month, after the door-stone.)
- The ring's answer on the second stick, brought to the post by the Stallreeve and cut by the Tally-keeper, Year 3, the sixth month, the seventeenth day. Taken as the town's word, to stand beside the first: that the rack is cut for seven and the seventh is not in it, that the hall does not know what it weighed and has looked for it; the four fired pieces against the first bronze and the sum, about a twentieth heavy; the chipped piece and the cracked piece against the eight; that the ten have not been weighed against the six since, and that the Tally-keeper has the figures; that the hall keeps no weight that anyone may not see weighed; and that the word go in the order said, with nothing added. Not taken: that the fifth is light by a hair and the hall says so; and that any captain may bring a scale to the hall and have it read. The one reason given: a scale is read at the gate, like any, and the hall is for the town's. (Told at the post.)
- The ring's word of the sixteenth day of the ninth month of Year 3, brought to the post by the Stallreeve on the seventeenth. The hall's paying for the spares of the third nest is the hall's cost: seven for, three against, one not voting. A motion that the ring itself cut a second stick for the carrier, of the six things it took in the winter, did not carry: five for, five against, one not voting. The one reason given: a stick cut on a tied vote is a word the ring is not agreed on. (Told at the post.)
- The ring's day, Year 4, the first month, the second day. From that market the old six are the town's weight and the eleven new pieces are what the market weighs by. The hall's ten fired pieces stand on the hall's bench and serve only to reckon bargains made before that day. (Seen, and told before the yard.) A bargain made before the day is ruled by the ten, as made. (The Stallreeve's rulings at the gate, Year 4, the first month.) This follows the vote on the town's weight above.
- The term of the Weightward's seat is three years and a day. (Said by the Stallreeve at the post, Year 4, the ninth month.) The ring has spoken of the term and set no day to speak of it again but its first sitting after the grain. (Told at the post.)
