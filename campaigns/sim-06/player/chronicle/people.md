# Who's who

As the Weigher knows them. Beliefs carry the year they were formed. Told as far as the first month of Year 3, at the gatepost.

## The Assay-master
Alive. A man, grey-haired, unhurried. Keeps the bench in the hall of the town's house, tests weights for the town and sits on the eleven. Has not given his name: "When you have a name to put in the pan, I will put mine in the other." Last seen: the hall, the landing day at dusk; not in the yard at the held moment. Changed his own ruling in front of the yard when the pieces were weighed (Year 1). Calls the god the Weigher, "for now" (Year 1). Carried the offer of the weighing to the eleven and said he would stand with his hand on the beam (Year 1). Was the old Weightward's friend (told by him, Year 1). Asked five things the god has not answered. A mortal (its own sense, Year 1).
Year 2: kept the god at the hall door at dusk and heard its five answers, "Yes" among them; holds it to the word "after". Has nothing to tell yet of the old Weightward's death and says he will tell it when he knows what there is to tell. Would not be called a keeper: he has a beam. Holds the second key to the chest of the old weights. Says he has known since the old keeper's laying out that the rack is not full, and told only the Stallreeve. Stood with his hand on the beam at the weighing, read the fifth level and then light, and said so before the ring. Says he has tested the town's scales for thirty years against pieces he did not know were heavy. Said of the seat that it is given when what it keeps is whole. Last seen: the hall, the evening of the weighing, at the held moment, asking "What are you?"
That evening, played on the Year 3 day: told by the god that it is a god. Said he will not say he believes it and will not say he does not. Noted that it denied taking the seventh unasked. Said the seventh would be twice the sixth by the run of the set. Would not keep the telling to himself: "I will not be alone with a second." Sent for the Stallreeve and locked the chest before him. Asked for a sitting. Went up the old keeper's loft on the third day after, with the Stallreeve and the clay head. Last seen: the hall door, that evening.
Told at the Year 4 morning. From the first market after the weighing he stood at the gate on market mornings, a little way off, arms folded, watching its hands, its face, and what it did with a reading that went against someone who could hurt it; he said nothing to it of the evening (Year 2). At the winter sitting he asked it to say before the ring what it had said of the seventh, and then said only that it was said to him in the hall and that he asked; that he would not say he believed it or that he did not; that he had held one thing alone for a year and would not be alone with a second. Went with the Stallreeve to the old keeper's house in the seventh month. Voted for the seat. In the tenth month, asked once, told it what he saw in the loft, and that he does not know what it means. Said the answer to a southern captain who asks which is the town's weight is whatever the ring decides, and not before. Year 3, the first month: opened the chest for the second weighing of the six, read the fifth light by a hair at the first trial and said he had learned that one. Last seen: a little apart at the gatepost, arms folded, the first market after that sitting.

## Bren
Alive. A woman of the channel folk who poles a reed boat on the islands west of the Bank Way. The first to call the god Weigher and the first to have a scale read (Year 1). Her balance was out on the selling side by one in twenty; she took up the cord herself. The god ate with her people on the landing day, and it was at her people's lamp that a thief's hand was stopped in the sixth month. Last seen: the landing.
Year 1, the eleventh month: answered for her household, asked once about the low-tide trips: they would say what the town says, and would tell the Stallreeve first if the question came from anyone else.
Year 2, told at the Year 4 morning: her household asked for a dawn reading at the fish landing in the sixth month. After the winter sitting she said aloud that she had fed it first and would feed it again.

## The Stallreeve
Alive. A man who runs the market and the watch. Set two of the watch at the gatepost "to keep the line, not to guard you" (Year 1). Brings quarrels of weight to be read and rules on them himself; has never asked the god to rule (Year 1). Had the "read at the gate" tokens taken down (Year 1). Asked whether the god claims a price of the thief. Carried the council's invitation to the gatepost himself. Last seen: in front of the gatepost, at the held moment.
Year 2: carried the god's yes and its terms up to the eleven, and brought back their answers. Gave leave for the reading-sticks on his own rule, no stick on a stall or a scale. Was told by the Assay-master, the evening before the weighing, that the rack was not full. Kept the door at the weighing and told the yard from the step what the ring had done. Last seen: the hall step, the day of the weighing.
That evening: saw the chest locked, and was told by the god itself that it is a god. Keeps the gate as it was, says nothing in the yard, and carried the asking for a sitting round. Asked it nothing about what else there is to it. Went up the loft on the third day after. Went among the boatmen at the landing one evening in the second month; the god did not hear what was said. Gave leave for the day at the pits.
Told at the Year 4 morning. Carried the Assay-master's asking round that night and named no matter; it went to the winter sitting. Looked at the god each dawn as a boatman looks at weather and said nothing of the evening (Year 2). Ruled the salted baskets short measure and not theft. Put its asking to the ring at the winter sitting. Put eight of the watch at the post at the market after and sent six home by midday. Went to the old keeper's house with the Assay-master, was at every landing among the boatmen through the spring, and in the eleventh month had the Alder dragged with twelve of the watch and the boatmen of a pair of households. Put up his hand to quiet the yard's cry of "Weightward". Asked in the tenth month whether the town wants a keeper's word sent down the coast, said, "Not this summer. Ask us after the grain sitting." Year 3, the first month: told the yard the ring's vote on the town's weight from the step, and at the next market brought the ring's yes and its asking for the words, and laid half a split stick at the foot of the gatepost. Last seen: before the gatepost, at the held moment.

## The Tally-keeper
Alive. A woman who keeps the kept halves of the town's split sticks. Has cut a split stick of the gate's readings since the first night and keeps it herself (Year 1). Asked the god to name a mark for her to cut. Holds the stick of the thief's price, and the other half of the council's stick. Last seen: a pace behind the Stallreeve, at the held moment.
Year 2: cut the mark the Weigher named, two pans, level, at the head of her stick of the gate's readings. Cuts the split reading-sticks for a pinch, with her own notch. Cut the thief's price as remitted, the twelfth month of Year 1. Cut every figure at the weighing, with an apprentice calling them to the yard. Last seen: the bench's end, the day of the weighing.
Told at the Year 4 morning. Cut the stick that carried its word to Ruddfold (Year 2, the second month). Said yes to sticks for boats, with additions of her own, and took the ship owner's gift of a measure of salt a month as the hall's, cut on her own stick where anyone can read it. Cut the making good of the salted baskets, and the taking of the key. Last seen: beside the Stallreeve at the gatepost with a cut stick, Year 3, the first month, at the held moment.

## Orrin
Alive. A kiln-master. Asked the god to bring its brass to the diggers' pits and say which clay is good (Year 1).
Year 2: the east kiln-master. Brought three lumps from loads that fired well to the pits day. Said the two sets do not look different, and that it was something he could not see in a pan. Fires a test tile of each load.
Year 2, the eleventh month: brought lumps of both kinds and a tile fired from each to the second day at the pits.

## The master of the potters
Alive. Sits on the eleven. Has fired a nest of eleven new clay standards in one kiln, unmarked, and asked the god to weigh them against the old and set the hall's standards by them (Year 1).
Year 2: carried the new nest up to the hall herself. It was weighed before the ring and does not agree with itself. She said the pieces were fired in one kiln so that they would shrink alike, and asked that the nest be left in the hall under the Assay-master.
Told at the Year 4 morning. Said at the winter sitting that if it was a god it was a gentle one, and that she would bring the second nest to the gate like anyone. Voted for the seat. Stood for the chest, with the head of the hill goods, at the second weighing of the six (Year 3, the first month). By the ring's vote she is to fire a nest to the old six in spring.

## The heads of hill goods and of salt, and the master of the smiths
Alive. Each sits on the eleven. The yard says they voted against asking the stranger to weigh the old weights. The head of salt is said to have spoken longest: a store of worth is made by agreeing what the weight is, not by finding out (Year 1, as the yard tells it).
Year 2, at the weighing: the head of the hill goods did the sum aloud, about a twentieth, and moved that the old six be the town's weight; it failed. The head of salt moved that the hall's pieces stand; it failed. He said: "A twentieth, on every sack that has gone out of this town for forty years." The master of the smiths asked the god where the seventh is, what it weighed, and whether it can find it, and had no answer; he said, "It is good at that." At the year's turn the master of the weavers is with them against the gate.
Told at the Year 4 morning. At the winter sitting of Year 2 the head of salt and the master of the smiths were against letting the yard in. The head of salt asked whether it can tell when a man is lying, heard what the Reading does, and said, "Then no bargain in this town is safe from you"; he will not ask it to read a bargain, and his people will not. The master of the smiths asked, "Can you be killed? What would it take to break you?", had no answer, and said, "It is good at that." All of them, with the master of the weavers, voted against the seat. The head of the hill goods stood for the chest at the second weighing of the six (Year 3, the first month).

## The old Weightward
Dead, the spring before the landing; learned Year 1. A man. Kept the hall's old weights. Said to have taken a chill in the spring flood and died in his bed within a fortnight (market talk). The Assay-master's friend.

## The clay head
Alive. Sits on the eleven. Put out bread and water at the weighing, which was put away. Moved the motion the ring carried, nine to one, and said the seat should be offered then (Year 2). Speaks well of the gate.
Year 2, the winter sitting: said it had told him what it could not do, which he had not met in a god before. Voted for the seat.

## The ship owner of the channels
Alive. Sits on the eleven; runs boats up and down the channels and has a landing of his own. Came to the god at the landing at dusk in the fourth month of Year 2 with an offer: every boat at his landing read at dawn, a measure of salt for every boat, at his landing and no other, before the Stallreeve. Played on the Year 3 day: the god refused "no other" and he took its terms, with a pinch for a boat where he had offered a measure. "I came down here to buy a year of you, and you have sold me a morning and kept the rest."
Told at the Year 4 morning. Struck the bargain not knowing what it is. Offered a gift of a measure of salt a month, which the Tally-keeper took as the hall's. In the sixth month one of his own boats was read with sand and grit in its salt; he came down and said, "Read the rest", said before the water that he had not known, and bore half the loss in the open. A week after: "It read my own boat against me in front of the water, and I would sooner it read mine first than the buyer's." The water says he paid for a show. Said at the winter sitting that he had bargained with a god over a pinch of salt and did not like the bargain the less. Voted for the seat.

## The head of reed and fish
Alive. Sits on the eleven. Asked the Assay-master at the weighing whether he had opened the chest alone (Year 2).
Year 2, the winter sitting: asked, "What else are you?", and said its answer was one a good broker gives. Voted for the seat.

## The old weigher of the hill kin
Alive. An old man of Ruddfold, his own name not given. Sat beside the head of the hill goods at the weighing with a bag of his own stones, which agree with the old six. Asked the god to weigh his stones before the ring and had no answer. Cut the figures on a stick of his own (Year 2).
Year 2, the second month: its word went up to him with the hill carriers (m-0002). No word back, as far as told.

## The salt-raker and the fish-drier of the landing day
Alive, unnamed. Their quarrel over ten weights of salt was the first weighing. He fills by his father's basket and made the salt good.

## The bonded man who reached for the nest
Alive, unnamed. A bonded man of a stallholder whose scale read out by about a tenth in the stallholder's favor. Held by the watch; the council set a price in measure, unclaimed. He gave his master's name; the yard says the council did not find the master sent him (Year 1).
Year 1, the twelfth month: his master brought the scale to the gate, mended; it read level, and the price was cut as remitted. The master said it was a cheap scale in the first place.

## The bonded net-mender
Alive, unnamed. Asked the god to reckon her debt and strike it. The council later took a twentieth off it (Year 1).

## An old woman of the flats
Alive, unnamed, not met. Word went round from her of the midwinter water four days ahead; it came half a day late at the height she gave (Year 1, as told).

## Hale the Raker
Long dead. Said to have led the salt people onto the bank in the flood years (an old salt-raker's telling, Year 1).

## The boat people
Seen at a distance or not at all; told of. Said to leave the channels at the lowest tides, in autumn and spring, and to go to the Maun Stair (the yard's talk, Year 1).

## The list-singers
Told of. Walkers who came when the oldest were young, sang names all night on the bank and traded nothing (an old woman's telling, Year 1).

## Other gods
None met. The god knows only that others like it came down when it did.

## The master of the coaster
Alive, name not given. A woman of about forty, master of a coaster of the southern harbor towns, with a mate and about a dozen hands; a boatman says she is out of Corrith. Came up to the town's own landing in the first month of summer, Year 2, traded some days, and asked the Stallreeve where to have her scale read. Last seen: at the head of the line at the gate, the tenth month, a ship's balance in her hand. No weight in the air from her as far as the line.
Told at the Year 4 morning. Her balance read level at the gate, and she paid a pinch of barley. Said each southern house reads its scales against its own weights, and that the council in Corrith keeps a set for quarrels that nobody swears is what it was. Named her price for telling every house on the coast: which is the town's weight, the old stones or the clay. Asked plainly, was told it is a god: "Where I come from we say a god ashore is a landlord. You take barley." Asked whether it would read a cargo at a quay of her coast, and what the town means to do about its weights. Offered no gift and gave no name. No weight in the air from her. Her coaster lay three more days and went down the channel (Year 2, the tenth month).

## The tide-reckoner
Alive, not met. Her word went to the line by the Stallreeve in the fifth month of Year 2: the Alder would crest over the lowest paths in four days. It did, at the height she gave and a few hours off the hour.

## The ship owner of the open water
Alive. Sits on the eleven; his coasters work the open water. Was away on the Gulf when the ring voted the seat (Year 2). Asked through his landing man for a dawn reading when his coasters were in, the fifth month of Year 2, and had it on the same terms; asked again at the winter sitting and had it. The Stallreeve says he will carry the keeper's word down the coast when his coasters go out past the Horn (Year 3, the first month).

## The master of the boatwrights
Alive. Sits on the eleven. Asked at the winter sitting whether the landing readings stand. Voted for the seat (Year 2).

## The master of the weavers
Alive. Sits on the eleven. Said at the winter sitting that he weighs wool and had not weighed this one. Voted against the seat (Year 2).

## The woman of the back houses
Alive, unnamed. Asked at the winter sitting of Year 2, "Can you cure a fever?", and had no answer. Did not come to the gate again that winter. The line said her child died in the last week of that month, with the cold.

## The raking household of the salted baskets
Unnamed. A household of the salt flats that had a thin season; its baskets were about one part in six sand and grit under a clean top. Made good the short part in clean salt, stayed from the gate a month, and then came with a sieve and had its baskets turned out first (Year 2).
