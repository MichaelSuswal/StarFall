# Messages

Told as far as the tenth month of Year 4, the fourteenth day, at the gatepost before the gate opens. Year 3 is told whole.

- m-0002. Sent Year 2, the second month. From the Weigher to the old weigher of the hill kin at Ruddfold. Carrier: the hill carriers going home up the Old Rudd Way, with the words cut on a stick by the Tally-keeper. Content: it will weigh his stones at the gate, any market morning, before any of the ring who want to see it, for a pinch; that the old weights and his stones agreed to the beam's tremble; and that it would like to hear what his people think the seventh was. Fate, as the Weigher knows it: the old weigher was at the gate with the hill carriers in the second month of Year 3, had his stones weighed there, and, asked what his people say the seventh was, gave a saying of his grandfather's. (An earlier line here read "no word back by the first month of Year 3"; it stood as far as that month.)
- m-0005. Given Year 3, the first month, the fifth day, at the gatepost; it goes when the carrier's coasters go out. From the Weigher, Weightward of the hall, by the eleven's asking, to the captains of the coasting houses of the southern harbors, as the player addressed it (`messages/m-0005.md`). Carrier: the ship owner of the open water, with the carrier's half of the split stick. Content: the keeper's word of the hall, not a price and not an offer: what the six old weights and the hall's fired pieces read in the Weightward's ones, that the fired pieces stay in use for bargains made before the first market of the next autumn, and that from that market the old six are the town's weight. Fate, as the Weigher knows it: given at the gatepost and in the carrier's hands. He has said, in the sixth month, that the ring's answer on the second stick changes nothing that he carries. It has not gone: his coasters go out in the first days of the tenth month. Nothing more is known. (That line stood as far as the seventh month of Year 3.) Told at the Year 6 morning: the Stallreeve told the carrier, on the twenty-ninth day of the ninth month of Year 3, what the Weigher would have him told, and he said it was on his stick. His coasters went out past the Horn on the tide of the fourth day of the tenth month and came back on the nineteenth of the twelfth. By his own account at the post, on the twentieth: he said the town's word at Corrith, on the quay, to the masters of the houses his coasters deal with, as cut and no more, and put no price on it; it was not put to the coaster master's house by name, since her house has another port along that coast where his coasters do not go; the council's people at Corrith did not ask him for it; nobody gave him an answer, a stick or a token to carry back. His half-stick lies with the Tally-keeper, fitted to hers.

None received, through the fourteenth day of the tenth month of Year 4. Nothing has come to the Weigher from anyone outside the town.
