# Messages

Told as far as the seventh month of Year 3, the fourteenth day, at the door of the potters' hall.

- m-0002. Sent Year 2, the second month. From the Weigher to the old weigher of the hill kin at Ruddfold. Carrier: the hill carriers going home up the Old Rudd Way, with the words cut on a stick by the Tally-keeper. Content: it will weigh his stones at the gate, any market morning, before any of the ring who want to see it, for a pinch; that the old weights and his stones agreed to the beam's tremble; and that it would like to hear what his people think the seventh was. Fate, as the Weigher knows it: the old weigher was at the gate with the hill carriers in the second month of Year 3, had his stones weighed there, and, asked what his people say the seventh was, gave a saying of his grandfather's. (An earlier line here read "no word back by the first month of Year 3"; it stood as far as that month.)
- m-0005. Given Year 3, the first month, the fifth day, at the gatepost; it goes when the carrier's coasters go out. From the Weigher, Weightward of the hall, by the eleven's asking, to the captains of the coasting houses of the southern harbors, as the player addressed it (`messages/m-0005.md`). Carrier: the ship owner of the open water, with the carrier's half of the split stick. Content: the keeper's word of the hall, not a price and not an offer: what the six old weights and the hall's fired pieces read in the Weightward's ones, that the fired pieces stay in use for bargains made before the first market of the next autumn, and that from that market the old six are the town's weight. Fate, as the Weigher knows it: given at the gatepost and in the carrier's hands. He has said, in the sixth month, that the ring's answer on the second stick changes nothing that he carries. It has not gone: his coasters go out in the first days of the tenth month. Nothing more is known.

None received, through the fourteenth day of the seventh month of Year 3. Nothing has come to the Weigher from anyone outside the town.
