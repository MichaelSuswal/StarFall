# Threads

Told as far as the seventh month of Year 3, the fourteenth day, at the door of the potters' hall.

## Open

- The town's weight. Opened Year 2 by the weighing. The old six and the hall's present pieces disagree by about a twentieth. The ring has named neither, by its vote of nine to one, until the seventh is found or given up and the nest fired again. The market stands on the hall's present pieces. Comes back at the first sitting after the next grain. Told at the Year 4 morning: the ring did not sit in the twelfth month of Year 2. At the first sitting after the grain, Year 3, the first month, the six were weighed again in its pans and on the hall's beam and read as before, and the ring voted the old six the town's weight, from the first market of the next autumn, bargains made before then reckoned by the present pieces, and the master of the potters to fire a nest to the old six in spring. Told at the Year 5 morning: the Weigher gave the keeper's word at the gatepost on the fifth day of the first month of Year 3. By it, the hall's fired pieces stay in use for bargains made before the first market of the next autumn, and from that market the old six are the town's weight. The master of the potters said at the post in the seventh month that the third nest is shaped and drying on its boards.
- The seventh old weight. Opened Year 2. The rack is cut for seven and holds six; the largest seat is empty and clean. The Assay-master says he has found it so since the old keeper's laying out, and that by the run of the set it would be twice the sixth. The god has told him it cannot find it and can only weigh a piece brought to it. The chest was locked before the Stallreeve, the seat unwiped. Told at the Year 4 morning: the ring voted at the winter sitting of Year 2 to have it looked for in every way open to the town. The hall's rooms, the old keeper's house, the landings, and the Alder dragged at the lowest water of the eleventh month: nothing that was a weight came up. By the ring's vote of Year 3 the seventh seat stays as it is. Told at the Year 5 morning: from the sixth day of the first month of Year 3 the Weigher looked for it by what it weighs, by its own reckoning of 3,072 in its ones, asking at its landing dawns for large stones. Thirty have come to the beam. An old net-maker's door-stone read 3,011, in the fourth month; the Assay-master said it was never cut. No other read within a twentieth of the figure. The old weigher of the hill kin gave a saying for the seventh and would not stand behind it. The ring has taken as the town's word that the hall does not know what the seventh weighed and has looked for it.
- The old keeper's loft. Opened Year 2. The ring agreed it be looked in by three. On the third day after the weighing the Assay-master, the Stallreeve and the clay head went up and came down within the hour, quiet. Nothing of what they saw has been said in the god's hearing. Told at the Year 4 morning: asked once, in the tenth month of Year 2, the Assay-master told the god what he saw: the old man's coat and boots on a peg, stiff with dried river silt to above the knee, unmoved, and nothing else out of its place. The ring has had it since the winter. He does not know what it means.
- The Weightward's seat. Opened Year 1. Empty since the spring before the landing; the council's to fill. The god has said it will answer after the weighing, and that answer is the player's own to give. The clay head said at the weighing that the seat should be offered; the Assay-master said a seat is given when what it keeps is whole; the ring did not vote. The council voted after the sowing of Year 2 to offer it, six for and four against with one away; the Assay-master spoke for it. Offered at the gatepost in the ninth month: a term of three years and a day, the Assay-master's hand on the beam beside its own, and the eleven's right to put the seat down at any sitting that three of them call. The god said yes before the yard, on those terms and its own spoken ones, and holds the half-stick. The key is to be taken in the ring. How the yard and the ring took its words is not yet told. Told at the Year 4 morning: the yard took up the cry of "Weightward"; nobody called a sitting to put the seat down. The god took the old keeper's key from its peg before the ring in the first days of the tenth month. The ring's rule for the chest: any pair of the ring, one of them of the four who voted against the seat.
- What the god is. Opened Year 1, the eleventh month, by its "Yes" to the Assay-master and its word to tell him what he asked after. He asked, and it told him, the evening of the weighing: it is a god. He will not say he believes it or that he does not, and would not hold it alone. It told the Stallreeve itself the same evening. The Stallreeve says nothing in the yard and carried the asking for a sitting. The god means to say it to the ring itself, first, and has asked to say it at the foot of the ring with the yard let in. Waiting on the ring. In the ninth month it said it before the yard at the gatepost, taking the seat: it is a god, and not their god. Told at the Year 4 morning: the sitting fell in the middle of the sixth month of Year 2, the yard let in. The god said it first, at the foot of the ring, and named the Reading when the head of salt asked whether it can tell when a man is lying. The town's line was twice as long at the next market. The old of the flats took bread out toward the Maun Stair. A coaster's master, asked plainly, was told too. Told at the Year 5 morning: in Year 3 it told a young carrier at the gate that it cannot be killed, and told a young stallholder what the Reading does and, when he asked further, that both pans show.
- The old Weightward's death. Opened Year 1. The Assay-master has nothing to tell yet and says he will tell it when he knows what there is to tell. The god has said it will not ask again unless he tells it.
- The gate. Opened Year 1. The god reads scales at the gatepost for a pinch in measure. The Stallreeve uses the readings and rules himself. Told at the Year 5 morning: on the fourteenth day of the second month of Year 3 the post stood empty for a day, by the Stallreeve's leave, with a pair of the watch at it, nobody read and no notch cut.
- The reading-sticks. Opened Year 1, the eleventh month. Cut by the Tally-keeper, by the Stallreeve's leave. The first stale stick was shown to the god in the third month of Year 2. Sticks for boats, from the fourth month of Year 2, on the Tally-keeper's own terms.
- Clay. Opened Year 1. The potters weigh diggers' clay by the basket; weight has not told good clay from bad. The god has said it will go to the diggers' pits for one day, after the weighing, and weigh there before diggers and kiln-masters. Ruling L-0037 says what its scales give of clay. It went in the last week of the second month of Year 2: three lumps from loads that fired well and three from loads that cracked do not look different in the pan. Orrin and the east kilns now fire a test tile of each load. By spring the potters said the test tiles do not tell. A second day at the pits, the eleventh month of Year 2: every lump much alike. The quarrel keeps.
- The potters' nest of eleven. Opened Year 1. Weighed Year 2: it does not agree with itself. Left in the hall. Fired again in the seventh month of Year 2, twenty-two pieces; the eleven chosen by the gate's readings agree to a hair and are kept at the potters' hall. No one has asked for that nest to be weighed before the ring. Told at the Year 5 morning: a third nest, to the old six, is shaped and drying on its boards (said by the master of the potters at the post, Year 3, the seventh month). The Weigher means to weigh each piece green before the firing and again fired and cooled. Held at the door of the potters' hall, the fourteenth day of the seventh month, on her question of what it wants of her boards.
- The low-tide trips of the boat people. Opened Year 1. The god asked Bren's household once; it does not ask to come and does not follow. Told at the Year 5 morning: gone from the channels about the lowest tide of the autumn of Year 3, and back.
- The dimming of the blue light. Opened Year 1. Seen by the god from the landing on the nights of Year 1, and on one night in each of the second, sixth and ninth months of Year 2; it would like to learn the light's habits. Told at the Year 4 morning: one night in the eleventh month and one in the twelfth month of Year 2. Told at the Year 5 morning: Year 3, the night of the twenty-second day of the first month, a pair of nights in the fifth month, and the night of the seventh day of the seventh month. A boy at the gate has asked whether the star is its own.
- The boats. Opened Year 2, the fourth month, by the ship owner of the channels. He asked for the god at his landing and no other; it refused that and he took its terms: loads set out and called at dawn at any landing whose owner asks, his first and standing, a pinch for a boat from the captain, the gate kept. Set before the Stallreeve. The sticks for the boats wait on the Tally-keeper. Told at the Year 4 morning: the Tally-keeper said yes to the sticks. Dawn readings ran from the fourth month; other owners asked for dawns; some captains would not show, and "I wasn't shown" is a saying on the water. In the sixth month the ship owner's own boat was read with sand and grit in its salt. Told at the Year 5 morning: the dawns at the landings went on through Year 3 as far as told. From the first month the stones for the search were weighed on the landings' beams at those dawns.
- A keeper's word down the coast. Opened Year 2, the tenth month, by the coaster's master's price (which is the town's weight) and the god's asking of the Stallreeve whether the town wants such a word sent; it sends none without their say. He said, "Not this summer. Ask us after the grain sitting." Year 3, the first month, after the ring named the town's weight: the Stallreeve brought the ring's yes and its asking for the words, with half a split stick laid at the foot of the gatepost; the ship owner of the open water is to carry it when his coasters go out past the Horn. Held; waiting on the god's words. Told at the Year 5 morning: played on the Year 4 day. The Weigher gave the words at the post. The first stick is the town's word (m-0005), and the carrier's half is with the ship owner of the open water, who carries it as cut. The second stick went up to the ring. On the seventeenth day of the sixth month the Stallreeve brought the ring's answer to the post: most of it taken as the town's word, and a pair of things not taken (that the fifth is light by a hair and the hall says so; that any captain may bring a scale to the hall). What was taken is not on the stick the carrier holds. The Stallreeve has asked what the Weigher would have cut for the carrier now; pending (`player/pending.md`). The coasters go out in the first days of the tenth month, and the word has not gone.
- The asks of Year 3 that met silence: a boy's "Is the star yours?", the Stallreeve's question of what is to be cut for the carrier now, and a netmaker's question about the height of the water. Opened Year 3. Kept as pending questions (`player/pending.md`).
- The lost house, the lost name and the debt. Left alone until the god says.

## Closed

- The salt quarrel of the landing day. Opened and closed Year 1: the bench ruled the salt short by a seventh of a weight and the raker made it good.
- Whether Bren's balance is a true scale. Opened and closed Year 1 by ruling L-0031: it is.
- The weighing of the old weights. Opened Year 1 by the god's offer to the Assay-master. The eleven asked it, seven to three; the god said yes before the yard and gave its terms; the eleven took them whole, eight to three. Closed Year 2, the first month: the six were weighed before the eleven with the Assay-master's hand on the beam, and every figure cut.
- The hand at the nest. Opened Year 1. Closed Year 1, the twelfth month: the god claimed no price; the master's scale read level at the gate and the Tally-keeper cut the price as remitted.
- The Tally-keeper's stick of the gate's readings, and her asking for a mark. Opened Year 1. Closed Year 1, the eleventh month: the mark is two pans, level, cut at the head of her stick.
- The Assay-master's five questions from the dusk meeting. Opened Year 1. Closed Year 1, the eleventh month: answered at the hall door in the god's own words. He asked three more that met silence, and has still not given his name.
- The coaster out of Corrith. Opened Year 2, the first month of summer. Closed Year 2, the tenth month: her balance read level at the gate; she answered what the Weigher asked her, named her price, was told what it is when she asked plainly, and went down the channel three days after.
- The old hill weigher's stones. Opened Year 2. He asked at the weighing to have them weighed before the ring and had no answer. The god has sent him a word by carrier: at the gate, any market morning, for a pinch, before any of the ring who want to see; and it asks what his people think the seventh was. No word back yet. The word went up with the hill carriers in the second month of Year 2 (m-0002). Closed Year 3, the second month: he was in the town with the hill carriers, and on the eighth day his stones were weighed at the gate, the three largest on the Assay-master's own beam: 48, 96, 193, 384, 768 and 1,536. Asked what his people say the seventh was, he gave a saying he would not stand behind, and went home with the figures on his own stick.
- The asks at the winter sitting that met silence: whether it can be killed, and whether it can cure a fever. Opened Year 2, the sixth month. Kept as pending questions (`player/pending.md`). Closed on the Year 4 played day: answered by the player. Each answer is given when the asker, or another, next asks at the gate, or in session, and is not carried. In Year 3 a young carrier was told that it cannot be killed, and an old woman of the back houses that it has no power to cure a fever. What would break it is not answered unless the ring asks in session. The master of the smiths has not asked again.
- The salted baskets and the raking household. Opened Year 2, the sixth month, at the ship owner of the channels' landing. Closed Year 3, the second month: by the Stallreeve's leave the Weigher walked out to the Flats at the lowest tide. The household turned out every basket before it was asked, and the basket the Weigher chose read clean salt.
