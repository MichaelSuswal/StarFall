## 2. At the bench

- The Weigher stepped inside the gate and spoke to the one who had turned, a channel woman with a hand balance: "Morning. Is the hall open to anyone, or only to people the Warden knows?"
- Told to it by her: there is no warden; the bench is the Assay-master's; it is open to anyone with a thing to weigh; the one behind the balance is the Assay-master; the quarrel is a salt-raker and a fish-drier from "the east channel" over ten weights of salt sold three days back, which she says came to nine and a bit on her stone at home.
- Asked whether it was waiting to weigh something, it said it is a weigher, passing through, curious about the hall (the player's given words).
- It moved to the doorpost, a few paces from the pans, scales on it, coat closed.
- Roll r-0002 (DM, odds 70): the bench's reading went wrong. The Assay-master set ten of the hall's small clay weights and called it "Ten. It is full."
- The True Measure, told to the player: the beam is honest; the salt equals the ten pieces as they lie; eight pieces agree, one (chipped at the foot) is light by about a twentieth, one (cracked from its mark) by nearly a tenth; against ten like the eight the salt is short by about a seventh of one weight. Nothing of cause or of which size is the town's right one.
- The fish-drier disputed it ("Which ten? That's the chipped one."). The Assay-master: the hall has ten of that size and no others; bring a better set and it will be weighed against these. In the yard: a laugh, "Ten's ten," and "It's been like this since the Weightward died, and it'll be like this till they seat another."
- The Weigher kept silent through the first minute. The salt is still in the pan. No one has asked it what the salt weighs.
- Names heard from mortals so far: the Assay-master (the office), the Weightward (the dead keeper's office). No place name has been told, so nothing new in map knowledge.

## 3. Speaking to the weighing

- The Weigher asked the Assay-master's leave to put the salt in its own pans on the bench. Leave given, not rolled: the Assay-master had just offered to weigh any better set against the hall's.
- It set its scales on the bench, weighed the salt against its nested brass, calling each: the whole nest but the two smallest cups. It said the count plainly.
- It then said, in the player's words: the beam is honest; the ten are not ten alike; eight agree; the chipped one and the cracked one are light; the salt is short by about a seventh of one weight against ten like the eight; it cannot say which size is the town's. It said nothing of the raker's honesty.
- To the raker: he may have been short-weighed himself. His answer: he fills by his father's basket, struck level, ten baskets to the load, the load weighed at the hall when the basket was made; he never took a pinch.
- To the fish-drier: would the missing seventh made good settle it? Her answer: yes; no quarrel with him if the hall's stones are wrong.
- The raker: if the bench says short he will make it good today from the morning's raking; the bench said full.
- The Weigher offered to leave its brass on the bench for the day, and said it is not here to take the hall's measure from the Assay-master but cannot watch a hall weigh short and say nothing.
- The Assay-master's answer: knows the two bad pieces and uses them because the hall has only ten of that size; the old weights they could be set right against are the Weightward's to bring out, there is no Weightward, and the council has sat on it since spring. Noted that the stranger never weighed the pieces and no eye sees a twentieth: demands each piece be weighed in the stranger's pans in front of the town. If guessed, the ruling stands. If known, the bench will say the salt is short, the raker makes it good, and then "you and I will talk about how you knew." Refuses to keep a stranger's brass on the bench; the stranger may weigh at the bench while watched. Asks: "Who are you?"
- Board as left: the cracked piece held out over the Weigher's pan; the salt in the Weigher's other pan; the scales on the bench, out of its hand but within reach; the yard at the door.
- Pending: the demand to weigh the pieces; "Who are you?" (`pending.md`).
- The Weigher has given no name and has not said what it is.

## 4. The ten pieces

- The player set the nest's sizes: seven cups, each twice the last, one to sixty-four (127 in all). Written on the power card as a dated entry.
- The Assay-master set the pieces down in the Weigher's pan one by one, the eight first as asked. Called: each of the eight, twelve and a little better than a half (about 12.6); the chipped piece, twelve less a hair; the cracked piece, eleven and something under a half (about 11.4). The salt is 124; ten like the eight would be about 126.
- The third and ninth were weighed again at the Assay-master's asking, then set against each other on the hall's own beam: the third sank, in sight of the door.
- The Assay-master's ruling, aloud: the salt is short by a seventh of one weight against the eight; the bench was wrong in its ten and says so; the raker makes it good today; no fault found in his basket or his word. The raker: "Today." The fish-drier took her hand off the bench.
- The Weigher answered "Who are you?" in the player's words (a weigher, down the old bank at first light, called the Weigher, another name lost) and said what it wants (a hall where nobody in the yard has to wonder whether the ruling is fair). It asked the Assay-master's name.
- The Assay-master gave no name: "When you have a name to put in the pan, I will put mine in the other." Said the Weightward's seat is the council's to fill and the council has not agreed on anything since spring. Has not yet had its talk about how the stranger knew: invited it back when the bench closes at dusk, with the brass, door shut.
- The Weigher took up its scales and nest; they are on it again.
- The channel woman called it "Weigher" and asked what it would take to try her balance in the yard. Two more in the line wait on the answer.
- Board as left: the Weigher inside the hall door, turned from the bench, scales and nest in hand; the woman before it with her hand balance held up; the line at the door; the Assay-master back at the bench with the next quarrel.
- No scale has yet been checked against the god's scales, so no true scale stands anywhere.
- No seal set, no licence given, no promise made.

## 5. The yard, the market, and the door at dusk (final save, at the close)

- To Bren (the channel woman; she gave her name): the fee is a dried fish. She poles a reed boat on the islands west of the Bank Way. Names told: Bren; the Bank Way (the path, f21).
- The Weigher set its scales at the foot of the gatepost, off the bench, and read her balance with brass from the nest set in each pan in turn. Rolls r-0003, r-0004, r-0005 (DM, odds 50 each): all three of the first scales were out. Bren's: left pan heavy by one in twenty (her selling side). A potter's boy's beam: one in thirty, toward the buying side. A hill man's charcoal balance: one in ten. Bren took up her cord herself; tried again, her pans agreed.
- It refused scrap silver from a woman at the back; she paid in barley.
- More came through the middle of the day, taken in line order, reading only. Not rolled singly: most were out by something, cord balances worst, potters' beams least, out both ways, none a cheat's scale by its look. One of the watch stood by for a time and left without speaking.
- It stopped when the light slanted and told those waiting it will be back tomorrow.
- Fees held: about a string of dried fish (less three spent), a fold of barley, two small fired weights of the town's making.
- In the market it listened. Told and heard: everything is priced in weights of salt and settled in kind; each trade has its own measure (basket, hand and bundle, string, piece and kiln load) and its own stones; two kiln-masters and a clay-digger quarrelling over grit in a boatload of flood silt; the hill corner thin until the carriers come down the Old Rudd Way (the sunken road, f23); the country is called the Reedmouths (r14).
- It bought a clay cup from a potter's wife for three dried fish. Nothing was weighed in the sale. In its own pan: forty-one, and sound.
