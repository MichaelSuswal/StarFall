## 1. The walk in

- The Weigher checked what it carries at the landing (scales, chalk, cord, the empty pocket still empty), then walked the path south-east along the bank top, about a mile, keeping out of the reeds.
- It stopped on the bank short of the first roofs and watched the town wake: boats out at first light and back loaded with reed, mats, baskets, fish and one load of river clay; pots and covered baskets carried in on the banks; the old sunken road nearly empty. Nobody went to a high place. No drum was heard.
- Roll r-0001 (DM, odds 30): it is a market day.
- It walked in unannounced with the traffic, kept to the path along the market's edge, and did not go among the stalls. No scale has yet been within its presence, so The True Measure has told it nothing.
- God-sense: nothing from anyone it passed within a few paces.
- It stands in the open gate of the town's house on the high bank. In view: the yard, the flood drum on its frame, the open door, a long dim room with a plank bench, fired clay weights along the wall, a balance, someone seated behind it, two people disputing over a basket, three or four waiting in the yard, one of whom has turned to look at it.
- No one has spoken to it. It has said nothing. No name has been told to it.
- Standing words the player gave for the gate, if asked: it is a weigher come to see the market; it weighs what is put on the pans and will check anyone's scale for a handful of grain. It does not say it is a god.
