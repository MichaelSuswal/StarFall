## Played at a held moment, Year 003

The ring's yes and its asking for the words, at the gatepost, the first month, the fifth day. Played live. It holds.

1. The fish seller's hand balance was tried first and called: out, the buying pan heavy by about one part in sixty.
2. The half-stick of the asking was taken up: yes to the eleven's asking as spoken.
3. The word was given at the post, before the yard, cut by the Tally-keeper, mended twice by the Weigher before the split (once after the Assay-master spoke), read back and split.
   - Taken by the Stallreeve for the eleven, the town's word, on the first stick. Its text, as cut, is `messages/m-0005.md`.
   - Carried by the Stallreeve to the eleven, each addition by itself, neither yes nor no, on the second stick: "The fifth is light by a hair, and the hall says so. The rack is cut for seven, and the seventh is not in it. The hall does not know what it weighed, and has looked for it. Four of those, set against the first bronze, sank against it. That is the sum the head of the hill goods did aloud in the ring: about a twentieth heavy against the old bronze. The chipped piece and the cracked piece are light against the eight, by about a twentieth and about a tenth. I have not weighed the whole ten against the old six piece by piece since, and I do not say more than I weighed. If the ring wants the figures, the Tally-keeper has them on her stick of the first day, in her own cuts. Any captain may bring a scale to the hall and have it read. It will be told what it does, which pan and how far, and nothing else. The hall keeps no weight that anyone may not see weighed." With it, the Weigher's asking "that it go in the order I said it and with nothing added".
   - "The second stick waits for the ring, as the Stallreeve said, and I'll not push it. If the ring says no to any of it, I'll want to hear which, so that the next word that goes is not the one that is cut short."
4. The first stick goes now, with the ship owner of the open water, when his coasters go out past the Horn. Sent to, in the player's words: "The word is sent to the captains of the coasting houses of the southern harbors, in the coaster master's own words: to the master who stood at this post in the tenth month of last year, and through her to every house she will tell. I put it to her house by name if the open-water owner knows it. If he does not, it goes to the master of whatever coaster of Corrith's he meets first, to be carried to the council of Corrith that keeps the set for quarrels."
5. To the carrier, in the Stallreeve's hearing: "Carry it as cut and nothing more, and if you are asked what the town's weight is, say what the stick says. If a captain asks what I am, tell him I am the Weightward of the hall and that the Weightward answers yes or no about a power of its own, or does not answer. Do not say more than I did. Do not sell it as a price. If a house wants to send a scale to be read against the hall's, say it may come here, and that I read it at the gate like any." He took the carrying as cut, the town's weight as the stick says it, and that it is no price. He would not promise the line on what the Weightward answers about itself, nor the line on a southern scale read at the hall, until the eleven have answered.
6. Told to the Stallreeve at the post: where the Weigher stands on the master of the smiths' ask (item 10).

## For the rest of Year 3, and standing

7. Prepared scenes, held for live play, not resolved. The nest the master of the potters is to fire to the old six in spring. Time: spring of Year 3, the days the world's to set (the ring's decision says "in spring").
   - First, beforehand, at the potters' hall, with the Assay-master and the Tally-keeper: "ask her to bring each piece to me green, before it is fired. I weigh each in front of them and cut the figure."
   - Second, at the kiln-opening, in person: "I cut the figure again once it's fired and cooled, in front of the same three. That gives her, and anyone else who fires weights, the loss from firing as a number, not a guess."
   - "I will also ask her to fire three spare pieces of each size so there is one to break. I'll set aside a day for it and ask the Stallreeve to have the watch keep the gate."
   - "I'll play the kiln scenes live when the night holds them for me."
8. A search. "I want to look for the seventh by what it weighs. Twice the sixth, by the run of them, is three thousand and seventy-two in my ones. On my dawns at the landings, I ask the ship owners and the captains to bring me any large stone they own that might be near it: anchors, ballast, door-stones, quern stones, hearth stones. I weigh each on the landing's beam with the owner watching, and I say only what it is. I'm not asking anyone to give anything up. If one turns out close, I tell the Stallreeve and the Assay-master first and the ring second."
9. A day away. "I ask the Stallreeve for one day, at the lowest tide, to walk out to the salt pans and look at the raking household that sent the salted baskets. I want to see the pans and weigh a basket there. The watch keeps the line, nobody is read, and the Tally-keeper cuts no notch for the day. I leave one thing unanswered when I go. The master of the smiths asked me what it would take to break me, and I will not answer that before I go or when I come back unless the ring asks me in session."
10. The two asks from the winter sitting of Year 2, answered.
   - The master of the smiths, "Can you be killed?": "No. I can be hurt, held or scattered, and I come together again." "What would break me" it will not answer. How it reaches him: "I say it when he next asks, or when I'm asked in session. I don't go to him." "If he or another asks me at the gate, I say it plainly. If the ring asks me in session, I say it there."
   - The woman of the back houses, "Can you cure a fever?": "No. I have no power for it. I am sorry about her child, and I would say so to her if she came to the gate." Not carried to her.
11. The Reading. "I let the Keeper's ruling on the Reading stand. If I'm asked a follow-up, I say the rest of it: that both pans show, and that anyone I read knows whether I mean my own terms."
12. Otherwise the god continues its course: the gate on market mornings, the landings on the other dawns, its standing answers, its nights at the landing. Earlier decisions stand unchanged.

Standing at the close: no seal set, no licence given, the Reading not used. Promised: the word, given; the potters' nest weighed green and fired; its answers to the pair of asks when next asked.
