# Pending questions: Year 2, during the play day

Campaign sim-06. Every question someone in the world has asked the god that the player has not answered. From the Keeper's release for Year 1. Each is to be put to the player in the next played day.

## Held, played

- Answered in play, Year 2 play day: yes, with terms spoken to the yard (rolling save, Year 2, section 1). The Stallreeve, at the gatepost, a market day in the high summer of Year 1, before the yard: the eleven ask the Weigher to weigh the hall's old weights before them, at the first market after the grain is in, with the Assay-master's hand on the beam. Half of a split stick lies at its feet. Held at the moment of contact.

## From the meeting at dusk on the landing day, the Assay-master's

1. "Why should the eleven take a stranger's scale for the town's standards?"
2. "Are you something more than a man with good brass?"
3. "Who sent you? Does a king of the river towns want his share of our market again?"
4. "Why did you ask how the old Weightward died?"
5. "What does the Weigher want of the hall after the weighing?"

## From the year

6. A stallholder, first month: "How long will you stay?"
7. A child in the line, first month: "Are you the drowned one's child?"
8. A hill carrier, second month, in jest: "Do you come from Hask?"
9. A salt-raker, second month: whether salt will do for the fee. (He paid in fish the next day.)
10. The kiln-master Orrin, third month: "Bring your brass to the diggers' pits and say which clay is good."
11. A potter with a ball of clay, third month: "Is this clay clean?"
12. A stallholder, third month: "May I say my scale was read at your gate?"
13. The Tally-keeper, fourth month: "Name a mark, and I will cut it."
14. A bonded net-mender, fourth month, after her owner's grain scale read out by about a twentieth in the owner's favor: "Reckon my debt for me, and strike it." (The council later took a twentieth off the debt.)
15. Two households, fifth month, over a reed island that split: "Who owns it? You say what is true." (They took it to the council.)
16. A stallholder, sixth month: to stand witness to a bargain. (Two of the watch stood witness.)
17. The Stallreeve, sixth month: "Do you ask a price of him?" The price is set and unclaimed.
18. The yard, seventh month, more than once: "Will you be our Weightward?"
19. The master of the potters, ninth month: "Will you weigh our new nest of eleven against the old and set the hall's standards by them?"
