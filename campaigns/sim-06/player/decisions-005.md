## Played at a held moment, Year 003

### The weighing green, at the potters' hall (the seventh month, the fourteenth day, and the twenty-fifth)

Played live. It holds.

1. The Weigher asked the master of the potters, at her door, for leave to weigh each piece green and again after the kiln, the pieces lifted only by her or her hands, and for three spares of each size, one of each to be broken if she allowed it. It did not go in until she stepped back.
2. She took the weighing on her terms: no hand but hers and her firemen's carries a green piece; one piece at a time to the bench; the Weigher says what a piece is and not what it should be. She took the spares, thirty-three pieces; the Assay-master said the hall pays for clay and charcoal for a proving, on his word, and that he tells the ring after. One kiln, one firing.
3. The twenty-two were weighed green on the fourteenth in the Weigher's pans, each set in by the master, called and cut by the Tally-keeper on a long stick, each foot marked. First and second of each pair: the one 13.3, 13.3; the two 26.6, 26.8; the three 40.0, 39.8; the four 53.0, 53.2; the six 80.0, 80.0; the seven 93.6, 92.8; the eight 107.0, 106.9; the ten 133.4, 133.3; the twelve 159.0, 160.6; the fourteen 186.6, 186.6; the fifteen 199.4, 198.9. The Assay-master saw the sevens and the twelves pair against pair on the potters' beam and said the beam agrees.
4. Asked by the master that the green figures stay on the stick until the kiln is opened, the Weigher said yes: "They stay on her stick until the kiln is opened. I don't cry them at the gate or the landing, and I don't say them over to anyone, even in passing." Asked by the Stallreeve or the ring, "I'll tell them it is on the stick and not mine to cry until the first", and it asks that the matter be put to her first. "I won't say a false figure, and I won't say a true one that you've asked me to hold." To anyone else who asks: "The master of the potters asked that the figures stay on the stick until the kiln is opened, and I've said yes. After the first you can have them from the Tally-keeper, as called." She took this. It asked that the three read the stick together on the first, green beside fired, before it is cried; she will be at the reading.
5. The breaking: she allowed one spare of each size, fired, on the first, and not green; she breaks, looks first, and keeps what is left.
6. The seventeenth, at the gate: the Weigher asked the Stallreeve whether the watch needs to stand at the gate on the twenty-fifth and the first. Neither is a market day and no watch was needed. It told him what it had promised about the green figures.
7. The twenty-fifth, at the potters' hall, before the same three: the thirty-three spares weighed green the same way, on a second stick. Third, fourth and fifth of each size: the one 13.3, 13.3, 13.4; the two 26.7, 26.6, 26.7; the three 40.0, 39.8, 39.9; the four 53.5, 53.5, 53.3; the six 79.8, 80.2, 79.4; the seven 93.4, 93.4, 93.7; the eight 106.3, 106.4, 106.8; the ten 132.7, 133.2, 133.6; the twelve 160.2, 159.6, 160.0; the fourteen 186.8, 186.1, 186.0; the fifteen 201.2, 199.7, 199.7.

### The kiln-opening (the eighth month, the first day), and on to the fourth day

Played live. It holds.

8. The Weigher stood clear and touched nothing. Every piece was set on the trestles by its mark, then weighed smallest first, each from the master's hand to the pan, the scratch read first, the fired figure cut beside the green. All fifty-five came out whole. First and second of each pair, fired and cooled: the one 12.3, 12.3; the two 24.6, 24.6; the three 37.0, 36.8; the four 49.1, 49.3; the six 73.7, 73.9; the seven 86.3, 86.0; the eight 98.4, 98.4; the ten 123.2, 122.9; the twelve 147.1, 147.7; the fourteen 172.5, 172.3; the fifteen 184.8, 184.0. The spares, third, fourth and fifth of each size: the one 12.3, 12.3, 12.3; the two 24.6, 24.6, 24.7; the three 36.9, 36.9, 36.9; the four 49.2, 49.2, 49.3; the six 73.6, 74.0, 73.5; the seven 86.4, 85.9, 86.2; the eight 98.3, 98.3, 98.5; the ten 122.9, 123.3, 122.8; the twelve 147.5, 147.5, 147.7; the fourteen 172.3, 172.5, 172.1; the fifteen 184.9, 183.9, 184.6.
9. The Weigher called only what the pan gave. It said nothing of the green figures aloud and nothing of the loss: "I do not say which pieces shrank most, or which size the fire changed least. The stick says it, and anyone can read it." Nobody asked it. No loss was cut as a figure.
10. One fired spare of each size (the fifth) was broken by the master, who looked first. A close grey body, no stone, no hollow; the halves together gave what the piece gave whole. She kept the halves. "I do not say which clay is better, and I do not say whether the kiln did well."
11. Both sticks were read through with the master and the Assay-master at the Tally-keeper's shoulder. The Assay-master saw the sevens and the twelves again on the potters' beam and said the beam agrees. The Weigher stepped aside: "I do not stand at it unless he asks me." He did not ask.
12. The pieces went up to the hall that day. On the second and third the master rubbed down at the hall's bench. On the fourth the Assay-master told the Weigher, before the yard, that eleven of the new pieces sit level with the old six and with one another on his beam, and that the ring's day for the town's weight stands. The Weigher was not at the beam and was not asked to call the eleven. The other eleven and the spares went back to the potters' hall. The sticks are the hall's, with the Tally-keeper.
13. The Stallreeve's question of the seventeenth day of the sixth month, answered at the gate on the second day of the eighth month, a market day: "Cut nothing more of mine. What the ring took is the town's word, and the first stick carries the part the ring let me send. The second is the ring's to cut or leave. If the carrier is asked about the fifth, let him say what the stick says and no more. If a captain asks me about the fifth, I say it is light by a hair on the Assay-master's beam, because that is what I called, and I say the ring did not take it as the town's word. I will not claim the ring's yes where it said no. I'd have the carrier told only this: that the town's word is what the stick says, and that the hall's pieces are the hall's and the old six the town's weight from next autumn's first market. If the ring wants more cut before the tenth month, I will say it in session. I ask nothing for the fifth or the scale." He said: "Nothing more of yours, then. That is an answer, and I had waited for one." Nothing was cut.

## For the rest of Year 3, and standing

14. After the Assay-master has said what the beam says: "I say nothing to the yard that the stick doesn't say, and I say it to anyone who asks, from the Tally-keeper's cuts."
15. Of the fifth, to a captain who asks the Weigher itself: "I say it is light by a hair on the Assay-master's beam, because that is what I called, and I say the ring did not take it as the town's word. I will not claim the ring's yes where it said no." "If the ring wants more cut before the tenth month, I will say it in session. I ask nothing for the fifth or the scale."
16. Asked whether it can tell the height of the water as the tide-reckoner does, said when the netmaker or another next asks: "No. The tide-reckoner tells the height of a flood days ahead, by what she feels, and it costs her the feeling in her hands for a season. I have no such thing. I can weigh what you bring to the pan. I can tell you the Alder is up, because I can see it, and that's all."
17. Otherwise the god continues its course: the gate on market mornings, the landings on the other dawns, its standing answers, its nights at the landing with its habit there. Earlier decisions stand unchanged.

Standing at the close: no seal set, no licence given, the Reading not used. No scene is prepared for later.
