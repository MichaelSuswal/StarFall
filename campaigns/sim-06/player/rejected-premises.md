None.

## Not rejections

- Question 8: the answer picks a point inside the range offered ("Closer to a great sorcerer than to the one-eyed man") and adds an extra: "but with one thing that is clearly beyond mortal reach."
- Question 15: the answer picks an offered option ("A knowing face to face") and adds an extra: "plus a faint pull when another god's goods or marks cross my markets."
- Question 16: the answer picks an offered option ("One body for me") and adds an extra: "If there is a way to act at a distance, it should work through agents, seals and contracts, and not through copies of me."

## Day 2

None. The player took the premise as given: one body, a seat, helpers and a court.

Requests not to be told something: none

### Bent by the DM

- The first asking, a single edge, was divided into three powers, priced apart.
- The Good-Faith Reading was trimmed from a reading of "good faith or lies" to a yes or no on each party's intent at the moment of agreeing. Two terms were added by the DM and accepted: the reading is the god's choice each time, and when it is only witness, each party learns of the others what it learned.
- The Seal's price was first set by the DM as both the cap and a darkening of all three powers. The player pushed back; the DM agreed and set it as the player asked. The DM added, and the player accepted, that seals broken while the Seal is dark are known at dawn without the party.
- Seals by factors was narrowed by the DM: four licences at most, given and revoked face to face, terms unknown to the god until told. The player asked only for "a standing licence that I can revoke".
- A true scale was defined by the DM as checked "in the god's presence". The player had said "checked against mine".
- The return was made countable by the DM (a season, a year, no less than three years) from the player's "slowly, and the weaker the true houses nearby, the longer it takes", and its hearing trimmed from "every measure struck in my markets" to the drift of it.
- The scales as the instrument: the DM added what happens when they are destroyed or held by another at a breaking of the body.
- The player's flaw (greedy for being right about a price) was ruled conduct, not a weakness, and buys nothing.

### Not rejections

- The player asked the DM to decide the dent's story and the empty pocket's purpose. The DM deferred both to the Keeper.
- The player asked what a god of measure looks like to mortals who have never seen coins, and what its people will weigh first. The DM answered neither: the first is found in play, the second is Day 3's.
- The seat, helpers and court were described on Day 2 and carried to Day 3 through the conduct profile.
- The reply opened "Standard mode, as before." The mode was set on Day 1 and was not asked again.

## Day 3

None rejected. The player took the premise: a people of about 5,000 with a town, a seat, a council and helpers.

Bent: the seat and the helpers are designed, not yet serving. The house, the council and the offices stand under the town's own law, in a town that does not know the Weigher, and the Weigher lands with no helpers and no seat of its own in fact. The cost was stated at the table: no one acts for it while it is away, its unplayed time runs on standing conduct alone, and the town goes its own way until each office is won in play. It is not a refused default, bears on no edge and buys nothing. The player: "I accept all four rulings, including the cost to my seat and helpers. I landed without being asked to, and I will have to win the town."

The people and the town are unnamed, by the player's choice, until a mortal names them in play. That is a valid answer and not a bend.

Requests not to be told something: none

Sealed requests to the Keeper: one, kept in the sealed record

### Bent by the DM

- The guide's third form speaks of helpers. The DM applied it to the seat as well, since the house stands as the town's at landing.
- "Good with small numbers", "quick to notice when a friend gives short measure" and "proud of being hard to cheat" were taken as a temper, able to fail, not a guarantee. The player agreed.
- The player had the Clerk of the Seals sending word when a seal breaks. The DM corrected it from the card: only the Weigher feels a breaking, and it tells the Clerk. The player accepted.
- The player had a bonded person's debt "weighed" at the hall. The DM said the scales know weight, count and purity of what is in the pans, not whether a sum is the sum agreed. The player accepted.
- A scale tested by the Warden in the Weigher's absence is not a true scale as the card means it. Said by the DM from the card; the player accepted.
- The tide-reckoner: the DM added that she cannot teach her knack at the player's telling and that it ends with her unless play makes another. Accepted with the read-back.
- A factor's rule was written as an oath, not a binding. The player confirmed it.

### Not rejections

- The player asked for a name for the town and said the people should choose it. The DM said the name is the player's to give or to leave; the player left it for play.
- The player asked what the people believe about the ground toward the hills and whether there is an old road. The DM did not answer; both are on the reconcile list.
- The player's wishes for the land and its past (the floods, the age of the market, the house on the highest bank, ships on open water, the oldest weights) are recorded as wishes or beliefs in `people-meeting.md`.
