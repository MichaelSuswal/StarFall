# How the Weigher meets its people

Campaign sim-06. Written by the DM at the close of creation Day 3, 2026-11-24. Read back to the player and agreed.

## What was agreed

They are already there, with their own history, and they do not know the Weigher is coming.

> The market stands, the house stands, and the hall is in it. It is a poor hall: a plank bench, a few clay standards cracked and mismatched, and a scale nobody trusts. The eleven already sit, as a loose council that argues and decides little. The three offices exist under other names, held by the people I described, and they know they are in office. They are waiting for something, but not for me. The old keeper of the standards died last spring and nobody has replaced them, which is why the hall is poor and the market quarrels. I come to a town that has a vacancy where a keeper should be, and I fill it.

> They do not know I am coming.

The Warden and the dead keeper are separate offices: "Two offices. I would keep a living Warden of the Hall under the town's own name for it, the chief assayer, patient and humorless, who tests weights for the town. Separately there is a vacant keeper's place, the keeper of the standards, and that is the one I take myself, by checking the town's standards against my own scales."

The Weigher lands with no helpers and no seat of its own in fact. See `hierarchy.md`.

## Standing conduct

Also written as dated entries in `player/conduct-profile.md`.

- First action: it comes to the town and means to fill the vacant keeper's place, "in the open, with the Warden watching", by checking the town's standards against its own scales. Where the house stands is the world's. (check with the player before acting)
- It brings the scale to the potters' clay first, salt second, ore third.
- The hall is open to anyone with a scale, for a small fee in measure; a failed scale is marked openly, never broken, and nobody is shamed.
- It rules only on questions of weight and takes other cases only if the council asks.
- Its first law, once anyone carries its word: a word of the Weigher's is its own only if the split stick matches.
- It accepts debt bondage and means to put its rules on debt labor into the town's law.
- Of a factor it asks an oath, a share, a stake and an open ledger, and owes standing, protection and cover.

## Heard, not promised

- That the town lets the Weigher fill the vacancy.
- That clay is the first thing brought to the scale, and that the people come to trust the scale: "Clay is the first thing they will bring to the scale and argue about".
- "If one of them hears the word 'scale' with 'god', they will laugh and then stop laughing."
- That the council brings the Weigher only questions of weight.
- That the town takes the rules on debt labor into its law.
- That the Warden argues with the Weigher and can say no to it; that the Reeve stays well liked; that the Clerk comes close to it. Each is who the mortal is, able to fail.
- That a factor who breaks the oath loses the house and their standing with it.
- That strangers come to feel it is safe to be wrong in the hall.

## For the Keeper to reconcile

1. How long the people have been there. The player's wish, with no demand: "I would like them to say that they came to the banks in the time of the great-grandparents' grandparents, which is long enough to have a story and short enough to remember a time before it." And: "If the night sets it differently, I will take what the night sets."
2. What they say came before them. The player's wish: "I would also like their oldest weights to be older than they are, and nobody can explain why. That gives me my old roads and old measures without my naming them."
3. That they are "descended from several small groups that were pushed together by the floods", and that the market "is older than any lord". Whether there is or was a lord is not said.
4. What lies past their borders, and what they believe of it. The player asked: "I would also like to hear what the people believe about the empty ground to the hills, and whether there is an old road." That the ground toward the hills is empty is the player's description, not a fact. The DM told the player it would be heard from a mortal or seen.
5. A belief that claims an origin: "There was once a larger one, and the story is that it drowned, and that is why the tide keeps coming." Also "small gods in every channel and bank, who must be fed with the first of a catch", and that "no god has ever stayed to count what it was owed".
6. The old keeper of the standards, who "died last spring" and was not replaced; the poor hall, the cracked standards and the scale nobody trusts.
7. Places the player wishes placed: the house "on the highest firm bank, at the point where the main track meets the main channel, so that boats and carts both come to its door", with "a gate that is never locked and a door that is" and no wall; a drum on the highest bank; salt pans on the flats; the hill country as the source of ore and charcoal.
8. Ships and open water: "Two are ship-owners, one from the channels and one from the open water", and factors who go "to the hill country and to the open water". Whether there are ships, and anywhere for them to go, is the world's.
9. The town's own names for the offices the player calls Warden, Reeve and Clerk, which the player did not give, and the office the Clerk's is to grow from.
10. The tide-reckoner's knack, priced by the DM at her own cost: fit it to how the world's magic works.
11. What holding a licence costs a factor beyond the oath, share, stake and open ledger the Weigher asks (the card's Night 3 fitting).
12. Whether the town holds debt-bonded folk as the player supposes, and on what terms now.
