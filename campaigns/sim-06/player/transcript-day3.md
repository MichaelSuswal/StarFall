## Player:

**Who they are.** I want them to be a river-and-reed people, and I want them mixed. They are about five thousand, descended from several small groups that were pushed together by the floods. Some are channel-dwellers who fish and cut reed, some are salt-rakers from the flats, some are clay-bank farmers and potters, and some are hill people who came down to sell ore and charcoal. They do not share one old kinship. What they share is that the market is the only place all four groups meet, so the market is the town's real center, and it is older than any lord. They are practical, a little suspicious, good with small numbers, and quick to notice when a friend gives short measure. I want them to be proud of being hard to cheat.

**How they live.** On the old raised banks, in houses of reed and clay on timber stilts or on mounds, joined by raised paths and flat-bottomed boats. They keep reed gardens and grain on the firm clay, nets and weirs in the channels, and salt pans on the flats. They fire pottery and cut reed for mats, roofing and baskets. From the hills they get ore and charcoal, and they work them with a bloomery at the pre-steel level you described. I am not naming any technology beyond that. For now, a measure is a thing carved and fired: standard clay weights, notched tally-sticks, and reed baskets of fixed volume.

**What they weigh first.** Salt is the obvious answer, so I will not start there. I want them to weigh the flood-silt clay that their potters use. It matters more than anyone realizes, because a good pot is what everyone buys, and the quality of the clay decides whether a kiln load cracks. Right now each potter judges clay by feel and by rumor, and every kiln-master suspects the clay-diggers of mixing grit into the load. Clay is the first thing they will bring to the scale and argue about, and it is cheap enough that nobody is ruined by being wrong. Salt comes second, and ore third, once they have learned to trust the scale.

**The seat.** A counting-house on the highest firm bank, at the point where the main track meets the main channel, so that boats and carts both come to its door. It has a hall of weights, a long room with a table of fired clay standards along the wall and a scale for anyone who wants to bring their own to be checked against mine. The house is mine, but the hall is open to everyone, and I want the town to see that. I do not want a wall. I want a gate that is never locked and a door that is.

**The court.** Not a throne room. I want a council of eleven, who sit in a ring on the hall floor. Four of them are the heads of the four trades: reed and fish, salt, clay, and hill goods. Four are guild-masters of the crafts, meaning potters, boatwrights, weavers and smiths. Two are ship-owners, one from the channels and one from the open water. One is the chief assayer. They argue in front of me, and I listen, and I rule when the argument turns to a question of measure. I do not decide who wins a quarrel about price, because the point of the house is that they settle it on a true scale.

**Who runs it when I am away.** Three offices.
- **The Warden of the Hall**, who keeps the standards and tests the weights. This should be the chief assayer. I want someone patient and slightly humorless, who will argue with me.
- **The Reeve of the Gate**, who runs the market itself: stalls, fees, disputes between buyers and sellers, and the watch. I want someone practical and well liked.
- **The Clerk of the Seals**, who keeps the register of every sealed bargain and every licensed factor, and who sends word when a seal breaks. I want this one close to me. It is the office I would license first.
I would also like a handful of factors, two or three to start, who go out with sealed goods and sealed agreements to the hill country and to the open water.

**How my word reaches the far end.** By hand and by custom, since they have no better way. The Reeve's runners carry my rulings, written as notches on a stick and spoken aloud at the gate. There are tally-sticks split in two, one half kept in the hall and one half carried, so that no message can be changed without the halves failing to match. I would like to keep this as my first law: a word of mine is only a word of mine if the stick matches. Boats carry the sticks to the far channels, and a drum on the highest bank calls the town to the hall when a ruling has been made.

**Things I want, with no demand attached.** I would like a name for the town. I will let the people choose it, and I will not suggest one. I would also like to hear what the people believe about the empty ground to the hills, and whether there is an old road. I said yesterday that I wanted old roads, and the DM said the world decides, so I will wait and see.
