# Decisions, Year 2

Campaign sim-06. Handed off by the DM at the close of the Year 2 played day, 2026-11-24 (Pacific). Decisions only, in the order given. The player's words are quoted where they set the terms. The first items were played at the held moment, late in the eleventh month of Year 1; the clock reads Year 2.

1. The council's asking: yes. Said before the yard, with the half-stick taken up and kept: "Yes. I'll weigh the hall's old weights before the eleven, at the first market after the grain is in, with the Assay-master's hand on the beam."

2. The terms of the weighing. Spoken to the yard and carried up by the Stallreeve: "I weigh each piece in my pans and call it in my cups, out loud, one at a time. The Tally-keeper cuts what I call, and the Assay-master checks each reading on his own beam beside mine. I don't set the town's standard. I tell you what each piece is now, and the eleven decide which is the town's weight. If the eleven want the new clay nest weighed against the old set the same day, the potters' master can bring it, and I'll weigh that the same way." Added by the player, to be carried up to the eleven as terms: "The weighing is done in the hall, in front of the eleven, and nothing leaves the hall. I don't carry a weight out." And: "The Tally-keeper cuts every figure I call, and the Assay-master's reading is cut beside mine. Where the two differ, both are cut." Of these two: "They only work if the eleven agree to them in advance, and I'd rather they were the hall's rules than my habits."

3. Its own conduct on the weighing day: "If a piece does not read true, or is more than ordinary craft, I say so in those words and do not guess a weight." And: "I take no fee and no gift for the day, from the hall or from any of the eleven." Before the weighing it asks the potters' master to bring the new nest to the hall and not to the gate, "so the eleven see it from the first."

4. The Weightward's seat. Said to the yard: it has not said yes; it will say after the weighing, not before. The answer is the player's to give at the table and is not given in unplayed time. In its words: "It turns on two things: whether the eleven take the terms as I gave them, and whether the Assay-master and I are still arguing as equals when the weights are down. If the seat is offered as a gift, I'll say no. If it's offered as an office with a limit and a check, I'll say it again then."

5. The Assay-master, at dusk when the bench closes, with the brass, held at the hall door. It says these to him and to no one else:
   - Why the eleven should take a stranger's scale: "They shouldn't, on my word. Take the Weightward's own weights, which are the town's, and weigh them in front of the eleven with your hand on the beam. I give you numbers you can check against any scale you trust. If the numbers hold, you have what you need. If they don't, you've lost an afternoon. I'm offering a measurement, not a standard."
   - Whether it is something more than a man with good brass: "Yes. I'm not going to give you the rest before the weighing, because a man who explains himself first is selling. I'll tell you what you ask after, and I won't lie to you about it."
   - Who sent it: "No one. No king. I came down the old bank alone, and I have nobody behind me that I know of."
   - Why it asked how the Weightward died: "Because the seat has stood empty a year, and I wanted to know whether the quiet was an ordinary one. I'm sorry he was your friend. I won't ask again unless you tell me."
   - What it wants of the hall after the weighing: "The weights set right and anyone able to check them. The seat filled by whoever the eleven choose, and if it isn't me, then someone who'll argue with me. I'd call you the likeliest, but that isn't mine to say."

6. The Tally-keeper's mark, named by the Weigher: "Cut two pans, level. Nothing else."

7. The thief's price. "I won't claim a price from a bonded man." It asked the Tally-keeper to cut the price as remitted if the scale of the bonded man's master reads level at the gate within the month. If that scale is not brought, or reads out, the price lies unclaimed as now. "I'm not asking for it either way."

8. The reading-sticks. Asked of the Stallreeve and the Tally-keeper together and granted in play (r-0010). The Weigher's terms: a split stick for a scale, one half the owner's and one the Tally-keeper's, carrying the day and the reading, saying it holds for that day and no longer, lapsing if the scale is not read again within a season; her fee a pinch in measure from the owner; nothing to the Weigher. It keeps the Stallreeve's rule (no stick on a stall or a scale) and the Tally-keeper's notch (one for each market a scale is not brought) as they are, will tell the Stallreeve his rule is a good one when he is next at the gate, and "will watch for the first stale stick."

9. The gate, for the rest of the year: "I keep the gate. I read the line in order, I take my fee in measure, and I take no silver." The fee: "Salt, fish, grain or a fired weight, the same measure for each, and no more than a pinch of any of them. I'll weigh the pinch in my pans and the raker can watch." A scale read out is read again when its owner brings it back, and the Weigher says what it finds.

10. What it says at the gate when asked:
    - How long it stays: "Till I've seen the town's weights set right. Past that I don't know. I don't walk off a quarrel half-weighed."
    - Whether it is the drowned one's child: "Not that I know. I couldn't tell you whose child I am. I've a name I answer to and one I've lost. That's all I have of it."
    - Whether it comes from Hask, to the hill carriers: "I've not been there that I know of, and I'd like to hear what a load of their iron weighs on your scale before it gets to mine. Where I came from I can't say. I came down the old bank."
    - Whether a stallholder may say a scale was read at its gate: "He may say it was read on a day, and what the reading was, which pan and how far. He may not say it is true."

11. Clay. To the potter's "Is this clay clean?": "I can tell you what's in the pan and how much of it is clay, if the scale gives it. Whether that's clean for your fire, I can't say. Bring me three lumps from loads that fired well and three from loads that cracked. I'll tell you what I read of each, and you tell me if the two sets look different. If they don't, I've told you nothing, and I'll say so." Until the Keeper rules on clay: "weight only, and if it doesn't read, I say it doesn't."

12. The diggers' pits, for Orrin. "After the weighing, not before." One day and no more, the gate left empty. Before it goes it asks the Stallreeve whether the watch will keep the line and the Tally-keeper keep her sticks. At the pits it weighs what the diggers bring up, in front of the diggers and the kiln-masters, and says what the pan says. "I can't tell which clay is good for a kiln, and I'm not going to pretend to."

13. Debts. "I don't strike debts, and I won't say I can." It will weigh the goods a debt is owed in, if they are brought, and will stand at the bench while a debt is reckoned and weigh the goods if the Assay-master asks.

14. The reed island, and the like: "I can't say who owns it, because owning is not a weight." It will weigh what each household says was cut, drawn or carried off, if brought. The rest is the council's.

15. Standing witness: "I'll stand witness to a bargain if both parties ask me, in front of the Stallreeve's watch if they like. I say what I saw and heard and nothing more. I won't tell either side whether I think the other means it, and I won't be held to a verdict on whether it was kept." (check with the player before acting, as the conduct profile marks it: no power is used at a witnessing in unplayed time.)

16. Its nights: "I sit at the landing, by the channel folk's lamp, where the boats come in. I don't sleep, and I don't pretend to. I listen to the water and the talk. I count the day's readings over in my head, and I watch the blue light, which I'd like to learn the habits of."

17. The low tides. "I also ask Bren's people, quietly and once, about the low-tide trips to the Maun Stair. I don't ask to come, and I don't follow. I only want to know what they would be willing to say if someone asked."

Standing at the close: no seal set, no licence given. Promised: the weighing, on the council's day, on the terms above. It has not said it is a god to anyone; item 5 gives the one word it means to say to the Assay-master.
