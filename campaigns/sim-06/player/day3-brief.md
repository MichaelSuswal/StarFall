# Day 3 brief: the land

For the DM, in words the player may hear. It says only what the land itself would show the god as it comes down. Nothing here is a distance, a direction or a promise of what is in view from one spot; those come later, from the map.

## The season
Early autumn.

## The country
Low, wet country at the head of a wide sheltered water, where several rivers come down and part into many channels before they reach it. Between the channels lie islands of reed. Toward the open water the ground goes to pale flats, bare and white-crusted where the tide leaves them. Behind, the land lifts into low hills. The firm ground is old raised bank, clay underfoot, with the wet on either side of it; it is country that is hard to cross except where the banks and channels allow.

In this season the reeds are tall and gone tawny, the mornings come in mist off the water, and the air has the first edge of cold. The rivers are low and brown after summer. Rain is on its way most days.

## Marks in plain sight
Smoke from more than one fire. A worn track along the top of an old bank.

## What is so of the world for a people
Mortal magic is simple, and it costs the worker. The Keeper fits a people's magic to the world on the night after Day 3.

The peoples here are at about this level: before the medieval era, no steel forges. That is where the world begins, not a ceiling. A god may bring in what it knows, but it is built here by the people, from what the world has, at the pace of the work, and the god says how it is to be made; nothing is granted by naming it.
