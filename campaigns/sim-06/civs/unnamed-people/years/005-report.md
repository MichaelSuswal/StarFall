# Report to the Keeper: Year 3 told again from the fourteenth day of the seventh month, and Year 4 to the fourteenth day of the tenth month, civ unnamed-people, pass one

Stopped at the held moment the release names, the fourteenth day of the tenth month of Year 4, at the opening of the gate. Not stopped earlier. Nothing is written past it in any public file.

Sources: `CLAUDE.md`, the civ role file, the pace rules and the encounter rules (sections 5 and 6), my whole folder (record, state, knowledge, lore, people files, year files and reports), my sealed folder, decisions-005, the conduct profile, `messages/m-0005.md` and the release for this turn.

Reckonings of mine, so the Keeper can overrule them.
- The calendar. Months of thirty days; the market falls on the day of a month that leaves two when divided by three, the second, fifth, eighth and so on, every month, so the second of every month is a market. The fourteenth of the seventh month of Year 3 and the fourteenth of the tenth month of Year 4 both fall on it.
- The nights the star dims, within the release's months. Year 3: the sixth of the eighth month (given), with the earlier five as the year file of the last turn has them. Year 4 as far as the stretch runs: the fifteenth of the second month, the twenty-second of the third, the ninth of the fourth and the nineteenth of the eighth, one night each. No count for Year 4.
- The boat people's days within the release's months. Year 3, the eighth month: the thirteenth to the seventeenth. Year 4, the first month: the ninth to the thirteenth; the eighth month: the twelfth to the sixteenth. Nobody followed.
- The ring's sittings in the stretch, on days with no market: the sixteenth of the ninth month of Year 3; the second of the first month of Year 4 at the ring's day; the fifteenth of the sixth month; the fifteenth of the ninth month. The ring's counts that the Stallreeve brought to the post are in the year file; the rest of what was said in those sittings is sealed.
- The ship owner of the open water's coasters, in the tenth month of Year 4. The release is silent on whether they went out. I wrote them lying loaded at his landing, waiting on a fair wind, on the twelfth to the fourteenth. If the Keeper has them gone, strike that line in the year file and the report.
- The old weigher of the hill kin did not come the third time. The head carrier brought his word. This is mine under the release.

## The stretch, in order

**Year 3, the eighth month, from the evening of the fourth.**
- The fourth, evening: the yard had the Assay-master's word from the hall door. Fifty-five whole. What each trade said of it, in the year file and lore.
- The fifth, a market: the Tally-keeper set the two sticks on her table for any who could read a notch and read them aloud to any who could not, figure by figure, and nothing more. About forty asked in the month.
- The sixth, night: the star dimmed. The Weightward at the landing by the lamp, watching it, running the cups, counting.
- The eighth, a market: a boy at the gate asked whether the star is its own. Silence.
- The ninth: the master of the smiths stood at her table, arms folded, had the fifteens read, nodded once and asked nothing. Orrin sat the afternoon there with a tile in his hand.
- The eleventh, a market: the heavy digger asked whether the stick said his clay was good, and was answered by item 10. Orrin asked for the figures of the two sixes and the two twelves, green and after, and was given them from the Tally-keeper's cuts. He worked the loss out aloud to the line, not to it, a thirteenth off the whole and not the same thirteenth twice. It read the next scale. The Tally-keeper cut nothing of it.
- The thirteenth to the seventeenth: the boat people gone. Sowing five weeks.

**Year 3, the ninth month.**
- The sixteenth: the ring sat, the door shut; the Weightward not sent for. The seventeenth: the Stallreeve brought the counts to the post. The hall's paying for the spares as the hall's cost, seven for, three against, one not voting (the master of the potters, whose spares they were). A motion to cut the second stick for the carrier from the ring's own six things: five for, five against, one not voting. A tie does not carry. One reason given: a stick cut on a tied vote is a word the ring is not agreed on. The Weightward was not asked for anything.
- The seventeenth: the head of the hill goods asked it as himself to cut the six for the carrier; answered by item 13.
- The twenty-ninth: the Stallreeve told the carrier, in its hearing, what it would have him told. The carrier said it was on his stick. The pair stands as he said it on the fifth of the first month.
- No three of the eleven called a sitting on the seat.

**Year 3, the tenth to the twelfth month.**
- The third, a dawn, no market: it read the loads of both coasters at the open-water landing; boat sticks cut for those who wanted them; the carrier on the plank with his half of the first stick. The coasters went out past the Horn on the tide of the fourth.
- No southern coaster came up in Year 3.
- The eleventh month, the eighth, a market: a trader from the Aldermark asked whether one of the old weights is light; answered by item 15.
- The twelfth month: the grain in. The potters' hall fired sets of pieces for the stalls. The nineteenth, evening: the coasters back. The twentieth, a market: the carrier laid his half stick on the Tally-keeper's table, it fitted, and he gave his account before the yard in the Weightward's hearing, in the words in the year file. The Weightward said nothing.

**Year 4, the first month.**
- The second, a market and the ring's day: the Assay-master opened the chest before the head of salt and the clay head, read the ring's word, asked it to call the eleven in its pans, and it did, each to a hair, and the Tally-keeper cut them. The ring set the eleven as what the market weighs by, the ten on the hall's bench for bargains made before the day only, and the clause and the table of the twentieth as they stood. An old man of the flats asked whether it goes now the weights are set; standing answer. The line was twice its length all day.
- Two bargains made before the day came to the gate and were ruled by the ten, as made, by the Stallreeve. The Weightward read the scales and said nothing of the bargains.
- The ninth to the thirteenth: the boat people gone.

**Year 4, the second to the fourth month.**
- The second month: the carriers came down and the old weigher did not. The carriers' scale read level. The night of the fifteenth the star dimmed; the seventeenth a boy asked; silence.
- The third month: rain; the kilns cracked loads as before; Orrin keeps a stick of his own of his tiles' green and fired weights. The star dimmed on the twenty-second.
- The fourth month: wet. The star dimmed on the ninth. The twentieth, a market: two households of netmakers asked it to stand witness to a weir lease; it stood.

**Year 4, the fifth to the seventh month.**
- The fifth month: a hard spell, fever in the back houses, five braziers. The eighth, a market: the Stallreeve told the tide-reckoner's word; a netmaker asked it whether it can tell the height of the water; answered by item 16. The Alder crested on the twelfth at the height she gave, a few hours before the hour; the feeling went out of her hands for the season. The twenty-third, a market: the woman of the back houses came to the gate; answered by its decided words.
- The sixth month: the ring sat the fifteenth, the door shut; the Stallreeve at the post on the seventeenth said it had set no word for the Weightward.
- The seventh month: the thaw; an ordinary flood foretold; the weirs mended.

**Year 4, the eighth to the tenth month.**
- The eighth month: the boat people gone from the twelfth to the sixteenth; the star dimmed on the nineteenth; the twentieth a girl asked in the boy's words; silence.
- The ninth month: the second year of the seat came round; the Tally-keeper cut the day; the line talked of a year to the term; the Stallreeve said the term is three years and a day. The ring sat the fifteenth with the door shut; the Stallreeve said on the seventeenth it had spoken of the term, set no day to speak of it again but the first sitting after the grain, and asked nothing of the Weightward.
- The tenth month, the twelfth, evening: the southern coaster came up the channel and tied up, the same hull with the same master. Her crew traded on the twelfth and thirteenth. She stayed by her boat, asked the landing who sits in the Weightward's seat now and which is the town's weight, and told it nothing; the landing told her. The thirteenth, a dawn: Bren told the Weightward at the fish landing. It did not go to her.
- The fourteenth, a market, before light: she came up from the landing in the dark and stood at the head of the line. She had said nothing. **I stop there.**

## How the god was met

- **The yard.** Took fifty-five whole as a thing nobody could account for, each trade for its own cause. Learned the fire's thirteenth from Orrin in a market and has it by heart. Took the ring's day as it takes a day it has argued over for two years: loud in the channel and the clay, quiet in the salt. Took its "from the stick" as the way it answers: it gives what it was asked and no more. Kept its question about the star at the gate: four asked by a boy, one by a girl, each met with silence and a hush from the line.
- **The Stallreeve.** Brought the ring's counts and one reason to the post and the Weightward's words to the carrier. Held the door and the yard on the ring's day. Ruled the old bargains by the ten. Kept two of the watch on the gate when the coaster came in.
- **The Assay-master.** Said the pieces sit level; told the ring of the hall's paying; asked it to call the eleven so the market has a figure for each; kept the ten on the bench.
- **The Tally-keeper.** Read her sticks to forty and cut the Weightward's calls and the carrier's account. Fitted the carrier's half to hers before the yard.
- **The master of the potters.** Would not call the load good. Carried the eleven up in a basket on her back and set them on the cloth. Fired sets for the stalls.
- **The ring.** Tied on the second stick; took the spares as the hall's cost; set the day; did not send for the Weightward in the stretch and asked it nothing; called no sitting on the seat.
- **The ship owner of the open water.** Carried the word as cut and gave account of it, the pair standing as he said. Kept his coasters at his landing in the tenth month of Year 4.
- **Orrin.** Asked for two pairs of figures and said what he made of them to the line.
- **An old man of the flats.** Asked whether it goes when the weights are set, and said "past that I don't know" is what a man says who has not decided.
- **The back houses.** One woman came to the gate who had not come since.
- **The hill kin.** The old weigher did not come down. The carriers' scale read level.

## What was done in the god's name

By a helper: nothing; none serves.

By mortals, where it said so or plainly took it for granted:
- The Stallreeve told the carrier what it would have him told, in the words it gave on the second of the eighth month, on the twenty-ninth of the ninth month, in its hearing.
- The Tally-keeper kept both sticks open to anyone who could read a notch and read them aloud, since it had said "after the first you can have them from the Tally-keeper, as called."
- The Assay-master told the ring the hall paid for the spares, as he had said at the potters' hall that he would.

By mortals, not in its name: the ring's tie and its reason; the Stallreeve's rulings on the old bargains, his braziers and his word on the term; the Tally-keeper's refusal to cut what was not called; the master of the potters' sets for the stalls; Orrin's remark on the fire and his stick; the landing's answer to the master of the coaster. No seal, licence, promise or ruling was given in its name, and it read no bargain and no person.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper

None that I have written as contested. Two things for the Keeper's eye that are not outcomes.
- **Orrin's stick of tile weights, from the third month of Year 4.** Orrin keeps the green and fired weights of his tiles, on a scale read at the gate. Whether the loss on a tile tells which loads will crack I have not written. If the Keeper wants it tried: the attempt is Orrin's; success means the fire's share on a tile says which loads will crack, failure means it does not and the kiln masters go on blaming the loading, the moon and the downs clay. Circumstances in my record: a stubborn practised kiln master (+10), a true scale read at the gate (+5), two years of tiles that told him nothing (-5), the clay of the cracked and the sound lumps equal in sand and shell at the pits (a fact, no factor). The rest of my stretch both ways: if it succeeds the east kilns do well from the winter and he says nothing of why; if it fails he fires them for the habit, as he does, and the quarrel keeps.
- **The tide-reckoner's call in the fifth month of Year 4.** I wrote it inside her priced knack, right in height and a few hours before the hour, and the feeling gone from her hands for the season. If the Keeper reads that differently, strike it.

## What touches anyone else

- **The south coast.** The carrier said the town's word at Corrith and brought back no answer, stick or token; he met none of the master's house, whose port is another town along that coast, and did not go to the council's people. No southern coaster came up in Year 3. A southern coaster came up on the twelfth of the tenth month of Year 4, the same hull and master as two summers before. Her words and business are the Keeper's, and the thread is held.
- **Ruddfold.** The old weigher did not come down the third time. The head carrier said so at the gate on the eighth of the second month. The hill kin have the ring's decision.
- **Upriver, the Aldermark.** Traders came down in the eleventh month of Year 3, had scales read and one asked whether the fifth old weight is light. No one came from the king. Upriver boats of Year 4's summer were not in before the fourteenth day of the tenth month.
- **The boat people.** Gone from the channels at the lowest tides, the eighth month of Year 3 and the first and eighth of Year 4. Nobody followed.
- **The tide-reckoner.** Called the winter water of Year 4, as above.
- **The ship owner of the open water's landing.** His coasters lay there loaded when the southern coaster came up. See the reckoning above.

## Asked of the god

"Answered" means by its decided words or its conduct profile. "Silence" means no decided words answer it, it met silence, and it is pending. "Held" means held.

1. Year 3, month 8, day 8, a boy at the gate: whether the star is its own. Silence. Pending. The line told him to hush.
2. Year 3, month 8, day 11, the heavy digger of the lower pit: whether the stick says his clay is good. Answered by item 10: "I do not say which clay is better, and I do not say whether the kiln did well."
3. Year 3, month 8, day 11, Orrin of the east kilns, saying his name: the figures of the two sixes and the two twelves, green and after. Answered by item 14: it gave them from the Tally-keeper's cuts. 4. Year 3, month 9, day 17, the head of the hill goods, as himself: whether it would cut the six things for the carrier now. Answered by item 13: "Cut nothing more of mine. What the ring took is the town's word, and the first stick carries the part the ring let me send. The second is the ring's to cut or leave."
5. Year 3, month 10, day 3, the captains at the open-water landing: to be read on their dawn. Answered by the standing terms of the dawn readings.
6. Year 3, month 11, day 8, a trader from the Aldermark: whether one of the hall's old weights is light. Answered by item 15, in its words.
7. Year 4, month 1, day 2, the Assay-master, at the ring's day: that it call the eleven in its pans. Answered by conduct: it weighs what is put in its pans and says what it is. It did.
8. Year 4, month 1, day 2, an old man of the salt flats: "whether, now the weights were set, it goes." Answered by its standing words: "Till I've seen the town's weights set right. Past that I don't know."
9. Year 4, month 1 on, the stallholders: their fired pieces read against the eleven. Answered by the standing terms of the gate: it reads what is set before it.
10. Year 4, month 2, day 17, a boy at the gate: whether the star is its own. Silence. Pending.
11. Year 4, month 4, day 20, two households of netmakers: to stand witness to the lease of a weir, asking nothing of the other thing. Answered by conduct: it stood and said afterward what it saw and heard, and no more.
12. Year 4, month 5, day 8, a netmaker, the one who asked on the second of the seventh month of Year 3: whether it can tell the height of the water as the tide-reckoner does. Answered by item 16, in its words.
13. Year 4, month 5, day 23, the woman of the back houses who asked across the ring in the sixth month of Year 2 whether it could cure a fever. She came to the gate and said who she was and that she had not come since, and asked nothing. Answered by its decided words, given when she came: "No. I have no power for it. I am sorry about your child."
14. Year 4, month 8, day 20, a girl of perhaps six, in the boy's words: whether the star is its own. Silence. Pending.

16. Year 4, month 10, day 14, before the gate opened: the master of the southern coaster at the head of the line. **Held.** The moment: a market day before light, the line forming in the yard outside the gate in the dark, the watch at the bar with their spears grounded and the light coming, the Weightward at the foot of the post with the nest of cups at its hand. She came up from the landing and stood at the head of it. She is the master of the hull that lay three days at the town in the tenth month of Year 2, a woman of about forty. She has said nothing in the Weightward's hearing; at the landing she asked who sits in the Weightward's seat now and which is the town's weight, and told it nothing. I give her no words and the Weightward none.

Not asked of it in the stretch: the ring asked it nothing in session and it was not sent for; the master of the smiths asked nothing at the gate or in session; no bargain was put to it to be read; no seal or licence was asked; the ring made no offer of the seat for a further term; no one from outside the town sought it out but the master of the coaster, held above.

## What the people tell their god

- **The Stallreeve** would tell it that the ring tied on the second stick and gave one reason, that he told the carrier its words and the carrier said they were on his stick, that the ring's day came and the old six are the town's weight and the eleven what the market weighs by, that he ruled the two old bargains by the ten, that the term is three years and a day and nobody has said otherwise, and that the southern coaster is in and asked the landing who sits in its seat.
- **The Assay-master** would tell it that the eleven sit level with the old six on his beam, that he asked it to call them so the market has a figure for each and that the ten are on the bench for the old bargains.
- **The Tally-keeper** would tell it that she read both sticks to forty and cut nothing that was not called, that the carrier's half fitted hers, and that her count holds.
- **The master of the potters** would tell it that fifty-five came out whole, that she will not call the load good, and that the other eleven sit on her shelves.
- **The ship owner of the open water** would tell it that he carries what is on his stick, gave account of it, and that the word went as cut.
- **Orrin** would tell it that the fire takes a thirteenth and not the same one twice.
- **The woman of the back houses** would tell it that she had wanted to hear it said at the post.
- **The head carrier** would tell it that the old weigher kept his stones at Ruddfold, having seen agreeing done.
- **The town** would tell it that it is proud of fifty-five whole and ashamed of how long the weight took, that the yard has "a thirteenth" and "both figures" by heart, that the boat people went at the lowest tides of the eighth month of Year 3 and of the first and eighth of Year 4, that the star dimmed on the sixth of the eighth month of Year 3 and on nights of the second, third, fourth and eighth months of Year 4, that a boy and a girl asked it about the star and were told to hush, and that the master of the southern coaster is at the head of the line.
