# What the people of the town know of their land

Campaign sim-06. Written by the Keeper on creation Night 3a, 2026-11-24, and kept by the Keeper each year. This is what the town's people know and say, by the names they use, with grid cells. It is not the god's map: the god learns a name only when a mortal says it. What they believe may be wrong.

## Their own ground

The town has no name; they call it the town. It stands on the old clay bank in the delta they call the Reedmouths (B3), where the track on the bank top, the Bank Way, meets the main channel of the river they call the Alder. Two more rivers come into the same delta, the Cand and the Rudd. They know every channel and reed island within some days' poling, and they know the channels move.

South of the town the ground goes to the Flats (B3), where the salt-rakers have their pans. From the town to the near edge of the Flats is 1.6 miles on foot over the marsh. Beyond the Flats is the Gulf (A4, B4), sheltered water. From the town to the river's mouth on the Gulf is 45.5 miles by boat downstream, 2 days.

## Toward the hills

East of the delta are the Pellow Downs (B3), low clay downs with diggers' pits and a few steadings. People call them "the old yards". An old road runs that way from the town, sunken and grassed over in places: the Old Rudd Way. Three days out along it, 58.3 miles on foot, are the Pellow Vaults, old sealed places in the downs that nobody opens and nobody builds beside.

Beyond the downs are the Rudd Hills (C3), where the ore and the charcoal come from. The hill people's village on the road is Ruddfold: 123.9 miles from the town along the Old Rudd Way, 7 days on foot, 13 days with a laden cart. The town's own hill folk have kin there. Iron beyond what the hills give comes, they say, from stone towns far to the east, a place they call Hask; no one from the town has been.

## Upriver

The Alder Road follows the river north from the head of the delta into the Aldermark (B2), an old kingdom of the river plain. Its nearer town is Wenlow: 198.5 miles from the town on foot, 12 days, the first stretch over marsh and the rest by the road. Its king's city is Aldermoot: 309.5 miles on foot, 17 days; by boat it is 30 days up the river and 12 days back down. The old people say a reeve of that king once sat on the bank and took a share of the market. No one has come for it in three generations, and the town says the market is older than any lord.

## The forest and the fen

West and north-west of the delta is the great forest, the Sutherwold (A3). Somewhere past it, on a slow river, live a fen people who bury their dead in mounds and buy salt. The town knows them only through boatmen. Over open ground it would be 192.8 miles on foot through marsh and forest, 26 days; nobody goes that way.

## The water

On the channels live the water folk: boat people, not like other people, who own no land and trade reed work at dusk for hooks and salt. The town deals with them and does not trust them.

Across the Gulf is a hilly horn of land, the Dunmar Horn (B4), with few people. Past its end is open sea. Ships from harbor towns on the south coast (C4) come into the Gulf a few times a summer; the town calls their chief port Corrith, as the sailors do. One of the town's ship-owners sends two coasters out past the Horn in summer.

## What they say of old things

That the town came to the bank in the time of the great-grandparents' grandparents, pushed together by the floods. That the oldest weights in the hall are older than the town. That there is a drowned stair on the Gulf shore east of the delta, the Maun Stair, and that a larger god drowned there, which is why the tide keeps coming.

## The offices, by the town's own names

The chief assayer is the Assay-master. The one who runs the market, its stalls, fees and watch, is the Stallreeve. The keeper of the kept halves of the split sticks is the Tally-keeper. The keeper of the old weights was the Weightward; the last one died last spring and the place is empty.
