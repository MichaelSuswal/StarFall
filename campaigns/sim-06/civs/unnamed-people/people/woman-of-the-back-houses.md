# The woman of the back houses

Unnamed: her name has not been said aloud in the Weightward's hearing. Filed by the civ agent, Year 5 night, only so far as the Weightward saw.

A woman of the short-stilted houses at the back of the bank, of the town's own people, no longer young. In the sixth month of Year 2, at the winter sitting, with the yard let into the hall, she stood up from the middle of it and asked the Weightward across the ring whether it could cure a fever. It gave her no answer then. Her child died in the last week of that month, in the cold, in the ordinary way. She did not come to the gate in the year that followed. The back houses said what they say, that a god of scales is a god of scales.

On the twenty-third day of the fifth month of Year 4, a market day in the hard spell, she came to the gate with a stallholder's cord in her hand to have it read, and said who she was and that she had not come since. She did not ask again. It said: "No. I have no power for it. I am sorry about your child." She said she had known it, and that she had wanted to hear it said at the post and not in the ring. She put a pinch of dried fish in its pan and went back along the bank.

Temper: plain, tired, not hard. Owes nothing and asks nothing.
