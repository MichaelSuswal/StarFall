# The master of the potters

Unnamed: the name has not been said aloud in the stranger's hearing. One of the eleven. Fixed by the civ agent in Year 2.

A woman of about 45, broad, of the clay-bank people, precise. She fired the nest of eleven new standards in one kiln so they would shrink alike, and kept them unmarked in the potters' hall until the weighing.

History: asked by the stranger at the end of Year 1 to bring the nest to the hall and not the gate, she carried it up herself in a reed basket on her back. At the weighing the nest was found not to agree with itself, about a thirtieth to a twenty-fifth either side of true and sitting about a twentieth heavy against the old weights. She asked that it be left in the hall under the Assay-master. She did not vote on the first or second motions, voted for the third, and in the ninth month voted for the seat. She fired a second nest in spring, twenty-two pieces in one kiln and eleven chosen by the gate's scale; whether it agrees with itself is the Keeper's.

Temper: proud of her work and not hurt by being shown it was wrong, only slowed. Says nothing in the ring until she has something to say.

This year: the nest, the second nest, the vote for the seat.

Year 2 told again and Year 3: fired the second nest in the seventh month of Year 2; it came out agreeing with itself to a hair (the Keeper's). At the winter sitting said that if it was a god it was a gentle one and brought the pieces to the line like anyone. Voted for the seat. At the first sitting after the grain stood for the chest as one of a pair, and agreed to fire a third nest to the old six in the spring, twenty-two pieces to choose eleven. Whether it holds is the Keeper's; see the report.

Year 3 again, from the fifth day of the first month, to the fourteenth day of the seventh month: brought the potters' hall's own scale to the gate on the fourteenth of the third month and it read level. Shaped the pieces of the third nest over the winter. Told the Stallreeve at the post on the fifth of the seventh month, in the Weightward's hearing, that they are shaped and drying on their boards.

Year 3, the fourteenth day of the seventh month, played live (Year 5 play day): at the door of her hall she let the Weightward weigh the twenty-two pieces of the third nest green, each carried to the bench by her own hands, and let the Tally-keeper scratch a mark on each foot. She agreed to shape three spares of each size, to be weighed green on the twenty-fifth and fired with the rest in one kiln, opened on the first of the eighth month, and to let a spare of each size be broken if asked. She asked that the green figures stay on the stick until the kiln is opened.
