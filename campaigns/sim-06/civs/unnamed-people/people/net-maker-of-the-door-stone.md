# The old net-maker of the door-stone

Unnamed: her name has not been said aloud in the Weightward's hearing. Fixed by the civ agent in Year 4, only so far as the Weightward saw.

A woman of about seventy, a net-maker of the landing, of the channel folk, small and straight, who keeps a house on the bank by the fish landing with a daughter's family under the same stilts. She came down the bank on the eighteenth day of the fourth month of Year 3, a dawn, on a hand sledge pulled by her two grandsons and a neighbor, with her door-stone, having heard the Weightward's asking from her captain son-in-law. It was an undressed river stone, rounded and grey, with a hollow worn in the top of its door side. It read three thousand and eleven in the Weightward's ones, sixty-one short of the figure it had named. She asked whether it was the seventh and was told it says only what a stone is. She asked whether it wanted her step for the hall and was told it was not asking anyone to give anything up. She said then it was her step, and took it home.

Temper: dry, unhurried, owes nothing. She said the stone had been at her husband's mother's door before it was at hers, and that it holds the door against the Alder when the water comes over the step. She refused four offers for it within the day and kept the half of the stick that the Tally-keeper offered her with the door.
