# The head of salt

Unnamed: the name has not been said aloud in the stranger's hearing. One of the four trade heads of the council. Fixed by the civ agent in Year 2.

A man of about 55, weathered and quiet, of the salt-rakers. Slow to speak and long when he does. He holds that a store of worth is made by agreeing what the weight is, not by finding out, and that a stick cut once cannot be recut.

History: voted against the offer in Year 1 and spoke longest. Spoke longest again against the terms before the harvest and lost, eight to three. At the weighing he moved that the hall's pieces stand as the town's weight and lost. He voted for the clay head's motion to wait, for its clause that every debt and bargain made before the day stand as made. The salt-rakers greeted him for a month with "read at the gate."

Temper: stubborn, not stupid. Will say a thing he thinks is true in a room that does not want it. Does not forgive a joke and does not repay one.

This year: spoke against the terms; moved the second motion at the weighing; spoke against the seat in the ninth month.

Year 2 told again and Year 3: pressed the Stallreeve in the first month on a matter no one would name. Was against the yard let in, against the seat, and moved that the stranger stand down from the gate. Took the raking household's part over the salt and said a short measure and a cheat are different things. At the winter sitting asked it outright whether it can tell when a man is lying, and said no bargain in the town was safe from it, and that he and his people would not ask. In the ninth month said a thing that can read a bargain should not keep the standards of what it reads. Voted against the old six in Year 3 and won his clause that bargains made before the change stand as made, and a year's notice. Said he would not need three to call it down if a day came. Stubborn, not stupid.

Year 3 again, from the fifth day of the first month, to the fourteenth day of the seventh month: kept his word that his people would not ask it to read a bargain. At the post on the seventeenth of the sixth month said in the open that he had voted against the fifth and the four fired pieces and the chipped and cracked, that a weight is what it is, the fifth is the fifth, and a weight is not light against itself.
