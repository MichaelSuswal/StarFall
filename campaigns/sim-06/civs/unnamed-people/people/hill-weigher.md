# The old weigher of the hill kin

Unnamed: his name has not been said aloud in the stranger's hearing. Fixed by the civ agent in Year 2.

A small bent man of the hill kin of Ruddfold, grey, once tall. He came down the Old Rudd Way alone, ahead of the carriers, at the head of the hill goods' asking, with a leather bag of his own stone weights on his back, and lodged in the head's house. He said nothing in the yard for the two mornings he watched the gate.

At the weighing he stood beside the head of the hill goods and set his stones against the old six on the hall's beam on the terms the eleven had given: his readings cut, nothing called in ones. His stones agreed with the old bronze at the first and second to the beam's tremble, ran a hair heavy at the third, agreed at the fourth and sixth, and went down a hair against the old fifth. He said the hill kin had sold by the hall's pieces and supposed them heavy for as long as the hall had had them, and had said nothing because a market is a market. He asked the stranger to weigh his stones in the ring and got no answer. He took the figures on a stick of his own, and went up the Old Rudd Way at first light the day after.

Temper: slow, exact, without display. Distrusts brass he cannot lift and said so by looking at the empty seat while the ring looked at the stranger.

This year: the weighing; the walk home.

Year 2 told again and Year 3: had the stranger's word by the carriers in the second month of Year 2 and sent back by the head of the hill goods' walker in the spring that he would come. Came down a second time in the middle of Year 3's autumn, a year older, in a carrier's cart, with his bag of six stones. Stood at the gate on the third market morning and had them weighed in its own cups before the head of the hill goods, the Assay-master, the master of the smiths and the Stallreeve: 48, 96, 193, 384, 768, 1,536, with the Assay-master's own beam brought for the three large stones. Said the hill kin's stones and the hall's agreed as far as anyone knew and that he had come to see it done. Asked what his people say the seventh was, he said it was a story, the six weigh what is and the seventh weighs what is owed, and that he would not stand behind it. Asked nothing of it. Went home with the carriers.
