# The Stallreeve

Unnamed: his name has not been said aloud in the stranger's hearing. The town's own office, the one the player calls the Reeve of the Gate (see reeve-of-the-gate.md). Fixed by the civ agent at the close of Year 1.

A man of 44, of the channel people, wiry and dark from the sun, a former boatman with a boatman's habit of reading weather in faces. Practical and well liked. He runs the market's stalls, fees, the watch and the runners, and has done so for nine years. He settles most quarrels at the stall with a sentence. He is quick to see who is lying and slow to say so. He keeps his own counsel on whether the stranger worries him.

History: he took the office after the last Reeve died of a fall from a barge. He took nothing from the yard at the gate and asked nothing of it, which he thinks is the only safe way to deal with a stranger. He knows the market is older than any lord and that the old people say a reeve of the Aldermark's king once sat on the bank and took a share. He does not want it said that the town handed its measure to a stranger, and he does not want a king's reeve to come asking why the town needed one.

Temper: careful, humorous, a little vain of his good name. Would rather a thing be done in the open and a little late than quickly in a back room. Likes the stranger's way of reading in the order of the line and says so when no one is listening.

This year: put two of the watch at the gatepost, brought every dispute of weight to the gate, moved four stalls under the hall's roof in the worst of winter, took on four more men, carried the council's invitation to the gatepost himself in the eleventh month, and had the Tally-keeper cut it on a stick first.

Year 2: carried the yes and the stranger's terms up to the hall, and was told his rule on the sticks was a good one. Gave leave and later answered for the watch and the Tally-keeper for the stranger's day at the pits. Held the yard at the hall door on the day of the weighing and told it in the open, and a little late, what the ring had done and what the four pieces had shown. Ruled the market stand on the hall's present pieces until the eleven said otherwise. Went down alone to the landing after the carriers' market in the second month and asked the boatmen about the old Weightward's last weeks (the Keeper's). Went up the loft with the Assay-master and the clay head. Had the two pans scraped from the stallholders' beams. Carried the eleven's offer of the seat to the gatepost in the ninth month (held, see report).
