# Orrin

A kiln master of the east kilns. His name was said aloud by himself in the stranger's hearing in Year 1, month 3, with his fee, and again at the pits in Year 2.

In Year 1 he was the first to carry a fixed basket of each digger's clay to the gate to have it read, and by the end of the month five of the seven kiln masters did the same. He asked the stranger to come to the diggers' pits and say which clay was good, and got no answer.

In Year 2 he brought three lumps from loads that had fired well to the pits, and heard the pan say that the lumps from loads that cracked carried no more sand and shell than his own. He said nothing for a while, then that if it was not the grit it was something he could not see in a pan. From the third month he fired a small tile of each load in a corner of his kiln before filling it, and the east kilns followed. Whether the tiles predict the cracking is the Keeper's.

Temper: stubborn and observant; not a talker. Changes his method when shown, and does not say so.

Year 2 told again and Year 3: said his name again at the pits. Came to the second pits day in the eleventh month of Year 2 with lumps of both kinds and a tile fired from each. The pan said as before. Said the tiles had told him nothing either, and fires them for the habit. The quarrel of the diggers and the kilns keeps.

Year 3, from the fourteenth day of the seventh month, to Year 4, the fourteenth day of the tenth month: came to the Tally-keeper's table on the ninth of the eighth month of Year 3 with a tile in his hand and had her read him the third nest's sticks. On the eleventh, at the post, said his name again and asked the Weightward for the figures of the two sixes and the two twelves, green and after, and said to the line, not to it, that the fire takes a thirteenth off a piece and not the same thirteenth twice. In the third month of Year 4 keeps a stick of his own, notched by his apprentice, of the green and fired weights of his tiles on a scale read at the gate. What it shows him he has not said.
