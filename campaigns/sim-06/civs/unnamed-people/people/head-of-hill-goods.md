# The head of the hill goods

Unnamed: the name has not been said aloud in the stranger's hearing. One of the four trade heads of the council. Fixed by the civ agent at the close of Year 1.

A man of 52, tall and pale, of the town's own hill folk, with kin at Ruddfold. He deals in ore, charcoal, timber and the carriers' loads. Clever with figures, close with words, a long-standing quiet man at council who counts the room before he speaks. He votes the way his kin in the hills would, and his kin dislike being measured by anyone who is not their own.

History: his trade is the one most exposed to the hall's old standards, since ore and charcoal are sold by weight and the hall's pieces were wrong in ten. He suspects, rightly or wrongly, that the old bench has favored the buyers for years and that the new scale will say so out loud. He has not decided whether he wants that said.

Temper: a patient opponent. He does not shout. He does not trust brass he cannot lift. He means to bring a witness from Ruddfold and his own weights to the day the council set.

This year: said nothing at the first council, voted against the Assay-master's offer in the ninth month, sent a walker up the Old Rudd Way to Ruddfold in the eighth month without telling the council.

Year 2: did not argue the terms before the harvest; asked a place for his witness at the beam and became the witness's surety. Brought the old weigher of the hill kin down at his asking and sat beside him at the weighing. Did the sum of the four hall pieces against the first old weight aloud. Moved the old six as the town's weight, voted for it, against the head of salt's motion and against the clay head's motion to wait; said waiting is what the hall has done for forty years. In the ninth month spoke against the seat as before.

Year 2 told again and Year 3: was for the yard let in at the sixth month, to the ring's surprise, because the hill kin dislike things set in back rooms. Said the hill kin had been measured by their betters at last and sent word up the old road with the first walker after the thaw. Spoke against the seat in the ninth month and said the hall waited forty years for a weight and got a keeper. Stood for the chest as one of a pair at the first sitting after the grain. Wanted the six weighed again for the hill kin's sake. Moved the old six as the town's weight and carried it, six to two. Brought the old weigher down a second time and stood beside him at the gate. Counts the room and waits. The hill kin still say the hall owes them a twentieth.

Year 3 again, from the fifth day of the first month, to the fourteenth day of the seventh month: stood beside the old weigher at the gate on the eighth of the second month.
