# The Tally-keeper

Unnamed: her name has not been said aloud in the stranger's hearing. The town's own office, the one the player calls the Clerk of the Seals (see clerk-of-the-seals.md): the keeper of the kept halves of the split sticks. Fixed by the civ agent at the close of Year 1.

A woman of 36, of the clay-bank farming people, broad and quiet, with a farmer's hands. Careful and dry. She learned the office from her mother's brother, who kept the sticks before her, and took it three years ago. She reads notches faster than most people read faces and trusts them more. The halves she keeps sit in a reed-lined chest under the hall and she has not lost one.

History: she was one of the three who watched the stranger on the first day and said nothing. She went home and began cutting a stick of the gate's readings that night, unasked: the date, whose scale, true or out, and by how much in the stranger's cups. She keeps one half in the hall and one half in her own house. She told no one for some weeks. The Assay-master found the halves and said nothing.

Temper: patient, watchful, a little afraid of mistakes. She does not volunteer. Asked a plain question she answers it plainly. She believes a word is only worth what its stick is worth.

This year: kept the stick of the gate's readings; cut the council's invitation on a split stick for the Stallreeve in the eleventh month; asked the stranger to name a mark so she could cut it, and met silence.

Year 2: cut the mark two pans, level, at the head of her stick of the gate's readings, and has cut it at every level reading since. Cut the bonded man's price as remitted when his master's scale read level in the eleventh month of Year 1. Cut the first five reading sticks, with her notch and her season's end; showed the stranger the first stale stick in the third month and it looked and said nothing. At the weighing cut every figure called and the Assay-master's beside it, both his readings of the fifth, the hill weigher's, and the nest, and read last year's figures from her stick when the Assay-master asked her plainly. Her apprentice called each figure to the yard once she had cut it. Asked nothing and volunteered nothing else.

Year 2 told again and Year 3: cut the stranger's word for the old weigher on a stick before the carriers came. Answered the stranger and the ship owner at the gate on boat sticks: yes, on her own additions (cut at the landing's table at the end of the dawn, the landing's mark on the back, none hung, a half to the captain and to the buyer if he wants one). Took the ship owner's offered measure of salt a month as the hall's and cut it on her own stick. Cut the salt household's making good. Cut the day the key was taken from the peg and the day each weighing was done. Cut a table of the twentieth for the change of the weight and hung it in the hall. Her count held.

Year 3 again, from the fifth day of the first month, to the fourteenth day of the seventh month: cut the word on a pair of sticks on the fifth and split both. Cut a stick for every stone weighed at the landings, at the landing's table, with the landing's mark on the back. Cut no notch on the empty day, the fourteenth of the second month. Cut the Stallreeve's report at the post on the seventeenth of the sixth month, thing by thing, and read it back. Walked with the Weightward and the Assay-master to the potters' hall on the fourteenth of the seventh month.
