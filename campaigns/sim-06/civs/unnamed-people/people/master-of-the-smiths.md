# The master of the smiths

Unnamed: the name has not been said aloud in the stranger's hearing. One of the eleven. Fixed by the civ agent in Year 2.

A man of about 48, heavy-shouldered, of the town's own people, blunt. He says a stranger's brass is a stranger's brass and has said it twice in front of the ring.

History: voted against the offer in Year 1. Asked at the sitting before the harvest that a smith's hand sit on the beam beside the Assay-master's, was refused, and did not come to the next sitting. At the weighing he asked the stranger across the ring where the seventh was, what it weighed and whether it could find it, and met silence. He voted for the head of salt's motion that the hall's pieces stand. In the ninth month he spoke against the seat.

Temper: asks what others will not and does not wait politely for the answer. Does not trust what he cannot lift.

This year: the question of the seventh; the sitting he missed; the vote against the seat.

Year 2 told again and Year 3: voted against the yard let in, seconded the head of salt's motion to stand the stranger down, asked it across the ring whether it can be killed and what it would take to break it. Said afterward that the ring's right to put it down is an edge he will hold to, and spoke against the seat in the ninth month for the reason that he would not seat what he could not break. Voted against the old six. Was allowed to look at the Assay-master's own beam at the gate when the hill weigher's three large stones were weighed, tried the pivot and the pans, and said it was a good beam. Has still not had his hand on the hall's.
