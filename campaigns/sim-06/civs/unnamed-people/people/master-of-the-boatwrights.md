# The master of the boatwrights

Unnamed: the name has not been said aloud in the stranger's hearing. One of the eleven. Fixed by the civ agent at the close of Year 3.

A man of about 46, broad, of the channel people, who asked at the weighing which weight he was to tell his men to use at the market the next morning. Voted against the first motion, against the second, for the third; for the seat in the ninth month, because the readings at the landing shortened his men's quarrels.

History: two households of his guild asked the stranger to stand witness to the sale of a boat in the last week of the sixth month of Year 2 and said they asked nothing of the other thing; they asked again in Year 3. At the winter sitting asked whether the landing readings stood. Did not vote on the old six, since the change of weight touches every plank he sells.

Temper: practical, worried by weights, loyal to his men.

Year 3 again, from the fifth day of the first month, to the fourteenth day of the seventh month: brought his yard's door-stone to the open-water owner's landing on the fourth of the third month, 2,760, because his men asked whether the seventh lay at their gate. On the twenty-fourth of the fourth month he and a second household asked it to stand witness to the lease of a boatyard, and said they asked nothing of the other thing.
