# The unnamed people: state at the close of Year 1

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent at the end of Year 1 (early autumn of Year 2 opens next). Opening figures are the starting state; each line below says why it moved.

population: about 5,040, in the unnamed market town and its raised banks, channels, flats and clay farms
  Why: about 190 born, about 150 dead, about 10 hill folk moved into the town to stay. The winter fever in the short-stilted houses at the back of the bank took the old and the very young in the ordinary way. No famine, no flood loss, no raid.

stores: grain and dried fish through the coming winter if the autumn catch is ordinary; the grain harvest was good and the bins are fuller than a year ago; salt in plenty; reed and clay without stint; charcoal and ore thin again, as every late summer, until the hill carriers come down
  Why: ordinary year, a long warm summer, a flood of ordinary height, and the deep-winter bins ran lower than the Stallreeve liked but never low. A few forges went cold in the last week of the sixth month when the charcoal ran out.

treasure: about as it was. The hall's fired weights (the ten, two of them chipped or cracked), the chest of bronze and scrap silver, and salt kept as a store of worth. More in private hands than anyone admits
  Why: the hall spent a little scrap silver on the potters' nest of eleven new clay standards, and took in about as much more from a busier market's fees. The stranger takes no metal and no silver, so none of it left the town that way.

trade: salt, pots, reed goods and dried fish out; ore, charcoal, timber and bronze in; a market every few days, less quarrelsome than last spring; upriver boats came down in summer, southern goods came up from the Gulf in the town's own boats
  Why: a place now exists where a dispute of weight can be settled in an hour in front of the yard, and the Stallreeve takes every dispute there. The town's boatmen and the ship-owner of the channels did well from the southern goods that came up in their boats. The hill carriers came down as always and went home pleased with the gate.

strength: a watch of sixty-four with spears, up from sixty; up to three hundred more at the call, few trained; no wall
  Why: the Stallreeve took on four more men in the sixth month, after the winter's crowded market, on his own word and the hall's grain. No drill beyond what the watch already does.

mood: practical, suspicious and short-tempered at market, but easier than a year ago; fed, housed and not afraid. Split about the stranger
  Why: most of the channel and salt folk and the potters like the gate and say so; the hill goods head, the master of the smiths and the head of salt do not like a stranger's brass setting a measure and say so. The council voted seven to three to ask the stranger to weigh the old weights before the eleven, after the harvest. Nobody wants it to go wrong in front of the town. The fever winter left a few houses short of an elder, and the town noted who sat with whom.
