# The unnamed people: state at the close of Year 2

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent at the end of Year 2 (early autumn of Year 3 opens next). Each line below says why it moved from the close of Year 1.

population: about 5,090, in the unnamed market town and its raised banks, channels, flats and clay farms
  Why: about 185 born, about 140 dead, about 5 hill folk moved into the town to stay. The wet winter's fever took the old and the very young in the back houses as it does, but fewer than last year, because the Stallreeve kept four stalls and two braziers under the hall's roof from the first frost and the old came there to sit. No famine, no flood loss, no raid.

stores: grain and dried fish through the coming winter if the autumn catch is ordinary; the grain came in well enough, a little under last year's good harvest; salt in plenty; reed and clay without stint; charcoal and ore thin again, as every late summer, until the hill carriers come down
  Why: ordinary year, a warm summer, a flood of ordinary height, a wet winter with its hard spells. The deep-winter bins were lower than the Stallreeve liked, not low. A few forges went cold for some days in the sixth month, as last year.

treasure: about as it was. The hall's fired weights (the ten, two chipped or cracked), the old six now known to be six of seven and kept in the chest, the potters' first nest of eleven in the hall and the second at the potters' hall, the chest of bronze and scrap silver, and salt kept as a store of worth. More in private hands than anyone admits
  Why: the hall spent nothing on the weighing; the fees of a busier market came to a little more than they were. The stranger takes no metal and no silver, so none of it left that way. The seventh weight is not found and the town counts it in the hall's store as lost.

trade: salt, pots, reed goods and dried fish out; ore, charcoal, timber and bronze in; a market every few days, louder than last year and no worse; the hill carriers came down in the middle of autumn and went home with the figures of the hall's weights; upriver boats came down in summer; a coaster of the southern harbor towns came to the town's own landing in the first month of summer and traded some days
  Why: the old quarrel about the bench came out at the weighing and the Stallreeve's ruling kept the market on the hall's present pieces, so nobody lost more than they thought they had. The clay trade is poorer for the cracking, which the pits day did not explain: the kiln masters know now it is not the grit, and have no other cause. The ship owner of the channels did well again from the southern goods.

strength: a watch of sixty-four with spears; up to three hundred more at the call, few trained; no wall
  Why: no change. The Stallreeve took no new men and the watch kept the line for a day at the pits.

mood: practical, suspicious, short-tempered at market, easier than a year ago about the gate and worse about the hall. The empty seat and the heavy pieces have been said aloud, which the town did not forgive and did not regret. Split about the stranger, and waiting
  Why: the channel folk, the potters and the clay head like the gate and say so; the head of the hill goods, the master of the smiths, the head of salt and now the master of the weavers like a stranger's brass less than they like what it showed. The eleven decided to wait. The town knows the eleven asked it to take the seat, that the answer has not come, and has not decided whether the stranger's silence is manners or an answer.

## Keeper, Year 1, pass two (kept as written by the Keeper)
- Month 6: the bonded man did not get the cup. He was taken at the landing and held by the watch. The Stallreeve asked the stranger at the gatepost whether it asked a price of him and got no answer. The council set a price in measure for a theft tried on a guest of the yard; the Tally-keeper holds the stick and the price lies unclaimed. The numbers above stand.
- The potters' sorting of clay by weight: by summer the kilns that sorted had cracked as many loads as before. Some potters now say the gate's scale is no use for clay; the digger with the heavy loads still sells low.
- The walker to the hill kin arrived and came back. Any witness comes down with the carriers next autumn.
- Month 11: the council's invitation was spoken at the gatepost and the half-stick laid at its foot. The stranger has not answered. That matter is open at the year's turn, and the day the council set is the first market of the new autumn.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The kiln-masters' test tiles did not tell which loads would crack. The quarrel of diggers and kilns keeps, and the tiles are called Orrin's folly or Orrin's patience by who one asks.
- The potters' second nest came out agreeing with itself to a hair. It is kept at the potters' hall.
- Held and open at the year's turn, in order: the Assay-master's question on the evening of the weighing; the channel ship owner's bargain at the landing; the eleven's offer of the Weightward's seat; the southern coaster's master at the head of the line. The numbers above stand.
