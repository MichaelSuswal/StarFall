The pans are read and the sticks are cut, and the nights go by at the landing as they have gone, with you awake by the channel folk's lamp and your thumbnail on the cups, smallest first, before you count. Each figure is what it was when you called it. The count runs on through sowing and grain, through rain and hard cold and a flood no higher than it should be, into a warm, dry summer with the wind standing wrong for the open water, and it stops in the grey before a market, at the foot of the post, with the bar still across the gate. Somewhere past the marsh the year turns. Here the light is only coming.
