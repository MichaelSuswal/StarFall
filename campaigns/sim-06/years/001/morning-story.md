(oog) Before you land

The Day 3 brief was written before the map was drawn, and the finished land differs from it in what is in view from the spot where you land. The track along the bank and the smoke are as the brief gave them: the town stands on the same bank as you, about a mile off. The low hills behind are not near ground. They show only as a low rise on the skyline, about 23 miles off to the east. The pale flats and the open water are not in view from the bank. They lie beyond the reeds.

Year 1. Early autumn, the hour before first light. The turning has stopped. You are standing where you landed, on the top of an old raised bank, with firm clay under your feet and the wet on either side of it. The air has the first edge of cold in it. Mist lies on the water and thins over the bank. You smell reed, wet clay and woodsmoke. Water is moving in a channel close by, low and slow after the summer, and rain is not far off. The coat is on you with what its pockets hold, and the scales hang at your belt where they hang, with their dent. It is dark still. A round blue light stands in the north-east, about a fifth of the way up the sky, wider than the moon and steady, and it gives light enough to walk by. You have no name for it. By it you can see the bank under your feet and the pale line of a path running along the bank's top. You feel no other like yourself. That knowing comes only at close quarters, and nothing here gives it.

As the light comes, the rest shows. This is low wet country, channels and reed islands and old clay banks, and the reeds stand tall and tawny on both sides of you. The bank lifts you about 12 feet above the wet. From its top the level ground shows out to about 3 miles, and a thing standing on the ground shows within about 2.

The path passes under your feet. About 4 miles of it are in view, coming from the north and going on to the south-east. About a quarter of a mile to the east a river channel runs from the north to the south, with about 4 miles of it in view.

About a mile to the south-east, on this same bank, there is a town: roofs, and smoke from more than one fire. By the town there is a road, about a mile from you, with about half a mile of it in view.

On the skyline to the east there is a low rise of hills, its nearest edge about 23 miles off. Of what lies beyond the reeds, nothing shows.

No person is in view. No one has spoken to you, and you have no name for the path, the channel, the town, the road, the hills or anything else in sight. The people of that town do not know you are coming, and nothing in it is yours.

(oog) A few plain things for the first morning.

You gave no name to your people or to their town on Day 3, so there is none for me to hold back. Both stay unnamed until a mortal names them in your hearing.

The map is attached, drawn at the landing frame. The sheet is about 358 miles wide and about 358 from top to bottom. It is that wide on purpose, to show the map's scale: at this scale you and everything your eyes take in from the bank sit on one mark, the small ring at the middle. The clear ground is the whole of the wet country you stand in, because the map clears a country at a time once you are standing in it. What you see from the bank is only what the story above gives. The hill country shows through cloud, as a skyline seen and not ground walked. The path, the channel, the town and the road carry no names, only your own words for them, because no one has told you any. No name given at creation is on the sheet. You can ask for the map at any time.

(oog) Settled for you

These are the Keeper's answers to what Day 3 left to the world.

The house you described stands in the town, on the highest firm bank where the track meets the main channel, with a gate and no wall. It is the town's own house.

The town already has a flood drum on that bank.

The salt pans are on the flats beyond the town.

The town has flat-bottomed boats and a few coasters, and both of the ship-owners you described sit on its council.

The offices you designed stand under the town's own names for them, which you will hear from the town.

The tide-reckoner is as priced at the table, with nothing changed. Her own cost is the whole price, and the mark on your card that left her to the Keeper's review is lifted.

Holding a licence costs a factor nothing. Each day on which a factor sets one or more seals costs that factor the night's sleep that follows. Lifting a seal costs nothing, and a factor feels no breaking. This is written on your card as a dated entry, and no term of the card changes.

The town holds debt-bonded folk, as you supposed. Its present terms are its own, and you will hear them from the town.

Your sealed request to the Keeper from Day 3 was received and ruled. The line from your private answer that I set beside it for the Keeper to weigh was received and ruled.
