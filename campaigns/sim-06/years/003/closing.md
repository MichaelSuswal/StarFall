The market empties and the hall's door is shut on what it keeps. You go down to the landing as you do every night and sit awake by the channel folk's lamp, with the brass at your belt, and count over what the year's pans gave you, salt and clay, barley, bronze and stone, each figure what it was when you called it. The boats lie dark on the water, waiting for the dawn and your reading, and the blue light stands where it stands. The year turns.
