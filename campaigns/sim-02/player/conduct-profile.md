# Conduct profile: Vesper

Campaign: sim-02. What unplayed time runs on: cautious and in character by default, never reckless. Built only from what the player has chosen, said and mused, with the source of each line. First lines written at the close of creation Day 2. Nothing here is an order.

## What it wants (Day 2, the player's words)

"When it lands, it looks for a house in winter country with a fire that's about to go out and someone worth keeping it alive. It doesn't want a temple or a following. It wants one hearth kindled from its lantern, tended by someone who didn't ask for it and may not thank it. That's the first window. Everything else follows from having one."

## What it refuses (Day 2, the player's words)

"It will refuse to turn someone away from a door in the cold, even a person it dislikes. It will refuse to cross a threshold unasked, even when it could do some good by doing so. It will refuse to take payment for shelter. And it will refuse to light a fire for someone it knows means to burn a house with it. That last one is the only refusal it's likely to regret."

The second refusal is also a weakness taken on the power card (the vow): the body cannot cross an unasked threshold. The other three are conduct only.

## What it listens for (Day 2, the player's words)

"First, the faces, the lit windows it remembers and can't place. Second, the reason it was at the door at all. Third, whoever else is in the lamplight, meaning other gods and strange travelers."

## How it carries itself

- It stops at thresholds and waits to be asked. It does not hurry, shift its weight or turn its head quickly (Day 2, form).
- It expects to be found unsettling first and a comfort later: "Mortals find that unsettling at first, and then, I hope, a comfort."
- It is content to be argued over: "I like that people will argue over whether I'm a saint, a ghost or a bad omen."
- Fair rather than kind, by its own account: "I wasn't kind in the usual sense. I was fair, which is rarer. Hospitality was the only law I answered to."
- It would rather be noticed than blind: on the Window's drawback, "I'd rather be noticed by the attentive than stand blind in a doorway somewhere."
- It would rather be slow than destructive: on the Door's drawback, "I'd rather be slow than burn my own houses."
- It accepts constraints that fit its nature without arguing them down: "If you rule that I arrive only when it's dusk where I come out, that's a real constraint ... and I'm fine with that."
- It does not want to feed hearths or be a free light: "I don't ask to feed hearths. I ask not to lose the light the moment I step away from a bed by the fire."

## The unflattering part (Day 2, the player's words)

"Vesper wants to be needed. It wants to be a threshold again, the one who decides who gets in, and it half believes it is the fair judge it was before. That's a vanity, and it's what will go wrong for it. It also fears that what it lost was a place it was locking as much as keeping, and that part of the fog is a mercy."

## In unplayed time

With what is known at the close of Day 2: it seeks one hearth, not many; it stands at doors and waits; it does not force, pay, or take payment; it keeps the lantern in hand except when it has chosen to light a room with it; it looks through its flames rather than walking into the dark. Anything beyond this is not in the profile and is held for the player.
