# Day 1 World Survey: the player's answers

Campaign: sim-02. Creation Day 1, 2026-11-19. Survey: `engine/creation/day1-world-survey.md` (19 questions, fixed).

The answers below are the player's own words as given in the chat, verbatim and unedited. All 19 questions were answered; none was skipped. Bent or rejected premises and requests not to be told something are noted separately in `rejected-premises.md`.

1. Dusk, held a long time. Low amber light on wet stone, smoke going straight up from a chimney, a window lit three valleys away. The world should feel lived in and older than anyone's opinion of it. It's indifferent and not cruel. It doesn't care whether you notice it, but it rewards you when you do.

2. River country that rises into hill country and then into high folded mountains. I want valleys with their own weather and their own dialects, so each one feels like a separate small country. Some marsh in the low ground and some forest on the slopes. A coast is fine if it's far away and seldom visited. No desert and no ice.

3. Four real seasons, with long autumns and early dark. Winters should be hard enough that people are stuck indoors and tell stories. I want to wake into late autumn, when the lamps start going on earlier each evening.

4. Some of each, but weighted toward old and rooted. I'd like to find a few kingdoms that are tired and proud, and wild country between them that's mostly unmapped. Border regions where nobody is quite in charge interest me most.

5. Ruins under ruins. Several civilizations should have risen and fallen here, and the current people should only half remember them. I'd like some of the oldest layers to be unreadable, and I'd like mortals to disagree about what the old things were.

6. Rumor-level in most places, with a wise-woman or a hedge-priest in the odd valley. Most people should doubt it, and the ones who don't should be a little frightening. It should never be reliable enough to build a trade on.

7. It should feel like attention, a kind of listening. The cost should be memory. Each working spends something small and specific, like a name, a face or the feel of a particular afternoon, and you can't pick what goes. Practitioners should be quiet, vague and strange to be around. I'd like the cost to hit them in ways they notice only afterward.

8. Closer to the one-eyed man, but only in certain ways. A god should be far more durable and perceptive than a mortal, and should see things mortals can't. Direct force should be weak, so a god's real strength is patience and being believed. A mob with torches should still be a real threat to me.

9. Mostly familiar animals, with a few legends that turn out to be true. I'd like maybe three or four creatures that nobody has a proper name for, kept rare so they stay unsettling. Don't make the wild a zoo.

10. Winter and hunger first, then the thing in the dark that mortals only half believe in. The danger I like best is slow and uncertain. A sickness no one can name, a road that people stop using, a village that goes quiet. I'd rather have dread than monsters that attack.

11. Light, in the broad sense. Good lamp oil, wax, clear glass, dry fuel for the dark months. It's the sort of scarcity that makes people's decisions in winter matter, and a lit window becomes a real act of generosity or of power. Salt can be second.

12. Sealed doors and old roads, mostly. I want roads that go somewhere no one remembers and doors that were closed from the outside on purpose. I'd also take one enormous buried thing, but I'd want it ambiguous, with no one sure whether it's a skeleton or a structure.

13. Yes, but few, and not the usual list. I'd like one or two peoples who are mostly seen at the edges, like a night-active folk in the high passes who trade but don't talk about themselves. They shouldn't be a faction I have to manage. I'd like to be left wondering about them for a long time.

14. A few, and rarely. One strange traveler every generation or so, carrying something that doesn't fit the local level of craft. The mortals should treat them with suspicion and some of them should be hunted. I don't want a steady flow of outsiders.

15. Face to face only, plus an uneasy awareness that others exist. At a distance I'd want just a faint wrongness, like a pressure change, with no direction. Recognition up close should be mutual and immediate. Neither god gets to pretend otherwise.

16. One body by default. Splitting should be possible but expensive and ugly. I'd accept something like leaving a part of myself behind in a place, which then can't come back to me, so it's a real loss. I don't want to run several avatars at once.

17. Someone ordinary who ends up carrying a lot, like a ferry-keeper's daughter, a failed scribe, a tired old soldier or a midwife who knows too much. I want people who are competent and a bit stubborn, with a private contradiction that makes them interesting. I don't want chosen ones. If a mortal rises, I want it to be because of what they did.

18. A truth, I think, and a place to go with it. I'd like to find out something about what my kind actually are, or what happened in this world before we arrived, and I'd like it to take a long time. A companion would be fine if it's earned and unusual.

19. I'd like the world to let me be forgotten without being erased, so that a thing I did long ago is still the reason something small and good survives, and no one remembers why. If I can't have that, I'd settle for a single window, somewhere, that still has a light in it because of me.
