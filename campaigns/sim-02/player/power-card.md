# Power card: Vesper

Campaign: sim-02. Written at the close of creation Day 2, 2026-11-19, from the card read back to the player and agreed ("Yes. The card is right, and the night can fall."). Description and Drawback text below is frozen. Rulings made at the table are marked "settled at creation"; the Keeper gives each its ledger id on Night 2.

## Form

Vesper, among gods; the name is what it was called before and the part of its past it still holds. Among mortals who do not know the name: the Doorkeeper, the Lamp-in-the-Hood, Dusk Coat; children, something that sounds like "the late visitor."

A tall, narrow figure a little over seven feet, thin enough to look like a doorframe someone dressed. A long coat the color of the last light on a wall, a dull amber that goes brown and then grey as you look. The hood is always up. Under it, where a face would be, a small steady flame the size of a fist, a lamp-flame with no lamp. It does not flicker in wind. Mortals can look at it without harm; it casts light on them and not on the god. The hands are ordinary and a little too still, with long fingers. The body does not shift its weight, turn its head quickly, or hurry. At a door it stops at the threshold and waits to be asked.

Bodies: one. A god in this world has one body and is in one place (`world-rules.md`).

Senses, as the player described them: out of a flame it sees, hears and smells as a tall mortal would, through the fire under the hood, and feels heat, cold and touch as a mortal does. The hood-flame lights a small circle of a few paces; past that, in true dark, it sees what a mortal sees by moonlight, which may be nothing. Its voice comes from the hood, low and level, and needs no flame. Any sense not named here is unsettled and is asked about in play, never inferred.

Pace: mortal pace, by the pace table.

God-sense, as set for this campaign (`world-rules.md`): a god knows another god only face to face, in person, within sight and speaking distance, each seen by the other; the knowing is mutual, immediate and sure, and tells only that the other is a god. At a distance there is nothing to feel. Every god knows from landing only that others like it came down when it did.

The god's own fire. The hood-flame is what Vesper is. At dusk it lights the lantern by tipping it toward the hood-flame; that is the one flame it makes, and every flame it ever kindles comes from the lantern. The fire burns without fuel only in the lantern, and only while the lantern is in the god's hand, within its hood-light, or set down in a room (see Hearth-light). Passed to a wick, or set farther off, it is ordinary fire from then on: the god's by the chain (Definitions), but fueled with oil and wood like anyone's, and out when the fuel is. The god can give a hearth its flame; it cannot give it fuel. Settled at creation. Ledger: L-0001.

When the body is broken (absence). Settled at creation. Ledger: L-0002.
- The hood-flame goes out, the coat falls empty, and the lantern stays where it lies, lit or not as it happens. The god is gone from that place. It is not dead; it is absent.
- It returns at the next local dusk through a flame of its own, if one burns anywhere, arriving as it is, without the lantern. The hearth it returns through is spent by the return: cold, and out of the chain for good.
- Until the lantern is back in its hand it cannot kindle and cannot step through the Door. The Window still works.
- If no flame of its own burns anywhere, it stays gone until someone lights the lantern from any fire. That lighting makes the lantern's fire the god's again, and the god returns through the lantern itself at the next local dusk, wherever the lantern is, in front of whoever holds it. That return spends nothing. (Agreed reading, Day 2: the god comes back in front of whoever lit it, and whoever is standing there then holds it.)
- Whoever holds the lantern holds the thing every flame of the god's comes from. The player accepted this as the exposure and asked that it not be softened.

### Definitions

- A flame of mine: fire lit from the god's lantern, or from a flame that was the god's, with the chain unbroken. A flame that is drowned, smothered or burned out is cold and the chain is broken for good, however it is relit (from flint, from a stranger's fire, or otherwise). The lantern's own fire is the god's. A flame of the god's carried in an ordinary lamp and set in a window stays the god's.
- Local dusk: the dusk hour at the place of the flame in question, not where the god stands.
- A threshold: the way into a place someone keeps: a house, a hall, a tent, a walled town at its gate. Where no one keeps it (a ruin, an open shed, a cave no one lives in) there is no one to ask and it is not a threshold for the vow.
- Asked over: a spoken or plain invitation from someone who lives in the place or keeps its door; or a hearth of the god's burning inside, kept by someone, which the god may step out of as an invitation already given. An invitation can be withdrawn; once it is, the god leaves by the nearest door and the threshold is shut to it until someone asks again.

### Things carried

- The coat.
- A plain iron lantern. It swings slowly as though someone were walking, even when the god stands still. As iron it is only a lantern; the fire in it is the god's own (Form).

## Powers

### The Window
Status: main
Description: Through any flame that is the god's, at any distance, the god can look: it sees what the flame's light reaches and hears what is said within that light. It sees nothing in the dark around the flame, nothing anyone thinks, and nothing that happened there before it looked.
Drawback: While the god looks, the flame it looks through burns wrong: too steady, and a shade too pale.
Limits settled at creation:
  - Player's first wording, kept as intent: "I want every lit lamp, candle or hearth in the world to be a window I can look through, anywhere, whenever I like." The DM priced it down to flames of the god's own (Definitions); the player accepted: "The chain rule is fair and I like it. Lose the light, lose the window."
  - The sign is present every time, only while the god is looking, and only to someone whose eyes are on that fire at that moment. What it means is known to no one in the world on the day the god lands and must be learned by noticing. The DM promised no one would be prevented from learning it; countermeasures come from the world. Settled at creation, at the player's request that the sign be "subtle, not invisible". Ledger: L-0003.
  - A hand over the flame shuts the window while the hand is there. Settled at creation. Ledger: L-0004.
  - Light and dark: works by day and by night, wherever a flame of the god's is lit.
  - Distance: any.
  - Frequency: one flame at a time, as often as the god likes.
  - The window shows what a fire lights and never why. Priced by shape against the mystery standard; the Keeper reviews on Night 2 and may add a limit, logged.
  - Touches no mind.
  - A flame of the god's in a lamp made by more than ordinary craft, or warded: unruled until it happens; goes to the Keeper then. An ordinary lamp is not this.
  - Other gods: a flame of the god's in another god's hand or sight is unruled until it happens.
  - The god's own people: no difference; a hearth they keep is the god's by the chain and shows the same sign.

### The Door
Status: main
Description: The god can step into a flame that is its own and come out of another flame that is its own, arriving as it is, with the coat and the lantern and whatever is in its hands, and nothing more.
Drawback: The god can only step out at local dusk at the flame it comes out of, the one hour each day, and must wait for it; one step a day.
Limits settled at creation:
  - Player's first wording, kept as intent: "a door I can step out of, so that I can arrive at any fire without crossing the distance." Priced down to flames of the god's own; accepted.
  - The player chose this drawback over the alternative offered (the exit flame spent by each arrival): "I'd rather be slow than burn my own houses."
  - Local dusk means dusk at the exit flame, not at the one left. The player asked for this reading and accepted it as a constraint ("I'd have to pick my exit by the sun"). Settled at creation. Ledger: L-0005.
  - Distance: any.
  - Carrying: what is in the god's hands. Nothing living comes through; a child in the god's arms stays on the near side. Settled at creation. Ledger: L-0006.
  - The lantern counts as a flame of the god's for entering: the god may step into its own lantern flame from wherever it stands. It cannot come out of the lantern, since the lantern comes with it. Settled at creation. Ledger: L-0007.
  - The god has nowhere to arrive until a first hearth is its own and kept by someone.
  - Without the lantern in hand the god cannot step at all, except the single return from absence (Form). Settled at creation. Ledger: L-0008.
  - Water: a drowned flame is cold; the chain is broken (Definitions).
  - Falling: not raised; unsettled.
  - Being watched: whoever is at the exit hearth sees the god step out of their fire.
  - Other gods: another god at either flame is unruled until it happens.
  - Scarcity: the Door carries no fuel and makes no light; see Form for the fuel limit on the god's fire.

### Hearth-light
Status: main (bought by a trade: the vow, under Weaknesses taken)
Description: The god's flame burns without fuel throughout one room the lantern is set down in, for as long as the lantern stays lit there.
Drawback: While the lantern is set down lighting a room and the god is not holding it, wherever the god stands, in that room or out in the cold, the god's hood-flame burns low, a candle rather than a fist, and past its own few paces the god sees in the dark as a mortal does.
Limits settled at creation:
  - Player's wording of the ask: "that the lantern's fuel-free burning extends to the room the lantern is set down in, not only the small circle of my hood-light. That would let me leave a flame in a house for the night without it going cold, though a wick I hand over still needs oil. I don't ask to feed hearths."
  - One room: one enclosed space under one roof, with walls and a door. Outdoors, or in a hall too large to call a room, the fuel-free burning is the hood-circle only (Form).
  - A wick lit from the lantern still needs oil.
  - The drawback applies wherever the god stands while the lantern is down and lit in a room. Agreed reading, Day 2, in the player's words: "That's the price of giving the room my light." Settled at creation. Ledger: L-0009.
  - While the lantern is set down it is out of the god's hand, so the Door is shut to the god (Door limits) and anyone may take the lantern (Form, absence).

  - (none yet)
Trades: bought by the vow (Weaknesses taken). Recorded both ways.

## Weaknesses taken

### The vow
What it is: the god cannot cross a threshold it has not been asked over. It binds the body, not only the god's word. Player's words: "I can't cross a threshold I haven't been asked over, and it binds the body, not just my word. A door barred to me stays barred, and a house that never calls me in stays shut. Stepping out of a hearth of mine into a home counts as invited, since someone kept the fire."
Its limits and edge cases (settled at creation; Ledger: L-0010):
  - The body does not pass an unasked threshold by any means: it cannot walk across, and it cannot be carried, pushed or dragged across either. No one can shelter the god unasked any more than they can seize it indoors.
  - Threshold and asked over: as in Definitions. A place no one keeps has no threshold for the vow.
  - A withdrawn invitation shuts the threshold again; the god leaves by the nearest door.
  - A hearth of the god's burning inside, kept by someone, is an invitation already given; the god may step out of it.
How it can be used against the god: a house, a hall or a town that learns what the god is can keep it out forever by never asking. A bar on a door is a wall to it. The player accepted this in those words: "A town that learns what I am can keep me out forever, and the bar on a door is a wall to me."
What it bought: Hearth-light.
