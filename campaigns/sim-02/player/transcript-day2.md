## DM:

## Player:

Here's what I'll stand in a doorway as.

**Shape.** A tall, narrow figure, a little over seven feet, thin enough that it looks like a doorframe someone dressed. It wears a long coat the color of the last light on a wall, a dull amber that goes brown and then grey as you look. The hood is always up. Under it is not a face but a small steady flame, a lamp-flame with no lamp, about the size of a fist, sitting where a face would be. It doesn't flicker in wind. Mortals can look at it without being hurt, and it casts light on them but not on itself. Its hands are ordinary and a little too still, with long fingers. It carries a plain iron lantern, unlit. What doesn't move is the body. I don't shift my weight, I don't turn my head quickly, and I don't hurry. When I come to a door, I stop at the threshold and wait to be asked. Mortals find that unsettling at first, and then, I hope, a comfort.

**Name.** Among gods, Vesper, because it was what I was named before, and it's the part I still hold. Mortals who don't know it will call me the Doorkeeper, the Lamp-in-the-Hood, or Dusk Coat. **Before the fall.** I kept a place of passage. I don't remember its shape, only that it was somewhere that things came through to be sorted, taken in or turned away, and that I decided which. **What I lost.** I lost the place itself, and I lost the people I let through, whose faces I remember only as lit windows. Worse, I lost the reason I was ever the one at the door. One more thing, since you said powers come later and I'd rather ask for it now. I want every lit lamp, candle or hearth in the world to be a window I can look through, anywhere, whenever I like, and a door I can step out of, so that I can arrive at any fire without crossing the distance. (oog) The whole of it, every fire in the world a window and a door, I will not give. Not because of the one-body rule; a step through a flame is still one body in one place. Because a god who can look out of any hearth and arrive at any hearth has no distance, and distance is most of what this world has to give you. Here is the version I would give, with what it costs. Push back where you want to.

The ground of both halves is a definition: a flame is yours when it was lit from your lantern, or from a flame that was yours, and the chain has never broken. A hearth that goes cold and is relit from flint, or from a stranger's fire, is not yours any more. You start with none. Every window you ever have is one you kindled, or one a mortal carried from your fire and kept alive. Lose the light, lose the window. That is the price built into the shape, and I think it is the one you were asking for on Day 1.

The window. Through any flame that is yours, at any distance, you can look: you see what its light reaches and hear what is said within that light. Nothing in the dark around it, nothing anyone thinks, nothing that happened there before you looked. Drawback, one of two, your choice: the flame you look through burns wrong while you are in it, too steady and a shade too pale, so anyone who learns the sign can know when the Doorkeeper is at their hearth and can put a hand over the lamp to shut you out; or, while you look, your own flame goes dark under the hood and your body stands blind and deaf wherever you left it, for as long as you are looking. I would take the first, but it is yours to take.

The door. You can step into a flame that is yours and come out of another that is yours, arriving as you are, with the coat and the lantern and whatever you can carry in your hands and nothing more. Drawback, again one of two: the flame you step out of is spent, it goes cold the moment you are through and can never be made yours again, so every arrival costs you one window forever and leaves that house dark that night; or you can only step out at dusk, the one hour each day, and must wait for it. The first costs you more, the second slows you more.

Two edge cases I will settle now unless you argue: a flame of yours that another god is looking at, holding, or standing beside is the Keeper's to rule when it happens, and the card will say so; and the window shows you what a flame lights and never why. If it ever reaches toward the great old things in this world, the Keeper reviews it tonight and may write a limit on the card. I am pricing by shape; I do not know the secrets either.

Three things I still need from you. First, the lantern. It is unlit as you describe it, and the whole shape above hangs on its flame. What lights it, how often, and does it do anything else beyond being iron and holding fire? If it does more than an ordinary lantern it is a power and I price it; if it is only a lantern, say so and it goes under Form. Second, your senses when you are not looking through a flame: the hooded fire where a face should be, does it see, hear, smell as a tall mortal would, or otherwise? Anything you do not describe I leave unsettled and ask about in play, and I will not guess. Third, whether you take the version above, or counter.
