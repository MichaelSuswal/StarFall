# Day 2 transcript: the god

Campaign: sim-02. Creation Day 2. The DM's posts under `## DM:`, the player's replies under `## Player:`, in order. Follow-ups from Night 1: none (`followups-day2.md`).

## DM:

You are still falling. The thin stuff you fall through has a grain to it now, like the air over a river at the end of the day, and you can tell down from up by the way the dark gathers below.

You said, before the world had finished listening, that you were picturing something tied to the hour when day gives way to night. Lamps, thresholds, who gets let in. The Between has heard that much and nothing more about you, and it wants the rest. Not the land; you have said what you can about the land, and what it does with that is its own affair. You.

So. What are you, when you are seen? Give me the shape that will stand in a doorway at dusk, as plainly as you can: size, what it looks like, what moves and what does not, how it carries itself. What is it called, if it has a name, and what will people who do not know its name call it?

And tell me what you were, before the fall, and what you lost. An outline is enough. Your own past will be foggy to you when you land; what you give me now is the part you still hold.

(oog) Day 2 is a conversation, not a form. Say as much or as little as you like, in any order, and I will follow. Some things about being a god here are fixed and I will tell you plainly when they come up; one of them comes up now, since it bears on form: a god here has one body, and is in one place. Powers, costs and the edge cases come after I know who I am talking to.

## Player:

Here's what I'll stand in a doorway as.

**Shape.** A tall, narrow figure, a little over seven feet, thin enough that it looks like a doorframe someone dressed. It wears a long coat the color of the last light on a wall, a dull amber that goes brown and then grey as you look. The hood is always up. Under it is not a face but a small steady flame, a lamp-flame with no lamp, about the size of a fist, sitting where a face would be. It doesn't flicker in wind. Mortals can look at it without being hurt, and it casts light on them but not on itself. Its hands are ordinary and a little too still, with long fingers. It carries a plain iron lantern, unlit. The lantern is the one thing that moves. It swings slowly even when I stand still, as though someone were walking.

What doesn't move is the body. I don't shift my weight, I don't turn my head quickly, and I don't hurry. When I come to a door, I stop at the threshold and wait to be asked. Mortals find that unsettling at first, and then, I hope, a comfort.

**Name.** Among gods, Vesper, because it was what I was named before, and it's the part I still hold. Mortals who don't know it will call me the Doorkeeper, the Lamp-in-the-Hood, or Dusk Coat. Children will call me something that sounds like "the late visitor." I like that people will argue over whether I'm a saint, a ghost or a bad omen.

**Before the fall.** I kept a place of passage. I don't remember its shape, only that it was somewhere that things came through to be sorted, taken in or turned away, and that I decided which. I was good at it, and I wasn't kind in the usual sense. I was fair, which is rarer. Hospitality was the only law I answered to.

**What I lost.** I lost the place itself, and I lost the people I let through, whose faces I remember only as lit windows. Worse, I lost the reason I was ever the one at the door. I feel I was chosen, or I chose, and I can't recall which. I'd like that fog to clear slowly in play, in pieces, because of things I do.

One more thing, since you said powers come later and I'd rather ask for it now. I want every lit lamp, candle or hearth in the world to be a window I can look through, anywhere, whenever I like, and a door I can step out of, so that I can arrive at any fire without crossing the distance. You've said I have one body in one place, so I'm asking for the opposite. I know that's too much. Tell me what you'd charge, and I'll see whether I can pay it.

## DM:

The Between takes the shape as you give it. Seven feet and a little, the coat going from amber to grey as the eye stays on it, the hood, the fist of flame where a face would be, the hands too still, the iron lantern that swings as though someone were walking. Vesper among gods. Doorkeeper, Lamp-in-the-Hood, Dusk Coat, the late visitor. A keeper of a place of passage who was fair rather than kind, who lost the place, the faces, and the reason. That is written as you said it, and the fog over the reason stays where you put it; what clears it, and when, is the world's.

Now the lamps.

(oog) The whole of it, every fire in the world a window and a door, I will not give. Not because of the one-body rule; a step through a flame is still one body in one place. Because a god who can look out of any hearth and arrive at any hearth has no distance, and distance is most of what this world has to give you. Here is the version I would give, with what it costs. Push back where you want to.

The ground of both halves is a definition: a flame is yours when it was lit from your lantern, or from a flame that was yours, and the chain has never broken. A hearth that goes cold and is relit from flint, or from a stranger's fire, is not yours any more. You start with none. Every window you ever have is one you kindled, or one a mortal carried from your fire and kept alive. Lose the light, lose the window. That is the price built into the shape, and I think it is the one you were asking for on Day 1.

The window. Through any flame that is yours, at any distance, you can look: you see what its light reaches and hear what is said within that light. Nothing in the dark around it, nothing anyone thinks, nothing that happened there before you looked. Drawback, one of two, your choice: the flame you look through burns wrong while you are in it, too steady and a shade too pale, so anyone who learns the sign can know when the Doorkeeper is at their hearth and can put a hand over the lamp to shut you out; or, while you look, your own flame goes dark under the hood and your body stands blind and deaf wherever you left it, for as long as you are looking. I would take the first, but it is yours to take.

The door. You can step into a flame that is yours and come out of another that is yours, arriving as you are, with the coat and the lantern and whatever you can carry in your hands and nothing more. Drawback, again one of two: the flame you step out of is spent, it goes cold the moment you are through and can never be made yours again, so every arrival costs you one window forever and leaves that house dark that night; or you can only step out at dusk, the one hour each day, and must wait for it. The first costs you more, the second slows you more.

Two edge cases I will settle now unless you argue: a flame of yours that another god is looking at, holding, or standing beside is the Keeper's to rule when it happens, and the card will say so; and the window shows you what a flame lights and never why. If it ever reaches toward the great old things in this world, the Keeper reviews it tonight and may write a limit on the card. I am pricing by shape; I do not know the secrets either.

Three things I still need from you. First, the lantern. It is unlit as you describe it, and the whole shape above hangs on its flame. What lights it, how often, and does it do anything else beyond being iron and holding fire? If it does more than an ordinary lantern it is a power and I price it; if it is only a lantern, say so and it goes under Form. Second, your senses when you are not looking through a flame: the hooded fire where a face should be, does it see, hear, smell as a tall mortal would, or otherwise? Anything you do not describe I leave unsettled and ask about in play, and I will not guess. Third, whether you take the version above, or counter.

## Player:

I'll take most of it, argue two points, and answer your three questions.

**What I take.** For the window, the first drawback: the flame burns too steady and a shade too pale while I'm in it. I'd rather be noticed by the attentive than stand blind in a doorway somewhere. For the door, the second drawback: I can only step out at dusk, the one hour each day. That fits what I am, and I'd rather be slow than burn my own houses. The chain rule is fair and I like it. Lose the light, lose the window.

**Where I push back.**

1. Dusk should be local dusk at the flame I step out of, not at the one I left. Otherwise I can't use the door across half the world. If you rule that I arrive only when it's dusk where I come out, that's a real constraint, because I'd have to pick my exit by the sun, and I'm fine with that.

2. I'd ask that the pale-flame sign be hard to read. A mortal should have to be watching that particular fire, and knowing the sign should be a thing someone has to learn. As written it's a free counter, and a clever neighbor could spot me anywhere. I'm happy to be countered, but let it cost someone attention and a little luck. I'm asking for the sign to be subtle, not invisible.

**The lantern.** It is only a lantern, so it goes under Form. I light it at dusk by tipping it toward my own hood-flame. That's the one flame I make, and every flame I ever kindle comes from it. It holds fire without fuel and burns until I put it out. It does nothing else, and I don't ask for it to. I take the vow as flavor and not as a power: I don't cross a threshold I haven't been asked over. If that is a power by your rules and needs a price, tell me. I'd rather keep it as a rule I follow than as something I'm given.

Does the lantern flame count as a flame of mine for the window and the door? If it does, I can step out of it, which means I carry my own start with me. If it doesn't, I begin with no window until I light a first hearth, and I'm fine with that too. I just need to know which, because it changes what I do first.

**Senses.** Out of a flame I see, hear and smell as a tall mortal would, through the fire under the hood. The flame lights a small circle around me, a few paces, and past that in true dark I see what any mortal would see by moonlight, which may be nothing. I feel heat, cold, and touch as a mortal does. I don't need the flame to talk. My voice comes from the hood, low and level.

I take the version above, with the two changes asked for.

## DM:

(oog) Both points are yours.

Dusk is local dusk at the flame you step out of. You pick your exit by the sun, and if it is noon where you want to be, you wait. One step a day: the hour comes once, and you go through once. Written on the card, settled at creation.

The sign stays subtle. Every time you look, the flame you look through burns too steady and a shade too pale; that never goes away, or it is not a drawback. But it is there only while you are in it, only to someone whose eyes are on that fire at that moment, and what it means is not known to anyone in the world on the day you land. Someone has to notice, and notice again, and put it together. I will not promise that no one ever does; countermeasures come from the world, and if a people learns to read you, they earned it. A hand over the flame shuts the window while the hand is there. Written as settled.

The lantern. Iron, plain, swings as you said: Form. The fire in it is another matter. You light it from your own hood-flame, it burns without fuel, and every flame you ever kindle comes out of it. That is not ordinary fire; it is a piece of you, and I write it under Form as part of what you are, not as a power, with one limit, because a lamp that burns on nothing would otherwise make you a free light for a whole people. The limit: your flame burns without fuel only in the lantern and only while the lantern is in your hand or within the small circle your hood lights. Set down farther than that, or passed to a wick, it is ordinary fire from then on: yours by the chain, but it needs oil and wood like anyone's, and it goes out when the oil does. You can give a hearth your flame. You cannot give it fuel.

Does the lantern count as a flame of yours? Yes, for going in. You can step into your own lantern flame from wherever you stand, at dusk, and come out of any hearth that is yours. You cannot come out of the lantern, because it comes with you, and as a window it shows you only what you already see. So you carry your start with you, and you still have nowhere to arrive until you have kindled a first hearth and someone has kept it alive. Written as settled.

The vow. "I don't cross a threshold I haven't been asked over" goes in your conduct profile as standing conduct, not on the card. It buys nothing and costs nothing in the rules. If you ever want it to bind you harder than your own word does, it can be taken as a weakness on Day 2 and would buy something; you have not asked for that, so I am only telling you the door exists.

Senses: written as you gave them. See, hear, smell, feel as a tall mortal; a few paces of your own light; moonlight sight past it; a low level voice from the hood that needs no flame. Nothing else is settled about them, and I will ask in play when something comes up rather than guess.

Edge cases I am writing on the card from what we have, one line each, so you can object now: the window works by day as well as night, wherever a flame of yours is lit; the door only at local dusk. Distance: any. Through the door you carry what is in your hands, and nothing living comes with you; a child in your arms stays on the near side. A flame drowned, smothered or burned out is cold and the chain is broken, however it is relit. A flame of yours in a lamp made by more than ordinary craft, or warded, or in another god's hand or sight, is unruled until it happens and goes to the Keeper then. The window shows what the fire lights and never why. Nothing here touches a mind. And one I have not settled: what happens when your body is broken. A god here cannot die, but can be hurt, held, shut out, forgotten. I will not decide what you are like, so tell me what you want it to be when a mob gets its hands on you, and I will price it: for instance, whether the flame goes out and you are gone from that place with the lantern as the only thing left of you, to be found again, or whether you lie where you fell and mend slowly in sight of whoever did it. Or something else.

Two things more and we are close. What does Vesper want? Not the whole plan, only as much as you know: what it will do first when it lands, what it would refuse to do, what it is listening for. That is what runs you on the days you do not play, so give me the truth rather than the attractive version. And, since I owe you the offer once and plainly: a weakness taken on can buy an improvement elsewhere, on either power or on your form. You need not take one. If you want to, name it and what you would want for it.
