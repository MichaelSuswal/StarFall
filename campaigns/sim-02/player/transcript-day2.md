# Day 2 transcript: the god

Campaign: sim-02. Creation Day 2. The DM's posts under `## DM:`, the player's replies under `## Player:`, in order. Follow-ups from Night 1: none (`followups-day2.md`).

## DM:

You are still falling. The thin stuff you fall through has a grain to it now, like the air over a river at the end of the day, and you can tell down from up by the way the dark gathers below.

You said, before the world had finished listening, that you were picturing something tied to the hour when day gives way to night. Lamps, thresholds, who gets let in. The Between has heard that much and nothing more about you, and it wants the rest. Not the land; you have said what you can about the land, and what it does with that is its own affair. You.

So. What are you, when you are seen? Give me the shape that will stand in a doorway at dusk, as plainly as you can: size, what it looks like, what moves and what does not, how it carries itself. What is it called, if it has a name, and what will people who do not know its name call it?

And tell me what you were, before the fall, and what you lost. An outline is enough. Your own past will be foggy to you when you land; what you give me now is the part you still hold.

(oog) Day 2 is a conversation, not a form. Say as much or as little as you like, in any order, and I will follow. Some things about being a god here are fixed and I will tell you plainly when they come up; one of them comes up now, since it bears on form: a god here has one body, and is in one place. Powers, costs and the edge cases come after I know who I am talking to.

## Player:

Here's what I'll stand in a doorway as.

**Shape.** A tall, narrow figure, a little over seven feet, thin enough that it looks like a doorframe someone dressed. It wears a long coat the color of the last light on a wall, a dull amber that goes brown and then grey as you look. The hood is always up. Under it is not a face but a small steady flame, a lamp-flame with no lamp, about the size of a fist, sitting where a face would be. It doesn't flicker in wind. Mortals can look at it without being hurt, and it casts light on them but not on itself. Its hands are ordinary and a little too still, with long fingers. It carries a plain iron lantern, unlit. The lantern is the one thing that moves. It swings slowly even when I stand still, as though someone were walking.

What doesn't move is the body. I don't shift my weight, I don't turn my head quickly, and I don't hurry. When I come to a door, I stop at the threshold and wait to be asked. Mortals find that unsettling at first, and then, I hope, a comfort.

**Name.** Among gods, Vesper, because it was what I was named before, and it's the part I still hold. Mortals who don't know it will call me the Doorkeeper, the Lamp-in-the-Hood, or Dusk Coat. Children will call me something that sounds like "the late visitor." I like that people will argue over whether I'm a saint, a ghost or a bad omen.

**Before the fall.** I kept a place of passage. I don't remember its shape, only that it was somewhere that things came through to be sorted, taken in or turned away, and that I decided which. I was good at it, and I wasn't kind in the usual sense. I was fair, which is rarer. Hospitality was the only law I answered to.

**What I lost.** I lost the place itself, and I lost the people I let through, whose faces I remember only as lit windows. Worse, I lost the reason I was ever the one at the door. I feel I was chosen, or I chose, and I can't recall which. I'd like that fog to clear slowly in play, in pieces, because of things I do.

One more thing, since you said powers come later and I'd rather ask for it now. I want every lit lamp, candle or hearth in the world to be a window I can look through, anywhere, whenever I like, and a door I can step out of, so that I can arrive at any fire without crossing the distance. You've said I have one body in one place, so I'm asking for the opposite. I know that's too much. Tell me what you'd charge, and I'll see whether I can pay it.
