# How Vesper meets its people

Campaign: sim-02. Written by the DM at the close of creation Day 3. Lines in quotation marks are the player's words.

## What was agreed

The Haldren are already there, with their own history. They are waiting for nothing and do not know a god has come down. They did not arrive with the god.

"I arrive alone, unknown and unasked, and go first to a door, though I don't cross it until asked. If no one asks, I stand at the threshold and wait, and that waiting is the first scene."

The door the player hopes for is Anwen Corve's, the house with the ford lamp. Which door the god comes to is the player's to choose in play from what it can see; where that house stands is the world's.

Those who come to keep a flame of the god's are not to be told what it is. "They know they were given a flame and nothing else. ... I'd like them to learn it slowly, or not at all, and I'd like the choice of telling to be mine." The player accepts that the pale, steady flame may give it away first.

## Standing conduct for the first morning

Decided by the player: go first to a door; do not cross until asked (also the vow, on the card); if not asked, stand and wait. Do not tell anyone what a kept flame is.

Not decided: what the god says when first spoken to, and what it says of itself. Held for the player.

## Heard, not promised

- That the oldest Haldren feel "a small chill of recognition they can't account for" when the coat comes to a door. What any mortal feels or does is theirs.
- That Corve "doesn't ask what I am."
- "No chosen heir, and no enemy at the gate on the first night."

## For the Keeper to reconcile

The DM cannot read the deep history. Each item below is written as the player's wish or the people's belief, confirmed by the DM as neither true nor false.

1. How long the Haldren have been in the valley. The player gave no span, only that they have "their own history."
2. Whether they are a remnant of something larger, and what that was. "A people living in the shell of something larger, who aren't sure whether they're its heirs or only squatters."
3. The walls running up the hillsides past present grazing: whose, and how old. The player reads them as a sign "there used to be more of them."
4. The line of fitted stone under the turf: the player calls it "the old fitted road" and wants "a few people who know the road under the turf is older than the walls." Recorded as a belief some Haldren hold.
5. Ostrey: "one stone town at the crossing," "a cluster of stone houses above the stepping stones, with a low wall and one gate." The brief shows smoke from more than one place and a crossing on set stones; it does not show a town, a wall or a gate. The vow's reading at the gate (power card, Day 3 entry) depends on whether there is one.
6. The lit window: the player hopes it is Corve's house near the ford, with the ford lamp. The brief says "a lit window, or what may be one."
7. How long the Corve family has kept the ford lamp, and why a lamp is kept at the ford.
8. The custom of the stool and cup "for the late visitor" on the first hard frost: where it comes from. The player wants it to predate the god and have nothing to do with it. On Day 2 the player also said children would come to call the god something like "the late visitor."
9. The custom of carrying the dead to the high ground: what is on the high ground.
10. The stories of "doors that shouldn't be opened and travelers who weren't what they seemed": stories only, by the player's account.
11. Trade "down the valley and over the passes": what lies down the valley and over the ridges, and who is traded with.
12. The rumor of one or two old women in the outlying steadings who "know things," and the player's wish that the cost be "the loss of small, specific memories." No mortal with a knack was created or priced.
13. The three old houses, the moot, the speaker by lot, the yearly gate-warden: the people's own order, set by the player; listed in case the deep history bears on how old the houses are.
14. The god's edge "the first night" works across a people and names winter as "first hard frost to thaw where the hearth stands." For the Keeper's review and ledger.
