# The Haldren: the people's record

Campaign: sim-02. Written by the DM at the close of creation Day 3, 2026-11-19, from the people read back to the player and agreed. Lines in quotation marks are the player's words, from `player/transcript-day3.md`. Stores, treasure, trade, strength and mood are the Keeper's to set on Night 3a. Anything about the land or its past is in the last section, as wish or belief, and in `player/people-meeting.md` for the Keeper to reconcile.

## Who they are

The Haldren (the player's name for them). Human. Hill and river folk. "They keep sheep and some cattle on the lower slopes, grow barley and oats in the walled fields, and cut peat and wood for winter. They make wool, cheese, cured meat and rough cloth, and trade what they can down the valley and over the passes. They're practical, a little stubborn, hard to impress. They don't pray much."

## How many, and how they live

About eight hundred, "spread along the valley floor in a few steadings and one stone town at the crossing." The town is Ostrey (the player's name), "a cluster of stone houses above the stepping stones, with a low wall and one gate." The game's default of about 5,000 was shrunk by the player's choice. Whether a walled town stands at the crossing is the world's to place.

## Their code

- No one is turned from a door in the cold.
- No hearth is let go out in winter if it can be helped.
- Both customs are the Haldren's own, older than any knowledge of the god and with no connection to it. "I'd like that custom to exist before I arrive, with no connection to me, so that I fit into it instead of founding it."
- The dead are carried up to the high ground and left, and a lamp is kept at the house for three nights. After that it is allowed to go out.

## Skills and culture

Herding, small grain, peat and wood cutting, wool, cheese, cured meat, rough cloth, small trade. Ordinary, and unpriced. They work at the level the world gives: before the medieval era, no steel forges.

## Priced edges and weaknesses

None on the people. The one edge the player asked for (that no Haldren sheltered at a hearth of the god's in winter could die of cold or hunger) is the god's flame acting, not anything the Haldren do. It was priced on the god, in a smaller shape, and is on `player/power-card.md` as a dated Day 3 entry (the first night). It works for any mortal, not only Haldren.

## Their magic

None described by the player. "At most, a rumor that one or two old women in the outlying steadings 'know things' and that it costs them. If the world wants a hedge-witch somewhere in the valley, it can place her, and the cost of what she does should be the loss of small, specific memories, as I said on Day 1. None of the Haldren I've named has it. Corve certainly doesn't." The rumor is recorded as a rumor. No mortal with a knack was priced at the table. The cost named is the player's wish; the world's terms for mortal magic are the Keeper's.

## What they believe

They pray to no one. "If someone claims a god, they'd give it a polite hearing and a cold look."

- "On the first hard frost, many houses set a stool by the hearth and leave a cup of something warm on it overnight, 'for the late visitor.' Nobody can say who the visitor is. It's a custom, kept the way people keep a custom they've stopped explaining." The player wants it to be theirs and nothing to do with the god. Where the custom comes from is the world's.
- "They tell stories about doors that shouldn't be opened and travelers who weren't what they seemed. They treat those as stories, with a shrug."
- They do not know a god has come down.

## How they are ordered, and how they settle disputes

No one person rules. A moot of householders meets at the autumn gathering and whenever a matter cannot wait. Three old houses (unnamed, left for play) carry the most weight; each has an elder whose word counts for more; they disagree. A speaker for the year is chosen by lot from among the three houses; the speaker runs the meeting and carries the moot's decision to the gate-warden, and has no other power. A gate-warden is chosen by the old houses for a year at a time and decides who is let into Ostrey. There is a quiet divide between the town and the outlying steadings.

Disputes: "Two houses in dispute must each host the other at a table before the moot will hear the matter. A feud isn't settled until each has eaten under the other's roof, and refusing to host is itself an admission of fault. It doesn't always work, but it's the custom."

None of these are the god's officers. See `player/hierarchy.md`.

## What the god is to them, in the player's intent

"Neighbors I'm responsible for, and not subjects. I don't want worship, a temple or a priesthood. ... They should be able to get on without me, and they should be worth keeping." On Day 3 they are not followers and do not know the god exists.

## Wishes and beliefs about the land and its past (not facts)

Recorded as the player's wish or the people's belief. The world decides. Listed again for the Keeper in `player/people-meeting.md`.

- That the Haldren are "a remnant, a people living in the shell of something larger, who aren't sure whether they're its heirs or only squatters."
- That the walls climbing the hills past where anyone grazes, and the fitted stone under the turf, mean "there used to be more of them."
- That "a few people ... know the road under the turf is older than the walls." A belief some Haldren hold.
- That they trade "down the valley and over the passes."
- That Corve's family "has kept the ford lamp for longer than the family can account for."
