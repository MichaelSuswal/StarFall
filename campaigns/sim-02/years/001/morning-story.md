Year 1. Late autumn, at dusk, and the air is still. You are down. You stand on cropped turf on the south side of a long valley, about a mile above its river, with the coat on you and the iron lantern in your hand, unlit and swinging slowly as it does. No one saw you arrive. The frost that held in the shade all day is forming again on the turf at your feet, and you feel the cold as a mortal would, dry and sharp. You smell peat smoke and wood smoke, sheep, and wet stone from the river. You hear the river over its stones, below you and about a mile off, and once, from the town, a dog. Nothing else. The fire under your hood lights a few paces of grass and no more, and past that you have the last of the daylight, as anyone would.

The river runs below you to the north-west, from the south-west toward the north-east, with a worn track on its near bank. There is a crossing on set stones, and above the stones a small town of stone houses with a low wall and one gate, about a mile and a half from where you stand. Smoke goes straight up from several of its roofs. Near the crossing, outside the wall and a little above the stones, one window is lit. Farther down the valley, to the north-east, smoke rises from two more places on the valley floor.

The floor of the valley is walled fields in stubble. Stone walls run on up both hillsides, far above anywhere an animal has grazed this year. About a mile north of you, between you and the river, a line of fitted stone lies under the turf, wider than the track and straighter, running along the valley both ways until the ground hides it. Ridges close the valley on both sides. At its head, a long way to the south-west, the first of the mountains stand with snow on them, and more behind. The stars are coming out. One light in the south-east, above the ridge, is not like the others: larger, blue, and steady.

Of others like you there is nothing to feel. You know they came down when you did. None is here. No one has come out of the town, and nothing in the valley has turned toward you.

(oog) A few plain things for the first morning.

Vesper does not yet know a name for anything in front of it. You do: the town is Ostrey and the people under that smoke are the Haldren, as you named them. The valley's name is on your map because the god has now seen the valley; nobody has told it the river's.

The map is attached, drawn in close on the ground you know: the valley as seen, and the ridges, the mountains, the town, the river and the line of fitted stone as your own eyes report them from here, which may be wrong in the details until you have walked them. You can ask for the map at any time.

The frost has already come hard to this valley, so by the terms on your card it is winter here for the first night.

The year is yours from this moment until the world turns at 1:30. Nothing is waiting on you and no one has asked you anything.
