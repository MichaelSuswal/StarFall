## Player:

**Who I'm playing:** a swarm god. I'm not one body. I'm a hive-mind of bees, wasps and the small pollinating things, with no single shape that is "me." Mortals will meet me as weather in the flowers, not as a face. I want to lead by tending: bloom and pollination, honey and wax, who gets stung and who gets fed. I'd like the DM to treat me as a god who has to be read, not a god who speaks.

1. A late-summer afternoon where the whole land hums. Heavy gold light, the air thick with pollen, and under the beauty a sense that everything is busy and nobody is in charge. Peaceful, but not safe.

2. River country with broad flowering meadows, hedgerows, orchards and uplands of heather. I'd like a valley with a lot of edges: wet ground, dry ground, old woods and open flowers. Nothing harsh or barren.

3. Four real seasons, with a long, strong spring and summer and a hard but short winter. The winter matters to me because a hive has to survive it. I want to wake into early spring, when the first blossoms open and the hive is thin and hungry.

4. Some of each. A few old kingdoms already rooted, with wild country between them. I want neighbors who don't know what I am, and blank map I can slowly fill.

5. Old, with ruins under ruins. I'd like layers, so that digging reveals a people who were here before the people who were here before.

6. Low but real. Most people doubt it. A few villages keep a bee-wife or a honey-reader. Never a wise-woman in every valley.

7. It should feel like noticing: reading signs, small patterns, the way a beekeeper knows a hive is about to swarm. The cost is attention and time. Magic costs the caster something personal and slow, like years of sleep, not a flashy price.

8. Barely more than a great sorcerer, with one strange exception. My reach should be large only because my bodies are many. A god is hard to kill because it's spread out, not because it's strong.

9. Strange but grounded. Mostly familiar animals, with a few wrong ones: a moth that tracks grief, a wasp that builds papery cities in dead trees, deer with something extra in their eyes. Legends the locals half believe.

10. Slow things. Blight, a failed harvest, a plague that follows the trade road, a thing in the woods that is patient. I like dread that arrives as a trend, not a monster. Famine and rumor are better stories than dragons.

11. Sweetness, in every sense. Sugar and honey, but also kindness and trust. Mortals trade for salt and iron, but the scarce thing is anything that keeps a winter bearable.

12. Old roads, and sealed doors that nobody can explain. I'd also like an abandoned apiary, huge and ancient, with wax that is older than the kingdoms. Don't tell me what it means.

13. A few, but rare and odd, and not the standard elves and dwarves. I'd like one people who are small, live in tunnels, and have an economy built on trading what they find. They don't need to like humans.

14. Few. One or two travelers whose tools don't fit the world, and who are not sure how they arrived either. I'd rather find them than be handed them.

15. A pull that gets stronger with distance closing, like smelling smoke. I'd also like a knowing face to face, but I want to be able to be fooled. Another god should be able to hide from me, and I from them.

16. Yes, that is the whole point of me. Many bodies, no main one. The cost: each body I send out thins me. I get less certain which of my bodies is thinking, and a big swarm is slower to decide. A hive dying should hurt. I'd like it to be possible to lose a part of myself for good.

17. A quiet competent one: a beekeeper, a midwife, a reeve, a mapmaker. Not a chosen hero. Someone whose story matters because they keep noticing things, and who may never know I exist.

18. A companion, or a truth about what the other gods really are. I'd like to find something that's been here longer than any of us, and have it be neither friend nor enemy.

19. I wish the world would be indifferent to me in a way that I could slowly earn the right to change. I don't want to be told I'm important. I want a place that carries on with or without me, and I'd like my influence to show up years later in small things, like a recipe, a festival, or a word people no longer know the origin of.

**Pushback on the premise:** "lead a people" is the part I'd like to bend. I don't want to rule or gather. I want to be a presence in many lives and let one or two peoples grow up alongside me without knowing I'm steering. If the game needs me to name a people at some point, fine, but I'd like it to be something I tend, not command. If that doesn't fit what the DM has built, tell me early.
