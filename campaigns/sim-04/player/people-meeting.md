# How the Thrum meets its people

Campaign: sim-04. Written by the DM at the close of creation Day 3, 2026-11-21. Lines in quotation marks are the player's words.

## What was agreed

The Sallowfolk are already there, with their own history. They are waiting for nothing and do not know a god is coming. They did not arrive with the god. The god arrives unknown to them and means to stay so until the player chooses otherwise at the table.

"They do not know I am a god."

The one mortal the player designed, Orrin Teal, does not know the god at landing. See `civs/sallowfolk/record.md`, Helpers.

The god has no voice among them. What it can show is "a pattern in the bees that a keeper may read as a warning, a welcome or a sign", which may be misread. A stung person "feels a hum when I'm near and gets nothing else."

## Standing conduct

No first action was stated. Decided by the player, and written to `player/conduct-profile.md`:

- In a year not played: "I keep out of sight. I stay a swarm among swarms, tend what I've tended, use the Working only for the short-hive duty and nothing else, and open the Listening no more than I must. I don't let myself be seen by Orrin or anyone else until I choose it at the table."
- "I would not sting anyone in an unplayed year."
- When a hive of theirs goes into winter short by its doing: "move stores from a surplus hive in my reach to the short one and not work that hive again that year."
- It does not intervene when a quarrel over a borrowed swarm goes to the bees.

## Heard, not promised

- That Orrin is "someone I can watch before I speak to them".
- "I'd like them to learn smoke control from me, if they ever do, but only by trial, not by gift."
- That no keeper finds out why her hive was short: "I'd rather you didn't give the keeper a way to find out." The DM answered that it will not hand her a way and will not shield the god from one; the card says worked bees are visibly not behaving as bees do.
- That the keeper of a short hive "will blame the weather, the bees or herself".
- That a keeper reads a pattern in the bees "as a warning, a welcome or a sign".
- That the bee-wives' craft stays "rumor in the other hamlets".
- That the Sallowfolk "remember this as story only, and ... have no idea what it meant" (the older people and their track).

## For the Keeper to reconcile

The DM cannot read the deep history. Each item is the player's wish or the people's belief; the DM confirmed none as true or false.

1. How long the Sallowfolk have been in the valley. Nothing was said. Left to the world.
2. What they say came before them. Belief, in the player's words: "I believe there was once a larger people here and that the old track along the valley foot was theirs. I'd like the Sallowfolk to remember this as story only, and to have no idea what it meant. Let the world decide whether that's true."
3. What lies past their borders. The player spoke of "other valleys" that want their tapers, "the region", "their neighbors" and "a stranger at the border". All left to the world.
4. The old saying that there is a luck over the valley and that swarms settle on the right houses. Held before the god arrives and not caused by it. Whether it has a cause, and what they believe it means, are the world's.
5. The market ground. Wish: "If the game needs a gathering place, make it a market ground at a river crossing that fills a few days a year." The reeves meet "at the river crossing". Whether and where a crossing stands is the world's.
6. The roofless stone hall. Wish: a "roofless stone hall by the old track" in or by the highest hamlet, "where the upland starts", where wild swarms "settle each spring ... and never take". The brief shows a track along the foot of the slope; the DM did not correct or confirm the player's placing. If no such hall is placed, the DM owes the player a line before landing on what Orrin has noticed instead.
7. The hamlets: "a dozen or so", unnamed, with one "highest hamlet, where the upland starts". Their number and places are the world's; the names are left for play.
8. The count of bee-wives. Wish: "perhaps one hamlet in three." The player asked to be told if the fitting changes it.
9. The name Sallowdene, for the valley. It is the people's name for their home and stands as that; whatever else the valley is called is the world's.
