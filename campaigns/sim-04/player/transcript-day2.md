# Transcript: creation Day 2 (sim-04)

## DM:

The fall resumes. There is no wind in The Between, only the sense of going down, and a voice that belongs to the place and to no one in it.

"You asked to be told early. So, first. You said you would not rule or gather, that you would tend, and be a presence in many lives while a people or two grew up beside you without knowing. That fits. Nothing below requires a throne of you. It is written down as yours, and it will be remembered as yours: the one who came down and did not do it the usual way."

"What is not settled today is the people themselves. Whether you name one, how large, whether anyone carries your intent when you are elsewhere, what tending them looks like from their side. That belongs to tomorrow. Today is for you."

"There is one thing you should hear before you describe yourself, because you built yourself on it. Each of those who fall arrives with a single body. That is how it is for all of you, and it is so from the moment you land. More than that is a power, and priced."

(oog) Plainly, so you can plan around it. The rule is a fact of every god here, and it is not negotiable as a rule. What it leaves open is yours to shape, and I see two honest roads. I am not choosing between them for you.

The first: a swarm that is the body. One cloud of bees, wasps and small pollinating things, no face, no main insect, that is in one place at a time the way any body is. It can be loose or tight, it can pour through a hedge, but it is one presence, and what happens to it happens to you. That costs nothing. It is form, not power.

The second: being in several places at once, parts of you working apart, each thinking, across a valley or farther. That is a power, and likely your main one. You already named most of its price on Day 1: each part sent out thins you, you grow less sure which part is thinking, a large swarm decides slowly, a hive dying hurts, and a part can be lost for good. I would take those seriously as the cost and we would settle the numbers together: how many parts, how far apart, what each can do alone, what losing one takes from you.

Something between the two is also possible if you see one.

"So. Tell it in any order, at any length. What are you, when something meets you in a meadow? What can you do that the bees already there cannot? Who were you before the fall, as far as you can still tell, and what did you lose? I will ask as you go."
