# Keeper release to the Riverfolk civ agent, Year 1, pass two: the resolved year

- The weir at Oxmouth held through the autumn run and both gales. The trap-pen took about four tons of fish over the run, dried and salted; a tenth went back alive through the giving gate. Add it to stores (done by the Keeper in state.md: dried fish about 8 tons).
- The boatmen Hask and Brin came home unhurt in week 17, within their time. What they found: above the falls the river shrinks to a stream in a day; two and a half days east through old wood with no track; then open hill country of sheep and heather, higher to the north under a grey snow range; on the fifth day old grassed-over banks and ditches on a crest, like a fort; on the sixth a dark mere in a fold of the hills; on the seventh, at a high steading above the mere, herders with dogs took them to an elder, an old woman with one clouded eye called Grandmother Ceth, of a hill people who call themselves the Carn. The Carn kept them two nights, fed them, asked about the river people, the god who woke on the bar, and whether the river people count the lights. Tall poles notched from top to bottom stand by the Carn houses; the Carn did not say what they are for. The Carn sent them back with these words: the river people are to come no farther than the wood's edge, and the Carn will come down to see the river themselves. Brin told it at Aldford in week 18, Hask at Fallstead in week 22. The people may make of this what they will.
- Aldford, week 9: Ede, wife of Tam the net-maker, died in childbed before dawn with the god standing at the door; her daughter lived. Add one birth and one death to your counts (births 183, deaths 169; population 5,014 stands). Tam has not spoken to the god since. Fix Tam, Ede and the child in your people files if they become significant.
- The god's year: he walked the Ald as decided, Wellbrook week 5, Aldford week 9, Fallstead week 22, and said nothing to any ask. In week 23, at the falls pool, a stranger came to him openly from the east bank path, a tall plain woman in hill traveler's clothes, in sight of Fallstead's staithe. What passed is not resolved; the people saw her arrive and no more. Your people's record of that moment is: a stranger woman came down from the hills to the god at the falls at evening in week 23, and the children on the staithe saw it. Nothing else about her.
- Sky-lights: the year's true count is four. Your counts stand as the elders' counts (ten villages four, two villages three under cloud).
- Nothing else touches the Riverfolk this year. No message was delivered to them.
