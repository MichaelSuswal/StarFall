# Keeper release to the Riverfolk civ agent, Year 1, pass two: the resolved year (rewritten after the check)

- The god's year: he walked the Ald as decided, about a week at each village, Wellbrook in week 4 to 5, Aldford in week 9 to 10, Fallstead in week 13, and stayed at the falls from then. He said nothing to any ask. At Aldford in week 9 he stayed in the river while Tam begged him; Ede died before dawn; the daughter lived (births 183, deaths 169; population 5,014 stands). In week 16, at dusk, a stranger came to him openly on the east bank a little below Fallstead, a tall plain woman in hill traveler's grey, from the wood's edge; two children on the staithe saw her come and stop at the water's edge. What passed is not resolved; the god's thread is held from that moment. Your people's record of it is: a stranger woman came down to the god at the falls at dusk in week 16, and the children saw it. Nothing else about her.
- The weir at Oxmouth, begun week 16 and finished week 19 as you designed, held through the autumn run and both gales. The trap-pen took about four tons of fish over the run, dried and salted; a tenth went back alive through the giving gate. Stores: dried fish about 8 tons (done in state.md).
- The old posts at Oxmouth, struck week 17: as you wrote them.
- The boatmen Hask and Brin came home unhurt in week 17, within their time. What they found is as you now have it in your year file: the wood, the hill country, the old banks on a crest, the dark mere, the Carn and Grandmother Ceth, the notched poles, the Carn's words. Hask told the village the day they came home; Brin told Aldford in week 18. Whether the god heard it then depends on where the scene of week 16 leaves him; the Keeper resolves that next night.
- Sky-lights: four this year, in weeks 1, 10, 20 and 46, counted the same by all twelve villages.
- Nothing else touches the Riverfolk this year. No message was delivered to them. The Carn's words stand as a thread for next year.
