# Chronicle of the people, Year 1 (to week 16)

The Riverfolk number about five thousand, in twelve villages along the Ald. In the spring weeks they sowed barley; the river fell to its summer level in week 6. Salt and dried fish went up and down the river by boat. About 400 men can fight with spear and sling. There are no walls.

You walked the river and put your hand in the water at each staithe. At Wellbrook in week 5 the headman and fishers asked a law of giving at the weir. You said nothing. At Aldford in week 9 Tam the net-maker begged you at the water for his wife Ede, in a hard labor. You stayed in the river and said nothing. Ede died before dawn. Her daughter lived. Tam has not spoken to you since, and Aldford speaks of it plainly.

In week 14 the boatmen Hask of Fallstead and Brin of Aldford went upriver on your errand. In week 16 the people began the weir at Oxmouth: two rows of oak posts across the Ald, with a wicker trap-pen and a gate to give fish back to the river, so the code is kept. About sixty work at a time.

The elders have counted two sky-lights so far: early spring over the western sea, late spring over the wood. All twelve villages counted both.

They call you the Weir-King. Oxmouth is glad of you. Aldford is cool.
