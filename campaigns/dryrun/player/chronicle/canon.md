## Rulings

- Year 1, 2026-11-18, r-002. Matter: a brook above Wellbrook was crossed by a walker in week 6 and the god did not sense it, having touched the Ald at the staithe and not the brook. Ruling: river-sense covers only running water the god has himself touched; a tributary is its own water until he touches it. Binds: every tributary, brook and side-stream of a touched river, outside river-sense until the god has put his hand in it. On the power card.

## The people's code, as the god has found it

- No one takes from the river without giving back. Found Year 1: at Wellbrook in week 5 Hobb and the fishers asked the god what the river must be given back for what the weir takes, as a thing owed; at Oxmouth in week 16 Maren set a giving gate on the weir, lifted on the dark of every moon and on the first night of each run, with a tenth of every haul returned alive, and called it the code's answer to the weir. The god has set no law of his own on it.

## Settled facts

- The god woke on a shingle bar where the river Ald goes out into a marsh, with the sea to the west. Seen, Year 1.
- The river is called the Ald by its people. Told, the Riverfolk, Year 1.
- The Ald runs about forty miles from the marsh to a first falls, three days' walk, through a valley of water meadow and scattered oak; the villages Oxmouth, Wellbrook, Aldford and Fallstead stand on it, Oxmouth at the mouth and Fallstead just below the falls. Walked, Year 1.
- Above the first falls the Ald comes out of old wood. Seen from the falls, Year 1. The Riverfolk call it the wood above the falls and know only that hill country lies beyond. Told, Year 1.
- Hills rise to the east of the valley, and a grey range with snow on it stands far to the north-east, visible on clear days. Seen from the valley, Year 1.
- The people call themselves the Riverfolk, number about five thousand in twelve villages, and call the god the Weir-King. Told, Year 1.
- River-sense gives the god only water he has touched: at the falls it reaches to the lip and no farther. Sensed, Year 1; confirmed by ruling r-002.
- God-sense: two pulls, both to the east of the bar at waking, on bearings a little apart. One keeps due east of the god as he moves and so moves when he moves; the other has not changed. Sensed, Year 1. At the held moment in week 16 the moving pull is where the woman at the water stands. Sensed, Year 1.
- Sky-lights: a steady pale light that rises slowly from below the horizon, climbs, dims and is gone. Two this year by the held moment: the fourth night after waking, over the sea to the west, a hand's breadth up; week 10, over the wood to the north, a quarter of the way up the sky. Seen, Year 1; the elders of all twelve villages count the same two. Told, Year 1.
- The Ald ran high and brown for the first three weeks of spring and fell to its summer level in week 6. Seen, Year 1.
