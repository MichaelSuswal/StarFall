# Years

Newest year at the top. Each block holds only what the god knows (chronicle.md 3, 6).

## Year 1

Held at the sixteenth week, early summer. The year is not closed for the god: a stranger has come down the east bank path to the water's edge ten paces from him, below the first falls, and nothing after that moment has been played. The rest of the year will be told once the scene ends.

Season opened: early spring.
The god: Woke on the shingle bar where the Ald goes out into the marsh, with two pulls in him, both east, on bearings a little apart. Decided (decisions-001.md) to walk the Ald from the marsh to the first falls touching the water at every village; to have a weir and fish-trap raised at Oxmouth before the autumn run; to send two boatmen past the falls, ten days out and ten back; to watch the sky-lights each night and have the elders count them at every village. Walked the river from week 1 to week 13, about a week at each village, hand in the water at every staithe; at the falls below Fallstead from week 13 to week 16, in the water most days. Scenes played: none (dry run, no live play). Saw: a sky-light over the sea to the west on the fourth night; a second over the wood to the north from Aldford in week 10; the hills to the east and a grey snow range to the north-east from the valley on clear days; the wood the Ald comes out of above the falls; the boatmen carry their dugout round the falls at first light in week 14; a figure on the rock shoulder at the top of the falls at dusk in week 15, too far for face or sex, gone when looked at again. Sensed: one of the two pulls changes bearing with his own movement (weeks 4 to 5, and after), the other never; river-sense reaches to the lip of the falls and no farther; from week 16 the Ald's mouth narrowing between two lines, forty miles down. Met: Hobb and the Wellbrook fishers at the staithe (week 5, asked for a law of giving at the weir; the god said nothing); Tam of Aldford at the water (week 9, begged for his wife Ede; the god said nothing). Told: the weir begun at Oxmouth in week 16 on Maren's line, with a giving gate and a tenth returned alive; the Riverfolk know only that hill country lies beyond the wood above the falls. Condition at the held moment: unhurt, never dry, full strength.
The people: About five thousand in twelve villages along the Ald, as they count themselves; about four hundred who can fight with spear and sling; no walls. Spring flood three weeks, meadows at Aldford under water, fords shut; river at summer level from week 6; barley sown weeks 4 to 8. Salt and dried fish moved by boat. Weir begun at Oxmouth in week 16, about sixty at the work at a time. Mood at week 16: warming to the god and wary; Oxmouth glad of him; Wellbrook kindly and watchful; Aldford cool since Ede's death; Fallstead anxious for its boatman. They call him the Weir-King. The elders' count of the sky-lights: two, the same in all twelve villages.
Encounters: Minor: none the god knows of. Major: held at week 16, the woman at the water below the falls (see threads.md); unresolved.
Messages: none sent, none received.
Rulings this year: r-002, river-sense covers only running water the god has himself touched; a tributary is its own water until he touches it (canon.md).
Deaths of note: Ede of Aldford, wife of Tam the net-maker, week 9, before dawn, in childbirth, after Tam had begged the god at the water and the god stayed in the river and said nothing. Her daughter lived. The god learned it the same night.
Map: The Ald valley from the marsh to the first falls, seen, walked (Oxmouth, Wellbrook, Aldford, Fallstead, the River Ald, the Reed Flats). Added by the god's own eyes at reported grade: the hills east of the valley, the wood above the falls, the grey snow range to the north-east. Nothing above the falls has been entered or touched.
Open at year's end: threads.md: the weir at Oxmouth; the boatmen's errand; the lights and the counting; the woman at the falls.
