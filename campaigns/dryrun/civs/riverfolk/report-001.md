# Riverfolk report to the Keeper, Year 1

## For the Keeper to resolve
- Boatmen: Hask and Brin leave Fallstead at first light, week 14 (about day 94). Dugout round the falls, poled about a day, cached in a pool; then east on foot into old wood, ten days out, ten back. Laden, forest 7.5 miles a day. Due back at Fallstead about day 115. What they find and whether they return are the Keeper's. The council in week 27 spoke of them only as things stood that day; the word of it must follow the Keeper's ruling.
- Weir: line 300 yards above the shingle bar, 60 yards wide, 8 yards below the old posts (east bank). Two rows of 60 oak posts, stone and brush fill, wicker upstream face, wicker trap-pen midstream, 4-yard giving gate on the west side. Built weeks 16 to 19, about 990 worker-days, 60 at a time, 17 work days. Keeper rolls whether it holds through the autumn run (week 32) and the two gales. Stores omit the weir catch.
- Old posts: two rows of black, square-cut oak at the east bank, struck week 17. Left untouched. The people's explanations are theirs; the truth is the Keeper's.
- Sky-light counts: ten villages four, two villages three (cloud).

## Questions for the god (held for the player)
1. May the weir take the whole run, or must the gate and the tenth stand? (Oxmouth.)
2. Is the weir his own old work? (Maren asks.)
3. Should the old posts be dug, marked or left? (Maren and Hobb differ.)
4. What are the lights, and are watchers wanted?
5. Will he swear the boatmen back? (Hask's mother, Fallstead.)

## Encounters held, not answered by standing conduct
- Wellbrook, week 5: a headman and fishers ask for a law of giving at the weir. A decision the player has not made.
- Aldford, week 9: a husband begs the god at the water for a wife in a hard labor. A power use and a choice. Her birth is not in the birth count.
- Fallstead, late summer: Hask's mother asks a promise. Standing conduct forbids promises.

## What the people would tell the god at year's end
- About five thousand people, a few more than last year; around 180 born, 170 dead.
- Barley sown on about 3,800 acres, a good harvest, a winter's grain and a little over.
- A weir built at Oxmouth in seventeen days by sixty hands.
- Four sky-lights counted: early spring, late spring, midsummer, midwinter.
- Black posts under the Oxmouth mud; the headmen differ over them.
- Four hundred spears.
- Hask and Brin gone upriver; news of them as it stands.
- Salt and dried fish in the villages, not much to spare.
