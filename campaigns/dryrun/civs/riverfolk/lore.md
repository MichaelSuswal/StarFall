# What the Riverfolk say

**The Weir-King.** "He woke on the bar and wet his hand at every staithe, so he is ours." At Oxmouth: he is the river's first weir-builder come back to mend his work. At Wellbrook: he is a guest, and guests are watched kindly. At Aldford: "He stood in the river and watched Ede die." He is the river's and the river does not come to the bed.

**The Carn.** Hill people beyond the wood, with notched poles and an old woman called Grandmother Ceth. Kin of the Weir-King, say some, since they ask after the lights. They will come down to see the river; keep to the wood's edge until they do.

**The river.** Gives and must be given to. "Take a fish, give a fish." The ones who went under took and did not give, and the sea took them back.

**The sky-lights.** Four this year. At Oxmouth: lamps of the drowned, climbing home; the posts were the first sign and the midsummer light the answer to a weir finished and a gift kept. At Aldford: marks of where the river will rise. Elsewhere: "the god's kin looking for him."

**The old posts.** Oxmouth: the Weir-King's first weir. Wellbrook: a drowned house and a grave. Nobody has dug.

**The headmen.** "Maren holds the mouth; Hobb holds the river's conscience." The council at Oxmouth is where the valley is one people.
