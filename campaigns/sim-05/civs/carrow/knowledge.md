# What the Carrow know of the land

Campaign sim-05. Kept by the Keeper; written on creation Night 3, 2026-11-23, and brought up to date each year. For the DM, for what mortals say. These are the people's own names and their own reckoning. A name here is known to the Carrow, not to Ostrane: it reaches the god only when a mortal says it. Grid cells are for out-of-game reference.

## Their own ground

They call their country the Carrow Plain (C1 and C2, running into B2): flat high grassland under the mountains, with the wet ground and the fields along the river. The river is the Braidwater, which runs down from the snow in many channels over gravel and changes its bars every melt. Carrowford (C1) stands on a rise above the ford, where the old paved road comes down to the water. A worn way they call the Bank Track follows the river up and down from the town.

They measure by their own ford-foot, pace and mile. Twenty-five ford-feet are a little more than twenty-five feet of anyone else's they have compared with; five ford-feet make a pace, and a thousand paces a mile. The iron bar and the bushel in the market wall are the standards.

## The old road

They call it the Old North Road. They did not build it and say people before them did. North of the town it climbs to a gap in the line of foothills, which they call the Varrun Gate (C1), a short day's walk: about seventeen miles by the road. South it runs straight across the plain to a market town where three roads meet, called Threeways (B2). Carters who have gone say ten days on foot by the road, about twice that with a wagon.

## North: the heights

Above the gate are the Varrun Heights (C1), ice and rock. A people live up there in holds cut into the mountain. The Carrow call them the hold-folk: short, broad, grey, not like other people, and exact about a bargain. They come down to the gate twice a year to trade copper for grain, salt and cloth, and they let no one go up. Their chief hold is said to be a week's climb above the gate. No one remembers them coming down in arms.

There is a tale of a shut door in the hillside by the gate. The hold-folk do not let anyone look for it.

## West: the steppe

West of the plain the grass goes on, drier and emptier. The drovers call it the Windrow Steppe (B1 and B2) and take herds to its edge. Far beyond it, three weeks on foot, there are said to be horse-herders who winter in a town of sod under the same mountains (A1 or A2). The Carrow have met a few of them at Threeways and none at home.

## South and east: the old kingdom

Traders say that south-east, down a great river called the Meld (C2), there is an old kingdom with a king's town, Meldry (C2), nearly three weeks on foot across open country. Its tolls are dear and its salt is dearer. Salt and iron both reach Carrowford by the old road, and both came short last year.

East are stony uplands (D1) that the Carrow know only as a grey line on a clear day and by the name the traders give them, the Greystone Uplands.

## Stories

Carters tell of a range of bare hills far to the south (C3) that looks like a great beast lying asleep. The Carrow have the story and nothing more.

## What they do not know

They have no chart. No one in the town has been past Threeways or above the gate. They do not know what lies beyond the heights or where the Braidwater ends.
