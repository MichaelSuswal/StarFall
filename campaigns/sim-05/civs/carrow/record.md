# The Carrow: the people's record

Campaign sim-05. Written by the DM at the close of creation Day 3, 2026-11-23, from what the player said and agreed at the table. Read back to the player and agreed. Stores, treasure, trade, strength and mood are the Keeper's to set on Night 3a. Where the town stands, and everything of the land and its past, is the world's.

## Who they are

A human people of river and plain, named the Carrow by the player. Their town is named Carrowford by the player.

> About five thousand, a river and plains people who have lived on this ford a long time. Most are farmers on the wet ground below the rise, with barley, beans and flax. A good share are drovers, keeping cattle and horses on the plain. The rest are the ferrymen, smiths, carters and traders who live off the road and the ford. They aren't great warriors and they're not meek either. They're a stubborn working people with a long habit of arguing over fences.

## How many, and how they live

About 5,000, the default. Nothing was argued about size.

> Their town is on the rise above the ford, wooden and stone, with a market, a granary and a wall of earth and timber that hasn't been tested lately. They keep early hours, because the melt and the floods punish late ones. They take pride in fair weights and honest measures, so a short bushel is the worst thing one neighbor can do to another.

The town has districts, each with a headman, and a market day at the ford market. A muster is called up in danger under a captain. The player means a levy drawn from the drovers and farmers, with about eighty paid and drilled soldiers in two companies ("roughly one in sixty of the people"). Ruled at the table as an ordinary town guard, not priced. Whether the eighty stand at landing or are raised in play was not said; the Keeper fits it, and their food and pay come out of the stores the Keeper sets.

## Their code

In the player's words:

- "They won't break a bargain made at the ford, with a witness and a drink on it."
- "They won't move a boundary stone at night. It's the one thing they're all ashamed of."
- "They honor the person who is early, who pays on the day and who stands their round."
- "They don't honor a long speech."
- "They're slow to trust a stranger, quick to test one, and loyal once they've decided."
- A short bushel is the worst thing one neighbor can do to another.

## Skills and culture

Farming (barley, beans, flax), droving (cattle and horses), ferrying, smithing, carting, trade, keeping counts and writing them. All ordinary, none priced.

Their own measures: "a 'ford-foot', a pace and a mile of their own, and they're proud of them."

## Priced edges and weaknesses

None. The player asked for none and the DM found none.

- The honesty of the Clerk of the Tally and the Judge of the Ford is character, oath and custom, with books open at the market. It is not a binding. In the player's words: "Their honesty should be earned and checked. It should fail sometimes, in play, and I'd handle it the way any ruler does." Not priced. Settled at creation. Ledger: pending, Night 3.
- About eighty paid, drilled soldiers in two companies: an ordinary town guard. Not priced. Settled at creation. Ledger: pending, Night 3.
- No default was refused for a step in any edge price. See Helpers.

## Their magic

> Nothing beyond craft that I know of. A midwife with herbs, a ferryman who knows the river's mood and a smith who talks to the iron. If there's more, the world decides, and I'd like to be surprised.

No mortal with a knack was designed or priced. Anything more is the Keeper's.

## What they believe

> They had small gods, the house-hearth, the river and the road, and they kept them with little rites and no priests. At a grave they say "the ground has you, and the ground keeps its word." After a bad harvest they say that the river was owed and didn't get it. When the river comes up in the night they put a lit candle in the window and go to the high ground without arguing. They had no god of lines. That's what they meant, in a way, when they said the old fences were nobody's.

They believe the old paved road was laid by people before them, and that they have lived on the ford a long time. They take their name from "the old word for the ford". These are the people's beliefs, for the Keeper to reconcile.

## How they settle disputes

The Judge of the Ford, "an old seat that hears weights, debts and fence cases, held now by someone respected and tired." Fence-viewers, "men and women who settle where a hedge or ditch runs." The fence quarrels "have been running for years and no one can end one."

## Helpers

Designed, not yet serving. The god lands with no helpers in fact. Recorded 2026-11-23, creation Day 3, with the player's yes.

The offices, and what stands at landing:

- The Reeve. Stands under the town's own law, "an elected office, a year at a time". Runs the town day to day: grain, labor, disputes, the market. Designed to speak for the god when it is away and to carry out what its decisions and standing conduct allow, and nothing more.
- The Judge of the Ford. Stands under the town's own law. Hears disputes over boundaries, weights and debts.
- The Clerk of the Tally. Stands under the town's own law, "a small office that keeps the market counts". Designed to keep the counts: people, grain, weights, tribute and tax.
- The Marshal. At landing, "a captain of the muster who's called up in danger, not a standing post". The god means to make it standing, over the levy and the paid soldiers, answering to the Reeve in peace and to the god in war.
- The Warden of Marks. Does not exist at landing. The god means to create it in play and to offer it to the best fence-viewer in the town. Designed to keep the stones and stakes of the god's lines, lead the yearly walk and keep the roll, "written, in duplicate, and one copy is kept away from the hall".

How orders are meant to travel, once the offices serve:

> I speak it to the Reeve, or write it in a short plain hand. The Clerk copies it and seals it. Runners carry it to the headman of each district, and he reads it aloud at the ford market on market day, where everyone can hear it and check it. The headmen send back a tally of the households that heard it. An order that comes back without a tally is treated as not delivered.

Until each office is won in play, no one acts for the god while it is away, its unplayed time runs on standing conduct alone, and the town goes on under its own law. Each office counts as a helper from the day its holder takes the god's orders. The unnamed holders are filed by office under `people/`.

## The god's place among them

None at landing. They do not know the god and were waiting for no god, "only for a fair judge". The god wants compliance with a standard, not worship. See `player/people-meeting.md`.
