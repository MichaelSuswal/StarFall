(oog) Before you land

Three lines of the Day 3 brief were written before the map was drawn, and the finished land changes them. The river is not directly below you. It is in view, about two miles off across the grass. The line of hills is in view, about ten miles off, with snow heights behind it and more snow heights very far off to one side, but the gap in it is not picked out from where you come down. The old paved road is in view, about a mile off, running toward the river. The ford where it meets the river is by the town and is not in view from the landing. The rest of the brief stands: the grassland, the worn track along the bank, the one thread of smoke.

The meeting point is settled as you wished it. The town stands on the rise above the ford, where the old paved road comes down to the river. You come down on a second low rise at the plain's edge, a few miles from the town.

Year 1. Early spring, first light, the hour before sunrise. You are standing where you came down, on a low grassy rise at the edge of a high plain, and it is cold. Frost lies white in the hollows and has already gone to wet on the rise. The ground gives a little underfoot. The low ground is sodden with melt, and the tracks are only beginning to dry. The air is still, and it smells of wet grass, old snow and, faintly, wood smoke. Cranes are calling somewhere toward the river. A lark is up. Your coat is damp at the hem. The chain is in your hand and the satchel is on your shoulder. The ground is known to you for one chain's length round you and no farther: a low rise of firm turf over gravel, falling gently away on every side. Past that length you have your eyes. You hold no ground.

Flat open grassland lies on every side, last year's growth brown with the green coming through it. Level ground shows out to about three miles, and things at ground level are picked out within about two.

About a mile to the east there is an old paved road, a straight pale line running from the north-east toward the south, with about three miles of it in view. About two miles to the north there is a river, braided, many channels between gravel bars, running high and the color of clay. About two miles of it are in view, going off to the north-west. A worn track follows its near bank.

About three miles to the north-east one thread of smoke rises straight in the still air. Nothing of what makes it is in view: no wall, no roof, no ford.

On the skyline to the north stands a line of bare hills, about ten miles off at its nearest, and behind it snow heights, very high. No gap shows in the line from here. Far to the west, about a hundred miles off, there are more snow heights on the skyline, small and sharp. In the sky a blue star stands well up to the south, paling as the light comes.

There is no person in view, and no beast nearer than the smoke. No one has spoken to you. You have no name for the road, the river, the hills or anything else in sight. You expect a people, as every god does. You do not know them and they do not know you. You know that others like you came down when you did, and nothing more. Of them you feel nothing, no pull and no direction.

(oog) A few plain things for the first morning.

Your people are the Carrow and their town is Carrowford. Both names are yours, from Day 3. No one has said either in your hearing, so the story does not use them yet. The smoke is the town's.

One thing the table left to the world, now settled: the eighty paid soldiers do not stand at landing. The town pays a watch of about thirty, for the gate, the market and the ford. The rest, and their drill, are raised in play.

The map is attached, drawn at the landing frame. The sheet is about 408 miles wide and about 307 from top to bottom. It is that wide on purpose, to show the map's scale: at this scale you and everything your eyes take in from the rise sit on one mark, the small star. The clear ground is the whole plain, because the map clears a country at a time once you are standing in it. What you see from here is only what the story above gives. The hills and the far heights show through cloud, as skylines seen and not ground walked. The road, the river and the track are each drawn as one line at full length, though you have seen only the few miles of each that the story gives. The smoke is marked by a tick from the star and named beneath the frame by your own name for the town. You can ask for the map at any time.

Nothing is waiting on you and no one has asked you anything.
