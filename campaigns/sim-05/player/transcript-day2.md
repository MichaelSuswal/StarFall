## Player:

I'll take it in order. What is falling is a claim. I'm not a thing that owns land. I'm the act of measuring it and holding it, with the chain, the stake and the boundary stone's authority all in one. I call myself **Ostrane**, and the people below can call me the Chain or the Measure.

**Body.** One body, tall and spare, built like a man who walks everywhere. The skin is the grey of old iron and the eyes are dull copper. The hair is cropped, and there is a ridge of fine scale-like lines along the forearms, like survey marks. I carry a chain of a hundred links that is part of me. It isn't a weapon. Every link is the same length, and that length is exact. I can't stand to see a crooked line, and it shows in how I stand.

**Nature and temper.** I'm patient and exact, and I'm ruthless when it pays. I don't rage. I count. I'll be early to everything and wait calmly once there. I'm bad at mercy offered for its own sake, but I keep my word to the letter, and I expect it from others to the letter as well. I whistle when I'm working something out, and I can't stop it.

**What I do.**
1. Wherever I stand, I know the true distance, slope and bearing of the ground, the way you know where your own hand is.
2. When I pace a boundary with the chain, the land inside it starts to feel mine. Those who live inside it feel it too, as a settled, ordered sense. It isn't coercion. It's more like the pull of a well-kept road.
3. What I hold, I hold. A border I've walked and my people have kept resists being argued, bribed or magicked out of existence more than ordinary ground does.

**What I can't do.** I can't feel land I haven't walked. I can't be in two places. An unmarked border is only a line on a map to me. I'm weak at the things that need no ground, such as sea, sky and the inside of a mind, and deep water at night unsettles me.

**The edge I'm asking for, deliberately too big.** I want to see every inch of my claimed ground at once, wherever I am. I'd know every army crossing, every wall breached and every road cut, in real time, as a map that updates in my head. I know it's stronger than it should be. Name the price.

**What I'd accept as a price.** The map could cost me attention, so while I read it I'm half absent from where my body stands. It could also be limited to ground I've paced in person, so growth costs me legwork. And it could show me the ground but not the people on it. I'd accept all three. If you want a heavier price, say so and I'll weigh it.

**What I want to be unlike.** Most gods want worship. I want compliance with a standard. I'd rather my people say "that's the Measure's mile" the way they say "that's a fair weight." I want to be the unit.

**What I fear.** Ground I can't read. The one mapped place that proves a map wrong.

I'll leave the court, the captains and the helpers for Day 3, as you said.

- Marked: a stone or stake set at the start and at every turning of the line, by you or by your people behind you.
- Kept: your people walk the line and stand its marks back up at least once in each year.

**1. True ground.** Where you stand, you know the true distance, slope and bearing of the ground around you, out to where clear daylight sight would reach from that spot, and it works the same in dark, fog or rain. It is the surface as it lies, with what is built on it as shape: a wall, a ditch, a road. It shows nothing under the ground, nothing inside a closed building, no person, no animal and no god.  (b) Afoot. Off the ground you lose more than the sense: carried on a horse, a cart, a litter or a deck you are sick and useless until an hour after your feet are down again. You walk to your wars.

**2. The paced claim.** When you pace a line closed and it is marked, the ground inside begins to feel yours. Everyone who lives inside feels it as a settled, ordered sense that the ground is held, and by whom. It comes on over a season of living there. It is a feeling and never an order: it makes no one obey, stay, love you or change a decision, and an enemy living inside feels it and may hate it. It claims no more than you personally pace. Another god who enters claimed ground perceives that it is held; anything past that, on a god, is unruled until it happens. **3. What is held.** A claimed line resists being unmade. Its marks do not drift, sink or get lost by accident or weather. Those who live inside do not forget or mistake where it runs, so a false survey, a forged deed or a bribed witness does not shift what people on that ground know. Mortal working aimed at moving, hiding or erasing the line costs its worker a good deal more than it otherwise would. It does not stop feet: an army walks across a held line as across any ground, and marks can be pulled up by hand. It does not stop a judge being bought; it means the bought ruling does not change what the ground's own people know. Against another god's working it is unruled until it happens. It holds only while the line is kept. Price, your choice of one:
 (a) The letter holds you. A boundary you have agreed by your given word you cannot cross under arms, and cannot pace across, until the other party releases you or throws down the marks themselves. Their breaking faith somewhere else does not release you.
 (b) No cheap surrender. You cannot cede, trade or redraw held ground by word or treaty. To give up or move a held line you must walk it out in person, link by link, the way you walked it in.

**4. The whole map.** This is the large one. Your three terms go on it as written, but by the rules I price under they shape the power and are not its price: paced ground only, half absent while you read, and the ground without the people. So: wherever you are, you may turn your attention to all your claimed ground at once and know it as it is at that moment, by the same sense as True ground. You know what has been done to it: a wall breached, a road cut, a bridge down, a fire, a flood, a field trampled flat. You do not see who, how many, or why. While you read, the body where you stand is half absent: it can walk and answer slowly, and it cannot fight, bargain or work the chain. It shows effects, never causes, and the Keeper reviews its shape tonight. It still needs a price the god would feel. Your choice of one:
 (a) It does not close. Every hurt to your claimed ground aches in you from the moment it is done, read or unread, wherever you are, until it is mended or the ground is given up. You cannot shut it out, it wears at your rest, and anyone who learns of it can cut a road to pull at you.
 (b) Blind beyond. Knowing your own ground whole costs you the rest: off claimed ground, True ground reaches only one chain's length around you instead of to sight. You know what you hold and almost nothing of what you mean to take until you have walked it.

One offer, made once: a weakness you take on, something you do not already pay, can buy one improvement elsewhere, such as the map showing bodies of people in number. You need not take one. If you say no, it stays closed for the rest of creation.
