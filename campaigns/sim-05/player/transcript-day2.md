# Transcript: creation Day 2 (sim-05)

## DM:

You are still falling. The dark around you has a grain to it now, like the back of an old map, and something in it speaks, from no side you could point to.

"You sent words down ahead of you. A shop at dawn, every tool on its hook, the roads already inked in, and more still to be taken. A conqueror, you said, with a seat, a court and captains. Those words went where they were going. Now I want the one who said them."

"Some things are so of every god that lands there, and you know them already, the way you know you have hands. You have one body, and you are in one place at a time. Of another god you feel nothing at a distance: no pull and no direction. You know one only by meeting it. When it stands before you the knowing comes on slowly, by nearness alone, and one who works at seeming mortal may hold it off for a while. Within arm's reach it is sure, and no god passes for a mortal at a touch. The knowing tells you what stands there. It tells you nothing of its temper, its powers or its mind."

(oog) Today is the god itself, in any order and at any length you like. I follow, ask, and put a price on anything that needs one. The seat, the court, the captains and the helpers belong to your people, and your people are Day 3's to settle, including what helpers cost. What you said about them yesterday is on the record and carries forward.

"So. What is it that is falling? Tell me what you are."

## Player:

I'll take it in order. What is falling is a claim. I'm not a thing that owns land. I'm the act of measuring it and holding it, with the chain, the stake and the boundary stone's authority all in one. I call myself **Ostrane**, and the people below can call me the Chain or the Measure.

**Body.** One body, tall and spare, built like a man who walks everywhere. The skin is the grey of old iron and the eyes are dull copper. The hair is cropped, and there is a ridge of fine scale-like lines along the forearms, like survey marks. I carry a chain of a hundred links that is part of me. It isn't a weapon. Every link is the same length, and that length is exact. I can't stand to see a crooked line, and it shows in how I stand.

**Nature and temper.** I'm patient and exact, and I'm ruthless when it pays. I don't rage. I count. I'll be early to everything and wait calmly once there. I'm bad at mercy offered for its own sake, but I keep my word to the letter, and I expect it from others to the letter as well. I whistle when I'm working something out, and I can't stop it.

**What I do.**
1. Wherever I stand, I know the true distance, slope and bearing of the ground, the way you know where your own hand is.
2. When I pace a boundary with the chain, the land inside it starts to feel mine. Those who live inside it feel it too, as a settled, ordered sense. It isn't coercion. It's more like the pull of a well-kept road.
3. What I hold, I hold. A border I've walked and my people have kept resists being argued, bribed or magicked out of existence more than ordinary ground does.

**What I can't do.** I can't feel land I haven't walked. I can't be in two places. An unmarked border is only a line on a map to me. I'm weak at the things that need no ground, such as sea, sky and the inside of a mind, and deep water at night unsettles me.

**The edge I'm asking for, deliberately too big.** I want to see every inch of my claimed ground at once, wherever I am. I'd know every army crossing, every wall breached and every road cut, in real time, as a map that updates in my head. I know it's stronger than it should be. Name the price.

**What I'd accept as a price.** The map could cost me attention, so while I read it I'm half absent from where my body stands. It could also be limited to ground I've paced in person, so growth costs me legwork. And it could show me the ground but not the people on it. I'd accept all three. If you want a heavier price, say so and I'll weigh it.

**What I want to be unlike.** Most gods want worship. I want compliance with a standard. I'd rather my people say "that's the Measure's mile" the way they say "that's a fair weight." I want to be the unit.

**What I fear.** Ground I can't read. The one mapped place that proves a map wrong.

I'll leave the court, the captains and the helpers for Day 3, as you said.

(One more answer was given privately, for the sealed record.)

## DM:

"Ostrane," the grain of the dark says, trying the weight of it. "A claim, falling. Then there are things a claim should know before it lands."

"A god cannot die there. But a body can be broken, or scattered, or shut out of the ground it stands on. Yours is iron-grey and walks everywhere, and it will be hurt sooner or later. When that body is broken, what do you wish happens to you? Tell me the wish first. The price comes after."

"And a claim is made by someone. You were something before you were falling, and you lost something on the way to here, or you would not be here. It will be foggy to you. Tell me only the outline: who Ostrane was, and what was lost."

"Last of these. You count, and you are ruthless when it pays. What will you not do, even when it pays?"

(oog) Now the plain part. First the terms your powers lean on, then each power as I would write it, with its price. Correct anything that is not what you meant.

One fact of that world that bears on your third power, given to every god: mortal magic there is simple, and it costs the worker. That is all I may say of it.

**Terms.**
- The chain: a hundred equal links, part of your body like an arm. It cannot be stolen from you; cutting it is a wound. Swung at someone it is an ordinary chain. I need one thing from you: how long is a link?
- Paced: walked on your own feet, on ground, the chain laid length after length, the line closing on its own start. Not ridden, not carried, not done by another.
- Marked: a stone or stake set at the start and at every turning of the line, by you or by your people behind you.
- Kept: your people walk the line and stand its marks back up at least once in each year.
- Claimed ground: what lies inside a line that is paced, closed, marked and kept.

**1. True ground.** Where you stand, you know the true distance, slope and bearing of the ground around you, out to where clear daylight sight would reach from that spot, and it works the same in dark, fog or rain. It is the surface as it lies, with what is built on it as shape: a wall, a ditch, a road. It shows nothing under the ground, nothing inside a closed building, no person, no animal and no god. It needs your feet on the ground: on a boat, a saddle, a cart or a floor above the earth it is silent. Shallows you could wade read as ground; deep water is blank. It is an information power, so it shows what is there and never why it is so. Price, your choice of one:
 (a) The true measure. You cannot state a measure you know to be false: a distance, a bearing, a boundary, a count, a weight. You may keep silent. You may not draw a false map or lie about a number, to an enemy or in a treaty.
 (b) Afoot. Off the ground you lose more than the sense: carried on a horse, a cart, a litter or a deck you are sick and useless until an hour after your feet are down again. You walk to your wars.

**2. The paced claim.** When you pace a line closed and it is marked, the ground inside begins to feel yours. Everyone who lives inside feels it as a settled, ordered sense that the ground is held, and by whom. It comes on over a season of living there. It is a feeling and never an order: it makes no one obey, stay, love you or change a decision, and an enemy living inside feels it and may hate it. It claims no more than you personally pace. Another god who enters claimed ground perceives that it is held; anything past that, on a god, is unruled until it happens. If a stretch of the line goes unmarked for a full year, the line is open and the feeling fades over a season, until you pace the gap again. Chaining a line is slower than walking it; I am setting it at a third of your walking pace, provisional, for the Keeper to review. Price, your choice of one:
 (a) The round. Every claimed line must be walked again by you, in person, chain in hand, once in every seven years, or it opens. The more you hold, the more of your years go to walking it.
 (b) No quiet ground. The feeling tells everyone inside exactly where your line runs and that they are within it: your own people, an enemy, a spy, a guest. You can never hold ground secretly, and you cannot deny a claim you have paced.

**3. What is held.** A claimed line resists being unmade. Its marks do not drift, sink or get lost by accident or weather. Those who live inside do not forget or mistake where it runs, so a false survey, a forged deed or a bribed witness does not shift what people on that ground know. Mortal working aimed at moving, hiding or erasing the line costs its worker a good deal more than it otherwise would. It does not stop feet: an army walks across a held line as across any ground, and marks can be pulled up by hand. It does not stop a judge being bought; it means the bought ruling does not change what the ground's own people know. Against another god's working it is unruled until it happens. It holds only while the line is kept. Price, your choice of one:
 (a) The letter holds you. A boundary you have agreed by your given word you cannot cross under arms, and cannot pace across, until the other party releases you or throws down the marks themselves. Their breaking faith somewhere else does not release you.
 (b) No cheap surrender. You cannot cede, trade or redraw held ground by word or treaty. To give up or move a held line you must walk it out in person, link by link, the way you walked it in.

**4. The whole map.** This is the large one. Your three terms go on it as written, but by the rules I price under they shape the power and are not its price: paced ground only, half absent while you read, and the ground without the people. So: wherever you are, you may turn your attention to all your claimed ground at once and know it as it is at that moment, by the same sense as True ground. You know what has been done to it: a wall breached, a road cut, a bridge down, a fire, a flood, a field trampled flat. You do not see who, how many, or why. While you read, the body where you stand is half absent: it can walk and answer slowly, and it cannot fight, bargain or work the chain. It shows effects, never causes, and the Keeper reviews its shape tonight. It still needs a price the god would feel. Your choice of one:
 (a) It does not close. Every hurt to your claimed ground aches in you from the moment it is done, read or unread, wherever you are, until it is mended or the ground is given up. You cannot shut it out, it wears at your rest, and anyone who learns of it can cut a road to pull at you.
 (b) Blind beyond. Knowing your own ground whole costs you the rest: off claimed ground, True ground reaches only one chain's length around you instead of to sight. You know what you hold and almost nothing of what you mean to take until you have walked it.

One offer, made once: a weakness you take on, something you do not already pay, can buy one improvement elsewhere, such as the map showing bodies of people in number. You need not take one. If you say no, it stays closed for the rest of creation.
