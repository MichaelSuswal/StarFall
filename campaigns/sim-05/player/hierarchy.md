# Hierarchy: bent, and empty at landing

Campaign sim-05. Written by the DM at the close of creation Day 3, 2026-11-23, with the player's yes.

The player designed a hierarchy and chose to meet a people who do not know the god. The form is designed, not yet serving. Ostrane lands with no helpers in fact. The player's words: "I land with no helpers in fact. The five offices are designed and not yet serving, and each becomes a helper only on the day its holder takes my orders. I asked for no edge, and it's right that I earn the offices one at a time."

## What the god has

At landing: no one. No office takes its orders.

## The offices as designed, and how each may be won

Each counts as a helper from the day its holder takes the god's orders, and not before. How each is won is play; nothing here promises it.

- The Reeve. Who: the holder of the town's elected office, a year at a time, who runs grain, labor, disputes and the market. Designed role: speaks for the god when it is away and carries out what its decisions and standing conduct allow, and nothing more. Won when the Reeve of the day takes the god's orders. The office is refilled each year by the town.
- The Judge of the Ford. Who: the holder of an old seat hearing weights, debts and fence cases, "respected and tired". Designed role: hears disputes over boundaries, weights and debts. Won when the Judge takes the god's orders.
- The Clerk of the Tally. Who: the keeper of the market counts. Designed role: keeps the counts of people, grain, weights, tribute and tax, and copies and seals the god's orders. Won when the Clerk takes the god's orders.
- The Marshal. Who: at landing, the captain of the muster, called up in danger. Designed role: a standing post over the levy and about eighty paid soldiers, answering to the Reeve in peace and to the god in war. Won when the god makes the post standing and its holder takes the god's orders.
- The Warden of Marks. Who: no one yet; the office is the god's own and is made in play. The god means to offer it to the best fence-viewer in the town. Designed role: keeps the marks of the god's lines, leads the yearly walk, keeps the roll in duplicate. "The Warden is the first office I'll try to win." Won when the god creates the office and someone takes it.

How orders are meant to travel once offices serve: spoken to the Reeve or written in a short plain hand; copied and sealed by the Clerk; carried by runners to the headman of each district; read aloud at the ford market on market day; a tally of the households that heard it sent back. "An order that comes back without a tally is treated as not delivered."

## The people's own order

Until the god is taken up, the Carrow run themselves: an elected Reeve, the Judge of the Ford, the Clerk of the Tally, a captain called up in danger, district headmen, fence-viewers, the ford market.

## What this means for every agent

- Until an office is won in play, no one acts for the god while it is away. Its unplayed time runs on `player/conduct-profile.md` alone, and the town goes on under its own law.
- No agent treats any of these mortals as serving the god before play has won them. The DM records each office won, with its date.
- Nothing was charged for this form and it bought nothing. No edge was priced on the Carrow.
