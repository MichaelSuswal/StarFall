# How Ostrane meets its people

Campaign sim-05. Written by the DM at the close of creation Day 3, 2026-11-23. Read back to the player and agreed.

## What was agreed

The Carrow are there before the god, with their own history. They do not know it. In the player's words:

> They don't know me. They've been waiting for nothing in particular. They haven't been waiting for a god, only for a fair judge, because their fence quarrels have been running for years and no one can end one. I walk up from the river, a tall grey stranger with a chain, and I begin by asking to see the oldest disputed fence. That's how they learn what I am, and I'll tell them the truth when asked.

The god lands with no helpers in fact. Its offices are designed and not yet serving (`player/hierarchy.md`).

## Standing conduct

Also written as dated entries in `player/conduct-profile.md`.

- First action: walk up from the river and ask to see the oldest disputed fence. Tell the truth when asked what it is.
- Set its foot and its chain in iron on the market wall beside the Carrow's own measures, with the ratio cut between them, and say plainly how the two compare. Do not tear theirs down.
- The Warden of Marks is the first office it will try to win.
- The first line is the first action of claiming. The fence-viewer and the gap are wishes: "I expect the world to place both, and I'll walk what it places."

## Heard, not promised

The player's hopes about what mortals will do. Each is theirs to do or not.

- That a god who sets the lines is welcome among the Carrow.
- That they show the god the oldest disputed fence.
- That the best fence-viewer takes the office of Warden of Marks, and that she is one who disagrees with the god.
- That the Reeve, the Judge, the Clerk and the captain of the muster come to serve.
- That both measures are lawful in trade for a year, that the market then weighs and measures by the iron, and that the old names stay in speech. "I'd rather they say 'ford-foot' and mean mine than lose the name."
- That the households along each line take up the yearly walk as part of their dues.
- From Day 1, repeated by the player today as wishes and not facts: "some wild country, some old kingdoms, a hard mountain neighbor and one far-off surveyor I'd want to hire."

## For the Keeper to reconcile

1. Where Carrowford stands. The player wishes it on the rise above the ford; the brief has the god come down on a low rise at the edge of a plain, above the river.
2. How long the Carrow have been on the ford. The player: "have lived on this ford a long time."
3. What they say came before them. They believe the old paved road "was laid by people before them."
4. The origin of their name: "from the old word for the ford." A custom that claims an origin.
5. What lies past their borders. The player said nothing and leaves it to the world.
6. Whether the gap in the hills can be seen from the town's walls. The player wishes to start the first line from there.
7. The town as described: wood and stone, a market, a granary, a wall of earth and timber "that hasn't been tested lately", districts with headmen, a ford market. Why the wall has not been tested lately is history.
8. The fence quarrels "running for years", and the old fences said to be "nobody's".
9. Their small gods of the house-hearth, the river and the road, kept with little rites and no priests. Recorded as the people's belief.
10. Their own measures: a ford-foot, a pace and a mile. Their sizes are the Keeper's.
11. Whether the eighty paid soldiers stand at landing or are raised in play. The player did not say.
12. Magic: the player designed none beyond craft and asks to be surprised.
