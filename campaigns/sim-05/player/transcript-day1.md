# Transcript: creation Day 1 (sim-05)

## DM:

(oog) Would you like to play mirror-mode, or in standard mode? In mirror mode, a few of the god's smaller gifts are drawn from you as a person. In standard mode, nothing is.

You are falling. Not through air. Through something thinner. Behind you is wherever you were, already too far to see. Below is a world that doesn't know you yet. This is The Between: the long descent from the place you left to the place you will land. Gods come down here to lead a people: to find them or gather them, to build, to rule, and to spread. That is what the world below expects of you, and what the others falling beside you expect of themselves. Others fall with you. You can't see them, but you can feel them, each telling the world below what they wish it to be. The world is listening to all of you. It won't grant everything, and you'll learn later which wishes it kept. Answer what speaks to you. Skip what doesn't.

1. When you imagine this world at its truest, what does it feel like? Give me a mood or an image.
2. What land calls to you? Mountains, marsh, coast, forest, steppe, desert, ice, river country, or somewhere stranger.
3. What should the seasons be like in this world? And when each new year finds you, which season do you want to wake into?
4. Past the borders of your own land, what do you hope to find? Wild country no one has walked, old kingdoms already rooted, or some of each?
5. How old is the human story here? Thousands of years, ruins under ruins, or people who arrived within living memory?
6. How much magic should mortals have? Never gone entirely and never everywhere. Somewhere between a rumor most people doubt and a wise-woman in every valley.
7. If mortals have magic, what should it feel like, and what should it cost them?
8. How much should a god stand out here? Like the one-eyed man in the kingdom of the blind, or barely more than a great mortal sorcerer?
9. How strange should the wild be? Familiar animals and a few legends, or creatures no one has a name for?
10. Beyond each other, what should mortals fear here? Name the kind of danger that makes a good story to you.
11. What should be scarce in this world, the thing mortals want most and have least, whether they trade for it, fight for it, or go without?
12. What would you like to find buried in this land? Ruins, bones of something enormous, old roads, sealed doors, or nothing at all?
13. Should there be peoples here who aren't human? If so, what are they like?
14. Should there be travelers who came from somewhere far away, few or many, and brought strange knowledge or tools with them?
15. You already sense that others like you have arrived. How do you hope gods will recognize each other: a pull felt at any distance, a knowing only face to face, or something else?
16. Can a god be in more than one place at once? One body, or several, and if several, what should it cost?
17. Sometimes a mortal rises with a story worth following. What kind of mortal do you hope this land produces?
18. What should a god be able to discover here that it can't yet imagine? A place, a power, a truth, a companion?
19. Last: one wish for this world you haven't dared say yet. The world only listens, but this is when it's listening.

## Player:

**Mode:** Mirror mode.

1. A working shop at dawn. Every tool on its hook, the maps flat on the table, the roads already inked in. It feels ordered, and it also feels like there is more still to be taken.
2. River country that opens onto high plains. I want flat, defensible ground with passes, so I can hold roads and build forts. The coast is a second choice. I'd like a harbor, but I don't want to start there.
3. Four real seasons, and the winters should be hard enough to limit campaigns. I'd like to wake into early spring, when the roads dry out and the armies can move.
4. Some of each. I want wild country to settle and old kingdoms to take or break. An empire needs both room and rivals.
5. Thousands of years, with ruins under ruins. Old roads and old walls are something to build on, and I'd rather inherit them than start from nothing.
6. Closer to a rumor most people doubt, with a wise-woman in the odd valley. Magic should be rare enough that law, drilled soldiers and good logistics still decide wars.
7. It should feel like craft, learned slowly and in the open. The cost should be time, health, and a debt owed to whoever taught you. It shouldn't be cheap or flashy.
8. I'd rather be well above a mortal sorcerer than the one-eyed man. I want to be a power rivals have to plan around, and I don't want to be an unbeatable one. If the gap is too wide the world stops pushing back.
9. Familiar animals and a few real legends. I want one or two things in the wild that no one can name, and I want the borderlands to be where they are.
10. Famine, bad winters, plague in the camps, and a neighbor who breaks his word. I want fear to come from supply lines and betrayal. I don't want it to come from monsters.
11. Salt and iron, or whichever one decides who can feed an army and who can arm one. I want people to trade for it, fight over it, and tax it.
12. Old roads and a sealed door. The roads should be useful on day one, and the door should wait.
13. Yes, but few, and one people should be a hard neighbor. I'd like a mountain folk with their own law and their own forts. They would be worth treating with and worth fighting.
14. A few travelers. One should be a surveyor or engineer from somewhere far off, with tools and methods nobody here has. I'd want that person hired, and I wouldn't want them killed.
15. A knowing only face to face. Distance shouldn't give you a free read on a rival. I want to have to meet them, at a border or across a table.
16. One body. If I want to be somewhere else, I send captains.
17. A hard, fair administrator, someone who keeps books, holds a town through a siege and can't be bought. A general would be fine too, but I'd take the administrator first.
18. A companion, or a truth about how the first gods fell. I can't picture it yet, which is the point.
19. I wish the empire could outlast me. I'd like laws, roads and habits that still work after the god who made them is gone.

I'd also like to start the survey on the record as a conqueror. I want a seat, a court and captains, and I'll decide on helpers once I know what they cost.
