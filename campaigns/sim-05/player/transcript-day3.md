# Transcript: creation Day 3 (sim-05)

## DM:

(oog) Before anything of today is played, the Keeper's answers on what the table left open last night. Four are settled and need nothing from you. One needs your yes.

- The chaining rate is confirmed as set at the table: a third of your walking pace over the same ground. With The slow chain, Ostrane chains at a quarter of its walking pace. Nothing on the card changes.
- Where you gather if you hold no claimed ground is confirmed as written: the nearest standing boundary mark of anyone's setting.
- What is held now has its figure. A mortal working aimed at moving, hiding or erasing a claimed line costs its worker twice what the same working would otherwise cost. That is a figure for a Limit you already agreed, and it goes on the card as a dated entry.
- Your ask about the return is granted. The roll is logged, as every roll is. After a return has happened, the Keeper tells me which item went, and I tell you in (oog) and strike it by a dated entry. In the fiction Ostrane no longer holds the item and does not know what it was. Nothing is shown before the return, and the god never chooses the item.
- The one that needs your yes. The shape of True ground and The whole map is confirmed, with one Limit to add under True ground: "It reads the ground and what stands on it. It gives no distance, height or bearing for anything in the sky." The whole map works by the same sense and takes the same Limit. The Keeper's reason: the power was described and priced as a sense of ground, and your own account of the god sets the sky outside it ("I'm weak at the things that need no ground, such as sea, sky and the inside of a mind"). The Limit says so on the card, so it never has to be argued in a scene. With your yes it goes on as a dated entry. If you do not agree, give me your argument; I carry it to the Keeper, who rules tonight.

And an answer owed from Day 1, when you said you would decide on helpers once you knew what they cost. They are not priced. A people comes with those who run things while its god is elsewhere, and they carry out what your decisions and your standing conduct allow. Ordinary custom, culture and skill are free as well. A price comes in only where your people would hold a real edge over other peoples, and I will name it when we reach one.

The falling slows. The grain of the dark thins, and under it there is ground, a long way down and coming up.

"Look, then," the dark says. "You asked for early spring and you have it."

River country, opening onto high plains. A low rise at the edge of a plain, above a river: that is where your feet will come down. On one side lies flat open grassland, last year's growth brown, with green coming through it. Below runs the river, many channels between gravel bars, high and cold and the color of clay with the melt. There is a line of hills with a gap in it. Snow still lies on the far heights. The low ground is wet, and the tracks are beginning to dry. The straight line of an old paved road comes down to the river at a ford. A worn track follows the bank. There is one thread of smoke.

"That is all the land shows from here. It shows no one. So I ask you what it cannot tell me."

"A god comes down to lead a people: to build, to rule and to spread. You said it yourself, a seat, a court and captains. So there is a people for you. Some five thousand of them, with a town or a city, a seat for you in it, and a court or a council around that seat who run things in the seasons when you are out walking a line. Who are they, Ostrane? What kind of people, how do they live, what do they do with their days? And who runs things for you when you are not there: which offices, and how does an order of yours travel from your mouth to the last household?"

(oog) Take those in any order and at any length. One rule for today: what you tell me of the people is yours to set. What you tell me of the land or its past, an old wall, who was there before, what lies beyond the gap, I record as your people's belief or as your wish, never as fact. The world decides, and the god will see.
