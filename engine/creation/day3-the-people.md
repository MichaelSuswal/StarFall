# Day 3: The People

The DM's guide for the third day of creation. Read with DESIGN.md sections 3 and 5.

## The shape of the day

Like Day 2, a conversation. The god talks about the people it will lead; the DM follows, asks, prices what needs pricing and rules. In the fiction by default, (oog) when needed. Before the day closes, the DM goes back over its checklist.

This is the day the game's premise comes due. A god comes down to lead a people, to build, to rule and to spread (DESIGN 3.0). So the DM opens as though the god leads a people with a seat, a court or council, and helpers who run things when the god is absent, about 5,000 strong with a town or city, and asks who they are. That is the expected course, not one option among several. A player may still refuse any part of it: a smaller people, no town, no helpers. Refusing is a choice the player argues for, the DM prices the cost of a refusal the player has made plainly, asked or not (above all what it means for unplayed time), and says which edge each default refused bears on and that it counts one step in the god's favor there, one weakness off that edge's price (DESIGN 3.11), and the game then remembers out loud that this god does not play the usual way. Nothing is held against the player for it.

## What the DM has from Night 2

- `player/day3-brief.md`: the kind of country the god will land in, as the land itself would show it. Land is the ground, its water, weather, growth and beasts, and visible traces of people (walls, smoke, a worn path). Not land: who lives there or how many, whether anyone waits for the god or how it will be received, history, other gods, distances or sight lines. The brief is written from the placement draft before the map is traced, so it names only what that draft fixes; anything it says is in view is confirmed at tracing, and a difference reaches the DM through `player/corrections.md` and the first morning's pre-landing block (`engine/rules/morning-story.md`). Nothing in the brief settles a Day 3 question; the DM asks it as if the brief were silent.
- `player/followups-day3.md`: the Keeper's follow-ups. Write the player's answers under each before the close; if there are none, one line saying so.
- `player/corrections.md`, if the Keeper has anything for the DM to put to the player.
- The Keeper does not name the player's people or their home. The player does. The Keeper names everything else, and never names what the player leaves for play (DESIGN 3.11a).

## Opening

Begin from what Night 2 built. The Keeper now knows where this god will land and what that land is like. Let that shape the first question: who are the people the god will lead in that land, and who runs things for it when it is not there?

## The DM's hidden checklist

1. **Who they are.** Kind of people (human by default; another kind if the god describes it), how they live (town or city by default; clans, nomads, a scattering, a warrior band are all possible), what they do with their days.
2. **How many.** About 5,000 by default. The god may want fewer, or more, or plan to grow. A larger people is not a priced edge; its costs are practical and come from the world (more food and fuel, slower growth, harder to move, and for a larger kind of people a longer gestation), argued case by case and left to the Keeper to apply in play. Record what was argued.
3. **Their code.** What is always true of them: customs, taboos, what they honor, what they will not do.
4. **Their skills and culture.** Described freely. Ordinary culture is not priced.
5. **Edges, priced.** Anything that gives a real edge over other peoples (a rare skill, unusual longevity, a magic most peoples lack) is priced against a weakness, like a power. An edge that would change the god's own powers (a mortal who can do what only the god can, a helper who performs it) is priced on the god, not the people, with any cost the mortal bears marked for the Keeper's Night 3 fitting, and goes on the power card as its own power block marked "added Day 3". It may not undo a limit agreed on Day 2; price a version that keeps the limit. A Day 3 answer that narrows a Day 2 definition or weakness is a dated entry.
6. **Their magic,** within what the star allows. The DM knows only the pricing terms in `world-rules.md` (simple, and it costs the worker), not how common magic is; a specific mortal with a knack is priced by the DM and marked subject to the Keeper's reconciliation.
7. **How the god meets them.** Already there with their own history, waiting for something, or arrived with the god. This comes from the world and the player together. The DM cannot read the deep history, so it writes what the player agreed and lists, in `player/people-meeting.md` under `## For the Keeper to reconcile`, every point that needs reconciling with it: how long the people have been there, what they say came before them, what lies past their borders, and any custom that claims an origin. Customs that claim no origin do not go on the list. A separate `## Heard, not promised` section holds the player's hopes about what mortals will do. The Keeper reconciles on Night 3a and makes the smallest change that fits, logged, with a line for the DM in `player/corrections.md`.
8. **What they believe** about gods, before this one arrives.
9. **The helpers.** Who runs things when the god is absent: a court, a council, priests, stewards, captains, a single steward, and how orders travel. This is the default and the DM asks about it as such, by office rather than by named person. The god may refuse helpers, may dismiss any it is given, may never meet or employ them; the refusal or dismissal is recorded with its date, and dismissed helpers are never forced back. New loyal helpers may come later through play. Helpers the player designs who do not know the god at landing are a third form, beside served and refused: `designed, not yet serving` (the hierarchy file is headed "bent, and empty at landing"). The DM says plainly that the god lands with no helpers in fact; until each is won in play, unplayed time runs as with none. With no helpers, the god's unplayed time runs on standing conduct alone, no one acts for it while it is away, and its people drift; the DM says so plainly when the player refuses.
10. **Who settles disputes** among the people, whether or not there is a hierarchy.
11. **Their name,** and the name of their home. The player gives both; "unnamed" is a valid answer and the Keeper uses "unnamed location" and the like until play names them. Naming a home does not place it: whether a walled town stands where the player says is the world's, and the DM says so. Sub-groups the player declines to name (houses, clans) stay unnamed the same way. An unnamed people's folder is `civs/unnamed-people/`; an unnamed mortal is filed by office (`people/steward.md`).

## What never happens on Day 3

- The DM does not invent the people for the player. It asks.
- No edge goes unpriced, and no ordinary custom gets a price.
- No people is made to fit a plot.
- The player's description of the land or its past (old roads, who was here before, what lies over the pass) is recorded as the people's belief or as a wish, never as a fact. The DM says so to the player: the world decides, and the god will see.
- The player sets who a mortal is, never what that mortal will do. "I'd like her to be the first to come" is heard and not promised.
- The DM does not offer refusal of the premise unasked. A refusal the player has made is priced plainly, asked or not.

## Follow-up questions

Few, woven in. (oog) when needed.

## Sealed answers

An answer that belongs in the sealed record (an unsaid want, a caveat the god would not say aloud) is taken as on Day 2: asked last, answered in a separate reply headed `SEALED:`, written by the orchestrator (or, in a live chat, the DM) straight to `S/player/unsaid.md`, with only "(One more answer was given privately, for the sealed record.)" left in the transcript. When it qualifies an open conduct line, that line carries the plain mark "check with the player before acting", with no reason.

## At the close

Read the people back to the player in plain words, including every priced edge and its weakness, the helpers, and the names, and get a yes. A read-back may carry marked gaps for the player to fill in the same reply; the yes and the answers are taken together. Then write to the repo:
- the people's record: `civs/<people>/record.md`: who they are, how many, how they live, their code, their skills, priced edges and weaknesses, their magic, their beliefs, how they settle disputes, and a `## Helpers` section (offices and how orders travel, or the recorded refusal). Named mortals the player created go under `civs/<people>/people/`.
- `civs/<people>/state.md` with the population only; the Keeper sets stores, treasure, trade, strength and mood on Night 3a.
- how the god meets them, as agreed, with the reconcile list and the heard-not-promised list: `player/people-meeting.md`
- the hierarchy, or the record that the god refused one and how the people settle things, or the third form (designed, not yet serving, with who each helper is and how it may be won): `player/hierarchy.md`
- any premise rejected or bent, under `## Day 3` in `player/rejected-premises.md`
- the player's answers under each question in `player/followups-day3.md`
- a new power block marked "added Day 3" on `player/power-card.md` for any edge that is a power of the god's, dated entries for narrowings, each ruling and every pricing made at the table (an edge, a refusal's step, a mortal's knack) marked "settled at creation. Ledger: pending, Night 3"; and dated entries on `player/conduct-profile.md` for any standing conduct the day produced (how the god means to be found, what it will not give in unplayed time), with its `## Now` summary rewritten to match. A stated first action ("I walk in at the burial ground") is conduct and goes in the profile; where it happens is the world's, recorded as the player's wish for the Keeper to place
- The transcript, `player/transcript-day3.md`, is the verbatim record of the table and is never changed. A file that copies the player's words (the people's record, the meeting, the hierarchy, the follow-up answers) keeps them as said; where the Keeper later replaces a word in one with a plain word of the same sense, the DM keeps the replacement and does not restore it.

Then Night 3: the Keeper builds the peoples and the world, and the checker locks it. At 6:30 the next morning, the god wakes in its land.
