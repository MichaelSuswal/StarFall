# Day 3: The People

The DM's guide for the third day of creation. Read with DESIGN.md sections 3 and 5.

## The shape of the day

Like Day 2, a conversation. The god talks about the people it wants to lead; the DM follows, asks, prices what needs pricing and rules. In the fiction by default, (oog) when needed. Before the day closes, the DM goes back over its checklist.

The game assumes a people with a town or city and a hierarchy of advisors who carry out the god's wishes. The player can reject any of that. If they do, the game accepts it without penalty and, from then on, remembers out loud that this god does not play the usual way.

## Opening

Begin from what Night 2 built. The Keeper now knows where this god will land, what that land is like, and something of the deep history there. Let that shape the first question: who lives in that land, and what does the god hope to find among them?

## The DM's hidden checklist

1. **Who they are.** Kind of people (human by default; another kind if the god describes it), how they live (town or city by default; clans, nomads, a scattering of loners, a small warrior band are all possible), what they do with their days.
2. **How many.** About 5,000 by default. The god may want fewer, or plan to grow.
3. **Their code.** What is always true of them: customs, taboos, what they honor, what they will not do.
4. **Their skills and culture.** Described freely. Ordinary culture is not priced.
5. **Edges, priced.** Anything that gives a real edge over other peoples (a rare skill, unusual longevity, a magic most peoples lack) is priced against a weakness, like a power.
6. **Their magic,** within what the star allows. Simple among mortals.
7. **How the god meets them.** Already there with their own history, waiting for something, or arrived with the god. This comes from the world and the player together: the people's history in the land, how long they have been there and what they remember, decides what fits. Reconcile it with the Keeper's deep history.
8. **What they believe** about gods, before this one arrives.
9. **The hierarchy,** if the god wants one: who advises, who rules under the god, how orders travel. If the god rejects hierarchy, record that it did.

## What never happens on Day 3

- The DM does not invent the people for the player. It asks.
- No edge goes unpriced, and no ordinary custom gets a price.
- No people is made to fit a plot.

## Follow-up questions

Few, woven in. (oog) when needed.

## At the close

Read the people back to the player in plain words, including every priced edge and its weakness, and get a yes. Then write to the repo:
- the people's record: `civs/<people>/record.md` and `state.md`: who they are, how many, how they live, their code, their skills, priced edges and weaknesses, their magic, their beliefs
- how the god meets them, reconciled with the deep history: `player/people-meeting.md`
- the hierarchy, or the record that the god rejected one: `player/hierarchy.md`
- any rejected premise, for the Keeper: `player/rejected-premises.md`

Then Night 3: the Keeper builds the world and locks it. At 6:30 the next morning, the god wakes in its land.
