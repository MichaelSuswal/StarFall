# Day 3: The People

The DM's guide for the third day of creation. Read with DESIGN.md sections 3 and 5.

## The shape of the day

Like Day 2, a conversation. The god talks about the people it hopes to find; the DM follows, asks, prices what needs pricing and rules. In the fiction by default, (oog) when needed. Before the day closes, the DM goes back over its checklist.

The game assumes a people with a town or city and a hierarchy of advisors who carry out the god's wishes, about 5,000 strong. The player can reject any of that. If they do, the game accepts it without penalty and, from then on, remembers out loud that this god does not play the usual way. A smaller people, no town and no hierarchy are real choices with real costs, and they count toward the price of the people's edges. With no hierarchy, the god's unplayed time runs on standing conduct alone.

## What the DM has from Night 2

- `player/day3-brief.md`: the kind of country the god will land in, as the land itself would show it. Nothing of who lives there beyond visible traces (walls, smoke, a worn path), nothing of history, nothing of other gods.
- `player/followups-day3.md`: the Keeper's follow-ups. Write the player's answers under each before the close.
- The Keeper does not name the player's people or their home. The player does. The Keeper names everything else.

## Opening

Begin from what Night 2 built. The Keeper now knows where this god will land and what that land is like. Let that shape the first question: who lives in that land, and what does the god hope to find among them?

## The DM's hidden checklist

1. **Who they are.** Kind of people (human by default; another kind if the god describes it), how they live (town or city by default; clans, nomads, a scattering of loners, a small warrior band are all possible), what they do with their days.
2. **How many.** About 5,000 by default. The god may want fewer, or plan to grow.
3. **Their code.** What is always true of them: customs, taboos, what they honor, what they will not do.
4. **Their skills and culture.** Described freely. Ordinary culture is not priced.
5. **Edges, priced.** Anything that gives a real edge over other peoples (a rare skill, unusual longevity, a magic most peoples lack) is priced against a weakness, like a power. An edge that would change the god's own powers (a mortal who can do what only the god can) is priced on the god, not the people, and goes on the power card as a dated Day 3 entry. It may not undo a limit agreed on Day 2; price a version that keeps the limit.
6. **Their magic,** within what the star allows. The DM knows only the level given in `world-rules.md`; a specific mortal with a knack is priced by the DM and marked subject to the Keeper's reconciliation.
7. **How the god meets them.** Already there with their own history, waiting for something, or arrived with the god. This comes from the world and the player together. The DM cannot read the deep history, so it writes what the player agreed and lists, in `player/people-meeting.md`, every point that needs reconciling with it (how long the people have been there, what they say came before them, what lies past their borders). The Keeper reconciles on Night 3 and makes the smallest change that fits, logged, with a line for the DM in `player/corrections.md`.
8. **What they believe** about gods, before this one arrives.
9. **The hierarchy,** if the god wants one: who advises, who rules under the god, how orders travel, who settles disputes. If the god rejects hierarchy, record that it did, and ask how the people settle things among themselves.
10. **Their name,** and the name of their home. The player gives both; "unnamed" is a valid answer and the Keeper leaves them unnamed until play names them.

## What never happens on Day 3

- The DM does not invent the people for the player. It asks.
- No edge goes unpriced, and no ordinary custom gets a price.
- No people is made to fit a plot.
- The player's description of the land or its past (old roads, who was here before, what lies over the pass) is recorded as the people's belief or as a wish, never as a fact. The DM says so to the player: the world decides, and the god will see.
- The player sets who a mortal is, never what that mortal will do. "I'd like her to be the first to come" is heard and not promised.

## Follow-up questions

Few, woven in. (oog) when needed.

## At the close

Read the people back to the player in plain words, including every priced edge and its weakness, and get a yes. Then write to the repo:
- the people's record: `civs/<people>/record.md`: who they are, how many, how they live, their code, their skills, priced edges and weaknesses, their magic, their beliefs, how they settle disputes. Named mortals the player created go under `civs/<people>/people/`.
- `civs/<people>/state.md` with the population only; the Keeper sets stores, treasure, trade, strength and mood on Night 3a.
- how the god meets them, as agreed, with the list of points to reconcile: `player/people-meeting.md`
- the hierarchy, or the record that the god rejected one and how the people settle things: `player/hierarchy.md`
- any premise rejected or bent, under `## Day 3` in `player/rejected-premises.md`
- the player's answers under each question in `player/followups-day3.md`
- dated Day 3 entries on `player/power-card.md` for any edge that touches the god's powers, and on `player/conduct-profile.md` for any standing conduct the day produced (how the god means to be found, what it will not give in unplayed time)

Then Night 3: the Keeper builds the peoples and the world, and the checker locks it. At 6:30 the next morning, the god wakes in its land.
