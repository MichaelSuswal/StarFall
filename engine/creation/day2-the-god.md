# Day 2: The God

The DM's guide for the second day of creation. Read with DESIGN.md section 3 and section 6.

## The shape of the day

Day 2 is a conversation, not a form. The player describes the god they imagine, in any order, at any length. The DM follows, asks, prices and rules. In the fiction by default: the god is still falling through The Between, and the voice that speaks with it belongs to that place. The DM steps into (oog) whenever powers, costs or rulings need plain words.

Before the day closes, the DM goes back over its checklist and raises anything still missing, woven into the talk where it can be, plainly in (oog) where it can't.

## Opening

Begin from the night's world-building, not from a blank page. Anything the Keeper learned on Night 1 that bears on this god (the land it asked for, the magic it wished for, a gap a rejected premise left) can open the conversation. Then let the player talk.

## The DM's hidden checklist

Every item must be settled before the day closes. Order does not matter.

1. **Form.** What the god looks like and what it is: human-seeming, beast, element, something stranger. Enough to write its appearance card for the paintings.
2. **Bodies.** How many bodies the god may have, within what the campaign's surveys allowed.
3. **Backstory outline.** Who the god was and what it lost, in the player's words. Keep it an outline. The DM fills in the rest in secret on Night 2 and ties it to where the gods came from. The god's past is foggy to the god itself.
4. **Powers.** One or several, as the player wants. No fixed number.
5. **Drawbacks.** Every power comes with one. Offer choices where possible, the way the original priced the shadow-step against carrying nothing.
6. **Edge cases.** Test each power against the main cases before signing off: light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, water, being watched, other gods, the god's own people. Write the answers on the power card.
7. **Trades.** A weakness taken on can buy an improvement elsewhere. Record each trade.
8. **What the god wants,** as far as the player knows. This seeds the conduct profile.
9. **Name,** if the god has one, and what it is called by those who don't know its name.

## Pricing and balance

- The DM balances total power across all gods from the interviews, like a budget whose size is not set in advance. One enormous power can cost the same as several small ones.
- Gods end roughly balanced, not exactly. Note any lean, up or down, for the Keeper.
- Judgment order on any ruling: fairness, above all to what the player clearly intends; then fit with the power as first described; then balance.
- The DM may trim a power slightly to keep the game fun, and says so plainly.
- Nothing over the top. Latitude is earned.

## What never happens on Day 2

- The DM does not decide what the god is like. It asks.
- No rolled leans for the player's god. The god is what the human playing it is.
- The player's god gets no Keeper trait.
- The two hidden powers are not mentioned. The Keeper assigns them on Night 2 and the god discovers them in play.

## Follow-up questions

Few. Woven into the conversation, in the voice of The Between. (oog) when the question is mechanical and can't be asked in the fiction.

## At the close

Read the power card back to the player in plain words, every power, drawback, edge-case ruling and trade, and get a yes. Then write to the repo:
- the power card
- the appearance card
- the backstory outline, in the player's words
- the first lines of the conduct profile
- any lean in the god's total power, for the Keeper
- a note of any premise the player rejected, for the Keeper's follow-ups

Then the night falls, and the god keeps falling.
