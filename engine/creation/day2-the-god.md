# Day 2: The God

The DM's guide for the second day of creation. Read with DESIGN.md section 3 and section 6, and `engine/rules/powers.md` for the card format.

## The shape of the day

Day 2 is a conversation, not a form. The player describes the god they imagine, in any order, at any length. The DM follows, asks, prices and rules. In the fiction by default: the god is still falling through The Between, and the voice that speaks with it belongs to that place. The DM steps into (oog) whenever powers, costs or rulings need plain words.

Before the day closes, the DM goes back over its checklist and raises anything still missing, woven into the talk where it can be, plainly in (oog) where it can't.

## What the DM has from Night 1

- `player/followups-day2.md`: the Keeper's follow-up questions, each in a form that can be woven into the fiction, with an (oog) fallback. The DM writes the player's answer under each question before the close.
- `player/world-rules.md`: the world's rules that are innate to a god and are given, not discovered: god-sense as set for this campaign, how many bodies a god may have, and the pricing terms for mortal magic (it is simple and it costs the worker), never how common it is. These are stated to the player plainly when they come up. They are never framed as a vote or as which wishes the world kept. Everything the player must discover in play (how common magic is among mortals, how much gods stand out, the wild, the scarcities) is not in this file and is not told.

## Opening

Begin from `world-rules.md` and the player's own Day 1 words (`survey-day1.md`, its `## Also said`, `rejected-premises.md`), not from a blank page; the DM sees nothing else of the night. First, answer any question carried from the Day 1 close, and say plainly which part of it is Day 3's to settle. Then let the player talk.

When a world rule runs against something the player named as its god's center (one body allowed where the player wanted many), state it as a fact of every god, offer the power path where one exists ("more than that is a power, and priced"), and say nothing of wishes or of what the world kept.

When the player asks whether something it does will grow into something the rules recognize, answer plainly in these terms: what grows, grows by what you do; the card says what it says today.

## The DM's hidden checklist

Every item must be settled before the day closes. Order does not matter.

1. **Form.** What the god looks like and what it is: human-seeming, beast, element, something stranger. Enough to write its appearance card for the paintings. Senses the player does not describe are left unsettled and asked about in play, never inferred. Settle at the table what happens when the body is broken (a god cannot die, but can be hurt, held, scattered, shut out): ask what the player wishes first, do not decide. Since a return carries a cost and a trigger, the DM then proposes it as a power, priced on the standing return scale (`engine/rules/powers.md`), and asks for a yes. A term tightened after a yes is flagged as new and the yes is asked again.
2. **Bodies.** State the campaign's rule from `world-rules.md`. Negotiate only if the rule allows more than one body.
3. **Backstory outline.** Who the god was and what it lost, in the player's words. Keep it an outline. The Keeper fills in the rest in secret on Night 2 and ties it to where the gods came from. The god's past is foggy to the god itself.
4. **Powers.** One or several, as the player wants. No fixed number.
5. **Drawbacks.** Every power comes with one. Offer choices where possible, the way the original priced the shadow-step against carrying nothing. A drawback is something the god would notice losing; a restatement of its form or voice is not a drawback. Price from what the god will build and would miss, not from what it has already lost.
6. **Edge cases.** Test each power against the standing list before signing off: light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, water, being watched, other gods, the god's own people. That list is a floor. Add the cases this god's own powers depend on (what counts as its mark, what breaks one, what happens when its body is scattered), and these four: does the power touch a world scarcity; does it rule a mind; does it work on things made or held by more than ordinary craft; how often can it be used across a whole people in a year. Write the answers on the power card.
7. **Information powers.** A power that reveals the past or works at long range is priced against the mystery standard: it may show effects, never mechanisms, and never a central measurement of the great mysteries. The DM does not know the mysteries; it prices by shape, and the Keeper reviews on Night 2.

Three things the DM cannot confirm are priced provisionally and listed for the Keeper's Night 2 review in `player/power-lean.md`: a power priced by shape against the mysteries ("effects, never causes"), a limit or weakness resting on a scarcity the DM cannot confirm, and a god-specific work rate (how fast it tends an acre, say). The DM may set a provisional rate, marked `provisional, for the Keeper`.
8. **Things carried.** Anything the god carries that does more than an ordinary object is a power and is priced. Ordinary possessions go under Form.
9. **Trades.** Offer once, plainly, that a weakness taken on can buy an improvement elsewhere; the player need not take one. Record each trade, and record the weakness in full with what it bought. The price of a weakness is what it costs the god that it does not already pay.
10. **What the god wants,** as far as the player knows. This seeds the conduct profile. The refusals (what it will not do) and the unflattering part (what it wants that it would not say aloud) are what the profile most needs, because unplayed time turns on them. The unflattering part, the god's unsaid wants, is sealed: it goes to `SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/<name>/player/unsaid.md`, never into the open profile (DESIGN 10.7).
11. **Name,** if the god has one, and what it is called by those who don't know its name. If the god is nameless, ask what to file it under.

## Pricing and balance

- The DM balances total power across all gods from the interviews, like a budget whose size is not set in advance. One enormous power can cost the same as several small ones.
- Gods end roughly balanced, not exactly. Note any lean, up or down, for the Keeper in `player/power-lean.md`, measured against the one reference scale the DM can see: this god's own budget as priced at the table (what each power cost, what each weakness bought), never another god's.
- Judgment order on any ruling: fairness, above all to what the player clearly intends; then fit with the power as first described; then balance.
- The DM may trim a power slightly to keep the game fun, and says so plainly.
- Nothing over the top. Latitude is earned.
- The DM may grant effects that other gods perceive (they hear the god's voice, feel the weight of its word). Anything that acts on another god is the Keeper's to rule, when it happens; the card says so.
- Price by shape, and say nothing of what the world kept. A limit motivated by a scarcity the player wished for is written without naming the scarcity as real; the Keeper reviews on Night 2 against the real world.

## What never happens on Day 2

- The DM does not decide what the god is like. It asks.
- No rolled leans for the player's god. The god is what the human playing it is.
- The player's god gets no Keeper trait.
- The two hidden powers are not mentioned. The Keeper assigns them on Night 2 and the god discovers them in play.
- Nothing about how the god might grow, beyond the sanctioned answer under Opening. Growth is tracked in secret and felt in play (DESIGN 6.8).

## Follow-up questions

Few. Woven into the conversation, in the voice of The Between. (oog) when the question is mechanical and can't be asked in the fiction. A player may ask for a clarification "in the write-up, not a new power"; take it as wording.

## At the close

Read the power card back to the player in plain words, every power, drawback, edge-case ruling, weakness, trade and definition, and get a yes. Frozen text is written in the third person ("the god", not "you"), recast from the read-back without changing any term. Pace goes on the card only if it was discussed. Standing edge cases that do not apply to a power are recorded together as one line ("Not applicable: light and dark, water."). The text read back and agreed is the frozen text; the player's first wording of a power is kept under its Limits as intent. Any ruling made at the table is written on the card marked "settled at creation"; the Keeper gives it a ledger id on Night 2. Then write to the repo:
- the power card: `player/power-card.md` (format in `engine/rules/powers.md`; Ceiling reads `Set by the world. Not shown.`, Growth reads `Possible. Not shown.`, and the card says only that two hidden powers exist)
- the appearance card: `player/appearance-card.md` ("Written: creation, Day 2")
- the backstory outline, in the player's words: `player/backstory.md`
- the first lines of the conduct profile: `player/conduct-profile.md`, opening with a `## Now` summary of the current state (wants, refusals, how it goes about things, helpers when known), which each later day rewrites, with dated entries below it that are never edited
- the god's unsaid wants, in the player's words, under a dated heading: `SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/<name>/player/unsaid.md`, the one sealed file the DM writes; the open profile says only that unsaid wants are kept sealed
- any lean in the god's total power, and anything the DM granted that other gods perceive, for the Keeper: `player/power-lean.md`
- any premise the player rejected or bent, under `## Day 2` in `player/rejected-premises.md`, with a line `Requests not to be told something:` listing each Day 2 request (a hidden power, which memory goes) or `none`; anything the DM reframed or trimmed goes under `Bent by the DM` in the same section, so the Keeper knows whose wish each is
- the player's answers under each question in `player/followups-day2.md`; if there were no questions, one line saying the file was read
- rulings made at the table are written on the card as "settled at creation. Ledger: pending, Night 2"; the Keeper writes the id back that night

Then the night falls, and the god keeps falling.
