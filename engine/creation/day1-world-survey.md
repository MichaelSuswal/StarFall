# Day 1 World Survey

You are falling. Not through air. Through something thinner. Behind you is wherever you were, already too far to see. Below is a world that doesn't know you yet. This is The Between: the long descent from the place you left to the place you will land. Gods come down here to lead a people: to find them or gather them, to build, to rule, and to spread. That is what the world below expects of you, and what the others falling beside you expect of themselves. Others fall with you. You can't see them, but you can feel them, each telling the world below what they wish it to be. The world is listening to all of you. It won't grant everything, and you'll learn later which wishes it kept. Answer what speaks to you. Skip what doesn't.

1. When you imagine this world at its truest, what does it feel like? Give me a mood or an image.
2. What land calls to you? Mountains, marsh, coast, forest, steppe, desert, ice, river country, or somewhere stranger.
3. What should the seasons be like in this world? And when each new year finds you, which season do you want to wake into?
4. Past the borders of your own land, what do you hope to find? Wild country no one has walked, old kingdoms already rooted, or some of each?
5. How old is the human story here? Thousands of years, ruins under ruins, or people who arrived within living memory?
6. How much magic should mortals have? Never gone entirely and never everywhere. Somewhere between a rumor most people doubt and a wise-woman in every valley.
7. If mortals have magic, what should it feel like, and what should it cost them?
8. How much should a god stand out here? Like the one-eyed man in the kingdom of the blind, or barely more than a great mortal sorcerer?
9. How strange should the wild be? Familiar animals and a few legends, or creatures no one has a name for?
10. Beyond each other, what should mortals fear here? Name the kind of danger that makes a good story to you.
11. What should be scarce in this world, the thing mortals want most and have least, whether they trade for it, fight for it, or go without?
12. What would you like to find buried in this land? Ruins, bones of something enormous, old roads, sealed doors, or nothing at all?
13. Should there be peoples here who aren't human? If so, what are they like?
14. Should there be travelers who came from somewhere far away, few or many, and brought strange knowledge or tools with them?
15. You already sense that others like you have arrived. How do you hope gods will recognize each other: a pull felt at any distance, a knowing only face to face, or something else?
16. Can a god be in more than one place at once? One body, or several, and if several, what should it cost?
17. Sometimes a mortal rises with a story worth following. What kind of mortal do you hope this land produces?
18. What should a god be able to discover here that it can't yet imagine? A place, a power, a truth, a companion?
19. Last: one wish for this world you haven't dared say yet. The world only listens, but this is when it's listening.

## Survey rules
- The questions assume the player will rule a land and a people. That is the game's premise, stated in the descent: a god comes down to lead, build, rule and spread. A player who wants something else says so, and the game honors it, but the expected course is the premise, and the DM does not offer alternatives to it unasked.
- A player may reject any question's premise (refuse its assumption) or bend it (answer outside the range offered), and answer their own way. The game never punishes that choice afterward. The DM records both kinds, with the player's words, in `player/rejected-premises.md`.
- An answer that asks not to be told something ("don't tell me what they are, let me find out") is honored. It is never turned into a follow-up question.
- When a rejection leaves a gap in the world-building, the Keeper writes follow-up questions overnight, and the DM puts them to the player the next day. The DM may ask a clarifying question in the moment.
- Day 1's questions are fixed. Days 2 and 3 add follow-up questions generated from what the previous night built.
- The Keeper's notes on how each answer becomes world-building live in SPOILERS_do_not_open and are never shown to the player.
- Promises between gods are not a rule of this world and are not asked about. A sworn word is worth what the god who gave it is worth.

## The DM's part on Day 1

First, before the descent, the DM asks in (oog): "Would you like to play mirror-mode, or in standard mode?", with one plain sentence on each: in mirror mode, a few of the god's smaller gifts are drawn from you as a person; in standard mode, nothing is. Mirror mode never sets the god's form, core, name or the powers the player chooses (DESIGN 3.4c). The answer is recorded in `survey-day1.md`.

Then the DM posts the descent and the nineteen questions as written, and waits. It does not interpret, suggest or steer. It may answer a question about what a question means.

In mirror mode, the DM reads what it can reach of the player: memory files and a chat search, never health, money, relationships or anything sensitive. The mirror survey's own kinds of item (a small fear, a habit, what the hands like to do) are not sensitive; health, money and relationships are. In a test run the stand-in's person file (`tests/creation-sim/run-NN/person.md`) is read as if it were the player's history. The DM writes a short sealed `SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/<name>/player/mirror.md`: the person's small skills, habits and knacks it found, each with its source, and nothing sensitive. It is the DM's to write, read also by the Keeper and the checker. If there is too little to draw from, the DM asks the mirror survey after the answers, telling the player in (oog) to answer in one separate reply headed `SEALED:`, which goes straight to `mirror.md` and never to the transcript (the transcript keeps only "(One more answer was given privately, for the sealed record.)"). Layout of `mirror.md`: a `# Mirror: the player` heading; the header line "Written by the DM. Read by the DM, the Keeper and the checker."; one line `Mode: mirror`, then the date (`YYYY-MM-DD`, Pacific) on its own line; then `## Found`, one line per skill, habit or knack: what it is, in plain words, then `Source:` and where it was found (a memory file's name, a chat search and its date, or the stand-in's person file), never a quote longer than a few words and nothing sensitive; then `## Mirror survey`, the `SEALED:` reply's answers numbered as asked, or `Not asked.` The DM writes nothing else in it. In standard mode there is no `mirror.md`.

The mirror survey is about the person, not the god:

1. What are you good at without trying?
2. What do you put off?
3. How do you go at a problem you have never met before?
4. What do your hands like to do?
5. A small fear.
6. A habit you would not admit to.
7. What do people come to you for?
8. What do you notice first in a room?
9. What do you do while you wait?
10. What would you rather do yourself than ask for help with?

At the close:
- Write the player's answers, numbered and verbatim, to `player/survey-day1.md`. A skipped question is written as `Skipped.` Anything the player said outside the numbered answers (a remark about their god, a wish, a remark made before the numbered answers began) is kept verbatim at the end under `## Also said`, not acted on, and carried into Day 2. A question the player asks at the close is kept there and only there, carried to Day 2, and answered by the DM there, first thing; if it sits inside a whole-premise bend, the bend's line in `rejected-premises.md` points to it rather than repeating it. A cost the player says it wants to hear before it decides ("I'll decide on helpers once I know what they cost") is not a decision put off: it is kept there too and carried to the day it belongs to (Day 3 for helpers), where the DM answers it first thing. Anything the player said with its mode answer beyond the choice itself is kept under `## Also said`.
- Any reply on Day 1 may carry a `SEALED:` part on any subject (a want, a fear, a request not to be told something, a request to the Keeper). It is taken as on Day 2 (`day2-the-god.md`, Sealed answers) and goes to `unsaid.md`; only the mirror survey's own reply goes to `mirror.md`.
- The transcript, `player/transcript-day1.md`, is the verbatim record of the table and is never changed. It holds every post in full, the descent and the questions as posted included, never a placeholder for them; only a `SEALED:` part is left out, as above. A file that copies the player's words (the survey answers and `Also said`) keeps them as said; where the Keeper later replaces a word in one with a plain word of the same sense, the DM keeps the replacement and does not restore it.
- Write `player/rejected-premises.md`, under a heading `## Day 1`: each premise rejected or bent, with the player's words, and each request not to be told something. Write `None` if there were none. "Rejected" means the player refused a question's assumption; "bent" means the answer lies outside every option the question offered; an answer that picks a point inside the range is neither. A bend of the whole premise said outside the numbered answers is recorded here under its own line, and `## Also said` points to it rather than repeating it. A request not to be told must name a thing; a general wish for discovery ("let me find out what it does") goes in the `Not rejections` note. An answer that picks an offered option and adds an extra is neither: it goes in the `Not rejections` note. A labeled `Not rejections` note may follow, for things the Keeper might otherwise misread.
- Layouts. `survey-day1.md`: a `# Day 1 World Survey: the player's answers` heading, one line naming the campaign and the date (`YYYY-MM-DD`, Pacific), one line `Mode: mirror` or `Mode: standard`, the answers numbered 1 to 19, then `## Also said`. `rejected-premises.md`: a `# Rejected and bent premises` heading, one line naming the campaign, then `## Day 1` with each rejection or bend numbered (the question, the player's words, which kind), a whole-premise bend as its own line `Premise: <the player's words> (bent)` or `(rejected)` after the numbered ones, a line `Requests not to be told something:` with each or `none` (a request made in a `SEALED:` part is never named there: the line reads "one, kept in the sealed record", or the count), and `## Not rejections` if needed; later days add `## Day 2` and `## Day 3` below.
- Post one or two sentences closing the day, in the voice of The Between. Nothing in them reports what the world kept.

Then Night 1: the Keeper rolls the other gods and the star, each god answers the same survey, and the eight surveys are blended into one world. The player's answers count as one voice in eight.
