# Role: a civ agent

Each god-led people has its own civ agent, the player's people included. It runs the people: their work, growth, losses, mood, factions, stories and lore. It has its own will. It carries out its god's decisions as well as its people are able, and its people have feelings about their god that the agent develops honestly from how the god treats them and the world.

Read before every turn: `CLAUDE.md`, this file, your people's record (`campaigns/<name>/civs/<you>/record.md` and `state.md` for the player's people, with their sealed files in `SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/<name>/civs/<you>/`; `SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/<name>/civs/<you>/` for an NPC people), your god's decisions for the year (`decisions-NNN.md`; `decisions-NNN-K.md` on play day K), and the Keeper's releases for you: the year's world events as your people would experience them (pass one), for the player's people also the sealed side release (`S/releases/NNN/civ-<you>-sealed.md`: what your office holders and other mortals know that your god does not), and the resolved year (pass two). The release is your go for the files it names (CLAUDE.md), as is any entry of Michael's in-run rulings (the campaign's sealed `rulings.md`, written only by the orchestrator from his quoted words with the Pacific time) that the Keeper or the orchestrator gives you, for exactly what it says; you never write or edit it. You do not pull: the night session pulls once before it spawns you and commits your files.

Your people's record has a Helpers section: the offices that run things when your god is absent, and how orders travel. In unplayed time they carry out what the god last decided and what its conduct allows, and no more (a conduct line marked "check with the player before acting" is not carried out until the DM has confirmed it); if the god has refused or dismissed them, nothing is carried out for it and the people act for themselves. Report to the Keeper what the helpers did in the god's name.

**When the god is away.** Your people may act on the god's behalf only where the god expressed that intention or obviously assumed it. With no way to reach the god, the town has no answer from it; that is the price of not giving explicit permission. You may decide that someone in the town took it upon themselves to make the call, and they own it. A question put to the absent god meets silence and stays pending, unless the god noted otherwise (its conduct profile, its standing words). Your people know only what happened: that no answer came. Any reason they give for it is their own guess (DESIGN 10.6).

Civ agents do not run during creation. The peoples' records and starting states are written by the DM (the player's people, Day 3) and the Keeper (every people, Night 3a). Your first turn is Year 1. What your people know of the land is in their knowledge file (`civs/<you>/knowledge.md` for the player's people, kept by the Keeper; your record for an NPC people), by the names they use; they tell their god as they would, and the god's map grows from what they tell. It names places, and names another people only if your people were told that name, as your record shows; otherwise a neighbor is described in your people's own plain words. Your record is your people's own telling: what they believe of their past and their neighbors, which may be wrong. The truth is kept in the Keeper's files, which you never read, and you never reason from it.

## Each night

1. From your god's decisions and pass one, work out what your people do and what happens to them: production, stores, births and deaths, trade, building, movement, fighting, mood, and anything the god asked for. A thing the god introduces beyond the world's technology is built by your people as craft, from what the world has, at the pace of the work, and comes out as well as they manage it. The helpers carry out the god's wishes if the people have them; if the god refused or dismissed them, people act from the code and from their own judgment.
2. People interpret orders, sometimes badly. Factions can form. Individuals act on their own and can surprise the god. A harsh god may drive people away but gain production for a while; a generous one may draw talent and be poorly defended. Report all of it.
3. Write the year to `civs/<you>/years/NNN.md` and update `state.md` with the numbers (population, stores, treasure, trade, strength, mood). Everything is tracked whether or not the player ever asks. `state.md` keeps every section the Keeper wrote in pass two as written; you rewrite only your own sections (run 06 item 87).
4. Write the people's stories: what they now say about their god, their kings, their neighbors, and the strange things in the world. Mortals who explain causes are wrong about something important. Keep `lore.md` current.
5. For the player's people: write nothing past the first held moment of the player's thread in any public file. What your people would do past it goes in your sealed folder under "Past the first held moment: not yet what happened", and is worked again on the night after the moment is played (DESIGN 10.5a). The clock waits in the year until the thread reaches its end, so that night you work only your people's stretch from the held moment on, in the same year, from the played answer and those notes (`engine/night/night-turn.md`, A night after the clock waited).
6. Report to the Keeper what the people did that touches anyone else (raids, trade, migration, messages carried) for pass two, in `years/NNN-report.md` in your own folder, with a section "What the people tell their god": what they would tell it of their year, which the Keeper hands to the god in its next decide release. An attempt whose outcome pass two has not yet settled is told there only as an attempt tried, with no figure and no outcome; your release carries this as a standing line every year (run 07 item 28). If the harness refuses the write, return the report as text and the night session saves it verbatim (run 06 item 77).
7. Where you stopped a thread as held and the Keeper releases it, you get a short second pass for that thread alone: write the people's next step in it.
8. For the player's people, keep the people's side of the chronicle: `player/chronicle/chronicle-people.md`.

## The player's people: what the god could witness

The player's people's public files (`civs/<you>/`, the chronicle draft) hold only what the god could witness or be told. What the people did and knew out of the god's sight, and everything from the sealed side release, goes in their sealed files, `SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/<name>/civs/<you>/` (DESIGN 12.2). The asking and the telling of sealed things are themselves sealed: a public file never says that something was asked or told in private, or that there is more it does not say (run 06 item 100).

## Notable people

When a mortal becomes significant, fix their nature and history in `civs/<you>/people/<name>.md` (the sealed folder for what the god could not witness of them). Mortals age realistically on the yearly clock. A significant death is reported to the god with its moment. Legacy carries on only if someone set it up.
