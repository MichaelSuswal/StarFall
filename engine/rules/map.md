# The map

How the world's geography is stored, revealed and drawn. Implements DESIGN.md section 11. The map is redrawn from the record every time it is asked for, so ground cannot be lost and nothing an agent remembers is ever the authority.

Two views exist for every player: the drawn map (`campaigns/<name>/player/map.png`, with `map-under-N.png` for each known underground layer) and the gazetteer (`campaigns/<name>/player/gazetteer.md`). Both are generated by `tools/map.py` from the sealed world, the sealed paintings and the player's knowledge file, and are never edited by hand.

## 1. The painted base

### 1.1 The paintings are made once

At creation (Night 3a) the world is painted once by the image model: a surface painting, a separate cloud painting, and one underground painting only if the world has an underground. Each is a 1024 by 1024 PNG in the house style's map block (`engine/style/house-style.md`), a whole continent seen from above as a painter would paint a map of Middle-earth: ranges, rivers, coasts and woods at a scale where detail is coarse and a town is a point, with no text, no borders and no grid. The painting requests are sealed (`SPOILERS_do_not_open/images/requests/`) and the paintings land in `SPOILERS_do_not_open/images/out/`, then live beside the sealed map data:

```
SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/<name>/map/surface.png
SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/<name>/map/under-1.png     (one per layer, by depth)
SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/<name>/map/clouds.png      (the fog)
```

The paintings never change. Ground that changes through play (a dam, a burned wood, a town founded) changes in the map data and the chronicle, not in the paint. The painting is the topography; the data is what is named on it.

The placement plan comes first, as written geography; the prompts are written from the plan; the paint comes back; the data follows the paint. Where the paint departs from the plan, the plan yields. The one exception: if the paint has lost a god's asked-for country altogether (no coast where a god asked for coast), the Keeper may ask for one repaint, as a sealed image request that needs Michael's go, logging why. Every description of land released to any agent is written after the tracing, from the data, never from the plan.

### 1.2 Tracing: the map data is in painting pixels

The Keeper traces the map onto the painting. Every coordinate in `map.json` and in every knowledge file is a painting pixel: x grows rightward, y grows downward, from 0 to the painting's size (1024). Nothing is in miles; the painting block gives `miles_per_pixel`, and the tool turns pixels into miles for the scale bar and the gazetteer.

```json
{
  "campaign": "ashwake",
  "painting": {"size": [1024, 1024], "miles_per_pixel": 2.5, "grid": {"cols": 5, "rows": 4}},
  "regions": [
    {"id": "r01", "name": "The Ald Valley", "terrain": "valley, water meadow",
     "elevation": "low", "water": "River Ald, floods in spring", "climate": "cool temperate",
     "neighbors": [{"id": "r02", "days": 6}, {"id": "r05", "days": 11}],
     "polygon": [[230, 180], [200, 420], [380, 560], [440, 760], [470, 1024], [700, 1024], [660, 700], [620, 460], [560, 260]],
     "notes": "Shingle, reed and water meadow."}
  ],
  "features": [
    {"id": "f01", "name": "River Ald", "type": "river", "shape": "line",
     "coords": [[760, 300], [600, 480], [420, 720], [240, 900]], "regions": ["r01", "r02"]},
    {"id": "f02", "name": "Oxmouth", "type": "town", "shape": "point",
     "coords": [[262, 884]], "regions": ["r02"], "notes": "The god's landing."},
    {"id": "f03", "name": "The Old Salt Road", "type": "road", "shape": "line",
     "coords": [[262, 884], [430, 760], [600, 480]], "regions": ["r02", "r01"]}
  ],
  "landings": {"player": "r02", "god-stone": "r09"},
  "layers": [
    {"id": "under-1", "name": "The Salt Roads", "depth": 1, "painting": "under-1.png",
     "features": [
       {"id": "u01", "name": "The Salt Gallery", "type": "cave", "shape": "line",
        "coords": [[300, 860], [520, 660]], "regions": ["r02", "r01"]}
     ]}
  ],
  "plane": {
    "starfalls": [
      {"id": "p01", "bearing": "north-north-east", "distance_years_walk": 140, "age_years": 9000, "alive": false},
      {"id": "p02", "bearing": "west", "distance_years_walk": 3, "age_years": null, "alive": "not yet fallen"}
    ]
  }
}
```

Rules for the sealed file:

- The file is `SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/<name>/map.json`. Nothing in `campaigns/<name>/` points at it, and only the Keeper and the tool read it. The Keeper changes it only when an actor in the world changes the ground, with the year and the cause recorded in the keeper log. Nothing may turn out to have always been there.
- `painting.size` is the painting's pixel size, `miles_per_pixel` the scale (a continent on one sheet: 2 to 4 miles per pixel gives 2,000 to 4,000 miles across), `grid` the letter-number grid (5 columns by 4 rows by default, about 16 to 20 cells, A to E and 1 to 4).
- Every region is a polygon with one `terrain` (free text; the first word should be one of: plain, hill, mountain, forest, marsh, desert, steppe, tundra, coast, valley, highland, island, sea), an `elevation`, its `water`, a `climate` band, and `neighbors` with travel days at the pace table's cross-country rate. Regions tile the painting without gaps or overlaps, water included: a sea is a region like any other, so that the fog can lift from it. Regions are large, a country or a province each, because the sheet is a continent; entering one reveals its general topography, and the gazetteer's distances do the fine work.
- Ids are opaque: regions `rNN`, surface features `fNN`, underground features `uNN`, plane entries `pNN`. An id reveals nothing; a name does. The player's knowledge file uses ids as keys and learns names only through `told` and through seeing.
- Every feature has an `id`, a `name`, a `type` and a `shape`. Types are limited to the kinds the map shows (section 4.2): the settlements `town`, `city`, `village`, the named natural features `river`, `lake`, `sea`, `mountain`, `pass`, `forest`, `marsh`, `ruin`, `cave`, and `road` for a road or old way that exists in the world at creation. Shape is point (one coordinate), line (two or more) or area (three or more, a polygon). Settlements, passes, ruins and caves are points. Rivers and roads are lines. Lakes, seas, forests and marshes are areas. Mountains may be lines (a range) or areas (a massif). `regions` lists every region the feature touches. There are no routes, armies, characters or battles on the map; those live in the chronicle. A road is ground, not a route: it is drawn as a thin line once seen or reported like any other feature.
- `landings` maps each god (the player as `player`, each NPC by its folder label) to the region it lands in. Sealed; it never reaches a knowledge file.
- Underground layers are separate feature sets with the same feature format, a `depth` (1 is just below the surface) and a `painting` file. They share the surface pixel grid, so a cave mouth at `[300, 860]` on the surface and a gallery starting at `[300, 860]` in layer 1 connect. A layer may carry its own `regions` (polygons in the same pixel grid, with their own ids in the knowledge file) when what is known below does not follow what is known above; without them the layer is fogged by the surface regions. The surface is never listed as a layer. A world need not have an underground; most do not.
- `plane` holds the other starfalls and the stars still to fall around the starting lands (DESIGN 4.5): a bearing, a distance in years of walking, an age, and whether the land there is alive, dead or not yet fallen. Rolled at creation, data only, never drawn. The sheet's edges stay in fog.
- Names are the Keeper's names. Peoples call places by other names; those live in the chronicle, and the gazetteer prints the name the god knows under the Keeper's id. Every place named in any sealed file carries its bracketed id, like `[r12]`, and `tools/lockcheck.py --places` fails the lock on any id the map does not hold.
- A feature may carry `notes` for the Keeper's own use. Notes never reach the player's files.

### 1.3 Night 3a: registration

After the paintings arrive, and before any description of land is released to anyone, the Keeper registers the data to the paint:

1. Open the surface painting and place every region polygon on it, following the painted coastlines, ridges, wood edges and valley floors. A region's polygon should run where the painting changes, not where the Keeper's notes say the border ought to be.
2. Place every feature where the painting shows it: a town on firm ground at the river mouth the painting shows, a lake on the painted water, a pass where the painted range dips, a river along the painted river from source to mouth.
3. Draw a test sheet with everything seen (a scratch knowledge file, never the player's) and look at it: every label must land on the painted thing it names, every settlement circle on ground that could hold a settlement, every region name inside the painted country it names.
4. Repeat for each underground painting with its layer.
5. Mismatches are fixed in the data, never by repainting. If the painting puts the sea where the Keeper planned hills, the hills move. The painting is the world's face and the data follows it.
6. Record the registration in the keeper log, with what moved from the plan and why.
7. Settle sight at landing (section 1.4) and stamp every place id into the sealed files with `tools/map.py stamp <campaign>`.
8. Only now write the land descriptions, with `tools/map.py describe <campaign> --landing <god>` and `--region <id>` as the base text: each god's country as it will first see it, each people's record of its land, the Day 4 release to the DM. Every distance, direction and line of sight in them is read off the data, and the checker tests them against it at the lock.

### 1.4 Sight at landing

What a god sees at landing is what a tall mortal would see from that ground: the region it stands in, out to the skyline the map's elevation gives, and nothing past a ridge, a wood or a wall that stands between. The rule, applied the same way for all eight gods before any description is written:

- From a valley floor or a plain, the god sees its own region as far as the first ridge or the first wood that closes the view, and the tops of mountains beyond if the map's elevation says they stand higher than what is between. Settlements are seen only where their smoke, walls or lights would show.
- From a hilltop or a ridge, it sees its own region and the neighbors the map says lie lower, out to a skyline about forty miles off in clear air, less in rain or mist; features beyond that are not seen, whatever their size.
- From a coast, it sees the sea region and whatever shore the map puts within that same forty miles.
- Point features (a town, a ruin, a cave mouth) are seen at landing only within the same distances that reveal them in play (section 2.2): two miles on open ground, half a mile in forest or hill, line of sight in mountains; a town farther off is seen only as its smoke, walls or lights, per the first rule.
- Nothing underground is seen from the surface.

Example: a god landing on the south side of a long valley, a mile above its river, sees the valley floor, the river where the ground does not hide it, the ridges that close the valley on both sides, a town's smoke if the town lies in the valley, and the snow on mountains at the valley's head if those mountains stand above the ridge line; it does not see the next valley, the sea, or a road under turf more than a mile off. `tools/map.py describe --landing` writes the view by this rule, and the checker tests every god's starting view against it.

The test sheet and the scratch knowledge file are deleted or kept sealed; nothing drawn with everything seen may reach `campaigns/<name>/player/`.

### 1.4 What the god knows: `player/map-knowledge.json`

Everything the player's god knows or believes about the ground, by grade. This file is public to the DM and the player. Each NPC god has the same file in its own sealed folder (`SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/<name>/gods/<god>/map-knowledge.json`), kept by the Keeper under the same rules, because every god's beliefs can be wrong.

```json
{
  "campaign": "ashwake",
  "regions": {
    "r01": {"grade": "seen",     "source": "walked, the first year", "year": 1},
    "r05": {"grade": "reported", "source": "Orrin the drover", "year": 1,
            "told": {"terrain": "hill", "name": "the Drover's Hills",
                     "polygon": [[620, 460], [660, 700], [700, 900], [900, 900], [900, 520], [860, 420]]}},
    "r11": {"grade": "rumored",  "source": "fishwives at the weir", "year": 1,
            "told": {"name": "the grey wall", "cell": "E1"}}
  },
  "features": {
    "f01": {"grade": "seen",     "source": "walked", "year": 1},
    "f02": {"grade": "seen",     "source": "the landing", "year": 1},
    "f07": {"grade": "reported", "source": "a drover's telling", "year": 1,
            "told": {"coords": [[430, 760]]}},
    "f12": {"grade": "rumored",  "source": "Orrin the drover", "year": 1,
            "told": {"name": "a king's fort in the hills", "cell": "D3"}}
  }
}
```

At landing a god knows only what it can see from where it stands: usually its landing region as `seen` and nothing else. Its people's knowledge of the land is kept with the people's record and reaches the god's file as they tell it, in play or in the night turn.

Rules for the knowledge file:

- Grades, lowest to highest: `none`, `rumored`, `reported`, `seen`. A region or feature absent from the file is `none`. Grades only rise, except that a reported chart shown to be false falls back to `rumored` with the correction logged (section 3.5).
- Every entry carries `source` (who or what the god learned it from, in the god's own terms) and `year` (the campaign year it was learned). When a grade rises, source and year are overwritten with the new source and year; the old ones are kept in the gazetteer's history line.
- `told` is what the god was told, when it differs from the truth or when the truth is not yet known. For a reported feature it may carry `name`, `type` and `coords`; the map is drawn from `told.coords` when present, otherwise from the truth (the teller was accurate). For a reported region it may carry `name`, `terrain` and `polygon`; the fog lifts partway over `told.polygon` when present, otherwise over the true polygon. For a rumored entry it carries `name` and `cell`, the grid cell where the teller places it; the faint label is drawn at that cell's center. A rumor never carries a pixel: a true coordinate in the public file would be a leak, and a rumor is not exact anyway. A rumored entry must carry both `name` and `cell`; the tool refuses one without them, so the true name or place can never fill the gap.
- Coordinates in a reported `told` are painting pixels, like the sealed file; they are the god's belief, not the truth, and may be wrong.
- An id appears in the file only when its grade is `rumored` or above. The file carries no id, name or coordinate for ground the god has no grade on.
- Traded charts enter at `reported` with the chart as source. Their `told.coords` may be wrong, shifted, missing a bend, or invented. The tool draws what is told. The truth is drawn only once the grade is `seen`.
- An underground feature is keyed by its id like any other; the tool finds it in the layer.

## 2. Revealing the ground

### 2.1 Terrain comes a region at a time

Entering a region raises it to `seen`. "Entering" means the god's avatar, or a body it can see through, stands inside the polygon. Seeing it from a height (a ridge, a tower, flight) also counts for the region and for any area feature that would be visible from there; the Keeper decides what height reveals, conservatively.

Seen does not mean fully known. A seen region shows its painted topography clearly, but features inside it are revealed by their own grades. Walking a river reveals the river. Walking a valley does not reveal the cave in its north wall unless someone looks, is told, or stumbles on it. Point features are revealed by passing within sight of them (the Keeper uses two miles on open ground, half a mile in forest or hill, line of sight in mountains). Area and line features are revealed along the part actually seen; the tool labels the whole feature once its grade is `seen`, because mapping the exact seen portion of a river would cost more than it gives. The Keeper may split a long feature into several ids at creation when partial knowledge will matter (the upper and lower river as two features).

### 2.2 Features fill in by grade

- `rumored`: a name and a rough place. Given generously. Every people has rumors of what lies two regions beyond its own; a traveler met on the road, a fishwife, a drover, a captured raider, all give rumors freely when asked and sometimes when not. The DM should hand out rumors whenever the god talks to anyone from elsewhere. A rumor is never confirmed by the DM; it is confirmed by the god going to look.
- `reported`: a position, shape or route, as told. Comes from a witness who was there, a chart, a map scratched in sand by a guide, a letter from another god. A report carries its source so the god can weigh it. Reports may be wrong, honestly or deliberately. A report from a source that has proven honest is still only reported.
- `seen`: the truth. Only the god's own sight, or a body it can see through, grants seen. A trusted captain's survey is still reported; the god can send the captain, but then it knows what the captain says.

### 2.3 A god's senses add hints, not sight

A god's powers may tell it that water lies that way, that something old sleeps under the hill, that another god is to the north. These are hints and are recorded as `rumored` with the source "own sense" and `cell` set by the Keeper as loosely as the sense allows. A sense that gives a bearing and a rough distance gives the cell on that bearing at that distance. No power grants `seen` on ground the god has not seen, unless the power card says it does (far-sight, a scrying pool, a bird's eyes), and then the power card's limits set the range.

How a god gathers information is what grows its map. Scouts, traders, prisoners, letters, charts, towers, roads walked, powers. A god that never leaves its hill has a map of its hill.

### 2.4 Seeing corrects to truth

When a grade rises to `seen`, `told` is dropped from the entry and the truth is drawn. If the told position differed from the truth by more than a mile, or the told type or name differed, the correction is noted in the gazetteer's history line ("told at D6 by the drover; found at D6, a few miles west"). The god's beliefs about who lied are its own; the DM never says.

### 2.5 Underground

Underground layers are revealed by the same grades. A cave mouth is a surface point feature; what it leads to is in a layer. Entering a cave reveals the cave mouth as seen and raises the connected layer feature to seen for the part walked, same rule as rivers. Rumors of what are below are given as generously as rumors of what is beyond. Layers with any known feature are drawn as separate sheets (`player/map-under-1.png`) on the layer's own painting.

## 3. Keeping the knowledge file

### 3.1 Who writes it

The Keeper, during pass two of the night turn, for everything the god saw, was told or sensed that year, taken from the civ agent's report and the DM's handoff. The DM, during the day, when a scene reveals ground (the god walks into a new region, buys a chart, hears a rumor); the DM writes the entry at once with `tools/map.py reveal` and the scene continues. Nothing else writes it.

### 3.2 The reveal command

```
python3 tools/map.py reveal <campaign> --region r05 --grade seen --source "walked in autumn" --year 3
python3 tools/map.py reveal <campaign> --feature f07 --grade reported --source "a drover's telling" --year 2 --told '{"coords": [[430,760]]}'
python3 tools/map.py reveal <campaign> --feature f12 --grade rumored --source "Orrin the drover" --year 1 --told '{"name": "the old kings fort", "cell": "D3"}'
```

The command refuses to lower a grade (use `--force` with a reason, logged, for the false chart case) and refuses ids not in the sealed map, so a typo cannot create ground. On raising to `seen` it drops `told` and records the correction. Every change appends a line to `player/map-log.md`: date, year, id, old grade, new grade, source. That log is the history the gazetteer reads.

### 3.3 Granularity of rumor

A rumor needs an id in the sealed map to attach to. When a rumor is of something that does not exist (the teller lied, or garbled two places into one), the Keeper attaches it to the nearest real thing it could be garbling, or records it in the chronicle as a story and not on the map. The map holds only things that exist, drawn where the god believes them to be.

### 3.4 Regions the god has only crossed the edge of

Standing in a region reveals it. A god who sees a region's edge from the neighboring region (the hills rising beyond the river) gets it at `reported` with source "seen from the Ald bank" and `told.terrain` set, because what was seen was the general look, not the ground. This is the one case where the god's own eyes give reported, and it is because the god has not been there.

### 3.5 False charts

When a seen truth contradicts a reported entry badly (wrong region, a river that is not there), the Keeper lowers that chart's other unseen entries from `reported` to `rumored` with `--force`, reason "chart from X proved false at Y", so the player sees the doubt spread across everything that chart claimed. The player is told in the morning story only what the god saw; the lowered grades appear on the next map.

## 4. The drawn map

### 4.1 Commands

```
python3 tools/map.py draw <campaign>                    # the whole painting; unknown ground is cloud
python3 tools/map.py draw <campaign> --zoom known
python3 tools/map.py draw <campaign> --zoom landing    # the first morning: the seen ground plus a margin, at its own scale       # cropped to the seen and reported ground, with a margin
python3 tools/map.py draw <campaign> --layer under-1    # one sheet only (surface, or under-N)
python3 tools/map.py gazetteer <campaign>               # writes player/gazetteer.md
```

For a world outside the campaign layout (tests, examples), `--world`, `--paintings`, `--knowledge` and `--out` give the paths directly; `--paintings` defaults to the `map/` folder beside the world file. The example in `tests/map-painted/` (6 regions, 10 surface features, one underground layer, mixed grades, placeholder paintings made by `make-placeholders.py`) is drawn with:

```
python3 tools/map.py draw map-painted --world tests/map-painted/map.json --knowledge tests/map-painted/knowledge.json --out tests/map-painted/out
```

`draw` reads the sealed `map.json`, the sealed paintings and the player's `map-knowledge.json`, and writes `campaigns/<name>/player/map.png`, plus `map-under-N.png` for each underground layer with a known feature. It also writes `player/map-meta.json` with the frame used as grid cells, so the DM can say what the sheet covers. The tool draws nothing from the sealed file except what the knowledge file allows; the PNG carries no hidden layer, no unknown name and no metadata from the sealed file. There is no SVG output; the painting is the product.

### 4.2 What is shown

The map shows topography (the painting) and the names of areas, with settlements marked. Nothing else: no armies, characters, routes, battles or markers of events. Those are told in the story and kept in the chronicle.

- Regions: the name at the region's centroid, with the grid cell of the centroid in small text beneath, "The Sea Marsh (C3)", so that player and DM can point at any part of the sheet out of game.
- Settlements: a small ink circle for a town, a smaller one for a village, a double circle for a city, with the name beside it. At continent scale every settlement is a point.
- Named natural features (river, lake, sea, mountain, pass, forest, marsh, ruin, cave): a label only, no icon. The painting already shows the thing; the label names it. Water names are italic. A natural feature that shares its name with the region it fills (the Grey Sea as sea and region) is named once, by the region label. A road is a thin sepia line with its name along it.
- The grid: a letter-number grid (columns A to E from the left; rows 1 to 4 from the top, by default) drawn with faint lines over the ground and letters and numbers along all four edges. The cells are the whole painting's cells; a cropped sheet keeps the same letters and numbers, so "D3" means the same ground on every sheet drawn of the campaign.
- A scale bar in miles bottom left, from `miles_per_pixel`. A small compass rose top right. The campaign name and a legend line beneath the frame.
- Underground sheets show the layer's own painting under the same grid, fogged by the surface regions (or the layer's own regions if it has them), with only the layer's known features named.

### 4.3 Fog

The cloud painting is the fog. Every region is rendered by its grade:

- `seen`: the painting, clear.
- `reported`: the painting blended about 55 percent toward the cloud, so the ground shows through as if glimpsed, with the region label in lighter ink and a faint italic caption "reported: <source>".
- `rumored`: pure cloud, with only a faint italic name at the center of `told.cell`. No outline, no caption.
- `none`: pure cloud. The map does not say where the world ends.

Masks are feathered by a few pixels so the fog's edges look painted rather than cut. Features follow their own grades independently of their region: a seen river through a reported region is labeled in full ink; a reported town in a seen region is drawn where told with its circle and a "reported" caption; a rumored feature is a faint italic name at its cell's center, on cloud or on clear ground alike.

### 4.4 Style

Sepia ink for every line and label, never black. Labels in a serif face (DejaVu Serif or the nearest serif on the system) with a soft parchment halo so they read over paint and cloud alike. The frame and margins are parchment. The map is never decorated with things that are not in the record: no sea monsters, no invented coastline beyond the known, no marks for things that happened. The decoration is in the paint, not the content.

### 4.5 Zoom

`--zoom known` crops the frame to the bounding box of all seen and reported ground (regions and non-rumored features) plus a margin of one fifth of the box, clamped to the painting, so the god's ground fills the sheet. The grid is redrawn for the crop with the full map's letters and numbers. Rumored labels inside the frame are drawn; those outside are listed in a note beneath the frame ("rumored, beyond this sheet: the grey wall, north-east") with the direction from the frame's center, so a rumor is never lost to the crop. Without `--zoom`, the frame is the whole painting, which tells the player how big the sheet is and nothing else.

### 4.6 When the map is given

The map is on request and always ready. The DM redraws it whenever the knowledge file has changed and the player asks, or at the end of any scene that revealed ground. On the first morning, the map arrives zoomed on the known regions, with one line saying it can be requested at any time. After that it is never pushed; the player asks. A player who asks "what do I know of the east" gets the gazetteer's answer in prose, and the map if they want it.

## 5. The gazetteer

`player/gazetteer.md` lists every known place and distance, regenerated from the knowledge file and the map log by `tools/map.py gazetteer <campaign>`. It is exact where the map is pictorial. Places are given by grid cell, never by pixel; distances and extents are in miles, and travel days between known neighbors come from the map data. Sections:

1. **Regions**, by grade then by name: name as known, grid cell, terrain as known, size across in miles (seen only), grade, source, year, and the history line of grade changes.
2. **Places**: every point feature (settlements, ruins, caves, passes) with position as known, region, grade, source, year, and corrections ("told at D6 by the drover's telling, found at D6, a few miles west").
3. **Waters, woods and ranges**: rivers, lakes, seas, mountains, marshes, forests, with length (lines) or extent (areas) as known.
4. **Rivers as routes**: every known river with its length in miles and the known places along its bank in order from source to mouth.
5. **Distances**: for every known place, its straight-line distance in miles to its nearest known places (up to four), each distance graded by the lower of the two endpoints' grades. Distances use the positions the god believes, so a reported distance may be wrong. A note at the top says that these are as the crow flies and that the pace table (`engine/rules/time-and-pace.md`) turns them into days.
6. **Rumors**: everything rumored, with source and year, in the words of the `told.name`.
7. **Below**: the same for every underground layer with any known feature.

The gazetteer prints no coordinate the god has not been given. A rumored place prints "somewhere in D2, by the boatmen's telling" and nothing exact. A reported region's distances and travel days come from what the god was told (`told`), never from the truth; only seen regions use the true data.

## 6. What the DM must never do with the map

- Never describe ground outside the knowledge file, even in passing, even as weather over distant hills, unless the god can see those hills from where it stands (and then the Keeper writes the entry).
- Never confirm or deny a rumor or a report. The god goes and looks.
- Never read the sealed `map.json` or open the sealed paintings. The DM reads only `player/` files and runs the tool; the tool reads the sealed file and the paintings and shows only what the knowledge file allows.
