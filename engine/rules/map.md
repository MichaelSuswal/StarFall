# The map

How the world's geography is stored, revealed and drawn. Implements DESIGN.md section 11. The map is redrawn from the record every time it is asked for, so ground cannot be lost and nothing an agent remembers is ever the authority.

Two views exist for every player: the drawn chart (`campaigns/<name>/player/map.svg`) and the gazetteer (`campaigns/<name>/player/gazetteer.md`). Both are generated by `tools/map.py` from two data files and never edited by hand.

## 1. The two files

### 1.1 The Keeper's world: `map.json` (sealed)

The complete world, surface and underground, fixed on Night 3 when the world locks. In the campaign layout it is the file `campaigns/<name>/world/map.json`, but because it holds the whole world it is sealed and actually lives at `SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/<name>/map.json`. Nothing in `campaigns/<name>/` points at it, and only the Keeper and the tool read it. The Keeper changes it only when an actor in the world changes the ground (a dam, a burned forest, a new road, a town founded or abandoned), with the year and the cause recorded in the keeper log. Nothing may turn out to have always been there.

Coordinates are a plain x,y grid in miles. x grows eastward, y grows northward. The origin is wherever the Keeper puts it; the extent is the campaign region (DESIGN.md 4.6, roughly Europe up to Eurasia). Polygons are lists of `[x, y]` points, closed implicitly.

```json
{
  "campaign": "ashwake",
  "units": "miles",
  "extent": {"x": [0, 1200], "y": [0, 900]},
  "regions": [
    {"id": "ald-valley", "name": "The Ald Valley", "terrain": "river valley",
     "polygon": [[100, 60], [140, 55], [150, 90], [110, 100]],
     "notes": "Shingle, reed and water meadow. Floods in spring."}
  ],
  "features": [
    {"id": "ald", "name": "River Ald", "type": "river", "shape": "line",
     "coords": [[150, 120], [135, 90], [112, 70]], "regions": ["ald-valley"]},
    {"id": "weirtown", "name": "Weirtown", "type": "town", "shape": "point",
     "coords": [[118, 72]], "regions": ["ald-valley"], "notes": "The god's landing."}
  ],
  "layers": [
    {"id": "under-1", "name": "The Deep Roads", "depth": 1,
     "features": [
       {"id": "salt-gallery", "name": "The Salt Gallery", "type": "cave", "shape": "line",
        "coords": [[118, 72], [160, 90]], "regions": ["ald-valley", "hill-country"]}
     ]}
  ]
}
```

Rules for the sealed file:

- Every region is a polygon with one `terrain` (free text, but the first word should be one of: plain, hill, mountain, forest, marsh, desert, steppe, tundra, coast, valley, highland, island). Regions tile the land without gaps or overlaps. Regions are 20 to 60 miles across, smaller than in the original game, so that entering one reveals a day or two of walking, not a province.
- Every feature has an `id`, a `name`, a `type` from: river, lake, sea, mountain, road, town, ruin, cave, forest, marsh, pass. Its `shape` is point (one coordinate), line (two or more) or area (three or more, a polygon). Rivers and roads are lines. Lakes, seas, forests and marshes are areas. Mountains may be lines (a range) or areas (a massif). Towns, ruins, caves and passes are points. `regions` lists every region the feature touches.
- Underground layers are separate feature sets with the same feature format and a `depth` (1 is just below the surface, larger is deeper). They share the surface grid, so a cave mouth at `[118, 72]` on the surface and a gallery starting at `[118, 72]` in layer 1 connect. The surface is never listed as a layer.
- Names are the Keeper's names. Peoples call places by other names; those live in the chronicle, and the gazetteer prints the name the god knows under the Keeper's id.
- A feature may carry `notes` for the Keeper's own use. Notes never reach the player's files.

### 1.2 What the god knows: `player/map-knowledge.json`

Everything the player's god knows or believes about the ground, by grade. This file is public to the DM and the player. Each NPC god has the same file in its own sealed folder (`SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/<name>/gods/<god>/map-knowledge.json`), kept by the Keeper under the same rules, because every god's beliefs can be wrong.

```json
{
  "campaign": "ashwake",
  "regions": {
    "ald-valley":   {"grade": "seen",     "source": "walked, the first year", "year": 1},
    "hill-country": {"grade": "reported", "source": "Orrin the drover", "year": 1,
                     "told": {"terrain": "hill", "name": "the Drover's Hills"}},
    "salt-flats":   {"grade": "rumored",  "source": "fishwives at the weir", "year": 1,
                     "told": {"name": "the burning hills", "near": [300, 40]}}
  },
  "features": {
    "ald":      {"grade": "seen",     "source": "walked", "year": 1},
    "weirtown": {"grade": "seen",     "source": "the landing", "year": 1},
    "salt-road": {"grade": "reported", "source": "a trader's chart bought in Weirtown", "year": 2,
                  "told": {"name": "the Salt Road", "type": "road",
                           "coords": [[118, 72], [200, 40], [290, 45]]}},
    "old-fort": {"grade": "rumored",  "source": "Orrin the drover", "year": 1,
                 "told": {"name": "a king's fort in the hills", "near": [190, 110]}}
  }
}
```

Rules for the knowledge file:

- Grades, lowest to highest: `none`, `rumored`, `reported`, `seen`. A region or feature absent from the file is `none`. Grades only rise, except that a reported chart shown to be false falls back to `rumored` with the correction logged (section 3.5).
- Every entry carries `source` (who or what the god learned it from, in the god's own terms) and `year` (the campaign year it was learned). When a grade rises, source and year are overwritten with the new source and year; the old ones are kept in the gazetteer's history line.
- `told` is what the god was told, when it differs from the truth or when the truth is not yet known. For a reported feature it may carry `name`, `type` and `coords`; the chart is drawn from `told.coords` when present, otherwise from the truth (the teller was accurate). For a reported region it may carry `name`, `terrain` and `polygon`. For a rumored entry it carries `name` and `near`, a single `[x, y]` where the teller places it; the faint label is drawn there. If a rumored entry has no `near`, the label is drawn at the true centroid, so the Keeper should set `near` whenever the rumor is vague about place.
- Traded charts enter at `reported` with the chart as source. Their `told.coords` may be wrong, shifted, missing a bend, or invented. The tool draws what is told. The truth is drawn only once the grade is `seen`.
- An underground feature is keyed by its id like any other; the tool finds it in the layer.

## 2. Revealing the ground

### 2.1 Terrain comes a region at a time

Entering a region raises it to `seen`. "Entering" means the god's avatar, or a body it can see through, stands inside the polygon. Seeing it from a height (a ridge, a tower, flight) also counts for the region and for any area feature that would be visible from there; the Keeper decides what height reveals, conservatively.

Seen does not mean fully known. A seen region gives its general topography with some uncertainty: the polygon and terrain are drawn exactly, but features inside it are revealed by their own grades. Walking a river reveals the river. Walking a valley does not reveal the cave in its north wall unless someone looks, is told, or stumbles on it. Point features are revealed by passing within sight of them (the Keeper uses two miles on open ground, half a mile in forest or hill, line of sight in mountains). Area and line features are revealed along the part actually seen; the tool draws the whole feature once its grade is `seen`, because mapping the exact seen portion of a river would cost more than it gives. The Keeper may split a long feature into several ids at creation when partial knowledge will matter (the upper and lower river as two features).

### 2.2 Features fill in by grade

- `rumored`: a name and a rough place. Given generously. Every people has rumors of what lies two regions beyond its own; a traveler met on the road, a fishwife, a drover, a captured raider, all give rumors freely when asked and sometimes when not. The DM should hand out rumors whenever the god talks to anyone from elsewhere. A rumor is never confirmed by the DM; it is confirmed by the god going to look.
- `reported`: a position, shape or route, as told. Comes from a witness who was there, a chart, a map scratched in sand by a guide, a letter from another god. A report carries its source so the god can weigh it. Reports may be wrong, honestly or deliberately. A report from a source that has proven honest is still only reported.
- `seen`: the truth. Only the god's own sight, or a body it can see through, grants seen. A trusted captain's survey is still reported; the god can send the captain, but then it knows what the captain says.

### 2.3 A god's senses add hints, not sight

A god's powers may tell it that water lies that way, that something old sleeps under the hill, that another god is to the north. These are hints and are recorded as `rumored` with the source "own sense" and `near` set by the Keeper as loosely as the sense allows. A sense that gives a bearing and a rough distance gives `near` on that bearing at that distance. No power grants `seen` on ground the god has not seen, unless the power card says it does (far-sight, a scrying pool, a bird's eyes), and then the power card's limits set the range.

How a god gathers information is what grows its map. Scouts, traders, prisoners, letters, charts, towers, roads walked, powers. A god that never leaves its hill has a map of its hill.

### 2.4 Seeing corrects to truth

When a grade rises to `seen`, `told` is dropped from the entry and the truth is drawn. If the told position differed from the truth by more than a mile, or the told type or name differed, the correction is noted in the gazetteer's history line ("reported by the trader's chart at 290,45; found at 262,58"). The god's beliefs about who lied are its own; the DM never says.

### 2.5 Underground

Underground layers are revealed by the same grades. A cave mouth is a surface point feature; what it leads to is in a layer. Entering a cave reveals the cave mouth as seen and raises the connected layer feature to seen for the part walked, same rule as rivers. Rumors of what is below are given as generously as rumors of what is beyond. Layers with any known feature are drawn as separate sheets (`player/map-under-1.svg`) with the surface drawn faintly beneath for bearings.

## 3. Keeping the knowledge file

### 3.1 Who writes it

The Keeper, during pass two of the night turn, for everything the god saw, was told or sensed that year, taken from the civ agent's report and the DM's handoff. The DM, during the day, when a scene reveals ground (the god walks into a new region, buys a chart, hears a rumor); the DM writes the entry at once with `tools/map.py reveal` and the scene continues. Nothing else writes it.

### 3.2 The reveal command

```
python3 tools/map.py reveal <campaign> --region hill-country --grade seen --source "walked in autumn" --year 3
python3 tools/map.py reveal <campaign> --feature salt-road --grade reported --source "a trader's chart" --year 2 --told '{"coords": [[118,72],[200,40],[290,45]]}'
python3 tools/map.py reveal <campaign> --feature old-fort --grade rumored --source "Orrin the drover" --year 1 --told '{"name": "a king's fort", "near": [190,110]}'
```

The command refuses to lower a grade (use `--force` with a reason, logged, for the false chart case) and refuses ids not in the sealed map, so a typo cannot create ground. On raising to `seen` it drops `told` and records the correction. Every change appends a line to `player/map-log.md`: date, year, id, old grade, new grade, source. That log is the history the gazetteer reads.

### 3.3 Granularity of rumor

A rumor needs an id in the sealed map to attach to. When a rumor is of something that does not exist (the teller lied, or garbled two places into one), the Keeper attaches it to the nearest real thing it could be garbling, or records it in the chronicle as a story and not on the map. The map holds only things that exist, drawn where the god believes them to be.

### 3.4 Regions the god has only crossed the edge of

Standing in a region reveals it. A god who sees a region's edge from the neighboring region (the hills rising beyond the river) gets it at `reported` with source "seen from the Ald bank" and `told.terrain` set, because what was seen was the general look, not the ground. This is the one case where the god's own eyes give reported, and it is because the god has not been there.

### 3.5 False charts

When a seen truth contradicts a reported entry badly (wrong region, a river that is not there), the Keeper lowers that chart's other unseen entries from `reported` to `rumored` with `--force`, reason "chart from X proved false at Y", so the player sees the doubt spread across everything that chart claimed. The player is told in the morning story only what the god saw; the lowered grades appear on the next map.

## 4. The drawn chart

### 4.1 Commands

```
python3 tools/map.py draw <campaign>                 # the whole frame; unknown ground is parchment
python3 tools/map.py draw <campaign> --zoom known    # cropped to the known regions, with a margin
python3 tools/map.py gazetteer <campaign>            # writes player/gazetteer.md
```

`draw` reads the sealed `map.json` and the player's `map-knowledge.json` and writes `campaigns/<name>/player/map.svg`, plus `map-under-N.svg` for each underground layer with a known feature. It also writes `player/map-meta.json` with the frame used, so the DM can say what the frame covers. The tool prints nothing from the sealed file except what the knowledge file allows; the SVG contains no hidden elements, no unknown names, and no comments carrying the truth.

### 4.2 What is drawn

- `seen` regions: the polygon in full, terrain tinted and textured, every `seen` feature inside drawn exactly and labeled.
- `reported` regions: the polygon as told (from `told.polygon` if present, else the truth), hatched more lightly, with a small italic "reported" mark and the source under the region's name.
- `rumored` regions: a faint italic label at `near` (or the true centroid), no outline, no fill.
- `none`: nothing. Fog is parchment. The chart does not say where the world ends.
- Features follow the same scheme independently of their region's grade: a seen river through a reported region is drawn exactly; a reported town in a seen region is drawn where told with its mark. Rumored features are faint labels at `near`.
- Underground features are drawn only on their layer's sheet.

### 4.3 Style

Old hand-inked chart, but readable. Parchment background (warm cream with a faint darker edge), sepia ink for every line, no pure black. Terrain fills are thin washes: pale green for plain and valley, tan for hill and steppe, grey-brown for mountain, dark olive for forest, blue-grey for marsh, sand for desert. Mountains are simple hatching: small inverted chevrons in rows along a range or filling a massif, heavier on the south-east face as if lit from the north-west. Water is wavy lines: three or four parallel waves inside lakes and seas, and rivers as a single wavering line widening toward the mouth. Roads are dashed. Forests are small round tree marks. Marshes are short horizontal dashes in groups of three. Towns are a small filled square with a dot, ruins a broken square, caves a half circle open downward, passes two short diagonals. Labels are a serif font (Georgia or the viewer's serif), upright for regions and settlements, italic for water, small capitals for seas. A scale bar in miles sits bottom left and a compass rose (eight points, N marked) top right. Reported marks are small italics in a lighter sepia. Rumored labels are the same italics at 40 percent.

The chart is never decorated with things that are not in the record. No sea monsters, no invented coastline beyond the known. The decoration is in the ink, not the content.

### 4.4 Zoom

`--zoom known` crops the frame to the bounding box of all seen and reported regions plus a margin of one fifth of the box, so the god's ground fills the sheet. Rumored labels inside the frame are drawn; those outside are listed in a small note at the frame's edge ("rumored, beyond this sheet: the burning hills, east") with the direction from the frame's center, so a rumor is never lost to the crop. Without `--zoom`, the frame is the whole extent of the world, which tells the player how big the sheet is and nothing else.

### 4.5 When the map is given

The map is on request and always ready. The DM redraws it whenever the knowledge file has changed and the player asks, or at the end of any scene that revealed ground. On the first morning, the map arrives zoomed on the known regions, with one line saying it can be requested at any time. After that it is never pushed; the player asks. A player who asks "what do I know of the east" gets the gazetteer's answer in prose, and the chart if they want it.

## 5. The gazetteer

`player/gazetteer.md` lists every known place, route and distance, regenerated from the knowledge file and the map log by `tools/map.py gazetteer <campaign>`. It is exact where the chart is pictorial. Sections:

1. **Regions**, by grade then by name: name as known, terrain as known, size across in miles (seen only), grade, source, year, and the history line of grade changes.
2. **Places**: every point feature (towns, ruins, caves, passes) with position as known, region, grade, source, year, and corrections ("reported at 290,45 by the trader's chart in year 2; seen at 262,58 in year 3").
3. **Waters and ranges**: rivers, lakes, seas, mountains, marshes, forests, with length (lines) or extent (areas) as known.
4. **Routes**: every known road and river as a route, with its length as known in miles and the known places along it in order.
5. **Distances**: for every known place, its straight-line distance in miles to its nearest known places (up to four), each distance graded by the lower of the two endpoints' grades. Distances use the positions the god believes, so a reported distance may be wrong. A note at the top says that these are as the crow flies and that the pace table (`engine/rules/pace.md`) turns them into days.
6. **Rumors**: everything rumored, with source and year, in the words of the `told.name`.
7. **Below**: the same for every underground layer with any known feature.

The gazetteer prints no coordinate the god has not been given. A rumored place prints "somewhere near 300,40, by the fishwives' telling" and nothing exact.

## 6. What the DM must never do with the map

- Never describe ground outside the knowledge file, even in passing, even as weather over distant hills, unless the god can see those hills from where it stands (and then the Keeper writes the entry).
- Never confirm or deny a rumor or a report. The god goes and looks.
- Never read the sealed `map.json`. The DM reads only `player/` files and runs the tool; the tool reads the sealed file and prints only what the knowledge file allows.
- Never invent a route, a pass or a ford to get the god somewhere. If no known route exists, the god travels overland at the pace table's cross-country rate, through regions revealed as it enters them, or finds a guide who gives a report.
- Never adjust the chart by hand. If the chart is wrong, the knowledge file or the sealed map is wrong; fix the record and redraw.
