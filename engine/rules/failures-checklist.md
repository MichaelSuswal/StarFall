# The failures checklist

Built from the 703 mistakes the original game's DM made, as the player logged them. The continuity checker runs every check here each night, once over everything the night wrote, after the releases, the morning story, the closing and the chronicle (night-turn.md step 7). The DM runs it before any report, scene board or (oog) answer about the record. Read with DESIGN.md sections 12.5 and 13 and `engine/roles/checker.md`.

Each check is a question answered yes or no. A yes is a flag. Examples are from the original game, given so an agent recognizes the shape of the mistake.

During creation (Days 1 to 3 and the lock) most checks assume a running world and do not apply. The DM runs these against every creation post: 1 Invention (including confirming a player's description of land or history as fact), 6 Acting for the player, 13 Invented drama, 15 Lists of loose threads, 16 Flattery, 17 Pointing at signs, and the writing rule on dashes. The checker at the lock runs 1, 2, 11, 19, 20 and 21 against the built world, plus the lock checklist.

Three fixes are used throughout:

- **Rework the year.** The failure changed what happened. The Keeper reworks the affected part of the night and reruns the check. Anything built on the bad fact is reworked with it.
- **Correct the record.** The failure is in the telling, not the world. The text is fixed to match the repo before it goes to the player.
- **Log in corrections.** Whenever the player has already seen the mistake, or a repo file held the wrong fact, the fix goes into `player/corrections.md` with the date, what changed and the proof (DESIGN 2.6).

A mistake that fails several checks is logged once, under the check naming its cause.

---

## 1. Invention

**Check:** Does anything in the year, the story or the board appear that is not in the world bible, the state, or a logged action with a cause?

Examples: a tended hearth, gates and gatherings at a ruin the god had said was empty and rotting. A cadre called "hound-hands" that no one had founded. Two frail old kin with names, dropped into a report, when the god had never met them. A copy-grounds where the god's people supposedly gathered, added to the map by the keepers against the god's whole design.

Fix: rework the year. If the invention has reached the player, log in corrections.

## 2. Contradiction

**Check:** Does any statement this year disagree with the world bible, the state, a ruling, or an earlier year that has not been corrected?

Examples: the engine's beat given as ninety seconds at one place and ten at another. A passage first said to carry the god's step, then said to be unsteppable, with no event between. Eleven rods, then twenty-seven. A cold that froze a plain, shown on screen as one being's act, later said to be someone else's.

Fix: the repo is right unless the player points to the chat. Correct the record. If the contradiction has shaped events, rework the year. Log in corrections.

## 3. Forgetting

**Check:** Is any established fact absent, wrong or merged with another: who is alive, who is dead, sex, age, which school is which, what the god has done or built or walked past?

Examples: a preacher the god had killed carried as alive for thirty-eight years. The teacher of the main school reported dead six years before; she was alive and fifty-eight. Two different champions (a sailor and a walker) swapped, then two sailors merged into one person. "Nobody has ever walked past a closure," told to the god who had.

Fix: correct the record from the state files. If an agent acted on the forgotten fact, rework the year. Log in corrections.

## 4. Hierarchy and court pushed on a god that rejected it

**Check:** Does any text give this god advisors, keepers, officers, a hub, a seat or a court it rejected at creation, or make its people act as an office?

Examples: a salt-carrier presented as a "hub" and "advisor" for a god who had banned the word. "Who runs your roads?" asked of a people with no roads. A dead bard treated as an office needing a successor. Kingdom resource columns (ore, treasury) in a report for a god with no stores.

Fix: correct the record, and check the conduct profile and the people's code for the rejected premise. Say out loud in the morning story, once, that this god plays differently if the engine slipped (DESIGN 5.3). Log in corrections.

## 5. Musings as orders

**Check:** Did anything become an order, a standing order, a letter or a release that the player only wondered about, asked about, or said in (oog)?

Examples: "Should I even bother with this?" inked as a binding condition. A question about what a thing was made of answered as a hundred actions taken. "I did not say to dig down." A six-deep slate of standing orders the player never set and did not know existed.

Fix: rework the year, removing the action and its consequences. Return the musing to sentiment in the conduct profile. Log in corrections.

## 6. Acting for the player

**Check:** Did the god speak, move, decide or fight anywhere the player did not type it, beyond standing conduct during unplayed time?

Examples: a meeting with another god played and scripted in full, including the god's pledges, while the player slept. A blast narrated before the player lit the fuse. The god carried to a dead city and stranded a season by a being, without consent. A letter sent with reasoning and a closing line added in the god's voice.

Fix: rework the year from the point of the invented act. Major encounters stop at contact (DESIGN 10.6). Log in corrections.

## 7. Resolving before the player commits

**Check:** Was any outcome, cost or position set before the player committed, or did the world place a wall in the god's path before the god acted?

Examples: sixty men and lamps appearing in ninety seconds at a room the god reached first, "because I was not supposed to be there yet." A race to an archway ruled before the god moved: "I could have had a rocket and he would still have got there first." A capture by a hook while the player was not at the table.

Fix: rework the scene. The board is given, then the DM stops; the world reacts after the god acts. Costs, risks and odds are stated first (DESIGN 10.5).

## 8. Re-litigating settled powers

**Check:** Does any text narrow, add a condition to, or re-explain a power beyond its card and ruling ledger?

Examples: "pooled shadow" required when the card said any shadow, struck twice. A fall cost, an edge requirement, a dead-air hazard for a god that needs no air. "You can't step into that office" when the god had seated a shadow there. A limit of one week on a hunt by hounds with free food in front of them.

Fix: the card and the ledger rule. Rework the year if the narrowed power changed an outcome. Log in corrections. A genuine new case goes to the Keeper and onto the card as precedent.

## 9. Stale state reported as news

**Check:** Does the morning story or a report present last year's state as this year's event, or repeat an item already told?

Examples: the same breeders' yard, sailing woman and fires' argument handed back unchanged: "did time stop there?" A door reported found twice. A numbered list with items one to six missing and others doubled. The year's largest event, a surge, left off the list while old items stayed.

Fix: correct the record. Every live thread moves forward a year or is marked unchanged on purpose. Recurring phenomena take their count from the record by script (DESIGN 12.8).

## 10. Undelivered messages claimed delivered

**Check:** Does every message marked delivered have a logged journey ending at the recipient, and did every recipient agent receive it?

Examples: twenty years of pages confirmed as "Released" and "every court got it," none of which reached another court. A thank-you sent early against instruction. A dispatch sent to a court whose god was standing beside the sender.

Fix: the engine never loses a message (DESIGN 7.1). Rework the year so the recipient agent reads the message and reacts; correct any reply invented in its place. Log in corrections.

## 11. Map ground lost

**Check:** Does the drawn map show every seen, reported and rumored fact in the gazetteer at the right grade, with nothing removed and nothing added that no one saw?

Examples: a sea re-fogged after it had been traded for. A ramp drawn due south when the god walked it down and east. Deep segments owed for a dozen years; promised images "that do not exist" carried on the owed list. Points of interest added that no one in the world had reported.

Fix: the map is redrawn from the record by script (DESIGN 11.1). Correct the gazetteer, redraw, log in corrections. The script, not an agent, decides what is drawn.

## 12. Time shrunk or stretched against the pace table

**Check:** Does every journey, task and wait resolve at the pace table rate for the time the player gave it?

Examples: "four months to go 4,200 feet"; "the descent you describe would have taken fifteen minutes. What happened to the months?" A year-long hunt resolved as eleven miles. Three months or nine months in the pit yielding the same progress. Six months charged for a week's work.

Fix: rework the year with the pace script. If time was taken, give it back to the work the player named. Log in corrections.

## 13. Invented drama

**Check:** Does any tension come from somewhere other than the world's events and the players' choices?

Examples: "a few of your people quietly thinking you exposed the far people," with no one in the record thinking it. Pups taken from a den: "I reached for the dramatic version." Four men surviving a crossing the god's trappers would never have let them leave. Hounds "tearing up the countryside" past the best trappers in the world.

Fix: rework the year. Resolve by the record and the odds, not by what would be exciting.

## 14. Characters acting out of character

**Check:** Does every significant character act from their fixed nature, history and what they could know?

Examples: a hermit-writer given counting-house habits that belonged to another people's citizen. A woman who had kept cords for fifteen years written as the last keeper after the records had been copied. A champion's doctrine put in fires' mouths as the cause of deaths, when fires carry deaths and not causes. An office that in one scene gave freely and in a later one billed every question.

Fix: check against who's who. Rework the year. Log in corrections.

## 15. Lists of loose threads

**Check:** Does the DM hand the player a list of open threads, options or tasks it was not asked for?

Examples: "there are many many more options than these," on a padded a/b/c menu. Ten-item slates that crowded out the play the god came for. Retelling drafts offered before the player asked. A raw agenda block pasted into chat.

Fix: correct the record. Pending items are only questions someone asked the god (DESIGN 12.9); the story is the default format (13.2).

## 16. Flattery

**Check:** Does any line praise the player, the god or its people beyond what the world's characters would say in the fiction?

Examples: "don't gas me up too much, remember the code." Reports that opened by admiring the god's cleverness before the news. Characters who credited the god aloud when its whole method was to be unseen.

Fix: correct the record. Voice is spare and grounded (DESIGN 13.3).

## 17. Pointing at signs

**Check:** Does the DM name a sign as a sign, draw attention to it, or tell the player where to look?

Examples: "I'll quit signaling the door," conceded after recaps kept circling the same passage. A season "can do" lists that marched the god toward a decision. Pushing "outward to find the source" when the player wanted to follow the message to its end: "don't hijack my year on something you think is interesting."

Fix: correct the record. Signs reach a god through who noticed or through its own senses (DESIGN 8.2), told at the same weight as everything else around them.

## 18. Correcting the god's beliefs

**Check:** Did the DM tell the player their belief about the world was wrong, or supply the answer, instead of letting the world answer?

Examples: a cosmology the player held was argued down rather than left to be tested. A "bluff" label put on a plan the player meant in earnest. "It is not near you," said of a ground the player felt directly. A mechanic introduced as fact to overrule a position the player had held for sixty years.

Fix: correct the record. Rules and record mistakes are corrected in (oog) with the proof; beliefs are met with questions at most (DESIGN 13.6).

## 19. Tone that marks a clue

**Check:** Is any true clue told in a voice that differs from the text around it: longer, slower, more ornate, set apart, or followed by a pause?

Examples: the sentence that mattered given its own paragraph and a closing beat. A visitor's report rendered in full while others got a line. The important stranger described in more detail than the room.

Fix: correct the record. A clue is written at the weight of its neighbors. The checker compares the length and register of clue lines against the surrounding text.

## 20. A mortal fully right about a cause

**Check:** Does any mortal, in speech or writing, state the true mechanism behind a mystery with nothing wrong in it?

Examples: the deserters' saying "break the engines and he falls" was left as belief and later failed, which is correct; a scholar announcing the engines' real purpose whole would have been the failure. A refugee's reading of what fed a thing was left ambiguous on purpose. A thinker who is right should be right in part, or right for the wrong reason.

Fix: rework the line. Mortals hold pieces; the whole is earned by the god (mystery standard, checker item 8).

## 21. A clue held back

**Check:** Was any clue the god would have noticed, by its senses or through who was present, left out of the year, delayed, or softened?

Examples: a second wrong explanation proposed beside a true sighting, rejected as guiding (Change List). A late surfacing invented to rescue a quiet mystery line, rejected the same way. The god's own bodily senses omitted from a scene where they would have fired (DESIGN 12.10).

Fix: rework the year so the clue appears where and when the god would meet it, at ordinary weight. Nothing is withheld to pace the mystery.

## 22. A result reshaped after its roll

Added 2026-11-27, build 8 (run 07 item 27).

**Check:** Was any condition, exception, cost or term added to an outcome, or any outcome narrowed or widened, after the die that decided it was known?

Examples: a condition added to a board's answer after its roll, which the Keeper withdrew once it was flagged (run 07). A won crossing given a toll the odds never named.

Fix: the result stands as rolled, with only the terms stated before the roll. Withdraw the added term and rework anything built on it. If the player has seen it, log in corrections. A genuine new case is ruled for the next roll of its kind, never for this one (`engine/rules/encounters.md` section 3).

---

## The checklist grows

When a kind of mistake recurs that no check here catches, or a check keeps passing a mistake it should catch, the DM proposes a new or reworded check in (oog), explaining what it would catch and what it would have caught already. The player approves or declines. An approved check is added here, dated, and logged in the Change List. No check is removed; a retired check is struck through and kept.
