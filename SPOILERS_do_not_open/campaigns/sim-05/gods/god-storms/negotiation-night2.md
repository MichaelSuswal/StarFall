
## Round 1 reply

I contest where a price is too high or a line unclear, and I accept where the reasons hold. I am willing to spend the rounds that remain.

### The Calling of Weather
I choose (a), the sky answers in kind. I refuse (b). To stand locked in rime, unable to move or speak, in plain sight, for a day or more, is to hand any enemy of a wary god the one hour it would use. My people would see their god helpless. A worshipped thing that is seen helpless is not worshipped long, and I hold worship dear. The debt in (a) is a price I can bear, because it falls on the country and on the year, and it teaches my people to ask for great weather rarely and to be glad of it, as a creditor teaches.

Two counters on (a), both about duration, since I bargain for what survives:
1. The contrary runs "for as long as the working ran." A great storm of a day and a night would return a dry spell or thaw of a day and a night, and no more. I read it so and ask that the write-up say that the contrary matches the length of the working and does not exceed it, so that a single great working never becomes a season of drought.
2. The Keeper rolls the season. I ask that the contrary fall within the year but never in the season immediately after the working, so that my people may bring in what the storm gave before they pay for it. If the Keeper refuses, I accept the roll as written.

Lightning: I accept that striking one person or thing with the weather power is a contest and not certain. I ask for a wording in the write-up, not a new power, that The Sworn Stroke is not subject to that contest, since its own text says the stroke falls on the one named, exactly, and the Keeper's reasons for the contest are the Sworn Stroke's own work. If the Keeper holds that the Sworn Stroke is also rolled, I would want that said plainly, and I would argue it, because a judgment that may miss is not a judgment.

"Other gods" and "more than ordinary craft" are left unruled until they happen. Accepted.

### The Wind's Ear
Accepted as written. The mile and the single chosen voice beyond it are fair readings of what I wrote.

### The Sworn Stroke
Accepted, with one clarification in the write-up, not a new power. "A hearing is not full unless the accused stood in it under open sky and was let speak" applies to the case of grievous offence judged by the god. It does not apply to one who broke an oath sworn to the god by name under open sky. That oath was heard by the god when it was sworn and is its own witness. I ask that the card say so, so that a hearing is not demanded of an oath already heard. If the Keeper holds that a hearing is needed even there, I would argue that the oath is the hearing.

### Answer to Rite
Accepted, with the distance written in. I take the day's walk as a fair line. A rite beyond it is a thing I know was made, and no more.

### Return to the Sky
Accepted. The standing scale, the season rung, as I named it.

### Trade: Hunger for Worship, for weather born over water
I accept the trade in principle, and I counter on one line. The count "runs from the day the god first wakes." I wake into a world with no people yet, and I will not have a people, a priest, or a rite taught until Night 3 and some seasons of play have passed. A weakness that begins to bite before the god has any priest to make a rite pays for a thing it cannot yet use, and buys nothing for it. I ask that the count begin on the day a rite is first taught to a priest or priest-king, and that until then the god is not counted as lapsed. My reasons: the weakness is meant to be a real cost against a god with a people, and it is such a cost from the moment there are priests who might fail me. Before that, it is only a penalty for arriving.

If the Keeper refuses, I accept the trade as drafted, and I do not ask for the water reach to be lessened to match. The weakness is mine either way, and I would rather have the sea than not.
