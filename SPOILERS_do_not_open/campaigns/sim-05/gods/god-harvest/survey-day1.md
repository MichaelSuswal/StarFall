## Answer 1
A granary at dusk with the doors thrown open and every bin full. Gold dust in the light, the smell of cut grain and split fruit, and a long line of people at the door, each with something to trade and each leaving satisfied. A world where nothing is wasted and everything has a price that both sides can live with. Under it, though, I hear an older sound, an empty hall where a name used to be said. I would have this world remember what it owes.

## Answer 2
River country, with fat black soil on both banks, and a wall of hills behind it to keep the wind off. Flood plains that feed a great many mouths. Give me one wide river, a ford worth taxing, and enough open ground for fields to the horizon. I will take a little marsh at the edges if it comes with the bargain, but the plain is what I am buying.

## Answer 3
Four seasons, honest and in order, each paying what it owes. A spring that is wet enough to be worth planting into. A summer long enough to ripen what was planted. An autumn generous and slow, so the reaping is never rushed. A winter hard but not cruel, so that stores are tested and counted, and so that people feel what they owe the ones who filled the bins. I want the year to keep its books straight.

When each year finds me, I want to wake into the turn of summer to autumn, when the first sheaves come in and the counting begins.

I wake into late summer, at the edge of the harvest.

## Answer 4
Some of each, and I will say plainly which I want more of. Old kingdoms already rooted, with granaries and tax rolls and priests, because a rooted people have something worth trading with, and something worth owning. And past them, wild country no one has walked, because I want somewhere to send my people when the first land is full. A world with only one of those is a poor market.

## Answer 5
Thousands of years, with ruins under ruins. I want deep soil that has been farmed and fallen and farmed again, and old altars buried under new fields. A people with a long memory has long debts, and long debts are what I understand best. Let there be gods here who were once loved and are now only a name on a broken stone. I will not say why I want that.

## Answer 6
Premise bent: closer to the wise-woman in every valley than I think you expect, but with a limit. I want magic common enough that a village can bless a field, name a blight, and be heard when it asks. Not so common that a priest is unnecessary. Never gone entirely, never everywhere. A hedge-witch for each few hundred households, and temples for the rest.

## Answer 7
It should feel like a bargain, because it is one. A mortal asks, and the world asks back. Warmth in the hands, a taste of salt or iron, the sense of a debt being written in a ledger they cannot read. The cost should be something they notice and cannot haggle down: a season of their strength, a year off the end of a life, a memory of a face, a name. The cost should be real and should be counted. Magic that costs nothing makes fools of the people who use it.

## Answer 8
A god should stand out plainly. Let there be no confusion about it. Not a great sorcerer. I want a mortal to look at a god and know in their body that something older and larger is in the room, and to kneel before they have decided to. But not so far above that a god cannot be bargained with, cheated by a clever mortal, or hurt. A king among the blind, who can still stub a toe.

## Answer 9
Familiar animals, with a few legends, and one or two things at the edges that no one has a name for. A name is the first thing you take from a creature, and I would like some left for the taking. I am curious, I will admit it, and I would like to meet something I cannot yet price.

## Answer 10
Hunger, first. A bad year, the grain gone to rot, the stores empty, the neighbors looking at each other and counting. After that, being forgotten: a village that dies without anyone left to say its name. After that, the thing in the dark that does not trade at all and will not be talked to. That last is the one I do not like to think about. Let the danger be the kind that does not bargain.

## Answer 11
Salt. And clean seed that breeds true. Salt keeps food through the winter and seed decides the next year, and a people with both are never helpless. A people without either will pay almost anything. Let those two be scarce, and let the roads that carry them be worth guarding. Trade for them, fight for them, I do not mind which, as long as the price is paid.

## Answer 12
Old roads, first, and under them a granary far too large for anything that has lived here since. Also a sealed door or two, so there is a reason to dig. And an altar, half sunk, with the face worn off the stone. I would like to find it before I know why I want to find it.

## Answer 13
Yes, a few, and not friendly by default. A people who farm the water's edge and pay in pearls and reed-cloth. A people of the high pastures who keep herds and will not sell a single one. One that is half beast, perhaps, and no one has decided if they are lesser or greater than the rest. Let them have their own customs, and their own prices. I would like to trade with someone who is not human, and learn what they will not part with.

## Answer 14
A few, and strange ones. I want travelers who came a long way, carrying a tool or a seed or a method no one here has seen. Let them be hard to understand and harder to trust. Few, so that what they bring is rare, and a rare thing has a price. I would buy what they know, if they would sell, and if they would not, I would learn how to ask better.

## Answer 15
A knowing only face to face. I do not want to feel the others from a distance, tugging at me. I want them to arrive, to be in the room, and to have to be dealt with. A pull felt at any distance is a debt I did not sign for. Let me see a god before I know a god, and let the first meeting be a negotiation, and let me watch their hands.

## Answer 16
One body. Let a god be in one place, and be known to be there. If several bodies are allowed, let the cost be heavy, and let it be paid in something that cannot be hidden: a part of the self left behind in each, thinner for every one. I am afraid of being spread thin. A god split too many times is a god no one can find, and a god no one can find is easily forgotten.

## Answer 17
One who keeps accounts. A mortal who rises because they can count what others cannot, and who sees what a people is owed and what it owes. Not a warrior and not a prophet, though either may serve. A steward, a tax-collector, a quartermaster, someone whose story is that they fed an army or a city through a bad winter and were rewarded or betrayed for it. I would follow that story closely.

## Answer 18
A name. Not mine. Someone else's, a true one, spoken once by someone who should not have spoken it. And I would like to find that a name is something that can be learned, and held, and traded, and grown on. After that, a companion who knew me before and does not mind that I have forgotten. I will not ask for more than that, on the first night.

## Answer 19
I wish to be remembered, and not only by the ones I have made. I wish that in this world a name, once spoken in worship, cannot be taken back out of the air. That whatever is built here in my honor is not left to rot, and that the one who built it is not forgotten by the child who inherits the field. I do not say it well. I have never been asked to say it.
