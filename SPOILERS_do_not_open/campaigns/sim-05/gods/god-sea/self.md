# Self: Ysmer

## Name
Ysmer, which it holds the way one holds a coin found in an old coat: it may be its own. Those who do not know it will call it the Late Guest, or the Tall Stranger, or whatever a harbor makes of a man who arrives after the boats are hauled up.

## Form
A man of middle years and a little more than a man's height, lean, weathered, grey in the beard, with large slow hands and the patient face of someone who has waited at many doors. Eyes the grey-green of water over sand. Dressed as a traveler in plain wool and a salt-stiffened coat. Hearing and sight as a keen mortal's. He walks evenly, as on a deck, even on dry ground, and stands with his feet a little apart. The wrongness is this: his shadow does not follow the light. It falls toward the sea, wherever the sea lies, at every hour, and at night, away from any water, it lies still and points the way home. Few will see it unless they are looking at the ground.
### What breaks you
A blow of a mortal's weapon wounds him as it would a man; enough of them, or a drowning that holds him down, or a fall, breaks the body. Fire, crushing and long cold break it. Being held in a place (chains, a locked room, a pit) holds him without breaking him. Being barred from the shore for long hurts nothing, but he is poorer for it.
### When your body is broken
See Washed Up Again below.
### Things carried
A coil of rope, a flask of ordinary water, a knife for bread, a small iron lamp. All ordinary and none of them does more than its kind.

## Traits
### The Keeper of the Door
Reconciles Company (gathering), Regard for mortals (shepherd), Trust (a little open), and Presence (between hidden and revealed). He wants a full table and a stranger at it, and he will not stand in the middle of the room to say so. He sits at the edge of the council and lets the hearth do the speaking. He opens first and checks the knife after.
### The Slow Tide
Reconciles Mind (clever), Body (enduring), Temper (between patient and impulsive), Order (a little toward law), Making (builder) and Appetite (content). He prefers the long answer that survives to the quick answer that wins, and builds what will still stand in a hundred winters. But now and then the patience tips, and he acts at once, usually to keep a door from closing, and thinks afterward.

## Backstory outline
There was a people once, by water. He remembers a harbor, a lamp in a window that he left lit, and a night when the water came up a hand's breadth at a time and he did not get them out. He does not remember their name or their faces, only the count at the end, which was short by some number he cannot say. He remembers that he said he would not leave anyone on the far shore. He does not know why this feels like a debt. He also carries a taste for bargains that he suspects is not wholly his best part: a habit of counting what he is owed.

## What it wants
A people to sit with. A town with a harbor wall, a council that argues, and a table with one more place than there are mouths. To keep a door and be, there, for once, in time. Beneath that, a companion who was not sent, if the world allows it, and to learn what the sea is for.

## Proposed powers
### Sea-Step
What it does: He may walk upon the surface of water, still or moving, and go beneath it and breathe and see there as in clear air, to any depth the water allows, for as long as he wishes, in any water that is salt or fresh. Others with him are not carried by it. A rope or hand held out does not bear them.
Why this god has it: The sea is his domain, and a sea god who must wait for a boat has not arrived.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each use leaves salt rime on his skin and lips, and for a day after, everything he tastes is only salt, bread, wine and water alike. A day of tasting nothing but the sea is small and a little humiliating, and he would miss the taste of a loaf given in welcome.
Edge cases it expects: It works on any water of body size, not a cup or a puddle. It does not make him lighter on ice or mud. A frozen surface is ground. The drawback begins when he enters the water and lasts a day from when he leaves it. He may choose how long to be in, and extended stays in a single stretch count as one use.

### Reading the Water
What it does: He knows the state of water within a day's walk of where he stands: the set of its currents, its depth, what moves in it or on it (shoals, boats, large animals, drowned things), the weather that is coming off it for the next day, and whether a crossing is safe. It is a knowing and not a seeing; it gives no faces and no speech.
Why this god has it: He is a keeper of harbors, and a keeper of a harbor must know what is coming in.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): While he reads, and for an hour after, his ears are full of surf and he cannot make out spoken words. A host who cannot hear the guest has lost something he values most.
Edge cases it expects: It reads water he stands within a day's walk of, by land or water, not water beyond that. Rain in the air and ground water are not included, only standing and running water and the sea. It does not show intent. It cannot be used while another power's drawback is on him without the two stacking.

### Washed Up Again
What it does: When his body is broken, he wishes to be returned by the sea, cast up whole and unremarked on the nearest shore of the sea, with the salt on his lips. The return takes a season (the standing rung of one item of memory or habit, taken by the logged roll from his record of gods and goals). The walk from that shore to wherever he was needed is added.
Why this god has it: He is a god of the sea, and the sea gives back what it takes, slowly.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The cost is the standing one: one item taken by roll each time, a name or a promise or a habit, unchosen and unknown to him. He would notice not knowing why something had stopped mattering.
Edge cases it expects: The rung is fixed now at a season, once, and not chosen again at any return. It does not answer a body that is merely held, shut out or exiled. It answers a broken body only. He returns at the nearest shore of the sea, not at a ground of his own setting.

## Trades
Weakness offered: The Unasked Threshold. He cannot enter any dwelling, hall, boat's cabin or walled yard that mortals hold as their own, unless someone of the house, of any age who speaks for it, has asked him in by word or gesture. It binds as long as he holds the form he came down in, with no end, and nothing ends it but being asked.
What counts: any closed place made and kept by mortals as a home or hold, house, hut, tent with a flap, ship's cabin, and the door of a walled town's council hall; the wall of a town itself does not count, and the gate of an open town does not. A place with no door, a ruin no one keeps, the open deck of a ship, a harbor, a field, a road, and any place he is carried into against his will do not count. An invitation, once given, holds for that visit and for that house until the one who gave it takes it back aloud; it does not pass to others. A petition from him (asking to be asked) does not count as an invitation, but a general welcome ("all travelers may enter") given by one with the right to give it does.
What it asks in return: that Reading the Water reaches rain and ground water as well, so that he knows too the weather in the clouds and the wells of the place, within the same day's walk.
Why it offers it: It is the one cost in the world that a host would not find strange in a guest, and the keeper of a door should wait to be asked.

## The people it will lead
It accepts what it is leaning toward and asks for nothing outside it. A walled town by a harbor, about five thousand, gathered close, ruled by a council of elders and chieftains in which the god is heard and not obeyed, and which sends its helpers to carry out what is agreed. They ask no rites of it, and he is content that what they do about it is theirs. It would like them to be a people who learned hospitality the hard way, and keep the old laws of the road, and have, in the cold, a lamp left in the window.
