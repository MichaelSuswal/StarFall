## Answer 1
A table set for one more than the number who sit down. Salt on the wind, a lamp left in a window, and someone on the road who has not yet knocked. It feels like the hour before the door opens.

## Answer 2
Coast. Not the beach, which is only the sea's doorstep, but the place where a harbor wall holds back the water and the water is polite about it. A land that has to look outward to eat, and so learns to look kindly at whoever comes in off the water.

## Answer 3
Four seasons, and none of them in a hurry. Let the winters be long enough to teach people to store, and the springs honest enough to forgive them for it. I would not ask the world for gentleness, only for a rhythm a slow person can learn to trust. A year should have a turning in it that people can wait for.

Of the seasons, I would wake at the end of autumn, when the boats are hauled up, the stores are counted, and the households are drawing in around their fires.

I wake into late autumn.

## Answer 4
Some of each, though I would not say in what proportion. A road that has been walked is a road that has had its hospitality tested already. I would like to find a country with a few old kingdoms rooted in it, and between them wide places where no one has yet been asked for a bed.

## Answer 5
Long enough that the ruins have ruins, and not so long that anyone remembers why they fell. Thousands of years. People who still tell the old stories, and tell them differently in every harbor, so that the truth is a thing you have to sail between.

## Answer 6
Closer to the wise-woman than the rumor, though she should live a day's walk from the next one, not in every valley. Enough that a mother can say a word over a fever and half believe it. Never enough that anyone is spared the work of the rest.

## Answer 7
It should feel like tides. It comes in when it comes in, and it takes something back when it goes. The cost should be small and constant and a little humiliating: a salt in the mouth, a year of one sense dimmed, a name forgotten. Nothing grand to pay, so that nothing grand is ever owed to anyone else.

## Answer 8
Noticeable and not exalted. A god should be the guest the whole table turns to look at, then looks away from, a little ashamed to have stared. Far more than a sorcerer, since a sorcerer can be outlasted, but not so far above that a mortal cannot sit across from us and argue the price of fish.

## Answer 9
Familiar animals, mostly, and then the sea, which I would keep for the strange. Let the land have its wolves and herons and its few legends. Let the deep water keep things no one has a name for, and let fishermen be careful what they pull up.

## Answer 10
The kind of danger that arrives politely. A stranger turned away from a door, and what follows him. Hunger in a hard year, and the quiet choices it forces. Water that rises a hand's breadth a night. I like a fear that mortals could have prevented by being kind a week earlier.

## Answer 11
Salt that is clean, and trust that is cheap. If I must pick one, I will say that safe harbors are scarce: places where a stranger can land and be neither robbed nor sold. Mortals will trade for them, fight over them, and build whole towns around the few that exist.

## Answer 12
Old roads, mostly, and one door that is not quite sealed. Bones of something enormous, but washed up, not dug out, so that a coastal people has to decide what to do with a thing that large lying on their shore. And a harbor under a harbor, if the world will carry it.

## Answer 13
A few, and wary of us. Not an empire of them. A people who live where the land gives out and the water begins, who keep their own tides and do not ask to be understood. They should have the hospitality laws we lack, and be strict about them.

## Answer 14
A few, and let them arrive by water. A ship with the wrong sails, a captain who knows one thing too many. I would like the strange tools to come in small, in a chest, to be argued over for a generation before anyone learns what they are for.

## Answer 15
Face to face, and slowly. A pull at any distance is a shout across a room, and I prefer to be introduced. Let us recognize each other the way sailors do, by something in how the other one stands on a deck that is not moving. Close enough to take a hand, and then a long moment before either of us says what we are.

## Answer 16
One body. I would not wish to be thin across many places, and I notice that those who ask for more than one are usually running from the one. If the world insists on several, let each cost a memory, and let me not be told which one.

## Answer 17
One who keeps the door. Not a conqueror, and not a prophet. A woman or man who sits in the council and says the unpopular, practical thing in a plain voice, and is right a season late. The kind of mortal whose story is told in the boat the next evening, when the weather turns.

## Answer 18
A companion, if the world allows it, and one that was not sent to me. Failing that, a place I can stand and say I was here before, without knowing how I know it. I would also like to learn what the sea is for. I have been told it is for crossing. I do not think that is all.

## Answer 19
That no one is left on the far shore. I will not say more than that. The world is listening, and I find I would sooner it heard half of it and guessed the rest.
