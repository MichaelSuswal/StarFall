# Survey, Day 1

## Answer 1
We feel a long table set out of doors, and the herds coming down to the water at dusk, and everyone counted. Warm, loud, and full. A world where nothing is missing from the count at day's end. We have been in a narrow place a long time. We want wide.

## Answer 2
Grass and forest where they meet. Open ground for the herds, with timber at its edge and a river running through, and hills behind for a wall to lean on. The edge country, where the hunt is best. We want land that is full of animals and has room for a walled town to sit in it.

## Answer 3
Four seasons, each one honest and each one lasting long enough to be counted. A green spring for calving and foaling, a long fat summer, an autumn of rut and harvest and the great hunts, and a winter hard enough to teach thrift but not hard enough to starve the herds. Let them keep their order, so that the law of the year can be written down and kept. We would wake into the autumn, when the herds are fattest and the hunt is lawful and the gathering is largest. We, bound once past all counting of seasons, would like to be woken by the year turning toward plenty.

We wake into autumn.

## Answer 4
Some of each, though we lean to the rooted. Wild country, so the herds have somewhere to run that is not ours. Old kingdoms, so there are neighbors to feast with, trade with, and learn from. We are curious, and a world with only empty land has nothing to ask. A world with only kingdoms has no beasts. We would sooner meet than conquer, and we would sooner know the boundaries than guess at them.

## Answer 5
Thousands of years, with ruins under ruins. Old enough that there are records to read and customs to inherit, and old enough that some things are lost and can be dug for. We want a story with depth beneath it. We want to be able to ask who stood here before, and have the ground, at least, answer.

## Answer 6
Closer to the wise-woman in every valley than to the rumor, though not quite so common. Say, a hedge-witch in most districts, known by name, trusted and a little feared. Never gone, never everywhere.

## Answer 7
It should feel like a bargain with something larger, and it should cost something plain and countable: a season of health, a measure of blood, a promise that must be kept to the letter. We like costs that can be written down. Magic that costs nothing has no law in it, and magic whose cost cannot be named is only a trap. Let the price be known beforehand and paid exactly.

## Answer 8
Plainly, and visibly. A god should stand out the way a mountain stands out of a plain. We have no wish to be a sorcerer whom the neighbors may doubt. We want to be seen, to be pointed at, to be found when the people look up. A great mortal sorcerer can be argued with and denied. A god, as we wish it, is simply there, and the people know it.

## Answer 9
Familiar animals first, in great number and variety, and then a margin of the unnamed at the far edges. A few creatures no one has a name for, so that the hunt is never finished and there is always one more thing to count. The strange should be rare enough that the herds can be known and the beasts of the wild can be reckoned. We are the keeper of beasts and we would like to be surprised by one, now and then.

## Answer 10
Hunger. Winter, drought, a blight, and the lean year when the count comes up short. Also the thing in the dark that takes the stragglers from the herd, and the beast that has learned to prefer men. Those make a good story. The fear should be of something that can be hunted and, with the right company and the right law, beaten. We dislike fears that have no edge.

## Answer 11
Salt. And with it, good iron. The thing mortals want most and have least should be the thing that keeps meat through the winter and keeps a blade keen. They will trade for it, fight over it, and build roads to it. Scarcity that makes roads is the best sort, because roads bring gatherings.

## Answer 12
Old roads, and under them the bones of something enormous. A great beast, long dead, with ribs like roof beams. We would like to stand inside it. Also a sealed door or two, because we are curious, and because a door that stays shut is a question we cannot leave alone. Though we would open it only with company beside us.

## Answer 13
Yes, a few, and let them be strange and orderly. Peoples with their own laws, their own tongue, and their own way of keeping animals or being kept by them. We want some who are shy and some who are proud, and we want to be able to sit at a table with them. Peoples who keep a law we can learn are the best neighbors.

## Answer 14
A few. Travelers who arrive with a cart of strange tools and strange knowledge, and who are glad to be asked about it. Few, so each arrival is an event, and a feast. We would take them in and ask them everything, and we would put what they know to work in the town. Many arriving at once would make a flood, and a flood has no law.

## Answer 15
Face to face, and by name. A pull at any distance is a leash, and we have worn one. We would rather the gods came to each other's tables and were known by looking, and by what they ate and said and kept. Let a god be recognized by presence, as a herd is recognized by its lead animal. A knowing only close by gives each of us time to prepare a welcome, and we would like to prepare a welcome.

## Answer 16
One body. We are large and we would like to be counted as one, a single presence that the people can find. If there must be more, let the cost be steep and plain: each additional body halves the strength of all, so that no god is thin everywhere and mighty nowhere. We distrust being in many places. A god who is everywhere is a god who has nowhere to stand.

## Answer 17
One who counts. A shepherd with a gift for keeping a tally of every head, who can tell by the count when something is wrong, and who will walk a long way to put it right. Also a hunter who knows when to stop. We want mortals with a sense of measure, who can be trusted with a law and a herd both, and who are not afraid to say a number out loud when it is bad.

## Answer 18
A companion. Something that has been waiting, and that is glad to be found, whether beast or person or neither. Beyond that, a place that is full to the brim of animals none of us have counted. We would like to discover that the herds go on farther than we imagined, and that there is an open door at the end of the count.

## Answer 19
We would like not to be bound again. That is the wish we have not said. Let there be no chain in this world that can hold a god for an age, or if there is, let it be a chain we can see, and name, and walk around. And let the table be full when we arrive, and let someone be glad of us.
