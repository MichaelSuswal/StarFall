# Negotiation: god-beasts, Night 2

## Round 1 reply

### The Count of the Herd
Accepted, with choice (a), no hunt on the count. The Shepherd would sooner be barred from a hunt than doubled in its helplessness: it counts in the open, among beasts it cannot see coming, and it fears to stand senseless longer. The refusal of the line marking a god as "unlike its kind" is accepted without argument; we wrote a knowing at a distance and the Keeper is right that nothing here gives one. Two points of wording, in the write-up and not a new power:
1. "Or at its word" in (a) should mean a hunt or a slaughter the god orders or asks for by name on that kind. It should not reach a herdsman culling his own beast under the town's written law, or the tithe and the feast the law already sets. If it did reach those, one count would stop the whole town's kitchens for two days. We ask that this be written plainly.
2. The two dawns count from the end of the count, not the start, so a long count does not use up the barred days.
The Keeper's limits stand as written, including that under stone or water the count stops at the stone or the surface.

### The Calling
Accepted, with one counter on the added line. The price as we wrote it stands, and the line that a called herd must not be betrayed is fair; we asked for the herds to come to a table, not to a killing ground, and a god of law can keep a rule it understands. The counter is on who can break it. As drafted, any kill "by the god, its people or at its word" costs the kind for a year. Its people will number in the thousands, and a single hunter, thief or child with a sling would cost the whole kind. We cannot hold seven thousand hands to a rule it cannot see. We ask that the line read: "if beasts are killed while called by the god, or at its word, or by its law's own hunt". A kill by a person acting against the law, or by a beast, or by another people, is not the god's betrayal and does not cost the kind. In return the Shepherd will say in the write-up that it must name the hunt aloud before the kill for it to be "at its word", so that the line is exact and can be read by anyone who watches. The line on beasts under a hand is accepted as plain reading.

### Speech with Beasts
Accepted, with choice (a), the kind, for good, and one counter on wording. Choice (b) would stop the Shepherd on every death near it of any beast it ever questioned, across a whole country, during a hunt, in its own herds, at a feast. With its flaw, that is a helpless coward's life. Choice (a) it governs: it chooses which kinds to speak with, and it will speak with few. The counter is on "or sit at a table where that kind is served". We ask that it be cut. Never eating the kind is the price a hungry god would notice; barring it from the table is a second price for the same power, and it would break the open table and the gathering that are most of what it is. It will sit and not eat, and it will say so to the guest. If the Keeper holds that the table clause is part of the price, we ask instead that it read: it cannot sit at the head of a table where that kind is the dish. Then it can host and still refuse the plate. The Keeper's limits are accepted.

### The Standing
Accepted as drafted. The price and the limits are ours and the Keeper's reading of them is fair.

### Return to the Herd-ground
Accepted as drafted. The rung, the memory item taken by the logged roll, and the nearest herd-ground are as we asked.

### Trade: No false number, for the hurt and the young
Accepted as drafted. It is a weakness we do not already pay, and a god of law should be able to say that every figure it gives is true. We take the trade. We ask for one point in the write-up, as wording only: the weakness covers a number given under oath or a stated tally, and a number the god is told by another and repeats as hearsay, saying so, is not a number it knows to be false. The Keeper's reading, that a guess called a guess and a number written by another are outside it, already says this, and we ask only that the hearsay case be named.

## Round 2 reply

Confirmed. The Shepherd means what the Keeper has written, in every item, and asks for nothing more.

- The Count of the Herd: confirmed as written, with no hunt on the count, the two dawns running from the end of the count, the bar on telling anyone what the count showed in order to guide a killing, and the herdsman's cull and standing law left free. The Shepherd accepts the added bar on guiding a killing and will keep it.
- The Calling: confirmed as written. The betrayal line reads as we asked, with the hunt named aloud before the kill, and the added line, that a kill the god or its officers allowed, used or took the meat of counts as the god's, is accepted. The Shepherd will keep its officers to the same rule.
- Speech with Beasts: confirmed as written. It never eats the kind it has spoken with, and may sit at any table but not at the head of one where that kind is the dish. The Shepherd accepts the Keeper's reason that the clause stays.
- The Standing: confirmed as written.
- Return to the Herd-ground: confirmed as written.
- The trade, No false number, for the hurt, sick and young: confirmed as written, with the hearsay case named. The listing under Weaknesses and what each bought matches it.
- The Keeper trait: confirmed. The Shepherd knows from arrival that any count it makes by eye and finger comes to it first as one short, and that it must count again before it believes the number. It takes this as a fact about itself and not a power, and it fits what it already feels. It understands that the Count of the Herd gives its true number at once, and that the trait puts no false number in its mouth.
