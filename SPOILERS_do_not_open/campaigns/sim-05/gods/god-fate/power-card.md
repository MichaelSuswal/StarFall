# Power card: Skerrit

Written: creation, Night 2, 2026-11-23. Confirmed by the god in its round 2 reply.

## Form

Skerrit. It dimly holds the name as the sound a greater thing once made when calling it to heel, and it keeps the name anyway, because a name is a mark on an account. Those who do not know it will call it the Factor, or the Tally-beast.

A small beast, about the size of a large marten: long in the body, short in the leg, brindled grey and rust, with a ruff of iridescent green and blue hair at the neck and shoulders that shimmers like a fly's back. Narrow face, quick hands with a thumb on each forepaw, a bare tail that it carries curled. Its eyes are round and pale gold, with dark pips in them like the marks on dice. Its voice is dry and quick, a merchant's voice, and it sounds larger than it looks.
Senses: sharp sight and hearing, and a very fine sense of smell, best for rot, damp, sickness and spoiled food, which it reads as a trader reads a sample. It moves on four legs at a quick scurrying pace, climbs well, and can sit on a shoulder or a pack frame. It can stand on its hind legs to deal with people at table height. It cannot lift more than its size allows.

One body; a god is in one place at a time.

What breaks it: Anything that can break a small animal body: a blow, a crushing weight, a fall onto stone, a blade, fire, drowning held under, a trap or snare, a cage that holds it (held, not broken). It can be shut out by a warded door or a sealed vessel it cannot pass. It cannot die. A broken body is a body struck beyond use, and it is gone from the place until it returns.

Knowing another god: at a distance the god feels nothing of another god, no pull and no direction. It knows one only by meeting it. The knowing comes on slowly, by nearness alone, and one who works at seeming mortal may hold it off for a while. Within arm's reach it is sure, and no god passes for a mortal at a touch. It tells what stands there and nothing of its temper, its powers or its mind.

Keeper trait: It answers to its name: when its name is called sharply, as one calls to heel, its body starts a step toward the caller before it can stop itself. Reach: one step, and no more. It compels no service and no answer, it is not an offer made in form and so does not wake the weakness on this card, and it touches no power.

When the body is broken: see The Return to the Marsh Edge, under Powers.

### Definitions

None beyond the words of the powers below.

### Things carried

A small set of brass scales and a pouch of weights, hung from a cord at its neck, ordinary and true. The weights are honest ones and nothing more than ordinary objects. It carries nothing else.

## Powers

### The Weighing
Status: main
Description: When a bargain is spoken aloud in the god's hearing, its terms stated and each party saying yes, and the god holds its scales or has its paws on the table, it learns for each party who spoke, as a plain yes or no, whether that party means at that moment to keep the terms as stated.
Drawback: Any term the god itself speaks or assents to in a weighed bargain binds it to the letter. It cannot knowingly break it, and no trick of wording frees it from a term it understood when it spoke: the meaning it understood is the meaning that binds.
Limits settled at creation:
- Only the terms as spoken, and only parties within the same room or, in the open, within arm's reach of the scales.
- It learns intent at the moment of the yes: not why, not whether the party can keep the terms, nothing else of the mind. A party who lies to itself is weighed by what it believes. One who meant it and later breaks it weighed true.
- Signs and writing with no spoken terms are not weighed. In a tongue the god does not understand it learns only that yes was said, and a term it did not understand does not bind it.
- The god may show the answer or keep it.
- Mind: it reads one thing and rules nothing.
- Other gods: another god in the bargain, weighed or not, is unruled until it happens.
- Being watched: a small beast with its paws on a table. Nothing else shows.
- Use: any bargain it attends, one at a time.
- Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water.
- Text, limits and price settled at creation, Night 2. Nothing disputed.
Rulings:
Trades: improved by the weakness The offer heard out (see Weaknesses taken): The Weighing also tells the god, for each party, a plain yes or no: whether that party believes it can keep the terms as stated. Settled at creation, Night 2. Ledger: L-0026.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Bill
Status: main
Description: A mortal who stands before the god, asks, and names a price aloud, may receive, if the god accepts the price, one small turn of chance, now: a throw that falls well, a ford that holds, a fever that turns, a lost thing found, a door that opens, a blade that misses. It is a single turn within what chance could do. The price is collected when it falls due, exactly as named, and the bill stands on the god's record whether or not the mortal remembers it.
Drawback: While any bill the god has written is unpaid and past due, the god moves no faster than a slow walk and cannot leap, climb or scurry. The god may forgive a bill, and each time it does, it can write no new bill until the next turn of a season.
Limits settled at creation:
- One turn of chance for one bill: where the matter would be rolled, the roll is shaded well in the asker's favor; where it is certain or impossible, nothing changes. No miracle, no raising the dead, no changing what is done.
- The price must be a thing the mortal can give or do with its own hands and days: goods, labor, a service, a tooth, a thing it owns. A promise of work through the nights of a season is labor, and a promise to answer to another name for a stated span is a service; both may be prices. A price that is not a thing given or done (a memory, a year of fortune, a name taken out of the world) cannot be taken as a bill.
- The god collects no more than was named. The bill compels no one: a mortal who will not pay is a debtor, and the bill stands unpaid.
- A bill falls due when the mortal set it, or at the next turn of a season. A debt binds heirs only if they agreed aloud. A bill may be paid early.
- No more than seven bills may stand open at once, and no mortal holds more than one.
- Mortals who ask and agree only. Never on another god, and a turn bought against a god's own act is unruled until it happens.
- It makes nothing: a turn of chance finds only what is there to be found.
- Being watched: the asking and the naming are spoken aloud and can be overheard.
- Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water.
- Text, limits and price settled at creation, Night 2. Ledger: L-0025, L-0014.
Rulings:
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### Clean Rot
Status: main
Description: By touch, the god draws the rot or sickness out of one thing: a store of grain, a well, a wound, a sick person, a spoiled barrel. What rot took is given back where the thing still exists to hold it, and the rot passes into the god's own body.
Drawback: What it draws out, it carries. For a season after each working (about ninety days) its coat falls patchy, its limbs ache, and flies gather and follow it; every mortal near can see the flies and smell the sickness, and it can hide neither. It can work no second Clean Rot while the first is on it.
Limits settled at creation:
- One thing for one working: one barrel, one well, one body. Never a land, a river, a whole granary or a plague. Out of a plague it takes one person, and the plague stays in the others.
- Rot and sickness only: not a broken bone, old age, hunger, or a poison that is no rot. A wound is made clean, not closed.
- The sickness cannot kill the god.
- Other gods, more than ordinary craft: a rot that is another god's work, or made by more than ordinary craft, is unruled until it happens.
- Not applicable: light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, mind.
- Text, limits and price settled at creation, Night 2. Nothing disputed.
Rulings:
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Return to the Marsh Edge
Status: main
Description: When the god's body is broken, it comes back after a season, whole, at the marsh edge nearest to where the body was broken: wet and uncertain ground, a reed bed or a ford. The walk from that ground to wherever it was needed is added.
Drawback: Each return costs the god one item of memory or habit, taken by a logged roll from its own record (what it holds of other gods, and its goals). The god does not choose it and is not told what went.
Limits settled at creation:
- The season rung of the standing scale, fixed now and never chosen at each return.
- A body caged, bound or shut out is not broken, and this does not answer it.
- No set ground; the nearest serves.
- Text, limits and price settled at creation, Night 2. Ledger: L-0027.
Rulings:
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

## Weaknesses taken

### The offer heard out
When a mortal stands before the god, states aloud both the terms and the price, and offers a hand or an open palm, the god must hear the whole offer to its end and weigh it. It may refuse the offer. It may not walk away, leap off or silence the speaker before the offer is finished. A bare demand, an offer in a tongue it does not understand, and an offer from another god do not count. The same offer said again with no change to its terms or its price is not a new offer; a changed offer is. Nothing ends it. Anyone who learns of it can hold the god in one place for as long as they can keep an offer going. It bought, on The Weighing: The Weighing also tells the god, for each party, a plain yes or no: whether that party believes it can keep the terms as stated. Settled at creation, Night 2. Ledger: L-0026.
