## Answer 1
A market at dusk, when the lamps are lit and the day's accounts are being settled. Noise, smoke, honest scales, and under it all a slow ticking, as of dice that have not yet stopped. A world where every thing has a price and every price is written down.

## Answer 2
Roads. River country first, wide slow water with ford towns and toll bridges, and a coast with enough harbors to make a rival nervous. Marsh edges too, where the ground is uncertain and people learn to read it. I do not want one fixed place. I want land that is crossed, not sat upon.

## Answer 3
Four seasons, and each one a different kind of market. Spring is loans and seed. Summer is the long caravan road. Autumn is the reckoning, when debts come due and the harvest is weighed. Winter is the closed ledger, a hard season, hard enough to be fair to everyone. Let the turns be regular and let the weather within them be a gamble. I would wake into autumn, at the reckoning, with the books open.
I wake into autumn.

## Answer 4
Some of each, and I want the proportions written plainly. Old kingdoms rooted along the rivers, with customs and tolls I can learn and bargain with. Wild country at the edges, enough that a moving people always has somewhere to go that is no one's. A world with nothing already rooted has no one to trade with.

## Answer 5
Thousands of years, with the ruins layered and the records poor. Old enough that contracts have been broken and forgotten many times over, and that there are debts nobody now living remembers incurring. Not so old that nothing is still owed.

## Answer 6
Present and rare, closer to a wise-woman in some valleys than a rumor, but never in every valley. Enough that a bargain can be witnessed by something more than paper. Not enough that anyone can skip the work of a fair trade.

## Answer 7
It should feel like borrowing. The working comes quickly and the bill comes later, and the bill is exact. The cost should be something the mortal can name in advance, a year of luck, a tooth, a name, a season of sleep, and should be collected whether or not they remember agreeing. No free working. No hidden charges.

## Answer 8
Noticeably, but not absolutely. A god should be a great power, not a miracle that makes the law pointless. Let mortals be able to bargain with us, refuse us, and sometimes outwit us. A god who cannot be haggled with is only a weather.

## Answer 9
Familiar animals and a good many legends, with a few things no one has a name for. Small strange things especially, the kind that live in the margins of a map. I would like some creatures that are useful and ugly at once, and that people are slow to thank.

## Answer 10
Rot and sickness, first. The slow kind of danger that comes in with the grain or the water and that no sword reaches. Then the debt that cannot be paid, and the thing that comes to collect. A good story is one where the cost was named at the start and everyone still acted surprised.

## Answer 11
Trust. Salt and iron are traded for, grain is fought over, but what is truly scarce is a stranger's word that holds. Let honest weights and kept promises be the rarest goods on the road, so that whoever can supply them is rich.

## Answer 12
Old roads, and sealed doors with terms carved on them. Ruins of markets and tollhouses, with ledgers in a script no one reads yet. Bones of something enormous are welcome, but I would rather the doors, and a long argument over who holds the key.

## Answer 13
Yes, a few, and not grand ones. Small peoples who keep their own accounts and do not love ours. Shy, exact, well hidden, difficult to cheat, and willing to trade at the edge of the woods if the terms are posted. I would like to be able to deal with them without ever being sure what they think of the bargain.

## Answer 14
Few. A handful of travelers from very far off, carrying tools and knowledge that do not fit anywhere here, and who have to pay full price for everything they learn. They should arrive in debt. I want to meet one and hear what they are owed, and by whom.

## Answer 15
Face to face only. At a distance a pull would be a leash, and I want no leash. Let us have to meet, look, and weigh each other, and let each be free to lie. I would add a small mark of recognition at touching distance, so that no god can pass as a mortal to another god, but only that.

## Answer 16
One body. I would rather be exactly where I am and answerable for it. If it must be allowed, let it cost dearly, a second body that is only a fraction of me and takes that fraction away while it lives, and that the rest of me can feel failing. No one should be everywhere for free.

## Answer 17
A mortal who keeps a hard bargain and keeps it justly. A judge, a factor, a debtor who pays, a witness who cannot be bought. Someone whose word is worth more than their sword. A story worth following is one in which a small person is owed something large, and gets it.

## Answer 18
A truth, I think. A ledger, somewhere, in which the true accounts are kept: who owes what to whom, and how it came to be so. I do not know that I want to read it. I would like to know that it exists, and to be able to read my own page.

## Answer 19
That no one greater comes down after us. Let the powers here be only as great as the ones falling now, and let there be no master on the stair, no one who calls in all accounts at once. Let what we owe be owed to the world, and to each other, and to no one above.
