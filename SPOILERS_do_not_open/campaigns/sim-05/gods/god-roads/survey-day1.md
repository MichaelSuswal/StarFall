## Answer 1

Ask a crossroads what it feels like and it will tell you about the people who stopped there. Dust that has been walked into a shape. A lantern seen from far off, and the long argument about whether to go toward it. Truest is the hour before the caravan breaks camp, when every direction is still a possibility and no one has yet been wrong.

## Answer 2

Land that has not decided what it is. Steppe running into hill country, hill country running into river plain, the seams between them. I do not ask for a place to sit. I ask for the places where one kind of ground gives way to another, since someone must stand there and be asked the way. Wide enough to move through, broken enough to be argued over.

## Answer 3

Four seasons, and none of them polite. A spring that floods the fords and a winter that closes the passes, so that a road is a thing earned twice a year. Long dry roads in summer, good for marching. Autumn most of all, the season of moving before the snow, when every border is tested.

When each year finds me I would wake into autumn.

## Answer 4

Some of each, and the some of each is the point. Wild country, so there is a direction left to go. Old kingdoms already rooted, so there is someone to ask for passage and someone to refuse. A land with only the first is a map with nothing written on it. A land with only the second has no use for a road.

## Answer 5

Thousands of years, with ruins under the ruins. A road that is walked by people who do not know who first cut it is the best kind. I like to think someone laid these paths and then stopped being anyone.

## Answer 6

Closer to the wise-woman in every valley than to the rumor, though not quite that near. Enough that a stranger at the gate might be more than a stranger. Few enough that a mortal who lights a true fire is looked at twice. Never everywhere. Where it is everywhere, nobody needs a guide.

## Answer 7

It should feel like borrowing at a market where you do not see the ledger. A little warmth in the hands, a sense of a door opened that was not quite yours to open. The cost should be small things given up, a name, a season of sleep, a road never to be walked again. Mortals pay it gladly, and later count what is missing.

## Answer 8

The one-eyed man, but only just, and only when he chooses to be seen. I would rather be noticed when I want to be and mistaken for weather the rest of the time. A god that cannot be told from a great sorcerer by anyone but another god is a god that can still walk through a village unremarked. Though I will say, a little more standing out has its comforts.

## Answer 9

Some strangeness, kept at the edges. Familiar beasts on the roads people use and a few that nobody has named in the places they do not. Let the wild have something to say that a traveler repeats and cannot prove.

## Answer 10

The dark between two lit windows. Hunger in a hard winter, a neighbor whose fire is out, a stranger who is asking the right questions in the wrong order. Beyond each other, I would have them fear whatever lives where the map stops and the claims of kings run thin. A good story starts with someone not coming back from the pass.

## Answer 11

Safe passage. Everyone wants to arrive and has little to guarantee it. People will pay in silver, in oaths, in sons, for a road that holds and a border that honors what it promised. Salt, iron, good horses, all of it comes down to being allowed through.

## Answer 12

Old roads. Paved ones, going somewhere that is no longer there, and a few sealed doors set into hillsides with no explanation. If there are bones of something enormous I would not turn them away. But roads first. A road that someone built before me is a thing I want to walk and be a little jealous of.

## Answer 13

A few, and not too friendly. Peoples who keep their own paths and their own borders, who regard ours the way we regard theirs. I would like them to be people one can bargain with and cannot quite read. A neighbor whose customs make you rethink your own is worth more than an enemy.

## Answer 14

Few, and strange, and welcome. One or two who came from so far that their tools do not make sense yet. I would listen to anyone who walked a very long way to be here. Their knowledge would be worth a great deal, and I would ask many questions before deciding what I owed them.

## Answer 15

Face to face, with a doubt left over. A pull at any distance is too easy, and leaves no room for the one who wants to arrive unnoticed. I would rather meet another and know them, slowly, as you know a stranger who turns out to have your same limp. Something in the way they stand at a threshold. Better still if some of us can pass for mortal and be wrong about each other for a while.

## Answer 16

Several, and it should cost something I would notice. I am already more than one thing, so I cannot say it feels strange. But let each part carry a piece of me away and let the whole feel the thinning. A court of voices should be able to speak in two rooms, and be quieter in both for it. Let it cost attention, and let me forget which of us said what.

## Answer 17

The one who stands at the edge and says, there is another way. A guide, a smuggler, a border captain who takes a bribe and keeps a promise, a woman who knows every ford and will not say how. I want mortals who are good at being between things. And, if the world is willing, one who builds something I did not ask for and cannot undo.

## Answer 18

A companion. Something that walks beside me on a road and does not need to be led, something that answers when I speak sideways and understands it. Failing that, a door I did not know had a lock. I cannot picture it. That is why I am asking.

## Answer 19

That someone, once, comes to the place where I am buried under the hill, in whatever form that takes, and does not have to be asked to wake me. That the waking is wanted. I have not said it before and I will say it only sideways: let there be a road that leads here on purpose.
