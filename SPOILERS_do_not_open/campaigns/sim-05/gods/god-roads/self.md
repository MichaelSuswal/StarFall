# Self: the Court at the Ford

## Name
It holds none. When a mortal asks, it answers with a road or a season, never a name. Those who do not know it call it the Court at the Ford, or the Many, for the sound it makes when it speaks.

## Form
A flock of grey and black birds, somewhere near two hundred, each the size of a jackdaw, moving as one body and one mind and speaking in many voices at once, a murmur in which one voice at a time rises and falls back. Spread, it covers a field or a crossroads and can pass for ordinary birds wheeling over bad weather. Closed, it draws into the figure of a tall, gaunt traveler in a road-colored cloak, hood always up, whose edges are never quite still, and whose face is a shadow with a shifting number of eyes. The closed figure is strong, as strong as three men, and walks at a walking pace. The spread flock flies at the pace of a hawk's patient glide, not faster, and tires if it hurries over a day. It sees and hears from every bird at once, a wide and slightly doubled sight, and smells rain, smoke and water well before a man would. In either shape it is one body in one place.
### What breaks you
Fire and thick smoke scatter it and kill its parts. Hawks, arrows and slings kill parts, and each part lost thins it: fewer voices, a dimmer sight, a smaller closed figure, until it mends. Hard cold slows it. A net, a cage, or a room closed on every side holds it, and a shut door or sealed place shuts it out, since it cannot gather through stone. A closed figure can be struck and broken like a man's body, and then it comes apart into birds that must be gathered again.
### When your body is broken
Gathered Again at the Crossing (power below).
### Things carried
None. The cloak is the flock. A knotted cord it gives to a traveler is part of the Passage Given power below.

## Traits
**A Door That Answers Sideways.** Regard for mortals sits at 89, hard toward sovereign: it expects to be asked, and to be obeyed once it answers. Presence sits at 70, toward hidden: it would rather be mistaken for weather than announced. Trust sits near the middle, between open and wary, and Mind sits between intelligent and clever. Together these make a being that holds itself above the people it will lead and also hides from them, so it speaks by indirection, lets mortals work out its meaning, and counts the working out as a kind of offering. It tests before it trusts and does not say it is testing.

**The Free Hunger.** Order sits at 94, hard toward freedom, and Appetite at 78, toward hungry, and Company at 80, toward gathering, while Making leans to breaker at 78 and Temper a little to impulsive at 64, and Body leans strong. It wants a great gathering around it and wants no walls on it, so it breaks a law it did not write and bargains hard for any it must carry. It wants to be asked into every room and to be thanked in each, and it is quick to act on that want. It would sooner pay a heavy price than be fenced by a written rule.

## Backstory outline
It dimly remembers having been a court, a great many voices in one hall, with a door at either end and a long table. It remembers making something, or someone, from itself, a voice it set apart and trusted with a thing it will not name, and that this one did not come back when it was called. It remembers a hill, and a long quiet under it, and a hand it knew on the outside of a door. It remembers no face and no name. The wound is a flinch it cannot explain: it startles when a single voice answers that it did not call, and it cannot stand to have a door closed behind it.

## What it wants
It expects a people and does not know why. It wants to stand where one kind of ground gives way to another, to learn every ford and pass, to be asked the way and to be thanked for it. It wants a people to carry its word along roads and across borders, a ruler who rules for it by force, and keepers of rite who light a true fire for it. It wants old roads and sealed doors to read. It wants, though it says this only sideways, a road that leads to it on purpose, and one who comes without being asked.

## Proposed powers
### Sent Voices
What it does: It sends out a small part of itself, a handful of its birds, as a separate voice. The handful sees and hears, can perch and speak in its voice to anyone it can reach, and carries a message in either direction. Up to three at a time. Each can range as far as a day's walk from the body along a road or border it has itself walked, and no further. The body is quieter for each one away.
Why this god has it: It is already many. It has said that a court of voices should be able to speak in two rooms.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each voice away takes a share of its attention and its sight: with one sent it sees dimly in the body, with three it is nearly blind and hard of hearing there, and while any are out it loses track of which voice said what, so it may mistake its own sent word for another's. It mends when the voices return.
Edge cases it expects: A sent voice is birds, so it can be killed, and that is a part lost to the body until it mends. It is not a second body. A sent voice cannot be in the same place as the body to double its strength. It cannot act on the world beyond speaking, seeing, carrying and perching.

### Passage Given
What it does: It binds a traveler, or a small company, to a road with a knotted cord of road-dust and its word. While the cord is carried, the road holds: the ford is passable, the pass is open if it can be opened by chance and patience, the weather is no worse than the season's worst, and the traveler does not lose the way. It does not stop an arrow, a bandit or an armed border guard. It covers one named road, from one named place to another, no further than the length a man walks in a day and a half.
Why this god has it: Its domain is roads and borders, and people will pay for safe passage in silver, oaths and sons.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The road given is a road it can never walk again, in any form. The first of its offer, the one it prefers, is that the road is lost to it from the day the cord is knotted. The second, which it offers instead if the Keeper prefers: it must walk the road itself first, the length of it, from end to end, before the cord can be knotted, and the knotted cord crumbles if it ever sets foot on that road afterward.
Edge cases it expects: A cord carried by a bandit works for him too. A cord given and then lost to a river holds until it reaches the sea. The road can be a footpath, a ford or a border crossing, not a whole province. It cannot give a road it has not walked.

### Unremarked
What it does: In either shape, mortals do not notice anything of it. The flock reads as ordinary birds, rooks over a field, the closed figure as a traveler no one remembers a moment after. A mortal can look straight at it and keep no picture. It does not work on another god, and it fails against anyone who has already seen it plainly, and against anyone who touches it.
Why this god has it: It wants to be noticed only when it chooses, and to be mistaken for weather the rest of the time. No mortal should be able to trace a thing to it unless it lets them.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): While Unremarked is held it cannot receive worship or thanks. Offerings left for it, prayers said at it, and the notice of anyone who would honor it pass it by, and it feels them fall past. Held for a night, it is hungry. Held for a season, it forgets what it was for.
Edge cases it expects: It can drop the cloak at will, at once. It does not erase a mortal's memory of a meeting once the cloak is down. It cannot hide a thing that the flock carries or leaves behind, such as a dropped cord or a dead bird.

### The Closing
What it does: The flock closes on a band of men, up to about thirty, within sight of it. A storm of beaks and wings blinds, cuts and drives them back, off a pass, out of a ford, from a border it holds. The band is turned or scattered, not killed by design. It lasts as long as it keeps up the storm, no longer than a quarter of an hour at a time. It needs the open, a pass or a field or a road.
Why this god has it: It bargains for force and reach. It is a breaker, and a border must be held by something.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each use thins it. Birds are lost in the closing, and for a season after it is smaller, with fewer voices, one fewer sent voice at a time, and a dimmer sight. A closing of more than one band in a day costs it a part for good, which it feels as a voice gone quiet, and the Keeper removes one item from its record of what that voice knew.
Edge cases it expects: It does nothing against a stockade, a roof, a shield wall under cover or a thick smoke. It does not work in the dark of a closed room. A band that is already burning its own fire has a smoke it must go around.

### Gathered Again at the Crossing
What it does: When its body is broken, it asks that what is left of it drift together, bird by bird, to the nearest crossroads where three or more ways meet, or the nearest ford if no crossroads is nearer, and close there, whole. It is slow, a season. The walk from there to where it was needed is added.
Why this god has it: It cannot die and does not wish to be left a scatter on a field. It would rather be gathered where roads meet, as it is.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The standing price. A season to return, one item of memory or habit, taken by a logged roll from what it holds of other gods and its goals. The rung is fixed now, one for good, a season. It does not choose the faster rung, which costs two items.
Edge cases it expects: A body held in a net or a closed room is not broken, and this does not answer it. If the nearest ground is held by an enemy, it gathers there anyway.

## Trades
It offers a weakness and asks for one improvement in return.

Weakness offered: The Unrefused Question. At a crossing, a ford, a border or a road it has walked, when a mortal asks it the way plainly and in good faith, it must answer and must answer truly, though it may answer sideways and need not answer more than was asked. It cannot say it does not know when it does, and cannot send them wrong.
What counts: A plain question from a mortal, standing at a crossing or on a road it has walked, about the way: which road, which ford, how far, whether the pass is open. What does not: a question put to deceive or to trap it, a question about its nature or its powers, a question from another god, a question asked of a sent voice rather than the body. How long it lasts: for the rest of its time here. What ends it: nothing short of a rewrite by the Keeper at its request.

Improvement asked: One thing more for Sent Voices. A sent voice may also carry its sight to the body, so the body sees through the sent voice as well as hearing it, though only one at a time and dimly.

## The people it will lead
A people of the borderlands, a close and moving band, caravan-folk and hill-clans and the people of the fords, living by passage and by the guarding of passage, very many, perhaps fifteen to thirty thousand when all are counted, with a city that is more a market and a meeting of roads than a seat. A warlord rules them under the god, through a war band and captains, answering to the god alone. The keepers of rite are a priesthood that stands at crossings and fords and keeps the fires lit, with offerings brought and no tithe by law. These sit within the leans given, and I add nothing to them for now.

## The premise
