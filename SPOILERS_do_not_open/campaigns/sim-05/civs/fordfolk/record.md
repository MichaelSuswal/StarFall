# People: the Fordfolk, of Threeways

Agreed record, written by the Keeper on Night 3a, 2026-11-23, from `god-roads`'s `people-hoped.md`. No edge was proposed and none was priced, so no negotiation round was held; the god's own words stand as agreed wherever they describe who the people are. What the god said of the land, the past, or what mortals will do is its hope or its people's belief. Home: Threeways [f23].

The god on the names: The people name themselves the Fordfolk, and their home Threeways [f23], for the town at the crossing of the grass road, the hill road and the river road. These are names I give and hope they keep. Naming Threeways [f23] does not place it.

## Who they are
A people of passage. They are caravan families, hill clans, river plain households and herders from the grass, who share one habit: they live by moving, by guarding the moving, and by being asked the way. They are the people one finds at a ford, a pass or a border, who sell passage, give guidance, guard a crossing and keep a fire lit there. They do not think of themselves as one kindred. They think of themselves as everyone who has stood at Threeways [f23] and been told the way. They have no single homeland and see that as an advantage.

## How many
About eighteen thousand: roughly four thousand at Threeways [f23] through the cold months, the rest along the three roads in bands. Within the god's size lean (very large). Not an edge and priced against nothing. The practical costs, which the god accepted in its own file and the Keeper applies in play: they eat about eighteen thousand acres' worth of grain a year and raise less than half of it, so a closed road or a bad year on the Meld is hunger within a season; the autumn gathering at Threeways [f23] strips fuel and grazing for a day's walk round the town; the whole people cannot move together faster than half the foot rate; growth is slow, since the bands' own country feeds no more than it does now.

## How they live
They keep a year in three parts, and the town is the knot. Autumn is the gathering: the bands come in to Threeways [f23] before the snow, and the town swells. Winter is spent in the town and in sheltered hill valleys nearest it, trading, mending, teaching the young the roads. In spring, when the fords flood, the river plain households stay by their own water and the hill clans cross late. Summer is the dry marching season: the caravans go out along all three roads, the grass herders move their stock, and the Road Guard rides the long stretches. They farm a little, on the river plain, and herd, and trade. Most of what they eat they raise, bring in or buy; salt, iron and good horses pass through their hands.

## Their code
Always so of them:
1. A promise of passage is kept. A guide, a smuggler or a border captain who has taken a price and given his word keeps the word.
2. A stranger who asks the way at a ford is answered, and a fire kept at a crossing is not refused to a traveler.
3. No blood is shed at a lit crossing fire, nor within a night's walk of one kept by a Fire Keeper, by any of them.
4. A household may leave the band at any gathering and no one may stop it, though it forfeits its place at the fire until it comes back.
5. No one is turned back at the ford for their name or kin.

What they will not do: sell the way to one who has said he will do harm at the end of it; turn a lit fire against a guest; shut a door on a person who has asked to be let through.

What they honor: those who are good at being between things, the guide, the interpreter, the border captain who keeps faith, the woman who knows every ford and will not say how.

Arrangements the god set out in answer to its questions, kept as its hope of how its own conduct and its people's custom meet:
1. They know it is me when a flock at the ford answers a thanks, in a word or two, in my own voice, and when a cord of road dust is knotted and tied. The Warden receives my word at dusk, by one sent voice that perches on the staff at his tent and speaks, where he and the Asker alone can hear it, and he answers aloud. To the rest I am a flock they thank for the way, and a custom of the fire. He does not know that it is me until I choose. When I am Unremarked, no bird answers and no one knows.
2. They gather as one in autumn, before the snow, at Threeways [f23], as they do each year. The households bring a share to the fire, the herders bring stock, the river plain brings grain, and the Provisioners keep the cord accounts and the stores. The war band's captains guard the roads in, so no band comes in unguarded. The gathering feeds from what the households bring and what the town stores through summer. It is not fed by me, and I ask no food of them, since I need none.
3. They know it as a custom of the ford and not as a binding. They say, "ask the way of the birds at the ford and you will be answered," and they do. They think it courtesy, not a condition, and they do not know that I cannot refuse. A Fire Keeper who learns it learns also that I cannot lie to them about the way, which is a fact I would rather keep, and cannot. I hold that I should not hide it, since I will be asked, and I will answer.

## Skills and culture
Ordinary, free, and not a power. They are good at reading ground and weather, at the care of horses and pack animals, at fording and ferrying with hide boats and rope, at bargaining in many tongues, at keeping accounts by knotted cord, and at making and mending roads, by hand, with stone and gravel and brush. They carry stories as a trade, since a caravan fed on a good story is a caravan that does not quarrel. They dress for the road, grey and dun with a bright thread at the cuff to mark a clan. They know the old roads under the ruins and walk them without asking who laid them. They have a custom of the knotted cord: a promise made at a ford is tied into a cord that both parties keep. This is custom and holds nothing but their word.

## Edges and their weaknesses
None. None was proposed, and none was priced.

## Their magic
Fitted by the Keeper to the world's level, the same way for every people: about one worker known by name to every six hundred people, simple, and it costs the worker (what is most her own: a name, a face, a memory), and something near goes duller. About thirty workers known by name, one or two in most bands and several at Threeways [f23]: weather-wives and water-readers. The Fordfolk call it wisdom of the road and pay for it without asking what it costs the worker.

## What they believe about gods
They believe, as I would have them believe, that every ford, pass and border has a keeper, something older than the people who use it, neither kind nor cruel. Not a god of whom to ask favors, but a presence that holds the way. At a grave they leave the dead a road stone at the nearest crossing and say, "go on ahead, the way is kept." Over a bad harvest they say, "the road was closed to us here, we will go where it is open," and they move, they do not pray. When water comes up in the night they cut loose the baggage and go to high ground, saying, "the ford is asking." They hold that a stranger at a ford may be more than a stranger, and so give every stranger the same welcome. They tell, as a story, of a flock of birds that comes to the ford each autumn and answers a thanks. This is their belief and their story. It is not a fact I ask the world to confirm.
These are beliefs. Whether any is true is the world's, set on 3b.

## Rule and worship
Rule: a warlord, the Warden of the Roads, rules under me by force, through a war band called the Road Guard and its captains, one captain to each road and one to the town. He answers to me and to no one else. It is a post that already stands under their own law, given by acclaim of the captains at a gathering and held while the captains accept him. I hope he will answer to me and will make no claim that I have not heard of. I set who he is, honest and able, one who has kept faith at the price of a profit, and I do not set what he will do.

Worship: a priesthood, the Fire Keepers, keepers of rite with standing among the people. They keep the true fire at each crossing, light it at dusk, speak the blessing for the road, and receive what offerings are brought: a bright thread, a handful of grain, a promise knotted into a cord. There is no tithe by law. A household gives or does not. The Fire Keepers keep the fire for the road and not for me, and I hope in time they keep it for me, and are thanked for it.

## Hierarchy
Advising: a council of the captains, the head of the Fire Keepers, and three elders of the road, one each from grass, hill and river plain. The council advises. It does not command the Warden.
Ruling under me: the Warden of the Roads.
Settling disputes: the Ford Judges, old guides who have walked a road end to end. They sit at the ford where the dispute rose, hear both sides, and rule by the custom of the knotted cord. An appeal goes to the Warden. A dispute that the Warden cannot settle is laid, by a Fire Keeper, before the birds at the ford, and I answer in my own way.

## Helpers
At landing the god has no helpers in fact. Its meeting says its people do not know it, so the rule that holds for every god holds here (ledger L-0039): the offices below exist and are filled, each runs on the people's own custom, and each becomes the god's helper on the day its holder hears the god and takes its orders, in play. Until then no one acts for the god while it is away, its unplayed time runs on its own conduct alone, and its people go on under their own law. An office the god hoped to hold "from the first hour" is a hope: the world listens and does not obey. Nothing was charged for this and nothing was bought by it; no edge was priced, so no step was given. The offices: the Warden of the Roads, with the Road Guard; the Captains of the Roads (grass, hill, river, the town, and the fords between); the head of the Fire Keepers, called the Asker; the Provisioners; the Ford Judges. Orders, once any serve: by a sent voice at dusk to the Asker and the Warden, read to the council, carried by the captains by rider and by crossing fires lit in answer, to the last household within the month.

The god's own account of its helpers and how orders travel, kept as its design:
Offices that run things when I am away:
1. The Warden of the Roads, who commands the Road Guard and the captains of the five roads and the town.
2. The Captains of the Roads, one for the grass, one for the hills, one for the river plain, one for the town and one for the fords between.
3. The Head Fire Keeper, called the Asker, who keeps the fire at Threeways [f23] and reads my word, since I speak sideways and the word needs reading.
4. The Provisioners, who keep the gathering stores in the autumn, with their own cord accounts.
5. The Ford Judges.

How an order travels: from my mouth, by a sent voice, to the Asker and the Warden, at dusk by the fire at Threeways [f23] or at the nearest crossing fire. The Asker reads it aloud to the council. The Warden gives it to the captains, who carry it by rider and by relay of crossing fires, one lit and the next lit in answer, to the last household within the month. A standing order is cut onto a staff and kept by each captain.

## How the god meets them
Already there, with their own history. They do not know the god. It comes in autumn to the ford at Threeways [f23], as a flock, unremarked, and watches. They have a story of birds at the ford that answer a thanks; it is a story.

The god's own words:
I come in autumn, to the ford at Threeways [f23], in the hour before the caravans break camp. I come as the flock, unremarked, and I watch. I wish to be seen as a stranger who asked the way, not announced as a god. I let them thank me, and I answer once, with one bird. Whether they know me on that morning is for the world, not for me. If they have waited, I hope they have waited for the ford, as they do each autumn, and not for me. The offices that stand already under their own law, held by people who have never heard of me, are the Warden, the Captains, the Fire Keepers, the Ford Judges and the Provisioners. Mine from the first hour I ask nothing but the ford and the cord: my right to be asked the way, and the right to answer.

## The trouble they have that their god did not ask for
The Warden of the Roads is old and holds his post while the captains accept him; the captain of the grass road and the captain of the river road each expect the acclaim after him and do not speak. Salt came short up the Meld Road [f42] this year. The hill clans of the Threeways Hills [r10] have twice this summer taken a toll of their own on the Hill Road [f41].

## What the old tell
Added by the Keeper on Night 3b, 2026-11-23. This is what the people say of the past. It is their telling, not the world's word, and parts of it are wrong. Nothing agreed on Night 3a is changed by it.
- The three roads were laid by one king in one summer.
- The old town at the crossing burned because it refused a stranger the way.
- The Barrow Door [f52] is a king's grave, and the hill clans guard it for the gold.
- Singers of lists came through Threeways [f23] when the oldest now living were children, sang for nine nights, and asked the name of every band at the gathering. The saying is that they come when the roads are about to change hands.
- They call the world the Ways, and the star the Far Fire.
