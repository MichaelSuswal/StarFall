# The Carrow: the world's side

Written by the Keeper on Night 3a, 2026-11-23. The public record is the DM's (`campaigns/sim-05/civs/carrow/record.md`); this file holds what the world answers and what the Keeper fits. Nothing here is told to the god; the people's own knowledge is in `campaigns/sim-05/civs/carrow/knowledge.md`.

## The items for the Keeper to reconcile, answered

1. Where Carrowford [f20] stands. As the player wished: on the rise above the ford of the Braidwater [f03], where the Old North Road [f40] comes down to the river, in the Carrow Plain [r07]. The god comes down on a second low rise at the plain's edge, about 3 miles south-west of the town, so that the town shows as its smoke, as the brief had it.
2. How long. Many generations: the Carrow count twelve Judges of the Ford before the present one and reckon about three hundred years. They are roughly right. The dates are 3b's.
3. What came before. The road was laid by people before them, as they believe: the kings of the fallen kingdom whose roads cross the whole land. The Carrow are not their heirs and do not claim to be. Who those kings were is 3b's.
4. The name. It is older than they are. It was the road-builders' word for a paved crossing, and it came with the place. They are right that it is the old word for the ford and wrong that the word was ever theirs.
5. Past their borders. North, the foothills and the Varrun Gate [f50], 17.4 miles, 0.9 days on foot by the Old North Road [f40], and above it the Varrun [the high-country people of r02], hard neighbors who trade at the gate twice a year. West, the Windrow Steppe [r06], open, and beyond it the horse-herders of the Orvan Steppe [r05]. South, the Old North Road [f40] to Threeways [f23], 197.2 miles, 9.9 days on foot, and the road people. South-east, the Meldrin Vale [r14] and the old kingdom. East, the Greystone Uplands [r03]. So: some wild country, an old kingdom, a hard mountain neighbor, as the player wished on Day 1. The far-off surveyor is not a place and is 3b's cast or no one.
6. Whether the gap can be seen from the walls. The line of foothills is on the skyline from the walls and from the landing, about 10 miles north. The gap itself, the Varrun Gate [f50], lies 17.4 miles from the town by the road and is not picked out by the eye from either place under the sight rule (a point is seen within two miles on open ground). The Carrow know where it is and can point the way. The first line can be started from there, a day's walk up the road; whether and when is the player's, and the open conduct line is held (below).
7. The town as described stands. The wall has not been tested lately because nothing has come down through the gap in living memory: the Varrun keep an old bargain made with the road-builders, and the kingdom downriver is tired. The Carrow do not know why they have been left alone and put it down to their own good sense.
8. The fence quarrels have run for years, as said. The oldest boundaries on the wet ground follow lines of set stones older than the Carrow, whose meaning no one remembers: those are the fences that are "nobody's", and no Judge has had a roll to settle them by.
9. The small gods of hearth, river and road are the people's belief. Nothing answers to those names.
10. Their measures. A ford-foot is a little longer than the god's foot: twenty-five ford-feet make twenty-six of the god's feet. A Carrow pace is five ford-feet. A Carrow mile is a thousand paces, 5,200 of the god's feet. The standards are an iron bar and a bushel in the market wall. The god will find this by measuring; the people can tell it their own figures.
11. The eighty. They do not stand at landing. The town pays a watch of about thirty for the gate, the market and the ford, under the Reeve. The captain of the muster is called up in danger, as the player said. The rest of the eighty, and their drill, are raised in play.
12. Magic. The world's level is the same here as everywhere: about eight workers known by name among the Carrow, fitted as for every people (about one to every six hundred), simple, and it costs the worker. The midwife's herbs are not all herbs; one of the ferrymen does more than read the river; the smith who talks to the iron only talks. The Carrow hold that the worker's debt is owed to whoever taught her, which is partly wrong. This is the surprise the player asked for, found in play.

## Helpers
None at landing, as agreed at the table (ledger L-0039). The Reeve, the Judge of the Ford and the Clerk of the Tally hold their seats under the town's law; the captain of the muster is not a standing post; the Warden of Marks does not exist. Each becomes a helper on the day its holder takes the god's orders. Until then no one acts for the god, and unplayed time runs on its conduct alone. The same rule is written in every NPC people's record.

## The trouble they have that their god did not ask for
The spring melt is high and the lower fields are under water later than usual. Salt and iron both came short up the road last year, and the smiths are reworking old tools. One of the fence quarrels has turned into a blood grievance between two districts.

## The sealed gifts and the people
The god's hidden powers and knacks (`player/hidden-powers.md`, drawn in mirror mode) are told to no one and nothing in this people is shaped to bring them out. Where they may meet the Carrow by the god's own action: the Carrow honor whoever is early, and the god's knack of arriving ahead of the hour will be seen by them as character before anyone thinks it a gift; ferrymen and carters will notice a knot that does not slip; the early-rising farmers plan by the weather and will notice a stranger who is right about rain. None of this is delivered, scheduled or hinted. Each shows only when the god does the thing.
