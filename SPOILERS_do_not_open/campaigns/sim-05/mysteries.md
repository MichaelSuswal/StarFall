## E01 The Widening Quiet
Kind: epic. Span: 60 years. Related to: L02, L03, L04, S04, S08.
Answers questions: no

### Truth
The living thing at the Sleeper's Brow [f65] is a hoarder of the deep lore's kind, older than the last fall. It is fed down the blue thread, on the nights the star dims, by those in the star who want the fall soon, the Crowned among them. It takes nothing by force. Those who live in sight of it stop holding to what is particular: first each other's names, then whose field is whose, then the village. Whatever spends the land faster feeds it: the heavy working of a land where nearly every valley has a worker, and above all the gathered working at the School at Ombrel [f66]. It cannot be killed, driven or starved by any force a god here holds, and force used on it is taken in. What holds against it is what is stored and said: a village whose names are written and said aloud together each year does not empty, however near; names carried out by the Venn are kept elsewhere; less great working slows the feeding. The answer, once found, still has to be done, year on year, by the peoples who live round the downs, most of whom have no god: no one god's people touch more than a part of the ring.

Its central measurements: how fast the ring of emptied villages widens; that the thread ends on the Brow and nowhere else; that a village which keeps its names said has lost no one. Each can only be earned. The ways: a god's own marks set at the edge of the warm ground and at the last named village and read again after years; a counter it sends to count hearths and names under the downs, more than once; an alliance that opens the rolls of Meldry [f27] or the reckoning tablets in the Hall of Terms [u03], read reign against reign; going to the Brow on a dim night; going to the Dry Market [f61] and reading its roll. No letter, envoy or visitor in the schedule carries any of them: the Venn bring names and no measure, and no scheduled traveler has been to the Brow.

### Body
The Sleeper's Brow [f65] and the Sleeper's Downs [r15]. The thread. The emptied and emptying villages of the Meldrin Vale [r14] nearest the downs. The Dry Market [f61]. The reckoning tablets in the Hall of Terms [u03]. The glass floor of the Elder Granary [u05].

### Cost if ignored
A village under the downs is found empty in Year 9, and another each tenth year after, each a little farther out than the last. From Year 49 people begin to walk up onto the downs in their sleep and are not seen again: first those of the nameless villages, then, within five years, sleepers from the nearest settled edge of every country that borders the downs, the Meldrin Vale [r14], the Fordmeet Plain [r11], the Underlee Plain [r16] and the Tollwater Reach [r24] alike. Meldrin, which sits on the river that feeds the trade of the peoples about it, loses the west of its vale.
Cost check: checked against the player's card (True ground, The paced claim, What is held, The whole map, The return, The slow chain; L-0001 to L-0013, L-0036): the card says a place held by more than ordinary craft "may read wrong or not at all. Unruled until it happens", so how the Brow reads is ruled when a god stands there and nothing here settles it beforehand; no cost contradicts a drawback. Checked against `god-roads` (Unremarked, The Closing, L-0020 to L-0022) and `god-fate` (The Bill, L-0025: no price in a name), whose peoples border the ring: the loss of names here is no price any god takes and no bill any god writes. Checked against `god-harvest` (The Taking of a Name, twelve names held): a name it holds is its own holding and is not what is lost here. L-0015 and L-0017 (nothing marked or carried at a distance): the thread is seen by eyes, not felt. None is contradicted.

### Clock or trigger
Clock: Years 9, 19, 29 and 39, a village; Year 49, the walking begins (schedule rows C06 and C07). Triggers: a gathering of workers past this star's threshold or one great working anywhere in the land brings every later year of this clock forward by ten (row T04); force used against the Brow brings it forward by five and returns those who used it without their names (row T01); the yearly saying of names taken up in a village stops the clock for that village (row T02).

### Clues
#### Names gone slack under the downs
Location: the villages of the Meldrin Vale [r14] within two days' walk of the Sleeper's Downs [r15]
What is shown: Seen. People there point, or say "her at the well". Headstones in the yards carry names down to a certain generation and are blank after it. Asked, they are not troubled by it. Earliest: any year a god or its people go there.
Readings: A custom or a taboo. Poverty, and the young leaving. A sickness of the mind in bad water. Something on the downs does it. The old kings' favor withdrawn, as the Meldrin say.

#### The roll with the blanks
Location: the Dry Market [f61]
What is shown: Seen. In a stone press, the last reeve's roll of stallholders: names entered, then the same stalls with the names struck through, then the stalls listed by place alone. No sign of fire or sack in the town.
Readings: A plague, with the dead struck off. A tax dodge. A reeve losing his wits. The townspeople stopped using names before they left.

#### The thread on a dim night
Location: the sky over the Sleeper's Downs [r15], as seen from high ground in the Meldrin Vale [r14], the Fordmeet Plain [r11] or the Underlee Plain [r16]
What is shown: Reported, then seen. For about an hour on a night when the star dims, a thin line of blue light stands between the star and the downs. Shepherds call it the plumb line. Earliest: Year 1, to anyone on that ground who is awake and looking.
Readings: The star drinks from the land. The star feeds something. The star speaks to the old kings. A trick of thin cloud.

#### No weather on the Brow
Location: the Sleeper's Brow [f65]
What is shown: Seen, by going. Snow does not lie there. No wind moves the grass. The ground is warm to the hand in frost. No bird nests on it. Rain can be watched falling all round and not on it.
Readings: A hot spring under the hill. Holy ground. A beast asleep, as the shape says. A grave of something that is still warm.

#### The dream on the downs
Location: the Sleeper's Downs [r15], within a day's walk of the Sleeper's Brow [f65]
What is shown: Seen, by sleeping there. Tall figures without faces standing on islands that drift on a still sea, counting something the dreamer cannot see. On a dim night the dream is clearer.
Readings: The dead of the old realm. The beast's own thoughts. The star's eyes. The dreamer's own fear of the place.

#### The tablets of things that ceased
Location: the Hall of Terms [u03], behind the Door of Terms [f53]
What is shown: Seen, only if the hall is entered. Clay and stone tablets, one stack for each reign of the realm: lists of things that were and are not. A fish of the Meld. A red barley. A way of saying "mother" on the lower river. Villages, by name. Each reign's stack is taller than the one before.
Readings: Clerks who listed everything. Losses in war and flood. A curse being reckoned. The land growing old. Something taking them.

#### The village that keeps the saying
Location: one village of the Meldrin Vale [r14], the nearest of all to the Sleeper's Downs [r15], unnamed in the record
What is shown: Seen. At midwinter its people stand in the yard and say every name in the village, the living and the dead of the last three generations, from a board kept in the headman's house. Its headstones are all cut. It is poorer than its neighbors and has lost no one that anyone can tell of.
Readings: A stubborn old custom of the realm. They are too poor to leave. Their well is good. The saying keeps them.

#### The floor that rings
Location: the Elder Granary [u05], under Orrenmark [f26], its lowest gallery
What is shown: Seen, only by going down past the cellars the town uses. A floor of blue-grey glass, the blue of the star, that rings when struck. The bins were cut down to it and no farther.
Readings: A floor the Binwrights made. Slag from a great fire. A thing from the sky. The top of something buried deeper.

### Branches
#### The god attacks the Brow, with fire, iron, trench or a great working
What the world does: Nothing on the Brow burns or breaks. Those who labor there come back, over a season, unable to say whose they are, a dozen or more. The clock comes forward five years. The thread is seen on the next dim night as always. It teaches that this is a being and that force is a gift to it.

#### The god forbids the downs and sets a watch round them
What the world does: The watch can be kept. The villages go on emptying at the same rate, because no one was going up. From Year 49 the watch turns back some of the sleepers and loses some of its own. It teaches that the harm does not come from walking there.

#### The god moves the nearest villages away
What the world does: Those moved who keep saying their names stay themselves; those who do not are as slack in the new place within a generation. The ground left empty is empty. The ring's next village is the next one out. It teaches that distance buys time and nothing else.

#### The god has the names written and said, in its own villages and as many others as will take it up
What the world does: Where the saying is kept, no village empties, from that year. Within a decade the difference between a saying village and its neighbor can be counted. It is the world's plain yes. It holds only for as long as the saying is kept and only where it is kept.

#### The god forbids all working among its own people
What the world does: Its people's small workers stop or hide. The old and the sick are worse off for it and say so. The clock does not move: the small workings were never the weight. It teaches that the spending that matters is the great and the gathered.

#### The god breaks up the School at Ombrel, or has it kept small
What the world does: Broken by force, its teachers answer with one working, the ground about Ombrel dies for a day's walk, and the clock comes forward ten years. Kept small by treaty with the Vessari, with its learners sent home, the feeding slows: each later year of the clock goes back by five. The towns of the coast do not forgive a god who shames the school.

#### The god worships the thing, or lets its people do so
What the world does: It gives nothing back. Those who pray toward it go slack faster than their neighbors. It teaches that this is not a god and not kin to one.

#### The god learns of it and does nothing
What the world does: The clock runs as written. The world does not punish beyond it.

### Never found
How old it is, and whether it was a beast once. Who in the star first fed it. What it will be after the next fall. Why it wants what it wants.

## L01 The Stone in the Grass
Kind: long. Span: 30 years. Related to: L03.
Answers questions: no

### Truth
The Fallen Stone [f64] is the Remainder of the deep lore: dross of the same far fall that threw the gods of this arrival, holding at lower weight the same memories they are made of. It fell on the night they came down. It dreams those memories at anyone who sleeps near it. A mortal dreams a crowd kneeling by a great river that is not in this land, and a long table laid out of doors. A god that touches it or sleeps by it dreams the same gathering from the seat that was its own, as each sealed past gives it, and on a second night one thing more of its own that it has lost; the stone never gives the whole. It lends a little of what it holds to those who pray to it, as a god's worshipers lend to a god, the other way about.

### Body
The Fallen Stone [f64], on the Windrow Steppe [r06]: waist-high, blue-grey, heavy past its size, warm.

### Cost if ignored
If no god has reached it by Year 19, the summer herders who have been sleeping beside it for luck, Orvanni and grass-road Fordfolk both, have by then a following of their own, some three hundred, with dream-tellers who recite what they dream: the grief and the loss of gods they have never met, said aloud at the horse fairs. It is no god's and feeds no god. If one god reaches it first, that god comes to know the shape of the others' pasts, which is a lever over each of them.
Cost check: checked against every card's return power and L-0016 (a memory price is one item removed by logged roll): the stone gives a god back nothing that a return has taken and removes nothing; a dream is not a record item until the god's own agent writes it. Checked against L-0013 (what the player's god lost is found only by its own action): the stone shows the player's god nothing unless it goes there and sleeps. Checked against L-0015 and L-0017: the stone cannot be felt at a distance by any god. Checked against `god-storms` (Answer to Rite, Hunger for Worship, L-0035) and `god-roads` (Unremarked): a following of the stone is not a rite to either and is no worship of either. None is contradicted.

### Clock or trigger
Clock: Year 19, the following stands, if no god has touched the stone (schedule row C08). Triggers: the first god to touch it (row T05); the stone moved from its place (row T06).

### Clues
#### The warm place in the grass
Location: the Windrow Steppe [r06], round the Fallen Stone [f64]
What is shown: Rumored from Year 2 at Kes Orvan [f21] and at Threeways [f23], among herders. A place where frost does not lie in a ring, and a stone in the middle that was not there before. Seen by going.
Readings: A piece of the star. A sign from the sky. A hot spring coming up. A herder's tale to keep others off good grass.

#### What the sleepers dream
Location: the Fallen Stone [f64]
What is shown: Reported by any herder who has slept there; seen by sleeping there. A crowd kneeling by a wide river, a table out of doors longer than a camp, and waking with a grief that is not about anything.
Readings: The stone is a god that did not wake. The stone is cursed. It shows the old realm. It shows where the gods came from.

#### The color and the weight
Location: the Fallen Stone [f64]
What is shown: Seen. It is the blue-grey of the star, and four men cannot lift what two should.
Readings: It fell from the star. It is metal, and worth digging for more. It is an egg.

#### The singers will not sing there
Location: the Venn, on their way across the Windrow Steppe [r06] in Year 7
What is shown: Seen, only by one who travels with them or watches. They turn aside to the stone, sit a night beside it in silence, and do not ask its name.
Readings: They fear it. They know it. It is one of theirs. They have nothing to give a thing that has no name.

### Branches
#### The god carries it home as a holy thing
What the world does: Its town dreams the kneeling crowd and the god's own grief. Within four years there is a devotion to that grief, which is remembering and not following, and lends the god nothing. The following on the steppe does not form.

#### The god buries or hides it
What the world does: Those who sleep above it still dream. The following forms ten years later, round a place and not a stone.

#### The god breaks it
What the world does: It breaks like stone. Each piece is warm and dreams more faintly. The pieces travel in saddlebags, and the dream is told at more fires.

#### The god sleeps by it, once or again
What the world does: It is given its own seat at the gathering, and on a second night one lost thing of its own, as its sealed past has it. Nothing is explained to it.

#### Another god reaches it first
What the world does: That god dreams its own seat, and, if it stays among the sleepers and listens to what they recite, comes to know the shapes of the others. What it does with that is its own.

#### The god leaves it alone
What the world does: The clock runs as written.

### Never found
The name of the far country. Whose the kneeling crowd was. Whether the stone could have woken.

## L02 The Crowned Gone Up
Kind: long. Span: 30 years. Related to: E01, L04.
Answers questions: yes

### Truth
Under this star a god that holds more than a certain weight of worship grows light and rises into the star. The measure is 80 on the lore's scale; a god newly come down stands near 20. The Crowned passed it one after another and rose: Hathra in 1,930 BA, Corvane in 1,790 BA, Ilmar, against its will, in 1,660 BA. They and older ones are in the star and can reach down. When any god's standing passes 64, they speak to it in dreams and offer a power; taking it costs weight held and brings the god nearer the measure. They are of the party that wants the fall, and soon.
Limit: The voices know what they were and what they want, which is the fall; they do not know what a risen god becomes after a fall, why gods are thrown, or anything the ground has done since each rose beyond what dreamers near the Brow carry up. They speak only into the dreams of a god whose standing has passed four fifths of this star's measure, or of anyone asleep within a day's walk of the Brow on a dim night; once in a night; in images and one plain sentence; and they never answer the same question twice. They cannot state a falsehood, and they leave out whatever does not serve the fall.
A god may keep below the measure by giving what it holds into people and places, as Ilmar tried too late; or carry more than the measure by binding itself to a mountain, a river or a people's one sung name, which then spends in its stead; or refuse to grow.

### Body
The Worn Altar [f59]. The Hollow Hall [f63]. The Roofless Hall [f62]. The rolls of Meldry [f27]. A god's own shadow. The star.
Limit: The rolls of Meldry [f27] hold what clerks entered, tolls, reigns, cases and oddities, from 2,150 BA to now, with gaps, and no cause for anything. Anyone the king's clerks admit may read them, one roll in a day; there is no index, and a question is answered only by the reader's own search.

### Cost if ignored
None while every god stays small. When a god's standing passes 64 the offers begin, and a god that takes them without understanding rises within a decade. A god that stands above 80 for three years together rises on the next dim night and is gone from the ground for good. The world warns three times: the shadow pales; its temple stone goes soft at the edges; its prayers come back as echoes. It does not warn a fourth time.
Cost check: checked against every card's Ceiling line ("Set by the world. Not shown.") and Growth line: nothing here shows the measure. Checked against `god-storms` (Hunger for Worship, Answer to Rite, L-0035) and `god-roads` (the trade, L-0021): each is bound to seek or to answer worship, and nothing here forbids or punishes worship below the measure; the warnings come to each alike. Checked against the player's card, which asks compliance and not worship: standing reads minds that hold the god's shape, whatever the god calls it. Checked against L-0016: a rising is not a return and takes no record item. None is contradicted.

### Clock or trigger
Triggers only: a god's standing passes 64 (schedule row T07); a god stands above 80 for three years together (row T08). Standing is read from `state/standing.md`.

### Clues
#### The altar that is smooth, not worn
Location: the Worn Altar [f59]
What is shown: Seen. The face is gone, but the stone is not pitted or chipped: its edges are soft, like a cake of salt licked by cattle. On its back, scratched small: "She went up like chaff. We had done nothing wrong."
Readings: Weather and a thousand years of hands. She was taken as a punishment. She was taken as a reward. She left them.

#### The word after the echo
Location: the Hollow Hall [f63]
What is shown: Seen, by going in and calling out. The call comes back, and after it one more word, soft, that was not called. It is the same word each time. Herders of the Sable Plain [r20] will not sleep there.
Readings: A trick of the roof. A ghost. The name of whoever built it. A word left over from a great many people saying it.

#### The hall with its roof taken off
Location: the Roofless Hall [f62]
What is shown: Seen. The roof beams were lifted out, not burned or fallen; the sockets are clean. Two standing stones and a notched third inside still frame the star exactly.
Readings: A temple to the sky. A place to watch the star, which has not moved. The Orvanni say a king went up through the roof. The stones were set for something else and happen to fit.

#### The king a hand above the floor
Location: the rolls of Meldry [f27]
What is shown: Reported, to whoever searches the rolls of the last Crowned reign. A clerk's entry, three years running: "the king stood this day a hand above the floor of the hall, and came down, and was not pleased to be seen."
Readings: A clerk's flattery. A sickness. Pride struck down. He was leaving.

#### The god's own shadow
Location: carried by any god whose standing has passed 64
What is shown: Seen, by the god. Its shadow is paler than a mortal's beside it. Mortals who love it cannot bring its face to mind when it is away.
Readings: Illness. The star's blessing. The star's hunger. Too much asked of it.

#### The offer in the dream
Location: carried by any god whose standing has passed 64; or the Sleeper's Downs [r15] on a dim night
What is shown: Seen, by the god. A figure with no face, on an island that moves, shows it something it cannot do and says what it would cost: "weight".
Readings: The beast tempts. The star rewards. A rival's trick. The old kings want company.

### Branches
#### The god, feeling light, asks more worship to root itself
What the world does: It grows lighter. The warnings come in order.

#### The god takes an offer
What the world does: The power is real and is written on its card with its drawback. Its standing rises by what was taken.

#### The god refuses
What the world does: Nothing is taken from it. Another voice, not the first, says: "Then you will be below when we come down, and that is also a place."

#### The god gives what it holds into people and places
What the world does: Its standing falls by what it gives. What it gave stays where it was put, and is more itself than its neighbors for as long as it is kept.

#### The god binds itself to a mountain, a river or a sung name
What the world does: It may stand above the measure and stay on the ground. The thing it bound itself to spends in its place: springs fail, bends soften, a people forgets everything that is not the god.

#### The god stays small
What the world does: It hears nothing, ever.

### Never found
Which voice is which of the Crowned. The measure as a number. What the first to rise was.

## L03 The Singers of Lists
Kind: long. Span: 30 years. Related to: E01, L01.
Answers questions: yes

### Truth
The Venn are carriers of the deep lore's kind. A name sung by enough mouths for enough years is kept, across empty country and across a fall. They take names from places that are failing and sing them where people still are. They are not kind and not cruel. They will carry a people's names if asked and taught them properly, and may carry them unasked if they judge the place worth it. This line comes through in Year 7 and in Year 36 and not otherwise within sixty years.
Limit: The Venn know the names they carry and the order they are sung in, and that a name carried long enough comes back; they do not know what any name was the name of beyond what the list's own words say, nothing of the far country but its names, and nothing of the Brow, the stone or the star. Anyone may ask them, by day, one question for each night that person has listened to them through; they answer in a line of the song or say they do not carry it; under compulsion they sing nothing.

### Body
The Venn, some forty walkers, on the way the deep history gives: down the Rimewater [f01], across the Windrow Steppe [r06] to Threeways [f23], the Meld Road [f42] to Meldry [f27] and Tollford [f29], and out through the Ilwemere [r23].

### Cost if ignored
A god that has not dealt with them by the end of the Year 36 passing has no way, inside sixty years, to have any name of its people kept beyond this land or past a fall. Nothing else is lost.
Cost check: checked against `god-harvest` (The Taking of a Name, twelve held; its Keeper trait, that no name given to it holds past the turning of the year): the Venn carry place and people names in their own mouths and put none into any god's keeping, so neither the twelve nor the trait is touched. Checked against `god-fate` (L-0025: no price in a name): the Venn ask no price. Checked against L-0013 and the player's sealed answers: names from the far country are in the list as places only, and reach a god only if it listens or asks. Checked against L-0017: word of their coming travels at the pace of those who carry it. None is contradicted.

### Clock or trigger
Clock: Year 7, the first passing; Year 36, the second (schedule rows C04 and C05). Trigger: if they are held by force, the second passing does not come (row T09).

### Clues
#### The tune without the words
Location: Ilwenstead [f32]
What is shown: Reported, to anyone who trades at the marsh edge and asks about strangers. The Ilwe hum a long tune their grandmothers heard from walkers they guided through, and have none of the words.
Readings: A hymn. A rowing song. A list. Something the Ilwe made up.

#### They ask the names and sing them back
Location: the Venn, wherever they sleep
What is shown: Seen. They trade nothing. They ask the names of every village within a day and sing them back exactly, then sing through the night.
Readings: Spies. Madmen. A holy order. Tax-takers for someone far away.

#### Names of places that are gone
Location: the Venn
What is shown: Reported by anyone who listens a whole night at Threeways [f23] or Meldry [f27]. Among the names are the old realm's name for the burned town at the three roads, and village names from under the downs that are in no living mouth.
Readings: They robbed the old rolls. Their forebears were of the realm. They have been here before, long ago. They invent them.

#### The same names behind the door
Location: the Fell Hall [u04]
What is shown: Seen, only by one who has been let in. The lists kept there hold the realm's village names, and the Doorward says them aloud when she wakes. Some are names the Venn also sing.
Readings: The Venn learned them from her. She learned them from the Venn. Two keepings of one thing.

### Branches
#### The god asks them what the songs are
What the world does: One of them says they carry names across the bare country, and that a name carried long enough comes back. Nothing more.

#### The god gives them its people's names, taught as they ask
What the world does: It takes a season of their teaching and delays them by it. They carry the names. Nothing is seen to come of it for decades; then, at the Year 36 passing, they sing them back, and say where else they have been sung.

#### The god gives them nothing
What the world does: Nothing happens, which is the answer.

#### The god holds them to learn the songs by force
What the world does: They sing nothing. No one of them holds the whole song. The Year 36 passing does not come.

#### The god asks them for the names of the far country
What the world does: They sing that part of the list, for a night. It is names, in order, and nothing about them.

### Never found
Where the song began. Whether any name they carry has yet come back.

## L04 The Doorkeeper's Stores
Kind: long. Span: 30 years. Related to: L02, E01, S05.
Answers questions: yes

### Truth
The last of the Crowned, Ilmar, tried to stay on the ground by giving what it held into things, and had the Varrun cut a hall for each: the realm's measures and boundary roll in the Stane Hall [u01]; its terms and its tablets of things ceased in the Hall of Terms [u03]; its names, and their keeper, in the Fell Hall [u04]; and a spring, capped, at the Capped Spring [f55] over the Capstone Chamber [u07]. What was given keeps what is stored with it more itself than anything outside. The Barrow Hall [u02] is not a store. It is a prison: Ummer, a small eater of the same kind as the thing at the Brow, chained in bronze by Corvane in 2,040 BA and walled in by Ilmar, with its name and its binder cut on the door.
The keeper in the Fell Hall [u04] is Tessaly Vaun, the Doorward, once Ilmar's chamberlain: neither a god nor any longer quite a mortal, glad of company, and tired.
Limit: The Doorward knows the names on her lists, the Crowned as she served them, and how Ilmar gave itself away; she knows nothing after 1,700 BA but what she has heard through the door, and nothing of why gods come or what the star is. She answers whoever has waited a night at the door without trying to force it; three questions at a waking, truthfully, in the realm's old speech; then she sleeps for a season. Of Ilmar's last years she says only that she was not there.

### Body
The Stane Door [f51] and the Stane Hall [u01]. The Barrow Door [f52] and the Barrow Hall [u02]. The Door of Terms [f53] and the Hall of Terms [u03]. The Fell Door [f54] and the Fell Hall [u04]. The Capped Spring [f55] and the Capstone Chamber [u07].

### Cost if ignored
None while the doors stay shut. The risk is that someone else opens one. The hill clans keep the Barrow Door [f52] and pay for the keeping with what they can take on the Hill Road [f41]; if they are stopped from taking it for two years together, the watch lapses, and five years after that herd-boys break the seal for the bronze. Ummer, loosed, is slow: within a season the hill villages within a day's walk begin to lose people as the work camp was lost, and the loss spreads a few miles in a year. The Stane Door [f51] forced, by anyone from below, ends two thousand years of the Varrun bargain: the gate is shut to trade and the Varrun come down in arms on whoever is nearest it.
