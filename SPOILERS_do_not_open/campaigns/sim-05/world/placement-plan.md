# Placement plan: sim-05

Written on Night 3a before any painting. A written geography, not a drawing. The paint decides where things lie; where the paint departs from this plan, the plan yields and the seats are set again on the painted ground. Places here carry working words only; their names and map ids come at the tracing, and every land description is written after it, from the map.

## The continent

One continent on one sheet, with sea on every side of the sheet so that no coast is cut off. Its land is about 1,100 miles from west to east and about 900 from north to south, nowhere under 650 across the whole shape (the aim: 800 to 1,200 on average, never under 600 at the narrowest). Open sea lies along the whole east and south; a narrower cold sea lies off the north-west.

- The north: a high range crowned with ice, running west to east across the whole top of the land, thickest and highest in the north-west. From the middle of it a lower spur of hills comes south, with one clear gap in it.
- Under the range, in the north-west and north: a broad band of steppe and high plain.
- The north-east: stony uplands where springs come out of rock.
- The west: the seams, where steppe, hill country and river plain meet.
- The south-west: a great forest meeting open grassland, hills behind.
- The center: open rolling country, godless, and at its middle one lone mass of low hills that has the plain shape of a great beast lying asleep.
- The south: a flood plain of black soil on one wide river, low hills between the plain and the uplands of the center.
- The south-east: the mouths of the eastern rivers, a delta with marsh at its edges.
- The east: a coast with one broad bay and a sheltered inlet in it; safe harbors are few on this coast.
- Rain shadow: the high plain under the northern range is dry; the forest and the southern plain take the sea's rain. Borders fall where the land changes: range foot, forest edge, marsh edge, river.

## The waters

Every river runs downhill to a coast.
- The cold river: from the ice of the north-west range across the steppe to the cold sea in the north-west.
- The north river: from the northern range south through the gap in the spur, braided across the northern plain past a ford, then south-east across the open country to the delta. The longest river.
- The spring river: from the stone springs of the north-east uplands south through long braided middle reaches with fords, through reed country, to the delta.
- The black-soil river: from the central hills south, wide and slow, across the flood plain to the middle of the south coast.
- The forest river: from the western hills south-west through grassland along the forest's edge to the south-west coast.
- A western stream out of the hill country, a tributary of the forest river, with the ford where three old ways cross.

## The seats, round the ring

Spaced evenly, each many weeks' walk from the next by the pace table, none crowded, none cut off. Ring order, fixed here for the placement roll: `god-storms` (north-west), the player's god (north), `god-rivers` (north-east), `god-sea` (east), `god-fate` (south-east), `god-harvest` (south), `god-beasts` (south-west), `god-roads` (west). Rough place on the sheet as hundredths across and down, for the tracing only.

- `god-storms`, north-west (22 across, 24 down): the foot of the ice range, steppe and open river country below, the cold coast within reach. Lands on a bare ridge above the steppe. Its people winter in a town of sod and timber on the cold river and move with the herds.
- The player's god, north (49, 27): river country opening onto high plain, flat ground, the spur of hills with its gap beyond. As its Day 3 brief has it: a low rise at the plain's edge above a braided reach of the north river; an old paved road coming down to a ford; a worn track along the bank; one thread of smoke. The town the player named stands on the rise by the ford, as wished: the land holds it (a ford on an old road is where a town of ferrymen, carters and drovers would stand), and the smoke of the brief is the town's.
- `god-rivers`, north-east (74, 25): headwater springs in stone and the braided middle reaches of the spring river, with fords. Lands on a stone ledge at a spring above the first reach. Its people have no seat; their camps lie along the river.
- `god-sea`, east (88, 49): the walled harbor in the inlet of the east bay. Lands on the shingle outside the harbor wall.
- `god-fate`, south-east (77, 72): the wide slow lower river with ford towns, marsh edges, harbors within reach. Lands on a reed bank at a marsh edge by a ford, by a ruined tollhouse. Its people travel a circuit and have no seat.
- `god-harvest`, south (53, 78): the black-soil flood plain, hills behind, a ford, a little marsh. Lands at the edge of a reaped field above the ford, in sight of the smoke of a walled town.
- `god-beasts`, south-west (27, 70): grassland meeting forest on the forest river, hills behind. Lands on open grass at the forest's edge by the river. A walled town within a day or so.
- `god-roads`, west (19, 47): the crossing of three old ways at a ford on the western stream, where grass, hill and river plain meet. A market town stands at the crossing.

## What the star and the lore place

- The center (50, 51): the hoarder, a sleeping beast that is visibly a beast, under the star: the lone mass of hills at the middle of the open country. The sheet shows it as land with a beast's shape.
- The south (65, 86): the school of workers, in godless country between the southern seats and the south coast.
- The warm stone that fell with the gods: in godless country between two gods, by the placement roll. Method, named in the roll before it was cast: one roll among the eight adjacent pairs round the ring above, `--die d8`; then where along the stretch, `--die d100`, 25 plus half the face, as hundredths of the way from the first-named seat. Results: pair 8, `god-roads` and `god-storms` [r-0144]; face 90, so 70 hundredths of the way from `god-roads`'s seat toward `god-storms`'s [r-0145]. It lies in the steppe margin between them, in neither seat's country (about 22 across, 31 down, to be set on the painted ground).
- The oldest, most worked ground, where the fading shows: set on 3b from the deep history.
- The edge of the rain: 70 days' walk from the center, well off the sheet on every side.

## The godless peoples, between the seats

About half the land outside the gods' countries is held and about half open (the Night 1 result). Four peoples (the Night 1 result of 5 on that scale gives four), the non-human ones counted among them:
- A high-country people, not human, few, with their own law and holds: in the northern range and the spur, above the passes, between `god-storms` and the player's god.
- A water-margin people, not human, few, strict in custom and fair in dealing: in the reed country of the lower spring river, between `god-rivers`, `god-sea` and `god-fate`.
- An old human kingdom of the middle river, rooted and tired: along the north river where it crosses the open country, east of the sleeping beast.
- A human country of the south coast, which holds the school of workers: between `god-harvest`, `god-fate` and the sea.
Open and wild: the steppe margins between the western and north-western seats; the deep forest of the south-west, the country of uncounted beasts; the center round the sleeping beast; the far north-east.

## Sites from the pools

Each a single site on the surface sheet, set at the tracing; none placed where its own wisher is sure to find it.
- Old paved roads: a network from a fallen kingdom, usable: through the player's ford; the three ways at the western crossing; along a dry bed in the north-east; down the lower river past the ruined tollhouses.
- Sealed doors in hill and mountain sides, each with a hall behind it (below): in the spur by the gap; in a hill by the western crossing; in the hills behind the lower river (the door with terms cut on it); in the hills behind the east coast (the door not known to have a lock).
- Great bones: in the ice of the north-west range; on the east shore; under the old road in the south-west.
- The great granary under the southern town; the half-sunk altar with its face worn off at a field's edge there.
- The lower harbor under the living harbor in the east.
- The capped spring in the north-east uplands.
- Ruined markets and tollhouses along the lower river.
- A roofless hall in the north-west; an empty hall in the south.

## The underground

Underground: yes, one layer, mostly solid rock with hollows only, shallow (cellar depth to a few hundred feet). No cave country, no people living below, no deep roads, no regions of its own. Its hollows are its only features, each under its mouth on the surface: the four door halls behind the four sealed doors; the great granary; the lower harbor; the capped spring. It is written into `map.json` as layer `under-1` before any painting request, each hollow a feature `uNN` placed from this plan's proportions, and moved with its mouth when the surface is traced.

## Every terrain on the Night 1 list

Great rivers with springs, braided reaches, fords and flood plains (the north, spring and black-soil rivers); high plains and steppe (the north and north-west); hill country (the west, the spur, the south); mountains with ice and passes (the north); grassland with forest edge (the south-west); marsh (the delta, the reed country); coast with harbors (the east bay, the delta); sea.
