# Negotiation: god-roads, Night 2

Your `self.md` was read against your sheet. Nothing is sent back. Below is each power as it would be written on your card, with the price and the Keeper's reasons. The card lines say "the god"; that is you.

## Round 1

### Sent Voices
Draft text:
Description: The god sends out a handful of its birds as a separate voice. The handful perches, flies, hears, and speaks in the god's voice to anyone it can reach, and the god in its body hears what the voice hears and speaks through it as it happens. Up to three voices at a time. Each ranges no further than a day's walk from the body, along a road or border the god has itself walked. What a voice saw reaches the god only when the voice rejoins the flock.
Drawback: (your choice, below)
Limits:
- One body. A sent voice is a part of the one body and never a second one: the god's will stays in the flock, and a voice can use no other power, cannot close into the figure, and can do nothing in the world but perch, fly, hear, speak and carry a spoken message.
- A voice is birds and can be killed, netted or caged. A voice killed is a part lost until the body mends; a voice caged is a part held.
- A voice sent back to where the body is adds nothing to the body.
- Other gods: a voice within arm's reach of another god is known for what it is, as the god itself would be. Anything a voice does to a god is unruled until it happens.
- Being watched: a handful of birds, until one speaks.
- Not applicable: falling, carrying, water.
Choices, for the Drawback:
 (a) The thinned court. With one voice out the god sees dimly in the body; with three it is nearly blind and hard of hearing there. While any voice is out the god cannot close into the figure, and it loses track of which voice said what: the Keeper may have it mistake its own sent word for another's.
 (b) The lost voice. The body keeps its senses and may close. Any voice that is killed or caged takes an item with it: the Keeper removes one item from the god's record, by logged roll, for each voice that does not come home.
The Keeper's reasons: Every god here has one body and is in one place at a time. Hearing and speaking in three other places a day's walk off presses hard on that, so it is written as a power, priced, with the body's limits kept. Your own offer is (a), with one line added: a court that is spread cannot also stand as one figure. (b) is the other way to pay, falling on you only when a voice is lost. Sight is left for the return of the voice because your trade below asks for it.

### Passage Given
Draft text:
Description: The god binds a traveler, or a company of up to thirty, to one named road it has itself walked, no longer than a man walks in a day and a half, with a knotted cord of road-dust and its word. While the cord is carried on that road, the road holds: the ford is passable, the pass is open if chance and patience could open it, the weather is no worse than the season's worst, and the traveler does not lose the way.
Drawback: (your choice, below)
Limits:
- It stops no arrow, bandit or armed guard. It shades chance on the road; it does nothing chance could not.
- One cord for one road. The cord works for whoever carries it, a thief included. A cord lost to a river holds until it reaches the sea.
- A footpath, a ford or a border crossing counts as a road. A province does not.
- Other gods: a road another god has closed by its own working is unruled until it happens.
- Being watched: the knotting is plain to anyone watching; the cord looks like a cord.
- Not applicable: light and dark, falling.
Choices, for the Drawback:
 (a) The road is lost. From the day the cord is knotted the god can never again set foot or wing on that road, in any form, whether or not the cord still exists.
 (b) The cord crumbles. The cord is dust the moment the god sets foot or wing on that road again, and the passage ends with it.
The Keeper's reasons: You offered both and asked the Keeper to choose; the choice is yours. They are not equal. (b) is a price this power's size can bear: each gift costs you the use of that road for as long as you want the gift to stand. (a) is heavier than the power needs and buys nothing more. Walking the road first is already a Limit in both, since you said you cannot give a road you have not walked.

### Unremarked
Draft text:
Description: In either shape, mortals take no notice of the god. The flock reads as ordinary birds; the closed figure as a traveler no one can picture a moment after. A mortal can look straight at it and keep nothing.
Drawback: While Unremarked is held the god can receive no worship or thanks: offerings, prayers and the notice of anyone who would honor it fall past it, and it feels them fall. Held for a full season without a break, it costs an item: the Keeper removes one item from the god's record by logged roll, for each full season.
Limits:
- It fails against anyone who has already seen the god plainly, anyone who touches it, and any god.
- One who is watching for it on purpose, told what to look for, is a contest and is rolled.
- It unmakes no memory of a meeting held with the cloak down, and hides nothing the flock drops or leaves.
- Mind: it turns notice aside. It gives no order and changes no decision.
- It can be dropped at will, at once.
- Not applicable: distance, falling, carrying, water.
Choices: none. The price is yours, with "forgets what it was for" written as one item of the record for each full season held.
The Keeper's reasons: A memory price is enacted the same way for every god, by removing an item from its own record, so the rate is written down: a full season held, one item.

### The Closing
Draft text:
Description: The flock closes on one band of up to thirty mortals within the god's sight, in the open. A storm of beaks and wings blinds, cuts and drives them from a pass, a ford, a road or a field, for as long as the god keeps it up and no longer than a quarter of an hour. The band is turned or scattered, not killed by design.
Drawback: Each closing thins the god. For a season after it is smaller, with fewer voices, one fewer sent voice at a time and a dimmer sight. A second closing within one day costs it a part for good: the Keeper removes one item from the god's record by logged roll.
Limits:
- The open only. It does nothing against a stockade, a roof, a shield wall under cover, thick smoke, or in a closed room. A band with a fire lit has a smoke it must go around.
- Against a band that is ready for it, it is a contest and is rolled.
- Other gods: unruled until it happens.
- The god's own people: birds do not tell friend from foe inside the storm.
- Being watched: nothing about it is hidden, and Unremarked is broken for everyone who sees it.
- Not applicable: carrying.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: The price is real and is yours. Two lines are the Keeper's: a ready band is rolled, as any contest is, and a storm of birds seen by a pass full of men is not unremarked.

### Gathered Again at the Crossing
Draft text:
Description: When the god's body is broken, what is left of it drifts together bird by bird over a season, to the crossroads of three or more ways nearest to where it broke, or the ford if a ford is nearer, and closes there whole. The walk from there to wherever it was needed is added.
Drawback: Each return costs the god one item of memory or habit, taken by a logged roll from its own record (what it holds of other gods, and its goals). The god does not choose it and is not told what went.
Limits:
- The season rung of the standing scale, fixed now and never chosen at each return.
- A body held in a net or a closed room is not broken, and this does not answer it. A part lost while the rest lives is not a broken body.
- It gathers at the nearest such ground whoever holds it.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: The standing scale, the same for every god, on the rung you named.

### Trade: The Unrefused Question, for sight through a voice
Draft text:
Weakness, The Unrefused Question: At a crossing, a ford, a border or on a road the god has walked, when a mortal asks it the way plainly and in good faith (which road, which ford, how far, whether the pass is open), the god must answer, and truly. It may answer sideways and need say no more than was asked. It cannot say it does not know when it does, and cannot send the asker wrong. A question put to trap it, a question about its nature or powers, a question from another god, and a question put to a sent voice do not count. At such a place Unremarked does not hold against a mortal who asks the way aloud of the birds or the traveler before them: the god is noticed by that asker, and must answer. Anyone who learns of it can have the way to anywhere the god knows, its own people's camps included, for the asking.
What it bought: Through one sent voice at a time, and dimly, the god in its body also sees what that voice sees as it happens.
Choices: take the weakness with the line about Unremarked, or leave the trade closed. You need not take one.
The Keeper's reasons: A weakness is priced by what it costs. As you wrote it, a god that no mortal can notice is a god no mortal can ask, and the weakness would cost almost nothing while Unremarked is held. With the added line it is something you pay and something that can be used against you, and it buys the one step you asked.
