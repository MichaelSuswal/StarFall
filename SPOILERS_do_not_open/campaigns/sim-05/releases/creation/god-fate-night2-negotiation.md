# Negotiation: god-fate, Night 2

Your `self.md` was read against your sheet. Nothing is sent back. One line of it reads two ways (your return) and is put to you below as a choice. Below is each power as it would be written on your card, with the price and the Keeper's reasons. The card lines say "the god"; that is you.

## Round 1

### The Weighing
Draft text:
Description: When a bargain is spoken aloud in the god's hearing, its terms stated and each party saying yes, and the god holds its scales or has its paws on the table, it learns for each party who spoke, as a plain yes or no, whether that party means at that moment to keep the terms as stated.
Drawback: Any term the god itself speaks or assents to in a weighed bargain binds it to the letter. It cannot knowingly break it, and no trick of wording frees it from a term it understood when it spoke: the meaning it understood is the meaning that binds.
Limits:
- Only the terms as spoken, and only parties within the same room or, in the open, within arm's reach of the scales.
- It learns intent at the moment of the yes: not why, not whether the party can keep the terms, nothing else of the mind. A party who lies to itself is weighed by what it believes. One who meant it and later breaks it weighed true.
- Signs and writing with no spoken terms are not weighed. In a tongue the god does not understand it learns only that yes was said, and a term it did not understand does not bind it.
- The god may show the answer or keep it.
- Mind: it reads one thing and rules nothing.
- Other gods: another god in the bargain, weighed or not, is unruled until it happens.
- Being watched: a small beast with its paws on a table. Nothing else shows.
- Use: any bargain it attends, one at a time.
- Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: This is the one power on your list that reaches into a mind, so it is written to the single yes or no you asked and nothing wider. The price is yours and is a real one for a bargainer: the quick lie is gone from every weighed table. Whether it reads a god is not settled here; anything that acts on another god is ruled when it happens, for every god alike.

### The Bill
Draft text:
Description: A mortal who stands before the god, asks, and names a price aloud, may receive, if the god accepts the price, one small turn of chance, now: a throw that falls well, a ford that holds, a fever that turns, a lost thing found, a door that opens, a blade that misses. It is a single turn within what chance could do. The price is collected when it falls due, exactly as named, and the bill stands on the god's record whether or not the mortal remembers it.
Drawback: While any bill the god has written is unpaid and past due, the god moves no faster than a slow walk and cannot leap, climb or scurry. (And your choice on forgiving, below.)
Limits:
- One turn of chance for one bill: where the matter would be rolled, the roll is shaded well in the asker's favor; where it is certain or impossible, nothing changes. No miracle, no raising the dead, no changing what is done.
- The price must be a thing the mortal can give or do with its own hands and days: goods, labor, a service, a tooth, a thing it owns. A price that is not such a thing (a name, a memory, a season of sleep, a year of fortune) cannot be taken as a bill.
- The god collects no more than was named. The bill compels no one: a mortal who will not pay is a debtor, and the bill stands unpaid.
- A bill falls due when the mortal set it, or at the next turn of a season. A debt binds heirs only if they agreed aloud. A bill may be paid early.
- No more than seven bills may stand open at once, and no mortal holds more than one.
- Mortals who ask and agree only. Never on another god, and a turn bought against a god's own act is unruled until it happens.
- It makes nothing: a turn of chance finds only what is there to be found.
- Being watched: the asking and the naming are spoken aloud and can be overheard.
- Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water.
Choices, on forgiving:
 (a) No forgiving. A bill is paid, or it stands until the debtor dies. The god cannot strike it off.
 (b) Forgiving at a cost. The god may forgive a bill, and each time it does, it can write no new bill until the next turn of a season.
The Keeper's reasons: As you wrote it, the god may forgive any bill at no cost, so the slow walk would last only as long as you chose to bear it, and a price the god can end at will is not a price. So forgiving is either closed or costs you the power for a while. The cap of seven keeps the power from being every roll of a whole people in a year. The rule on what a price may be is the Keeper's reading of the world: the god is a creditor of goods and labor, which a mortal can hand over; what a mortal is cannot be handed over a table.

### Clean Rot
Draft text:
Description: By touch, the god draws the rot or sickness out of one thing: a store of grain, a well, a wound, a sick person, a spoiled barrel. What rot took is given back where the thing still exists to hold it, and the rot passes into the god's own body.
Drawback: What it draws out, it carries. For a season after each working (about ninety days) its coat falls patchy, its limbs ache, and flies gather and follow it; every mortal near can see the flies and smell the sickness, and it can hide neither. It can work no second Clean Rot while the first is on it.
Limits:
- One thing for one working: one barrel, one well, one body. Never a land, a river, a whole granary or a plague. Out of a plague it takes one person, and the plague stays in the others.
- Rot and sickness only: not a broken bone, old age, hunger, or a poison that is no rot. A wound is made clean, not closed.
- The sickness cannot kill the god.
- Other gods, more than ordinary craft: a rot that is another god's work, or made by more than ordinary craft, is unruled until it happens.
- Not applicable: light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, mind.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: The price is yours and is honest: a season of flies for each working, and no second working under it. Your line on when the season ends read two ways, so it is written as a season from the working, about ninety days.

### The Return to the Marsh Edge
Draft text:
Description: When the god's body is broken, it comes back after a season, whole, at a marsh edge: wet and uncertain ground, a reed bed or a ford. (Which one is your choice, below.) The walk from that ground to wherever it was needed is added.
Drawback: Each return costs the god one item of memory or habit, taken by a logged roll from its own record (what it holds of other gods, and its goals). The god does not choose it and is not told what went.
Limits:
- The season rung of the standing scale, fixed now and never chosen at each return.
- A body caged, bound or shut out is not broken, and this does not answer it.
Choices: your text says both "the nearest marsh edge" and "to the place where it fell", and then that you set no ground. Those cannot all hold. Choose one:
 (a) The nearest. The marsh edge nearest to where the body was broken.
 (b) A set ground. The marsh edge nearest to where you first came down into this world, wherever you were broken. If that ground is gone on the day, the nearest marsh edge serves.
The Keeper's reasons: The standing scale is the same for every god: the nearest ground of its kind, or a ground set now if that gives no advantage the nearest would not. Either reading of your words fits it. Neither costs more than the other; the walk is added in both.

### Trade: The offer heard out, for one thing more
Draft text:
Weakness, The offer heard out: When a mortal stands before the god, states aloud both the terms and the price, and offers a hand or an open palm, the god must hear the whole offer to its end and weigh it. It may refuse the offer. It may not walk away, leap off or silence the speaker before the offer is finished. A bare demand, an offer in a tongue it does not understand, and an offer from another god do not count. Nothing ends it. Anyone who learns of it can hold the god in one place for as long as they can keep an offer going.
What it bought: The Weighing also tells the god, for each party, a plain yes or no: whether that party believes it can keep the terms as stated.
Choices: take it as drafted, or leave the trade closed. You need not take one.
The Keeper's reasons: The weakness is real: it can be used to pin you. It buys one step. The step you asked, whether a party can in truth keep its terms, is not a thing a weighing of the party can reach: it would be a knowing of the world and of what has not happened yet, and no power here is written to tell what will be. What the scales can reach is the party: whether it believes it can. That is offered as the one thing more.

Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons.
