# Release: god-storms, Night 2

## Why you come down
Gods come down here to lead a people: to find or gather them, to build, to
rule, and to spread. That is what the world below expects of you.

## What is so of every god here
Knowing another god. At a distance you feel nothing of another god: no pull and no direction. You know one only by meeting it. When another god stands before you, the knowing that this one is no mortal comes on slowly, by nearness alone, and one who works at seeming mortal may hold it off for a while. Within arm's reach it is sure, and no god passes for a mortal at a touch. The knowing tells you what stands there. It tells you nothing of its temper, its powers or its mind.

Bodies. One body. A god is in one place at a time.

Mortal magic, for pricing. It is simple, and it costs the worker.

You need no food, and sleep is a choice. You move at the pace of your form.
You cannot die. The peoples you will find are at about this level: before the
medieval era, no steel forges. That is where the world begins, not a
ceiling. You may bring in what you know, but it is built here by the people,
from what the world has, at the pace of the work, and you say how it is to be
made; nothing is granted by naming it. Every power carries a drawback you
would notice losing. There is no set number of powers. Nothing is over the
top.

## The people you lean toward
Rule: a mortal ruler under the god, a priest-king. One ruler answers to the god and rules for it: a priest-king, who rules through rite and the god's visible presence.
Worship: a priesthood. Keepers of rite with standing among the people, offerings brought, no tithe by law.
You build your people within these on Night 3, or give your reasons not to.
Your people come with helpers who run things when you are away. Who your
people are, and who those helpers are, is settled on Night 3.

## What you said, and asked
No follow-up.

## The season you wake into
Spring.

## Options
- When your body is broken. Say first what you wish happens to you; the price comes after. A return carries a cost and a trigger, so it is written as a power and priced on one standing scale, the same for every god: a season to return costs one item of memory or habit; a month to return costs two items; nothing is faster. The faster return costs more. The item is not yours to pick: it is taken by a logged roll from your own record (what you hold about other gods, and your goals). You return at the nearest ground of your kind, or at a ground you set now instead if that gives no advantage the nearest would not; the walk from that ground to wherever you were needed is added. The rung is fixed now, one for good, never chosen at each return. A body that is held or shut out is not broken, and a return does not answer it.
- Prices. Each power needs a drawback you would notice losing. You may offer your own. Where a price could go two ways you are given both in full and you choose one. A condition that only shapes a power (where it works, what it does not show) is a limit, not a price, and buys nothing.
- A trade, offered once. A weakness you take on, something you do not already pay, can buy one improvement elsewhere, such as one of your powers showing or reaching one thing more. You need not take one. If you say no, it stays closed for the rest of creation.
- Things carried. An ordinary thing costs nothing. Anything that does more than an ordinary object is a power and is priced.
- A name. Your name, if you have one, and what those who do not know it call you.
- Wording. You may ask for a clarification "in the write-up, not a new power"; it is taken as wording.
