# Negotiation: god-beasts, Night 2

Your `self.md` was read against your sheet. Nothing is sent back. Below is each power as it would be written on your card, with the price and the Keeper's reasons. The card lines say "the god"; that is you.

## Round 1

### The Count of the Herd
Draft text:
Description: The god names one kind of beast aloud and waits, and it knows the number of that kind within a day's walk of where it stands and where each lies, and whether a stranger or a predator has come among them or one has been taken. It counts tame beasts, wild ones, and a kind no one has a name for, as a number it cannot name.
Drawback: (your choice, below)
Limits:
- One kind for one count. A few heads take a breath; a great herd takes a long quiet, up to an hour.
- While it counts it stands rooted, deaf to speech and blind to anything that is not a beast, and it cannot stop a count once begun. These are conditions of use, written here and not as the price.
- Beasts only: nothing of people or of gods. A god in a beast's body counts as one head of the kind it looks like, and nothing marks it. A person in a beast's shape counts as a beast.
- It keeps the number afterward, not the knowledge of what has changed since.
- Under stone or deep water it counts as far as the stone or the surface, and no further.
- It shows what is there, never why.
- More than ordinary craft: a thing in a beast's shape that is not a beast, or a beast made or held by more than ordinary craft, may count wrong or not at all. Unruled until it happens.
- Light and dark, being watched: the same by night; a giant standing still, speaking a word.
- Use: as often as it likes.
- Not applicable: falling, carrying, mind.
Choices, for the Drawback:
 (a) No hunt on the count. A kind the god has counted may not be hunted or slaughtered by the god, or at its word, until the second dawn after the count. The count cannot lead that day's hunt or that day's feast.
 (b) The long stillness. After the count ends, the god stays rooted and senseless for as long again as the count took, and never less than an hour.
The Keeper's reasons: Standing still and deaf while you count is how the power works; under the rule every god is priced by, a condition of use is a Limit and buys nothing. Knowing every head of a kind for a day's walk round, and where each lies, is a large knowing, so it needs a price you would feel: either the count cannot serve the hunt you love, or the helplessness is doubled. One line is refused as you wrote it: no power marks a god at a distance. You know another god only by meeting it, and a count that marked one head as "unlike its kind" would be a knowing at a day's walk. The hurt, the sick and those with young are left out of the base text because your trade below asks for them.

### The Calling
Draft text:
Description: By voice, with the horn or without, the god calls one kind of beast, and those of that kind within a day's walk that are free to come, come to it: tame ones to follow, wild ones to gather near, calm, as at a watering place at dusk. A called beast will not hurt the god or its people while called. The god holds them gathered until it releases them with a word.
Drawback: While any herd is called, the beasts' hunger and want press on the god: it can hold its mind on nothing else until they are fed or released, and they must be fed from its people's stores or the land. And the call may not be betrayed: if beasts are killed while called, by the god, its people or at its word, that kind will not answer the call again for a year.
Limits:
- It gives no command. A beast whose fear grows stronger than the call bolts: fire, a predator among prey.
- A beast under a hand does not come: ridden, led, yoked, tethered or penned. It strains and stays.
- One kind at a time, and no other until the first is released.
- Predators come gentle and are still predators. Called beasts are not shielded from anyone else's spear.
- A kind with no name is called only if the god knows it exists. Peoples who wear beast-forms are not called.
- Other gods: a god in a beast's body is not called. A herd another god holds by its own working is unruled until it happens.
- Being watched: the call is heard as far as a horn carries, and the coming of the herds is plain.
- Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water, mind (of people).
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: Your price stands, and one line is added to it. A call that could gather every deer for a day's walk into a killing ground, any day of the year, would feed a whole people without work; the line on betrayal lets you call for a hunt and makes each such hunt cost you that kind for a year. The line on beasts under a hand is the plain reading of "it does not command them": a rider's horse is held by its rider.

### Speech with Beasts
Draft text:
Description: The god understands what beasts mean to say, and says to them what it means, as a person talks to a person. Their meaning is simple: fear, want, warning, welcome, grief, the way to water.
Drawback: (your choice, below)
Limits:
- A beast knows what a beast knows, can be wrong, and can lie as beasts do. Insects and fish say little.
- It shows what a beast saw, never why it happened.
- The god chooses whether to speak; it cannot be forced to.
- A person under a beast's shape speaks as a person. A god in a beast's body is spoken with as the god chooses to answer.
- Not applicable: light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, water.
Choices, for the Drawback:
 (a) The kind, for good. The god can never again eat the flesh of any kind of beast it has spoken with, or sit at a table where that kind is served.
 (b) The last moment. The god feels the death of every beast it has ever spoken with that dies within a day's walk of it, and each such death holds it still and speechless for the space of a hundred breaths.
The Keeper's reasons: Every god here needs no food, so giving up the meat of one beast you chose to speak with costs almost nothing: you could always speak to one and eat its herd-mate. A price must be something you would notice losing. Each of these is: the first takes kinds off your table for good, the second makes every creature you have questioned a hand on your shoulder when it dies.

### The Standing
Draft text:
Description: When the god wills it, its antlers kindle with a pale steady glow, visible across a valley at dusk or a plain by day. Beasts in sight of it still and turn to it, and people who see it know that a god stands there.
Drawback: While the antlers burn, every wild thing within a mile bolts or gathers, and no hunt can be made there. The glow cannot be held longer than a day and a night together; then the antlers ache, and the god must stand dim for as long as it burned.
Limits:
- One who does not look is not moved. It gives no command, heals nothing and burns nothing.
- A hand held over the brow puts it out.
- Other gods: a god who sees the light sees a light. It knows what stands there only by nearness, as every god does.
- Being watched: it is made to be watched.
- Not applicable: falling, carrying, water, mind.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: A small power at a small price, both yours. "It cannot go unseen while it glows" is the power itself said again and is left out of the price; the spoiled hunt and the dim hours are the cost.

### Return to the Herd-ground
Draft text:
Description: When the god's body is broken, it wakes whole after a season in open grazing country, at the grazing ground nearest to where it broke on which a herd of a hundred beasts or more feeds. The walk from there to wherever it was needed is added.
Drawback: Each return costs the god one item of memory or habit, taken by a logged roll from its own record (what it holds of other gods, and its goals). The god does not choose it and is not told what went.
Limits:
- The season rung of the standing scale, fixed now and never chosen at each return.
- A body held, chained or shut out is not broken, and this does not answer it.
- If no such herd feeds anywhere near, the nearest wide grass with the largest herd in reach serves. Fenced or walled grazing counts if beasts feed there.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: The standing scale, the same for every god, on the rung you named.

### Trade: No false number, for the hurt and the young
Draft text:
Weakness, No false number: The god cannot speak or write a number it knows to be false: a count, a measure, a sum, a tally, a price or a weight, in its own words or under oath. It may stay silent or refuse to answer. A guess it calls a guess, a number it does not know, a story, a song, a riddle, a boast that is plainly a boast, and a number written by another, do not count. Nothing ends it. Anyone who learns of it can trust every figure the god gives and can read its silences.
What it bought: In the same count, with no more stillness, The Count of the Herd also tells the number of that kind that are hurt, sick or with young.
Choices: none. You need not take the trade.
The Keeper's reasons: The weakness costs a ruler something it does not already pay: it can never bluff a strength or hide a shortfall with a figure. It buys the one step you asked.
