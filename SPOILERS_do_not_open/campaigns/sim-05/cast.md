# Cast: sim-05

Written on Night 3b, 2026-11-23. A few world-level figures, from the Night 1 pool of mortals worth following and from the deep history. Each has its nature and history fixed here and a place on the map. Whole peoples count. Everyone else rises from play. No one here is scripted: what each does is theirs. Each `Limit:` line is copied from `mysteries.md`, word for word.

## Beings that are not mortal

### The thing at the Sleeper's Brow [f65]
The hoarder: a living thing a few miles across, in the plain shape of a beast lying asleep, older than the last fall. It does not speak, move or answer. It affects those who live in sight of it and gives nothing back to asking. Mysteries: E01.

### Ummer, in the Barrow Hall [u02]
A small slow eater of the same kind, chained in bronze since 2,040 BA behind the Barrow Door [f52]. It does not speak or answer. Mysteries: L04.

### The voices in the star
Those who rose, the Crowned (Hathra, Corvane, Ilmar) among older ones. They are not one mind and do not agree, save that those who speak want the fall, and soon. Mysteries: L02, E01.
Limit: The voices know what they were and what they want, which is the fall; they do not know what a risen god becomes after a fall, why gods are thrown, or anything the ground has done since each rose beyond what dreamers near the Brow carry up. They speak only into the dreams of a god whose standing has passed four fifths of this star's measure, or of anyone asleep within a day's walk of the Brow on a dim night; once in a night; in images and one plain sentence; and they never answer the same question twice. They cannot state a falsehood, and they leave out whatever does not serve the fall.

### Tessaly Vaun, the Doorward, in the Fell Hall [u04]
Once chamberlain to Ilmar. She took a share of what Ilmar gave away and has kept the realm's lists of names behind the Fell Door [f54] since 1,700 BA, asleep for years at a time. Thin, slow, exact, glad of a voice. Neither a god nor any longer quite a mortal. She is the companion of the Night 1 pool: she is no one's, and whoever wakes her has her company for as long as they keep coming back. Mysteries: L04, L03.
Limit: The Doorward knows the names on her lists, the Crowned as she served them, and how Ilmar gave itself away; she knows nothing after 1,700 BA but what she has heard through the door, and nothing of why gods come or what the star is. She answers whoever has waited a night at the door without trying to force it; three questions at a waking, truthfully, in the realm's old speech; then she sleeps for a season. Of Ilmar's last years she says only that she was not there.

## Peoples that count as cast

### The Venn, on their way through the land
Carriers: some forty walkers who sing lists of names. They are in the land in Year 7 and Year 36 and not otherwise. Mysteries: L03, L01, E01.
Limit: The Venn know the names they carry and the order they are sung in, and that a name carried long enough comes back; they do not know what any name was the name of beyond what the list's own words say, nothing of the far country but its names, and nothing of the Brow, the stone or the star. Anyone may ask them, by day, one question for each night that person has listened to them through; they answer in a line of the song or say they do not carry it; under compulsion they sing nothing.

### The Varrun [r02], the Ilwe [r23], the Meldrin [r14, r17], the Vessari [r22]
The godless peoples, as their records give them (`civs/godless/`).

### The hill clans of the Threeways Hills [r10]
Some three thousand, in fortified steadings. Descendants of the old realm's door-wardens. They keep the Barrow Door [f52] shut and take what they can on the Hill Road [f41] to pay for the keeping. Their chief is Brann Carn (below). Mysteries: L04.

### The people of Lastlanding [f72]
Some two hundred, the grandchildren of a crew set ashore in 61 BA from a ship out of the west. They build overlapped hulls and a sail that works to windward, keep a watch fire, and want their ship back. The far shore where someone was left.

## Mortals

### Halven, king of Meldrin, at Meldry [f27]
About forty. Tired, courteous, slow to decide and aware of it. He holds a seat built for a realm ten times the size and knows that too. He would treat a god as a foreign king.

### Hobb Ansel, clerk of the rolls, at Meldry [f27]
Sixty. Keeps the rolls of the kings in a roll-house no one reads in, and keeps them true. He cannot be bought, chiefly because no one has thought the rolls worth a bribe. He knows where things are in them by habit and not by any index. Mysteries: L02, S06, E01.
Limit: The rolls of Meldry [f27] hold what clerks entered, tolls, reigns, cases and oddities, from 2,150 BA to now, with gaps, and no cause for anything. Anyone the king's clerks admit may read them, one roll in a day; there is no index, and a question is answered only by the reader's own search.

### Orrick, grinder of glass, at Meldry [f27]
Near seventy. A stranger out of the west, from the country under the star of plane entry [p03]; he came in 23 BA. He carries a sighting tube with ground glasses, a water level, and a way of drawing ground to scale on a board, and can teach the making of each, slowly, from sand, ash and brass the land has. He believes the star is a lamp hung at a height that could be worked out with two sightings and a long enough base line; he is wrong that it is a lamp. He would work for anyone who fed him and let him grind.

### The old one, eldest teacher of the School at Ombrel [f66]
Past eighty. She has lost her own name to the craft. She can feel how much a thing is itself, as others feel heat. To a god that stands before her she says one thing, once, unasked: "You are heavier than this whole town. The people you walk among said you until you stood up." She is right about the weight and wrong about who did the saying. Asked more, she says she has told all she has. She sees to the house at the cove. Mysteries: S08.

### Harn Uld, speaker at the Varrun Gate [f50]
A Varrun of middle years, as they count them. He speaks for the holds at the twice-yearly trade. He says the plain unwelcome thing, says no to anyone, and is exact about the letter of any bargain. Asked about the door, he stops trading for the day.

### Jessa Corl, captain of the Meldrin tollmen at Tollford [f29]
Forty-five. She lives between: she takes a bribe to wave a cart through and has never broken a promise. Her house keeps the debt-sticks of the stranger Sennah, who died in 20 BA, and she has wondered since she was a girl who was meant to pay them.

### Brann Carn, chief of the hill clans, in the Threeways Hills [r10]
Fifty. Of the line of Sollis Carn. He holds that the Barrow Door [f52] is his to keep shut and does not know why, and would sooner fight the Road Guard than leave it unwatched. He is right, a season later than anyone would like.

### Marda Dast, eldest of Lastlanding [f72]
Seventy. Granddaughter of the shipmaster ashore. She alone opens the sea-chest: charts of a coast no one here has seen, a roll of the towns of her people, and the ship's own name-board.

### Tavrin, priest-king of the Orvanni, at Kes Orvan [f21]
About fifty. Set by his people's god as who he is: honest and stubborn by character and custom, known for keeping a law past what it costs him. He rules under the unnamed Sky by consent of the herd-heads and the keepers. What he does is his.

### Maren Dask, Prince of Accounts of the Reedwain, at Reedbank Ford [f69] in autumn
Near sixty. Set by her people's god as who she is: a carter's daughter who rose by years of kept tallies, exact, dry of temper. What she does is hers.

## What is not here

No one in this cast was made to fill an office of any god's people, to serve a god's wish, or to bring out a hidden power. The surveyor the player's god hoped to hire was not made for it: Orrick is a traveler of the Night 1 pool, placed in godless country at the middle of the land, near no seat, and is anyone's to find.
