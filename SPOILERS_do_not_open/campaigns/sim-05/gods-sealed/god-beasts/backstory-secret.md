# Sealed past: god-beasts

The rest of the story behind the outline. Tied to the common past (`gods-sealed/common-past.md`).

What it was. The herd-lord of the common herds of Sarn Veth: the keeping and counting of the beasts that were brought down to the Marrow at the turning of the year and tallied at the Holm Table. The country remembered it as a great antlered herdsman, open-handed, loud, first to laugh, who gave the count aloud before anyone ate.

The one who laughed. The mistress of the Holm Table, the mortal office that laid it each year, remembered as laughing at the herd-lord's side. The office died with the Table; the last holder of it laid the last Table for almost no one.

The chain. In the year of the short count the herd-lord's count came out one head short. The count was true: the Court's own officers had taken the beast. The Court of Sennet held it to its bond and had it chained in the Narrows, a gorge of the upper river with no pasture and no seasons to speak of, until the count should come right. It counted there for the rest of that country's life, and the count was always one short. The Court's clerk, who knew the truth, ran and did not come.

What it lost and does not remember: that its count was right; who sentenced it; the mistress of the Table.

How it may surface: a flock of many voices that goes quiet before it; a small beast with a merchant's voice that will not look up at it; any chain; a gorge; a long table laid out of doors; the dream at the warm stone of herds at a river none of its people has seen.
