# Sealed past: god-storms

The rest of the story behind the outline. Tied to the common past (`gods-sealed/common-past.md`).

What it was. The upland thunder of Sarn Veth: the hard clear weather off the high ground, one of the two weathers the country knew, thanked and bargained with at the Holm Table. The country remembered it as a severe antlered rider on the ridge, just, formal, slow to move, the one who kept its word about the frost.

The sibling. The other weather was Hesk, the wet thunder off the sea. Each year at the Table the Court of Sennet stood between them and the year's weather was struck as a bargain: so much rain, so much frost. The two thunders answered each other across the valley, and that sound was the country's sign that the year was in balance.

The breaking. When the Court was shut under its hill nothing stood between them. Hesk came in off the sea and would not go back, and drove the tide up the Marrow onto Calder Hythe. The upland thunder came down and broke Hesk for good. It was just: the harbor was drowning. It was also too late for the harbor, and afterward there was one weather and one thunder, and nothing answered.

What it lost and does not remember: Hesk's name; that it waited a night too long before it struck, weighing the justice of it; that the hall with the long table was not its own hall.

How it may surface: its own thunder, which no hill gives back; a harbor people; a flock of voices that feels, to it, like something that ought to be standing between it and someone; the dream at the warm stone of two storms over a river.
