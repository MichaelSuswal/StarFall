# Sealed past: god-sea

The rest of the story behind the outline. Tied to the common past (`gods-sealed/common-past.md`).

What it was. The harbor custom of Calder Hythe, the town at the mouth of the Marrow: a lamp lit each dusk in a seaward window, and a count of boats in, kept by whoever kept the door of the harbor house. The town remembered the custom as a tall patient man who came in last and sat at the foot of the table, and a place was always laid for him.

The night. When Hesk drove the tide up the river mouth, the water rose in the town a hand's breadth at a time. The lamp was lit and stayed lit. The count of boats in came out short, and the count of people out by the upriver boats came out shorter. The custom had promised, in the town's own words, that no one would be left on the far shore; the far shore was the sea side of the harbor bar, where the fishing families lived, and they were the ones left.

After. The survivors were fed at Brede, at the price of their names. Calder Hythe stopped being said. Some of them had first gone on up the Marrow to the Liberty of Asken and been kept outside its line. The habit of counting what is owed comes from here: the town was owed a rescue, a welcome and its own name, and collected none of them.

What it lost and does not remember: the name Calder Hythe; the number the count was short by; that the far shore was a real bar of shingle with houses on it.

How it may surface: a harbor with a lamp in a window; a people who will not say where they came from; a second, older harbor under a living one, if it ever goes down to look; a walker of boundaries who feels, on meeting it, that it owes this stranger something; a carrier's song with a harbor's name in it.
