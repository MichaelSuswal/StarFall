# Sealed past: god-fate

The rest of the story behind the outline. Tied to the common past (`gods-sealed/common-past.md`).

What it was. The clerk of the Court of Sennet: a small, exact office the Court made out of itself to keep its tallies, weigh what was brought in fee, and keep the hill door by the door-tally. The country remembered it as a quick little beast on the Court's table, the one who knew what everything weighed, and called to heel by its name.

What it did. In the year of the short count it knew the missing head had gone to the Court's own table, and that the herd-lord's count was true. It asked for the matter to be reopened and was called to heel. In the next session it went out, shut the hill door on the Court from the outside, and ran with the door-tally. It did not go to the Narrows and it did not unchain the herd-lord: it ran the other way. That is the missing weight in the pan.

The rot. The fee-store of the Court was its charge, and after the flight it rotted where it lay; the country remembered the clerk afterward both as the one who let the stores spoil and as the one who had always kept them sound.

What it lost and does not remember: that it shut the door itself; the door-tally and what it was for; that someone stayed chained because it ran.

How it may surface: a flock that speaks in many voices, at whose call its body starts a step; a giant it cannot meet the eye of; a sealed door in a hillside with terms cut on it; a set of old weights with one missing; the dream at the warm stone of a long table seen from on top of it.
