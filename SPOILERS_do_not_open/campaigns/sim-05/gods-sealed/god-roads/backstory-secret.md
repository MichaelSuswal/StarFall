# Sealed past: god-roads

The rest of the story behind the outline. Tied to the common past (`gods-sealed/common-past.md`).

What it was. The Court of Sennet: the moot of many voices that sat in the hall cut into Sennet Hill, a door at either end and a long table between, and that came out once a year to sit at the Holm Table. It was never one judge. The country remembered it as a murmur with one voice rising at a time, and as the thing every road led to when a matter could not be settled at home.

The one it made. The Court made its clerk out of itself: one voice set apart to keep the tallies and the door, and trusted with the door-tally, the count by which the hill door was opened and shut. In the year of the short count the Court's own officers took one head from the common herds for the Court's table, the herd-lord's count came out short by it, and the Court sentenced the herd-lord to the chain sooner than own the fault. The clerk knew. In the next session it shut the hill door from outside, on the Court as it sat, and ran.

The long quiet. The Court sat shut under the hill to the end of that country, hearing nothing, waiting for a hand on the door. Without it nothing held the two weathers apart, and the breaking of the second weather followed.

What it lost and does not remember: that the sentence on the herd-lord was unjust and that it knew; what the door-tally was; that the hand on the outside of the door was its own clerk's.

How it may surface: a small beast that will not come when called and will not look at a door being shut; a giant with a dread of chains who, face to face, makes the flock fall silent for no reason either can give; a hill with a door in it; the dream at the warm stone of a crowd kneeling by a river, which is the Holm Table seen from the Court's seat.
