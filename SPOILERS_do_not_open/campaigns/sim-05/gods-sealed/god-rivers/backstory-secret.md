# Sealed past: god-rivers

The rest of the story behind the outline. Tied to the common past (`gods-sealed/common-past.md`).

What it was. The ferrying habit of the Marrow in Sarn Veth: the way that river was crossed and followed, remembered as an old man with a pole who knew every ford and took no toll but a fair one. Roads followed the river because the ferrying did. Before that, older, it was the habit of the bank hunters who tracked game along the water; the hunter became the ferryman when the people stopped hunting and started crossing.

The mortal. The keeper of the Spring of Arle, the oldest spring of the Marrow, a woman remembered only as Arle's keeper, who sat with the ferryman at the spring each thaw and was the one person it was ever said to wait for. She died of the foul water in the years after the drowning of Calder Hythe, at her own spring, which the flood had not reached and the sickness had.

The night. In the drowning of Calder Hythe the ferrying took boats up the Marrow all night, and not everyone. The count it kept that night it has lost.

The follower followed. After the drowning the river below the meadows ran foul. Where the ferrying habit went, the water was remembered as clean, so the thirsty of Brede's country followed it upstream, patiently, year on year, never hurrying, always there at the next ford. It could not serve them all and could not bear to be leaned on, and it kept moving. That is the thing in the reeds that does not hurry.

What it lost and does not remember: Arle's keeper, her face, that she died of water; the count of the boats; that the ones who followed it wanted only to drink.

How it may surface: a spring kept by one woman; a crowd of the thirsty walking behind it; a dream, near the warm stone, of a river it has not seen here and a night of boats; a carrier's song that holds the words "Spring of Arle". None of these says what it is.
