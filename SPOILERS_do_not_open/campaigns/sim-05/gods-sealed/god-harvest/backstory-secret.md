# Sealed past: god-harvest

The rest of the story behind the outline. Tied to the common past (`gods-sealed/common-past.md`).

What it was. The corn-name of Brede: the word said by everyone at once at the turning of the year for the keeper of the great granary-hall on the black soil below the water meadows, with the horned face cut in the hall's stone. The country remembered it as a heavy, patient reeve, half ram, who fed everyone and to whom everyone owed.

The names. After the drowning of Calder Hythe the harbor's survivors were fed at Brede. The price was entry in the Brede tally under the corn-name: they said Brede's word at the turning and their own town's name was not said again. The people of Tharrow Green, cut out of the line at Asken, came down and were entered the same way. Brede grew by every name it took in. This is where its hunger for names comes from, and why a name feels to it like a thing that can be held and built on.

The forgetting. The river at Brede ran foul after the drowning, and Brede's people began to drift upstream after cleaner water. The Holm Table stopped being laid. With no gathering there was no saying of the word. The face on the stone wore away. The granary stood, far too large for the few left to fill.

What it lost and does not remember: the word itself; that it took other peoples' names to grow; that it was fed from, more than loved.

How it may surface: a granary far too large; a stone with a worn face; a harbor people and a boundary people, met here, toward whom it feels it is owed and cannot say what; a carrier's song that stops one word short.
