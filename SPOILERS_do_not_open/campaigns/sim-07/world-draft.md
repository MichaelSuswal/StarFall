# World draft: sim-07 (Night 1)

The first draft, from the star and the eight surveys. Nothing here is placed. Placement happens on Night 3. Counts and reasons are in the keeper log.

## Tone
An old, much-repaired world standing at the edge of a turn: the thaw, the tide line, the hour before the cutting, the breath before thunder. Small kept warmth against an honest cold; hearth smoke rising straight; the sound of water under everything. Low light, gold or grey-bright. Palette within the house style: ochre, umber and gold, with grey thaw light. Regional colors: salt wind on the coast, thunderheads over the high grass.

## Climate and seasons
Climate's temper: 4 on the scale. Four real seasons, hard winters that lock rivers and cost the careless, a thaw with floods, a long working summer with a dry stretch late, a wet autumn. Harder by latitude, height and distance from the sea. All the world goes through the seasons at the same time: one count of months, and month N is the same month, in the same season, in every country.

Waking month: March, the first of spring, the month of the thaw

It is month 1 of every country's count and the month of the landing, the same for every god.

## The star, as it bears on the land
- Late phase: a large, low, bright star. Fading is visible in about a dozen places: the oldest and most-worked ground and the far edges. The descent can be measured inside forty years. The fall is centuries off.
- Rain density at its highest. See Magic.
- The living radius is 80 days' walk from the center; past it, the Pale.
- At the center point stands the hoarder: a stone circle that is one organism. The surface sheet must show it.
- All three long-game strategies are present: the hoarder, the carriers, the thinners.
- No Remainder fell. There is no fallen stone anywhere in this world.
- The star's content is ice and long memory; its ascended are mostly the very old, who speak in weather and offer little.
- The ceiling is in `star.md` only.

## Magic
Level 9, final: a worker in nearly every valley, and a school in the south. It stays simple. One system: a worker asks, and something answers a little late. Nothing is made. The worker pays, in warmth, sleep and years first, then in memory and name, and the paying shows on them. The rest is the deep lore's (Truth 4) and is found in play.
Local understandings, each partly wrong, unplaced: that it is a craft practice improves; that it is a debt in a ledger; that it is a bargain with the thing asked; that it is heat; that it is weather.

## The two rules
- God-sense: a god knows another god only close at hand, about as near as one can make out a face, as nearness and weight; certain once the two stand before each other; it comes to both at the same nearness. Nothing at a distance, no direction, never a count.
- Bodies: one body. No cost line.

## How much gods stand out
6. A god is plainly more than a great mortal worker, and most mortals who meet one know it, but not at a glance across a field and not past doubt. With a worker in every valley, a great mortal worker is a real presence beside a god.

## The wild
5. Familiar animals where people live. True legends at the edges. Nameless things in the far, high, deep and wet places. One set of rules for creatures everywhere.

## Past the borders
5. About half the land, by area, is held by godless peoples and half is open. Old rooted kingdoms lie mostly far off; unwalked country lies between and near.

## Age of the human story
10. Thousands of years, ruins under ruins. The deep history must reach that depth, under a late star.

## Non-human peoples
3. Few: about two kinds, few of each. Candidates, unplaced: an old people of long memory, of the deep and the water margins, who build in their own way and answer to no god; and one rarer kind from among a tall people of the ice, a small people of the marsh, and a quick, short-lived, bright people. None a servant, none an enemy by nature, all hard to read.

## Travelers from afar
1. None, apart from the carriers the star requires, who come on their own clock. No settled strangers at the start. The pool of what travelers might bring (a tool or technique; a way of counting) is kept, unplaced, for 3b to weigh against the lore.

## Terrain the world must contain
More than one great river, with high springs, braided lowlands, confluences, a floodplain, a delta and marsh; low clay hills with woodland; a wide plain; high grass upland or plateau; stony, ore-bearing high country; mountains that keep their snow late; a hard cliff coast with coves and harbors.

Placement wishes, one per god, all unplaced:
- player: river country with clay banks, low hills and woodland, a hill to quarry, good rain; no mountains or desert in its own country.
- god-storms: high open plateau and broken upland, long grass, mountains at its edge.
- god-fire: high stony country with ore in the cut banks, wood near, strong wind, a river below, mountains behind.
- god-rivers: river country: high springs, braided lowlands, meeting waters, marsh and delta at the edges, mountains behind.
- god-sea: a hard coast of cliffs and coves, a harbor, rivers running down into it.
- god-harvest: river country opening onto plain, flood soil, hills in sight, forest at the edges.
- god-healing: cold rivers and old stone, a marsh at the foot of snow-keeping mountains.
- god-dead: a river valley with hills behind it, a defensible mouth, high ground above the flood line.

## The underground
Underground: yes, location not set
Mostly solid rock, with a few joined hollows only: "the deep ways", the cave-ways of a deep-dwelling people under the high country, reaching from shallow caves to far below the valley floors; and "the turnings", one made site of many turnings that may or may not join them. Unplaced. Doors, rooms, barrows and single deep places are sites on the surface sheet and are not part of the layer.

## Pools

### Fears
Widespread: hard winters; floods and drought; the failed harvest and slow famine; sickness that travels by water and grain; neighbors who covet stores.
Rare candidates, unplaced: the fear of the sky, a thing above that could come down; the made thing or trusted servant that turns; a taker of herds in the dark; the unwatched fire; the unquiet dead, as a people's belief only; a master that follows the one who fled, as a mortal tale only.

### Scarcity
World scarcities: good fuel (seasoned wood and timber), and salt.
Local shortages: clean water (coasts, marsh, wells that turn); good stone; metal that holds an edge; grain that keeps; lasting brick and tile where fuel is thin; a cure in a plague year.
Tempers of the peoples, never rules: trust is slow and oaths are held dear; days are short and counted.

### Buried things
Widespread: old roads; sealed doors; in several places, the bones of something enormous.
Rare candidates, unplaced: very old kiln sites under the soil; an unlit forge behind a door; sealed round stone granaries; a structure of many turnings; a burying ground older than any living people, its names worn off; roads that run on under water; bones of a thing that fell from the sky; bones held in ice.

### Mortals worth following
Kinds the peoples tend to produce: the stubborn keeper or builder who finishes things and will tell a god no; the widow who holds a yard, a hearth or a wrong; the one who goes first over water; the captain who holds a column through a bad winter; the healer who will not leave the sick house; the judge who says before the council that a thing was done wrong; the hard bargainer who is no one's instrument. Candidates for the small cast, unplaced, none scripted.

### Discoveries
Widespread threads: where the gods came from and what became of the place they left (the lore's, with no stone to carry it here); a companion that is neither god nor mortal (the long-game bodies); why things fall and what makes a thing last (the lore's center).
Rare candidates, unplaced: a place warm under cold ground that no one lit; a name found in the ground or in a stranger's mouth; ground the rot does not take.

### Undared wishes
Each a candidate thread, a place, a being or a custom, never a rule; all unplaced; none to be placed where its own god is certain to find it.
- player: a work still standing and in use after its maker is forgotten.
- god-storms: that a maker may see what became of its work.
- god-fire: to be let stay by one fire, and not called back.
- god-rivers: that what comes of a death is not wasted.
- god-sea: a way out of every cage.
- god-harvest: that a lost thing can be found, and is lighter than feared.
- god-healing: a place too far down to be followed to; to say where it came from without fear.
- god-dead: that nothing be forgotten that ought to be remembered.
