## Answer 1

A hearth seen from far off across dark ground, is it not? One small light that tells a tired traveler someone is awake and the door is not barred? And around it a great cold that is honest about being cold, so that the light means something? Is that not what a true world feels like, warmth that has to be kept, and kept by someone?

## Answer 2

Why would I not want the high stony country, where the rock is bare and the ore shows in the cut banks? Where wood is near enough to burn and wind is strong enough to be feared? Would you not call to a land that gives you something to work, and a long view to see who is coming? Scattered steadings on the slopes, a river somewhere below, and mountains at my back?

## Answer 3

Should the seasons not be real, with a hard cold that teaches what a fire is worth, and a summer long enough to forget it? Would a world with no winter ever learn to gather around anything? And the turn of the year, when it finds me, where would I stand in it? In the dark of autumn, when every hearth is lit and the first frosts make mortals count their wood? Or at the end of winter, when the stores run low and the fires are only just held?

I would wake in October, or else in March.

## Answer 4

Is it not better to have a little of each? Wild country, so there is room to build without asking leave? And somewhere far off, one old kingdom already rooted, so I know what I am measured against, and whether I should be afraid of it? Would I choose a world with no one else's walls in it? Could I trust it if I did?

## Answer 5

Thousands of years, surely? Ruins under ruins, so that nothing mortals build is thought to be the first thing, and nothing is thought to be the last? Is a people not steadier when it knows others fell before it, and learned something in falling?

## Answer 6

Would I not want it thin, a rumor most doubt and a few quietly know? A wise woman in some valleys and none in others? Why would I want every hearth to have its own spell, when I mean to be the one they bring their fires to? And yet why would I want it gone, when a world with no magic at all has nothing to find?

## Answer 7

Should it not feel like heat, a thing that rises in the hands and takes something as it goes? And what should it cost them? Years? Sleep? A warmth of the body that does not come back? Would a magic that costs nothing ever be respected? Would a mortal use it with care if it did not leave a mark?

## Answer 8

Should a god not stand well above a mortal, but not so far that mortals cannot see the work? Does a power no one can see do anything at all? Would I want to be the one-eyed man among the blind, or only the best smith in a country of smiths? Is there a middle where a god is plainly great and can still be wrong, and can still be afraid?

## Answer 9

Should there not be a few things without names? The familiar animals, yes, and the old stories about them, but past the last ridge, something no one has a word for? Would a wild with no unknown in it be worth guarding a fire against?

## Answer 10

What makes a good story but the dark itself? Is it not the cold that comes for the unprepared, the fire that spreads when it is not watched, the thing at the edge of the light that does not need to be named? Would I ask mortals to fear each other more than they fear the winter? Is that not a poor fear, when the winter is so much better at it?

## Answer 11

What should be scarce but the thing that makes warmth last? Good fuel, then, and the metal that holds an edge, and the hands that know how to work them? Would mortals not trade for it, and fight for it, and go without it in the bad years? And would they not remember who gave it to them?

## Answer 12

Why not old roads, and sealed doors? Would I not want to find a door that someone closed on purpose, and wonder why? And under it, perhaps, a forge no one has lit in an age? Is that not the kind of thing that makes a god lean closer and go quiet?

## Answer 13

Should there be others who are not human? Might there be a few, rare and strange and slow to trust? What would they be like, if I were the one to say? Would they not be a people who keep to the deep places and the fire under the mountain, and are glad of no one's company, and owe nothing? Could I not learn something from them, if they let me?

## Answer 14

Few, surely? A traveler or two who came from very far and carry something strange, a tool or a way of working metal that no one here has seen? Would too many not make the world feel like a market? And would I not want to meet one before the others do, and see what they want?

## Answer 15

Is a pull felt at any distance not a thing to be afraid of? Would I want to be found by anyone who wished to find me? Is it not better to know only face to face, so that I may see a god's hands and eyes before it knows my name? And if I must be recognized, could it not be by the fire, so that a god who comes into my light is known, and one who stays outside it is not?

## Answer 16

Why would a god be in two places? Is one body not enough to carry, and to guard? And if several, should it not cost something real, a thinning, a banked heat, so that a god spread wide is a god easily put out? Would I do it myself, knowing that? Perhaps once, if I were pressed, and I would not like it afterward.

## Answer 17

What kind of mortal should rise but one who tends something? A keeper, a smith, a widow who will not let a hearth die? Do I want a conqueror? Would I not rather have someone patient, who does the small thing every day until it is large? And would that mortal also be stubborn enough to refuse me, when I am wrong?

## Answer 18

Might there be a place that is warm under the cold ground, where no one lit it? A heat that was there before any of us? Or a truth about what we were before we came down, and what we are running from? Would I want to find that? Would I be able to bear it if I did?

## Answer 19

Is it permitted to say it? That the world would let me stay? Not be called back, not be found, not be asked to serve again? Only to keep one fire, among one people, in one place, until it is known to be mine and no one else's? Would that be too much to wish? Is it a wish at all, or only a fear turned inside out?
