## Answer 1
A field at the hour before the cutting. Every stalk heavy and bowed, the light low and gold, and the whole crop leaning one way as if it had been told something. Underneath it, the sound of a great many people moving together. It is a world that feeds on itself and is better for it. Ha. Yes, that one. Ripe, and a little frightening, because what is ripe is about to be cut.

## Answer 2
River country that opens onto plain. Wide soil that floods and gives it back, with hills close enough to see from the fields and forest at the edges for those who want to hide from me. I want land that rewards the ones who work it and punishes the ones who sit still. Flat enough to march across, rich enough to be worth taking.

## Answer 3
Four seasons, and they should be honest ones. A hard winter that thins the herds and the weak plans. A wet spring that tests the seed. A long hot summer that tests the patient. And an autumn that pays out in full for those who endured the rest, with no promise that it pays everyone. I do not want a gentle year. A gentle year breeds people who have never been afraid of the cold.

I would wake in September, or else in August.

## Answer 4
Some of each, and I want them in that order. Wild country first, so there is room to move into and nothing yet claiming it. Then old kingdoms rooted at the far edges, because a people on the move needs something to meet, trade with, and eventually press against. A world with only wilderness has no one worth taking a harvest from. A world with only kingdoms has no room to roam.

## Answer 5
Thousands of years, with ruins under ruins. I want my people to walk over the bones of people who also thought they would last. Let the soil be full of old plowing. Someone has fed on this ground before and been forgotten. That is a lesson I would like the ground to teach without my speaking.

## Answer 6
Closer to the wise-woman in every valley than the rumor, but never more than that. Magic should be common enough that a village has someone who knows when to cut and when to wait, and uncommon enough that no one is certain it is magic. It should never be a thing that excuses a failed crop.

## Answer 7
It should feel like a bargain with the thing being asked. Grain, weather, beasts, blood, each one answers to its own price. It should cost mortals something they would rather keep. A season, a year of their strength, a child's first word, the memory of a face. Never nothing. The magic that costs nothing makes no one careful, and a careless people starves.

## Answer 8
A god should stand out a great deal. Not a great sorcerer. A thing the mortals have no word for, that the whole valley goes quiet for. They should be able to tell, from across a field, that something larger than them has arrived and that it expects to be obeyed. I did not come down to be one voice among many. Ha. Though I would also not mind being laughed at, if it came to that.

## Answer 9
Strange, at the edges. Familiar animals near the villages and the roads, so that people can plan their lives. Past the last fence, things no one has a name for, because a people who can name everything they fear have stopped being brave. I would like the wild to have a few creatures that look like they belong to no one's idea of how the world was made. Half-things, even. I would not be alone.

## Answer 10
Famine, first and last. The slow kind, that arrives in a good-looking year and takes the stores before anyone has counted them. After that, the thing in the dark that comes for the herds, and the neighbor who knows you are hungry. A good story begins with an empty granary and a long road.

## Answer 11
Grain that keeps. Not gold, not iron, not salt, though people will fight over those too. The surplus. The year's extra that lets a people stop moving, or lets a war band eat through the winter. Whoever holds a full store holds the others. Mortals should trade for it, steal it, and kill for it, and a few should go without and remember who let them.

## Answer 12
Old roads, and old granaries. Sealed ones. Great round stone stores with their doors shut and no one alive who knows what was put in them or why they were closed. Also bones of something enormous, because I want the herds to have been bigger once. I would like to open one door and be sorry.

## Answer 13
Yes, a few, and not friendly by default. Peoples who do not farm, or who farm in a way I do not understand, so that my people must decide whether they are neighbors, rivals, or prey. They should be old, and they should have long memories of the humans. I would like them to be worth fighting and worth learning from, in that order.

## Answer 14
Few. One or two at a time, and they should be hard to read. A traveler who brings a tool or a seed or a method no one here has, and who does not stay. I want my people to have to decide what to do with a stranger who knows more than they do. Many travelers would make the strange ordinary.

## Answer 15
Face to face only. A knowing that arrives when we are close enough to be a danger to each other. I do not want to feel the others at any distance. I would rather meet them across a table or a field, with my own people watching, and find out in the first breath what they are. Distance should make us guess. Guessing keeps gods honest.

## Answer 16
One body. A god in many places at once is a god who is nowhere, and I want to be somewhere. If a god must be in several places, let it be through hands, a captain, a ruler who speaks for it, and let the cost be that those hands may close on their own. Splitting the body should cost the splitting god its certainty about which part is itself.

## Answer 17
A mortal who commands. A captain who can hold a column through a bad winter, or a woman who can read a field and a man in the same look. Someone who is willing to rule and willing to be ruled by something above. I want a mortal who will be loyal and still be dangerous. I do not want a saint. Ha, a saint would starve.

## Answer 18
A name. Or a thing that works like one. Something that was mine and is not now, found in the ground or in a stranger's mouth, so that I can pick it up and see whether it fits. Beyond that, a companion that is not a god and not a mortal, that I did not make.

## Answer 19
That something I lost can be found again, and that it is not as heavy as I fear. That is all I will say about it. The world is listening. Let it keep that.
