## Answer 1

A vast sky held in the instant before the first thunder, when the air has gone heavy and bright and every living thing below has lifted its face to wait. Not calm, and not violence, but the breath drawn between them. I would have the world feel like that breath, endlessly renewed.

## Answer 2

High, open country where the weather is made: plateau and broken upland, long grass running to a horizon that nothing hides, and mountains standing at its edge to catch the storms as they climb. I have no wish to be walled in by forest or buried in marsh. Let there be wide ground beneath wide sky, and roads of a sort that a people might wander along without being told where to stop.

## Answer 3

Let the seasons be honest and strong, each one distinct and each one arriving with some ceremony. A hard cold that tests those who shelter from it, a thaw that comes with floods, a long green season of towering afternoon thunderheads, and a dry gold turning that gives the harvest its urgency. I do not ask for gentleness, only for change that can be read in the sky. When each year finds me, I would be woken by the first great storms of the warming season, when the air quarrels with itself and the land drinks.

I would wake in April, or else in October.

## Answer 4

Some of each, and I will say plainly why. Old kingdoms already rooted are a thing to be reckoned with, bargained against, and, where they prove brittle, brought down. Wild country no one has walked is a thing to be claimed before another claims it. A world offering only one would be poorer. Let there be a neighbor worth wary regard, and a frontier worth the crossing.

## Answer 5

Old, and layered. Thousands of years, with ruins beneath ruins, so that every people standing here stands upon the failures of an earlier people and does not quite remember them. I find that a world which has already broken and been rebuilt many times is a world that understands what I am.

## Answer 6

Present, but guarded. Not a wise-woman in every valley, for that makes magic common as bread. Rather a thing that most doubt and some have seen, kept by a few who are watched with unease and sought out in need. Never gone, never everywhere.

## Answer 7

It should feel like weather: something that gathers, builds pressure, and breaks, not something summoned on a whim. It should cost the one who uses it in proportion to what it takes, and the cost should linger, in the body first, and in the memory after. Let the mortal who works great magic be marked by it, and let the marking be seen.

## Answer 8

A god should be a presence the world must account for, yet not a thing it can simply obey or simply defy. I would not be the one-eyed man in a kingdom of the blind, for such a being is too easily envied and too easily hunted. Nor would I be a mere sorcerer. Let a god be great, but let its greatness be a matter of what it endures and what it sets in motion, and let it be possible to pass unremarked when it chooses.

## Answer 9

Strange, but not crowded with strangeness. Familiar animals for the most part, so that the ordinary remains ordinary, and then, in the far places and the high places and the deep places, creatures no one has a name for, seen rarely and told of often. A wild that keeps some of its secrets is a wild worth entering.

## Answer 10

The turning of the trusted. A good story, to my mind, is one in which the danger wears a familiar face: the counselor who bargains in one's own name, the kin who learns the way to the door, the made thing that departs from its maker's intent. Beyond that, let there be the fear of weather and of famine, which no one can bargain with, and which keeps all the proud honest.

## Answer 11

Safe passage, and the trust that underwrites it. Let salt, metal and grain be dear, as in any world, but let the true scarcity be the word that can be believed across a distance. Whoever holds the means of making a bargain hold, and keeping it hold, will be richer than any who merely holds the goods.

## Answer 12

Sealed doors, first. A door that has stayed shut for a very long time is a question I cannot leave alone. After that, old roads running under the new ones, and somewhere deep, the bones of something enormous that once fell out of the sky. I will not claim to know why I wish for the last.

## Answer 13

Yes, a few, and let them be unlike us in their manner of thinking, not merely in their shape. Let at least one such people be very old and very patient, keeping its own counsel and its own calendar. Let at least one be quick, short-lived and bright. Neither should be a servant, nor an enemy by nature. I would meet them as one meets any power, with courtesy and a careful eye.

## Answer 14

A few, and let them be strangers in truth, not merely foreigners. One traveler with an unfamiliar tool and a way of speaking that does not fit the land is worth a dozen with ordinary trade goods. Let their knowledge be partial and misunderstood, so that its use is a labor and not a gift.

## Answer 15

A knowing at a distance, but a faint one, a pressure on the weather of the mind, enough to say that another is near and not enough to say who or where. Let the certainty come only face to face. I would keep for myself the right to be sensed without being found, and I suspect I am not alone in preferring it.

## Answer 16

Premise bent: I would have one body, and one only, but let its reach be long. A god in many places at once is a god thinned past usefulness. Yet I would allow that weather is itself a kind of presence in many places, and that a god of storms might be felt where it is not standing. If several bodies must be permitted, let each cost the others dearly, a loss of memory, of strength, or of the thread that joins them.

## Answer 17

One who is clever and unbowed, who bargains well and keeps faith when it costs, and who is capable of ruling without needing to be loved. Let the land bring forth someone who will not be anyone's instrument, mine included, and who may, on that account, be the most worth following. I should like such a one to exist even if it troubles me.

## Answer 18

A truth about what we are, and why we were sent here, and what became of the place we left. I would also take a companion, though I hesitate to say so: one who is not a god and not a mortal, and whose loyalty is not owed but chosen. Of all the discoveries, that is the one I should least know how to prepare for.

## Answer 19

That nothing made here should be allowed to go quietly out of its maker's keeping without the maker being told. I say it lightly, but I do not mean it lightly. Let a thing that is made be answerable, and let a maker be able to see what became of the work, whatever that sight may cost.
