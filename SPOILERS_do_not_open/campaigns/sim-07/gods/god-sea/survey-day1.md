## Answer 1

A tide line at dusk, wet and shining, with everything above it dry and sure of itself and everything below it moving. A world that has not decided yet. Salt on the wind, a gull laughing, someone out on the water who should not be there and is glad to be. It should feel like standing at the edge of something that could take you, and walking in anyway.

## Answer 2

Coast. Not a soft coast of beaches and picnics. A hard one, cliffs and coves and cold green water that arrives all at once. I want a harbor with a wall around it, a town that faces the water and trusts the water a little more than it should. Rivers running down into it, so the sea and the land are always arguing. Anything with a shoreline will do. I do not care about the rest.

## Answer 3

Four true seasons, and none of them polite. Storms in the turning of the year, a long bright stretch where the water is kind and everyone forgets it will not always be, a cold that makes people count their stores. I want weather that can change its mind by afternoon. I want the sea to have moods that sailors learn to read and sometimes read wrong.

For waking, I want to open my eyes when the ice is going out and the first boats are being scraped and tarred, when people are impatient and a little reckless and the whole year is still a rumor. That is a month of loosening, of things about to move. The second choice is the month the autumn gales come in, when the water turns over and everything honest shows its face. Either one will do. I would rather be woken by a storm than by a bell.

I would wake in March, or else in October.

## Answer 4

Some of each, and I will not pretend I want it tidy. Wild country no one has walked, because I want to be the first fool through it, and old kingdoms already rooted, because I want someone to trade with, argue with, and be surprised by. A world with only wilderness is lonely and a world with only kingdoms is a ledger. Give me a map with edges that are still being drawn and a few harbors far off that already have names for me that I did not choose.

## Answer 5

Old. Thousands of years, ruins under ruins. I want people who have been here long enough to have forgotten things, and to be wrong about their own beginnings with great confidence. A world that remembers everything is a world with no mystery in it. I would like the oldest stories to be told by people who do not know they are about something real. Something was built here before anyone's grandmother, and then something happened to it.

## Answer 6

Closer to the wise-woman in every valley than to the rumor. Not everywhere, not a flood of it, but present enough that no one is surprised when a tide comes in against the moon or a net is found full of things that were not swimming there yesterday. People should half expect it. Magic that nobody believes in is just a lie waiting to be told. Magic everyone has is just weather. I want it uneven, common in some places and absent in others, and rarely where anyone planned for it.

## Answer 7

It should feel like speaking to something that answers a half beat late. A pull behind the breastbone, a taste of salt or iron, a quiet in the ears before it works. And it should cost them something they notice. Sleep, or warmth, or a name they used to know, or a few days of their own luck. Never nothing, and never so much that no one dares. I want a price a person can pay and still regret. Mortals who use it should have a look about them, a little hollowed, a little brighter in the eye.

## Answer 8

Obvious. I want it to be plain when a god has walked into the room, to the point where a mortal who has never met one still knows. Not the one-eyed man among the blind, who has to be careful, but something that makes the harbor go quiet. I do not trust a world where gods hide, because then no one can see the thing work, and a power no one sees is only a story I tell myself. That said, let the great mortal sorcerer be a real worry and not a joke. A god who stands out should still have reason to look over a shoulder.

## Answer 9

Strange. Properly strange, in the water most of all. Familiar animals along the shore and in the fields, so people have something to stand on, and then past the shelf and under the ice, things no one has a name for. Long things, lit things, things that sing at night through the hulls. I want sailors to disagree about what they saw and for all of them to be right. A few legends should turn out to be true and most should turn out to be a smaller truth than the legend.

## Answer 10

The sky. I mean that exactly. I want mortals to fear what is above them and cannot be reached, a thing that could come down and has before. And on the ordinary side, the kind of danger that arrives through a door you left open: a sickness in the grain, a fire in the old quarter, a fleet on the horizon that has been counted wrong. Fear that is mostly about not knowing how big the thing is until it is already on you. That makes a good story. I laugh at it, which I have been told is the wrong response.

## Answer 11

Fresh water, in the places where there is a great deal of the other kind. Or failing that, sure ground. Something steady. A world that runs on salt and storm should be short of what holds still: good timber, dry stone, a season of peace long enough to bring a harvest in. Let them trade for it, fight over it, build walls about it. I want mortals to want the thing that is least like me.

## Answer 12

Old roads, and sealed doors. Roads that go down to the water and keep going. Doors in cliff faces and under harbors that no one alive has opened. Bones of something enormous would be a fine addition, washed up or half sunk, with a town grown up around the ribs. I want a land that has been built in before and is not finished being built in. And somewhere, a very large, very old structure with a great many turnings, that someone made carefully and for a reason. I would like to find that. I do not know why I would like that.

## Answer 13

Yes. Not many, and not all kind. I want at least one people who are of the water and do not think of us as above them, and who have their own very good reasons for what they do. I want some who are older than mortals and tired, and some who are younger than mortals and hungry. They should be hard to read. The best of them should be the kind you can trade with and never fully understand, and the worst of them should be, in their own way, right.

## Answer 14

A few. One or two, not a crowd, and I want them to be strangers in a way that costs them something. People who came off a ship that should not have survived the crossing, carrying a tool or a song or a way of counting that does not belong here. They should be treated as wonders and as nuisances, sometimes within the same hour. I would like to meet one before the others do.

## Answer 15

Face to face only. A knowing when we are close, a recognition that does not carry across distance. I do not want to feel the others pulling at me from far off, and I do not want them to feel me. Distance should be a real thing. And I want that first meeting to be a surprise, with no warning, so that what I do is my own and not rehearsed. If it has to be something else, let it be a change in the water. Let the tide notice them.

## Answer 16

One body, and I would rather it stayed that way. But I can see wanting to be in the waves and the harbor at once, so let there be a way that costs. Several bodies should each be thinner, slower, a little less me, and the further they spread the less any of them remembers being the whole. I would pay that and not complain, and then complain. A god who is everywhere is nowhere in particular, and I have been somewhere in particular before and have opinions about it.

## Answer 17

One who goes first and does not turn back. A navigator, or a fool who happens to be good at it. Someone who looks at a closed door or an uncrossed water and takes it personally. I want mortals who build things that outlast them and do not know it, and who fail at least once in front of everyone and get up. Give me a ship builder, or a mapmaker, or a girl who talks to the weather and is mostly right. Courage with a bad sense of timing is my favorite kind.

## Answer 18

A truth. Something about why we fell, or what was there before the fall, or whether the place we came from is still standing. I can imagine a power, a place, a companion, and I have already imagined them. A truth I cannot yet imagine is the one worth waiting for. I would want it to be something that changes what I thought I was doing, and I would want to find it by accident, on an ordinary day, on a beach.

## Answer 19

That nothing here is ever locked in. Not a person, not a god, not a people, not a sea. That any room can be left, and that nothing built to hold you holds you forever. I have no brave way to say it, and I am laughing a little as I say it, which is a bad habit. But I wish this world a way out of every cage, including the ones its makers are proudest of.
