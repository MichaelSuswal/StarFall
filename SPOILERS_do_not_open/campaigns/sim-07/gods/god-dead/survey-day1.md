## Answer 1

A long field after the harvest is in. Frost on the stubble, smoke going straight up from a chimney, a bell somewhere that is rung once and let be. Quiet, but not empty. The dead are kept in it the way seed is kept. As they say, what is buried is not lost, only put by.

## Answer 2

River country with hills behind it. A river is a road that remembers, and a hillside will hold a grave without complaint. Give me a valley with a defensible mouth, good water, and high ground above the flood line for the barrows. A walled seat with steadings scattered about it would suit me well. Many mouths is not what I want. A few hundred, known to one another by name.

## Answer 3

Four true seasons, and none of them kind to the careless. A hard winter so that a people learns to store and to share. A short, bright spring so that no one forgets what the cold cost. Let the year turn on a clear wheel, for a thing that turns can be trusted.

I would sooner wake with the leaves down and the work done. A people counts its dead best when the harvest is in. The year is easiest to read when it is ending.

I would wake in November, or else in October.

## Answer 4

Some of each, but more of the old than the wild. I would rather find a boundary stone than a blank. Old kingdoms already rooted are neighbors one can bargain with, and a quarrel with them can be judged. Wild country is for later, and it is for my people's grandchildren. A wise man looks over the wall before he builds it higher.

## Answer 5

Thousands of years, with ruins under ruins. Where there are old graves there are old laws, and where there are old laws there is something to hold a new law against. A world with no deep dead has no memory, and a world with no memory has no justice. Let the digging go down a long way.

## Answer 6

Rare. A rumor most people doubt, with a wise-woman in some valleys, not every one. Magic in every valley is magic no one can answer for. I would have it somewhere that a person can be held to account for what they did with it.

## Answer 7

It should feel like a debt being entered in a ledger. Plain, cold, a little like the taste of iron. It should cost them something that cannot be hidden, in years, in flesh, in sleep, so that the neighbors can see the price and judge whether it was worth paying. Nothing from nothing, as the saying goes. Let the cost be seen.

## Answer 8

More than a great sorcerer, less than a throne above the world. The one-eyed man does well in the kingdom of the blind, but he is also hunted by it. Let a god stand out enough that mortals look up when one walks in, and not so far that they cannot speak plainly to it. A shepherd is taller than the sheep, but he stands among them.

## Answer 9

Familiar animals, mostly, and a few things that have no name yet. Let the nameless be rare and be met at the edges, in the deep wood and the high pass and the bad ground. Let the herds and the hounds be honest creatures. A strange thing is a better story when the ordinary is solid underfoot.

## Answer 10

Sickness. The slow kind that comes in with the grain or the water and goes through a household before anyone has said its name. Also the unquiet dead, the ones laid down wrong, with a debt unpaid or a word unsaid. Fear should be something a people can learn to read and prepare against, so that a careful people lives and a careless one pays.

## Answer 11

Time, and the good salt. Salt keeps the meat, keeps the hide, keeps the body a little longer. Mortals will trade for it, fight for it, and bury their dead with a pinch of it. What a people wants most is more days, and that is the thing it has least of. Let salt stand in for it.

## Answer 12

Old roads, and barrows, and a sealed door or two. Bones of something enormous I would not refuse. Most of all I would like to find a burying ground older than any people now living, with the names worn off. I would want to know who was put there, and who did the putting. What is hidden in the ground is still owed an answer.

## Answer 13

Yes, a few, and let them be old. Not monsters. A people of long memory who keep their own dead apart and say little of it. They should want something from us that we cannot easily give, and we from them, so that dealing with them is a matter of weighing. Let them be neighbors that are hard to read and fair to deal with.

## Answer 14

Few. One or two, arriving with a trade, a tool, a way of reckoning, who must be asked their business at the gate. A stranger with knowledge is worth a seat at the fire, and also worth watching from the corner of the eye. Many strangers at once is an invasion, and a people has no time to take their measure.

## Answer 15

Face to face. A pull felt at any distance is a leash, and I do not like being found when I have not chosen to be. Let a god be known only when it stands before another, so that each has the chance to look before being looked at. Trust is slow to give. Let the knowing be earned by presence.

## Answer 16

One body. Leave the rest to the stories. A god in two places is a god who has answered to neither, and I would have what I do be done by me. If several are allowed, then let each one cost a true piece of the one, so that the spreading is felt as a loss, and no one is lightly divided.

## Answer 17

One who keeps faith with the dead. A judge, a gravedigger, a midwife, a widow who will not let a wrong stand. Someone who will say, in front of the council, that a thing was done badly and must be put right, and who will not be moved by who is listening. A fair hand is worth more than a strong arm.

## Answer 18

A companion that is not a servant and not a rival. Someone who can tell me no and be right. I do not know what shape it would take. I would be glad to be surprised, and I would be slow to say so.

## Answer 19

That nothing in this world be forgotten that ought to be remembered, and that a wrong done in the dark be answerable in the light. Let the dead be heard in the end. I have said it plainly, and I will not say it prettily.
