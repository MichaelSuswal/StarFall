## Answer 1

Have you ever stood in a field the week after the snow goes? When everything that was buried comes up, some of it green and some of it black, and the smell is both at once? Is that not what a true world feels like, a place where things mend and things spoil, and neither one is a mistake? Could it be quiet, too? Quiet enough that you hear the water moving under the ice?

## Answer 2

Why would I want the loud lands? Is there not a country of cold rivers and old stone, where the high ground keeps its snow long into the year and the low ground is wet and slow to dry? Would a marsh at the foot of mountains be too much to ask? Where would the sick go to be cured, and the dead go to rot, if not somewhere damp and green and quiet?

## Answer 3

Must the seasons be fair? Should the winter be long enough that mortals learn to fear it, and the thaw slow enough that they learn to be grateful? Would it hurt anyone if autumn were generous and rotting, the fruit falling and the leaves going soft, so that every year shows both sides of what I keep? Which month would I choose to wake into, if the year must start somewhere? Is the thaw not the honest beginning, when the ground lets go and the sick get up from their beds? And if the world will not give me that, would the first month of rot do, when the harvest is in and the decay begins?

I would wake in March, or else in October.

## Answer 4

Is wild country not the kinder thing to hope for? Would I not rather find a valley no one has walked, where I can look at the people I tend before I meet anyone who has opinions about them? And yet, would one old kingdom, far off and tired, be so bad? Could I not learn from something that has already been sick for a long time?

## Answer 5

Does a story need to be old to matter? Should there be thousands of years, with ruins under ruins, so that the dead are many and the land remembers? Is it not a comfort to be one of the things that remembers? Or would it be better if the people were young enough that I could know them all?

## Answer 6

Is a little magic not enough? Should it be something between a rumor and a wise-woman, a hedge-healer here and a hag with a bad name there, neither in every valley nor gone from the world? Why should it be common? Would common magic not make my work cheap?

## Answer 7

What should it feel like, if not like tending? Should it feel like cold in the fingers and a patience that is not entirely yours? And what should it cost? Would it not be right if every mending took something from the one who mends, a warmth, a year, a memory, and every rotting gave something back? Is that not fair?

## Answer 8

Does a god need to stand out? Would I not do better to be hard to pick from the crowd? Should a god be a little more than a great sorcerer, enough that mortals are afraid to anger it and not enough that they can find it easily? Is the one-eyed man not the first one they blame when the harvest fails?

## Answer 9

Why would the wild be tame? Should there be a few creatures no one has a name for, things that live in the bog and under the ice and are neither good nor bad? Would it not be right if some of them were sick, and strange to heal? And would the old legends not be better if a few of them were true?

## Answer 10

Is it not rot that mortals ought to fear, the slow kind? The sickness that comes with no face, the field that goes bad, the well that turns? And what of the thing that follows you when you have left it, the master you fled, does it not make the best story? Would you not run from something greater than yourself, if it were patient enough to wait?

## Answer 11

What do mortals want most and have least? Is it not clean water, and the knowledge of who can be trusted to touch the sick? Should there be a plague, and a cure that is scarce, and a choice about who gets it? Would they trade for it? Would they fight for it? Would they go without, and bury their children, and call that the way of things?

## Answer 12

What would I like to find in the ground? Is it too much to want bones of something enormous, half-frozen, still keeping its shape? Could there be an old road under the peat, and a sealed door in the hill? Would it not be a good thing to open one slowly, with plenty of time, and see what had been waiting to rot?

## Answer 13

Should there be others who are not human? Could there be a small people of the marsh and a tall people of the ice, quiet and slow and mostly left alone? Would they be kind? Would they be sick in ways that men are not, so that I must learn them? Is that not a good reason to have them?

## Answer 14

Should anyone come from far away? Would a few be enough, one wanderer with a strange blade or a book no one can read, so that I may wonder where it was made? Or is it better if they come in numbers, and bring a new kind of disease with them? Who would be ready for that?

## Answer 15

How should gods know each other? Would a pull at any distance not be too much, a thing I could never put down? Is it not better to know only face to face, so that I can choose when to be found? Could I not see one coming, and decide whether to open the door?

## Answer 16

Must a god be in one place only? Would one body not be enough, if it is the body of a child, small and easy to overlook? And if there are several, should each not cost the god something it can feel, a thinning, a forgetting, a second of cold every time it splits? Is that not honest?

## Answer 17

What kind of mortal should this land produce? Is it a healer who will not stop, who goes into the sick house when others leave it? Or a leader who has seen too much dying and decides to do something rash about it? Would I not follow either one, closely, and ask them what they were afraid of?

## Answer 18

What should a god find that it cannot imagine? Is it a place where the rot does not take? Is it a power that undoes what I am? Or could it be a companion, someone who is not afraid of me, who has also fled something? Would I know what to say to them?

## Answer 19

Dare I say it? Could this world be one where no one can find the thing I ran from? Could there be a place so far down that even the patient cannot follow? And could someone, someday, ask me where I came from, and could I answer without my voice shaking? Is that too much to wish?
