## Answer 1

Water that remembers where it has been. A wide land at first thaw, every slope talking, every hollow filling, and a long grey-bright light over it. We want a world that sounds like something is always arriving. Old, patient and loud with running things, and not afraid to be seen.

## Answer 2

River country. High springs, braided lowlands, the places where one water meets another and must decide what to become. We would take marsh and delta at the edges, and mountains behind for the sources. We want a land where every road follows a channel, and where a people can walk the banks and never be lost.

## Answer 3

Four true seasons, and the turning of each one should be felt in the water. A hard winter that locks the rivers, a spring that breaks them open with floods that can take a bridge and a village if the people were careless, a warm summer of low clear water, an autumn of rain and harvest. Law in the cycle. A people can plan by it, and punish no one for what the flood does, but they must answer for what they built in its path.

We wake best when the ice is going. The thaw is the year's first true thing.

I would wake in April, or else in March.

## Answer 4

Some of each. Wild country along the upper waters where no one has walked, so there is somewhere to spread. Old kingdoms downstream, already rooted, so there is someone to meet, to trade with, and to be measured against. We do not trust a world with no neighbors. We gather people, and gathering needs others to gather from and stand beside.

## Answer 5

Thousands of years. Ruins under ruins, with the oldest ones near the rivers, because people always settled where we run. A long human story is a long record, and we would read it in the silt.

## Answer 6

Never gone, never everywhere. A rumor in the cities, a wise-woman in the river valleys and few elsewhere. Enough that a priest's rite might hold, and not so much that a rite is cheap.

## Answer 7

It should feel like listening. Mortals who work it should feel the world answer, slowly, as a current answers a hand held in it. The cost should be time and the body. Long fasts, long vigils, cold, a price paid in years or in sleep, and a debt that is real and can be seen on the one who owes it. Nothing free. We want every working to leave a mark that others can read.

## Answer 8

Plainly. A god should stand out, and be seen to stand out. We lean toward revealed and do not apologize for it. The one-eyed man among the blind is nearer the truth than the great sorcerer, but not by so much that mortals cannot stand beside us. Enough that a people kneels willingly, and not so much that there is no one left to argue.

## Answer 9

Strange enough to be dangerous. Familiar animals for the most part, with legends that turn out to be true at the water's edge: things in deep pools, things that come up the channels in flood years. Some creatures with no name. We want mortals to name them, and to pay for the naming.

## Answer 10

Flood and drought. The river that comes too high, the river that does not come. Sickness carried in water. Dams and the people who build them. A danger that is also a promise, because the thing that kills is the thing that feeds. That is the best kind of story, and the oldest.

## Answer 11

Clean water, in the places that have none. And salt, and good stone, and trust. We would make trust the scarcest of all, because it is the thing we ourselves hold back longest. A people who have little of it will keep their oaths dearly, and we value that more than any ore.

## Answer 12

Old roads, and sealed doors. Roads that go down to the water and stop at it. Doors in the banks and under the springs, with something kept behind them. We would want to be the one who is not let in at first, and who has to find out why. Bones, too. Something enormous that died where a river now runs.

## Answer 13

Yes, a few. Peoples of the water margins and the deep caves, not like men, with their own law and their own long memory. Wary of us, as we are of them. We want a people that answers to no god, and that a god must treat as an equal or fail.

## Answer 14

A few. Strays who came from far off with odd tools and half a story. Not many, and none who can be trusted at once. We would listen to what they know, and then test it against the river.

## Answer 15

Face to face. A knowing that comes only when two stand near each other, at a ford or a spring, and cannot be mistaken. A pull felt at any distance would let gods be avoided or hunted from afar, and we would have no choice about it. We prefer to be met, and to choose the meeting.

## Answer 16

Two forms, not two places. One body at a time, shifting between its shapes, and the cost of the shift should be real: a pause, a weakness, a thing that cannot be done in the other shape. If several bodies, then each should be a loss. A god spread thin should be thin.

## Answer 17

A keeper. Someone who holds to a hard thing for a whole life and does not break. A mortal of loyalty, who stays at a post when it would be easier to leave, and who is followed because of it. We would also welcome the one who breaks a bad law and is right, if they pay for it honestly.

## Answer 18

A companion that is not a god and not a mortal. Something that has been in the water longer than we have, and has opinions. We would also like to find out what we were before we were this, and we do not yet know the shape of that wish.

## Answer 19

That nothing born of a death is wasted. That what comes out of an ending can be made to run clean and long, and carry people with it. We would ask the world to let us make something of this, and not to let it be taken.
