# Mysteries: campaign sim-03

Written by the Keeper on creation Night 3b, 2026-11-21, in template 7.8 of the creation runbook. One epic, four long, thirteen short. Every clue was placed tonight and is findable from the lock on; none is held back and none is added later. In each clue, SEEN, REPORTED or RUMORED says how it would first reach a god, and "earliest" is the first year the world itself pushes it anywhere; a god that goes and looks finds it in any year. ## E01 The Country Left Unsung
Kind: epic. Span: the whole campaign, about 60 years. Related to: L02, L03, S13.
Answers questions: yes

### Truth
The gods who came down are what is left of one far country, thrown clear the night its star fell (plane entry [p01]). Each is a thing that country did or leaned on for so long that it outlived the people; none was a person, and the wound each carries belonged to no one. The Warm Stone [f42] is a lump of the same throw that did not become a god. The country's names were never carried out: its keeping-house refused them to the Canters, and what was kept there was taken in by the thing that now walks as one of the gods, whole but for one name that was only ever said aloud at a graveside and never written. That one name lies in the fog of two other gods. The Canters walk a round of the settled country in Years 3 to 5 and again in Years 36 to 38. A name they are given they carry and sing, and names they have sung long enough have come back before: Ansa's Spring [f58] is one. If the gods who hold the pieces bring them together and give them, the far country's names are sung again and each god whose share was given finds its fog thinner: the Keeper then releases to that god the part of its sealed past that its given names belong to. No one god holds enough to do it alone.
The Canters answer questions.
Limit: The Canters know the names they carry, where they were given each, and that a name carried long enough has sometimes come back; they do not know why, what a god is, what the stone is, or what happened in the far country after they left it. They will not say a held beat's name, because they do not have it. They answer only at night, only a question about a name or a place, only by singing the part of a list that holds it, and for any one asker only one question in a night. They sing nothing under compulsion, since no one of them holds a whole list.

The central measurements, and every way each can be earned. First, what the stone dreams to a god itself: only by that god going to the stone and sleeping with a hand on it. Second, that another god's fog holds the same country: only by meeting that god and being told, by a counter sent to it, or by an alliance. Third, how many beats the newest list holds silent and which of them the keeping god fills: only by a god being present through a night of the singing with that god beside it, which takes that god's own going or an alliance with it. Fourth, the one name never written: only at the stone, by one of the two gods whose fog holds it. None of these is carried to any god by a letter, an envoy or a visitor. The Canters come on a clock, but they come to peoples and not to gods, they sing what they sing to whoever is there, and what they sing holds no name of the far country that matters and no count: the rests are heard only by one who sits the whole night and counts for itself.

### Body
The Warm Stone [f42], in open hill pasture of the Old Low Country [r15]. The Canters, on their round (C01, C02). The gods' own fogs. The held beats in the Canters' newest list. Ibbet at Farwalker's Cot [f60], whose line walked out of the same country (S13).

### Cost if ignored
The stone is found by Averley's [f39] shepherds in the first winter. If no god has reached it by Year 24, a small priesthood of the stone stands in the Old Low Country [r15], no god's own, that sleeps beside it and recites the kneeling crowd and the unseen river as the return of Averley's [f39] first kings; it then tells the gods' own wounds to anyone, wrongly, and the regent uses it. If one god reaches the stone before the others, that god learns the shape of the others' pasts, which is a lever over them. If nothing is given to the Canters by the end of their second round, nothing of the far country is ever carried, and no god's fog thins by this road.

### Clock or trigger
C01, the Canters' first round, Years 3 to 5 [r-0174]. C02, their second round, Years 36 to 38 [r-0175]. T01, the stone found and slept beside. T02, the priesthood of the stone, Year 24 if no god has reached it [r-0177]. T03, a god at the stone.

### Clues
#### The melted ring
Location: The Warm Stone [f42]
What is shown: SEEN by Averley's [f39] shepherds, REPORTED at the city by spring of Year 1, RUMORED along the Meadow Road [f32] from Year 2. A blue-grey stone, heavy for its size, lying in a ring of bare wet turf while snow lies everywhere else. It is warm to the hand in every season.
Readings: a piece of the star; a thing a god dropped or sent; a hot spring's cap; a sign for the regent, or against him.

#### What sleepers dream
Location: The Warm Stone [f42]
What is shown: SEEN by anyone who sleeps within a few paces. A crowd kneeling in a long hall with a laid table, and a broad river no one here has seen, with a gate across it. Two shepherds who had never met dreamed the same river.
Readings: the stone is a god that did not wake; the stone remembers Averley's [f39] first kings; the stone is where the gods came from; the dream is a sending from a rival.

#### The god's own dream at the stone
Location: The Warm Stone [f42], and the god's own body
What is shown: SEEN by a god that sleeps with a hand on it. The same hall and river, and in it the one place its own fog comes from, clear for the length of the dream; it wakes with two or three names in its mouth that belong to no place here.
Readings: the stone is the god's own past; the stone is a dead sibling; the stone lies and fits itself to whoever sleeps; the names are names of this land's own lost places.

#### The held beats
Location: the Canters, wherever they sing on their rounds (C01, C02)
What is shown: SEEN by anyone who listens through a night of their singing. Their lists are names, some of places here that no longer stand. The newest list has rests in it: a beat held silent where a name should be, many of them, and one longer than the rest.
Readings: a mourning custom; names they are forbidden to say; names stolen from them; a list of the dead with the nameless counted.

#### The song stops
Location: the Canters, in the presence of the god that kept the far country's roll
What is shown: SEEN. When they sing the newest list within that god's hearing, the singing falters at the first held beat and stops. If the god stays and they begin again, the god hears itself say a name aloud at each held beat but the long one.
Readings: the Canters fear that god; that god is cursed to speak; the names are that god's own victims; the Canters are doing it to that god.

#### What another god dreams
Location: any other god, met face to face
What is shown: REPORTED by that god, if asked and willing. Its fog holds the same river, or the same hall, or the same wood, from another side.
Readings: the gods are kin; the gods are one thing broken; that god is lying to draw the asker out; all gods everywhere dream alike.

### Branches
#### A god carries the stone home as a relic
What the world does: its people sleep near it and dream the hall and the river. Within four years its town has a cult of the stone's grief, which is not worship of the god and adds nothing to it. The Old Low Country [r15] counts the stone stolen.
#### A god gives the Canters only the names it dreamed at the stone
What the world does: they take them and sing them. That god's fog thins by its own share and no more. The held beats that were filled are sung as names from then on.
#### The gods who hold the pieces give them together
What the world does: the Canters sing the whole list for a night, including the long beat, and carry it out. Each god whose share was given is released the matching part of its sealed past. In later years a trader from off the sheet has heard one of those names sung far away.
#### A god holds the Canters to learn their lists by force
What the world does: they sing nothing. Their second round does not come to that god's country, and they tell no one why.
#### A god breaks or buries the stone
What the world does: it breaks like stone and each piece is warm. The dreams go on at each piece, fainter. Nothing else happens.
#### No god does anything
What the world does: T02 runs as written, C02 passes, and the cost above is paid in full.

### Never found
Whose memory any one god is made of, by name. Why the far country's last things were shaped like leaders. What the long held beat's name meant to the people who said it.

## L01 The Debt Not Called
Kind: long. Span: about 30 years of looking, with a clock at Year 47. Related to: L02, L03, L04.
Answers questions: no

### Truth
The Sleeper [f44] is alive, older than the fall before, and asleep. It takes in what makes things particular from everything near it, slowly, and it is fed from above: on a night when the star dims, a thread of blue light comes down and lands on it. One of those who rose from this ground long ago sends that thread. Over twelve centuries it emptied the first city of the bowl of its names and then of its people. The Hethren have slowed the same thing in themselves by not looking at it and by holding a child's name back. It is small for its kind and slow, and under this star it wants nothing soon. From Year 47 it begins to draw sleepers onto the Dun Back [r09]: first the unnamed children of the nearest camps, then others. Force used against it is taken in like anything else.

### Body
The Sleeper [f44] on the Dun Back [r09]. The thread on dim hours (P01). The Nameless Mounds [f57]. The Hethren's customs. Ansa's Spring [f58].

### Cost if ignored
From Year 47 the camps nearest the Dun Back [r09] lose sleepers, a few each year, who walk onto the hill at night and are not found. Within ten years more it reaches whoever winters in the Gap Vale [r10] and anyone a god has settled in the bowl.

### Clock or trigger
C03, the drawing of sleepers, from Year 47 [r-0176]. T04, a people settled within two days of the Dun Back [r09]. T05, force used against the Sleeper [f44].

### Clues
#### The word for it
Location: the Hethren, at Hethmoot [f43] or any camp
What is shown: RUMORED from Year 1 wherever Hethren trade. Their word for the long hill is also their word for a debt not yet called. They do not graze, dig or sleep on it and do not look at it long. They say that big things are asleep and that looking wakes them.
Readings: a holy place; a burial taboo; bad grazing dressed as custom; a real beast they are afraid of.

#### The seventh winter
Location: the Hethren camps nearest the Dun Back [r09]
What is shown: REPORTED by any Hethren asked about children. A child has no name of its own until its seventh winter. In the camps that winter nearest the hill it is the ninth. Old women say a name given young "does not take."
Readings: a custom against grief when small children die; a tax dodge at the winter count; fear of a spirit that steals named children; something on the hill.

#### Warm ground
Location: The Dun Back [r09]
What is shown: SEEN by anyone who goes. Bare dark ground where frost does not lie long and no bird nests. The flocks grazed nearest it are all one dun color and carry no earmark that can be read.
Readings: a hot spring under the hill; old burning; a breed kept on purpose; poor soil.

#### The beast in the hollow
Location: The Sleeper [f44]
What is shown: SEEN by anyone who climbs to the hollow. A sleeping animal a mile or two long, plainly an animal, breathing perhaps once in an hour. The hill round it has the same outline many times larger.
Readings: a god asleep; the hill's child; a thing that fell with the star; the last of a kind that the great bones belong to.

#### The thread
Location: The North Heath [r08] and The South Heath [r22], and the high ground at The Table Gap [f15]
What is shown: SEEN from the bowl and its rim on a dim hour, REPORTED by Hethren riders in any year one falls (P01). The star goes dull for about an hour, and a thread of blue light stands from it to the middle of the bowl.
Readings: the star drinks from the hill; the star feeds the hill; the star speaks to something; marsh fire seen from far off.

#### Posts with no marks
Location: The Nameless Mounds [f57]
What is shown: SEEN by anyone who digs. The lowest mounds hold post stones cut with house marks, many and different. Higher up the marks grow fewer and plainer. The top course has none.
Readings: a conquest that forbade the marks; a people who forgot how to carve; a change of faith; whatever is on the hill did it.

### Branches
#### A god attacks the Sleeper [f44]
What the world does: fire, iron and poison do nothing that lasts. Those who did the work come back unable to say their own names. The clock C03 comes ten years sooner.
#### A god tries to wake or speak to it
What the world does: it does not wake. A god that sleeps in the hollow dreams of new rivers cutting across a country it almost knows, and is a little less itself for a season.
#### A god moves people off the heath, or keeps them from settling there
What the world does: C03 is put back by as many years as the bowl stays empty of sleepers within a day's ride, up to twenty.
#### A god has the hill honored
What the world does: the honoring is taken in. C03 comes five years sooner for each decade of it, and the nearest camps stop naming children at all.
#### A god watches and does nothing
What the world does: C03 runs as written.

### Never found
How long it slept before the fall before. What it will be when a star next comes down here, which is far beyond any year this campaign reaches. What Ummer called it.

## L02 The Three Before
Kind: long. Span: about 30 years. Related to: E01, L01.
Answers questions: yes

### Truth
A god grows heavier in itself as more mortals hold it in mind, and there is a point past which it grows light and leaves the ground for the star. Three gods came down here 2,470 years ago. One grew fast and rose. One saw its own lifting begin, kept itself small for eight hundred years, and rose anyway when its own stewards swelled its remembrance against its word. One gave itself away piece by piece to springs, beasts and stones, never lifted, and at last walked off the sheet. Those who rose are in the star and can reach down. When any god now on the ground passes 59 on the lore's scale, they speak to it in dreams and offer a power for weight. A god that stays above 74 for three years rises on the next dim hour. The world shows this three times first: in the god's shadow, in its stones, in its prayers coming back late.
The voices answer questions.
Limit: The voices know only what a risen god knows: that rising is not an ending, what they themselves were, and the power each offers. They do not know what the star is for, whether they are one or many, or what becomes of them later, and they say "we do not know" to those. They can be asked only in a dream, only by a god that has passed 59 or that sleeps in the hollow of the Dun Back [r09] on a dim hour, one question in a dream, and they answer from their own side: none of them thinks rising a loss.

### Body
The empty paved court at Averley [f39]. The King's Stair [f54] and the laid seat at Brennhal [f12]. The Hound Spring [f64], the Walked-Round Shrine [f53] and the Hart's other givings. Hollow Seat [f51] and the Old Stair [f52]. A god's own shadow.

### Cost if ignored
A god that seeks ever more remembrance without knowing this rises, and its people are left as the basin was left. A god that takes the voices' offers without understanding them rises within a decade.

### Clock or trigger
T06, the voices, when a god passes 59. T07, three years above 74.

### Clues
#### The soft court
Location: Averley [f39], the empty court
What is shown: SEEN by anyone admitted. The paving stones are rounded at their edges like stones long in a river, though no water runs there. A word said in the court comes back a breath late. A darker pattern of knots runs across the paving.
Readings: great age and many feet; a builder's trick of sound; a holy place worn by kneeling; something that sat here changed the stone.

#### Became something more
Location: Averley [f39], the court rite
What is shown: RUMORED wherever Averley [f39] trades. They say their first kings became something more and watch the court. The rite is held facing the empty middle.
Readings: ancestor worship; a claim that props up the crown; a king who was a god; a king who died badly and was hidden.

#### The king who lifted
Location: Brennhal [f12] and The King's Stair [f54]
What is shown: REPORTED by old Arroway if asked about the seat. The last king left the table and did not come back; one telling has it that long before, he was seen to rise a hand's breadth off his chair and sit down again white. They lay his seat each harvest. They say he left in anger at his stewards.
Readings: a king murdered at a feast; a king who walked out over the passes; a god taken up as a reward; a god struck down for pride.

#### The spring that keeps a nose
Location: The Hound Spring [f64]
What is shown: REPORTED by the oldest Thessen hound-keepers as a tale: a spring in the deep wood where a hound that drinks keeps its nose to the end of its life. SEEN by anyone who finds it and waits some years with a hound. A worn stone lip. A man's bones and a boar spear beside it (S12).
Readings: good water; a saint's or a king's blessing; a thing the wood does; a god's gift that outlived the god.

#### Leavings at the stair
Location: The Old Stair [f52] and Hollow Seat [f51]
What is shown: SEEN. Under the Ussel's small leavings at the foot of the stair lie older ones of another make, a hand deep. Hollow Seat [f51] has no burned course and no broken gate; its store pits were emptied in order.
Readings: a plague; a people who went to war and lost elsewhere; a people called away by something; a people who hid and are still hidden.

#### Lighter after the feast
Location: the god's own body
What is shown: SEEN by the god itself, earliest when a year of plenty and a year of want have both passed. It is stronger in a season when its people hold it in mind and less when they are too worn to. Far later, past 59: its shadow paler than a mortal's beside it.
Readings: gods feed on joy; on offerings; on the season; on being thought of.

### Branches
#### A god, feeling light, demands more worship to root itself
What the world does: it grows lighter. The shadow, then the stones, then the prayers show it, in that order. After three years above 74 it rises on the next dim hour, and play goes on from inside the star.
#### A god gives power away to mortals, beasts and places
What the world does: its reach grows and its own weight does not. What is given stays given whether or not the god stays.
#### A god keeps itself small
What the world does: nothing. It may rule for centuries and never hear a voice.
#### A god takes a voice's offer
What the world does: the power is real. The god is nearer 74 by the weight of it.
#### A god refuses
What the world does: no punishment. Another voice, unlike the first, says that below is also a place.
#### A god digs up the court at Averley [f39]
What the world does: older paving, then older, then, very deep, blue-grey glass (L03). The kingdom takes it as an act of war.

### Never found
The three's own names for themselves. Where the Hart went after it left the sheet. Which of the voices was which king.

## L03 The Roof That Held
Kind: long. Span: about 25 years. Related to: E01, L01.
Answers questions: yes

### Truth
A star came down on this ground 22,000 years ago. It ended everything that stood here and made the country that is here now. A few things that were dense enough in themselves came through it unchanged: the Sleeper [f44], and the Graith. Others of the Sleeper's [f44] kind did not, and their bodies lie in the rock. Under everything, very deep, is the floor that night left: blue-grey glass that rings. What is kept hard enough comes through; what is not is made over.
The Graith answer questions.
Limit: The Graith know what their own people saw and did: that the sky came down once, that they sat it out in the hall behind the Sealed Door [f49], that some of them woke and some did not, and what the land was like before. They do not know why it happened, what a star is, whether it will happen again or when, and they say so. They believe the rock saved them. One elder holds the telling and gives it once in its life, to the first asker who waits at Graithdeep [f46] without entering through one full round of seasons and asks plainly; after that it is not told again in that elder's life. Other questions are answered at the rate of one a season, in few words, and a question about the Door is not answered.

### Body
Graithdeep [f46] and The Sealed Door [f49]. The Long Bones [f38] and The Stone Keel [f61]. The lowest gallery under Swallet Mouth [f37]. The ground under the court at Averley [f39].

### Cost if ignored
Nothing is lost while it is left alone, but the telling is given once: if another god's asker hears it first, no one else hears it for that elder's life, which is longer than the campaign. If the Sealed Door [f49] is forced, the Graith shut Graithdeep [f46] to every outsider and say nothing more to anyone.

### Clock or trigger
T08, the telling given. T09, the Sealed Door [f49] forced.

### Clues
#### The roof held
Location: Graithdeep [f46]
What is shown: REPORTED by the Column, who have it from generations of leaving worked stone at the mouth: the Graith's one greeting is "the roof held," and they call human peoples and their rulers alike the loud brief ones.
Readings: a miners' blessing; a boast about their halls; a memory of an earthquake; a memory of something worse.

#### The floor that rings
Location: Swallet Mouth [f37], the lowest gallery
What is shown: SEEN by anyone who goes about two miles in and far down, past the burial niches. The limestone ends on a floor of blue-grey glass, smooth, that rings like a bowl when struck.
Readings: a slag floor from an old smelting; a frozen lake of some mineral; a floor laid by the Burying Folk; something that fell from the sky.

#### The Long Bones [f38]
Location: The Long Bones [f38]
What is shown: SEEN. Curved pale shapes in the rock face over hundreds of paces, each thicker than a house, set like ribs. The unnamed people keep away and say the ground there is owed.
Readings: bones of a beast; roots of a stone tree; a wreck of some great built thing; the hill's own bedding weathered strangely.

#### The same shape
Location: The Long Bones [f38], set beside The Sleeper [f44]
What is shown: SEEN only by one who has looked at both. The ribs in the rock and the flank of the living beast curve alike.
Readings: one died and one sleeps; the beast is the child of the bones; chance; the same god made both.

#### The shut door
Location: The Sealed Door [f49]
What is shown: SEEN. A door of fitted stone in a rock face, with no handle and no gap, and Graith marks renewed across its seam. A Graith is always within sight of it.
Readings: a treasury; a tomb; a prison; a way down to the old road.

### Branches
#### A god waits out the asking, or has someone wait
What the world does: after a full round of seasons the elder comes to the mouth and tells it, once, in about forty words. T08 fires.
#### A god forces the Sealed Door [f49]
What the world does: T09. Inside are the glass hall and the seated ones. The Graith neither fight nor speak, and are not seen above ground again in that god's time.
#### A god digs out the Long Bones [f38]
What the world does: they are bone all the way through, and there is no end to them in the rock. Years of labor; the unnamed people will not work there.
#### A god takes the glass for a thing to be mined
What the world does: it cuts like glass and holds an edge. Nothing else comes of it.

### Never found
The Stone Keel [f61], most likely, which no one living has seen. What the country before was called. Whether anything human came through.

## L04 What the Cord Costs
Kind: long. Span: about 25 years. Related to: L01.
Answers questions: no

### Truth
A working makes nothing. It moves what makes a thing particular from whatever is nearest to somewhere else, and the nearest particular thing a worker has is herself. Small workings cost the worker slowly. A great one strips the ground and the workers together. The bowl round the Dun Back [r09] is the one country on the sheet that is going plain of itself, and it loses a kind about every 28 years. Elsewhere under this star the land keeps its kinds unless someone spends them: a god that gathers more than eight workers under its patronage in one country, or lets one working be done that moves a river or breaks a siege, gets a bare round in that country and then loses a visible kind there every ten years.

### Body
The Bare Reach [f59]. The workers among every people, three to five in each, and what each has forgotten. The staff stone of Ysolt Arne. The law of Averley [f39] against knot workers at court. The thin brown bowl.

### Cost if ignored
The bowl loses a kind in Year 9 and again in Year 37 whatever anyone does. A god that builds a school of workers or allows a great working pays in its own country as above, and has a number of its people left blank.

### Clock or trigger
C04, the bowl loses a kind, Years 9 and 37 [r-0180, r-0181]. T10, a school of more than eight workers under one patron. T11, a great working.

### Clues
#### Grass with no seed
Location: The Bare Reach [f59]
What is shown: SEEN, and REPORTED by any Weirfolk herder as bad grazing. A round of ground a mile across where the grass sets no seed and every plant is the same plant. A waist-high stone with a cord pattern stands at its middle.
Readings: salt; an old burning; a curse on whoever cut the river; the price of cutting the river.

#### The worker who forgets
Location: any people's worker, at her own hearth
What is shown: REPORTED by her neighbors, SEEN by a god that watches a working. She warms a room, and the jar beside her frosts or the dog does not know her for an hour. She is vague about her own past, and the older she is the vaguer.
Readings: age; drink; the cold has to go somewhere; she pays with herself.

#### The blue in the hand
Location: any worker, at a hard working
What is shown: SEEN by a god close enough. A flicker of blue at the heart of the working, the color of the star.
Readings: the star blesses the work; the star watches it; the star is paid by it; a trick of tired eyes.

#### No knots at court
Location: Averley [f39]
What is shown: REPORTED by any clerk. No worker of knots may serve the crown or stand within the inner ring. The reason given is that a queen was once deceived by one.
Readings: court jealousy; an old treason; fear of what workers can do; memory of what a great working cost.

#### The lark that is gone
Location: The North Heath [r08] and The South Heath [r22]
What is shown: REPORTED by Hethren from Year 9 (C04). The crested lark of the heath is not heard that spring or after. Old riders say the cheese has tasted of less each year of their lives.
Readings: a hard winter; hawks; old people's talk; the hill.

### Branches
#### A god bans working among its people
What the world does: the few workers go on in secret or leave; some old people who were kept warm by them die in the next hard winter. No kind is saved by it, because the small workings were never the spend.
#### A god gathers workers into a school
What the world does: past eight, T10. A bare round appears near the school within two years and a tenth of the school is left blank each decade.
#### A god orders a great working
What the world does: it works. T11: a bare round a mile across, most of the workers left blank, and that country loses a kind every ten years after.
#### A god tries to restore the Bare Reach [f59] by planting or by its own power
What the world does: what is planted grows as the same plain grass. What a god gives of itself there stays, and is that much less of the god.

### Never found
Where what is spent goes. Why some can find the grip and most cannot.

## S01 Westcellar Under the Fall
Kind: short. Span: 4 to 8 years. Related to: none.
Answers questions: no

### Truth
In the thaw of 620 BA the west wall of the fifth pass fell on the older ring's fifth hold, with about 300 inside and the Marshal Edda Corr. The hold's rock cellars are whole under the fall, with that winter's dead and a ring's worth of bronze. The slope above has stood since and is sound in a freeze and rotten in a thaw.

### Body
The Shut Pass [f47]. The horn at Greybay Hold [f21].

### Cost if ignored
The Column goes on four holds where five once stood. In Year 3 the late thaw (C07) brings down a further length of the same wall, and anyone digging there that spring is under it.

### Clock or trigger
C07, the late thaw in the Scree Marches [r03], Year 3 [r-0205, r-0206, r-0207].

### Clues
#### The horn no one owns
Location: Greybay Hold [f21]
What is shown: SEEN. A marshal's horn of an old pattern, found on the scree about thirty years ago.
Readings: dropped by a traveler; a grave good washed out; from a hold that is gone.
#### The ring that does not close
Location: The Cairn Way [f20] and the cairn lines west of Greybay Hold [f21]
What is shown: SEEN by anyone who walks the old cairns. A line of them runs on west to the Shut Pass [f47] and stops at the fall.
Readings: an old summer pasture; a road to a people now gone; a fifth hold.

### Branches
#### The Column or its god digs in a freeze
What the world does: in two winters' work the cellars are reached: the dead, the bronze, sound dry store rooms. A fifth hold can be made of it.
