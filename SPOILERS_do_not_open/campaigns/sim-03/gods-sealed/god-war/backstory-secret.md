# Sealed past: god-war

The rest of the story behind the outline, tied to `gods-sealed/common-past.md`. No agent reads this. It reaches the god only through play, and never as a cause.

The outline it gave: a hall with the smoke gone gold, many voices saying one thing; a warm hand on its shoulder; a bell that went on longer than it should; a long held stillness and a slow cold; an extra chair laid.

The rest. It is the host of Tal Sedder, the hall above the Ondre in Veshkarn where that country's quarrels were settled at one long table, with a seat always laid for whoever had not yet come. Its peace was the hall's peace: inside the walls no hand was raised. The oath of the scale was sworn across its table.

What it lost and does not remember: the oath was broken at the same table. Later, on the night of the two closings, the bell-wardens sealed the hall with the table laid and the lights burning, to keep the country from ending by letting nothing more leave it. The host was inside. The bell rang until morning and then stopped, and no one came to open the doors, because by then no one remembered the hall was anything but a mound. It was bound and it was forgotten, in that order, and it despairs because the last thing its people did with it was close the door.

How this might surface: an ache at any bell; a seat laid by habit; a blue-grey stone that dreams a kneeling crowd in a hall; the way a room goes quiet when it stands.
