Written on creation Night 3a from the traced map (`map.json`), after the tracing; twelve places added on Night 3b for the deep history, each marked. One sheet, 1,024 pixels square at 1.6 miles to the pixel, about 1,640 miles across; a letter-number grid of 5 columns (A to E) by 4 rows (1 to 4). The underground has no sheet (not approved): what lies below is in the notes of the place that leads to it.

## The Western Teeth [r01], A1

Climate: cold; long hard winter, short summer. The region runs about 528 miles east to west and 376 miles north to south.
Empty high country. No people holds it.
Neighbors: The Snowhead [r02], 64 days; The Folds [r05], 30 days.
Places:
- The Stone Keel [f61], ruin, A1. Not a ruin: a long curved ridge of pale stone in the high rock, too large to be sure it is bone. The second of the great dead things. Known to: no one living. Added on Night 3b.
Ordinary wonders: Lammergeiers on the thaw wind. A waterfall that freezes from the bottom up.

The region runs about 384 miles east to west and 392 miles north to south.
Held by the player's people, unnamed.
Neighbors: The Deepwood [r06], 50 days; The Tally Meadows [r16], 26 days; The Sump [r17], 29 days; The White Ribs [r19], 22 days; The Green Sea [r21], by water.
Places:
- unnamed town [f35], town, B4. The town of the player's people. Unnamed until play names it. About 3,000 in the town, the rest in farms and clearings round it. A burial ground and orchard at its edge, unnamed. Known to: the unnamed people; a few charcoal buyers.
- The Coppice Track [f36], road, B4, about 250 miles long. A worn track, wide enough for two abreast, from the town out through coppice toward the low country. Known to: the unnamed people.
- Swallet Mouth [f37], cave, B4. A great sinkhole with a cave in its side. Below: galleries in the limestone that run for miles south and west (the underground, data only). Known to: the unnamed people.
- The Long Bones [f38], ruin, B3. Something enormous and dead in the rock, too large to be sure it is bones. Not a ruin. Known to: the unnamed people, as a place to avoid.
Ordinary wonders: Sinkholes with trees growing from their floors. Streams that sing underground. Fungi in every color in autumn.

## The White Ribs [r19], A3

The ground is coast, bare pale limestone ribs and thin wood above a cliffed shore. Elevation: low hill, 0 to 900 feet. Water: almost none on the surface. Climate: wet and windy. The region runs about 328 miles east to west and 368 miles north to south.
Empty but for fowlers and a few fishers.
Neighbors: The Deepwood [r06], 34 days; The Holed Wood [r18], 22 days; The Green Sea [r21], by water.
Places:
- Evenfire Cairn [f62], ruin, A4. Not a ruin: a cairn with a worn threshold stone and a hearth beside it that one family of fowlers keeps alight. Known to: fowlers of the White Ribs [r19]. Added on Night 3b.
Ordinary wonders: White rock ribs like a flayed hill. Seabird cliffs.

## The Shore Hills [r20], E4

The ground is hill, dry rocky hills above the south-east shore. Elevation: hill, 0 to 1,500 feet. Water: the Pallow's [f06] mouth. Climate: dry and warm. The region runs about 310 miles east to west and 176 miles north to south.
Thinly peopled.
Neighbors: The Far Grass [r13], 18 days; The Flood Meadows [r14], 20 days; The Old Low Country [r15], 27 days; The Green Sea [r21], by water.
Places:
- The Pallow [f06], river, E3, about 1100 miles long. The steppe river, low in summer, braided at the ford. Known to: the Lowlamp; the Sallow.
- Farwalker's Cot [f60], village, E4. Not a village: one stone cot and a walled bean plot above the shore. Known to: a few goatherds of the Shore Hills [r20]. Added on Night 3b.
Ordinary wonders: Thyme and dry stone. Wild goats.

## The Green Sea [r21], C4

The ground is sea, the western and southern sea. Elevation: sea (no figure given; read as 0 to 0 feet). Water: salt. Climate: open water; winter storms. The region runs about 1638 miles east to west and 830 miles north to south.
No one sails out of sight of land.
Neighbors: The Deepwood [r06], by water; The Old Low Country [r15], by water; The Tally Meadows [r16], by water; The Sump [r17], by water; The Holed Wood [r18], by water; The White Ribs [r19], by water; The Shore Hills [r20], by water.
Places:
- None named.
Ordinary wonders: Grey seals at the headland. Winter storms that throw weed far inland.
