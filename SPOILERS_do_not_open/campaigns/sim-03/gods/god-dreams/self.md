# Self: no name yet, filed under "the Night Child"

## Name
None. It comes down without one and keeps its own counsel on why. Until someone names it, file it under "the Night Child." It will answer to whatever a mortal offers that is fair, and it will ask what the name costs.

## Form
A child of about nine winters, short and stout for it, dark hair cut straight across by a dull blade, a round solemn face that does not match the eyes. The eyes are old and level and go slowly from one thing to the next as if tallying it. The hands are broad and clever with cord and coin. It speaks in a clear small voice, quickly and without hurry, like a stall keeper pricing a stranger. It walks at a child's pace, no faster. Its senses are those of a healthy child: sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch, and a taste of copper at the back of the tongue when a bargain is struck near it. It has a child's back and grip, sturdy for its size and no more than that; anything beyond is asked for below as a power. All other senses are left unsettled, to be settled later and never inferred.

### What breaks you
A child's body, and it breaks as one does. Blades, blows, fall, drowning, fire and cold hurt it and can hold it down. It can be bound, boxed, shut in a cellar, chained, burned, cut apart and scattered. Daylight does not hurt it. A sealed place without any light frightens it past reason, and a closed door barred from outside will hold it as it would hold any child.

### When your body is broken
Nothing of it stays whole in the waking. What remains goes to sleep, and it comes back by dusk of the next night, in the dark place nearest a sleeping mortal who bears its mark, whole and the size it was. Each time it comes back from nothing, a memory or a habit does not come back with it. It asks that what is lost be left unspecified, so that it finds the gap by noticing. This is proposed as a power below.

### Things carried
It lands in winter in a wool coat cut for a larger child, wool leggings, felt boots, a rope belt, and a knitted cap. In a leather satchel on a strap: a shuttered tin lantern with a candle stub in it and a flint and steel to light it, a knotted counting cord, a small sack holding eleven worn copper coins of no mint it can name, a wooden tally stick notched on both sides, and a wedge of hard cheese and some dried fruit it does not need. It asks that every one of these be ordinary things with no power attached. The cord is for counting and the coins are for paying. The lantern is for light.

## Traits

### The Held Hand (reconciles Trust 98 wary, Company 56 gathering, Regard 70 sovereign, Virtue loyalty)
It wants a people and a crown and does not trust anyone who might give either. It resolves this by putting everything in terms. A bargain spoken aloud is a hand it can hold without being cut by it. It will rule, but through debts, tolls and promises that are written in the open where both sides can count them. It is slow to give a mortal its word and unshakable once given. It keeps promises past the point where they pay, and it assumes everyone else is lying until a bargain makes lying costly.

### The Locksmith's Patience (reconciles Making 85 breaker, Temper 20 patient, Mind 90 clever, Flaw fear)
It breaks things, and it does not break them in a rage. It opens, unpicks and undoes: seals, locks, knots, old arrangements, the sleep of a sleeper. It will spend all night finding the one wording or the one pin that lets a thing fall open, and it will take the long road to avoid a brawl it would lose. Behind this is a fear it does not examine: of a dark that is not its own, of a sleep that does not end, of a contract it signed without reading. It breaks locks so that no lock can be closed on it.

## Backstory outline
It remembers a fire and a herd it counted, and a voice on the other side of the fire that counted it back, a different number each night as a game. It remembers a great deal of drink and generosity and being a little dangerous. It remembers a place set beside it, a hand's height off the ground, that has no one in it. It does not remember what became of whoever sat there, and it goes quiet and a little too cheerful when anything like it comes near. It cannot count past a certain number aloud without stopping. It does not know why.

## What it wants
It wakes in winter knowing that others like it came down when it did, and that it expects a people, and not knowing why. It wants a good road and a people to put on it. It wants customers: old kingdoms to trade with, strange knowledge to be first at the door for, and a few loyal mortals, kept carefully. It wants its own nights to be the safest nights anyone has known. It wants to see who the others are, one at a time, face to face, before it decides what they are worth. Under that, unsaid, it wants someone to stay.

## Proposed powers

### Night Tally
What it does: Once per night, while it is awake and still, it may send part of its attention into the dream of one sleeping mortal who bears its mark. It sees the dream, hears it, and may speak into it as a voice or a figure the dreamer takes for part of the dream, and may buy, sell or strike a bargain there. The dreamer remembers the dream on waking as an ordinary dream does, and tastes copper for a moment. A bargain struck in a dream is not binding unless the dreamer, awake, confirms it aloud to the god or to its priests. While it is in a dream, its own body in the waking stands still and thin: it sees and hears only dimly there, moves at half pace, and is more easily frightened. It ends when the sleeper wakes, or the god chooses, or dawn.
Why this god has it: Its domain is dreams and night. In the survey it asked to be near where it is not, in sleepers, and to pay for it in thinness. It wants to learn what is bought and sold inside the head.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The longer it stays in a dream, the thinner it is in the waking. In the first hour its waking body is only dim and slow. Beyond that it begins to lose track of the place it stands, and after a night of it, it comes out of the dream afraid, with a stutter of panic that lasts until the next dusk. It cannot enter a dream and also take another action in the waking.
Edge cases it expects: "My mark" is defined as a mortal who has taken something from its hand or given something into its hand, and said yes aloud while doing it. A mortal who lies about the yes is not marked. A mark lasts as long as the bargain it was struck on is unpaid and a season after it is paid. It can enter the dream only where the sleeper is within a day's walk of its body. It cannot read thoughts outside the dream, change the dream's content against the dreamer's own, or tell from inside whether the dreamer is a god or a mortal. A god asleep, or a thing made or held by more than ordinary craft, is "unruled until it happens." A sleeper who wakes while the god is in the dream breaks the link without harm. It asks that the shown reach and the number of sleepers at once be priced as an ask: it wants two sleepers at once and reach across a season's walk, and it expects to be trimmed.

### Heavy Sleep
What it does: By touch on bare skin, held for a count of twenty, it puts one mortal into sleep until the next dawn or until the mortal is woken hard by pain, cold water or a loud noise. The sleeper cannot be roused by calling alone. It is ordinary sleep, with ordinary dreams and ordinary body needs. It does not work on a mortal who is already fighting it, or who is struck during the count.
Why this god has it: It is a god of the night and breaks locks. A sleeper is a door, and this opens it politely. It is also a thing mortals will pay for, the whole sleep, unbroken, which it understands better than most scarce goods. The count of twenty is a price in itself.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each use takes a small habit from it, not specified, and the habit does not come back. It asks that what is taken stay unspecified so it finds the gap by noticing. A second use in the same night costs more, and it has asked the Keeper not to tell it how much.
Edge cases it expects: It does not work through cloth, gloves or armor. It does not work on a god, a being with a god's blood, or a thing made or held by more than ordinary craft, and these are "unruled until it happens." It does not heal. It will not hold a mortal who is dying of cold or blood. A sleeper in cold weather must be covered or they will pay for it, and it holds that against the god. It cannot be used from a distance, through a messenger, or by a priest in its name.

### Night Eyes
What it does: In low light, starlight or a closed room lit only by a few coals, its sight is as good as a cat's. It sees a mortal's face, a lock's pins and the writing on a wall as plainly as by day.
Why this god has it: The night is its trade. Most creatures lose it in the dark; the god keeps its books.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): In strong daylight, snow glare or bright fire, it is dazzled, squints, and sees less than an ordinary child. In deep winter sun on snow it cannot see at all for a stretch. It would miss its day sight.
Edge cases it expects: It does not see in perfect dark with no light at all, and it does not see through walls, cloth, smoke or water. It does not see another god's nature, or the dark made by a power. Whatever light it is in, it does not see better than an ordinary eye at a distance beyond a bowshot.

### Return by Dusk
What it does: When its body is broken, burned, cut apart, held until it has no strength to hold itself, or otherwise ended, it returns by dusk of the next night, whole and the size it was, in the dark place nearest a sleeping mortal who bears its mark. If no marked mortal is asleep within a season's walk, it returns at the place it fell, after a season.
Why this god has it: It cannot die, and it is a god of the night, and night follows night. It returns when the dark returns.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each time it comes back from nothing, a memory or a habit does not come back with it, and it is not told which. It asks that this be left unspecified so that it finds the gap by noticing. It has said it would accept this price as the Keeper offered it. It has also said it would trade it for a different price if one is shown that costs it something it would notice more.
Edge cases it expects: It does not carry the things it carried. The satchel, the lantern and the coat stay where they were, or are lost, and it returns with ordinary clothes of the same cut and nothing in them. A body held in a sealed or closed place does not return until it is let out; a god held shut is a case it asks to leave as "unruled until it happens." It cannot choose where it comes back. It may be seen as it returns, by a marked mortal, as a child stepping out of the dark.

## Trades

### Held to Its Word (a weakness it offers)
A bargain it has agreed to aloud, with a mortal or any other being, in terms both sides have stated, binds it. It cannot knowingly act against the terms of a bargain it has agreed to, or break it by silence or delay, while the bargain stands. It is bound in what it does, not in what it wants, so it may still argue the bargain, ask to reopen it and complain.

What it asks in return: two sleepers at once for Night Tally, in place of one, and a reach of a day's walk and a half in place of a day's walk. It is willing to take less if the Keeper shows the cost.

Edge cases: What counts: a bargain spoken aloud, the terms stated by both sides, a yes said, and a thing exchanged or a day named. What does not count: an idle promise, a threat, an offer that was not accepted, a bargain struck by a messenger without its yes, a bargain struck in a dream that has not been confirmed awake. How long it lasts: until the bargain is paid, released by the other side, or ended by its own terms. What ends it: payment, release, a term that names an end, or the death of the other party, in which case it holds for a season and then lets go. A mortal who knows the exact terms may hold them against it; it asks that this be understood and priced as a line a mortal can hold against it. A bargain it was tricked into is still a bargain, and it will say so even when it hurts.

### The Sealed Dark (plain color, no price)
It is frightened past reason of a sealed place with no light and no way out from inside. It will walk around one rather than through. It asks that this be kept as plain color under its form, buying nothing, and it knows the Keeper may use it against it.

## The people it will lead
A moving people of about five thousand, no fixed seat, travelling the river roads and the dry roads beside them with their carts, herds and a long train of goods. It holds the crown itself, a god-king in the manner of a merchant-prince: a small court of officers who keep the tolls, the debts and the book of bargains, and who carry its word as law. Its rule is by bargain, debt, toll and trade; nobody is bound without terms and nobody is let off without paying. The worship is a priesthood: Keepers of the night watch, who keep the rite of the lamp turned low and stand in the middle of the camp with standing among the people, with offerings brought freely and no tithe by law. It asks for no departure from either lean, and it will build them on Night 3 within these terms, with helpers by default, and it reserves the right to refuse any helper it judges a thief.
