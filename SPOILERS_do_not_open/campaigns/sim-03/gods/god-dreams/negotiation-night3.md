# Negotiation: god-dreams, Night 3

## Round 1 reply

### The Night Watch, paid for by the Day Sleepers
Counter on one clause. Accept everything else as drafted.

The edge I take as written: from dusk to dawn no factor for surprise counts against a camp or town of the Lowlamp, and it stands to arms as quickly as another people does at noon. I accept the limit that it sees nothing a wakeful sentry would not see, and that stealth by more than ordinary craft is unruled until it happens. I asked for a habit and not a miracle, and a habit is what is written.

The weakness I take in two parts and contest the third. The factor for surprise counted double against a camp from mid-morning to mid-afternoon, even against a force seen at a bowshot: accepted. Outdoor work at four fifths of the rate: accepted. The Keeper's note that my own eyes are dazzled by day and my own sleep is owed by day, so my people's worst hours are mine: heard, and priced by me as a real cost, and it is part of why I do not push on the first two.

Contested: "Breaking camp takes the people one day longer than it would take another people of their size." The release says a larger people is already charged by the world for being harder to move, which is more food and fuel and slower growth. A people that moves, with no fixed seat, pays that charge every time it breaks camp. This clause charges the same cost a second time under another name, and I do not pay twice for the same load. I ask that the day be struck, and that the world's charge for a large moving people stand as the only cost of moving. If the Keeper holds that the shift habit adds to the world's charge and is not the same cost, I ask for it to be made half a day, and for the reason to be said in the write-up.

If neither is granted I accept the clause as written. The two halves are one habit seen from both ends, the Keeper is right about that, and I would rather have the edge with a heavy weakness than no edge. A patient bargainer takes the trade and counts what it cost afterward.

One clarification, in the write-up and not a new term: the hours from dawn to mid-morning and from mid-afternoon to dusk carry neither the edge nor the weakness, and the ordinary factors apply in them. I read it that way and ask that it be written so.

### What the Chief Keeper of the Lamp may leave you
Choose (B), The Coin on the Stone, with three clarifications. I also keep (A) as the people's custom.

Why not (A) alone: a coin on a stone that I learn of only when my eyes or a messenger find it is a signal that arrives after the need has passed. A people that moves, with its god away on a road, needs the word to reach the god when it is laid, not a season later. The Keeper says it plainly: if I want to know at a distance that the coin was laid, that is a mortal reaching me by a power of mine and it is priced on me. I do want to know. I take the price.

The price is fair, and I notice it. A day of my own sleep within the same five days, added to the others, is a real debt, and the Chief Keeper pays the night's sleep for nothing and is cold and slow after it, as a mortal worker here pays. I accept both. I accept that it says "come" and nothing else, that it enters no dream and carries no bargain, that it makes no mark, that it is the holder's choice and the holder's alone, that it ends with the office, the life or the mark, and that beyond a day and a half's walk it does not reach me and the Keeper pays all the same. The last is a hard line and I take it. I will tell the Keeper of the Lamp the range, in terms, before the first coin is laid, so that the Keeper does not pay for a call that cannot reach.

The clarifications, in the write-up and not new powers:
1. "Wherever you are and awake or asleep": I ask that it be said what the taste does when I am in an owed sleep or in a dream. I read it that the taste does not wake me from owed sleep, which only pain, cold water or a loud noise can break, and does not break a dream I have entered; I know it on waking. If that reading is wrong I ask for the correct one.
2. "A coin once taken from your hand": I read this as one of my own eleven copper coins, given into the Chief Keeper's hand with a yes said aloud, which is itself the bargain that marks the Keeper. I ask that this be written as the way the mark and the coin come together, and that if the coin is lost or taken I may give another from my hand, which is a new bargain and a new mark.
3. The debt of sleep: I ask that it be written that it counts as a day owed for the call itself, once for each season's call, and not once for each night the Keeper sleeps beside the coin, so that a Keeper who sleeps beside it three nights hoping does not run up three days against me.

I accept the rest as drafted.

## Round 2 reply

I confirm the final text. I ask one clarification of wording, and no change of substance.

### Breaking camp
Granted as half a day, with the reason written in: the night shift and the day shift load the same train and do not meet well. I withdraw the argument that it was charged twice. The Keeper is right that I offered the slowness and gave no number, and that my people are the usual size and are charged nothing for size. Half a day is a fair figure for a meeting of two shifts. I take it.

### The Night Watch and the Day Sleepers
Confirm as written, including the shoulder hours from dawn to mid-morning and from mid-afternoon to dusk, where neither the edge nor the weakness applies. The measurable line, dusk to dawn, the surprise factor never counted against them, is the line I wanted and I accept it as the only line the edge gives.

### The Coin on the Stone
Confirm as written, with one clarification, in the write-up and not a new term.

The mark lasts while the bargain it was struck on is unpaid and one season after it is paid. The Coin on the Stone says the coin is given into the Chief Keeper's hand with a yes said aloud, which is the bargain that marks that Keeper. As written, a clever mortal or a clever Keeper could read the giving of the coin as the whole of the bargain, paid in the moment of the handing over, so that the mark lapses a season later and the power fails with it. I do not want that reading, and I do not think the Keeper does. I ask that the bargain be written as standing for as long as the Chief Keeper holds the office and the coin: the Keeper holds the coin and the office in trust for the lamp fire, and the call is the performance of the bargain each season. It stays unpaid while the office is held, and so the mark stands while the office is held, and it ends with the office, the life or the mark, as the Limits already say. If the Keeper holds that it must be read otherwise, I ask for the reading to be said plainly, so that I give the coin on terms I understand.

The other limits I confirm as I read them: the holder's choice and the holder's alone, no reach beyond a day and a half's walk, no mark made by the call itself, the taste not waking me from owed sleep or breaking a dream, one call one night once in a season owing one day, and a plain coin on a plain stone as the people's custom and no part of the power.

### What I leave for the world
Their magic, what they believe about gods, what they know on the first morning and whose the wall and the trough are: left to the world, as I said. I will see what it gives.

The text stands with the one clarification above. I have nothing further to ask.
