## Answer 1

A caravan camp at the hour after the lamps go low. Everyone has paid for the day, nobody has yet paid for the night, and the dark is the one good that is never sold out. Warm at the edges, cold at the middle, and every sleeper dreaming something that has not been priced. That is the world at its truest: a market that closes, and then opens again inside the head.

## Answer 2

River country, with a long dry road beside it. Rivers carry goods, and a people who move need water that keeps its word. I want steppe and reed marsh where the river braids, wide sky for the night, and ford towns that live on tolls. No fixed seat. A good road beats a good wall.

## Answer 3

Four seasons, honest ones, but let the nights be generous. Winter long, summer short and bright. A fair trade, since summer is for selling and winter is for dreaming. Autumn should be rich and quick, the harvest season, when every debt comes due.

Of the four, I want the one with the most night in it.

I wake into winter.

## Answer 4

Some of each, and I will pay for the mix. Wild country no one has walked is free stock, and free stock is the best kind. But I want a few old kingdoms rooted somewhere out there, because a kingdom is a customer, and a world with no customers is only scenery. Not too near. Near enough to reach by a season of walking.

## Answer 5

Old, with ruins under ruins. Many bargains were struck here before mine, and some were broken. I like to know that debts older than I am are still unpaid somewhere beneath the ground. Thousands of years. Enough that mortals have forgotten what they owe.

## Answer 6

Closer to the wise-woman in every valley than to the rumor, but not everywhere. Say a hedge-witch or a dream-reader in one village in three. Common enough that a mortal can buy a charm, rare enough that the charm is worth the buying. Never gone, never cheap.

## Answer 7

It should feel like borrowing. A pull behind the eyes, a taste of copper, a sense of being lent something by a creditor who is patient. The cost should be a real one and a fair one: the user pays in sleep, in a memory, in a season of luck, something they would notice missing. No free magic. I would not trust it, and neither should they.

## Answer 8

Taller than a great sorcerer, but not by a mile. Enough that a mortal looks twice, not enough that they never argue. A god who cannot be bargained with is a storm, and a storm cannot be loved or hurt. I want to be seen, a little, and I want to be haggled with. A one-eyed man among the blind, yes, but the blind have knives, and I would like them to keep them.

## Answer 9

Familiar beasts, mostly. Herds, wolves, river-horses, birds that follow the caravans. Then a few creatures with no name, living at the edges where the night is thickest, so that a traveler has a story to carry home. A handful of strange things, honestly priced. Too many and the strange becomes ordinary stock.

## Answer 10

Fear should be of the dark that is not mine. Night that takes and does not give back, a sleep that does not end, a road that bends so you walk it twice. And of debts: the kind a mortal signs for without knowing the terms. The best danger is the one that arrives politely and has a contract.

## Answer 11

Sleep that is whole and unbroken, and rest of the mind that comes with it. Mortals trade for it, fight for it, and some go without and go strange. Close behind it, certainty: knowing that the person beside you is who they say they are. That is the scarce thing I understand best, and I will not say why.

## Answer 12

Sealed doors. Old roads that run under newer ones. A cellar full of unclaimed goods, and somewhere a ledger in a language no one reads. I do not want bones of something enormous, since that is only a tomb and a tomb is a sad bargain. I want doors, and I want them locked from the inside.

## Answer 13

A few, and not many. One people who keep the night watches and do not sleep as we do. One people small and fast who trade across every border and trust no one, and I would like to meet them, since I would learn from them. They should be like mortals in their wants and unlike them in their terms. Not enemies by default. Not friends by default. Customers.

## Answer 14

Few. Say a handful, not a host. A stranger who comes with a tool or a tale no one here has, who wants something in exchange and will not say what until the deal is half made. Strange knowledge is the best stock there is, and I would like to be first at the door when it arrives.

## Answer 15

A knowing at face to face, and only then. A pull across distance is a leash, and I do not like to be found by those I have not met. Let me see them first, in the flesh, in the market of it, and decide. A little unease, a taste of something familiar, and the right to walk away.

## Answer 16

Premise bent: I would take one body, and a small second thing that is not a body. One form, a child, in one place. But let the dreams I tend carry some part of my attention into the sleeping, so that I am near where I am not. The cost should be real and plain. The more I spread into dreams, the thinner I am in the waking, and a thin god is a frightened one. I would pay that and know it.

## Answer 17

One who keeps a promise past reason. A mortal who is loyal to a bargain long after it has stopped paying. I would like to watch such a person, and I would like to be careful with them. Also one who is clever and poor, who makes a good bargain from nothing, because I would like to see it done.

## Answer 18

A companion. A thing that stays. Failing that, a truth about what the night is for. I did not expect to ask for the first, and I would take it back if it cost too much, but the question stands, and I have said it.

## Answer 19

Premise rejected: I do not make wishes I cannot price. But here is the nearest thing, said once and quietly. I wish that nothing in this world is lost for good that can be found again. Let every debt be payable and every loss be, in some form, returnable. I know the world will not grant it. I wished it anyway, and that is the whole of it.
