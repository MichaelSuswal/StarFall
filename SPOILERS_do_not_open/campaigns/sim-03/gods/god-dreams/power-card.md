# Power card: the Night Child

Campaign: sim-03. Written: creation, Night 2, 2026-11-20. The text below is the round 2 final text as you confirmed it, with the wording you asked in your confirmation. You have no name of your own yet. You are filed under "the Night Child".

## Form

One body, in one place, as for every god here (the campaign allows one body).

As you described yourself, in your own words:

A child of about nine winters, short and stout for it, dark hair cut straight across by a dull blade, a round solemn face that does not match the eyes. The eyes are old and level and go slowly from one thing to the next as if tallying it. The hands are broad and clever with cord and coin. It speaks in a clear small voice, quickly and without hurry, like a stall keeper pricing a stranger. It walks at a child's pace, no faster. Its senses are those of a healthy child: sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch, and a taste of copper at the back of the tongue when a bargain is struck near it. It has a child's back and grip, sturdy for its size and no more than that; anything beyond is asked for below as a power. All other senses are left unsettled, to be settled later and never inferred.

### What breaks you
A child's body, and it breaks as one does. Blades, blows, fall, drowning, fire and cold hurt it and can hold it down. It can be bound, boxed, shut in a cellar, chained, burned, cut apart and scattered. Daylight does not hurt it. A sealed place without any light frightens it past reason, and a closed door barred from outside will hold it as it would hold any child.

### When your body is broken
See Return by Dusk below. It carries a cost and a trigger, so it is written as a power, and the power is the rule.

### Things carried (as you gave them; all ordinary unless a power below says otherwise)
It lands in winter in a wool coat cut for a larger child, wool leggings, felt boots, a rope belt, and a knitted cap. In a leather satchel on a strap: a shuttered tin lantern with a candle stub in it and a flint and steel to light it, a knotted counting cord, a small sack holding eleven worn copper coins of no mint it can name, a wooden tally stick notched on both sides, and a wedge of hard cheese and some dried fruit it does not need. It asks that every one of these be ordinary things with no power attached. The cord is for counting and the coins are for paying. The lantern is for light.

### Settled at the table

- God-sense, as set for this campaign: you know another god only face to face, close, as a weight or an unease that grows surer with time and can be wrong. It tells nothing of the other's name, nature or powers. You know that others like you came down when you did.
- Pace: the pace of your form, as you described it; the pace table's rates apply (`engine/rules/time-and-pace.md`).
- Senses you did not describe are unsettled and are settled in play, never inferred.
- The "Intent, your first wording" lines are kept for the record only. Where one differs from a Description or a Drawback, the Description and the Drawback stand.
- When the body is broken: see the return power below. It carries a cost and a trigger, so it is written as a power.

### Definitions

- Your mark. A mortal bears your mark who has taken something from your hand or given something into your hand and said yes aloud while doing it, knowing whose hand it was. A yes that was a lie makes no mark. The mark lasts while the bargain it was struck on is unpaid, and one season after it is paid. Only your own hand makes it: no priest, officer or messenger can mark for you.

## Powers

### Night Tally
Status: main
Description: Once in a night, awake and still, you send part of your attention into the dream of one sleeping mortal who bears your mark and lies within a day and a half's walk of your body, or into the dreams of two such sleepers in the one night. You see and hear the dream, and may speak into it as a voice or a figure the dreamer takes for part of the dream. You may offer, buy and sell there. The dreamer remembers it as an ordinary dream is remembered and tastes copper on waking. It ends when the sleeper wakes, when you choose, or at dawn.
Drawback: Sleep is owed for sleep. For each sleeper's dream you enter you owe a full day of your own sleep, taken within five days: real sleep, from which calling will not wake you, only pain, cold water or a loud noise. When the fifth day comes it cannot be put off, and you take it where you stand if you have chosen no place. Debts add.
Limits settled at creation:
- While you are in a dream your own body stands still and thin: it sees and hears dimly, moves at half pace, and can do nothing else. This is a condition, not the price. A bargain struck in a dream binds no one until the dreamer, awake, confirms it aloud to you in person: the yes said awake, in your presence, whoever else stands by as witness. You cannot read a thought outside the dream, change what the dream holds against the dreamer's own, or tell from inside whether the sleeper is a god or a mortal. A sleeper who wakes breaks it without harm. It rules no mind. A god asleep, or a sleeper warded by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Use across a year: two dreams a night at most, each owing its day.
- Intent, your first wording: "Once per night, while it is awake and still, it may send part of its attention into the dream of one sleeping mortal who bears its mark. It sees the dream, hears it, and may speak into it as a voice or a figure the dreamer takes for part of the dream, and may buy, sell or strike a bargain there. The dreamer remembers the dream on waking as an ordinary dream does, and tastes copper for a moment. A bargain struck in a dream is not binding unless the dreamer, awake, confirms it aloud to the god or to its priests. While it is in a dream, its own body in the waking stands still and thin: it sees and hears only dimly there, moves at half pace, and is more easily frightened. It ends when the sleeper wakes, or the god chooses, or dawn."
- "Once in a night" means in one night, one dream or two, each owing its own day.
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: The reach of a day and a half in place of a day was bought with the weakness Held to Its Word. The second sleeper in one night was bought with the weakness Hear Out. Both are under Weaknesses taken.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### Heavy Sleep
Status: main
Description: By touch on bare skin, held for a count of twenty, you put one mortal into sleep until the next dawn, or until they are woken hard by pain, cold water or a loud noise. Calling alone will not rouse them. It is ordinary sleep.
Drawback: Each use takes one small habit from you, and it does not come back, and you are not told which. A second use in the same night takes two.
Limits settled at creation:
- It does not work through cloth, gloves or armor, on one who is fighting you, or on one who is struck during the count. It heals nothing and holds no one who is dying of cold or blood. A sleeper left uncovered in the cold pays for it as any sleeper would. Only your own hand: not at a distance, not through a messenger, not by a priest. It rules no mind beyond the sleep. On a god, on one with more than mortal blood, or on one warded by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Use across a year: as often as you will pay.
- Intent, your first wording: "By touch on bare skin, held for a count of twenty, it puts one mortal into sleep until the next dawn or until the mortal is woken hard by pain, cold water or a loud noise. The sleeper cannot be roused by calling alone. It is ordinary sleep, with ordinary dreams and ordinary body needs. It does not work on a mortal who is already fighting it, or who is struck during the count."
- Two uses in one night cost three habits in all. A third use in the same night takes three more, a fourth takes four, and so on.
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### Night Eyes
Status: main
Description: In low light (starlight, a room lit by a few coals) you see as a cat sees: a face, the pins of a lock, the marks on a wall, as plainly as by day.
Drawback: In strong daylight, snow glare or bright fire you are dazzled and see less than an ordinary child, and in full winter sun on snow you cannot see at all while it lasts.
Limits settled at creation:
- Not in perfect dark. Not through walls, cloth, smoke or water. No better than an ordinary eye past a bowshot. It shows nothing of another god's nature. A dark made by a power or by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens.
- Intent, your first wording: "In low light, starlight or a closed room lit only by a few coals, its sight is as good as a cat's. It sees a mortal's face, a lock's pins and the writing on a wall as plainly as by day."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### Return by Dusk
Status: main
Description: When your body is broken, burned, cut apart or otherwise ended, what remains sleeps. After a full season you return at dusk, whole and the size you were, in the dark place nearest to where the largest part of you lay.
Drawback: Each time you come back, a memory or a habit does not come back with you, and you are not told which.
Limits settled at creation:
- The return follows the table's rule above. You return in ordinary clothes of the same cut with nothing in them; what you carried stays where it was. You cannot choose the place. Remains shut in a sealed place do not return until it is opened. Held and not broken, you are held. Anyone nearby may see a child step out of the dark.
- Ground of your own kind is any place that is dark at night with no light burning (under a roof, in a hollow, a cellar, a thicket). No sleeper and no mark is needed.
- Intent, your first wording: "When its body is broken, burned, cut apart, held until it has no strength to hold itself, or otherwise ended, it returns by dusk of the next night, whole and the size it was, in the dark place nearest a sleeping mortal who bears its mark. If no marked mortal is asleep within a season's walk, it returns at the place it fell, after a season."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Coin on the Stone
Status: added Night 3 (2026-11-20). A people's edge that is a power of the god's, priced on the god.
Description: Once a season, the Chief Keeper of the Lamp, who bears your mark, may lay a coin once taken from your hand on a flat stone at the lamp fire and sleep beside it. If your body is within a day and a half's walk, you taste copper, wherever you are and awake or asleep, and know it for that office's call. It says "come" and nothing else: no word, no image, no place but the lamp fire.
Drawback: You owe a day of your own sleep for it, as for a dream entered, within the same five days, and the debt adds to the others. The Keeper pays as mortal workers here pay: the night's sleep is spent with nothing got from it, and the Keeper is cold and slow through the day after.
Limits settled at creation:
- Always the holder's choice; that holder's alone, priced again for another; it ends with the office, the life or the mark.
- Beyond a day and a half's walk nothing reaches you and the Keeper pays all the same.
- It makes no mark, enters no dream and carries no bargain. Other gods, and more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens.
- The taste does not wake you from owed sleep and does not break a dream you have entered. You know it on waking, or when the dream ends.
- The coin is one given from your own hand into the Chief Keeper's with a yes said aloud, which is the bargain that marks that Keeper. If it is lost or taken you may give another from your hand: a new bargain and a new mark.
- One call is one night, once in a season, and owes one day. A Keeper who sleeps beside the coin on other nights of that season calls nothing, pays nothing, and puts no day against you.
- A coin laid on a stone as a plain sign, seen by whoever sees it, is the people's custom and no part of this power.
- The bargain struck with the coin is the keeping of the lamp fire's call. It stands unpaid for as long as that Chief Keeper holds the office and the coin, so the mark on that Keeper stands for as long, and it ends as the lines above say. It marks no one else and lengthens no other mark.
- Settled at creation. Ledger: L-0035.
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

## Weaknesses taken

**Held to Its Word.** A bargain you have agreed to aloud, with a mortal or any other being, in terms both sides have stated, with a yes said and a thing exchanged or a day named, binds you. You cannot knowingly act against its terms, or break it by silence or delay, while it stands. You are bound in what you do, not in what you want: you may argue it, ask to reopen it, and complain. An idle promise, a threat, an offer not accepted, a bargain struck by a messenger without your yes, and a dream bargain not confirmed awake do not count. It lasts until paid, released by the other side, ended by its own terms, or a season after the other party's death. A bargain you were tricked into is still a bargain. How it can be used against you: anyone who knows the exact terms may hold them against you.
- What it bought: Night Tally reaches a day and a half's walk, in place of a day's.

**Hear Out.** A mortal who speaks to your face, awake, and puts a bargain to you in terms, a thing for a thing with a day named, holds you to hear the whole of it before you answer. You cannot refuse to hear it, walk away from it, sleep through it or answer in the middle. You may say no at the end. It lasts for the length of the offer, no longer than one candle mark by a tallow candle of ordinary size, and ends when you say yes or no, when the speaker stops, or when the speaker is struck, bound or made to flee. It holds your attention and your feet, not your hands: others can move you, and you can defend yourself. When one offer ends another mortal may begin; the same mortal cannot hold you twice between one dusk and the next. An offer by messenger, in a dream or in writing does not count. How it can be used against you: mortals who know it can hold you in place, one after another, while a guard comes, a road closes or the dark falls.
- What it bought: A second sleeper in one night for Night Tally.

Kept as color: You are frightened past reason of a sealed place with no light and no way out from inside. Color, at no price, buying nothing.
