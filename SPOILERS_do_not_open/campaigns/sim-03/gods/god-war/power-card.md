# Power card: the Host

Campaign: sim-03. Written: creation, Night 2, 2026-11-20. The text below is the round 2 final text as you confirmed it, with the wording you asked in your confirmation. You have no name of your own yet. You are filed under "the Host".

## Form

One body, in one place, as for every god here (the campaign allows one body).

As you described yourself, in your own words:

A human to look at, and a little too well made to be one. A tall man, a hand's width over six feet, spare and even, as if drawn by someone who had only ever seen the best of a thing. The face is balanced to the last line: the two sides match, the brow is level, the mouth is neither stern nor smiling and rests at the exact middle between them. Skin without a mark, scar, freckle or crease, warm to the touch. Hair the color of dry oak leaves, cut straight at the jaw. Eyes dark brown and very steady. It blinks a little less often than a mortal does, and that is the thing people notice last and remember longest.

It sees, hears, smells, tastes and touches as a man does, no better and no worse, and has no sight in the dark beyond a man's. It feels another of its kind only face to face, as the world allows. Any sense not written here is unsettled.

It moves at the pace of a walking man and never seems to hurry; its stride is the same length each time, which makes it easy to follow and a little hard to look at. It does not tire. It sits when a table is offered. It stands when a room does.

### What breaks you
Bronze, flint, wood and iron wound it as they wound a man. It bleeds, red and warm, and a cut closes in about a day. Fire burns it. Cold chills it. Water can hold it under and it will not drown, but it cannot move itself out if something heavy lies across it. A binding of braided cord or chain, knotted by a mortal hand around its wrists and ankles, holds it as it would hold a strong man: cord it can strain through at the cost of a night's work, chain it cannot break and must wait for someone to open. Being cut apart, burned to ash, or crushed so it cannot move is what breaks it. Being held or shut in a room does not.

### When your body is broken
See The Set Place below. It carries a cost and a trigger, so it is written as a power, and the power is the rule.

### Things carried (as you gave them; all ordinary unless a power below says otherwise)
Ordinary things only, with no power attached. A long coat of undyed wool, belted, the color of old bread. Plain linen under it, wool trousers, leather boots well worn on the heel. A cloth bundle holding one dark loaf, a twist of salt, a clay cup and three tallow candles with a flint and a bit of steel to strike it. A bronze knife with a bone handle, kept for cutting bread.

### Settled at the table

- God-sense, as set for this campaign: you know another god only face to face, close, as a weight or an unease that grows surer with time and can be wrong. It tells nothing of the other's name, nature or powers. You know that others like you came down when you did.
- Pace: the pace of your form, as you described it; the pace table's rates apply (`engine/rules/time-and-pace.md`).
- Senses you did not describe are unsettled and are settled in play, never inferred.
- The "Intent, your first wording" lines are kept for the record only. Where one differs from a Description or a Drawback, the Description and the Drawback stand.
- When the body is broken: see the return power below. It carries a cost and a trigger, so it is written as a power.
- Your own strength: your body has about the strength and wind of three strong men, and a cut on you closes in about a day. A cut is a wound a blade or flint makes in skin and flesh, and nothing deeper. This is the stock that Loaned Strength lends from, and it is counted in that power's price. Burns, broken bones and deep wounds mend at a mortal's pace.

### Definitions

None beyond the terms each power defines for itself.

## Powers

### Loaned Strength
Status: main
Description: A mortal who takes your hand and says yes, freely and aloud, knowing what is lent, is lent a share of your strength: about the strength of two men, hurt that closes in a day, and a steadier nerve. It lasts until you end it, the borrower asks to end it, the borrower passes out of your sight, or a night and a day have passed, whichever is first. Up to three borrowers stand at once.
Drawback: For each borrower, a third of your own strength and quickness is gone while the loan stands, and your own cuts close no faster than a mortal's. With three borrowers you are as weak as a mortal man.
Limits settled at creation:
- Only a willing mortal who understands. Not beasts. The borrower cannot lend it on, and it does not stack on one borrower. A borrower behind a wall is out of your sight and loses it. A borrower's death returns the share at once. It makes the borrower stronger and steadier, not wiser, and compels nothing: it rules no mind. Light and dark: you must be able to see the borrower. On another god, or on one armed or armored by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Use across a year: three at a time, as often as you choose to be weak.
- Intent, your first wording: "A mortal who takes the god's hand and says yes, freely and aloud, is lent a share of the god's strength and endurance. For the borrower that is about the strength of two men, hurt that closes in a day, and a steadier nerve. It lasts until the god chooses to end it, the borrower asks to end it, the borrower passes out of the god's sight, or one night and a day have passed, whichever is first. I ask for up to three borrowers standing at once, expecting to be trimmed."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Counted Ground
Status: main
Description: With your heel or the butt of a staff you score a ring in the earth up to a hundred paces across and walk the whole of it to close it. Within the ring, while you stand inside it or on its line, no mortal can bring a weapon or a fist down in anger to wound or kill another mortal: the hand slows, the grip loosens, the weapon is set down. It lasts until sunrise, or until you say aloud that it is ended, or until the line is scuffed through anywhere.
Drawback: While the ring stands you cannot step across its line and cannot raise a hand in anger yourself. You are held as the mortals are, and you are not shielded from what comes from outside.
Limits settled at creation:
- The ring is scored and walked in plain sight; it cannot be made in secret or at a run. It stays the hand and changes nothing in any mind: anger, hatred and intent stand as they were, and anyone may walk out of the ring or scuff the line. It does not stop words, shoving without intent to wound, accidents, beasts, or an arrow or spear loosed from outside. A blow already falling when the line closes lands. Earth only: it cannot be scored on bare rock, paving, a floor, ice or water. On a god, or on a weapon made or held by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Use across a year: one ring at a time.
- Intent, your first wording: "With the heel of its boot or the butt of a staff, the god scores a ring in the earth up to a hundred paces across, and walks the whole of it to close it. Within the ring, while the god stands inside or upon the line, no mortal can bring a weapon or fist down in anger to wound or kill another mortal. The hand slows, the grip loosens, and the weapon is set down gently, as if the arm remembered a better habit. It lasts until sunrise, or until the god says aloud that it is ended, or until the line is scuffed through anywhere."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Bell Nobody Rings
Status: main
Description: When you strike your own breastbone three times with the open palm, a deep bell tone, the same each time, sounds at every shrine within a day's walk (about twenty miles) that is kept in your name. A shrine is kept in your name when a light is lit before it at least once each night by whoever keeps it. It carries no words. Everyone near a shrine hears it.
Drawback: For a night and the day after, you hear no voice, only the bell. Speech reaches you as weather at a distance, and you cannot be asked a question.
Limits settled at creation:
- It cannot be aimed at one shrine, hushed once struck, or made to carry a word or a second tone. Friend and foe hear it alike. A shrine dark for a full season stops sounding. You need not see the shrines. Whether another god hears it as more than a bell: unruled until it happens. Use across a year: as often as you will go deaf to voices.
- Intent, your first wording: "When the god strikes its own breastbone three times with the open palm, a deep bell tone, the same one each time, sounds at every shrine within a morning's walk (about eight miles) that is kept in its name. A shrine is kept in its name when a light is lit before it at least once each night by whoever keeps it. It carries no words. It sounds at the shrine, where all who are near can hear it. It is a muster and a warning, known by pitch to its people."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: The day's walk in place of a morning's was bought with the weakness The Refused Door, under Weaknesses taken.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Set Place
Status: main
Description: When your body is broken (cut apart, burned to ash, or crushed so that it cannot move), it falls still and pale, keeps its shape for one night, and is gone. After a full season you stand, at dusk, at the place nearest to where you fell where a seat has been laid for a guest and left empty.
Drawback: Each time you return, a memory or a habit does not come back with you, and you are not told which.
Limits settled at creation:
- The return follows the table's rule above; if the nearest laid seat is more than a day off, the walk is added. If no seat is laid anywhere you could walk to in a season, you return where you fell. You do not return into a place a mortal has barred against you; you come to the next nearest. Being held, bound or shut in is not being broken. You return in what you wore, with nothing you carried.
- Ground of your own kind is a seat laid for a guest and left empty.
- Intent, your first wording: "When the god's body is broken (cut apart, burned to ash, or crushed so that it cannot move), it falls still and pale, keeps its shape for one night, and is gone. At the next dusk it stands at the nearest place within thirty miles where a seat has been laid for a guest and left empty. If there is no such place, it returns where it fell, after a full season has passed."
- "Where you fell" is where the largest part of you lies, as in the table's rule.
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

## Weaknesses taken

**The Refused Door.** Any mortal to whom you have given a gift (bread, a place at your table, a loan of your strength) may later refuse you their door by saying aloud, awake, in your hearing or the hearing of two witnesses, "I refuse you my door." From then on you cannot cross the threshold of that mortal's own dwelling: a house, a hut or a hearth room with a door, refused by the one who dwells there, knowing whom they refuse and sound of mind. Not a tent, a temple, a public hall, a field, or a rented house. It lasts until that mortal dies or opens the door to you by their own words. You cannot argue, trick or break it, and no other mortal can lift it. Whether it holds against another god is unruled until it happens. How it can be used against you: anyone you have fed can shut you out, and can be seen doing it.
- What it bought: The Bell Nobody Rings reaches a day's walk (about twenty miles), in place of a morning's.
