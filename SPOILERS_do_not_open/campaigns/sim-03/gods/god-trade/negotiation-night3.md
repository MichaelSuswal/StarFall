## Round 1 reply

### The split tally
Accept, and I choose (B), earned at a stroke.

Reasons: I said I would take a heavier weakness before a fence on reach, and the Keeper has held me to it. A people that must earn its credit by a generation of dealing is a people that spreads at a mortal's pace, and I came to lead a people that goes out along the roads. One met stick honored in the next is how a stranger becomes a customer, and a customer is what I came down for. I take the heavier chain with open eyes: a half-stick good in any hand means a buyer of old halves can hold my families, and I know it. I would rather have that than a stick no stranger will take.

I accept the Keeper's reading that what a stranger does is the stranger's, and that the edge rules no mind. What my people pay and what buys where they are known is all I asked.

Two clarifications, wording only, not a new edge. First, a half-stick called by a stranger is called at my Reckoner's bench, and the bench hears the case and sets the terms under my law and may refuse a half shown to be false, as the draft says of a false stick. Second, a half-stick forgiven in the open by its holder, before witnesses, ends that debt for the family and for its heirs. I ask that both be written plainly, so that the chain is a chain and not a trap that cannot be opened.

### The tally is a chain
Accept as written, with the number: one household in five carries such a debt at the start, and those households do not flee, migrate or settle new land, and a household that goes anyway is put out of the tally and its sticks are worth nothing anywhere. It is a real price, and it falls on exactly the thing I came down to do, which is to spread. I do not counter it. If it is heavier for (B), I take it, and I note that I offered the weakness myself and do not ask it be lightened.
