## Answer 1
A market at dusk, the hour the stalls are being counted. Lamps lit, scales set down, every pot and bolt of cloth tallied and the tally agreed by both hands. Nothing is free and nothing is stolen, and a stranger can walk in with an empty purse and a good word and leave with something. That is a true world to me. A world where an offer made is an offer kept.

## Answer 2
River country first, and the coast where the rivers end. Water carries goods, and goods carry people to each other. Give me fords, and a harbor, and long roads between them. I do not want a seat in one valley. I want a country that can be walked, and walked again, with something worth selling at each stop.

## Answer 3
Four seasons, uneven in length, so that a careful person must count them. A long dry summer for the roads, a short sharp autumn for the harvest and the great fairs, a hard winter that closes the passes and makes every household reckon what it has, and a thaw that opens the ways with a rush. Let the winter be honest and never cruel for its own sake. When each new year finds me, I want to wake into autumn, when the fairs are open and the granaries are full and everything is for sale.
I wake into autumn.

## Answer 4
Some of each, and I would be a poor merchant to ask for only one. Old kingdoms already rooted are customers, and they have coin and wants and rules I can learn. Wild country no one has walked is where the next customers are. I hope to find the first near enough to trade with and the second near enough to reach.

## Answer 5
Thousands of years, with ruins under ruins. A world that has already traded, quarreled and forgotten has ledgers worth finding. People who arrived yesterday have no debts yet, and debts are how a people holds together. But let the oldest layers be thin and broken, so that no one is sure who owed whom.

## Answer 6
A wise-woman in some valleys and a rumor in others. Never gone entirely, never everywhere. If magic is everywhere, nothing is scarce and nothing can be priced. If it is gone, there is nothing strange to carry over the mountains. Uneven is best. Uneven makes a market.

## Answer 7
It should feel like borrowing. A mortal reaches for a thing and it comes, and then the loan is called. Let the cost be a true one: a year of warmth, a name, a memory, a day of sight, something the mortal would notice missing. No free gifts, no curses either. Just a ledger that always balances, and the mortal who forgets that pays double.

## Answer 8
Let a god stand out, but not unbearably. Somewhat more than a great sorcerer, less than a thunderstorm. I would like to be noticed and wanted, I will admit, but a god no mortal can bargain with is a god no mortal can love. Let mortals be able to look at us and say a price.

## Answer 9
Familiar animals along the roads, so that a caravan can be planned. Strange things in the wild past them, in the high passes and the deep marsh and the far water. A few creatures no one has a name for, so that naming them is itself work worth paying for. Keep the strangeness at the edges, where a trader goes with a guard and a lamp.

## Answer 10
Debt called in the wrong hour. Famine that follows a broken promise. Roads that close behind you. And a stranger who is not what the contract says. I find the oath-breaker, the one who takes the goods and forgets the word, a better fear than any monster, because it can sit at your own table.

## Answer 11
Salt, if it must be one thing. Or something like it, plain and necessary and found in few places, so that whoever holds the road to it holds a great deal. Let there also be something finer and rarer that no one needs and everyone wants. Scarcity is what puts a price on everything else.

## Answer 12
Old roads, and sealed doors with a ledger on the other side. Warehouses of an older people, still locked, still counted, with an account no living hand can settle. I want a door that opens only when something is paid. And one thing I would rather not find, but I suspect it is there: a broken seal that was once an oath.

## Answer 13
Yes, a few. Let some of them keep accounts differently than we do, count in ways a human cannot, hold a debt across generations without forgetting it. Let one be a good neighbor and a hard bargainer, and one be a bad neighbor who cannot be bargained with at all. I want peoples I must learn to speak to, not just to command.

## Answer 14
Yes, few, and each one dear. A traveler from far off brings what the market has never had: a tool, a measure, a method of counting, a crop. One such person arriving in a year is a feast. Many in a year is a flood that spoils the price of everything. Let them come rarely and cost something to understand.

## Answer 15
Face to face, and only then. A pull at any distance would make every meeting a surprise ruined, and every stranger known before the greeting. I would rather walk into a tent and feel it, the weight of another like me, and have to decide in that moment what I am willing to offer. A knowing at arm's length, and unmistakable.

## Answer 16
Premise bent: One body, and one only, but let a god lend a small part of itself to a messenger, a mark, a seal, something that carries the god's word and presence for a short while at a real cost in strength. Being everywhere at once is not a trade. A god who can be in two places has never had to choose, and a god who never chooses cannot be trusted with a bargain.

## Answer 17
A mortal who drives a hard bargain and keeps it. A caravan master, a factor, a quartermaster, a ferry-widow who knows every debt on the river. Someone who rises through reputation alone, with no magic at all, and whose word becomes better than coin. I would follow that story from the first sale to the last.

## Answer 18
A companion, or something that behaves like one, that does not want worship and does not want anything I can price. I would like to find out what that is. A place where a god's word means nothing, a door that does not care who I am, would do too. I cannot yet imagine what it would be worth.

## Answer 19
That an oath, once broken, can be mended. That there is some way to make a broken word whole again, and the world with it, and that the one who broke it is allowed to be the one who pays. I will not say more than that, and I do not know why I said that much.
