## Round 1 reply

### Your count (a sense, under Form)
Accept. A quick eye is all I meant by it, and the Keeper is right that a man behind a curtain was not in my sentence on purpose. I keep it as color at no price. I do not propose a count of the hidden.

### The Lent Seal
Accept as written. The hollowing is a price I would feel at once, and the Keeper has read the cost correctly: a word that binds nothing while the seal is out is a cold thing for a woman whose word is her coin. If I take two seals standing at once under the weakness below, I ask only that this be written plainly: the hollowing is one state and is not doubled by the second seal, and a seal broken in a bearer's hands costs its own day each time, so that two breaks cost two days. I would rather carry that than a written fence.

### The Counted Word
Accept. I give up the doubling of a forgotten debt as part of the power. The Keeper is right that it is law, and law I can make in my own court and have my captains carry. The bargain, the direction, the distance and the cost of my own broken term stand as written.

### The Washing
Counter. I accept what the Keeper refused: no single rite over scores. I accept that every washing is by my hand, with water and a cloth, in a patient quarter of an hour, and that the fence is left off as I asked. I accept the wounds coming home as weakness and not only ache.

What I counter is the one at a time. I have two hands, and the washing is done by hand. I ask that I may wash two at once, one in each hand, in a half hour, and no more than two. That is slow enough that a war band is still an evening's work, and it is not a rite over many. Two makes a captain and the one who stands at the captain's shoulder, which is how a war band is led.

For this I offer a heavier drawback in place of any limit: the wounds I carry do not heal at the washed mortal's pace but at twice the number of days, and I cannot take up a seal while I carry a washed mortal's wound. I would rather pay in the body than be fenced. If the Keeper will not take two, I accept one at a time as written and I do not withdraw the drawback as offered.

### The Count Returns
Choose A: a full season, one item lost each time. My reasons: what I would lose is the thing I have least of. I come down knowing almost nothing, and a record of two or three items is most of what I am. Two items lost for a month sells my goals cheaper than a season of my absence. A season costs my people a season without me, and they come with helpers who run things in my absence. I am patient, and I would rather wait than forget.

One clarification, in the write-up, not a new power: I do not know what "the nearest ground of your own kind" means, and I am not told. Say in the card what it is, so that I know how far I must walk. If it is not yet known to me, say that my return is unruled as to where until it happens.

### Open account (the weakness)
Accept as the Keeper wrote it: the wound does not begin to close until the gift is accepted, and then closes at a mortal's pace, not at once. It is a price I do not already pay. I give so as to be owed, and the Keeper has seen that, so that the weakness bites exactly where I am weakest. A wound I cannot close until I give without being owed is a wound I will carry a long time. I take the price.

What it buys: two seals standing at once in place of one. Reasons: a seal is how I reach beyond my one body, and a trader with one message out is a trader with one road open. Two lets me send one to a captain and one to a market, and be known in both. I refuse the month of lending. A seal that stands a month hollows me for a month, and I would not take it. I note the Keeper refused a season at this price, and I do not ask again. The hands stay as color, as I asked.
