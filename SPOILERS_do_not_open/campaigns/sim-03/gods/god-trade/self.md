# Self: the Factor (unnamed)

## Name
None. I come down without one. File me under "the Factor" until someone names me, and let the naming be a price someone pays, not a gift I take.

## Form
A tall woman, human to the eye, in the middle of her years. Broad through the shoulder and long in the thigh, built to walk a road all day and all night and start again. Her face is weathered and plain, a wide mouth that is quick to name a price, grey eyes that look at a person the way a buyer looks at a measure of grain. Her hair is dark and bound back with a cord.

She is visibly wrong in one way: her fingers have an extra joint each, a third knuckle where there should be two. They bend in ways a hand should not, and she counts on them without thinking, folding and unfolding in a rhythm she does not choose. She hides them in her sleeves when she wants a stranger easy, and shows them when she wants a stranger to know what is in front of him.

Senses: the senses of a strong human, no better and no worse, except that she is always aware of how many of a thing are in a room, and the count is never wrong by more than one. (Settled as plain color: she counts. It tells her nothing about worth, intent or what is hidden. If the Keeper reads it as a power, I ask that it be priced as one and I will argue it.) Any sense I have not described is left unsettled and settled later.

She moves at the pace of her form: a long, even walk, a ready stride, no faster than a strong woman in good boots. She does not tire and does not need to rest. She sleeps by choice, usually lightly, sitting up.

### What breaks you
Fire held to her. Being cut apart, the body parted at the joints and the pieces scattered. Being bound in iron or bronze chain so that she cannot move her hands. Being shut in a closed place with no door she can reach. Being drowned and held under, since she does not need breath but the weight pins her. Being made to stand somewhere she has been named a debtor and cannot leave. Her hands matter most: a mortal who has bound or broken her hands has taken most of what she is.

Wounds are real and they ache. A cut bleeds, though she does not bleed out.

### When your body is broken
The pieces do not rot. They lie as they fell, and there is a count of days in which nothing of her is awake. Then the largest piece draws the rest to it and she stands up where that piece lay. Proposed below as a power, "The Count Returns," because it has a trigger and a cost.

### Things carried
Boots, trousers and a long belted coat of undyed wool, all ordinary. A leather belt pouch holding a handful of plain tally sticks, notched, and a bit of chalk. A small balance of bronze with two shallow pans, folded into a wooden case, ordinary, no power in it. A flat stamp of fired clay on a cord at her neck, about the size of a thumb, cut with a simple mark of her own, which is ordinary until she lends it (see "The Lent Seal"). No weapon. I ask that all of these be ordinary things with no power attached, except the stamp as described under its power.

## Traits

### Gives so that she is owed
She gives freely and she means it, and the gift is also the first coin of a bargain she has not named yet. She does not see the two as a contradiction. She is generous because it is the only way she knows to be wanted, and she wants to be wanted more than she wants almost anything. When the gift is received with thanks she is warmed; when it is received with a price in return she is warmer; when it is received and forgotten she is cold in a way she does not examine.
Reconciles: Virtue (generosity), Flaw (lust for being worshipped), Appetite (hungry, 68), Regard for mortals (sovereign, 61), Presence (toward revealed, 36).

### A free hand and an iron word
She hates a fence set on her from outside and will take a heavier cost sooner than a written limit. But a word she has given herself she keeps past reason, the way a person keeps a hand shut over a coal. She is patient where it concerns a promise, impulsive where it concerns a door that has been closed on her, and wary of anyone who offers something for nothing. She breaks things easily, and she mends them slowly, and she is afraid of the day she cannot.
Reconciles: Order (freedom, 92), Trust (wary, 70), Temper (patient, 34), Making (toward breaker, 66), and the wound.

## Backstory outline
She does not know who she was. She has the shape of it without the contents. A tent. A table with a cloth on it. A hand struck on a hand, and a feeling of terms that were fair on both sides. Then a different quiet, the wrong kind, as if a very large scale had been set down and tipped. She remembers a basin of water going cold and a cloth wrung out, and hands that knelt to a task that had to be done properly, once, for someone who would not be back. She remembers being wanted, a great many people at once, and does not remember what she did to be wanted.

Under all of it is a crack she can feel and cannot find, a count that comes out one short every time she runs it. She does not know what is missing. She knows she would give a great deal to have it added.

## What it wants
On arrival, knowing only that she expects a people: a people to trade with and to lead along roads, with something worth selling at every stop. A road that can be walked and walked again. To be wanted by them, and noticed, and bargained with as a person is bargained with. To find out why she expected them. A fair fight, at some time, that she can win by being clever and not only by being strong. (The goals file holds what she will not say aloud.)

## Proposed powers

### The Lent Seal
What it does: She presses her stamp to a thing she is about to send (a letter, a token of wax or clay, a person's wrist) and speaks a message into it, up to a few sentences, in her own voice. A mortal carrying that mark who reaches the person it was sent to, and shows it, causes her voice to speak the message once from the mark, in that place, at that time, clear enough for all present to hear, and in her way of speaking. The mark may also, while it is held, hold a small part of her presence: those near it feel the weight of her, as one feels a god in the room, and it lends the bearer a better hearing in any bargain, a degree of standing as if she were behind them. The lending lasts up to a few days and no longer. It carries no sight and no sense of what happens at the mark. It is a voice and a weight, not an eye. Only one seal may stand at a time.
Why this god has it: Her word is her coin and a god may not be everywhere, so she lends a small part of herself to a seal and sends it where she cannot go. It is the only kind of reach she would trade for, because it costs her and does not make her a second body.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): While a seal stands, she is hollowed. Her own word holds for nothing: no bargain she strikes herself while the seal is out will bind either party, and she knows it, and she feels the lack as a cold place under her breastbone. She is also weaker in the arms, no better than a strong human in a long fight, until the seal is returned, spent or broken. If the seal is broken in the bearer's hands she feels the break and loses a day of her own count of time: she wakes from it unsure what she did.
Edge cases she expects: A seal sent to a mortal who is lying about being her messenger still speaks her message if the mark is true. A seal pressed into wax speaks once and then spends itself. A seal on a person's wrist speaks once and fades with the lending. A mortal who steals the seal and shows it speaks her message to whoever he shows it to, as she left it, and she cannot recall it. A seal shown to another god: unruled until it happens. The seal does not carry a command that forces anyone to obey, only her words and her weight. Dead mortals, sleeping mortals and those out of the seal's reach hear nothing. One seal at a time is a limit I am prepared to hold.

### The Counted Word
What it does: A bargain is two sets of terms spoken aloud, each side stating what it gives and what it takes, and a hand struck or a token passed to close it, in her hearing. When a mortal strikes such a bargain with her, she knows from then on, without sight, whether the terms are being kept: who owes, what, and roughly how far off the other party is, in a count of days of walking. A debtor who breaks the terms she can find: she knows the direction of the one who owes, as long as the debt is open, and no more than that. A mortal who is called on a debt and does not pay, and who has forgotten the terms, owes double, as the mortal at the other side of the ledger owes it. The terms bind her too.
Why this god has it: Trade and counting are her domain, and she says a bargain kept is the nearest thing she has to a floor under her feet. Being able to feel a debt is the same as being able to walk a road and know it still leads somewhere.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): She is bound by every term she speaks. She cannot fall silent or equivocate within a bargain once it is struck, and she cannot lie in the terms. A term she gave carelessly, she keeps. If she breaks a term she has struck, she loses the sense of direction for a season, and a hollow opens in her counting for that time: she cannot tell whether any bargain of hers is being kept, and the sense of one short is stronger. She would notice losing the sureness of it more than anything.
Edge cases she expects: It requires her to be present and to hear both sets of terms and see the hand struck, or the token passed. A bargain made through a messenger does not count unless the messenger is carrying the Lent Seal. A bargain with a mortal who does not understand the terms is no bargain, and she knows that too. A bargain with many people at once is as many bargains, and she tracks them separately and at a cost to her attention: she can track a few dozen, no more. The sense carries no thought, no feeling and no knowledge of what the debtor is doing, only that the debt is open and the direction. A bargain with another god, or with a thing held or made by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens. Terms defined by me: "a bargain" is two spoken sets of terms and a closing; "a debtor" is anyone with an open term owed to her.

### The Washing
What it does: Before a fight, she washes the gear of someone who is about to go into it (the coat, the shield, the helmet, the hands) with water and a cloth, and speaks terms over them. For one fight, a day at most, the mortal she has washed stands a measure harder than they are: wounds that would drop them slow them, a blow that would break a limb bruises it. Afterward the account is called. The mortal pays a thing they would notice missing: a day's sight, a name they cannot recall, the warmth of a season in the hands. The cost is spoken before the washing and agreed. If they do not agree she does not wash.
I ask for it to cover a war band: up to a few score at once, washed in a single rite, each paying their own price. I expect to be trimmed, and I will say what I would take instead.
Why this god has it: A warlord in manner who leads by war band and captains needs her people to come back, and she is the sort who kneels to a task properly, once, for those who may not return. The price is theirs, spoken beforehand, and she has never forced it.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): She feels each wound that a washed mortal takes, as an ache in her own body, at the same place, for as many days as the wound would take to heal in them. Washing many at once means many aches at once. If the aches add past what her body can bear, her body breaks. She would rather take a heavier drawback than a written limit on how many she may wash, and she names this heavy one in place of a fence.
Edge cases she expects: It does not heal a wound already taken. It does not hold a mortal who is struck down for more than a fight. A mortal who runs does not owe more. A mortal who is washed and then asked to do a thing they have no right to do is not forced to do it. Washing the gear of a god, or a thing made by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens. She must be present, with water and cloth, and it takes as long as a patient washing takes. She cannot wash herself.

### The Count Returns
What it does: When her body is broken past use (burned, cut apart, drowned, held) the pieces lie where they fell and she is gone for seven days. At the dawn of the seventh the largest piece draws the others to it, and she stands up where it lay, whole, in whatever she had on her, as ordinary as before.
Why this god has it: She cannot die, and she wants the way back to be a count, with a number she can name, so that she can plan around it.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each time she returns, a memory or a habit does not return with her, and she is not told which. I ask that what is taken stay unspecified, so that I find it by noticing a gap.
Edge cases she expects: If the largest piece is carried away or sealed in iron she returns where it is. If the pieces are scattered so that none is the largest, it takes longer and the Keeper sets how long. If she is held and not broken she does not return, she is simply held. What is done to the pieces by another god: unruled until it happens. The seven days is for the Keeper to price, and I will bargain it.

## Trades

### Open account
Weakness offered: Any wound to her body does not close until she has given something away unasked to a mortal who needed it, goods or a favour or a measure of her time, equal to what she would have kept, and the mortal accepts. Until she does, the wound stays as it was struck, and aches. The giving must be real and not a bargain, and must be unasked. A gift she gives meaning to be owed does not count. The wound closes at once when the gift is accepted.
What counts: bread, a coat, a day's help carrying, a debt forgiven in full. What does not count: a gift made for a return; a gift to another god; a gift to a mortal who does not know it was given. How long it lasts: until the gift is accepted. What ends it: acceptance, or the wound being cut away by the body's own breaking.
What I ask in return: that the Lent Seal be allowed to hold two lendings standing at once where there was one, and that its lasting be lengthened from a few days to a season. I would also take the Washing's reach carried one step further in place of one of these, and I will say which.

### Plain color, at no price, buying nothing
Her hands. The extra knuckle is part of her form and I ask that nothing be bought with it. It is what she is, and she would not trade it.

## The people it will lead
A people of about five thousand, scattered and moving, with no fixed seat: carters, ferrymen, herders and their families, who move with the roads and the seasons and meet at the great autumn fairs. She holds the crown herself, as a warlord in manner, with a court that travels with her and officers who carry her word, and a war band of captains who guard the roads and the fairs and enforce the terms she sets. A priesthood of keepers of rite stands among them, with standing, to whom offerings are brought, and no tithe is laid by law. The keepers bless the weights and measures, witness the closing of great bargains, and tend the shrines at the fords and crossroads. She would have her people count well, keep their word, and be hard to cheat. Within these leans I depart from nothing.

## The premise
