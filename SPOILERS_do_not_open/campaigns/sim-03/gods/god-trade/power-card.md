# Power card: the Factor

Campaign: sim-03. Written: creation, Night 2, 2026-11-20. The text below is the round 2 final text as you confirmed it, with the wording you asked in your confirmation. You have no name of your own yet. You are filed under "the Factor".

## Form

One body, in one place, as for every god here (the campaign allows one body).

As you described yourself, in your own words:

A tall woman, human to the eye, in the middle of her years. Broad through the shoulder and long in the thigh, built to walk a road all day and all night and start again. Her face is weathered and plain, a wide mouth that is quick to name a price, grey eyes that look at a person the way a buyer looks at a measure of grain. Her hair is dark and bound back with a cord.

She is visibly wrong in one way: her fingers have an extra joint each, a third knuckle where there should be two. They bend in ways a hand should not, and she counts on them without thinking, folding and unfolding in a rhythm she does not choose. She hides them in her sleeves when she wants a stranger easy, and shows them when she wants a stranger to know what is in front of him.

Senses: the senses of a strong human, no better and no worse, and the count settled below. Any sense not described is left unsettled and settled later.

She moves at the pace of her form: a long, even walk, a ready stride, no faster than a strong woman in good boots. She does not tire and does not need to rest. She sleeps by choice, usually lightly, sitting up.

### What breaks you
Fire held to her. Being cut apart, the body parted at the joints and the pieces scattered. Being bound in iron or bronze chain so that she cannot move her hands. Being shut in a closed place with no door she can reach. Being drowned and held under, since she does not need breath but the weight pins her. Being made to stand somewhere she has been named a debtor and cannot leave. Her hands matter most: a mortal who has bound or broken her hands has taken most of what she is.

Wounds are real and they ache. A cut bleeds, though she does not bleed out.

### When your body is broken
See The Count Returns below. It carries a cost and a trigger, so it is written as a power, and the power is the rule.

### Things carried (as you gave them; all ordinary unless a power below says otherwise)
Boots, trousers and a long belted coat of undyed wool, all ordinary. A leather belt pouch holding a handful of plain tally sticks, notched, and a bit of chalk. A small balance of bronze with two shallow pans, folded into a wooden case, ordinary, no power in it. A flat stamp of fired clay on a cord at her neck, about the size of a thumb, cut with a simple mark of her own, which is ordinary until she lends it (see "The Lent Seal"). No weapon. I ask that all of these be ordinary things with no power attached, except the stamp as described under its power.

### Settled at the table

- God-sense, as set for this campaign: you know another god only face to face, close, as a weight or an unease that grows surer with time and can be wrong. It tells nothing of the other's name, nature or powers. You know that others like you came down when you did.
- Pace: the pace of your form, as you described it; the pace table's rates apply (`engine/rules/time-and-pace.md`).
- Senses you did not describe are unsettled and are settled in play, never inferred.
- The "Intent, your first wording" lines are kept for the record only. Where one differs from a Description or a Drawback, the Description and the Drawback stand.
- When the body is broken: see the return power below. It carries a cost and a trigger, so it is written as a power.
- Your count: you always know how many of a thing are in plain view in the room or the ground in front of you, and the count is never off by more than one. It counts what you could see by looking. It tells nothing of worth, intent, or anything hidden, covered or behind you. Color, at no price.

### Definitions

None beyond the terms each power defines for itself.

## Powers

### The Lent Seal
Status: main
Description: You press your stamp to a thing you are about to send (a letter, a token of wax or clay, a person's wrist) and speak into it a message of up to a few sentences. When a mortal carrying that mark reaches the one it was sent to and shows it, your voice speaks the message once from the mark, there and then, for all present to hear. While the mark is carried, those near it feel something of your weight, and the bearer is heard as one who has you behind them. The lending lasts up to a few days. It carries no sight and no sense of what happens at the mark. Two seals stand at a time.
Drawback: While a seal stands you are hollowed. No bargain you strike in your own person while it is out binds either party, and you are no stronger in the arm than a strong mortal. If the seal is broken in the bearer's hands you feel it and lose a day: you wake from it unsure what you did.
Limits settled at creation:
- It travels at the bearer's pace and can be lost, stolen or stopped as any carried thing can. A thief who shows it gets your message as you left it; you cannot recall it. A wax or clay mark is spent once it speaks; a mark on a wrist fades with the lending. The weight compels no one: no mortal must obey, agree or believe, and a bargain made under it binds only as any bargain does. The dead, the sleeping and those out of earshot hear nothing. It rules no mind. Shown to another god, or set on a thing made or held by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Use across a year: two at a time, each a few days.
- The hollowing is one state and is not doubled by a second seal; it lasts while either stands. Each seal broken in a bearer's hands costs its own day.
- Intent, your first wording: "She presses her stamp to a thing she is about to send (a letter, a token of wax or clay, a person's wrist) and speaks a message into it, up to a few sentences, in her own voice. A mortal carrying that mark who reaches the person it was sent to, and shows it, causes her voice to speak the message once from the mark, in that place, at that time, clear enough for all present to hear, and in her way of speaking. The mark may also, while it is held, hold a small part of her presence: those near it feel the weight of her, as one feels a god in the room, and it lends the bearer a better hearing in any bargain, a degree of standing as if she were behind them. The lending lasts up to a few days and no longer. It carries no sight and no sense of what happens at the mark. It is a voice and a weight, not an eye. Only one seal may stand at a time."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: The second seal standing was bought with the weakness Open account, under Weaknesses taken.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Counted Word
Status: main
Description: A bargain is two sets of terms spoken aloud, each side saying what it gives and takes, closed by a struck hand or a passed token, in your presence and hearing. When a mortal strikes such a bargain with you, you know from then on whether its terms stand kept, open or broken, and while a debt to you is open you know the direction of the one who owes and roughly how many days' walk off. No more than that: not what the debtor does, thinks or feels, and not why a term broke. You hold a few dozen such bargains at once and no more.
Drawback: You are bound by every term you speak in such a bargain. You cannot lie in the terms, equivocate inside them or fall silent on them, and a term given carelessly is a term you keep. If you break one, the sense is gone for a season: you cannot tell whether any bargain of yours is kept.
Limits settled at creation:
- Both sides must understand the terms; a bargain not understood is none. A bargain through a messenger counts only if the messenger carries the Lent Seal. Anyone who learns how you are bound may hold your own terms against you. The power doubles no debt and enforces nothing: what a broken term costs the debtor is your law and your captains' work, not the power's. Distance makes no difference to the knowing. A bargain with another god, or with a thing made or held by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. It rules no mind.
- Intent, your first wording: "A bargain is two sets of terms spoken aloud, each side stating what it gives and what it takes, and a hand struck or a token passed to close it, in her hearing. When a mortal strikes such a bargain with her, she knows from then on, without sight, whether the terms are being kept: who owes, what, and roughly how far off the other party is, in a count of days of walking. A debtor who breaks the terms she can find: she knows the direction of the one who owes, as long as the debt is open, and no more than that. A mortal who is called on a debt and does not pay, and who has forgotten the terms, owes double, as the mortal at the other side of the ledger owes it. The terms bind her too."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Washing
Status: main
Description: Before a fight you wash the gear and hands of one who is about to go into it, with water and a cloth, by your own hand, and speak terms over them. For that one fight, a day at most, the one you washed stands a measure harder: a wound that would drop them slows them, a blow that would break a limb bruises it. Afterward the account is called, and they pay what was named and agreed before the washing: a thing they would notice missing. If they do not agree, you do not wash.
Drawback: You carry every wound a washed mortal takes, in your own body, at the same place, as that wound's pain and weakness without its bleeding, for as many days as it takes to heal in them. They add. If they add past what your body can bear, your body breaks.
Limits settled at creation:
- Each is washed by your hand, in the time a patient washing takes, about a quarter of an hour for each; you may wash two together, one under each hand, in half an hour. There is no rite over many at once. No number is fenced: your time before the fight and your own body are the limit. It heals nothing already taken and holds for the one fight only. It compels no one to fight or obey. You cannot wash yourself. The mortal's price falls due even if they ran. Washing a god, or gear made or held by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Use across a year: every fight you are present before.
- Intent, your first wording: "Before a fight, she washes the gear of someone who is about to go into it (the coat, the shield, the helmet, the hands) with water and a cloth, and speaks terms over them. For one fight, a day at most, the mortal she has washed stands a measure harder than they are: wounds that would drop them slow them, a blow that would break a limb bruises it. Afterward the account is called. The mortal pays a thing they would notice missing: a day's sight, a name they cannot recall, the warmth of a season in the hands. The cost is spoken before the washing and agreed. If they do not agree she does not wash."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Count Returns
Status: main
Description: When your body is broken past use, the pieces lie where they fell and do not rot, and you are gone. After a full season the largest piece draws the others to it and you stand up where it lay, whole, in whatever was on that piece.
Drawback: Each time you return, a memory or a habit does not return with you, and you are not told which.
Limits settled at creation:
- The return follows the table's rule above. If the largest piece is carried away you return where it is. If it is sealed in metal or shut in a closed place, you do not return until it is let out. If the pieces are so scattered that none is largest, the season begins when a mortal or chance brings two together. Held and not broken, you are held. What another god does to the pieces: unruled until it happens.
- Ground of your own kind is wherever the largest piece lies; no walk is added.
- Intent, your first wording: "When her body is broken past use (burned, cut apart, drowned, held) the pieces lie where they fell and she is gone for seven days. At the dawn of the seventh the largest piece draws the others to it, and she stands up where it lay, whole, in whatever she had on her, as ordinary as before."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

## Weaknesses taken

**Open account.** A wound to your body does not begin to close until you have given something away unasked to a mortal who needed it (goods, a favour, a measure of your time) and the mortal has accepted it. The giving must be real: unasked, not a bargain, and not meant to be owed. A gift to a god, or to a mortal who does not know it was given, does not count. Once the gift is accepted the wound closes at a mortal's pace from that day. How it can be used against you: one who learns it can keep you wounded by keeping the needy from you, or by refusing what you offer.
- A gift counts when the mortal knows it was given at the moment of accepting it. A mortal forgetting it afterward does not undo it.
- What it bought: Two seals stand at once for The Lent Seal, in place of one.

Kept as color: The third knuckle of each finger. Part of your form, at no price, buying nothing.
