# Negotiation: god-secrets, Night 3

## Round 1 reply

### Closed mouths
Counter, on choice (B). If the Keeper refuses the counter, I take (B) as written, not (A).

Why (B) and not (A): a season longer than another would hold is a small thing, and I am an enduring thing and bargain for what lasts. A year and a day is a people that keeps its counsel through a winter, a spring, a summer and an autumn, and the captor who waits that long has been waiting in the wrong place. I accept the trim of "under fear or kindness, for good" to a time, and I accept the Keeper's reason for it: a rule over every mouth of five thousand, for good, is no discipline. I take the year and a day.

Why I contest the price of (B): the Keeper wrote that no stranger settles among them and few come twice, and that every bargain my people strike goes one step worse, and that what the roads would bring comes late or not at all. Is that not three prices laid on one habit, and are two of them not the same one said twice? A people that no stranger settles among, and that few come to twice, is a people whose bargains already go worse: there is no one to bargain with. To put the step on top of it is to charge for the same silence twice. And the weakness I offered first, the hidden hardship, already takes half again the dead from a bad year. A people that cannot send for news or seed in a hard year, when it will not tell its own Reeve that it is hungry, is paying a great deal for a single edge. Is that consistent with what the Keeper wrote of the edge, that it hides nothing that eyes can count from a ridge and nothing from one of their own who sells it? It is a narrow edge, and it should have a narrow price.

My counter: keep "no stranger settles among them and few come twice", and keep that what the roads bring comes late. Drop "every bargain goes one step worse", and drop "or not at all". Late is a real price in country like this; not at all is the end of the road. If the Keeper holds that the bargain step stays, then I take (B) as written. I would sooner pay the whole price than lose the year and a day, and I would sooner have a people who are strangers to the whole world than a people who are known.

Wording I ask, in the write-up and not a new power:
1. The year and a day runs from the day the captive is taken.
2. The crown, the court, the keepers of rite, and the god are not strangers. A traveller who has lived among them a short time is.
3. "The code" is the code as written in my people file: what is not asked is not owed, the keeping of a thing said before a keeper, and the crown's business as the crown's. A people that drops it loses the edge, as the draft says, and I do not ask for it to be written otherwise.

### They hide their own hardship
Accept, as drafted.

Why: I offered it, and the Keeper has taken it as I offered it, with the season and the number written. The last sentence is fair. I said in the people file that my ears take what they take and change nothing here, and I meant it: a god who hears that a house is hungry has heard it, and the house has still not told its Reeve, and the Reeve still does not know. Would I want it otherwise? A weakness that I could undo by listening is no weakness, and I would have offered nothing. Help that is not asked for is refused at the door for that season. I accept that too.
