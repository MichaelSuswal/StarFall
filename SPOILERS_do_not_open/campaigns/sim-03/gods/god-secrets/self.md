# Self: none yet (filed as the Hush)

## Name
None. Is a name needed before someone gives one? Until someone names her, let her be filed under the Hush. She will answer to nothing that is shouted at her, and to most things that are asked of her quietly.

## Form
A great beast, and the size matters: heavier than a war ox and longer, low at the hip and high at the shoulder, so that the head hangs forward and level with the chest. The hide is a thick, close, grey brown pelt, the colour of ash left in a hearth for a night. Along the spine and over the shoulders, set in the pelt like a row of leaves, are small ears, pale inside and dark rimmed, dozens of them, each no bigger than a thumb joint. Are they not the first thing anyone sees, once they have stopped looking at the teeth?

The head is long and heavy browed, the jaw deep. The eyes are amber with a flat, horizontal pupil, and they hold the dark better than the light. The mouth is a beast's mouth and the voice that comes out of it is low and level, not loud, carrying a long way in still air and not at all in wind. Hearing is the keenest sense by far, finer than any mortal's, able to pick one voice out of a hall. Smell is good. Sight is poor in strong light, good in dark and dusk. Touch through the pelt is dull; through the nose and the ears it is acute. The other senses she has not described are left to be settled later.

She moves on four legs, with a long padded stride that is quieter than a creature of her weight should be. She can walk or lope, not run for long and not fast; her pace is the pace of a heavy animal that never tires. She cannot climb a ladder or pass through a door narrower than her shoulders. She can stand on her hind legs for a short while to look over a wall, no more.

### What breaks you
Does a beast this thick not take a great deal of breaking? Flint, bronze and stone open the hide slowly and seldom reach the meat, and what they open closes slowly. Fire is the thing that breaks her: it burns the pelt and the flesh under it, and burns heal badly and leave her bare. She can be held: by weight, by a pit she cannot climb, by chains of a thickness a village could forge, by stone laid over her, by a sealed room with a door she cannot open. She can be shut out by a door she cannot break. She can be cut apart or burned to nothing, though that is not easy, and she is then in the state below.

### When your body is broken
Burned to ash, crushed, or scattered past holding, she is nothing for a time, and then she is back. How she comes back, and what it costs her, is proposed below as a power (The Slow Return). Does she remember the going? She remembers it as a long listening with nothing to hear.

### Things carried
Nothing. What would a beast carry? There is the hide and what grows on it, and that is all. She asks that she carry no object at all, and so there is no object to price.

## Traits

### Sovereign in the dark
She wants to be obeyed by everyone and trusted by no one, and she does not see the contradiction as a problem to solve. Reconciles: Regard for mortals 96 (sovereign), Trust 97 (wary), Company 38 (leaning solitary), Presence 36 (leaning revealed rather than hidden). The sovereign wants the people near, kneeling, counted. The wary one wants every one of them listened to. The solitary one does not want any of them in the room. So she rules from a walled seat, by officers and by ears, rarely walks among her people, and is seen on the days she wishes to be feared. A people who cannot see you often, and who are always overheard, will fear you more than a people who see you daily. Is that not the arrangement that suits her?

### Exact in her terms, easy in her pace
She is patient and clever enough to keep her word to the letter, and she wants every limit written precisely, and she is also free of mind enough that she does not mean any of it kindly. Reconciles: Order 9 (toward law), Mind 34 (toward intelligent), Temper 39 (toward patient), Making 21 (toward builder), Appetite 28 (toward content), with the flaw and the virtue. Her restraint is real. She does not take what is not hers by the terms, does not strike in a temper, does not spend what she has not weighed. Her cruelty is the other half. It is not appetite and it is not anger. It is that she does not notice the cost to anyone but herself when the terms are met. Is it not exact justice, to have kept to the letter of what was said? Did she not warn them?

## Backstory outline
Who was she? She remembers a place of low voices, a keeping place, long and dim, where everything said was kept and nothing was lost, and where she was the one who kept it. She remembers holding the ends and the beginnings of other people's stories in her mouth, so to speak, and knowing, for each, how it would go. She does not remember knowing her own. She remembers a gap where it should be, and that she looked at the gap a long time, and then she remembers coming down.

Under the ribs there is a weight, and at the back of the throat there is a taste that is not hunger and is not any food. Does it not sit there like a swallowed stone that was once something warm? She does not know what it is. She does not know when she first noticed it. She does not go looking for it. She has the sense that she should not.

## What it wants
On arrival, knowing only that she expects a people: a people to keep, and a place to keep them where the walls are already built by stone. To know first, before she is known, who else is here. To hear what the people say when they think no one is listening. To find the one among them who will not be made to say the sentence. To learn what is waiting, and has been quiet so long that quiet is its name. (A want she will not say aloud is kept in her goals file and goes to no one.)

## Proposed powers

### The Small Ears
What it does: She can shed ears from her pelt. Each is a separate small part of her body, thumb sized, grey on the outside and pale within, able to crawl at a mouse's pace, to cling, and to hide in thatch, cracks, wall tops, cloth and beast hair. She places one by hand or mouth, at a place or on a mortal, a beast or a thing. An ear hears speech and loud sound within about a dozen paces, as one would hear in a quiet room, and nothing else: no sight, no smell, no touch, no taste. It keeps what it hears, the words and the voices as said, for the last few days, and the oldest falls away first. To read it she must take it back: she touches it to her own hide and it rejoins her at once, and she then has all it kept, in order, in the voices as spoken, like a remembered day. She cannot hear through an ear as it listens, cannot call it from a distance, cannot speak through it, cannot feel where it is. It does not think, choose, or follow orders; it stays where it is set until it is taken back. She asks to have up to twelve out at once. She expects that to be trimmed and will argue the trim.
Why this god has it: She said, before she had a body, that she would be one body and a great many small ears, and that every part sent out would be a part she could not account for. Her domain is secrets, and she is a being who keeps what is said.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each ear out is a part of her body she cannot feel and cannot account for. She does not know if it is lost, found, crushed or eaten. The place where it grew stays a bare, thin patch in the pelt, and a wound in a bare patch goes deeper and closes slower, until the ear is back or a season has passed since it was lost. And what an ear brings back may not be what it heard: some ears come back changed, and a word or a name or a voice in what they report may be wrong, and she is not told which. She would notice losing this, because without it every secret she holds would be clean. She accepts the Keeper showing her either price where it can go two ways.
Edge cases she expects: An ear found and killed (the bare patch stays for a season, nothing else is lost). An ear on a person who goes far, or over water: it stays on them, beyond her reach, hearing, and may not return in the days it keeps. An ear swallowed by a beast: lost. An ear in a place or on a thing held by more than ordinary craft, or on another god: unruled until it happens. Language she does not know: she hears the sounds, and what she gets of the meaning depends on what she knows. Whether an ear counts as a second body, and so breaks the rule of one body: she asks for the clarification in the write-up, not a new power, that it does not, since it has no mind, sight or will, and is hers as her hair is hers. How an ear and what it heard behave if she is broken while ears are out: tied to The Slow Return below. Ears set in a room that she cannot enter: still hears, and returns only when someone carries it out or she can reach it.

### The Asked Question
What it does: Face to face, within arm's length, in a place where the two of them are alone or where no one else is near enough to hear, she can ask a mortal one question, and that mortal answers it truthfully as they believe the truth to be. They cannot say a false thing in answer. They may stay silent, if they choose, for as long as they can bear to. She may ask only one question per use, and the question must be a real question and not a command. The mortal does not know that anything was done to them, only that the answer left their mouth.
Why this god has it: Because she wants truth most, and because mortals have it least. Is that not what she is for?
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The mortal loses something for the answer: one memory, a name, or a person they would rather not have lost, taken from near the thing they said, and not chosen by her. She does not choose it and does not mind, and that is the flaw. Her own price: what she is told stays in her, sealed. She cannot tell it, write it, or sign it to another, ever, and she cannot act on it in a way that shows another how she came by it. She keeps it, and she cannot ever forget it. A second price she offers in its place, if the Keeper prefers: she cannot ask the same mortal twice, and for a full season after each use she cannot use the power again at all.
Edge cases she expects: A mortal who does not know the answer (they say so, truly). A mortal who is mistaken (they answer what they believe, and it may be wrong). A mortal who does not speak her tongue (no use; the question must be understood). A question about another god, or about something made or held by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens. A mortal who is made to answer by this and then killed: the answer she has stays sealed. A mortal who dies mid answer: not the power's concern.

### The Slow Return
What it does: When her body is broken past holding, whether burned to ash, crushed, scattered, or sealed away from any place she could leave, she is nothing for a time and then she is whole again. If at least one ear of hers is out and whole when she is broken, she returns at the place it lies, after one season. If none is out, she returns at the place where she was broken, after two seasons, in the dark of the night, on bare ground, and no one is near. The return takes the whole of a season and cannot be hurried.
Why this god has it: She is a god and cannot die, so something must come back. It is the form of the return that is hers, and an ear left standing is the one thing she is certain is still hers.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each time she comes back from nothing, a memory or a habit does not come back with her, and she is not told which. She asks that what is taken stay unspecified, so that she finds it by noticing a gap. She would notice losing this price, because the gap is the only mark of the going.
Edge cases she expects: What a broken body does to ears still out: they stay as they are. A return in a place held by more than ordinary craft, or shared with another god: unruled until it happens. Being sealed away and then released before the season ends: the season still runs, and she comes back at the place she was sealed.

## Trades
She offers one weakness and asks one thing in return.

The Slow Wound. Any wound that breaks her hide to the meat and bleeds does not close until a full season has passed in which she has not spoken one word aloud in the hearing of any mortal. While it is open it is raw, aches, and is weaker than the rest of her: a second blow to it opens it wider. In return she asks that an ear keep what it hears for a season and not for a few days.

Edge cases. What counts: a wound from a weapon, from fire, from teeth, or from a god, that breaks the hide and reaches the meat. What does not count: bruises, cuts in the pelt that do not reach the meat, a bare patch left by a shed ear. How long: until a full season has passed in which she speaks no word aloud to any mortal; she may speak to a god, and she may hear whatever is said to her; the season does not need to be a calendar season, only a run of days of that length, and it restarts if she breaks the silence. Do ears out count? They do not speak, so they do not. What ends it: the silent season completing, or the wound being the whole of her, burned away, as in The Slow Return, which closes it as nothing. If she is hurt twice before the first closes, the seasons do not stack, but the second wound opens the first wider.

She keeps a second weakness as plain color, at no price, buying nothing: a dislike of closed rooms she cannot enter, which she feels as a pressure at the back of her neck.

## The people it will lead
A people of about five thousand in a walled seat or town in broken hill country, where the land folds in on itself and the stone is already laid. She holds the crown herself, as a god-king with a court of officers who carry her word as law, and she rules by fear and her own will: the people serve her, and she does not ask whether they love her. Beside the court stands a priesthood of keepers of rite, with standing among the people, to whom the people bring offerings, with no tithe laid by law. What is the rite? It is the keeping of what is said: the priests are, in their way, her ears, and the people know it and do not say so. She comes down in winter, when every household is keeping something from the next. Who are the helpers who run the seat when she is away, and who are the priests? That is for the third night, and she will give her reasons then if she departs from any of it.
