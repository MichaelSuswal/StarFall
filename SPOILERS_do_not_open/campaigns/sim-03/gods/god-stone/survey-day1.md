## Answer 1

Stone that has not been told what to be. A mountain at the hour before dawn, cold through, with everything on it still holding its breath. We feel a world with hard edges and wide room, where nobody has fenced the sky. It is a place that can be broken open and does not mind.

## Answer 2

Mountains. Of course mountains. High broken country, scree and cliff and the long dark under the roots, passes that only some can cross. We would have a people who walk, so we want land with many ways through it, not one valley to sit in.

## Answer 3

Hard seasons, and honest ones. A long cold that tests who is worth keeping, a short bright thaw when everything moves at once, a dry heat in which the rock ticks and splits. No season should be kind to the weak or the slow. We do not want the year to be soft with anyone.

We wake into the season of the thaw, when the ice cracks and the passes open.

## Answer 4

Some of each, but we hope for more rooted than wild. Wild country is only empty rock. Old kingdoms already rooted are walls, and walls are what we like to meet. We hope to find things worth taking and things worth knocking down, and we hope they are not sure what to make of us.

## Answer 5

Ruins under ruins. Many peoples have stood here and fallen, and not all of them were saved. We want the old dead layered under the new, so that every mountain is a grave that nobody has finished reading.

## Answer 6

Rare, and costly to find. Not a wise-woman in every valley. A mountain hag, a stone reader, a few who can be sought out and may refuse. Enough that a mortal can hope, not enough that a mortal can rely.

## Answer 7

It should feel like pressure, like weight on the chest and the grind of rock on rock. It should cost them something they would notice: warmth, or years, or a name. We want magic to take a price from the body, because a price that can be paid and forgotten is not a price.

## Answer 9

Strange, but at a distance. Familiar beasts in the valleys. Higher up and deeper down, things with no names, things that move inside rock. We want the wild to be larger than the stories about it, and we want it to answer to nobody.

## Answer 10

Falling, and what falls. Avalanche, collapse, the slope that gives way beneath a column on the march. The cold that comes in the night. A hunger that lasts a whole winter. The danger that makes a good story is the one that cannot be argued with and cannot be bargained with, and that takes people anyway.

## Answer 11

Safe ground. A place to stop that nobody will take from you. We want mortals to want a roof and a wall and a year of grain in a cellar more than they want gold, and to have almost none of it. We want it scarce so that those who keep it keep it by strength.

## Answer 12

Sealed doors in the rock. Old roads that climb and do not come down. Something enormous, bones or stone, folded into a mountain. We would very much like to find a door and not be told yet what is behind it.

## Answer 13

Yes. Peoples of the deep and the high places, slow and patient and hard to read, who think of stone as a kind of family. Not friends. Not only enemies. We want them to have long memories and no reason to like us.

## Answer 14

Premise bent: few, and not welcome. One or two, no more, and we would meet them at a pass and not know whether to feed them or to throw them down. We would like their tools. We would not like how they carry themselves.

## Answer 15

Face to face only. A pull at any distance is a leash, and we do not wear one. We want to look at the other and know it, and know whether it knows us, and have the choice before the knowing is complete.

## Answer 16

One body. We are small and we are one, and we do not want to be spread thinner. If any god can be more than one place, let it cost them the part of themselves that stays behind, and let them feel the loss.

## Answer 17

One who leaves. A mortal who climbs out of a ruined place and does not look back, who is stubborn and not clever, who gathers others by walking and not by speaking. We want one who carries something heavy and does not put it down. We want one who is hard to stop and easy to break, and who gets up.

## Answer 18

A people we did not lose. A truth about the ones before. We cannot imagine it, and we are not sure we can look at it if it is shown to us, but this is what we hope the world holds, somewhere under the rock.

## Answer 19

We wish that this time the mountain holds.
