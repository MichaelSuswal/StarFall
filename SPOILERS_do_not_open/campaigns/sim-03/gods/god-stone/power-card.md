# Power card: the Child of the Scree

Campaign: sim-03. Written: creation, Night 2, 2026-11-20. The text below is the round 2 final text as you confirmed it, with the wording you asked in your confirmation. You have no name of your own yet. You are filed under "the Child of the Scree".

## Form

One body, in one place, as for every god here (the campaign allows one body).

As you described yourself, in your own words:

A child of about nine winters. Thin, narrow-shouldered, a hand shorter than a grown woman. Dark hair always full of grey dust no matter how often it is shaken out. A flat, still face, too still for the age. Eyes the color of wet slate, which do not blink as often as a child's should. Bare feet, the soles hard as horn. Hands small and scarred across the knuckles, as if they had been hitting rock for a long time and we do not remember why.

Senses are ordinary: sight, hearing, touch, smell, taste, as a healthy child has them. Nothing beyond them is claimed here; anything more is proposed below as a power. We move at a child's pace on a child's legs, and we do not tire. We go up broken ground well, on all fours when it is steep, and we do not mind the drop.

### What breaks you
A child's body, and no more than that. Blade, flint, bronze, club, fire and a long fall all hurt us and can cut us apart. Cold gets in. A heavy fall of rock can crush us, and rope, chain, a pit or a wall built around us can hold us. A door we cannot force shuts us out, and we are small enough that a grown man can carry us off under one arm. Our body endures: pain does not slow us, and we keep walking on wounds. That is stubbornness, not safety, and it does not stop the body from breaking.

### When your body is broken
See Return From the Rock below. It carries a cost and a trigger, so it is written as a power, and the power is the rule.

### Things carried (as you gave them; all ordinary unless a power below says otherwise)
Ordinary things, with no power attached, and we ask that they be written as ordinary:
- A coat of grey wool, too big in the shoulders, belted with a cord.
- A coil of plain hemp rope, about twenty paces.
- A hammer with a flint head bound to a wooden haft, the kind a mason or a quarryman could make. It is only a hammer.
- A leather pouch with a handful of small stones in it. They are only stones.

### Settled at the table

- God-sense, as set for this campaign: you know another god only face to face, close, as a weight or an unease that grows surer with time and can be wrong. It tells nothing of the other's name, nature or powers. You know that others like you came down when you did.
- Pace: the pace of your form, as you described it; the pace table's rates apply (`engine/rules/time-and-pace.md`).
- Senses you did not describe are unsettled and are settled in play, never inferred.
- The "Intent, your first wording" lines are kept for the record only. Where one differs from a Description or a Drawback, the Description and the Drawback stand.
- When the body is broken: see the return power below. It carries a cost and a trigger, so it is written as a power.

### Definitions

None beyond the terms each power defines for itself.

## Powers

### Stone-Hearing
Status: main
Description: With both bare palms or both bare soles flat on living rock, or on worked stone joined to living rock, you feel through that connected stone, out to about a bowshot, the weight and movement of what stands or walks on it, and where the stone is hollow, cracked or ready to give. You learn how many, roughly how heavy, and which way; that there is a hollow, and where the stone will split under load. No faces, names, speech or purpose. You hold it while the contact lasts, and what you felt is gone from you at the next dawn.
Drawback: After each use your hands are cold and numb for the rest of that day: you cannot grip, bind a rope or swing the hammer well.
Limits settled at creation:
- While you listen you are deaf to the air and see poorly, and for a short while after; this is a condition, not the price. It does not pass through earth deeper than a hand over the rock, timber, standing water, or ice thicker than a hand. A wall of mortal masonry carries if it stands on living rock, not if it stands on soil. A gap of soil breaks it. It tells a hollow and never what is in it, whether it was made or grew, or its shape past the bowshot. It never tells a person from a beast, or a god from either. It does not work while you are carried or held off the ground. Light and dark make no difference. It rules no mind. A god on the stone, and anything made or held by more than ordinary craft, is unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Use across a year: limited by the day of numb hands each use costs.
- Intent, your first wording: "With both bare palms, or both bare soles, pressed flat on living rock or on worked stone that is joined to living rock, we feel through that connected stone the weight and movement of things standing or walking on it, and where it is hollow, cracked or ready to give. The reach is out to about a bowshot along the connected rock. We learn how many things are moving on the rock, roughly how heavy, and which way, and we learn where it is hollow beneath, and where it will split under load. We do not learn faces, names, speech or purpose. We hold it for as long as the contact lasts and no longer; there is no memory of it beyond the day, and it is gone from us when the palms lift."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### Fault-Word
Status: main
Description: You lay both hands on living rock, or worked stone joined to it, and say the word. The stone breaks along its own flaws, not where you choose to the finger. At the word, at once, a span of stone up to a bowshot across gives way: a ledge, a gate's footing, a stretch of wall, a slope-face. A span is any shape of connected stone that fits inside a circle a bowshot across.
Drawback: The rock answers twice. Within a day of each breaking, somewhere on the same connected rock within a day's walk, a second fall comes that you did not ask for and cannot place; the Keeper rolls where. And for as long as the rock keeps falling and a full day after, you move at half pace and can lift nothing heavier than the hammer.
Limits settled at creation:
- Living rock and worked stone joined to it. Not loose earth, sand, ice or timber. It makes no rock. Sound, unflawed stone fails you until it has been worked on, hammered, or cracked by weather or fire. You cannot choose what is left standing, and you cannot be sure who is below; if you stand on the slope you are on it, and you take harm from the fall as anyone would. Not twice on the same connected stone in one season. Being watched: everyone near hears the stone go. Your own people get no exemption. It rules no mind. A god in the path, and anything made or held by more than ordinary craft, is unruled until it happens. Use across a year: the season's rest on each stone, and the second fall each time.
- Intent, your first wording: "We lay both hands on living rock, or on worked stone that is joined to it, and say the word. The stone breaks along its own flaws, and the break runs out through the connected rock to as far as one slope or one wall-face we can see in front of us. A hillside gives way, a ledge drops, a wall comes down, a pass closes or a footing goes from under a column. It breaks along the stone's own faults and not where we choose to the finger. The ask is large and we know it: we ask for a whole slope, not a rock. We will take it trimmed."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: The bowshot in place of thirty paces was bought with the weakness Wet Ground, under Weaknesses taken.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### Return From the Rock
Status: main
Description: When your body is broken past use (cut apart, crushed, burned through, drowned), it goes grey, cracks, and lies as a small stone of that place. You are not awake in that time. After a full season the stone splits and a child steps out whole at the bare rock nearest to where the stone lies, with whatever of your things still lies beside it.
Drawback: Each time you come back, a memory or a habit does not come back with you, and you are not told which.
Limits settled at creation:
- The return follows the table's rule above. If the stone is carried off you wake where it was carried. If it is crushed to dust the season begins again from that day. A stone sealed away from all bare rock (in a box, in a pot of earth, sunk in a marsh) does not split until bare rock touches it. Held and not broken, you are only held. It undoes no harm done to your people while you lay. A god who finds the stone: unruled until it happens.
- Ground of your own kind is bare living rock; dressed stone joined to living rock counts.
- Intent, your first wording: "When our body is broken past use (cut apart, crushed, burned through, drowned, held in a way that ends it) it goes grey and cracks and lies as a small stone of that place. In that time we are not awake and cannot act or sense. After some days, a count the Keeper sets, the stone splits and a child steps out whole at the nearest bare rock to where it fell, naked of nothing, with our coat, rope, hammer and pouch beside it if they are still there. If the stone is carried off we wake where it has been carried. If it is crushed to dust we wake later, in the dust."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### Still as Rock
Status: main
Description: Standing or crouching still and silent against living rock or a stone wall, you are seen by mortals and beasts as rock, shadow, or a heap of grey dust, and not as a child. It holds while you neither move nor speak and no one touches you.
Drawback: While you hold it the cold of the rock goes into you, and when it ends, by your choice or not, you cannot move your limbs for about a hundred breaths and your hands have no feeling for an hour.
Limits settled at creation:
- It does not hide you from touch or smell, from one who watched you settle, or from a mortal who already knows where you are; a careful searcher in the right place can find you. Not on open ground with no rock at your back. Light and dark make no difference. A god looking: unruled until it happens. It rules no mind.
- Intent, your first wording: "When we stand or crouch against living rock or a stone wall, still and silent, mortals and beasts that look at us see rock, shadow or a heap of the grey dust on our coat, and do not see a child. It holds as long as we do not move or speak and as long as no one touches us."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

## Weaknesses taken

**Wet Ground.** Marsh, bog, fen, flooded field, mud flat, or any ground where water stands over the foot drags at you. For each full day on such ground you grow weaker and slower, until after about seven days you can only crawl. It does not kill and does not hold you. Rain, a stream over bare rock, a ford crossed in less than a day, snow on rock and damp earth do not count. It falls away a day for each day on stone or on dry ground over rock. And an unbroken ditch or ring of standing water a pace wide or more stops your body in both directions. The water must stand and be unbroken: a gap a body can pass opens it, and it ends when the water is drained or the line is broken. Whether a mortal dug it knowing or it lies there by chance makes no difference; what you can walk around does not hold you. Running water is not standing water. How it can be used against you: a mortal who learns it can hold a threshold against you with a filled ditch, or pen you in a ring of one.
- "A pace wide or more" is the width of the standing water across the line, measured at its narrowest unbroken point.
- What it bought: Fault-Word breaks a span a bowshot across at the word, in place of thirty paces.

Kept as color: You cannot abide the sound of bells and will not be in a room with one. Color, at no price, buying nothing.
