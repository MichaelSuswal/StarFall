## Round 1 reply

### Stone-Hearing
Accept. A short reach, effects only, and a price that takes our hammer hand for the day. We have no quarrel with it. The dull hearing is how it works, and we accept that it is not counted as price. We ask for one clarification in the write-up, not a new power: that "by the next day" means the memory of what we felt is gone at the next dawn, so that the limit is exact.

### Fault-Word
Accept the Keeper's drawback, and take choice (A), fast and small: at the word, at once, a span of stone up to about thirty paces across. Reasons: we decide fast and we mean to. A slope that groans for an hour and needs a day of hammer work first is a power for someone who plans, and we are not that god; the child that cannot do something at the moment the ledge is under the column will lose the column. We take the second fall as a true price. A people on the march through passes will notice it, and we will learn to place ourselves and our people with it in mind. We will not argue that a slope at a word is a fair thing to ask. We accept the trim, said plainly. We ask for one clarification in the write-up, not a new power: that a "span of stone" is any shape of connected stone that fits inside a circle thirty paces across, so that a long thin ledge counts.
Note: if the Keeper accepts our choice on the weakness below, this power is raised one step. See that item.

### Return From the Rock
Accept, choice (A): a full season, one item lost each time. Reasons: we would sooner lose the one thing than two, and we have no hurry that a month would serve. A god that is broken has been broken because something larger than it has happened to its people, and a month does not undo that. We accept the table's rule on the walk, on being held, and on sealed stones. We accept that the item is chosen by lot and not told to us, as we asked. One clarification in the write-up, not a new power: that "ground of your own kind" means bare living rock, and that dressed stone joined to living rock counts as it does for our other powers.

### Still as Rock
Accept. Small, and paid for. The hundred breaths and the hour are exact, and we want every limit written exactly.

### Wet Ground
Choice (B), with the tooth added. We accept that a ditch or ring of standing water a pace wide stops our body in both directions, so that a mortal who knows it can hold a threshold against us or pen us in. In return it buys Fault-Word one step: the fast breaking at a bowshot across in place of thirty paces, with no day of hammer work.
Reasons: We want force and reach, and we bargain for it. The weakness is real in this form. A mortal who learns the line can use it against a small body, and we are wary of that; it is the kind of cost we would notice. But the line is only a danger to a god that is looked at closely and known, and we stay behind our captains and do not let ourselves be known. We hold the secret by hiding, which is what we do anyway. Choice (A) would buy only a day's memory lengthened or a longer reach on a power that tells us what is under us; it does not make us hard to stop at the moment it matters. Choice (C) buys nothing.
We ask three clarifications in the write-up, not a new power. First, that the ring or ditch must be standing water and unbroken, with no gap a body can pass, and that a gap closes the line. Second, that the line stops us only while the water stands and only while the mortal keeps it, and that it ends the moment the water is broken, drained or crossed by anything but our body. Third, that a mortal who does not know the line gets nothing from it by accident: standing water in a ditch that we simply walk around does not hold us, and only a ring or ditch that we cannot go around does.
The bells stay as color, at no price, buying nothing.

We keep the choice at (B) and not at (A) with a counter. We will argue the point if the Keeper wishes to hear it again.

## Round 2 reply

### Your reasons, answered
We accept the refusals. A line that a dog can end by jumping it is no line, and we did not need to be told that to see it. We asked for it in the hope of a smaller cost, and we say so plainly. The tooth sits in our body and not in the digger's knowledge, and we take it so: a ditch of standing water that lies there by chance stops us as surely as one dug against us. We will go round standing water as we go round a cliff we cannot climb, and we will learn the ground. We take the Keeper's plain word that the bowshot is the largest single stroke the table has sold. We paid for it with a line a mortal can hold and with a second fall, and we know the price.

### Final text
Confirmed. We confirm the Form, What breaks you, When your body is broken, Things carried, Settled at the table, Definitions, all four powers, Wet Ground, the bells as color, and the Keeper trait, with the two wording points below and nothing else. A change of substance is not asked.

Wording points, in the write-up and not new powers:
1. Keeper trait. We ask that the card say what the number is and what it is not, so the limit is exact: it gives how many of our people are on the march and that the number has dropped, and it does not give who, where, or why. We do not mean it as a power, and we do not ask for more than the Keeper wrote. We accept the trait as written. It fits us. We would rather feel the loss and not know whose it is than be spared it.
2. Wet Ground. We ask that "a pace wide or more" be read as the width of the standing water across the line of the ditch, measured at its narrowest unbroken point, so that a ditch with a narrow neck is not argued about at a threshold.

Nothing else is wrong with it. We confirm.
