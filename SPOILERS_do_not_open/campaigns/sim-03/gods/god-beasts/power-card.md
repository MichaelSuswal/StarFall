# Power card: the Half Thing

Campaign: sim-03. Written: creation, Night 2, 2026-11-20. The text below is the round 2 final text as you confirmed it, with the wording you asked in your confirmation. You have no name of your own yet. You are filed under "the Half Thing".

## Form

One body, in one place, as for every god here (the campaign allows one body).

As you described yourself, in your own words:

What does a half thing look like? Is it not a tall person's frame, lean and long in the shank, that ends in a beast's parts where a person's should not? A narrow head with a wolf's long muzzle and a person's wide, watching eyes, amber at the rim. Ears that turn. Fur the colour of dry leaves down the back and across the shoulders, thinning to bare grey skin on the hands, which are a person's hands with the nails grown thick and dark. The legs bend backward at the knee, as a hound's do. About the height of a tall man, and lighter than he would think.

What do I sense with? A beast's nose, first, and a beast's hearing, so that the wood is mostly read in smell and small sound. Eyes that see well in dusk and poorly in hard noon glare. Nothing past these. Is it not best to leave the rest unsettled and let it be settled later?

How do I move? Quietly. A long, loping walk that can hold its pace from dawn to dark, and a short run that I seldom use. I do not hurry. Is hurry not how the hunt is lost?

### What breaks you
Fire, first. Flame burns the fur and the hide under it, and the burn is slow to leave. Cords and nets next: plaited green withy, rope of sinew, a pit with a lid, anything that holds the legs and keeps me from open ground. A spear or a flint blade can open me, and bone can break, and a body that is hurt is a body that is hurt: it aches, it limps, it bleeds. A hard blow to the skull can leave me senseless for an hour. Walls and closed doors shut me out as they would anyone. I cannot die, but is that a comfort?

### When your body is broken
See Scattering below. It carries a cost and a trigger, so it is written as a power, and the power is the rule.

### Things carried (as you gave them; all ordinary unless a power below says otherwise)
A cloak of grey hide, cured, worn at the shoulders. A belt of plaited cord. A small knife of knapped flint with a bone grip, the kind a hunter keeps for skinning. A bundle of dried herbs and a few sinew threads in a pouch at the belt. Ordinary things, every one. None of them does anything but what such a thing does. Where would I have got them? I do not know. They were on me.

### Settled at the table

- God-sense, as set for this campaign: you know another god only face to face, close, as a weight or an unease that grows surer with time and can be wrong. It tells nothing of the other's name, nature or powers. You know that others like you came down when you did.
- Pace: the pace of your form, as you described it; the pace table's rates apply (`engine/rules/time-and-pace.md`).
- Senses you did not describe are unsettled and are settled in play, never inferred.
- The "Intent, your first wording" lines are kept for the record only. Where one differs from a Description or a Drawback, the Description and the Drawback stand.
- When the body is broken: see the return power below. It carries a cost and a trigger, so it is written as a power.

### Definitions

None beyond the terms each power defines for itself.

## Powers

### The Old Track
Status: main
Description: Standing where a living thing has passed, you read its track by smell and sign: what kind it was, how long ago, its health, whether it hunted or was hunted, and which way it went. You can follow a track up to a year old, through rain and over water, as a hound follows a scent. It works on beasts and on mortals. You read what a body leaves on the ground and nothing else: what passed and where, never why.
Drawback: Each time you follow a track to its end, a memory leaves you and does not come back, and you are not told which. And a track once taken up holds you: you cannot set it down until you reach what made it or the track is lost to you; only being held stops you.
Limits settled at creation:
- Reading where you stand costs nothing; only following to the end costs. One track at a time. A creature that was carried left nothing. A thing that leaves neither scent nor sign gives you nothing. A trail crossed by others stays readable and takes longer. Light and dark make no difference to it. Water: a track goes cold in water wider than a bowshot unless it comes out on the same bank within your sight. It rules no mind. A god's track, and the track of anything made or held by more than ordinary craft, is unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Use across a year: limited by the time each following takes and the memory it spends. Information: it shows what happened on the ground, never a cause.
- Intent, your first wording: "Can I not read the passing of a creature on the ground? When I stand at a place a living thing has passed, I may read its track by smell and sign: what kind it was, how long ago, its health, whether it was hunting or hunted, and where it went. I can follow a track that is up to a season old, through rain and over water, as a hound follows a scent. It works on beasts and mortals. It does not work on a thing made or held by more than ordinary craft, and what it does to another god is unruled until it happens."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: The year's age in place of a season's was bought with the weakness The slow wound, under Weaknesses taken.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Held Breath
Status: main
Description: Standing quiet in dusk or shadow, in cover or at the edge of trees, you are not noticed by any beast or mortal who is not already looking at you. In dusk or dark, moving slowly, you may cross open ground unremarked. One who does see you takes you for something smaller: a stray hound, a tall hunter, a trick of the light, and cannot afterward trace the sight to you.
Drawback: The beast stays on you. For as long as you held it, and never less than a day, you have a beast's mouth and paws: you cannot speak words, and your hands cannot grip, tie or cut.
Limits settled at creation:
- It hides the sight of you and of what is on you. It does not hide your tracks, your scent or your sound from a hound or a careful ear. It fails in bright open noon, against anyone already watching you, and against one who knows you and is looking for you. Indoors it needs shadow deep enough to stand in. A crowd gathered to look at you is not fooled. It touches only a mortal's memory of the sight of you and changes nothing else in any mind; what was written down, marked or told to another stands. Against a god, unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption.
- Those who looked at you while you were hidden remember the meeting as a dream by the next dawn, your face first. This is a condition of the power and not its price.
- Intent, your first wording: "Can I not be unseen where the wood stands still? When I stand quiet in dusk or shadow, in cover or at the edge of the trees, no beast or mortal who is not already looking at me will notice me. If I move slowly, I may cross open ground unremarked. When someone does see me, they take me for something smaller: a stray hound, a tall hunter, a trick of the light. A person cannot trace the sight to me afterward."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Hard Bargain
Status: main
Description: Meeting a beast, you offer it a bargain: what you give (a kill, a safe den, a season's guarding) and what you ask (a task, a following, a guard). If it accepts, it holds to its side and you to yours. Not a command. One beast at a time; a bargain lasts up to a season.
Drawback: You keep what you promise, to the letter. Break a bargain and the power is gone from you for a season, and every beast you have ever bargained with knows it and will not treat with you for that season. And while a bargain stands you carry one want of that beast, the one it most shows when the bargain is struck (its hunger at dusk, its fear of fire, its restlessness when the season turns), and it pulls at what you do. The want ends the moment the bargain ends: by completion, by the beast breaking its side, or by the season running out.
Limits settled at creation:
- It works on beasts, the working kinds and the old wary ones. Anything that is a person, whatever its shape, is not a beast: with it you can only talk, as anyone can. A beast that did not agree does not obey. A beast may break its side, and you are then released. One bargain stands at a time. A beast bred, bound or made by more than ordinary craft, and anything of another god's, is unruled until it happens. It cannot be struck through a helper or on behalf of your people's herds: only by you, with the beast in front of you.
- Intent, your first wording: "When I meet an animal and offer it a bargain, it may take it. Not command: bargain. I say what I will give, a kill, a safe den, a season's protection, and what I ask, a task, a following, a guard. If the animal accepts, it holds to its word and I hold to mine. It works on beasts that can work beside a mortal and on the old, wary ones too. One animal at a time. A bargain may last a season."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### Scattering
Status: main
Description: When your body is cut apart, burned or crushed past use, you come apart into small beasts of the place. They scatter, and over a full season they drift to the old quiet wild place nearest where the largest part of you fell, gather there, and stand up as you, whole in body. While scattered you are nothing that knows itself. You do not choose it, and a wound is not a breaking.
Drawback: Each time you stand up again, a memory or a habit does not come back with you, and you are not told which.
Limits settled at creation:
- The return follows the table's rule above: a full season, one item lost; if the nearest old wild place is more than a day off, the walk is added. If what is left of you is shut in (a lidded pit, a closed room, a box, a net drawn tight), the small beasts do not form until it is opened. Small beasts eaten on the way add a season each time most of them are lost, and no other cost. No wild place near, or the small beasts caught and held by more than ordinary craft or by a god: unruled until it happens. What you carried stays where it fell.
- Ground of your own kind is an old quiet wild place.
- Intent, your first wording: "When my body is cut apart, burned, or crushed beyond use, I come apart into small beasts of the place: crows, voles, moths, a hare. They scatter, and over about a season they drift back to the nearest old, quiet wild place and gather there and stand up as me. During the season I am nothing that knows itself. When I stand up, I am whole in body, and I am the same, less one thing."
Rulings: none yet.
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

## Weaknesses taken

**The slow wound.** A wound from fire, or from a mortal's weapon of flint, bone, bronze or wood, does not close until you have spent a full season in stillness in an old wild place at the edge of a wood: not moving on, not hunting, and not meeting a mortal's eye. Until then it stays open, aches and slows you. A season broken by moving, hunting or a mortal's eye begins again. A fall, cold, and the breaking that scatters you do not count. How it can be used against you: anyone who learns it can keep a wound open by keeping you from the wood or by looking you in the eye.
- What it bought: The Old Track follows a track up to a year old, in place of a season.

Kept as color: You do not like an unlit hearth. Color, at no price, buying nothing.
