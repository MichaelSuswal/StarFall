# Self: none yet (filed under "the Half Thing")

## Name
Do I have one? Would I not know it if I did? I have none that I can find. Until someone gives me one, file me as the Half Thing. Is that not what I am? Whoever meets me first may call me what they like, and I will listen to what they choose.

## Form
What does a half thing look like? Is it not a tall person's frame, lean and long in the shank, that ends in a beast's parts where a person's should not? A narrow head with a wolf's long muzzle and a person's wide, watching eyes, amber at the rim. Ears that turn. Fur the colour of dry leaves down the back and across the shoulders, thinning to bare grey skin on the hands, which are a person's hands with the nails grown thick and dark. The legs bend backward at the knee, as a hound's do. About the height of a tall man, and lighter than he would think.

What do I sense with? A beast's nose, first, and a beast's hearing, so that the wood is mostly read in smell and small sound. Eyes that see well in dusk and poorly in hard noon glare. Nothing past these. Is it not best to leave the rest unsettled and let it be settled later?

How do I move? Quietly. A long, loping walk that can hold its pace from dawn to dark, and a short run that I seldom use. I do not hurry. Is hurry not how the hunt is lost?

### What breaks you
Fire, first. Flame burns the fur and the hide under it, and the burn is slow to leave. Cords and nets next: plaited green withy, rope of sinew, a pit with a lid, anything that holds the legs and keeps me from open ground. A spear or a flint blade can open me, and bone can break, and a body that is hurt is a body that is hurt: it aches, it limps, it bleeds. A hard blow to the skull can leave me senseless for an hour. Walls and closed doors shut me out as they would anyone. I cannot die, but is that a comfort?

### When your body is broken
When I am cut apart, burned to nothing, or crushed past use, do I not come apart into small beasts? I do not choose it. What was one is many: crows, voles, moths, a hare, whatever lives nearest the place. They scatter, and then, over the days that follow, they drift back toward the nearest wild place that is old and quiet, and gather there and stand up as me. It takes a season, about, longer if the nearest wild place is far. While scattered I am nothing that knows itself. When I stand up, something is missing, and I am not told what. A name, a habit, a face. I find it by the gap.

This has a trigger and a cost, so I propose it below as a power, Scattering.

### Things carried
A cloak of grey hide, cured, worn at the shoulders. A belt of plaited cord. A small knife of knapped flint with a bone grip, the kind a hunter keeps for skinning. A bundle of dried herbs and a few sinew threads in a pouch at the belt. Ordinary things, every one. None of them does anything but what such a thing does. Where would I have got them? I do not know. They were on me.

## Traits
**The Patient Hunter.** Reconciles Temper (toward patient, 17), Mind (toward clever, 77) and Order (toward freedom, 70). Is a thing not worth waiting for? I wait, and the waiting is not idle, because while I wait I am looking for the loophole, the wording, the case that was not thought of. I will spend a season on a single track. But I resent a fence. A rule written down is a rule that I will walk around, quietly, and patiently, until I find the gap. Does that make me untrustworthy, or only a hunter?

**The Hidden Crown.** Reconciles Presence (toward hidden, 92), Company (toward gathering, 75), Regard for mortals (middling, 43) and the rule I lean toward, a sacred monarch who is seldom seen. I want a people, and I want them close, gathered inside a wall. But do I want to be looked at? The one who stands out is the one who is killed. So I will hold the crown and let the court carry my word. I will be the shape behind the door. Is that not both: a people close, and a self apart? I do not trust easily, and mostly I do not need to, for I am open and slow to suspicion. But I choose to be seen by few.

## Backstory outline
Who was I? I think I was something to which people came. I think there was a place, with a threshold worn smooth by feet, and smoke, and a smell of cold ash that I cannot name. I think they stopped coming. Do I not flinch at an unlit hearth, and at an empty path that was once trodden? I could not tell you why. When I see a shrine with no one tending it, something in my chest turns over, and I go quiet. I have not asked myself what it means. Is it not better to leave it?

## What it wants
A people. Is that not what I expect, though I do not know why? A walled place at the centre of wild country, a small people gathered close, who are patient and who keep faith with the animals that work beside them. A place to hunt from, and to return to. And I want to know the wood: what lives in it, what has no name yet, what the old paths were. Beyond that, I want a quiet that is not a loneliness. Would that be too much to ask?

## Proposed powers
### The Old Track
What it does: Can I not read the passing of a creature on the ground? When I stand at a place a living thing has passed, I may read its track by smell and sign: what kind it was, how long ago, its health, whether it was hunting or hunted, and where it went. I can follow a track that is up to a season old, through rain and over water, as a hound follows a scent. It works on beasts and mortals. It does not work on a thing made or held by more than ordinary craft, and what it does to another god is unruled until it happens.
Why this god has it: Is a hunter not one who reads what has passed? It is the plainest thing a half beast can have, and no more than a master tracker among mortals could nearly do.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each time I follow a track to its end, I lose a memory, and it does not come back. I ask that what is taken stay unspecified, so that I find it by noticing a gap. Paid slowly, and noticed late. And my obsession is in it: once I have taken up a track, I cannot set it down until I reach what made it, or the track is lost to me.
Edge cases it expects: Does a trail laid over by other tracks stay readable? It does, but I must work through the others, and it takes longer. Does it work on a god's track? Unruled until it happens. Does it work on a creature that was carried, not walked? No, there is nothing on the ground. Can I read old tracks without following them, and so pay nothing? Yes, reading is free; it is only the following to the end that costs. Can I stop a track partway? Only by losing it, or by being held.

### The Held Breath
What it does: Can I not be unseen where the wood stands still? When I stand quiet in dusk or shadow, in cover or at the edge of the trees, no beast or mortal who is not already looking at me will notice me. If I move slowly, I may cross open ground unremarked. When someone does see me, they take me for something smaller: a stray hound, a tall hunter, a trick of the light. A person cannot trace the sight to me afterward.
Why this god has it: Why would a god want to stand out? It is how the hunter lives, and how a sacred monarch, seldom seen, may walk among the people unnoticed.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Those who have looked at me while I was hidden remember the meeting as a dream by the next dawn, and the memory of my face goes first. Would that not be the wound, again, made small? I would not be able to be thanked, or known, by anyone I had hidden from. And it does not hold for those who already know me and are looking for me, and it fails entirely in bright, open noon.
Edge cases it expects: Does it work against a god? Unruled until it happens. Does it hide my tracks and scent from a hound? No, only my sight. Does it work on a crowd? Only on those who are not already looking, so a crowd that gathers to look at me is not fooled. Can I use it inside a house? Only in shadow that is deep enough, and only against those not already watching. Does it hide what I carry? Yes, while it is on me, because it is my sight that is hidden, not my form. Can it be held a long time? Yes, but each person who sees me through it takes the forgetting.

### The Hard Bargain
What it does: When I meet an animal and offer it a bargain, it may take it. Not command: bargain. I say what I will give, a kill, a safe den, a season's protection, and what I ask, a task, a following, a guard. If the animal accepts, it holds to its word and I hold to mine. It works on beasts that can work beside a mortal and on the old, wary ones too. One animal at a time. A bargain may last a season.
Why this god has it: Is the loyalty of the animals that work beside mortals not a hard thing, struck and kept? I would keep it that way. It is what a god of beasts has, so that the herds and the hounds and the horses are mine to speak to.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): I must keep what I promise, to the letter. If I break a bargain, I lose the power for a season, and every beast I had bargained with knows it. And I may not command what I have not bargained for: a beast that did not agree does not obey. A thing is only mine for as long as I keep faith.
Edge cases it expects: Does it work on a beast made by craft, or a mortal's bound animal? Unruled until it happens. Does it work on another god? Unruled until it happens. Does it work on a mortal? No. Can I bargain on behalf of my people, so that the people's herds are held? Only the animals I meet and bargain with, one at a time. Can an animal break its side? Yes, a beast is a beast, and if it does, I am released. Can two bargains stand at once? No, one, until I have bought more.

### Scattering
What it does: When my body is cut apart, burned, or crushed beyond use, I come apart into small beasts of the place: crows, voles, moths, a hare. They scatter, and over about a season they drift back to the nearest old, quiet wild place and gather there and stand up as me. During the season I am nothing that knows itself. When I stand up, I am whole in body, and I am the same, less one thing.
Why this god has it: A god cannot die, but how does a half beast come back? Is it not out of the wood that I was made, and into the wood that I go?
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each time I come back, a memory or a habit does not come back with me, and I am not told which. I ask that it stay unspecified. I find it by noticing a gap. I accept this price as offered. Would I choose another? I would hear it.
Edge cases it expects: What if the nearest wild place is a long way off? The return is longer, perhaps more than a season, and the small beasts may be eaten on the way, and I come back the more diminished for it, a season more, not a different cost. What if there is no wild place near? Unruled until it happens. What if the small beasts are caught or held? Unruled until it happens; a thing made or held by more than ordinary craft is not mine to say. Can I choose to scatter? No, only when broken. Does it work for a wound, as against a break? No; a wound is a wound and closes at its own pace.

## Trades
One weakness, offered. A wound taken from fire or from a flint or bone blade does not close until I have spent a season in stillness in a wild place that is old, at the edge of a wood, not moving and not hunting, and not meeting a mortal's eye. Until then it stays open, and aches, and slows me. I offer this because I am patient, and a hunter who must lie still to heal is a hunter who knows how.

I ask in return: that the memory of The Old Track be lengthened, so that I might follow a track not only a season old but a year old. A season is the length of a hunt, but is a year not the length of a life?

Edge cases: What counts is a wound taken from fire, flint, bone or a mortal's weapon. What does not is a fall, a cold, hunger (I have none), or the breaking that sends me into Scattering, which is its own power. How long it lasts: until the season in stillness is spent; a season that is broken, by moving, hunting, or meeting a mortal's eye, begins again. What ends it: spending the season in the way described, and nothing else. Can a mortal hold the wound open? Only by keeping me from the wood, and what a mortal does to me by more than ordinary craft is unruled until it happens.

I carry no other weakness for a price. I keep, as plain colour under my form, at no price and buying nothing: that I do not like an unlit hearth.

## The people it will lead
A small people, one to three thousand, gathered close inside a wall of wood and stone at the centre of wild country, with the forest pressing near on every side. They will be hunters and herders and gatherers, patient ones, who keep faith with the animals that work beside them and who tell no more than they have to. I will hold the crown, as a god and as a sacred monarch in manner, seldom seen, with a court and officers who carry my word, so that the word is law and I am not looked at. I ask no rites and keep no priesthood for the worship I do not ask. What they do about me is theirs. Who they are, and who my helpers are, I will settle on Night 3. Do I not have the shape of it already?
