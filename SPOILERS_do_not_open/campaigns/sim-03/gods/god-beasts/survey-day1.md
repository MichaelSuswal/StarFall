## Answer 1
Have you ever stood at the edge of a wood at dusk, when the animals have stopped speaking and have not yet started again? Is that not the truest hour? A held breath, many eyes, and nothing yet decided.

## Answer 2
Where do the beasts go when they want to be left alone? Forest, old and deep, with a river country at its edge and marsh where the two meet? Is it too much to ask for a place with a wall at its center and wild country pressing close on every side?

## Answer 3
Should the seasons not turn firmly, so that every creature knows what is coming and when to hunt and when to hole up? Four seasons, each with a clear edge, but is a long autumn too much to ask, when the herds move and the hunt is best? When each new year finds me, would I not wish to wake in autumn, with the leaves down and the tracks easy to read?
I wake into autumn.

## Answer 4
Why would I want only one of them? Wild country no one has walked, yes, for the beasts there have never been named, but would I not also want a few old kingdoms rooted far off, so there is someone to trade with and someone to watch? Some of each, but more wild than rooted?

## Answer 5
How long must a story be before a people forget they were ever strangers to the land? Thousands of years, surely, with ruins under ruins? And is it not true that a thing once honored and then forgotten leaves its shape in the ground, whether or not anyone remembers the name?

## Answer 6
Should magic be everywhere, or should it be like a game animal, rare enough that you must learn to find it? Never gone entirely, and never everywhere. A hedge-witch here and there, a rumor most people doubt, and a few who do not?

## Answer 7
What does anything feel like that has teeth? Should it not feel like a hunt, a stalking, a patience that tightens into a leap? And what should it cost, if not something the mortal has been keeping for a long while, a year, a name, a scrap of memory? Would it not be fair that the price is paid slowly, and noticed late?

## Answer 8
Why would a god want to stand out? Is the one-eyed man in the kingdom of the blind not the one the blind come to kill? Should a god be barely more than a great mortal sorcerer, then, or only a little more, so that it may walk among them unnoticed and be mistaken for something smaller?

## Answer 9
Who wants a wild that has been fully named? Familiar animals, mostly, so the hunt means something, but a good number of creatures no one has a name for, in the deep places, where the old ones keep to themselves? Is that not the right measure of strange?

## Answer 10
Is the best danger not the kind you cannot see until it is already beside you? A hunger in the dark, a sickness that follows the herds, a thing in the long grass that learns? And is there not something fearful in being forgotten, in being a people no one remembers, in a land that does not remember them?

## Answer 11
What do mortals want most and have least? Is it not safe harvest, or clean water, or salt, or iron? Would it be too cruel to make it something quieter, like the loyalty of the animals that work beside them, so that every tamed beast is a hard bargain, struck and kept?

## Answer 12
What lies under the ground, if not the things that were once loved and then left? Old roads, certainly, and shrines with no one tending them, and the bones of something enormous, picked clean, with a path worn around them by creatures that still come to look? Would that be so much to ask?

## Answer 13
Should there be others who walk on two legs and are not human, or on four and speak? Yes, a few, and wary ones, with their own customs about the hunt and the dead. Would they not keep to the edges, and trade only with those who show patience?

## Answer 14
Could a traveler not arrive from very far away and be, for a season, the most interesting thing in the land? A few, not many, carrying tools no one here can name, and a knowledge of other beasts and other dead? Should they not be few enough that each one is a story?

## Answer 15
How do wolves know one another across a valley? Is it a pull, faint at a distance, sharpened by nearness, but is it not better that only a face-to-face meeting makes certain? A feeling that something is there, and then, close, the knowing? Would that not leave room for being mistaken, and for hiding?

## Answer 16
Why should a god be spread thin? One body, I would say, and is it not fairer that any second form should cost something real, a part of the first, a hunger, a forgetting? Could a hunter be in two places and still be a hunter?

## Answer 17
Is the mortal worth following not the patient one? The tracker who waits a season for a single shot, the keeper of the dead who knows each name, the child who befriends what others fear? Should such a one rise without a crown, and be tested by what they refuse?

## Answer 18
What can a god discover that it cannot yet imagine? Would it be a companion, a creature that has no kin and no name, that chooses to follow? Or a door under the ground to the place where the forgotten go? Could I not hope to find, at the last, something that remembers?

## Answer 19
Might I wish to be remembered, and does that sound foolish to a god? One wish: that what was once honored and then lost is not lost, only waiting, and that the world, if it is listening, will keep a place for it. Is that too small a thing to ask, or too large?
