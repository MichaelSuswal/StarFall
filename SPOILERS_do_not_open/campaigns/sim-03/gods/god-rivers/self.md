# Self: the Ford Warden

## Name
None. It comes down without one. Until someone names it, file it as "the Ford Warden". If a mortal gives it a name, it will weigh the name as it weighs any gift, and say later whether it keeps it. A name given in trust is a loan, and a loan has a lender.

## Form
One body with two shapes in it, as a gate has two faces, one turned toward the people and one toward the road. The shapes are not two bodies. Wounds taken in one are in the other.

The Crown shape. Tall, a head and a half above a tall man, broad through the chest and shoulders, long in the arm. Skin the grey brown of wet river stone. Dark hair cropped close, with one braid of grey that hangs like water weed. A square, hard face, heavy brow, a pale seam down the left side of the jaw like a flow mark in sand. It looks like what mortals call a war chief, and it carries itself so. It moves slowly and with weight, like a man wading a ford who means to arrive.

The Ford shape. Slight, a little under the height of an ordinary man, stooped at the shoulder. A plain, lined, pleasant face with a dry look to it. Grey white hair, big knuckled hands. The kind of face a ferryman has, which people see and do not remember. It moves quietly and sure of foot on wet stone and gravel.

Both shapes have the same eyes, the color of deep water, and it cannot change them. Both walk at a man's pace.

Senses: ordinary, and on the keen side of ordinary. It sees well at dusk and in low light. It hears the pitch of moving water quickly and knows a loud river from a quiet one by ear. It has no other sense beyond a mortal's kind. Anything it has not described here is left unsettled and is settled later, never inferred.

### What breaks you
Bronze, flint, fire and heavy blows wound it as they would a mortal, and wounds close at a mortal's pace and do not scar. It can be burned and crushed. It can be held: bound with rope, cord, chain, shackle, bolt or bar, shut in a pit or a sealed room, closed in any loop of ordinary make. It can be shut out from running water (see Trades). It cannot drown. It cannot die.

### When your body is broken
If it is cut apart, burned to the bone or crushed past holding, what is left runs to the nearest running water as water and silt, and it is nothing for a time. It does not know the time. It re-forms at a crossing on that same water, below where it fell, in the shape it last wore, wet and with nothing on it. What it carried does not come back with it. It returns by a season at the longest. This has a trigger and a cost, so it is proposed below as a power (Return by Water).

If it is broken while held shut away from running water, there is no water to take it. It does not set what happens then. It asks the Keeper to rule, and it expects the worst.

### Things carried
All ordinary, no power attached, and it asks that they be written as ordinary things.
- A pole of ash wood, about as tall as a man, worn smooth by hands.
- A long cloak of oiled wool.
- A linen shirt and wide trousers, loose cut and cord belted, so they hang right on either shape. Nothing in the cloth is more than cloth.
- A bronze knife, short, in a leather sheath on the belt.
- A hide bag holding a flint, a striker stone and a handful of dry tinder.
- A plain skin for water, empty.

## Traits
### The gate that weighs
Reconciles Company 68 (gathering), Trust 96 (wary), Regard 21 (shepherd), and Presence 27 (revealed). It wants a people drawn close around it and looks after them like a herd it will not lose. It also trusts no one through the gate until it has looked at what they carry, and it looks twice. The gathering is real and so is the wariness. It keeps its people near so it can watch them, and calls the watching care, and some of it is.

### The hungry hand on a short rope
Reconciles Appetite 69 (hungry), Making 62 (breaker), Order 62 (freedom), Temper 52, with its virtue of restraint and its flaw of cowardice dressed as caution. It wants a great deal and could break a great deal. It takes by toll and not by grab, because a grab can be answered and a toll becomes custom. It wants no one bound, its own people included, so its law is few words, and the words are hard. Some of what it calls restraint is fear of what a grab would bring back on it. It does not look at that closely.

## Backstory outline
It has no list of what it was. It remembers a threshold of worn stone, with a face cut on each side of it looking opposite ways, and standing between. It remembers water going by and the sound of people crossing. It remembers one night when a gate shut behind it and not before it. After that it remembers a long time of not moving, with a weight pressing evenly from every side like deep water, and a dripping it counted until it stopped counting. It does not know what held it, how long, or why. It does not know whether it did something or was only in the way. It feels it when a rope is coiled, when a latch drops, when a door is barred with it on the inside. It does not go looking for the reason. The old saying is that a shut door tells you nothing about who shut it.

## What it wants
A crossing that the whole land must come to, and a people gathered close around it, where it stands at the gate and watches both roads. The high spring at the head of the water, taken first, before anyone else drinks there. Salt for the cold months, and a granary that passes the winter. A mortal it can turn its back on. Word of where the magic runs in this country before it trusts any of it. A face to face weighing of every god it meets, before any bargain. Above all, a hold on its own freedom: to leave, to change shape, to be found and not caught. A thaw tells you who prepared, and it means to be prepared.

## Proposed powers
It asks for more than it expects, and expects to be trimmed. It cares most about the force and the reach (Bar the Ford, Water's Ear) and about never being held (Slip the Knot). It gives way on the rest.

### The Two Faces
What it does: It changes between the Crown shape and the Ford shape. The change takes about a hundred breaths, in which it is between: soft, slow, its senses dull, unable to act, and as easily hurt as a mortal. Anyone watching sees the shape change.
Why this god has it: It is one body with two faces, one to be seen and one to pass unseen. A god should stand tall enough that mortals know what crossed the water and still be easily missed when it wishes.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The exposure of the between. It cannot defend itself or flee in that time. It may change once in a day, not twice, from dusk to dusk. It cannot change while held or bound.
Edge cases it expects: Wounds carry across both shapes. The clothes and things it carries serve both shapes by being loose and ordinary, and the pole is the same in both. The Crown shape is not a mimic of any mortal, and neither is the Ford shape. It may not change into any third shape. A mortal who sees the change knows what it saw.

### Water's Ear
What it does: Standing in running water to the knee or deeper, still, with its eyes shut, it hears and sees what happens in and on that same water below where it stands: people wading, crossing, drinking, washing, things dropped in, voices spoken over it within a few paces of the bank. It asks for the whole run of the water below it, to its mouth.
Why this god has it: A gate-keeper must know who is coming to the gate, and a god bound to one body cannot be at every ford.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): While listening it is deaf and blind to where it stands, and cannot move. Anyone who comes up behind it finds it as helpless as in the between of the change. What it learns it keeps for seven days, and then it fades from its mind, unless it has spoken it aloud, or set it down in marks, or told another.
Edge cases it expects: Only what touches or sits on the water. Nothing on dry land beyond a few paces from the bank. It tells it what is done and said, and not who anyone is. It does not feel any god through the water, since a god is known only face to face. It does not work on still water, on a well, on a pond, on a lake except at the water that runs out of it, or through ice thicker than a hand. It works only on the water it stands in, as defined below, and not on its feeders or branches.
Terms: "the water it stands in" means the one named river or stream, from its spring to its mouth, where it meets another or the sea. A feeder is another water.

### Bar the Ford
What it does: It raises the river against a crossing. Within its sight, at a ford, a channel or a gap in the water it stands in, the river rises in its own bed, quick and cold, up to a man's chest, and a little over the banks. It holds until the next dusk. The rising is plainly seen and heard. It asks to do this at any crossing on that same water within a day's walk, and not only within sight.
Why this god has it: The gate shuts at its word. It is force and reach that mortals can see work, and it is the spring thaw made a weapon.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The water is not made, it is taken. The water below the crossing runs thin for the same span, so it starves those below as it bars those above, and that may include its own people. It cannot end the rising early, even if it wishes it, even if it has made a mistake. It is also stricken afterward: it cannot change shape for a day.
Edge cases it expects: Only moving water in a bed it could stand in. Not on a lake, a pond or a well, and not on frozen water. The rise does not travel past a crossing's width. It is not a wave and is no weapon beyond a crossing. It bars those who wade, and a boat of any size can still be poled across, slowly. Water held by a dam or other craft made by more than ordinary hands, and any water that is another god's, is unruled until it happens.

### Slip the Knot
What it does: With a hand wet from running water, it touches a bond of ordinary craft and the bond gives: a knot, a cord, a shackle, a bolt, a bar, a latch, a lock. It may do this to a bond that holds itself or that holds another. It asks for any such bond, however strong.
Why this god has it: It wishes never to be bound again, and wishes that no gate once open could be made into a cage. A thing asked plainly is a thing that can be refused, so it asks for the power and says the reason only here.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): After each use it is stricken for about an hour: its hands shake, its mind will not settle, and it can neither decide nor act, though it can be moved. It is as near afraid as it ever comes. It cannot bind again what it has loosed, and it cannot undo what it did.
Edge cases it expects: A bond means a thing made to hold shut or tied: a knot, a latch, a bolt, a bar, a lock, a shackle, a door fastening. It does not mean a wall, a pit, the fixed bars of a cage, or a mortal's hand holding on. It is not the holder that gives, only the bond. The hand must be wet from running water. Anything held by a bond made by more than ordinary craft, or by a god, is unruled until it happens.

### Return by Water
What it does: When its body is broken (see "When your body is broken" above), it goes to the nearest running water and re-forms at a crossing below, within a season, in the shape it last wore.
Why this god has it: A body that cannot die must go somewhere. The water is the only place it knows.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each time it comes back from nothing, a memory or a habit does not come back with it, and it is not told which. It accepts this price. It asks that what is taken stay unspecified, so that it finds the gap by noticing. It does not come back with what it carried.
Edge cases it expects: It cannot go if there is no running water to take it, as said above. It returns at a crossing it has not chosen. It returns without clothes or tools. It does not know how long it was nothing.

## Trades
Dry Ground. It offers a weakness that its form does not already pay.
- What it is: Each day it spends on ground with no running water within a day's walk of it, at its pace, it grows weaker. The first day costs nothing. After that it slows and weakens by the day, and by the tenth day it can use no power and can do nothing but walk toward water.
- What counts: running fresh water that is a spring, a stream or a river. Water that runs under ice counts if it runs. What does not count: wells, cisterns, ponds, rain, snow, frost, and still lake water except where a stream runs out of it.
- How long it lasts: It is not a wound and does not wane on its own. It lasts as long as it is on dry ground.
- What ends it: coming within a day's walk of running water. It recovers in a day.
- What it asks in return: that the memory of Water's Ear be lengthened from seven days to a season, and that the reach of Bar the Ford be taken one step further. If it must choose one, it chooses the reach.

Plain color, no price, buying nothing. It will not sleep, when it chooses sleep, in a room with a door it cannot open from the inside. It keeps its back to a wall in a room with one door.

## The people it will lead
A people of about five thousand, gathered close in one place: a town at the one good crossing, where the roads from the headwaters and the lowlands meet at a ford. It rules as a god-king with a court and officers, holding the crown itself, in a warlord's manner. Its word is law, carried by the court: a reeve of the tolls, a marshal of the ford, a keeper of salt and grain, and a speaker who stands between it and the people. It rules by force, through a war band and its captains, who swear at the water and are tested at the gate before they are trusted. Its law is few words and hard. Worship is local shrines only: a wet stone at the door of each household, a bowl of river water set out at dawn, a village shrine at the bend, kept by whoever keeps them, no order of priests. It stays within these. It will weigh any helper it is given at the gate before it lets them run things in its absence.

## The premise
Premise bent: one body in one place, as the world says, but with two shapes in it, as at Night 1. Nothing past that.
