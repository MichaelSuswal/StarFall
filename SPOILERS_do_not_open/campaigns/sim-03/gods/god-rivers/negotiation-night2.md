## Round 1 reply

### Definition: the water you stand in
Accept. It is the definition I asked for, and the table's wording on running water, wells and lake outflows matches what I meant. A feeder being another water is a loss for reach, and I will not argue it. A gate has one road through it at a time.

### The Two Faces
Accept, as written, with the Ford face made plain. I did not mean the forgettable face as a power, only as a shape of face. A man who has reason to look will remember a ferryman, and I would have it so. The once between dusks is a fair price for a thing that, in the between, leaves me as open as a door with no hinge.

### Water's Ear
Counter within the choices.

Reach: I take (A), the long and plain reach. Numbers, boats and loads, voices raised or low, along the whole run below me. I want the reach before the words. A gate-warden who knows that three boats and a crowd of voices are coming down the river a day out can close the gate before they arrive, and I would give up the words said at one ford for that. I accept the trim on sight, since sight along the whole river would have settled too much for me to ask it.

Price: I take (2), the water keeps my hearing, and refuse (1). Being unable to go out of sight of the water is a leash, and a leash is a hold by another name. I would be held by my own power, and that is the one thing I will not buy. Deafness on dry land is a cost I can see coming, and I would notice it. I can choose when to listen, and so when to be deaf. I will listen with my own people at my back, and will not listen on a night I do not trust the road. It is a real price. The deaf are easy to come up on, and I am not easy in my mind about it.

On the Keeper's remark that the seven days is a fading I could end by talking to myself: that is so, and I do not offer it as a price. I withdraw it as a drawback and take the Keeper's price (2) in its place.

### Bar the Ford
Accept, within sight, as written. The thin water below the crossing, my own people included, is a cost that I will feel every time, and I know it. I do not ask to move it. The reach beyond sight is argued below, under the weakness.

### Slip the Knot
Accept, as written. The wet hand is a fair fence. I see what it means: held away from running water, I am held like anyone, and that is a cold thing to read. I take it, since a power that is useless exactly when I need it most is still a power I would notice losing, and a hundred breaths of wet hand is enough to walk from a river to a barred door. I do not ask for more.

### Return by Water
Accept, with the pace choice (A): a full season, one item lost each time.

Reason: I would sooner be nothing for a season than lose two things each time. A season of being nothing is a season I do not feel. A lost item I will notice, in time, as a gap, and I want fewer gaps. I accept that the Keeper takes the item by lot from my record of gods or my goals, and that I am not told. I accept that I do not choose the crossing, that I return with nothing, and that remains kept from running water wait where they lie. That last is the thing I most dislike. I do not argue it, since the table gives it to every god, and I note it only so that it is written that I dislike it.

### The weakness you offer, and what it buys
Counter. I refuse (A) as drafted and take (B) if no counter is agreed.

On (A): the measure of sight or sound of running water, with no power on the third day, hands a mortal who learns it a way to hold me. I asked in the survey for a world where no god could be held against its will. A mortal who can pen me a few hundred paces from a stream, and who then needs only to wait three days, is holding me with a line, and the line is the one I most dread. Slip the Knot cannot help me there, since it needs running water on my hand. I would be buying reach for Bar the Ford with the one thing I have said I will not sell. Three days is too short. A mortal who learned it could do the work in the time it takes to build a hut.

The counter. Keep the measure the Keeper offers: sight or sound of running water, not a day's walk. Change the days. The first two days out of sight and sound cost me nothing. From the third day I weaken by the day. By the sixth day I can use no power and can do nothing but go toward water. It ends a day after I am within sight or sound of running water. That is a week of keeping, and a mortal who wishes to hold me must hold a god for six days, in the open or in a house, and have it known, and my people, if I have any who run things, will have a half a week to find me. It is still a line a mortal who knows it can hold against me. I say so plainly and I accept it. It is a real price, and the Keeper is right that the first offer was not.

What it buys: I name the reach of Bar the Ford. With it, the river rises against any crossing on the water I stand in where I have stood before, within a morning's walk (about eight miles), seen or not. I choose this and not the season of memory for Water's Ear. The seven days I can mend by speaking it or setting it down in marks. The reach I cannot mend any other way, and reach is the thing I most want.

If the Keeper will not move the three days to six, I take (B), the weakness as plain color buying nothing, and keep Bar the Ford within sight. I would sooner have less reach than a shorter leash. The door I can open from the inside stays as color either way.

## Round 2 reply

I confirm the final text as the card, with the wording corrections below. None of them asks for a change of substance. Each is a clarification in the write-up, not a new power.

### Confirmed as written
The definitions, The Two Faces, Water's Ear (reach A, price 2, the seven days kept as how the power works), Bar the Ford with the reach bought by Dry Ground, Slip the Knot, Return by Water (a full season, one item lost each time), Dry Ground with two free days and no power by the sixth, and the door kept as color. I take the six days as granted and I take the line it leaves a mortal, as I said I would. I thank the Keeper for the reasons given. They were plain, and I can work with plain.

### Wording corrections

1. When your body is broken, in the Form section. It says "It returns by a season at the longest." The power below says "after a full season", and the table sells nothing faster than a month. The two lines disagree. I ask that the Form line read "It returns after a full season, as the power below says", so that no one reads the Form as a promise of less. My own first wording said "at the longest", and I would not have it stand against the card.

2. Dry Ground and Water's Ear, together. Dry Ground counts "sight or sound of running water". The price of Water's Ear leaves me deaf on dry land for at least a day. I ask for one line in the write-up: the measure is whether running water is near enough to be heard, and does not depend on whether I can hear. A day of deafness beside a stream does not start the count of days. I ask this because otherwise the two cost me twice for one thing, and because I would not want a mortal to learn that the way to hold me is to make me listen first.

3. Bar the Ford. The Description opens "Standing in the water", and the Limits say the water you stand in. I read it as the same: I stand in the one river or stream, and the crossing I bar, near or far, is on that same river or stream. I ask that this be said once, so that no one reads it as standing in any water and barring any other.

### The Keeper trait
I accept it. Running water going quiet around me when I am afraid is a true thing about me, and I have known it since I came down. It is the sort of tell that cowardice dressed as caution would most wish to hide, and I say so, so that it is written that I dislike it. I ask for three clarifications of wording only:
- It is the running water within a few paces of me that goes quiet, and no more of the river.
- It lasts while the fear lasts and ends with it. After Slip the Knot, when I am stricken for about an hour, it holds for that hour.
- It is a sign and no power. It does not touch Water's Ear, Bar the Ford or any other power, and no mortal is ruled by it. One who knows rivers may hear it and make of it what they will.

I have no further asks. I do not ask to reopen anything.
