## Answer 1
A ford at dusk, with water moving under it and both banks in shadow. Still enough to cross, quick enough that no one forgets the crossing. A world where every road comes to a gate, and every gate has someone watching it.

## Answer 2
River country. Springs in the high places, braided streams, wide slow water at the bottom, and a single good crossing that the whole land must come to. Still water breeds rot, so I want the water moving. Where the river bends, people gather, and where people gather, someone must stand at the gate.

## Answer 3
Four seasons, and none of them kind to the careless. A hard winter that tests the granary, a loud spring when the ice breaks and the rivers rise, a long summer, and a lean autumn that rewards those who laid by. Rivers that flood on schedule and rarely off it. A saying has it that the thaw tells you who prepared. I would wake into the spring, when the ice goes out of the water. I wake into spring.

## Answer 4
Some of each, though I lean to the rooted. Wild country is only a road not yet taxed. Old kingdoms already established are neighbors worth watching, and a neighbor you can watch is safer than a neighbor you cannot. Keep a few wild stretches near the headwaters, for the springs are there, and I want no one else drinking at the source first.

## Answer 5
Ruins under ruins. A land that has been lost and found many times. Many a field has been plowed over a dead man's door. The deeper the story, the more there is to take, and the more there is to be wary of.

## Answer 6
A wise-woman in some valleys, a rumor in others. Magic never gone and never everywhere. The well that serves everyone is the first one poisoned, so let it be uneven, and let me learn where it runs before I trust it.

## Answer 7
It should feel like a current under the skin, a pull that can be followed but not held. It should cost them. Something returned to the water: a year, a name, a warm hand, a memory. A mortal who borrows should feel the debt in the body, and no one should be able to pay it twice.

## Answer 8
Premise bent: I would have a god stand out, but not so far that every eye finds it. Tall enough that mortals know what crossed the water, not so tall that they never stop looking. A god should be a great deal more than a sorcerer and still be easily missed by those who do not know to look. Let the gap be real, and let it be possible to hide.

## Answer 9
Familiar beasts and a good many legends, and at the edges a few things with no name. Let some of the legends be true. A river should hold something old at the bottom that no one has a word for. The unnamed things should be rare, and wary of us as we are of them.

## Answer 10
Betrayal from within. A gate left open at night by the one who was trusted to shut it. Mortals should fear the neighbor with a grievance, the winter that outlasts the grain, and the thing that wears a friend's face. Walls fail from inside before they fail from out.

## Answer 11
Salt, and trust. Salt for the cold months and the keeping of meat. Trust is scarcer than salt, and dearer, and no one can trade for it, only earn it slowly and lose it at once. A people with salt and without trust is a poor people.

## Answer 12
Sealed doors, and old roads under the river mud. Roads that were built to reach somewhere, and a door at the end of one that is still shut. I would like something buried that was meant to hold, and that has been holding a long time.

## Answer 13
Yes, a few. Not many, and not friendly by default. A river folk who keep to the reeds and trade by night, and speak little. A people of the high rock, hard to deal with and harder to cheat. I want them to have their own laws, and to keep to them, so a bargain with them means something.

## Answer 14
Few. One or two travelers at a time, arriving in the wrong season, with a tool or a story that does not fit the land. I would welcome them at the gate and look at what they carry before I look at what they say. Strange knowledge is a gift, and gifts have a price someone has not named yet.

## Answer 15
Face to face only. A knowing at the threshold, when both stand at the same gate, and not before. A pull at any distance is a leash, and I have had enough of those. I would sooner meet a god and weigh it than feel it from afar and be weighed.

## Answer 16
Premise bent: one body, but two forms in it, the way a gate has two faces. Not a second body in a second place. If a god can stand in many places, it can be broken in many, and I have no wish to be found in pieces. Let the cost of changing from one form to the other be real, a moment of exposure while the shape is between.

## Answer 17
A gate-warden, a ferryman, a river-pilot. A mortal who knows the crossings and who comes and goes. One who keeps an oath when the oath is costly, and who knows when not to swear. A clever one, not a loud one, with a good memory and a dry sense of humor.

## Answer 18
A companion who does not need to be watched. Something I can turn my back on. And, failing that, a spring no one has found, at the very top, where the water comes out of the dark clean and cold and belongs to no one yet.

## Answer 19
I wish never to be bound again. I wish that no god, mine or another's, could be held against its will, and that a gate once open could not be made into a cage. I do not ask for it plainly because a thing asked plainly is a thing that can be refused.
