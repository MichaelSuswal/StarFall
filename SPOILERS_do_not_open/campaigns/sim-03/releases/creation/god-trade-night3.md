# Release: god-trade, Night 3

This release is your whole instruction for tonight and your go for exactly the file it names.

## Why you come down
Gods come down here to lead a people: to find or gather them, to build, to
rule, and to spread. That is what the world below expects of you.

## The land you come down into

### The season
Autumn.

### The country
Low river country running down to a coast. A wide river and its lesser streams go slowly through water meadow and worked land to the sea, shallow enough in places to be forded, and there is a shore where the river meets salt water, with shelter for boats. The land is open and long to the eye, with stands of willow and alder along the water.

It is the season of reckoning. The fields are stubble, the meadows are cut, and the river is low before the rains.

### What the land shows of people
Roads, long and rutted, that come down to the fords from more than one side. Smoke in several places, far apart. Stubble and stooks. Poles set in the water at a ford. Drag marks of keels on the shore.

That is all the land shows from where you come down. Nothing here says who made these things. There is no distance, direction or line of sight in this: those come from the map, after it is drawn. Nothing of the past, and nothing of anyone or anything else that came down.

## What is so of the world for a people
The peoples of this world are at about this level: before the medieval era, no steel forges.

Mortal magic: it is simple, and it costs the worker. The Keeper fits a people's magic to the world tonight.

## The people you lead
You come to a people with a seat, a court or council, and helpers who run
things when you are not there: about five thousand, with a town or city.
Who are they? What kind of people, how they live in country like this, what they do with their days, and who runs things for you when you are not there?

Rule: a god-king with a court and priesthood, a warlord in manner. The god holds the crown itself, with a court and officers; its word is law, carried by them. A priesthood stands in it only as the worship lean gives one. As a warlord, it rules by force, through its war band and captains.
Worship: a priesthood. Keepers of rite with standing among the people, offerings brought, no tithe by law.
Build within these, or give your reasons not to.

A larger people is not priced against a weakness; it costs what the world makes it cost (more
food and fuel, slower growth, harder to move, a longer gestation for a larger
kind of people).

## Options
Each is open to you once, on the same terms as to any god. None is advised and none is expected.
- Begin wherever you like and say as much as you like. You are told what a thing costs before it is theirs.
- Helpers are offices, not named persons: a court, a council of elders or heads of houses, priests, stewards, captains, or a single steward, in any mix and at any size. With each goes the question of how orders travel: who hears you, and who carries it on. Whoever holds an office is a mortal with their own wants; none is a puppet. What helpers do is act for you inside your standing conduct and your last decisions when you are away.
- Everything in how a people lives is ordinary and costs nothing: its work, its customs, its manners, its code, how it buries its dead, how it is ordered and how it settles disputes.
- A craft your people are proud of may be left as their craft and their pride, with its worth beside other peoples' left to the world. If you want it to be a sure edge over other peoples, say so and it is priced against a weakness of theirs.
- You may ask for an edge stronger than it should be and have it priced. An ask may be refused as asked and priced in a smaller shape; you may take it, argue it or leave it.
- An edge that has a mortal do what only you can do is priced on you, as a power added to your card tonight, and it may not undo a limit agreed when your powers were priced; a version that keeps the limit can be priced. For size only: one holder of one office who, once a season, by a power of yours, may leave you one wordless thing where you will find it; the mortal pays in the body as mortal workers here pay, you pay something more at the taking up, it is always the mortal's choice, it is that mortal's alone, and it ends with the office or the life.
- Their magic may be described by you or left to the world. A particular mortal with a knack is priced, and fitted to the world by the Keeper.
- What they believe about gods before you may be described by you or left to the world, with a wish of yours kept on record.
- How you meet them is yours to say: they are already there with their own past, or waiting for something, or they arrived with you. What they know of you on the first morning, and what your helpers know, is part of it. Where you come down and what the first morning holds are the world's; how you mean to go about it is recorded as your conduct.
- Who settles a quarrel between two families, a theft or a boundary: say who, or leave it to the world.
- How long they have been in this country by their own telling, and what they say was there before them: say it, or leave both to the world.
- Anything you say about the land or its past (what a track is, what was here before, what lies beyond) is recorded as your people's belief or as your wish, not as fact. The world decides, and you will see.
- You set who a mortal is, never what that mortal will do. What you hope a mortal will do is heard and not promised.
- Their name, and the name of their home, are yours to give or to leave unnamed. Any office-holder, house or place of theirs you may name or leave unnamed. "Unnamed" and "leave it to the world" are full answers.
