## Round 1

### How the table prices two things for every god
These two are not offers. They are how this table prices the same thing for any god, and they are written into your card where they apply.
- A loss of memory or habit. Where a drawback takes "a memory or a habit" and you are not told which, the Keeper removes one item from your own record (your record of other gods or your goals) for each time the price falls due, chosen by lot among the items, and you are not told what went. You asked that it stay unspecified; it does.
- A return from a broken body. A full season, at the nearest ground of your own kind to where the largest part of you lies, is paid for by one item lost. A return in a month is paid for by two items lost each time. The table sells nothing faster than a month. If the nearest ground of your kind is more than a day off, the walk to it at your own pace is added to the time. Being held, bound, buried or shut in is not being broken: you stay where you are. Remains kept from the ground of your kind do not return until it reaches them or they are let out. What another god does to your remains is unruled until it happens.

### The Old Track
Draft text:
Description: Standing where a living thing has passed, you read its track by smell and sign: what kind it was, how long ago, its health, whether it hunted or was hunted, and which way it went. You can follow a track up to a season old, through rain and over water, as a hound follows a scent. It works on beasts and on mortals. You read what a body leaves on the ground and nothing else: what passed and where, never why.
Drawback: Each time you follow a track to its end, a memory leaves you and does not come back, and you are not told which. And a track once taken up holds you: see Choices.
Limits: Reading where you stand costs nothing; only following to the end costs. One track at a time. A creature that was carried left nothing. A thing that leaves neither scent nor sign gives you nothing. A trail crossed by others stays readable and takes longer. Light and dark make no difference to it. Water: a track goes cold in water wider than a bowshot unless it comes out on the same bank within your sight. It rules no mind. A god's track, and the track of anything made or held by more than ordinary craft, is unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Use across a year: limited by the time each following takes and the memory it spends. Information: it shows what happened on the ground, never a cause.
Choices: how the track holds you. (A) As you wrote it: once you take up a track you cannot set it down until you reach what made it or the track is lost to you; only being held stops you. (B) You may set a track down by your own will, and that creature's track is then closed to you for good: you can never read or follow that one creature again.
The Keeper's reasons: A master tracker reads much of this; what is a god's is the season's age and the rain and water. The memory is a price you would notice, and the rate is your own words, one for each track followed to its end. The hold is your own flaw written down; by itself a flaw restated buys nothing, so it stands beside the memory and not in place of it, and you may have it either way.

### The Held Breath
Draft text:
Description: Standing quiet in dusk or shadow, in cover or at the edge of trees, you are not noticed by any beast or mortal who is not already looking at you. In dusk or dark, moving slowly, you may cross open ground unremarked. One who does see you takes you for something smaller: a stray hound, a tall hunter, a trick of the light, and cannot afterward trace the sight to you.
Drawback: See Choices. The drawback you offered (those who saw you while hidden remember it as a dream, your face first) is written as a condition of the power, at no price.
Limits: It hides the sight of you and of what is on you. It does not hide your tracks, your scent or your sound from a hound or a careful ear. It fails in bright open noon, against anyone already watching you, and against one who knows you and is looking for you. Indoors it needs shadow deep enough to stand in. A crowd gathered to look at you is not fooled. It touches only a mortal's memory of the sight of you and changes nothing else in any mind; what was written down, marked or told to another stands. Against a god, unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption.
Choices: the price. (A) The forgetting spreads. On any day you hold the Breath, every mortal who met you that day, hidden or in the open, your own court among them, has the meeting as a dream by the next dawn: what you said to them that day is half kept at best, and your face is gone from it. (B) The beast stays on you. For as long as you held it, and never less than a day, you have a beast's mouth and paws: you cannot speak words, and your hands cannot grip, tie or cut.
The Keeper's reasons: Being forgotten by those you hid from is what a hidden thing wants; it costs you nothing you would notice, so it is a condition and not a price. A price has to be something you would miss. (A) costs the one who rules through a court; (B) costs the one who hunts and speaks.

### The Hard Bargain
Draft text:
Description: Meeting a beast, you offer it a bargain: what you give (a kill, a safe den, a season's guarding) and what you ask (a task, a following, a guard). If it accepts, it holds to its side and you to yours. Not a command. One beast at a time; a bargain lasts up to a season.
Drawback: You keep what you promise, to the letter. Break a bargain and the power is gone from you for a season, and every beast you have ever bargained with knows it and will not treat with you for that season. And see Choices.
Limits: It works on beasts, the working kinds and the old wary ones. Anything that is a person, whatever its shape, is not a beast: with it you can only talk, as anyone can. A beast that did not agree does not obey. A beast may break its side, and you are then released. One bargain stands at a time. A beast bred, bound or made by more than ordinary craft, and anything of another god's, is unruled until it happens. It cannot be struck through a helper or on behalf of your people's herds: only by you, with the beast in front of you.
Choices: the standing price. (A) You pay first and keep off its kind: what you promised is given in full by your own hand before the beast serves, and while the bargain stands you may not hunt or harm any beast of that kind. (B) You take on one of its wants: while the bargain stands you carry one want of that beast (its hunger at dusk, its fear of fire, its restlessness when the season turns), and it pulls at what you do.
The Keeper's reasons: "Keep your word or lose the power" is a price only a careless god pays, and you are not careless. A drawback is something that comes with every use. Either choice does.

### Scattering
Draft text:
Description: When your body is cut apart, burned or crushed past use, you come apart into small beasts of the place. They scatter, and over a full season they drift to the old quiet wild place nearest where the largest part of you fell, gather there, and stand up as you, whole in body. While scattered you are nothing that knows itself. You do not choose it, and a wound is not a breaking.
Drawback: Each time you stand up again, a memory or a habit does not come back with you, and you are not told which.
Limits: The return follows the table's rule above: a full season, one item lost; if the nearest old wild place is more than a day off, the walk is added. If what is left of you is shut in (a lidded pit, a closed room, a box, a net drawn tight), the small beasts do not form until it is opened. Small beasts eaten on the way add a season each time most of them are lost, and no other cost. No wild place near, or the small beasts caught and held by more than ordinary craft or by a god: unruled until it happens. What you carried stays where it fell.
Choices: the pace. (A) A full season, one item lost each time. (B) A month, two items lost each time.
The Keeper's reasons: The price you accepted is the one the table sets for a season. It is no way out of being held.

### The weakness you offer, and what it buys
Draft text:
The slow wound. A wound from fire, or from a mortal's weapon of flint, bone, bronze or wood, does not close until you have spent a full season in stillness in an old wild place at the edge of a wood: not moving on, not hunting, and not meeting a mortal's eye. Until then it stays open, aches and slows you. A season broken by moving, hunting or a mortal's eye begins again. A fall, cold, and the breaking that scatters you do not count. How it can be used against you: anyone who learns it can keep a wound open by keeping you from the wood or by looking you in the eye. What it buys: The Old Track follows a track up to a year old.
The Keeper's reasons: Part of this your form already pays, since you wrote that a burn is slow to leave, and that part buys nothing. The part you do not already pay is the weapon wounds, the season lost to stillness, and the handle it gives a mortal. That is enough for the year. It is accepted as offered. The unlit hearth stays as color at no price.

## Form

One body, in one place, as for every god here (the campaign allows one body).

As you described yourself, in your own words:

What does a half thing look like? Is it not a tall person's frame, lean and long in the shank, that ends in a beast's parts where a person's should not? A narrow head with a wolf's long muzzle and a person's wide, watching eyes, amber at the rim. Ears that turn. Fur the colour of dry leaves down the back and across the shoulders, thinning to bare grey skin on the hands, which are a person's hands with the nails grown thick and dark. The legs bend backward at the knee, as a hound's do. About the height of a tall man, and lighter than he would think.

What do I sense with? A beast's nose, first, and a beast's hearing, so that the wood is mostly read in smell and small sound. Eyes that see well in dusk and poorly in hard noon glare. Nothing past these. Is it not best to leave the rest unsettled and let it be settled later?

How do I move? Quietly. A long, loping walk that can hold its pace from dawn to dark, and a short run that I seldom use. I do not hurry. Is hurry not how the hunt is lost?

### What breaks you
Fire, first. Flame burns the fur and the hide under it, and the burn is slow to leave. Cords and nets next: plaited green withy, rope of sinew, a pit with a lid, anything that holds the legs and keeps me from open ground. A spear or a flint blade can open me, and bone can break, and a body that is hurt is a body that is hurt: it aches, it limps, it bleeds. A hard blow to the skull can leave me senseless for an hour. Walls and closed doors shut me out as they would anyone. I cannot die, but is that a comfort?

### When your body is broken
When I am cut apart, burned to nothing, or crushed past use, do I not come apart into small beasts? I do not choose it. What was one is many: crows, voles, moths, a hare, whatever lives nearest the place. They scatter, and then, over the days that follow, they drift back toward the nearest wild place that is old and quiet, and gather there and stand up as me. It takes a season, about, longer if the nearest wild place is far. While scattered I am nothing that knows itself. When I stand up, something is missing, and I am not told what. A name, a habit, a face. I find it by the gap.

This has a trigger and a cost, so I propose it below as a power, Scattering.

### Things carried (as you gave them; all ordinary unless a power below says otherwise)
A cloak of grey hide, cured, worn at the shoulders. A belt of plaited cord. A small knife of knapped flint with a bone grip, the kind a hunter keeps for skinning. A bundle of dried herbs and a few sinew threads in a pouch at the belt. Ordinary things, every one. None of them does anything but what such a thing does. Where would I have got them? I do not know. They were on me.
