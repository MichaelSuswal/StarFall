# Negotiation: god-stone, Night 3

Your people, priced. You proposed one edge and one weakness, and a ring of holds in place of one seat. Everything else you wrote is ordinary life and costs nothing: the circuit, the march order, the Code, the stone craft (left as their pride, its worth the world's), the cairn signs, the war bands, the shrines, the offices. Your people's name, the Column, stands as you gave it. What you left to the world (their magic, what they believe about gods, what the court knows on the first morning, whose the trodden way and the cairns are) is fitted tonight and you will see it.

## Round 1

### A ring of holds in place of one seat
Draft text: The Column has no town. It has a ring of winter holds, three or four, and stops only in winter. Your reason (no hold worn out, no valley eaten bare) is taken and the ring stands.
The Keeper's reasons: A people ordinarily comes with a town, and you have turned that down. Nothing is charged for it. It counts in your favor in the price below. Its cost is practical and is said plainly: there is no market others come to and no wall the whole people can stand behind; work that wants a fixed hearth the year round (charcoal, smelting, casting, a store of seasoned timber) is done only at the holds and only by the few households who stay; and when you are away from the Column, your word reaches it only at a halt, from an officer who is there.

### Pass Craft
Draft text: On mountain ground (pass, scree, rotten snow, high ridge), the whole Column on the move, with its old, its young, its beasts and its stores, travels better than any other whole people would. See Choices for the measure. It holds only on mountain ground, only for the Column moving in its march order with its Pass Wardens ahead, and only on ways that carry its cairn signs or that its Pass Wardens have read within the season. It adds nothing in a fight, on low ground, on a plain or in forest. A band of the Column without Pass Wardens moves as any band does.
Choices: the measure. The pace table moves a whole people at half the foot rate, and a warband at four fifths of it.
(A) Pace. On mountain ground the whole Column moves at four fifths of the foot rate, as a warband does, in every season the pass is open. Its losses to the mountain are those of any host.
(B) Losses. On mountain ground the whole Column moves at two thirds of the foot rate, and what it loses to the mountain in a crossing (people and beasts, to fall, cold, slide and rotten snow) is half what another host of its size would lose.
The Keeper's reasons: You asked for twice the pace and a tenth of the losses and said you expected a trim. Both at once would make the mountain no mountain for five thousand people, and every other people that crosses a pass pays it in full. Four fifths against a half is already more than half again as fast. Half the losses is the most a craft can buy against a slope that goes whether or not it is read. One or the other. The edge scales across the whole people by its nature, which is why it is held to one kind of ground.

### Few Holds
Draft text: All of the Column's winter grain and winter shelter are in its three or four rock holds, fixed, and known to anyone who has followed the Column for one round of the circuit. The Column cannot winter anywhere else: a winter caught outside the holds costs it one person in ten and one beast in four. The loss of one hold (taken, flooded, burned or fouled) takes a third of the winter's grain and a third of its shelter. In the open season each hold is kept by no more than twenty households and no band. In the cold months a fifth of those who bear arms sit at the holds and march nowhere.
The Keeper's reasons: A people with one walled town also keeps its grain in one known place, so "fixed and known" is a thing most peoples already pay and buys little by itself. What you pay beyond them is three things, and they are written in: the holds stand nearly unguarded for half of every year; you cannot winter away from them; and a fifth of your fighters are pinned in the cold months. With those written, and with the town you turned down counted in your favor, Few Holds pays for Pass Craft at either measure above.

Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons. Accepting is a choice you have, not the default.

## Round 2

### Your reasons, answered
- The ring of holds: accepted by you as said. Your clarification is granted in the Keeper's words: a hold may be added in play by the Column's own labor at the pace table's rates for cutting rock, and that changes the ring. It does not lighten the weakness: see the last line of Few Holds.
- Pass Craft: you took (B), and gave your reason (the people who do not fall). Both clarifications are granted as asked and written in.
- Few Holds: accepted as written. Your clarification is granted as asked.

### Final text

**The ring of holds.** The Column has no town. It has a ring of winter holds, three or four at the start, and stops only in winter. Uncharged. Practical costs, applied in play: no market others come to; no wall the whole people can stand behind; work that wants a fixed hearth the year round (charcoal, smelting, casting, seasoned timber) is done only at the holds and only by the households who stay; when the god is away from the Column its word reaches the Column only at a halt, from an officer who is there. A hold may be added in play by the Column's own labor, at the pace table's rates for cutting rock.

**Edge: Pass Craft.** On mountain ground (pass, scree, rotten snow, high ridge), the whole Column on the move, with its old, its young, its beasts and its stores, travels at two thirds of the foot rate where another whole people travels at half, and what it loses to the mountain in a crossing (people and beasts, to fall, cold, slide and rotten snow) is half what another host of the same size and kind would lose making the same crossing in the same season. It holds only on mountain ground, only for the Column moving in its march order with its Pass Wardens ahead (one office, its people spread along the front of the Column), and only on ways that carry its cairn signs or that the Pass Wardens have read within the season, meaning the thaw or the freeze in which the crossing is made. It adds nothing in a fight, on low ground, on a plain or in forest. A band of the Column without Pass Wardens moves as any band does.
Measurable line: mountain ground, whole people, pace multiplier 0.67 in place of 0.5; crossing losses halved.

**Weakness: Few Holds.** All of the Column's winter grain and winter shelter are in its rock holds, fixed, and known to anyone who has followed the Column for one round of the circuit. The Column cannot winter anywhere else: a winter caught outside the holds costs it one person in ten and one beast in four. The loss of one hold (taken, flooded, burned or fouled) takes a third of the winter's grain and a third of its shelter. In the open season each hold is kept by no more than twenty households and no band. In the cold months a fifth of those who bear arms sit at the holds and march nowhere; the Marshal divides that fifth among the holds and may move it between them without changing the number. If the ring grows past four holds, the loss of one hold takes a quarter, and never less.
What it bought: Pass Craft at the measure above. The town turned down was counted in your favor.
