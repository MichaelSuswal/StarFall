# Negotiation: god-rivers, Night 2

Your powers, priced. You proposed five powers and one weakness that buys.

A note on the line you wrote under "The premise". Two shapes in one body is the form you have, and one body in one place holds for you as you say. It is recorded as your form, not as a refusal of why gods come down.

## Round 1

### How the table prices two things for every god
These two are not offers. They are how this table prices the same thing for any god, and they are written into your card where they apply.
- A loss of memory or habit. Where a drawback takes "a memory or a habit" and you are not told which, the Keeper removes one item from your own record (your record of other gods or your goals) for each time the price falls due, chosen by lot among the items, and you are not told what went. You asked that it stay unspecified; it does.
- A return from a broken body. A full season, at the nearest ground of your own kind to where the largest part of you lies, is paid for by one item lost. A return in a month is paid for by two items lost each time. The table sells nothing faster than a month. If the nearest ground of your kind is more than a day off, the walk to it at your own pace is added to the time. Being held, bound, buried or shut in is not being broken: you stay where you are. Remains kept from the ground of your kind do not return until it reaches them or they are let out. What another god does to your remains is unruled until it happens.

### Definition: the water you stand in
Draft text: The one river or stream you are standing in, from its spring to where it meets another water or the sea. A feeder is another water. Running water is fresh water moving in a bed: a spring, a stream or a river, under ice if it still runs. Wells, cisterns, ponds, rain, snow and still lake water are not, except where a stream runs out of a lake.

### The Two Faces
Draft text:
Description: You change between the Crown shape and the Ford shape. The change takes about a hundred breaths, in which you are between: soft, slow, dull in every sense, unable to act, and as easily hurt as a mortal. Anyone watching sees it.
Drawback: The between leaves you unable to defend yourself or flee. You may change once between one dusk and the next, and not at all while held or bound.
Limits: One body: wounds carry across both shapes. No third shape, and neither shape copies any mortal. The Ford shape's forgettable face is an ordinary plain face and no power: people may fail to remember it as they fail to remember any ferryman, and one who has reason to look will remember it. Both shapes keep the same eyes. Being watched: one who sees the change knows what they saw. Against a god's sight: unruled until it happens.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: This is your form, with its cost written. Accepted as you wrote it, with the Ford face made plain so it is not read later as a second power of hiding.

### Water's Ear
Draft text:
Description: Standing still in running water to the knee or deeper, eyes shut, you hear what happens in and on the water you stand in, below you. See Choices for how far and how much. You learn what is done, never who anyone is or why.
Drawback: See Choices. What you learned stays with you seven days and then fades, unless you have spoken it aloud, set it down in marks, or told another.
Limits: While listening you are deaf and blind to where you stand and cannot move; anyone may come up behind you. This is a condition, not the price. Only what touches or sits on the water, and sound made within a few paces of the bank. Nothing of dry land beyond that. Not on still water, a well, a pond, a lake except where a stream runs out of it, nor through ice thicker than a hand. Only the water you stand in, not its feeders or branches. It never tells a god from a mortal. Light and dark make no difference. It rules no mind. Water held or turned by more than ordinary craft, and water another god holds: unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Use across a year: limited by the price below.
Choices: the reach. (A) Long and plain: the whole run below you to its mouth; you hear wading, crossing, drinking, washing, boats and their loads, numbers, and things dropped in; voices only as voices (how many, raised or low), with no words; no sight. (B) Short and close: one day's walk of water below you; all of the above, and the words spoken within a few paces of the bank at crossings; no sight.
And the price. (1) The water keeps you. For as long as you listened, and never less than a day, you cannot go out of sight of that water. (2) The water keeps your hearing. For as long as you listened, and never less than a day, you are deaf on dry land.
The Keeper's reasons: Sight and words along a whole river to its mouth would settle most of what a river country has to tell while you stood still, so it is trimmed, and said plainly. What you offered as the drawback is how the power works (helpless while listening) and a fading you could end by talking to yourself. A price is something you would notice losing: your freedom to leave, or your ears.

### Bar the Ford
Draft text:
Description: Standing in the water, you raise the river against one crossing within your sight on the water you stand in: a ford, a channel or a gap. Over about a hundred breaths the river rises in its own bed there, quick and cold, to a man's chest and a little over the banks, and it holds until the next dusk. It is plainly seen and heard.
Drawback: The water is taken, not made. Below the crossing the river runs thin for the same span, and it starves those below as it bars those above, your own people among them. You cannot end it early, even if you were wrong. And you cannot change shape for a day after.
Limits: One rising at a time. Only moving water in a bed you could stand in: not a lake, a pond, a well, or frozen water. The rise does not travel past the crossing's own width and is not a wave. It bars those who wade; a boat can still be poled across, slowly. One caught in the ford as it rises has the hundred breaths to get out. It rules no mind. Water held by a dam or other work of more than ordinary craft, and any water another god holds: unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: Force that can be seen, with a real price downstream and no way to take it back. Accepted within sight as you wrote it. The reach beyond sight is a step the weakness below may buy.

### Slip the Knot
Draft text:
Description: With a hand still wet from running water, you touch a bond of ordinary craft and it gives: a knot, a cord, a shackle, a bolt, a bar, a latch, a lock, a door fastening, however strong. It may be a bond that holds you or one that holds another.
Drawback: After each use you are stricken for about an hour: your hands shake, your mind will not settle, and you can neither decide nor act, though you can be moved. You cannot bind again what you loosed.
Limits: The hand must be wet from running water, and stays wet enough for about a hundred breaths after it leaves the water; rain, sweat, a cup or a skin of carried water will not serve. A bond is a thing made to hold shut or tied. A wall, a pit, the fixed bars of a cage and a hand holding on are not bonds. Only the bond gives, not what it is fixed to. It rules no mind. A bond made by more than ordinary craft, or by a god: unruled until it happens.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: "Any bond, however strong" is given, because the wet hand is the fence: held away from running water, you are held like anyone. The hundred breaths are written so that a skin of river water at the belt is not a key.

### Return by Water
Draft text:
Description: When your body is cut apart, burned to the bone or crushed past holding, what is left runs as water and silt to the nearest running water, and you are nothing for a time. After a full season you re-form at a crossing on that same water below where you entered it, in the shape you last wore, wet and with nothing on you.
Drawback: Each time you come back, a memory or a habit does not come back with you, and you are not told which.
Limits: The return follows the table's rule above. You do not choose the crossing. What you carried does not come back. If you are broken where no running water can take what is left (shut in, or on ground with none in reach), what is left lies where it is and does not return until running water reaches it or it is carried to running water. Held and not broken, you are held.
Choices: the pace. (A) A full season, one item lost each time. (B) A month, two items lost each time.
The Keeper's reasons: You asked the Keeper to rule on being broken away from water and said you expected the worst. It is the same rule every god has: remains kept from the ground of their kind wait. "Within a season at the longest" becomes a full season, the table's price for one item.

### The weakness you offer, and what it buys
Draft text:
Dry Ground, as you offered it: each day on ground with no running water within a day's walk weakens you after the first, and by the tenth day you can use no power and can only walk toward water; it ends a day after you come within a day's walk of running water.
The Keeper's reasons, and what it buys: As offered this is mostly a price you already pay, since every power you have needs running water under you or on your hand, and in river country ground a full day from any stream is rare. As offered it buys nothing. See Choices.
Choices: (A) With teeth: the measure is sight or sound of running water, not a day's walk. Each day out of sight and sound of it weakens you, and by the third day you can use no power and can do nothing but go toward water; a mortal who learns it can pen you by holding you a few hundred paces from a stream. That buys one thing, and you name which: Bar the Ford reaching any crossing on the water you stand in that you have stood at before, within a morning's walk (about eight miles), seen or not; or what Water's Ear tells you staying with you a season in place of seven days. (B) Kept as you offered it, as color under your form, at no price, buying nothing. The door you can open from inside stays as color either way.

Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the expected one. You may counter with a drawback of your own in place of the Keeper's.
