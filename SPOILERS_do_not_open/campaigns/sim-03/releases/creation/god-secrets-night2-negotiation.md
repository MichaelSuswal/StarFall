# Negotiation: god-secrets, Night 2

Your powers, priced. You proposed three powers and one weakness that buys, and asked for one clarification.

## Round 1

### How the table prices two things for every god
These two are not offers. They are how this table prices the same thing for any god, and they are written into your card where they apply.
- A loss of memory or habit. Where a drawback takes "a memory or a habit" and you are not told which, the Keeper removes one item from your own record (your record of other gods or your goals) for each time the price falls due, chosen by lot among the items, and you are not told what went. You asked that it stay unspecified; it does.
- A return from a broken body. A full season, at the nearest ground of your own kind to where the largest part of you lies, is paid for by one item lost. A return in a month is paid for by two items lost each time. The table sells nothing faster than a month. If the nearest ground of your kind is more than a day off, the walk to it at your own pace is added to the time. Being held, bound, buried or shut in is not being broken: you stay where you are. Remains kept from the ground of your kind do not return until it reaches them or they are let out. What another god does to your remains is unruled until it happens.

### The clarification you asked for (in the write-up, not a new power)
Draft text: An ear is a part of your one body, as your hair is. It has no mind, no sight and no will, it is in no way you, and you are in one place. It breaks no rule of bodies.
The Keeper's reasons: Granted as wording. It is kept true by the limits below: an ear that could be steered, heard through from afar, or spoken through would be a second body, and none of those is in the text.

### The Small Ears
Draft text:
Description: You shed ears from your pelt. Each is a thumb-sized part of your body. You set one by hand or mouth at a place, or on a mortal, a beast or a thing, and it clings and hides within a pace of where you set it and then stays. It hears speech and loud sound within about a dozen paces, as one hears in a quiet room, and nothing else. It keeps the words and voices of the last few days, the oldest falling away first. To read it you take it back: touched to your own hide it rejoins you, and you then have all it kept, in order, in the voices as spoken. Up to six are out at once.
Drawback: Each ear out is a part of you that you cannot feel or account for: you do not know whether it is lost, found, crushed or eaten. Where it grew stays a bare thin patch, and a wound in a bare patch goes deeper and closes slower, until the ear is back or a season after it is lost. And what an ear brings back may not be what it heard: when an ear rejoins you the Keeper rolls, and one time in six a word, a name or a voice in what it kept is wrong, and you are not told that it is or which.
Limits: You cannot hear through an ear while it listens, call it, steer it, speak through it, or feel where it is. It does not think, choose or follow orders. Only you can set one and only your hide can read one; another may carry an ear back to you. An ear found and killed is lost. An ear on one who travels stays on them. A tongue you do not know comes back as sound. Being watched: an ear can be seen by anyone who looks closely where it sits. Water: an ear under water hears nothing. It rules no mind. An ear in a place or on a thing held by more than ordinary craft, or on another god: unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Information: it brings back what was said, and what a speaker says is so is only what that speaker believes. Use across a year: six out, each to be set and fetched by your own body.
Choices: the number and the keeping. (A) Six out, each keeping the last three days. (B) Twelve out, each keeping only the last day and night.
The Keeper's reasons: You asked for twelve expecting a trim. Twelve ears each keeping several days is every room of a walled seat heard at once; the budget is in ears times days, and you may spend it either way. You offered two prices and accepted either: both are taken, since the bare patch alone is small (your hide seldom reaches the meat) and the false word is the one you would notice. The rate, one in six, is set here because you gave none.

### The Asked Question

A question about another god, put to a mortal, is answered as any other; the power used on a god, or on one warded by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Light and dark make no difference.
Choices: your own price. (A) Sealed and slow. What you are told stays in you: you cannot tell it, write it or sign it to anyone, ever. You may use the power once in a season, and never twice on the same mortal. (B) Paid in kind. The Keeper's reasons: This is a power that rules a tongue, so it is priced hard. The mortal's loss is not your price: you wrote yourself that you do not mind it. "Cannot act on it in a way that shows how she came by it" is refused as a price because no one could ever hold you to it. What is left of your two offers is put together as (A); (B) is the same power paid for out of yourself.

### The Slow Return
Draft text:
Description: When your body is broken past holding (burned to ash, crushed, or scattered), you are nothing for a time and then whole again. After a full season you return in the dark of a night, on bare ground under open sky, at the place nearest to where the largest part of you lay, with no one near.
Drawback: Each time you come back, a memory or a habit does not come back with you, and you are not told which.
Limits: The return follows the table's rule above. Being sealed away is being held, not being broken, and brings no return. Ears out when you are broken stay as they are and keep what they have. A return in a place held by more than ordinary craft or by another god: unruled until it happens.
Choices: the place and the pace. (A) Where you fell, a full season, one item lost. (B) Where you fell, a month, two items lost. (C) At the nearest whole ear of yours that is out, however far, after a full season and the walk from where you fell to that ear at your own pace; that ear is spent in it, every other ear out is lost with what it kept, and two items are lost.
The Keeper's reasons: You wrote "sealed away from any place she could leave" among the ways of being broken. That would turn every prison into a door, and no god at this table is sold that. Returning at an ear is a move across country, which the table's rule does not give; it is offered at a price as (C), with the walk added so that it is never faster than going there.

## Form

One body, in one place, as for every god here (the campaign allows one body).

As you described yourself, in your own words:

A great beast, and the size matters: heavier than a war ox and longer, low at the hip and high at the shoulder, so that the head hangs forward and level with the chest. The hide is a thick, close, grey brown pelt, the colour of ash left in a hearth for a night. Along the spine and over the shoulders, set in the pelt like a row of leaves, are small ears, pale inside and dark rimmed, dozens of them, each no bigger than a thumb joint. Are they not the first thing anyone sees, once they have stopped looking at the teeth?

The head is long and heavy browed, the jaw deep. The eyes are amber with a flat, horizontal pupil, and they hold the dark better than the light. The mouth is a beast's mouth and the voice that comes out of it is low and level, not loud, carrying a long way in still air and not at all in wind. Hearing is the keenest sense by far, finer than any mortal's, able to pick one voice out of a hall. Smell is good. Sight is poor in strong light, good in dark and dusk. Touch through the pelt is dull; through the nose and the ears it is acute. The other senses she has not described are left to be settled later.

She moves on four legs, with a long padded stride that is quieter than a creature of her weight should be. She can walk or lope, not run for long and not fast; her pace is the pace of a heavy animal that never tires. She cannot climb a ladder or pass through a door narrower than her shoulders. She can stand on her hind legs for a short while to look over a wall, no more.

### What breaks you
Does a beast this thick not take a great deal of breaking? Flint, bronze and stone open the hide slowly and seldom reach the meat, and what they open closes slowly. Fire is the thing that breaks her: it burns the pelt and the flesh under it, and burns heal badly and leave her bare. She can be held: by weight, by a pit she cannot climb, by chains of a thickness a village could forge, by stone laid over her, by a sealed room with a door she cannot open. She can be shut out by a door she cannot break. She can be cut apart or burned to nothing, though that is not easy, and she is then in the state below.

### When your body is broken
Burned to ash, crushed, or scattered past holding, she is nothing for a time, and then she is back. How she comes back, and what it costs her, is proposed below as a power (The Slow Return). Does she remember the going? She remembers it as a long listening with nothing to hear.

### Things carried (as you gave them; all ordinary unless a power below says otherwise)
Nothing. What would a beast carry? There is the hide and what grows on it, and that is all. She asks that she carry no object at all, and so there is no object to price.

### Settled at the table

- God-sense, as set for this campaign: you know another god only face to face, close, as a weight or an unease that grows surer with time and can be wrong. It tells nothing of the other's name, nature or powers. You know that others like you came down when you did.
- Pace: the pace of your form, as you described it; the pace table's rates apply (`engine/rules/time-and-pace.md`).
- Senses you did not describe are unsettled and are settled in play, never inferred.
- When the body is broken: see the return power below. It carries a cost and a trigger, so it is written as a power.
