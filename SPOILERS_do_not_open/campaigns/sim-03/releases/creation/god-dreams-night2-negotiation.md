# Negotiation: god-dreams, Night 2

Your powers, priced. You proposed four powers and one weakness that buys.

## Round 1

### How the table prices two things for every god
These two are not offers. They are how this table prices the same thing for any god, and they are written into your card where they apply.
- A loss of memory or habit. Where a drawback takes "a memory or a habit" and you are not told which, the Keeper removes one item from your own record (your record of other gods or your goals) for each time the price falls due, chosen by lot among the items, and you are not told what went. You asked that it stay unspecified; it does.
- A return from a broken body. A full season, at the nearest ground of your own kind to where the largest part of you lies, is paid for by one item lost. A return in a month is paid for by two items lost each time. The table sells nothing faster than a month. If the nearest ground of your kind is more than a day off, the walk to it at your own pace is added to the time. Being held, bound, buried or shut in is not being broken: you stay where you are. Remains kept from the ground of your kind do not return until it reaches them or they are let out. What another god does to your remains is unruled until it happens.

### Definition: your mark
Draft text: A mortal bears your mark who has taken something from your hand or given something into your hand and said yes aloud while doing it, knowing whose hand it was. A yes that was a lie makes no mark. The mark lasts while the bargain it was struck on is unpaid, and one season after it is paid. Only your own hand makes it: no priest, officer or messenger can mark for you.
The Keeper's reasons: Your own definition, with two things made exact: the mortal knows whose hand it is, and the mark cannot be made by proxy. Otherwise a toll taken by an officer would mark a whole people in a year.

### Night Tally
Draft text:
Description: Once in a night, awake and still, you send part of your attention into the dream of one sleeping mortal who bears your mark and lies within a day's walk of your body. You see and hear the dream, and may speak into it as a voice or a figure the dreamer takes for part of the dream. You may offer, buy and sell there. The dreamer remembers it as an ordinary dream is remembered and tastes copper on waking. It ends when the sleeper wakes, when you choose, or at dawn.
Drawback: See Choices.
Limits: While you are in a dream your own body stands still and thin: it sees and hears dimly, moves at half pace, and can do nothing else. This is a condition, not the price. A bargain struck in a dream binds no one until the dreamer, awake, confirms it aloud to you in person. You cannot read a thought outside the dream, change what the dream holds against the dreamer's own, or tell from inside whether the sleeper is a god or a mortal. A sleeper who wakes breaks it without harm. It rules no mind. A god asleep, or a sleeper warded by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Use across a year: one dream a night.
Choices: the price. (A) Sleep is owed for sleep. For each night you spend in another's dream you owe a full day of your own sleep, taken within three days: real sleep, from which calling will not wake you, only pain, cold water or a loud noise. (B) The count goes. From the end of each dream until the next dusk you cannot reckon: you cannot count past your fingers, read a tally, or hold the terms of a bargain in your head.
The Keeper's reasons: What you offered as the drawback (thin in the waking, afraid after a whole night) is how the power works and a mood you already carry; neither is a thing you would notice losing. Either choice is. You asked for two sleepers and a season's reach as an ask to be trimmed: it is trimmed to one sleeper and a day's walk, and the weakness below may buy a step.
One wording is changed and you should check it: you wrote that a dream bargain is confirmed "to the god or to its priests". It is written as to you in person. A bargain a priest can close on your behalf from a dream is a reach across a whole people.

### Heavy Sleep
Draft text:
Description: By touch on bare skin, held for a count of twenty, you put one mortal into sleep until the next dawn, or until they are woken hard by pain, cold water or a loud noise. Calling alone will not rouse them. It is ordinary sleep.
Drawback: Each use takes one small habit from you, and it does not come back, and you are not told which. A second use in the same night takes two.
Limits: It does not work through cloth, gloves or armor, on one who is fighting you, or on one who is struck during the count. It heals nothing and holds no one who is dying of cold or blood. A sleeper left uncovered in the cold pays for it as any sleeper would. Only your own hand: not at a distance, not through a messenger, not by a priest. It rules no mind beyond the sleep. On a god, on one with more than mortal blood, or on one warded by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Use across a year: as often as you will pay.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: A small power at a heavy price, and the price is your own. You asked not to be told what the second use costs; the god is not told, and the card you read says only "more". Tell the Keeper in your reply if you would rather the card you read carried the number, since you will see it here.

### Night Eyes
Draft text:
Description: In low light (starlight, a room lit by a few coals) you see as a cat sees: a face, the pins of a lock, the marks on a wall, as plainly as by day.
Drawback: In strong daylight, snow glare or bright fire you are dazzled and see less than an ordinary child, and in full winter sun on snow you cannot see at all while it lasts.
Limits: Not in perfect dark. Not through walls, cloth, smoke or water. No better than an ordinary eye past a bowshot. It shows nothing of another god's nature. A dark made by a power or by more than ordinary craft: unruled until it happens.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: An even trade of day for night. Accepted as you wrote it.

### Return by Dusk
Draft text:
Description: When your body is broken, burned, cut apart or otherwise ended, what remains sleeps. After a full season you return at dusk, whole and the size you were, in the dark place nearest to where the largest part of you lay.
Drawback: Each time you come back, a memory or a habit does not come back with you, and you are not told which.
Limits: The return follows the table's rule above. You return in ordinary clothes of the same cut with nothing in them; what you carried stays where it was. You cannot choose the place. Remains shut in a sealed place do not return until it is opened. Held and not broken, you are held. A marked mortal nearby may see a child step out of the dark.
Choices: the pace. (A) A full season, one item lost each time. (B) A month, two items lost each time.
The Keeper's reasons: Back by the next dusk is refused, and so is returning beside a marked sleeper anywhere within a season's walk. The first makes a broken body no setback at all; the second carries you across a country in a night, which is a second body in all but name. The table sells no god a return faster than a month, at any price.

### The weakness you offer, and what it buys
Draft text:
Held to Its Word. A bargain you have agreed to aloud, with a mortal or any other being, in terms both sides have stated, with a yes said and a thing exchanged or a day named, binds you. You cannot knowingly act against its terms, or break it by silence or delay, while it stands. You are bound in what you do, not in what you want: you may argue it, ask to reopen it, and complain. An idle promise, a threat, an offer not accepted, a bargain struck by a messenger without your yes, and a dream bargain not confirmed awake do not count. It lasts until paid, released by the other side, ended by its own terms, or a season after the other party's death. A bargain you were tricked into is still a bargain. How it can be used against you: anyone who knows the exact terms may hold them against you.
The Keeper's reasons, and what it buys: This is more than your nature. Your trait keeps a word past profit by choice; this takes the choice away and hands the terms to whoever knows them. It buys one step, and you name which: two sleepers in one night in place of one, each paying the price of its own night; or the reach lengthened from a day's walk to a day and a half. Not both at this price. The sealed dark stays as color, at no price.
Choices: the two purchases above, or keep it as color.

Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the expected one. You may counter with a drawback of your own in place of the Keeper's.
