# Negotiation: god-trade, Night 3

Your people, priced. You proposed one edge and one weakness. Everything else you wrote is ordinary life and costs nothing: the roads and boats, the fairs, the code, the tallies as a custom among themselves, the bench, the keepers who bless the weights, the offices, the heralds. A herald who bears your mark is your Lent Seal, already on your card, and nothing new. What you left to the world (their names, their magic, what they believe about gods, what they and your helpers know on the first morning) is fitted tonight and you will see it.

## Round 1

### The split tally
Draft text: Among your own people a matched tally is proof and is not argued; that is their custom and is free. The edge is what it is worth outside. A debt cut on a split stick of this people is paid, and is known to be paid: see Choices for how far that is known. Where it is known, a factor of your people is given goods on the stick alone, with no surety and nothing in hand, for up to one season's carriage, and a half-stick of theirs passes from hand to hand as a thing of worth. It compels no one. A stranger who does not know the custom, or does not care, owes it nothing. A stick shown to be false at the bench ends the credit of the house that cut it.
Choices: the reach.
(A) Earned over time. At the start the stick is honored by those peoples and houses that have traded with yours since before you came (who they are is the world's, and you find them). With any other people it is earned in play, by a generation's dealing or by whatever the world answers to what your people do. The weakness is as you offered it, written below.
(B) Earned at a stroke. As (A), and further: any people or house that has once had a stick of yours met in full, on the day named, honors the next. For this the chain is heavier by one link: a half-stick is good in any hand. Whoever holds the half, your own people or a stranger who bought it, begged it or took it, may call the debt at your Reckoner's bench, and the bench enforces it against your own.
The Keeper's reasons: You asked that a stranger who knows the custom honor it. What a stranger does is the stranger's, and no edge of yours rules another people's mind. What can be yours for certain is that your people pay, and the standing that buys where they are known. You said you would take a heavier weakness before a fence on reach, so the wider reach is offered at a heavier weakness, and the heavier weakness is one a mortal can use: a buyer of old halves holds your families.

### The tally is a chain
Draft text: A household that holds an unmet stick against it may not leave the country of the tally until it is settled, or its other half is given to a kinsman who will carry it. A debt unpaid passes to the heirs until it is settled or forgiven in the open. At the start, one household in five carries such a debt. Those households do not flee, do not migrate and do not settle new land; a new settlement draws only on the rest. A household that goes anyway is put out of the tally, and its sticks are worth nothing anywhere.
The Keeper's reasons: This is a real price: it slows the spread you came down to lead, and it does so by a number. It is taken as you offered it, with the number written so that it can be applied.
