# Negotiation: god-secrets, Night 3

Your people, priced. You proposed one edge and one weakness. Everything else you wrote is ordinary life and costs nothing: the folds and the seat, the code, the stonework (left as their pride, its worth the world's), the low singing, the binding of the dead, the court, the Reeves, the keepers of rite, the Gatherers, the closed season. What you left to the world (their names, their magic, what they believe about gods, what they and the court know on the first morning, whether they are waiting) is fitted tonight and you will see it.

## Round 1

### Closed mouths
Draft text: This people tells a stranger nothing of its numbers, its stores, its strength, its roads through the folds or who stands where. Any attempt by one not of this people to learn those things by talk, trade, gifts, drink or living among them takes the worst step against it that the odds allow, for as long as the people hold to the code. See Choices for how it stands under force. It is a discipline and no magic: it hides nothing that eyes can count from a ridge, nothing a power takes without asking, and nothing from one of their own who chooses to sell it.
Choices: under force.
(A) As any people, but later. One of them taken and put to fear, pain or long kindness holds a season longer than another would, and then breaks or does not, as mortals do. The weakness is as you offered it, written below.
(B) Shut for a year. One of them taken says nothing of those things for a year and a day, whatever is done, and only then breaks or does not. For this the weakness is heavier by one part: no stranger settles among them and few come twice, so every bargain your people strike with another people goes one step worse for yours, and what the roads would bring (news, craft, new seed) comes late or not at all.
The Keeper's reasons: You asked that a captive say nothing "under fear or kindness". For every mouth of five thousand, for good, that is a rule over minds and no discipline, and it is trimmed to time. The longer time is offered at a price that is the same habit turned inward: a people that gives nothing gets little.

### They hide their own hardship
Draft text: A failed harvest, a sickness or a hunger in a house or a hamlet is kept inside it. It is not told to the Reeve, the court or the crown for one full season after another people would have cried out, and the deaths from it are half again what they would have been. What your own ears take is yours, as your card has it, and changes nothing here: the houses still do not tell, the Reeves still do not know, and help that is not asked for is refused at the door for that season.
The Keeper's reasons: It is a real price and it is taken as you offered it, with the season and the number written. The last sentence is there because your ears could otherwise make the weakness one you do not pay: you may know, and the people still pay for not telling.

Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons. Accepting is a choice you have, not the default.
