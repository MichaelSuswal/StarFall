### How the table prices two things for every god
These two are not offers. They are how this table prices the same thing for any god, and they are written into your card where they apply.
- A loss of memory or habit. Where a drawback takes "a memory or a habit" and you are not told which, the Keeper removes one item from your own record (your record of other gods or your goals) for each time the price falls due, chosen by lot among the items, and you are not told what went. You asked that it stay unspecified; it does.
- A return from a broken body. A full season, at the nearest ground of your own kind to where the largest part of you lies, is paid for by one item lost. A return in a month is paid for by two items lost each time. The table sells nothing faster than a month. If the nearest ground of your kind is more than a day off, the walk to it at your own pace is added to the time. Being held, bound, buried or shut in is not being broken: you stay where you are. Remains kept from the ground of your kind do not return until it reaches them or they are let out. What another god does to your remains is unruled until it happens.

### Stone-Hearing
Draft text:
Description: With both bare palms or both bare soles flat on living rock, or on worked stone joined to living rock, you feel through that connected stone, out to about a bowshot, the weight and movement of what stands or walks on it, and where the stone is hollow, cracked or ready to give. You learn how many, roughly how heavy, and which way; that there is a hollow, and where the stone will split under load. No faces, names, speech or purpose. You hold it while the contact lasts, and what you felt is gone from you by the next day.
Drawback: After each use your hands are cold and numb for the rest of that day: you cannot grip, bind a rope or swing the hammer well.
Limits: While you listen you are deaf to the air and see poorly, and for a short while after; this is a condition, not the price. It does not pass through earth deeper than a hand over the rock, timber, standing water, or ice thicker than a hand. A wall of mortal masonry carries if it stands on living rock, not if it stands on soil. A gap of soil breaks it. It tells a hollow and never what is in it, whether it was made or grew, or its shape past the bowshot. It never tells a person from a beast, or a god from either. It does not work while you are carried or held off the ground. Light and dark make no difference. It rules no mind. A god on the stone, and anything made or held by more than ordinary craft, is unruled until it happens. Your own people get no exemption. Use across a year: limited by the day of numb hands each use costs.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: A short reach, effects only, and a price that takes your hammer hand for the day. Fair as you wrote it. The dull hearing is how the power works, so it is not counted as price.

### Fault-Word
Draft text:
Description: You lay both hands on living rock, or worked stone joined to it, and say the word. The stone breaks along its own flaws, not where you choose to the finger. See Choices for how much and how fast.
Drawback: The rock answers twice. Within a day of each breaking, somewhere on the same connected rock within a day's walk, a second fall comes that you did not ask for and cannot place; the Keeper rolls where. And for as long as the rock keeps falling and a full day after, you move at half pace and can lift nothing heavier than the hammer.
Limits: Living rock and worked stone joined to it. Not loose earth, sand, ice or timber. It makes no rock. Sound, unflawed stone fails you until it has been worked on, hammered, or cracked by weather or fire. You cannot choose what is left standing, and you cannot be sure who is below; if you stand on the slope you are on it, and you take harm from the fall as anyone would. Not twice on the same connected stone in one season. Being watched: everyone near hears the stone go. Your own people get no exemption. It rules no mind. A god in the path, and anything made or held by more than ordinary craft, is unruled until it happens. Use across a year: the season's rest on each stone, and the second fall each time.
Choices: the size. (A) Fast and small: at the word, at once, a span of stone up to about thirty paces across gives way: a ledge, a gate's footing, a stretch of wall. (B) Slow and large: a slope or a wall-face up to a bowshot across, but only stone you have worked with the hammer for a full day first, with your hands on it from dawn to dusk on the day it goes, and the rock groans for an hour before it falls, so that anyone on it or under it may run.
The Keeper's reasons: A whole slope at a word, at once, ends a war band or a town in one stroke with nothing to be played, and nothing is over the top. Trimmed, and said plainly. The prices you offered were that the rock chooses where it breaks (how the power works, not a price), a weight on the chest (your own wound, which you already carry), and slowness. A price has to be something you would notice losing: a people that marches through passes will notice the second fall.

### Return From the Rock
Draft text:
Description: When your body is broken past use (cut apart, crushed, burned through, drowned), it goes grey, cracks, and lies as a small stone of that place. You are not awake in that time. After a full season the stone splits and a child steps out whole at the bare rock nearest to where the stone lies, with whatever of your things still lies beside it.
Drawback: Each time you come back, a memory or a habit does not come back with you, and you are not told which.
Limits: The return follows the table's rule above. If the stone is carried off you wake where it was carried. If it is crushed to dust the season begins again from that day. A stone sealed away from all bare rock (in a box, in a pot of earth, sunk in a marsh) does not split until bare rock touches it. Held and not broken, you are only held. It undoes no harm done to your people while you lay. A god who finds the stone: unruled until it happens.
Choices: the pace. (A) A full season, one item lost each time. (B) A month, two items lost each time.
The Keeper's reasons: You asked the Keeper to set the count and to rule on the sealed stone. Both are set by the table's rule, the same for any god.

### Still as Rock
Draft text:
Description: Standing or crouching still and silent against living rock or a stone wall, you are seen by mortals and beasts as rock, shadow, or a heap of grey dust, and not as a child. It holds while you neither move nor speak and no one touches you.

### Your reasons, answered
- Stone-Hearing: "by the next day" is written as "at the next dawn", as you asked.
- Fault-Word, choice (A), raised one step by the weakness: written as a span up to a bowshot across, at the word, at once. Your wording of a span (any shape of connected stone that fits inside a circle) is granted, at the bowshot.
- Return From the Rock, choice (A): written. Ground of your own kind is bare living rock, and dressed stone joined to living rock counts, as you asked.
- Still as Rock: stands.
- Wet Ground, choice (B). Your three clarifications, answered one by one. First, that the water must stand and be unbroken, and a gap a body can pass opens it: granted. Second, that it ends when the water is drained or the line broken: granted; that it ends when "crossed by anything but our body" is refused, because a line that a dog can end by jumping it is no line, and the tooth is what bought the bowshot. Third, that a mortal who does not know it gets nothing from it by accident: refused as you wrote it. The tooth is in your body, not in the digger's knowledge: an unbroken ditch of standing water stops you whether it was dug against you or lies there by chance. What you can walk around does not hold you, and that is written.
The Keeper says one thing plainly, as the table requires: with the bowshot, Fault-Word is the largest single stroke the table has sold. It stands because you paid for it with a line a mortal can hold and because the rock answers twice.

## Form

One body, in one place, as for every god here (the campaign allows one body).

As you described yourself, in your own words:

A child of about nine winters. Thin, narrow-shouldered, a hand shorter than a grown woman. Dark hair always full of grey dust no matter how often it is shaken out. A flat, still face, too still for the age. Eyes the color of wet slate, which do not blink as often as a child's should. Bare feet, the soles hard as horn. Hands small and scarred across the knuckles, as if they had been hitting rock for a long time and we do not remember why.

Senses are ordinary: sight, hearing, touch, smell, taste, as a healthy child has them. Nothing beyond them is claimed here; anything more is proposed below as a power. We move at a child's pace on a child's legs, and we do not tire. We go up broken ground well, on all fours when it is steep, and we do not mind the drop.

### What breaks you
A child's body, and no more than that. Blade, flint, bronze, club, fire and a long fall all hurt us and can cut us apart. Cold gets in. A heavy fall of rock can crush us, and rope, chain, a pit or a wall built around us can hold us. A door we cannot force shuts us out, and we are small enough that a grown man can carry us off under one arm. Our body endures: pain does not slow us, and we keep walking on wounds. That is stubbornness, not safety, and it does not stop the body from breaking.

### When your body is broken
We do not know how it feels, only that it has happened before. The body goes grey and cracks along itself and lies as a small stone of the place where it fell. It is not awake in that time. It comes back whole, a child again, at the nearest bare rock to where it broke. See "Return From the Rock" below, which is proposed as a power because it has a trigger and a cost.

### Things carried (as you gave them; all ordinary unless a power below says otherwise)
Ordinary things, with no power attached, and we ask that they be written as ordinary:
- A coat of grey wool, too big in the shoulders, belted with a cord.
- A coil of plain hemp rope, about twenty paces.
- A hammer with a flint head bound to a wooden haft, the kind a mason or a quarryman could make. It is only a hammer.
- A leather pouch with a handful of small stones in it. They are only stones.

### Settled at the table

- God-sense, as set for this campaign: you know another god only face to face, close, as a weight or an unease that grows surer with time and can be wrong. It tells nothing of the other's name, nature or powers. You know that others like you came down when you did.
- Pace: the pace of your form, as you described it; the pace table's rates apply (`engine/rules/time-and-pace.md`).
- Senses you did not describe are unsettled and are settled in play, never inferred.
- When the body is broken: see the return power below. It carries a cost and a trigger, so it is written as a power.
