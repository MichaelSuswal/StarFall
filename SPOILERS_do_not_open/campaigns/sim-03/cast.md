# Cast: campaign sim-03

Written by the Keeper on creation Night 3b, 2026-11-21. A few world-level figures, each with its nature and history fixed here, each placed. Everyone else rises from play. Office-holders among the eight god-led peoples are unnamed until play names them, and nothing the player left unnamed is named here. Every being that answers questions carries its limit, word for word as in `mysteries.md`.

## Beings that answer questions

### The Canters
A walking people, about 140, human-seeming, who ask the names of things and sing them back. No home on the sheet; on it only on their rounds (event schedule C01, C02), by the Pale Road [f26] and round the settled country. Neither kind nor cruel. They want nothing a god can give, and they do not worship. History: the deep history, "The carriers and the thinners". Mystery: E01.
Limit: The Canters know the names they carry, where they were given each, and that a name carried long enough has sometimes come back; they do not know why, what a god is, what the stone is, or what happened in the far country after they left it. They will not say a held beat's name, because they do not have it. They answer only at night, only a question about a name or a place, only by singing the part of a list that holds it, and for any one asker only one question in a night. They sing nothing under compulsion, since no one of them holds a whole list.

### The voices
Those who rose from this ground: two of the three who came before, and older ones no record names. They are not on the ground and are met only in dreams. Mystery: L02.
Limit: The voices know only what a risen god knows: that rising is not an ending, what they themselves were, and the power each offers. They do not know what the star is for, whether they are one or many, or what becomes of them later, and they say "we do not know" to those. They can be asked only in a dream, only by a god that has passed 59 or that sleeps in the hollow of the Dun Back [r09] on a dim hour, one question in a dream, and they answer from their own side: none of them thinks rising a loss.

### The Graith, and the elder who holds the telling
About 800, under the eastern heights, entered at Graithdeep [f46]. The elder who holds the telling is called Uln in these records; it has not yet given the telling in its life. Among the Graith no ruler's word and no god's word carries any weight: they hear it, and wait for it to pass. Mystery: L03.
Limit: The Graith know what their own people saw and did: that the sky came down once, that they sat it out in the hall behind the Sealed Door [f49], that some of them woke and some did not, and what the land was like before. They do not know why it happened, what a star is, whether it will happen again or when, and they say so. They believe the rock saved them. One elder holds the telling and gives it once in its life, to the first asker who waits at Graithdeep [f46] without entering through one full round of seasons and asks plainly; after that it is not told again in that elder's life. Other questions are answered at the rate of one a season, in few words, and a question about the Door is not answered.

### The clerks of Averley [f39]
The keepers of the rolls in the second ring of Averley [f39], some forty, under the regent. Mystery: S02, S10.
Limit: The clerks know what is written in the rolls of the present line, 600 years of them: debts, boundaries, tolls, births at court. They do not know what was never written or what the league wrote, and they do not know that any entry is false. They answer a stranger only on a written request sealed by a named house or ruler, one matter to a request, in about a season, for a fee in salt weight; they do not answer on the regency or the heir.

### The Sallow at the night market
The reed folk of the Rattlereed [r12], dealt with at the edge of the reeds below Nightstake [f45]. Their cord-mother now is Sey, called Third-Thumb, who holds every cord of debt on the upper river and can say each from memory. Mystery: S07.
Limit: The Sallow know what moves on the river and in the reeds by night, and what their cords record of their own bargains. They do not know the dry country or what happens by day. They answer only at the night market, only for a thing given first, one question to a gift, and they do not answer a question about another customer.

### Ibbet
About sixty, at Farwalker's Cot [f60]. The last of a line that walked from the far country. Patient, incurious, kind to goats. Mystery: S13, E01.
Limit: Ibbet knows only what her mother and grandmother told her on the walk: that the sea of their country had gone to salt, that its star hung low enough to light the night, that speech there had gone all one way, and how to grow the bean. She cannot say the country's name or any name in it; those did not last the crossing. She believes the star there was coming down to drink the sea. She answers anyone who sits with her, as often as asked, and says "I was not told" to the rest.

## Beings that do not answer

### The Sleeper [f44]
The Sleeper [f44], in the hollow of the Dun Back [r09]. Older than the fall before. Asleep. It does not wake, speak or bargain within any span this campaign reaches. Mystery: L01.

### The Warm Stone [f42]
The Warm Stone [f42]. It is not a being. It dreams at whoever sleeps near and answers nothing. Mystery: E01.

## Mortals

### Osmer, regent of Averley [f39]
At Averley [f39]. A salt-house lord of about fifty, eleven years regent. Careful, tired, honest in small things, and certain the kingdom is safer in his hands. He does not want a god in the low country. Mystery: S11.

### Linnet, heir of Averley [f39]
At Averley [f39]. Fourteen at the arrival. Bookish, watched, fond of the regent and beginning to doubt him. Mystery: S11.

### Marit Low
At Saltsteps [f40]. Keeper of the drowned roll. About forty-five. She knows the name of every one the sea and the pans have taken in 200 years, and writes the dead debtors down as paid. She keeps no crown's office and wants none.

### Coll Wren
At Evenfire Cairn [f62]. A fowler of about thirty who keeps his family's fire alight for the one who used to come by. He asks nothing of anyone, kneels to nothing, and would share his fire with whoever came.

### Hale's daughter
A Hethren child of eight winters in the camp that winters nearest the Dun Back [r09], gathering at Hethmoot [f43]; not yet given a name of her own, and called by her mother's. She is not afraid of the long hill, has slept at its foot more than once, and has come home each time.

### Jass
On the Meadow Road [f32]. A Marran carrier of about forty, put out of the tally for leaving with a stick unmet. He walks the road with about sixty others in the same case and bargains for all of them, well, with nothing in hand.

### Benna
At Lowstair [f11]. The hamlet's midwife, about seventy. She is keeping Lowstair [f11] alive through its hungry winter by deciding who eats, and has told no one outside it.
