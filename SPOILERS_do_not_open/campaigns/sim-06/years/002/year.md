## The tail of Year 1: the unnamed town [f20], from the held moment to the year's end
The played day (the DM's record), late in month 11 of Year 1: at the gatepost the god took up the half-stick and said yes to weighing the old weights; spoke its terms; asked where the weights are kept and who holds the key; named a mark for the Tally-keeper (two pans, level); said it would answer on the Weightward's seat after the weighing; would claim no price from the bonded man, remitted if his master's scale read level within the month. A stallholder's scale read out by about one part in forty in her favor [DM r-0006, DM r-0007, DM r-0008] and level on a second trial [DM r-0009]. At the next market the reading-sticks were granted by the Stallreeve and the Tally-keeper [DM r-0010], on his rule and with her notch; five cut. All five DM rolls stand: four are successes or draws, none failed, so none can be overruled.
- The talk at the hall door, the same dusk, resolved on the player's handed-off words (decision 5; L-0032). The Assay-master did not ask it in. It said the five things. He took each as the people's report gives: he will carry the weighing as an afternoon's work; he holds it to the word "after"; "That you know of" was the careful part; he has nothing to tell yet of his friend's death and will tell it when he knows; he would not have it said that he is the likeliest for the seat. Three things he asked that the words do not answer met silence and are pending: that it say "a measurement, not a standard" to the eleven itself on the day; where it learned to weigh; why it asked the Stallreeve about the key. He said nothing of the rack.
- The word to the Stallreeve on his rule, the ask to the master of the potters, and the one quiet question to Bren's household were made as decided. Bren answered for the household: they would say what the town says, that the water folk go out at the lowest tides and come back, to the Maun Stair [f27] by the salt-rakers' telling, to meet kin by the channel folk's, and that none of them has followed.
- Month 12: the bonded man's master brought his scale, mended; it read level [r-0285; stated odds 65, rolled at 70 with the sealed factor; success either way]. The price was cut as remitted.
- The eleven took the terms whole before the harvest, eight to three [r-0286; stated odds 75, rolled at 80 with the sealed factor; success either way].

## The player's god and the unnamed town [f20], Year 2
- The weighing, month 1, resolved on the handed-off terms (L-0038). The Assay-master opened the chest before the ring and lifted out the rack: six weights in seven seats, the largest seat empty and clean (mystery S01's first clue, now seen by the god). He said aloud that he had found it so when he first opened the chest after the laying out. The god weighed the three bronze in its pans and the three stone on the hall's beam and called 48, 96, 192, 384, 766 and 1,536, as The True Measure gives (fixed facts of pass one). The Assay-master's beam agreed, but that he first read the fifth level and then light; both of his readings were cut beside the one call. Four of the hall's pieces went down against the first bronze; the head of the hill goods did the sum aloud: the hall's pieces run about a twentieth heavy. The potters' nest was weighed and does not agree with itself. The hill kin's weigher set his stones against the six on the beam and took the figures home on his own stick.
- Asked of the god in the hall and not answered by its words (silence, pending): the master of the smiths, where the seventh is, what it weighed, whether it can find it; the hill weigher, to weigh his stones before the ring. Answered by its words: bread and water refused (decision 3); what the fifth should be (decision 2: it says what a piece is now).
- The ring named no weight the town's. Three motions; the third carried nine to one: no weight named until the seventh is found or given up and the nest fired again; the market on the hall's present pieces; debts reckoned as made; nothing in the hall filed, mended or carried out; the chest opened only before two of the eleven; the matter back after the next grain. The ring agreed to look in the old keeper's loft with three.
- Held, month 1, the evening of the weighing: the Assay-master, alone with it in the hall, door shut: "It is after... What are you?" The god gave its word to tell him after; the asking is held for the player (L-0038).
- The loft, the third day after: the Assay-master, the Stallreeve and the clay head went up. What they saw: the old man's coat and boots on a peg, stiff with dried river silt to above the knee, unmoved (mystery S01's second clue). They came down quiet and told the ring nothing this year. The god was not there and was not told.
- Month 2, the carriers came; some days after, the Stallreeve went down alone to the boatmen at the landing in the evening and asked about the old man's last weeks. A boatman told him: he lent the old man a punt on a night of the spring flood and got it back with silt in the bottom and a wet rope (mystery S01's fourth clue). The god, at the lamp, saw him come and speak apart with them and heard nothing of it [r-0290; stated odds 50, rolled at 55 with the sealed factor; failed either way]. The Tally-keeper was not asked for the year's witnessings and has said nothing of the clerk's visit.
- The day at the pits, late in month 2: the gate left with a watchman, the line kept, no notch cut. Six lumps weighed before the diggers and kiln-masters: weight, clay, and not clay (L-0037). The two sets read alike, about a twentieth part sand and shell in each. The god said, in its decided words, that the sets do not look different and that it had told them nothing about the cracking. Cause of the reading: the loads that crack are cut with clay from the old yards on the Pellow Downs [r15], which is clay, with no temper (mystery L05). The people now know it is not grit and have no other cause.
- From month 3 the kiln-masters fired test tiles; they did not tell which loads would crack [r-0288; stated odds 70, rolled at 75 with the sealed factor; failed either way]. A tile cut from one lump does not stand for a load mixed unevenly. The quarrel of diggers and kilns keeps.
- Held, month 4: the ship owner of the channels, at the landing at dusk, offered a bargain: every boat at his landing read at dawn before unloading, a measure of salt a boat, the god at his landing and no other. It would bind the god and change its footing: held.
- Month 6: it stood witness to a boat sale between two households of boatwrights and said what it saw and heard (decision 15; no power used).
- Month 7: the potters' second nest came out agreeing with itself to a hair [r-0289; stated odds 80, rolled at 85 with the sealed factor; success either way]. It is kept at the potters' hall. Two households asked who owns a split reed island: owning is not a weight (decision 14).
- Held, month 9: the eleven, six to four with one absent, offered the Weightward's seat, by the Stallreeve at the gatepost: a term of three years and a day, the Assay-master's hand on the beam beside the god's, and the eleven's right to put the seat down at any sitting that three of them call. A half-stick laid at the post. Never answered for the player.
- Held, month 10: Captain Maud Sennet joined the line at the gate and stands at its head. A world-level figure reaching the god in unplayed time. What she carries is in `held-coaster.md` beside this file.
- The gate through the year on the decided terms; every reading true (the card). The Tally-keeper's reading-sticks ran; she showed the god the first stale stick in month 3. Some stallholders carved two pans on their beams and the Stallreeve had them scraped.
- The year's numbers: the people's `state.md`. About 5,090.
- Names said aloud in the god's hearing: Ruddfold [f25] (the hill weigher, in the ring); the Maun Stair [f27] (Bren, late in Year 1); the Alder, the River Alder [f03] (the Stallreeve, month 5); Corrith [f74] (a boatman, month 10, of the coaster's home port: his guess; she is of Port Sennet [f75]).
- Hidden powers and knacks: none surfaced. No food that does not feed was set in its pans; no pledge was pressed on it; no old bargain's terms were disputed in front of it. Growth: nothing earned; no attempt.

## god-sea and its people
Answered the Warden-Monarch at the tide door (it does not know the name Brennis; it will take no roof); answered the Master of Timber through her. Sound Timber three times in the year, weeks apart, each as the card gives: a quay step-board, month 2; the keel of the stand-by boat, month 11; the first keel, joined in Year 1, hauled clean after a year. Listened on the first night: the coast's ordinary day, no hull that was not the town's. Two lamp tenders shown how to tend its lamp. Month 5: a crab boat caught in a long swell came in with all four, the mouth calm under the god's lamp, rightly tended [r-0296; odds 75; success]. That water will not take the god for a season after. Spring: it swam the harbor bed, the skerries and the floor of the nearer bay by night. What is there: under the quay, the bell and the jar the town already knows; on the harbor bed inside the mouth, under the line the chain runs, ballast stones and the broken lower timbers of two boats of the town's own build, not long in the water (the truth of mystery S02, shown as what lies there and no more); beyond the Garrow Skerries [f31], nothing made by hands within the nearer bay. The old places of that coast are farther: 36.8 miles and more by the tool. No surge came. No hidden power's moment came.

## god-dreams and its people
Said from the wall that it does not know why the star dims. The stone cutters showed it the lowest cuts at the Ellow Scar [f51]; it claimed none. Heard the Warden of the Wall. The western party came home in month 1. Keeping Sleep two households a night, three on three named nights of deep winter, as the card gives, with what the card says of her after seen by the wall's men. A flame lit by a household from its own kept flame is a shrine and no third household (L-0034). Month 6: the Night Sitter's household was kept; the Night Sitter did not sleep (L-0039: Keeping Sleep takes a household's fear and does not pay a worker's price). The old Warden of the Door named his successor.
The salt party and m-0001: six set out in the third week of month 2, 4 days down the River Ellow [f07] to its mouth, found no ship there, and went round the shore on foot, laden, in bad weather [r-0294; odds 60; failed: delayed]. They reached Port Sennet [f75] in month 3, were given bread and three nights, and the leader spoke the words on the third night to the factor of the house that sets the price of salt for the bend. Delivered. They bought salt at the harbor's price from another house's pans. The answer given them to carry: the house will set a fair standing price by the year, for stone of the scar cut to its order (mystery S06, the branch where the god sends for salt another way and the House names terms). The coast road was shut by the gales; they wintered at the harbor and came up the river at high water, home in month 8 with the salt and the answer. The town had a second lean winter for salt. Word of the guest at the bend, half a woman and half a hind, who keeps a lamp, is now common talk in Port Sennet [f75].

## god-rivers and its people
Asked the council for a hearing on the ford and had the ferry keepers cut a second cord of low-water marks. Told the children plainly where it woke. Summer: by barge down Wellen Brook [f12] to Wellen Ford [f47], 25.1 miles, with stops at the reed hamlets; six days at the ford. It stood in the River Wellen [f08] to the knee: no pull (no god stands in or on that river). The carriers said others now go a southern way. Warm hands twice, as the card gives: both recovered, the cold paid. Late summer, the short listening below the gate under watch: nothing came against it. What the water told: the deep water lies farther from the ford on the side away from the weirs; fewer crossings; nothing strange from the hills; no pull. Autumn: it told the council what was measured, seen and heard, as counsel; the council had a mark set to read by at the autumn low water. Month 7: the strayed herd, part brought home, part lost to the reed [r-0295; odds 55; failed by 6]: about thirty-five goats and three cattle home. No knack's moment is on the record.

## god-healing and its people
Went to the Reckoner first. Read the moss of the mounds: living moss, sound, nothing foul in it and nothing ailing it that the Reading knows (L-0040). Read the rise for new graves: sound; the first burials went there, after a quarrel. Sat at the Hearth-Holder's hearth in summer, in the open. Read the wells downstream: those kept open below the bank burials foul as before; those drawn off last year fainter, still foul at the bottom; the two emptied steadings' wells foul. Rotted a drowned ox out of a weir above a wellhead, once, at the card's price. Nine mendings, each as the card gives, each cost paid. The winter count was lower in the steadings whose wells were closed. The tremor left its hands at the thaw.

## god-beasts and the Longwalk
Answered the eldest head through the Houndwarden: what it wants, once. The council will not send it off and will not take its word. The wolves of the calving were not turned on the three nights: about thirty calves lost [r-0291; stated odds 80, rolled at 85 with the sealed factor; failed either way]. The dry month: the turns at the river held [r-0292; stated odds 60, rolled at 65 with the sealed factor; success either way]: about eighty cattle, thirty goats and twenty sheep lost; about sixteen children dead of the flux at fouled pools. It kept the three clans' margin for a month on their leave. The Listening reads a gathering as hurt, afraid or resting and no more: a herd with coughing beasts in it reads as hurt in part, and not with what (L-0041). The cutters, as it read them in months 7 and 8: about eighty, in three camps with draught oxen, a day's walk deeper into the winter woods than last year. The cough came back in month 9 in the two clans' cattle; five died. In winter the two clans' herds were kept on their own ground at the downwind end of the camp, on their own browse, with the coughing beasts penned: the sick herds wintered apart before Year 3, so row S04-c is met. S04-a will stay in the two clans' beasts and S04-b does not come. The trade party saw the holds' own oxen coughing; the band now says the cough came from the stone towns, which is true, and they do not know it.
