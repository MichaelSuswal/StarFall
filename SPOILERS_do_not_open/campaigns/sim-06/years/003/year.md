## Year 2 again: the unnamed town [f20], from the evening of the weighing
Fixed by play (the DM's record, Year 3 played day): the hall and the hall door in month 1; the landing in month 4; the gatepost in month 9. Voiced by the Keeper: the gate in month 10 (`held-coaster-played.md`).
- Month 1, the evening of the weighing. To the Assay-master's "What are you?" the god said it is a god, and the rest as decision 1 gives. It asked him to hold it until he had decided what the ring should hear [DM r-0011, the god's attempt, stated odds 75, rolled 78, told as refused]. He refused in the words told: he would not be alone with a second thing. The chest was locked before the Stallreeve, the peg key untouched, the seat unwiped. The god told the Stallreeve itself (decision 2). He asked nothing of what else it is [DM r-0012]. The asking for a sitting went round that night with no matter named.
- What the year carried from that evening (the keeper log, pass one, has the rule applied): no pair of heads joined the Assay-master to call a sitting out of turn for a matter no one would name, and it went to the ring's winter sitting. From month 1 to month 6 the word was held by the Assay-master and the Stallreeve, neither alone with it. Neither told anyone.
- The third day: the loft looked in by three, as Year 2 has it. Month 2: the carriers; the god's word for the old weigher went up the Old Rudd Way [f23] with them (m-0002, delivered at Ruddfold [f25] that month; not rolled, no hazard in play). The Stallreeve among the boatmen, as Year 2 has it; within the week he told the Assay-master what the boatman said (the people's agent's telling; taken out of the public files at the check and kept here), and the pair of them told the clay head. They agreed to bring loft and punt to the ring at the winter sitting. This overtakes Year 2's line that he told no one.
- The pits day, the stale stick, the scraped marks, the tiles [r-0288]: as Year 2 has them.
- Month 4, the landing: the god refused "my landing and no other" and put back its terms; the ship owner of the channels took them [DM r-0013, success]. Set before the Stallreeve the same evening, with his additions. The Tally-keeper took on sticks for boats with additions of her own. From then the god read loads at dawn at his landing on days with no market, and from month 5 at other owners' landings that asked.
- Month 6, early: one of the ship owner's own boats, in from the flats with raked salt in baskets, read about five parts salt and one part not, basket after basket [r-0325, r-0326, r-0327, r-0328]. The god called each as it read (L-0037). Cause: a raking household with a thin season bulked its baskets under a clean top; the captain bought by the basket; the ship owner did not know. The Stallreeve ruled it short measure and not theft; it was made good; captains now turn out every fifth basket unasked.
- Month 6, the middle: the ring's winter sitting [month by r-0323]. The eleven let the yard in, nine to two [r-0324; stated odds 50, rolled at 55 with the sealed factor; success either way]. The god said first, standing at the foot of the ring, that it is a god, and the rest of decisions 1 and 6; that it weighs and they decide; that it does not ask to be Weightward and does not refuse it yet. Asked by the Assay-master, it said before the ring that it did not take the seventh. The head of salt asked whether it can tell when a man is lying [r-0329, the world's question, success]; it answered in the player's standing words (decision 7), naming what the Good-Faith Reading does and does not do, and that it will not use it on anyone in the town without their asking. Asked and met with silence: the master of the smiths, whether it can be killed and what would break it; a woman of the back houses, whether it can cure a fever. In the shut hall afterward, without the god: the three told the ring what they saw in the loft; the head of reed and fish asked the Tally-keeper plainly and was told of the clerk's visit (mystery S01's third clue, now the ring's); a motion that the god stand down from the gate fell nine to two; the ring voted to have the seventh looked for.
- The looking. The hall's rooms, the old man's house and kin, the boatmen of every landing: nothing, and nothing was there to find (the weight is in the river; there is only the one punt). Not rolled. Month 11, at the lowest water: the Stallreeve, twelve of the watch and the boatmen of a pair of households dragged the reach below the hall's landing and the reaches either side for ten days [r-0330; stated odds 70, rolled at 75 with the sealed factor; failed either way, 76]. Silt, old pots, a sunk boat. The seventh weight lies where it lay, in the main channel of the River Alder [f03] below the hall. The god was at the gate and the landing and saw the drag; it was told what the town was told, that nothing came up.
- Month 9, the gatepost: the eleven had voted six to four, one away, knowing what it had said in month 6. The scale in the god's hand read out [DM r-0014, DM r-0015]. It said again that it is a god and took the seat on the eleven's terms and its own (decision 4). The yard cried "Weightward" and the Stallreeve put up his hand.
- Month 10, first days: the ring sat and the god took the old keeper's key from its peg before the ring. A keeper of the standard is seated: row S01-b is met. Row S01-a still comes in Year 6; what it meets is changed. The ring's rule: any pair of the eleven may stand for the chest, one of them of the four who voted against.
- Month 10, the gate: Captain Maud Sennet, as `held-coaster-played.md` has it. Her balance read level. She learned from its own mouth that it is a god, and sailed in three days. Asked at dusk before the Assay-master whether the town wants a keeper's word sent down the coast, the Stallreeve said not this summer.
- Month 10, after she went: asked once, quietly, the Assay-master told the god what the three saw in the loft: the coat and boots, stiff with dried river silt to above the knee, unmoved (mystery S01's second clue, now the god's). He did not tell it of the punt or the clerk.
- Month 11: a second day at the pits at the diggers' asking; the lumps read alike, as before (L-0037; mystery L05). The upriver traders. Month 12: the grain. Two more dim nights, months 11 and 12, as Year 2 has them.
- Overtaken, of what Year 2's record and the DM's telling held: that the Assay-master waited unanswered; that the ship owner said only that he spoke and it listened; that the half-stick lay untouched and the yard's cry went unanswered; that the council after the grain waited on the god's answer; that no one in the town had been told.

## The player's god and the unnamed town [f20], Year 3
- Month 1, the first sitting after the grain: the ring took the god's offer and the six were weighed again before it: 48, 96, 192, 384, 766 and 1,536, as before (The True Measure; the fixed facts of Year 2). The ring voted the old six the town's weight, six to two with three not voting, from the first market of Year 4's autumn; bargains made before are reckoned by the present pieces; the master of the potters is to fire a nest to the old six. The seventh seat stays as it is.
- Held, month 1, the first market after that sitting: the Stallreeve, for the eleven, said yes to a keeper's word sent down the coast, to go with the open-water ship owner's coasters, asked the god for the words, and laid a half-stick at the gatepost. The god had asked whether the town wanted such a word and said it would send none without their say; the words themselves the player has not given. Held, not met with silence (L-0038: an answer the god has as good as promised is not given by silence in unplayed time). The thread stops there. The coasters went out in month 10. Whether a keeper's word went with them turns on the held answer and is provisional; as resolved with the moment unanswered, none did. Nothing else in the year turns on it.
- Month 2: the old weigher of Ruddfold [f25] came down with the carriers. His stones were weighed at the gate before four of the ring, the three largest on the Assay-master's own beam brought down: 48, 96, 193, 384, 768 and 1,536. Asked what his people say the seventh was, he gave a saying of his grandfather's, that the six weigh what is and the seventh weighs what is owed, and would not stand behind it. It is a people's saying. It is wrong about the hall and near a true thing by chance (the due is counted in the largest weight: mystery S01); no one acted on it.
- Asked and met with silence: a boy, twice, whether the star is the god's; a netmaker, whether it can tell the height of the water as the tide-reckoner does.
- Month 4: at the Assay-master's asking it checked six scales he had passed; a pair read level and four a hair out. It stood witness to a boat sale again, no power used.
- Month 7: the potters' third nest, fired to the old six, agrees with itself and with them to a hair [r-0331; stated odds 80, rolled at 85 with the sealed factor; success either way]. The date set for the town's weight holds.
- The gate and the landings through the year on the decided terms; every reading true. The ring did not sit to put the seat down. In summer the Aldermark's traders asked in the yard what the keeper of the town's weights is and were told what the town says.
- The year's numbers: the people's `state.md`.
- Names said aloud in the god's hearing: "the Horn" (the Stallreeve, month 1; the Dunmar Horn [r17]); the Aldermark [r10] (a trader, month 11); the Maun Stair [f27] (an old woman, at the winter sitting); Ruddfold [f25] and Corrith [f74] again.
- Hidden powers and knacks: none surfaced. No pledge was pressed on it. Salt cut with sand is a matter of purity, which the card's main power reads; no food that weighs true and does not feed was set in its pans. No old bargain's terms were disputed in front of it. Growth: nothing earned; no attempt.

## god-sea and its people
Met the Warden-Monarch at the tide door and answered her notches: it does not cause the dimmings and does not know what does. m-0003: its words for the Eldest Midwife, carried by the Warden-Monarch's mouth in the fourth week of month 1; delivered; no hazard, not rolled. The Midwife named a bedside by the water; the god came once, at the lowest tide at dusk in month 2, said what it counts, showed its shape at her asking, and gave no answer to her four questions, since its decisions give none. Water's Hearing on the first night after: the coast's ordinary day. Sound Timber twice, as the card and L-0042 give: the heel of the chain post, month 4; a crab boat's keel, month 11. Month 5: the east cove's long-liner came in with all three under the god's lamp, rightly tended [r-0339; odds 80; success]. That water would not take it for a season after. The Harbormaster, asked through the Warden about a pair of boats under the chain's line, answered that the town lost them the autumn before the keeping came, that the chain was down by his order, and that he takes a tide and not a word; in month 8 his lead and grapnel along the line took nothing that is a hull [r-0340; odds 80; failed]. The timbers lie where the god saw them. Spring: it swam west along the shore by night to the Drowned Causeway [f33], 66.5 miles by the shore, where the oldest net-mender's slip of hide said the old tellers put Low Garrow. What is there: the causeway's cut stones running out under the water, worn round at the edges; at its shore end, under sand and weed, the footings of walls, a line of quay stone and post holes; nothing kept by anyone, nothing made within a lifetime, no other like itself. It marked them and told no one. The Banked Coal and Crew Count knacks were exercised. No hidden power's moment came: it did not take hold of the chain.

## god-dreams and its people
Answered the Warden of the Wall in the yard of the watch and told the four at its door what they sit beside. Gave the Keeper of the Count its list of afraid days. Its sixth decision rested on a mistaken memory (that the western party had not come home); the families and the Warden of the Door told it they came home in Year 2, and a father asked who had told it otherwise. Keeping Sleep as the card gives, three households on three named nights; the wall's men saw at those dawns what it had told them they would. It sat in the Night Sitter's yard in month 4 and kept the kettle and the lamp; of three who sat the winter beside her, one found she could [r-0338; odds 55; success]. The court accepted the harbor house's offer. m-0004: the harbor party of eight set out on the twenty-fifth day of month 2 in the court's boats with stone, linen, pots and oil; it was delayed [r-0336; odds 60; failed by 1]: a boat grounded on a bar below the scar under its stone, no ship was at the mouth, and they went round the shore on foot with the samples. They reached Port Sennet [f75] in month 3; the leader spoke the words on the third night to the factor of House Corran; delivered. The House took the samples, sold them salt at a standing price below the carriers', and named its order: sixty blocks a year dressed to a cord it gave, lifted by its own ship at the river's mouth (mystery S06, the branch where the god treats with the House: it wants stone). The gales shut the shore; they wintered at the harbor. There the leader heard the quay talk [r-0337; odds 70; success]: at the market town in the western delta a stranger who reads scales for a pinch of barley has been seated as keeper of the weights and says before the market that it is a god. They came up the river at high water, home in month 8, all eight, with salt, the order, the cord and that word. The town had a third lean winter for salt. The Watch-Wakeful knack was exercised. No hidden power surfaced: the yard she sat in held the fevered, and no one there was failing that night.

## god-rivers and its people
Answered the ferry child at the fire and the ford keeper at the ford. Sat the council at the flood. Summer: by barge down Wellen Brook [f12] to Wellen Ford [f47], then three days down the River Wellen [f08] with a pair of ferry keepers and a cord walker, about 90 miles at 30 miles a day, turning at dusk of the third day, and about eight days back up at 12 miles a day. Below the ford the river leaves the Wellen Hills [r25] after 25.5 miles and goes into the Braidwater [r26]: many channels, reed and standing water. Met there: reed cutters at their staithes, and at dusk on the second evening a band of the water folk, who traded reed work for a knife and answered nothing about what they are. What was learned of the carriers: the reed cutters say the salt for the stone towns now comes up from the sea in the south and does not need the ford (mystery S05, the second half of its truth, as a people's report). The god stood in the river to the knee each dusk and dawn: no pull; no other like itself stands in or on that river. At the ford, the one listening: within twenty miles the deep water lies farther again from the ford stones on the side away from the weirs, the bed behind each weir is shoaled, fewer laden beasts crossed than last year, and nothing like itself is in that water. Nothing came against its sleep; not rolled, as last year. Autumn: it told the council what was measured, seen and heard. No knack's moment came.

## god-healing and its people
Went to the Reckoner first. Three test wells were dug on the rise with the elders' leave; it read each before any drank and at the season's end: sound both times. The old elder's well: foul as before, read before his steading; he keeps it. Summer, about fourteen days alone: up the River Morrow [f02] and its creeks within the Morrow Fen [r13] and to the first trees at the fen's western edge. What it read: the river and every creek and spring above the bank burials clean; the ground under the bank burials foul, and the wells below them; nothing foul coming down from upstream; old burial mounds on higher ground sound. The foulness begins at the newest bank graves and stops where the low ground's wells stop (mystery S07, as effects). The moss of the Morrow Mounds [f56], read at midsummer and after the first frost: living, sound, nothing ailing it that the Reading knows, and nothing in it that common moss lacks; the old women had found as much with their tongues since month 2 (row L05-d). An old sitter asked whether it was the god's doing; its decisions give no answer. Seven mendings as the card gives, each cost paid, not rolled, as in both earlier years. It told the Spearward it would turn the rot on no store. No hidden power's moment came.

## god-beasts and the Longwalk
Asked the council, through the Houndwarden, that she may act on its reading of wolves the same dusk; the Speaker gave her that leave for the calving only and took no word of the god's. The wolves were turned on the three nights; about six calves lost [r-0332; stated odds 85, rolled at 90 with the sealed factor; success either way]. Summer: it read the herds at each pool. Ruled (L-0045): the Listening gives a gathering's place and its state and does not give what a gathering is doing; "drinking" is the god's own inference and it said so. On its word of a herd hurt on consecutive dusks the Marchwarden moved the camps off one pool; three children had died of the flux there. The three clans refused it a second month at their margin. The council's word to the holds about the first stands was heard and nothing was promised [r-0333; stated odds 25, rolled at 30 with the sealed factor; failed either way]. Months 7 and 8: the cutters, read from more than a mile: three gatherings of people with draught beasts, working and unafraid, a little deeper in. (The truth, not read: about ninety cutters in three camps.) Autumn: the trade party came home with half the salt wanted and grey soft iron; its beasts were kept apart a month. Winter: row S04-a, confined. A herder asked what the cough is; the Listening cannot say (L-0041), and no answer was given. The Windward Sense knack was exercised. No hidden power's moment came.

## god-secrets and its people
Put the far bins to the Granary Warden as his own to name. He took it on his own terms. The far run was filled by the sack before a tally-keeper of each of the families that keep that end, and the count of the whole store squared. The Keeper's figures, which the people's own are to be read by: the roll's old stock 9,950 sacks; what stood, 6,630; the gap, 3,320, a third of the roll (mystery S03, the branch where the granary is measured). The families say the roll is wrong. Row S03-a is met early: the shortfall is known in Year 3, cried at market as the Warden's count, and no search or charge came of it. Winter: the Clerk gave it an evening with the old league's tallies. He put the sticks in its hand to count. A hidden power surfaced: holding the third and then one other, it read the marks as their makers would (Tally-Reader; its condition met, a stranger's tally handed to it). What that stick says: a count of grain and salt owed by the old factory to a salt house of the western delta at its closing, never struck. It said nothing of it; for a day after it could have spoken no false figure of that account, and no one asked. Spring: the channel sounded, a little shallower. Summer: up the River Iss [f11] with a boat family's crew, 39.7 miles, four days up with a day lost, three weeks at the boat families' landing at the river's head on the Iss Levels [r07], where the Iss Track [f37] comes down from the plain, and home in a pair of days, back on the thirteenth of month 11. Met there: the boat families' kin and drovers of the Ansel Plain [r04]. Its listening brought it this [r-0341; stated odds 50, rolled at 55 with the sealed factor; success either way]: the drovers say the pass herders beyond the plain have had a guest at their winter hall for three winters, a woman with a staff and a key whom the whole people feed and call Guest, and whom no one has seen eat. The Figure-Memory knack was exercised.

## god-thresholds and the Sarrow
Answered the Steward, the Mother of the Hearth and the herder. Judged the grass by the sill law at the sill table; the captain confirmed the line. Two counts of the stores; sharing by hearth from the first snow. The toll houses' cords, laid out at the hall, agree with each other and with the hall. Against her own knowledge of who crossed Stair Pass [f62] and Far Notch [f63]: the trains on the cords are the trains that crossed, near enough; the toll laid up for them is half what the old cords give for as many (mystery S08's first clue, seen sharper). Row S08-c is not met: nothing was counted at the houses before the winter. Month 4: the Reading of the Host on the senior Doorwarden, in the open: no, he means no harm to a guest at that table, and he learned the same of her. She taught him the oath in person that winter; no lesser mark was laid. Months 5 to 8: the stores failed some weeks short of the thaw [r-0334; odds 55; failed]: the last fortnight on dried meat, herd and seed; about three hundred dead over the ordinary, the old and the youngest; the sowing short. Month 6: row S08-a: the commander of the war band raised the toll by half from the thaw; she gave no word on it. Month 7: the Marshal and the first captain walked the houses unannounced and counted stores, beasts and roofs; they left with the watchmen's word again [r-0335; odds 60; failed]: the stores were counted, and each house had an account of a good year's barter that the captain could not break. The chancery's letter came with the first train and was read at a rite fire in her hearing; her decisions give no answer; the commander answered that the Sarrow owe no share to a king they have not met. Summer: at the Kennoch Gates [f64] she saw the cutting and no flower; the oldest herders say the line of the grass has come down a bowshot in their lives. At each fire she asked what "as Marda held it" means and kept each answer as that fire's. The Table-Seating knack was exercised.
