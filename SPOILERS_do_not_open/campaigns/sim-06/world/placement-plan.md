# Placement plan: sim-06 (Night 3a)

Written geography, not a drawing, written before any painting. Working names only; map names and ids come at tracing, and where the paint departs from this plan the plan yields. No release takes a distance, a bearing or a sight line from this file.

## The continent
One continent, wider than it is tall: about 1,100 miles east to west and 850 north to south at its broadest, about 650 across at its narrowest by the overall shape, about 1,000 across on average. A margin of sea on every side. A cold grey sea to the north. A deep sheltered gulf biting into the south-west coast, about 250 miles long and 120 wide, open to the south-west by a mouth narrower than its body. Open coasts east and south.

High ground: a broad highland of gorges and passes filling the north-west, and a broken spine of hills and low mountains running north to south down the middle-east, from near the north-east coast to the hills behind the south coast. Lowlands: the west, the country round the gulf, the south, the south-east. Weather comes off the western and northern seas, so the west and north are wet and the ground east of the spine is drier grass.

At the middle of the land, inside the ring of seats and in no seat's country, a round bare upland heath about forty miles across, pale and treeless, with open rolling plain around it.

## Rivers
Every river runs downhill to a coast. Nine systems, far apart.
1. The gorge river: rises in the north-west highland and runs north through gorges to the cold sea.
2. The west river: rises on the highland's western foothills and runs slow and winding west through forest and marsh to the west coast.
3. The long gulf river: rises on the southern face of the north-west highland and runs south-east then south across the western plain to the head of the gulf.
4. The middle gulf river: rises on the plain south-west of the central heath and runs south-west to the head of the gulf.
5. The hill gulf river: rises in the low ore hills east of the gulf and runs west to the head of the gulf. Rivers 3, 4 and 5 part into many channels and reach the gulf together in one wide delta, with reed islands, and pale salt flats on its seaward side.
6. The south river: rises at the south end of the spine and runs south, with one wide bend under stony hills, to the south coast.
7. The spring river: rises from springs in low hills in the south-east and runs south-east into a braided lowland of many channels and marsh, to a shifting delta on the south-east coast.
8. The east rivers: two short rivers rise on the spine's eastern face in wooded uplands and run east across broad grass to the east coast, joining before the sea.
9. The bend river: rises at the north end of the spine and runs north-east to the north-east coast, with a great slow bend and marsh in its lower course.

## The eight seats
A ring, spaced evenly in rough terms, each in the country its god asked for, each many weeks' walk from the next, none crowded and none cut off.
- god-sea: the north coast at its middle. A cold broken shore of skerries, coves and small harbors under headlands; thin soil; hills behind.
- god-secrets: the north-east. Firm ground inside the great bend of the bend river, marsh at its edges, a walled town.
- god-beasts: the east. Wooded uplands on the spine's eastern face, falling to broad grazing plains crossed by the east rivers, wet ground in the hollows.
- god-rivers: the south-east. Springs in low hills, the braided lowland, fords and floodplain, the shifting delta.
- god-dreams: the south. The wide bend of the south river under stony hills, dark bottom land, a quarry scar, a town on the stony rise above the bend.
- the player's god: the south-west, at the head of the gulf, where the three gulf rivers part and meet in the delta. An old levee of firm clay at the head of the delta; reed islands and channels; salt flats toward the open water of the gulf; low hills behind, to the east, with ore and clay; ground hard to cross except by bank and channel. As the Day 3 brief described it.
- god-healing: the west. The lower west river, slow, with marsh at its edges, forest standing behind, flat country, no mountains in sight, a field of old mounds.
- god-thresholds: the north-west. The highland of gorges, passes and fords on the gorge river, where the ways between the west and the north must go.

## The player's wishes for its land (Day 3), placed or not
- The house on the highest firm bank where the main track meets the main channel: placed. The town stands on the old levee at the head of the delta, and the worn track runs along the levee to it.
- The drum on the highest bank: ordinary; with the town.
- Salt pans on the flats: placed, on the salt flats seaward of the delta.
- The hill country as the source of ore and charcoal: placed, the low hills east of the gulf (river 5).
- Ships and open water: placed in part. The gulf is sheltered water and boats work it; beyond its mouth is open sea, and there are harbor towns on other coasts for ships to go to (the league, below). How far the town's own boats go is its people's record.
- An old road toward the hills: placed. An old road, sunken and grassed over in stretches, runs from the delta's head east into the ore hills and on.

## Peoples without a god
About half the land is held by some people and half is open. Four godless peoples, by the scale (the blend gave 5 for what lies past the borders).
- The old river kingdom: on the western plain along the long gulf river, upriver of the delta by some weeks, between the gulf, the west and the central plain. A few old cities, proud and rooted. About forty thousand.
- The hill holds: mining holds along the middle and south of the spine, a league of small stone towns. Iron is theirs to sell and they know it. About fifteen thousand.
- The harbor league: a string of harbor towns on the east coast between the grazing plains and the braided lowland, sea traders in coasting ships. About twenty-five thousand.
- The water folk: not human. A people of the water margin who own no land and give their dead to the water, living in boat bands on the lower rivers and sheltered coasts of the south and south-west. About three thousand in all, in bands of a few score.
The second kind, the slow long-memoried people, was a rare candidate and came up present as one small enclave [r-0143]: a quarter of a few hundred in the old river kingdom's chief city, keeping their own law of guest and debt and their own books. They hold no land and are counted with that kingdom's country, not as a fifth people.

## Open country
The central plain and the heath; the hills behind the north coast; the forest of the far west beyond the mounds; the dry grass between the spine and the harbor league; the coast and hills south-west of the gulf's mouth; the northern end of the spine.

## The center
The center point is the middle of the round heath. On it stands a ring of great standing stones about three miles across. The ring is smaller than the sheet can paint: the heath is asked of the painter by its size, and the ring is data only, placed at tracing.

## The warm stone
By the lore it lies between a pair of seats, in godless country. Placed by roll among the eight adjacent pairs round the ring of seats: face 8, god-thresholds and god-sea [r-0141]. Where along the stretch: face 99, so 25 plus 49.5, 74.5 hundredths of the way from god-thresholds's seat toward god-sea's [r-0142], which falls in the open hills behind the north coast, nearer the coast seat and outside its country. Data only.

## Terrain on the Night 1 list, each present
Several large river systems far apart (nine); the sheltered gulf with its delta, salt flats and reed islands; a second braided lowland with marsh (south-east); a cold broken coast with small harbors (north); low hills with ore, clay and springs (east of the gulf; the south-east spring hills); floodplain and marsh (south river, west river, bend river); forest behind slow lowland water (west); wooded uplands falling to grazing plains (east); a belt of gorges, passes, fords and narrows (north-west).

## Underground
None. The world has no underground (Night 1). Buried things are sites on the surface sheet, placed as data at tracing and on 3b.
