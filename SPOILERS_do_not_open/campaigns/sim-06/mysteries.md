## E01 The Long Lowering
Kind: epic. Span: 60 years and more. Related to: L01, L02, L03, L04, L05, S09.
Answers questions: yes

### Truth
The star over Whitmoor [r09] has been sinking since 1,668 BA and sinks faster each century. It stands 94 miles over the Whitmoor Ring [f76] tonight and comes down in Year 485. When it comes down it ends everything particular under it and makes the ground new, as the last fall did 60,400 BA. A thing held densely enough comes through as itself: a name sung by many mouths for many years, a thing a god has bound itself to, a gift a god has shed into a place or a breed. No god can stop the fall. The answer that has to be carried out, once found, is what to bring through and how, and how long the land lasts before it. Every great working of mortal magic brings the fall a year nearer; a land that keeps its particular things holds the date. Carrying names through needs the carriers' consent and many peoples singing, which is more than one god's people.
The central measurements, each earned and none delivered:
- That the star is lower, and by how much a year. Earned by a god's own marks at one place read fifteen or more years apart; by a mortal the god set to keep marks; or by standing at the Gannel Stones [f80] and seeing where the old frame points.
- That the sinking quickens. Earned by a second interval on the god's own marks; or by reading the notches on the Gannel Stones [f80] and learning their dates, which only the Mollen's books at Aldermoot [f70] hold and which the Mollen give for a price.
- That the star comes down on the Ring. Earned by going to Whitmoor [r09] on a dim night.
- That a held thing came through the last fall. Earned by opening the Kennoch Gates [f64], which the Sarrow hold, or by going below ground at Haskhold [f72], which the holds forbid to strangers.
None of these is a scheduled visitor. The carriers come on a clock and bring no measurement: they do not know the star's rate or its day.

### Body
The star. The Gannel Stones [f80]: two uprights and a notched third on the grass of the Hollin Reaches [r08], whose frame now points about a hand's breadth above the star. The blue-grey glass under the old workings at Haskhold [f72] and behind the Kennoch Gates [f64], and the one whole stone in it, cut with the name Ulmarre. The Mollen of Aldermoot [f70], whose books alone date the notches.
Limit: The Mollen of Aldermoot know every bargain entered in their books since the Assize's founding, the parties, the terms and the day; they do not know why any party acted, anything never entered, or anything of the star, the gods or the fading; they answer only what is asked exactly, to one who pays the search price in advance, one question to a search; and of a matter not in the books they say it is not in the books.

### Cost if ignored
Nothing falls on a god for not measuring. What it loses is the means to read the other clocks: the losses of the fading places, the Ring's hunger in Year 38, and the voices will each arrive unexplained. Every great working, anywhere, by anyone, brings the fall one year nearer and the Ring's clock one year nearer.
Cost check: no agreed weakness or ruling bears on it. Checked: god-rivers's Calling the water (L-0016) is a god's power and no mortal working, so it moves no clock; the player's information powers (L-0006) take no central measurement, as that entry already found.

### Clock or trigger
Clocked: the descent itself, read from the figures in `deep-history.md`. Triggered: each great working. Rows E01-a and E01-b in `event-schedule.md`.

### Clues
#### The names for the star
Location: every people (the delta's name for it is in `world-bible.md`; the others in each record)
What is shown: peoples far apart call the star by names that say it is low or near, and the oldest songs call it high. RUMORED, earliest Year 1.
Readings: it has always hung low here and the songs flatter it; it has come lower; the old singers lived somewhere it looked higher.

#### The frame that points at nothing
Location: Gannel Stones [f80]
What is shown: sighting between the two uprights and across the top of the third, the line passes about a hand's breadth above the star. The third stone has three notches down its near face, the lowest still well above where a line to the star would cut it. REPORTED by plainsfolk as "the stones that miss", earliest Year 4; SEEN by a god that goes.
Readings: the stones framed a star that has moved; the ground has heaved and the stones lean; the stones were for the moon or a hill fire and never for the star; the notches are a tally of something else.

#### A god's own marks
Location: wherever the god sets them
What is shown: two marks fixed in stone and read again after fifteen years show the star lower by a little; a second fifteen years show a little more than the first. SEEN, earliest Year 16 if set in Year 1.
Readings: the star is sinking; the stone settled; the land between is rising; the eye errs.

#### The surveyors' line
Location: the Mollen quarter of Aldermoot [f70], in the Ninth Book
What is shown: an entry of wages paid to surveyors in the Assize's first years, "for the stones on the grass, a finger under the old kings' mark". REPORTED, only if a god or its envoy pays the Mollen's price for a search.
Readings: the star had dropped a finger by then; the surveyors set their notch carelessly; the "old kings' mark" was itself wrong.

#### The glass that rings
Location: the old workings under Haskhold [f72]
What is shown: miners say the oldest adit ends on a floor of blue-grey glass that rings under a pick and will not take a wedge. REPORTED by a hold smith in drink, earliest Year 9.
Readings: slag from a furnace of the Kennoch kings; a floor someone laid; a thing from the sky.

#### The stone in the glass
Location: behind the Kennoch Gates [f64]
What is shown: a cut stair down to the same glass, and set in it, whole and unmelted, one dressed stone with a name on it. SEEN only by whoever opens the gates.
Readings: someone cut into the glass and set it there; it was there when the glass was made and did not burn; it is a grave marker.

### Branches
#### The god decides the ground is sinking, or the stones have shifted
What the world does: nothing answers. Levees built or villages moved uphill cost the labor and the years they cost. River gauges and tide marks do not agree with the star marks, and a god that keeps both sees it.

#### The god sets marks and waits
What the world does: the marks read true. After the first interval the star is lower; after the second, lower by more.

#### The god tries to hold the star up, or pull it down, by a great working
What the world does: the working is done at its cost, a dead patch is left where it was worked, the fall comes a year nearer and the Ring's clock a year nearer. The star does not answer.

#### The god stores something
What the world does: names given to the carriers are carried (L04). A thing the god binds itself to, or sheds a gift into, is treated differently by the fading from what lies beside it: it keeps its kinds while the next valley loses one.

#### The god never measures
What the world does: the clocks run as written. Nothing further is taken for not knowing.

### Never found
Why the land keeps accounts. What Ulmarre was. Whether anything has ever not faded.

## L01 The Ring That Eats
Kind: long. Span: 38 years. Related to: E01, L03, L05.
Answers questions: no

### Truth
The Whitmoor Ring [f76] is one living thing, older than the last fall, that eats what the star rains thickest at the center. It is fed from above on dim nights by the thread. It wants the fall and will be under it. Being looked at long, it wears names out of the people who look: the herders of the rim have none for each other. It grows as the land fades, and in Year 38 it is large enough to draw sleepers to itself.

### Body
The Whitmoor Ring [f76], about a hundred and fifty pale stones in a circle about three miles across at the middle of Whitmoor [r09]. The thread. The rim herders, about three hundred, whom the plainsfolk call the pointers.

### Cost if ignored
From Year 38 the rim herders walk into the Ring in their sleep and are found at dawn standing among the stones, alive, unhurt and without speech. From Year 40 it reaches the nearest settled edges: plainsfolk camps on the Cander Plain [r16] and the Hollin Reaches [r08] first, then the eastern steadings of the Aldermark [r10], then any hamlet a god's people have by then planted within four days' walk of the heath.
Cost check: god-dreams's Keeping Sleep and Walk the Dream (her card's Descriptions and Drawbacks) are not contradicted: whether a household she keeps is drawn is unruled until it happens. No ledger ruling bears on it.

### Clock or trigger
Clocked: Year 38 [r-0178]. Triggered: each great working anywhere brings it one year nearer; each hundred of the rim herders settled away from the heath before Year 30 puts it one year later, three at most. Rows L01-a to L01-c in `event-schedule.md`.

### Clues
#### The thread
Location: Whitmoor [r09], seen from its rim
What is shown: on a dim night a line of blue light stands from the star to the middle of the heath for as long as the dimness lasts. Farther off only the dimming is seen. REPORTED by plainsfolk, earliest Year 2; SEEN from the rim.
Readings: the star drinks from the heath; the star feeds something there; a fire on the heath lights the mist.

#### The stones
Location: Whitmoor Ring [f76]
What is shown: the stones carry no tool mark, are warm to the hand in frost, and the turf inside the circle has no seed heads. A stone pushed over is upright again after the next dim night. SEEN by a god that goes.
Readings: a hot spring under the heath; a holy place tended in secret; a thing alive.

#### The pointers
Location: the rim of Whitmoor [r09]
What is shown: the herders have words for sheep, weather and every tool, and none for one another. Asked a person's name, they point at the person. An old one, if a god stays a night, says the stones do not like to be called. SEEN and REPORTED by a god that goes.
Readings: a custom; a ban laid by some old ruler; poverty of speech; the stones did it.

#### The sleep on the rim
Location: the rim of Whitmoor [r09], asleep
What is shown: whoever sleeps there dreams of tall shapes with no faces, counting, and of islands that move on a grey sea. SEEN, the god's own.
Readings: the dead of an old people; the star's own thoughts; the Ring's.

### Branches
#### The god attacks the Ring
What the world does: fire does nothing to stone. Stones pulled down stand again. Men kept at the work for a season come home unable to say their own names. Nothing else is taken.

#### The god settles the rim herders elsewhere, or gives them names
What the world does: those who leave the heath take up names again over some years, the young first. The Ring's clock goes later as the trigger gives.

#### The god worships it, or has its people do so
What the world does: it gives nothing back. The worshippers lose their names faster.

#### The god visits and does nothing
What the world does: the clock runs as written.

### Never found
How old it is. Whether it knows it is looked at.

## L02 The Warm Stone's Kin
Kind: long. Span: 23 years. Related to: E01, L04.
Answers questions: no

### Truth
The unnamed fallen stone [f77] is a piece of what the far fall threw that did not gather into a god. It holds the same memories as the gods, thinner, and dreams them at whoever sleeps near. The gods are the same stuff gathered denser: each is what a dead country held longest. A god can learn from the stone that it is a remainder and that the others are too. It cannot learn from it where that country was.

### Body
The unnamed fallen stone [f77], warm, blue-grey, heavy for its size, in a scorched hollow of the Bren Hills [r05], 74.5 miles on foot over open ground from the unnamed harbor town [f30] and 209.8 from god-thresholds's landing. The shepherds who saw it fall.

### Cost if ignored
If no god has reached it by Year 23, the shepherds of the Bren Hills [r05] have raised a cairn over it and a following has formed that sleeps beside it and recites what it dreams: a kneeling crowd, a river, a door being broken, a chain not closed. The stone lends them hold in return, so the following is no god's and grows. If one god reaches it before the others, that god learns the shape of the others' pasts, which is a lever.
Cost check: no agreed weakness or ruling bears on it. Checked: L-0010 (the player's sealed want is applied to nothing; this cost is the lore's own trigger and was placed by rolls r-0141 and r-0142).

### Clock or trigger
Triggered: Year 23 [r-0179] if no god has reached it; whichever god reaches it first. Rows L02-a and L02-b in `event-schedule.md`.

### Clues
#### The night it fell
Location: the Bren Hills [r05], among the shepherds; then the unnamed harbor town [f30], by the wool trade
What is shown: shepherds tell of a light that came down the night before a certain dawn and of a stone in a burned hollow that stayed warm through the winter. RUMORED, earliest Year 2 on the Garrow Shore [r01].
Readings: a piece of the star; a sign; a thing to sell.

#### What sleepers dream
Location: the unnamed fallen stone [f77]
What is shown: those who sleep within a few paces dream of a great crowd kneeling at a river none of them has seen, with their hands in the water. Some dream other things: a door broken in, a lamp passed from hand to hand. REPORTED by shepherds, earliest Year 5.
Readings: the stone is a god asleep; the stone is cursed; the shepherds are telling tales to strangers.

#### What a god dreams there
Location: the unnamed fallen stone [f77]
What is shown: a god that touches it and sleeps dreams the same crowd, and one thing in the dream is its own and known to it. SEEN, the god's own.
Readings: the stone is a dead sibling; the stone is where the god came from; the stone is the god's own past, stolen.

### Branches
#### The god carries it home as a holy thing
What the world does: its town dreams the god's wound. Within about four years there is a following of the god's grief, which is remembering and not following, and does not feed the god.

#### The god buries it, sinks it or breaks it
What the world does: buried or sunk, it dreams at whoever is nearest, and the shepherds dig. Broken, each piece dreams more faintly and the pieces travel.

#### The god shows it to another god
What the world does: both dream. What each does with what it learned of the other is theirs.

#### The god leaves it
What the world does: the Year 23 trigger runs.

### Never found
Where the far country lay, as a place one could walk to.

## L03 The Stair Up
Kind: long. Span: 30 years. Related to: E01, L01.
Answers questions: yes

### Truth
A god grows by being held in mind. Under this star there is a weight past which a god lightens and rises into the star: 73 on the scale where an arriving god stands near 20. From 58 the voices of those already risen begin to reach it. Past the weight there are four things a god can do: rise; bind itself to something very much itself (a mountain, a river, a people's one sung name), which then spends faster; give its power away into people and places and so stay under; or stop growing. Maunath rose from the Maun Stair [f27] in 416 BA and took none of the other three.
Limit: The voices that come down know what was true of their own countries and of rising; they do not know the year of the fall, what a risen god becomes after it, or anything the land has done since each of them rose; they reach only a god whose standing has come to 58 or one asleep inside the Ring on a dim night; they speak in weather and old images of islands that move, never in plain words, at most once in a dim night; and to most askings they give nothing.

### Body
The god's own shadow. The Maun Stair [f27]. The steps of the old high house at Aldermoot [f70], soft at their edges. The bronze bell and the jar of notched sticks under the quay of the unnamed harbor town [f30], which are what Hernaud left when it chose to be forgotten. The star.

### Cost if ignored
A god whose standing reaches 58 begins to be spoken to in sleep. The voices offer little, under this star, and what they offer is always a power that costs weight. A god that stays above 73 for three years together rises on the next dim night. The world warns three times, in the shadow, in the god's own house stones, and in the voices, and not a fourth.
Cost check: no card names the weight; each sealed power file carries it as its ceiling line. No agreed weakness or ledger ruling bears on it.

### Clock or trigger
Triggered: a standing of 58; three years together above 73. Rows L03-a and L03-b in `event-schedule.md`. The measure is `state/standing.md`.

### Clues
#### The larger one that drowned
Location: the unnamed town [f20], and the Maun Stair [f27]
What is shown: the delta people say a larger god drowned at the stair and that this is why the tide keeps coming. The lowest steps are under water at every tide. RUMORED, earliest Year 1.
Readings: a god drowned; a god left by sea; a flood story with a god put into it; a god went somewhere else from there.

#### What the boat people saw
Location: the water folk of the Gulf of Maun [r31]
What is shown: an old boat-mother, asked at dusk and paid in hooks, says her mother's mothers saw "the heavy one go light at the steps", and will not say the rest. REPORTED, only if asked.
Readings: it rose into the air; it died and they will not say so; a tale told to land people for hooks.

#### The soft steps
Location: the old high house at Aldermoot [f70]
What is shown: the stone steps of the Assize's high house are rounded like river stones though little walked, and a chancery roll says the patron "went down to the sea in the hundred and eighty-fourth year". SEEN and REPORTED by a god or envoy that goes.
Readings: old stone weathers; the house was holy and the stone blessed; something was wrong with the patron near the end.

#### The bell under the quay
Location: the unnamed harbor town [f30]
What is shown: a promise kept with no one to keep it for: a lamp, a bell no one answers, a jar of sticks no one reads. SEEN by anyone in the town.
Readings: the people's own custom, grown up without a god; a god that died; a god that left; a god that is still under the water and silent.

#### The god's own lightness
Location: the god's own body
What is shown: past a standing of about 50 the god's shadow pales and it is harder to be angry; past 58 it dreams weather that is not this land's. SEEN, the god's own.
Readings: the star's favor; the star's hunger; a sickness of gods; the natural size of a god.

### Branches
#### The god, feeling light, asks for more worship to root itself
What the world does: it lightens faster. The three warnings come. If it stays above the weight three years it rises, and its people are left as the Assize was.

#### The god takes what a voice offers
What the world does: the power is real and the weight is added. Nothing is hidden in the bargain and nothing is explained.

#### The god refuses a voice
What the world does: nothing is taken. On a later dim night another image comes, of the same islands seen from under water.

#### The god gives its power away into people and places
What the world does: its standing falls with each gift, the gifts hold, and the country round them keeps its kinds a little better.

#### The god binds itself to a thing
What the world does: the weight is carried and the thing spends faster: a mountain's springs first, a river's bends, a people's memory of everything but the god.

### Never found
What Maunath is now. Whether Hernaud is anywhere.

## L04 The Counted Names
Kind: long. Span: 36 years. Related to: E01, L02.
Answers questions: yes

### Truth
A name sung by enough mouths for enough years is dense enough to cross faded country and to come through a fall. The carriers are a people who live by this: they walk names from dying countries to living ones and plant them. A line of about forty has kept a round here since 317 BA, every 36 years. Their forebears crossed the gods' own country long before it fell, and the figures they have sung here ever since are why several peoples were keeping a place for someone before the gods came. They will carry a country's names out if they are given freely, and unasked if they judge the country worth it.
Limit: The carriers know the names in their lists and what each was the name of, as it was told to them; they do not know what the gods are, how fast the star sinks or when it falls, and they have no list of what is to come; anyone who feeds them and sits the night may ask a few questions between lists; they answer plainly or say they do not know; and they sing nothing and answer nothing under compulsion.

### Body
The carriers, on the road in Years 7 to 9 and again from Year 43. The sea-door of Port Sennet [f75], which they named in 245 BA and whose name the town says under it. The kept places: the low seat of the unnamed spring town [f45], the throne kept in the fen at the unnamed court steading [f55], the empty place at the bend town's table [f50], the unlatched door of the unnamed winter city [f60].

### Cost if ignored
A god that has not dealt with the carriers by the end of their round in Year 9 has nothing in their lists, and nothing of its country goes out before Year 43. A god that holds them by force loses the Year 43 round for the whole land: they do not come.
Cost check: no agreed weakness or ruling bears on it. Checked: god-thresholds's guest's law (L-0020) and god-secrets's Kept Word are the gods' own and are not touched by a people's passing.

### Clock or trigger
Clocked: the round begins in Year 7 [r-0169] and returns in Year 43 [r-0170]. Triggered: by how each god deals with them. Rows L04-a to L04-c in `event-schedule.md`.

### Clues
#### Places kept for someone
Location: the unnamed spring town [f45], the unnamed court steading [f55], the unnamed bend town [f50], the unnamed winter city [f60]
What is shown: peoples far apart, with no dealings, each kept a seat, a place or a door for someone not yet come, and none can say when the custom began. SEEN by a god that meets any two of them.
Readings: the gods were foretold; the peoples were made ready by some earlier god; every people keeps such a custom and it means nothing.

#### The year the singers came
Location: any settled people, among the old
What is shown: people past forty remember walkers who sang lists all night, asked the name of every village and sang it back, and traded nothing. REPORTED if asked, from Year 1.
Readings: spies counting villages; a holy order; mad folk; a people with a trade no one understands.

#### The door's name
Location: Port Sennet [f75]
What is shown: crews say one word aloud as they pass in under the old sea-door, and call it the door's name. No one in the town can say what tongue it is. SEEN by anyone who sails in.
Readings: a founder's name; a charm against wreck; a word from some other country.

#### The lists
Location: wherever the carriers stop, Years 7 to 9
What is shown: among thousands of names sung, some are of villages that stand now, some of places gone, and some that no one here has ever heard. SEEN by any who sit the night.
Readings: invented; a dead tongue; the names of real places very far off.

#### What a carrier says
Location: the carriers
What is shown: asked what they do, one says they carry names across the bare country, and that a name carried long enough comes back. REPORTED, only if a god speaks with them itself.
Readings: a way of speaking; a faith; a plain account of a trade.

### Branches
#### The god gives them its people's names
What the world does: the names are learned and sung. In later years a trader from far off may have heard one sung elsewhere.

#### The god holds them to learn the songs
What the world does: they sing nothing. The songs are not in any one of them. The Year 43 round does not come.

#### The god asks them its own lost name
What the world does: they do not know which name is whose. They sing what they carry, the far country's old place names among the rest, and whether the god knows one for its own is the god's.

#### The god lets them pass
What the world does: they pass. Nothing is carried.

### Never found
Where the first of them came from. What they do with a name once its country is gone.

## L05 The Thinning Ground
Kind: long. Span: 30 years. Related to: E01, L01.
Answers questions: no

### Truth
A place fades when it has spent more of what makes things particular than the star gives back. It is patchy, and strikes the oldest, most storied and most worked ground first: twelve places now. A faded thing is still a thing and no longer a particular one: clay with no temper, wheat that weighs true and feeds thin, a river that is only water. Mortal magic is the same spending done by hand. The worker moves what makes one thing particular into another, and pays first with herself, toward her own name. A gathering of more than twenty workers under one patron in one place, or one working that moves a river, breaks a siege or calls a country's weather, leaves a dead patch and tips that country into overdraw. The Assize's school on the Pellow Downs [r15] did both.

### Body
The twelve fading places in `deep-history.md`. The old yards on the Pellow Downs [r15]. The Stopped Spring [f48] and the Dry Braid [f49]. The Kings' Acres round Aldermoot [f70]. Any worker grown old in her craft.

### Cost if ignored
Each of the twelve places loses one kind a decade, the first in the year its row gives. From Year 31 every god-led country that is in overdraw (it holds a dead patch, a school, or one of the twelve) loses one kind a decade as well. A school or a great working in any country adds a dead patch there, makes hollow people of the workers used up, and brings that country's next loss ten years nearer.
Cost check: the peoples' workers as fitted (each record's magic section; L-0024 for the tide-reckoner) are a few in a town and far under the threshold, so none is contradicted. A factor's lost night of sleep (L-0025) is the same small cost and no great working. god-rivers's Calling the water (L-0016), god-beasts's storm and every other power of a god are not mortal magic and move none of these clocks.

### Clock or trigger
Clocked: the twelve rows, and the Year 31 row. Triggered: a school or a great working. Rows L05-a to L05-n in `event-schedule.md`.

### Clues
#### The worker who answers to what she does
Location: any town; in the unnamed town [f20], the tide-reckoner
What is shown: an old worker is called by her work and not by a name, and does not seem to miss one. SEEN, earliest Year 1.
Readings: age; a custom of respect; the work took it.

#### What a working takes
Location: wherever a worker works
What is shown: when a worker warms, mends or finds, something near goes dull for a while: a jar frosts, a dog does not know its master for an hour. At the heart of a hard working there is a blue flicker. SEEN by a god that watches closely, earliest Year 3.
Readings: the cold has to go somewhere; the star blesses the work; chance.

#### The old yards
Location: the Pellow Downs [r15], beside the Old Rudd Way [f23]
What is shown: a stretch of down where the grass has no seed heads and the clay, dug and fired, cracks whatever is done to it. The delta's kiln-masters blame the diggers for grit. REPORTED at the unnamed town [f20], earliest Year 1; SEEN by a god that goes.
Readings: the diggers cheat; a salt or a burn in the ground; a curse on the vaults nearby; the ground is worn out.

#### Wheat that feeds thin
Location: the Kings' Acres round Aldermoot [f70]
What is shown: bread from the oldest fields fills and does not sustain, and those who can afford it buy grain from the newer villages. REPORTED by river traders, earliest Year 4.
Readings: a blight; tired soil; the millers adulterate it.

#### The river that is only water
Location: the Dry Braid [f49], and the Stopped Spring [f48]
What is shown: a bed where water lies and does not run, and nothing in it has a particular kind; some days' walk off, a spring under a great flat stone with a hand-shaped hollow in it. REPORTED by the spring town's people, earliest Year 2.
Readings: a river dammed and silted; a river cursed; a river whose spring was stopped.

### Branches
#### The god bans mortal magic among its people
What the world does: the workers stop or hide. The old ones who were kept warm by neighbors for their work go cold. No clock moves either way: the small workings were not the spending.

#### The god gathers workers into a school, or orders a great working
What the world does: the work is done and is strong. A dead patch is left, the used-up workers go hollow, and the country's next loss comes ten years nearer.

#### The god treats a faded water as drought
What the world does: channels dug to it carry water in, and within a season that water is like the rest.

#### The god has things kept particular
What the world does: where names are sung, breeds kept apart and old customs held, the losses slow, and a kind thought lost may be found again in that country.

### Never found
Whether anything, anywhere, has ever not faded.

## S01 The Seventh Weight
Kind: short. Span: 6 years. Related to: S09, S10.
Answers questions: no

### Truth
The last Weightward of the unnamed town [f20] sank the largest of the seven old standard weights in the main channel of the River Alder [f03] on a night of the spring flood, so that the Aldermark's old due on the market, which is written in that measure, could never be struck. He was past seventy and stood in the water to his waist. He died nine days later of a fever in the chest and his age. No one killed him. This answers ruling L-0029.

### Body
