# Sealed past: the player's god

The rest of the story behind the outline, tied to the common past (`gods-sealed/common-past.md`). No agent reads this, the DM included.

## The rest of the story
It is the pattern Tolcarrath held of the reckoner at the door of the House of the Even Pan: not one man, the office, remembered as one figure across some thirty holders. The city's memory of it was double from the start, and that is the god's fog. It was the one who never gave a false measure; children were told so. It was also the one who signed the last tally.
The last tally was true by weight. The reckoner of that season knew the grain was wrong and had no measure for what was wrong with it; it weighed what it should and counted what it should. The reckoner signed. The city ate it through the winter and starved on full bellies. So there is a debt, and the god's sense that it both owed and was owed is exact: the house owed the city its winter, and the city owed the house eleven years of unpaid dues that the house had carried so that the poor quarters could eat. Neither sum was ever struck. Whose the larger is, nothing recorded settles, and the Keeper does not settle it either: the pattern holds both, and that is the truth of it.
The shorter word under "Weigher" is Dhen: what the city called the reckoner, from the sound of a pan coming to rest. It went out of use the night the door was broken.
The dent: the crowd that broke the door threw the house's own master weight, the even stone, and it struck the door scales. The dent is the mark of the house's standard thrown at the house's reckoner by the people it was kept for.
The empty pocket: the reckoner carried the even stone in that pocket, against the body, so that the standard never left the one who answered for it. The stone was thrown and not seen again. The city remembered the reckoner afterward with the pocket empty, and so it is empty, and the god keeps it so without knowing why. It is for the standard.

## What was lost and is not remembered
The name Dhen. The name of the house and of the city. The faces of the poor quarters it carried on the books. That the last tally was true by every measure it had. That the pocket held the even stone.

## How it might surface
Effects only; nothing here is scheduled, and nothing is placed where this god is sure to meet it.
- Food that weighs true and does not feed exists in this world too, wherever the land has spent itself; the god may meet in a pan the thing it once signed for.
- The carriers sing lists of names from countries that are gone. Whether Tolcarrath, the house or Dhen is among them is set on Night 3b, by the same rule as every god's lost names.
- The warm stone that fell with them dreams a kneeling crowd and an unseen river at those who sleep near it. A sleeper may dream a door being broken.
- god-secrets carries a tally stick it cannot read. It is the house's door tally for the last season.
