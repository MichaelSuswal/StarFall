# Release: god-dreams, Year 3, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 3, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 4, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 4 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- In the yard of the watch you told the Warden of the Wall his rules are yours to keep, and told the four at your door what they sit beside. The captain asked how a man is to know the standing body from the sleeping one; one of the four asked whether they are to move you if fire comes into the yard.
- The Keeper of the Count has your word on the shrine flame and your list of afraid days beside his cords.
- The court judged the harbor house's offer and accepted it. Eight went down the river on the twenty-fifth day of the second month with stone, linen, pots and oil, and your words by heart.
- Keeping Sleep went as you set it: a pair of households a night by lot, three on three named nights of deep winter. At those dawns the wall's men saw what you had told them they would see.
- You sat in the Night Sitter's yard on a night she sat, and kept the kettle and the lamp. She said a sitter is not taught: a girl sits and finds she can or does not. The seat's crier called for young women. Of three who sat the winter beside her, a hamlet girl found she could.
- The families of the four who went west told you the four came home in the first month of last year, thin and alive, as you were told then. A father asked: "Who told you they had not come home?"
- You walked the hamlets within a morning of the wall in spring and lit a flame only where asked. The headmen asked whether a dim night in the eighth month was yours, and you said you do not know.
- The harbor party did not come home before the cold. A boat grounded on a bar under its stone, no ship lay at the mouth, and they went round the shore with the samples. They were given bread and three nights. The leader spoke your words on the third night to the factor. The house took the samples, sold them salt at a standing price below the carriers', and named its order: sixty blocks a year dressed to a cord it gave, to be lifted by its own ship at the river's mouth. The gales shut the shore and they wintered at the harbor. They came up the river at high water, home in the eighth month, all eight.
- What the leader heard on the harbor's quays, listening as you told him to, and brought home: at a market town in the delta of the great river in the west, a stranger who reads scales for a pinch of barley has been seated as keeper of the town's weights, and says before the whole market that it is a god. No one at the harbor asked him whether another like you stands on that coast. He heard of none there.
- The iron from the mining town came greyer and softer from late winter. The town had a third lean winter for salt.
- You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- The captain of the wall: how a man is to know the standing body from the sleeping one.
- One of the four: whether they are to move you if fire comes into the yard.
- The father at the second house: "Who told you they had not come home?"

## What the Keeper ruled
- Nothing was put to the Keeper.

## The sky
The star dimmed on six nights of your year, more again than last year: one in the first month, a pair in the fifth, a pair in the seventh and one in the eighth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people count fourteen such nights since you came.

## Your people at the year's turn
The town and the kept households hold you as the one awaited. The court has the harbor's order and the cord.
