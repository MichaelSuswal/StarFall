# Release: god-sea, Year 3, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 3, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 4, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 4 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- At the tide door you answered the Warden-Monarch's notches: you do not cause the dimming and do not know what does. She said your words back whole, keeps the count on a stick, and carried your words to the Eldest Midwife by her own mouth in the fourth week of the first month.
- The Midwife said it was the first thing out of that water to tell her what it failed at, and named a bedside: the eldest of the smokers, past knowing, in the last house before the racks on the lowest step. The Warden gave the house to the water and you heard it. You came once, at the lowest tide at dusk in the second month, said what you count, and showed your shape when she asked and kept still. She offered the clerks' list of the year's dead each autumn, through the Warden; it is in the jar.
- The night's listening after the first meeting: the coast's ordinary day. No hull that is not the town's or a known coaster's. Nothing that is not tide.
- Sound Timber twice: the heel of the chain post in the fourth month, and in the eleventh the keel of the headland cove's crab boat, chosen by the Master. Both are sound.
- In the fifth month a long-liner of the east cove ran for the mouth in a heavy swell with three aboard. You lit the lamp at the water's edge and the tenders kept it right. All three came in. The harbor water would not take you for a season after, and you lay off the point.
- Through the Warden you asked the Harbormaster about a pair of boats under the chain's line. He answered that the town lost them the autumn before you came, nine men, that they were seen as a pair of lamps running for the mouth after dark and then none, that the chain was down by his order and the book says so, and that he takes a tide and not a word. In the eighth month he worked a lead and grapnel along the line on a pair of mornings and took nothing that is a hull. You heard the Warden tell his answer to the water in the ninth month.
- The oldest net-mender's answer was in the jar on a slip of hide: the old tellers put the drowned town at the shore end of the old causeway whose cut stones show at the lowest tides, west along the shore. In spring you swam there by night, 66.5 miles by the shore. What is there: the causeway's cut stones running out under the water, worn round at the edges; at its shore end, under sand and weed, the footings of walls, a line of quay stone and post holes. Nothing kept by anyone. Nothing made within a lifetime. You marked them and told no one.
- You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- The Eldest Midwife, at the bedside: which people you did not keep, and whether it was Low Garrow; whether you count the dead as kept or as lost; what you want of the town.
- The Harbormaster, through the Warden: "Whoever asks under the chain's line is asking whether the chain was up." If the keeping knows anything of his chain it can say it to him and not to her.

## What the Keeper ruled
- Sound Timber twice in the year, weeks apart, is inside your card and last year's ruling.
- The lamp tended by the tenders for a night and a day, with you at the water's edge when it is lit, is inside last year's ruling.

## The sky
The star dimmed on six nights of your year, more again than last year: one in the first month, a pair in the fifth, a pair in the seventh and one in the eighth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people count fourteen such nights since you came.

## Your people at the year's turn
About 2,031. The court has done what it did the last winters. The Harbormaster found nothing under his line and says so.
