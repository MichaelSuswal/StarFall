# Release: god-thresholds, Year 3, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 3, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 4, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 4 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- At the ford the older watcher gave the count: trains as many as last year or near it, shorter, with fewer beasts. In the hall you answered the Steward, the Mother of the Hearth and, through her, the herder. The Steward asked what he is to do if the offerings fall short of the cord.
- You judged the grass at the sill table by the sill law; the captain of the column confirmed the line aloud. One household called it fair and did not like it.
- The counts: the Steward's, about twelve days short of the thaw at the full ration; the hearth mothers', about twenty. The stores were shared by hearth from the first snow, the war band last, and you told the captains yourself.
- The cords of every toll house were laid out in the hall by first snow. They agree with one another and with what was laid up at the hall. Against what you know of who crossed the pass and the notch you hold: the trains on the cords are the trains that crossed, near enough. The toll laid up for them is half what the old cords give for as many.
- In the fourth month the senior Doorwarden of the lower columns sat at your table and ate. The Reading: no. He means no harm to a guest at that table, and he learned the same of you. You taught him the oath that winter. No lesser mark was laid.
- The stores failed some weeks short of the thaw, for all the sharing and a cut ration. The last fortnight was eaten on dried meat, herd and seed. About three hundred died over the ordinary, the old and the youngest. The sowing was short.
- In the sixth month Kessan gave the order that the toll is raised by half from the thaw, and did not ask your leave. The Mother of the Hearth asked in your hearing whether the toll is the host's to raise or the sill's.
- At the thaw the Marshal and the first captain walked the far houses with no word sent, and counted stores, beasts and roofs. New roofs, good horses and full smokehouses, as last year. Each house had its account of a good year's barter, and the captain could not break one. They brought back the watchmen's word.
- With the first train after the thaw a letter came from the chancery of the river kingdom, to whoever holds the ford. It was read aloud at a rite fire in your hearing. It claims an old due: a yearly share of the toll of the ford and the passes, owed to the king as heir of the old power whose wardens kept the road, in a measure no one at the hall uses. The keeper of rite asked whether it should be answered. Kessan answered by the clerk's mouth that the Sarrow owe no share to a king they have not met; Idris Veld said the sill would hear a claim made in person.
- In summer you went up to the old cutting at the high gates and saw the cutting and no flower. The oldest herders say the line of the grass has come down a bowshot in their lives. You told the herder so.
- At each fire of the lower columns you asked what "as Marda held it" means, and hold each answer as that fire's.
- Five strangers were brought to you by the Marshal's order, all ordinary.
- You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- The Steward: "And if what is brought is less than the cord?"
- The Mother of the Hearth, in the hall: whether the toll is the host's to raise or the sill's.
- The keeper of rite who read the letter: whether it should be answered.

## What the Keeper ruled
- Nothing lay outside your card.

## The sky
The star dimmed on six nights of your year, more again than last year: one in the first month, a pair in the fifth, a pair in the seventh and one in the eighth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people count fourteen such nights since you came.

## Your people at the year's turn
About eighteen thousand, fewer by the winter's dead. The toll stands raised by half.
