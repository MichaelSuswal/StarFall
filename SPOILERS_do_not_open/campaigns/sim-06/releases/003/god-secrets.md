# Release: god-secrets, Year 3, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 3, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 4, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 4 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- The Granary Warden took your proposal on his own terms: he told the heads of the families himself, took the facing down himself, and kept you on the bench by the door. The far run was filled by the sack before a tally-keeper of each family, and the count of the whole store squared. By the Warden's count: the roll's old stock 9,950 sacks; what stood, 6,630; the gap, 3,320, a third of the roll. Both heads say the roll is wrong. Neither says where the grain went.
- At the council the families were heard before any blame. A motion to search houses and yards found no second. The count was cried at market as the Warden's, the far run's figure by itself. No one was put out of office.
- The Clerk gave you an evening with the old league's tallies in his house and put the sticks in your hand to count. Your count was right where his was out by one. When you held the third stick, and then one other, the marks opened to you: you read them as the hands that cut them would have. The second says this: a count of grain and salt owed by the old factory here to a salt house of the delta in the far west, at the factory's closing, and never struck off. You said nothing of it. For a day after you could not have spoken a false figure of that account. No one asked you for one.
- In spring the crews sounded the channel again: a little shallower at the gate than last spring. The Clerk has both springs on sticks.
- In the tenth month you went up the river as a weigher on a boat family's crew: 39.7 miles, four days up with a day lost to the current. The string lay three weeks at the boat families' landing at the river's head, on the low plain, where a drovers' track comes down from the grass country. The steersman said "Ivo Strand's water" over the side as they cast off. You came down in a pair of days and were back on the thirteenth of the eleventh month.
- What you heard there, among the drovers: the pass herders beyond the plain have had a guest at their winter hall for three winters, a woman with a staff and a key, whom the whole people feed and call Guest, and whom no one has seen eat. The drovers think it a herders' tale and tell it anyway. No one there looked for you. You turned the key on nothing.
- A girl of the boat families asked whether you make the star go dim, and you said you do not know.
- You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- Nothing asked of you went unanswered. The reed cutter's daughter asked whether you would hear from her what her father began; you told her to keep it until she knows whether it is hers to give.

## What the Keeper ruled
- Your word on the sky is noted; no ruling was asked.
- What happened with the tallies is yours, and is written on your card: any tally stick, knotted cord or private merchant's mark you hold in your hand, you can read as its maker would; for a day after, you cannot speak a false figure about that account. Counts and marks only. The one tally stick you carry stays shut to you.

## The sky
The star dimmed on six nights of your year, more again than last year: one in the first month, a pair in the fifth, a pair in the seventh and one in the eighth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people count fourteen such nights since you came.

## Your people at the year's turn
The store's shortfall is known and named. The Warden keeps his office.
