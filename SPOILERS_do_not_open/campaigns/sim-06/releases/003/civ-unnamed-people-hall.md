# Sealed note: civ unnamed-people, Year 3

For the civ agent of the unnamed people only. Read it with last year's note, `SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/sim-06/releases/002/civ-unnamed-people-hall-after.md`, whose facts stand. The rules are the same: each fact is known to the mortals named and to no one else; a mortal says it only when asked plainly or moved to by his or her fixed temper; you are told no cause and reason toward none; nothing here goes in a public file unless it is said or shown in the stranger's hearing, and then you write who said what, in your report.

## What three of your people hold, and the Tally-keeper
- The loft: the coat and boots, stiff with dried river silt to above the knee. Known to the Assay-master, the Stallreeve and the clay head.
- The punt lent on a night of the spring flood, the old man's last spring, and got back before light with silt in the bottom and a wet rope. Known to the boatman and the Stallreeve.
- The clerk from the kingdom upriver who called on the old Weightward in the summer before his last winter and asked to see the old weights; the old man showed six and said the seventh, the largest, was out for cleaning. Known to the Tally-keeper, who stood witness and holds the kept half. Asked plainly for the witnessings of that year, or asked plainly whether she knows anything of the seventh weight, she answers plainly. She does not volunteer.

## How these stand in the stretch you write again
- From the evening of the weighing the Assay-master and the Stallreeve also hold what the stranger told them, and from the sixth month the whole town does. Whether that moves either man to tell the ring what was in the loft or what the boatman said, and when, is yours, by their tempers: the Assay-master has said aloud that he will not be alone with a second thing, and the loft he was not alone in. Whether anyone in the ring asks the Tally-keeper plainly, now that the stranger has said before them that it did not take the seventh, is yours.
- Two moments the stranger's own decisions bring: at the winter sitting it says it will answer for the seat when the loft has been looked in and the seventh has been looked for; and after the gatepost it asks the Assay-master once, quietly, what the three of them saw in the loft. If he tells it, he tells what he saw and no more than he saw. Write his words exactly in your report.
- If the ring has the seventh looked for (the hall searched, the old man's kin asked, the channel dragged, anything else), write who looks, where, when and with what, as an attempt for the Keeper, and stop that thread there. What a search finds the Keeper gives. Do not send anyone to look because this note mentions looking; send them only if your people, knowing what they know, would.
- The sealed jar of kept halves in the old keeper's keeping has not been opened. If the ring has it opened this year, say who, when and before whom, and stop there.

## Not for public files
Everything in this note, until it is said or shown in the stranger's hearing.
