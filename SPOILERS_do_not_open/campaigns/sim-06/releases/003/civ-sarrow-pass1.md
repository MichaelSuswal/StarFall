## The year
Year 3. Your people's year opens in autumn and runs round the four seasons to autumn again. Months are counted from that opening: month 1 is the first month of autumn.
Your god has been among your people for a second year, as your Year 2 files and the Keeper's line on them tell it. Where the Keeper's line differs from your own Year 2 file, the Keeper's line is what happened. Its decisions file says what it means to do this year, in order; that is its intent, and what comes of each step among your people is yours to work out. Where it answers something a mortal asked earlier, the mortal hears the answer when and how the file says it is given. Where its decisions rest on something your people know to be otherwise, your people say so to it as people would.

## The world this year, as your people met it
- The weather. An ordinary year. The first snow came at its usual time and lay as it does. The passes opened at the thaw in the ordinary way.
- The stores. This is the winter the thin tolls tell. Whatever your own state gives was laid up, counted once or twice, the stores are short of the thaw: by the fifth month anyone who can read the Steward's cords can see that they will not last to the opening of the passes at the ordinary share. How they are shared, who goes short and what it costs is yours. If it comes to deaths beyond the ordinary, write it as an attempt for the Keeper with everything that bears on it.
- The toll. In the sixth month the one who commands the war band gives the order that the toll at the ford and the passes is raised by half from the thaw, to fill the stores. That he gives it is settled; it is what he has said he would do if the stores failed. What is said to him in the hall before and after, and by whom, is yours. Your god's decisions say nothing of the toll's rate, and it gives no word on it this year that its decisions do not give.
- The crossings. By the ford's own tally the beasts and trains crossing are down a little again. The toll laid up at the hall is as thin as last year, near half of what the old cords show. The traders of the river kingdom came as last year, and paid the raised toll after the thaw with complaint.
- The cords of the toll houses. If the Marshal's riders bring every house's cord for the year to the hall by first snow, they come, the far houses' too. Laid side by side they agree with one another and with what was laid up at the hall. What your god makes of them against a count of its own is the Keeper's; write only that the cords were laid out, who sat by, and what the mortals at the table said.
- The far toll houses. If the Marshal and a captain walk to the houses in spring with no word sent ahead and count stores, beasts and roofs there, what they find and bring back is an attempt for the Keeper: write who goes, when, what they do at each house, how the watchmen meet them, and write the year both ways, briefly. What anyone sees who goes that far is what was there last year: new roofs, good horses and full smokehouses at the two farthest houses.
- A letter. With the first train after the thaw, in the seventh month, a letter came from the chancery of the river kingdom, addressed to whoever holds the ford. It claims an old due: a yearly share of the toll of the ford and the passes, owed, it says, to the king as heir of the old power whose wardens kept the road, and unpaid within living memory. It names a figure in a measure no one at the hall uses. Who receives it, who can read it, whether it is brought to your god and what anyone answers is yours.
- The high grass. The blue pass flower did not come up again on the old cutting at the high gates. Anyone who goes up to the cutting in summer sees the cutting and no flower. What the oldest herders say of how the line of the grass has moved in their lives is yours, from your record.
- The ground. From the ford to the far pass with no keeper is 1.9 miles on foot; from the winter city, 2.4 miles.
- In late autumn, the third month, cranes went over in flights for some days, toward the plain, as every year.
- No stranger out of the ordinary came by the road this year, unless your own telling of the year brings one from among the ordinary travelers.
- Six nights this year the star dimmed, more again than last year. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. One fell in the first month of your year, a pair in the fifth, a pair in the seventh and one in the eighth. Anyone awake under a clear sky saw it. What your people make of such nights, and of their coming oftener, is theirs.

## What is yours to decide, and what is not
Yours: everything your people do, think, say and feel, every mortal among them, how each office holder meets the god and whether each takes its word, their work, stores, births and deaths, quarrels and stories. Mortals have their own will. They do not know the god's plans; they meet what it does, as it does it.
Not yours:
- An outcome that is uncertain and contested (a fight, a search, a persuasion of someone outside your people, a boat in a swell, a craft tried beyond what your people know). Do not settle it. Write it in your report as an attempt: who tries what, when, what success and failure would each mean, and every circumstance from your record that bears on it. The Keeper gives the odds and rolls. Write the rest of your year both ways where it turns on that outcome, briefly.
- What a god's power does or shows. Say what the god is seen to do and what your people make of it. Where your year turns on what a power did, say so in your report and the Keeper will settle it.
- Anything about another people or about land beyond what your record gives. Give no distance or travel time that is not in your record or in this file; say where a party set out for and by what way, and the Keeper prices the trip.
- The name of your people or their home, and the name of any office holder your record leaves unnamed: these stay unnamed unless a moment in your year has a mortal say a name aloud, and then you write who said it, when and to whom.
