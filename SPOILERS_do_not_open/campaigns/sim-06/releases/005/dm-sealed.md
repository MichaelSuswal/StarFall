### The days
- The count. Year 4, month 10, the first month of summer. Markets on days 14 and 17. Day 14 opens at first light with the gate shut.
- The weather, days 14 to 17: warm, dry. The wind stands foul for the open water through day 15 and comes fair on the morning of day 16.
- The ship owner of the open water's coasters go out past the Horn on the first fair wind: the tide of day 16, unless he chooses to wait a day for something to carry.
- The southern coaster means to go down the channel after the market of day 17, wind or no. Her master says so if asked how long she lies.
- The sky. On the night of day 14 the star dims as before, seen from the landing: tell it only if play reaches that night, as what the Weigher sees, with no count.
- Day 16: the first upriver boats of the summer tie up at the town. Tell it only if play reaches that day. If the Weigher is at a landing where they lie, or asks their boatmen for news, they say what river boatmen are saying this summer: that bread from the oldest fields round the king's town fills and does not sustain, that those who can afford it buy grain from the newer villages, and that the beer there has been thin two harvests. They have their own reasons for it, a blight or tired soil or the millers. They offer it as talk, to anyone.
- The ring has set no sitting. Three may call one. See the held moment for what calls it.

### Figures
- The coaster master's ship's balance, in its leather case: out. The buying pan is heavy by about one part in sixty. It read level at this gate in the tenth month of Year 2; a cord has stretched at sea since. She does not know it and believes it level. Read at the gate it is called as it reads. Mended and brought back, it reads level. It has been a true scale since Year 2 only for as long as it read true: it is not one now, and is one again from the trial at which it reads level.
- The eleven of the market, the old six, the ten on the bench: as the public sheet gives them. Nothing has changed them.
- Prices. Her crew have traded salt, pots and reed mats for southern goods at the landing in the town's scales, at the ordinary prices of a summer. The Weigher's fee for a reading is a pinch, from her as from anyone; she pays it in salt. A price a scene needs beyond that is rolled by you.

### Held moment: the coaster's master at the head of the line
Facts:
- When and where. Year 4, month 10, day 14, a market day, first light, the gate not yet open; the gatepost, the line forming. About fifty in the line behind her by the time the gate opens.
- She is first in the line by her own doing: she came up in the dark. Nobody gave her the place and nobody has disputed it.
- The word the Weigher gave in Year 3 named her: "to the master who stood at this post in the tenth month of last year, and through her to every house she will tell." It reached her as quay talk from another port, months late, after every house on her coast had it free.
- No weight is in the air. She is a mortal. Nobody like the Weigher is in the town.
- She carries no message from anyone. Nobody has sent her.

Parameters:
- The coaster's master: wants her balance read before anything else, in front of the line; wants to hear from the Weightward's own mouth which is the town's weight and what its pieces are, and to carry that to her own port; wants the hall's word to go south by her hull first each summer, before any delta hull says it on another port's quay. Will carry a word of the hall to her own port this summer for no more than her balance read and the word given her first, as cut, with a half-stick if one is cut, and will say it free on her own quay. Will say, to a plain asking and shortly, what the south made of the first word: that it was old news but for the figures and the day, and that masters there will reckon delta goods by the old six now that the market does. Will sell, for a price she names, her judgment on anything the Weigher asks her to judge. Will not carry a word as an order of the Weigher's to anyone. Will not promise to say what the Weightward is in any words but her own: she has said on her quay since Year 2 that it says it is a god, and goes on saying it. Will not be party to a seal ("I will shake on it. I will not be marked."). Will not have a bargain of hers read once she is told that both pans show ("Then we would both know. No."). Will not wait past the market of day 17. Accepts her balance called out, slowed and not angered; has the cord mended at her boat and brings it back at the market of day 17 to be read again. Accepts a no to her offer, and then carries this summer's word all the same and sells what she hears at the landing as she pleases. Will not accept being told the word that went to Corrith was hers to wait for. Stance: square at the head of the line, the case under her arm, looking at the cups and not the face. Opening words: "Weightward. Two summers ago I asked you which was the town's weight, and you had no answer for the town. Now I hear on another port's quay that you sent me one. Read my balance. Then tell me to my face what you sent."
- If asked plainly whether she has heard of any other who says it is a god, or who is taken for one: she says, shortly, as a thing every deckhand at her port knows and no news of hers: "They say the town at the bend of the river east of us keeps a guest with a lamp, one its court was waiting for. Salt carriers' talk. I have not seen it." She knows no more of it and says so. She does not offer it.
- If asked for news: she asks what kind, and names a price before she tells any: the first carrying, or a year's readings of her house's balances without the pinch. What she has for sale is the trade of her coast: which houses are short of timber, that a pair of houses at her port are near a feud over a lost cargo, what salt fetches at each haven. None of it concerns the Weigher's own matters. Invent no news for her beyond these.
- The Stallreeve: takes on his own word that she is read first, as the head of the line; that the Weigher says to her face what it called and what the ring has said, which is its own speech; and that the town's word as cut is read to her from the kept half by the Tally-keeper. Will not, on his own word, have a half-stick cut for her or name her a carrier of the town's word: that goes to the eleven. Carries her offer of the first carrying up to the eleven, if it is put to him, neither yes nor no. Stance: at the gate, a hand on the bar. Opening words, if the Weigher looks to him: "She is at the head of it. Read her."
- The Tally-keeper: wants every reading and every word said at the post cut once, as said; will read the kept half of the first stick aloud if the Stallreeve says so; will cut what is called; will not cut a half for a carrier without the eleven's say, and will not cut a figure she did not hear. Stance: at her table by the post, the kept half and the carrier's half laid side by side on it, her knife in her hand. Opening words, if spoken to: "The half is here. Say who it is read to."
- The coaster master's mate: wants nothing of the Weigher; will hand her the case and take it back; will not speak for her. Stance: a pace behind her left shoulder, eyes on the watch. Opening words, if spoken to: "Ask the master."
- The eleven, if anything of hers is carried up. Three of them call a sitting for day 16, a day with no market, the door shut. If the asking before them is that the town's word, as already cut on the first stick, be cut again for her to carry to her own port: they take it. If the asking before them is that she have the first carrying of the hall's word each summer: they take that too: the hall's word goes south by her hull first each summer. The Stallreeve brings each answer to the post at the market of day 17 with its count, and the Tally-keeper cuts what was taken. If nothing is carried up, no sitting is called.
- The ship owner of the open water, if sent for or met: wants his coasters out on the first fair wind and his own carrying kept; will say what he carries, as cut; will say to her face, if she asks, that he found none of her house at Corrith; will not dispute a second carrier for another port; will not give up his own carrying to Corrith. Stance: on the plank of his nearer coaster, or, if sent for, at the back of the yard with his arms folded. Opening words, if spoken to: "I carry what is on my stick. Ask me what is on it."
- Outcomes fall in:
  1. The balance read and called as it reads. She takes it, mends it, and is read level on day 17.
  2. The Weigher tells her to her face what it called and what the ring said. She has what she came for at first hand. She says she will say it on her own quay.
  3. A word cut for her to carry. It needs the eleven: asked through the Stallreeve, the ring sits on day 16 and takes it; the stick is cut on day 17 and she goes down the channel with it after that market.
  4. Her offer of the first carrying. The player's to take, refuse, or put to the ring. Put to the ring, it is taken: the first carrying each summer is hers. Refused, she carries this summer's word if one is cut and promises nothing after.
  5. The Weigher asks her to carry something beyond the town's word (a word of its own, a question to a house or a council, a word for anyone it has heard of). She hears it, says what it is worth to her to carry, and carries a plain word of its own for the same price as the town's, as hers to say in her own way; a question she prices as news.
  6. The Weigher offers a seal or a reading. Refused, as above.
  7. The Weigher says nothing but the reading. She takes the reading, says "Then I have your answer from another port's quay," and goes back to her boat. She sails after the market of day 17.
- To roll: nothing at this board. The balance, what the Stallreeve takes on his own word, and what the eleven answer are facts, above. A question outside it is rolled by you, its odds stated first.

Reasons (for you only; share them, lie about them in a figure's voice, or leave them unsaid):
- She wants her balance read first because she trades by it and means to stand on it when she speaks.
- She wants the word at first hand because she named that price two summers ago, the town then answered it, and the answer was said free on a rival port's quay before it reached her.
- She will carry this summer's word for the reading alone because a keeper's word at first hand brings buyers to her quay for everything else she sells.
- She wants the first carrying because news is worth what it is worth only while nobody else has it.
- She will not be sealed or read because a master who lets a landlord's mark onto her dealings has a landlord.
- She does not offer what she has heard of the bend town because it is common talk at her port and she sells nothing that is common talk; she says it shortly if asked, as she answered the Weigher's first questions in Year 2.
- She will not wait past the seventeenth because her summer's run has other havens in it.
- The Stallreeve carries anything that binds the town to the eleven because they gave him no leave to judge it, last year or this.
- The Tally-keeper cuts no half without the eleven's say because a carrier's half is the town's hand, and she has cut only what was said for as long as she has held the knife.
- The mate keeps silent because on her deck nobody speaks for the master ashore.
- The ship owner keeps his own carrying because the eleven named him and Corrith is his trade; he lets another port go because his coasters never went there.
- The eleven take a second carrying because the word is already theirs and already said, and give her the first carrying because the south coast's trade is worth more to the town than which hull is first.
