## What your people meet, by month
- The weather. An ordinary year. The first snow came at its usual time and lay as it does. The passes opened at the thaw in the ordinary way.
- The harvest. The seed came through whole and the sowing was a full one; the harvest of last summer was ordinary. What was laid up is what your own state gives.
- Salt. It is short at the turn of the year, as your own files give. Trains that carry salt are few on every road this year: two came over before the snow, small ones, of other peoples, and their masters asked a high price. Whether the hall buys, at what, and what is given for it are yours. Whether the meat put by and the stores last to the thaw at the share your people set is an attempt for the Keeper: write what was counted, by whom, how the salt was shared, whether the seed was sealed and kept, and write the year both ways, briefly.
- The riders beyond the houses. Write who is named, what they carry, the day they leave the farthest house and what leave they have. From the winter city to the far end of the road is 68.2 miles on foot, 5 days. What they meet beyond it, what is answered them and when they are home is the Keeper's: write the hall's winter and spring both ways where it turns on their news, briefly.
- The crossings. In autumn trains of other peoples crossed as last autumn. Settled: from the thaw, in the seventh month, trains of the river kingdom came over the road again, fewer than before the toll was raised, about half as many. Their masters say the toll is not what kept them. Some of their draught beasts were coughing, a dry cough, and two trains came short-handed of beasts. What the watchmen, the Marshal and the hall say of it is yours.
- No envoy of the river king came this year.
- The farthest toll house on a train day. What your god sees when it stands in that door, and what its own powers do, are the Keeper's; write what the keeper of the house and his household did and said when it stood there without warning, from what your record gives them to hold, and write the judging at the sill both ways if it comes to one. What anyone sees from outside at the two farthest houses is what was there before: new roofs, good horses and full smokehouses.
- The high grass. The blue pass flower did not come up. At the cairns the line of the grass stands where the first notch was cut, by the herders' eyes.
- No stranger out of the ordinary came by the road this year.

## When your god is away
Your people act in your god's name only where it said so, or plainly took it for granted. Otherwise they have no answer from it, and may not act as if they had one; someone among them may take it on themselves, and that is their act, not the god's. A question put to your god while it is away meets silence and stays pending, unless its standing words answer it. Your people know only that they were not answered.

## What is yours to decide, and what is not
Yours: everything your people do, think, say and feel, every mortal among them, how each office holder meets the god and whether each takes its word, their work, stores, births and deaths, quarrels and stories. Mortals have their own will. They do not know the god's plans; they meet what it does, as it does it.
Not yours:
- An outcome that is uncertain and contested (a fight, a search, a persuasion of someone outside your people, a boat in a swell, a craft tried beyond what your people know). Do not settle it. Write it in your report as an attempt: who tries what, when, what success and failure would each mean, and every circumstance from your record that bears on it. The Keeper gives the odds and rolls. Write the rest of your year both ways where it turns on that outcome, briefly.
- What a god's power does or shows. Say what the god is seen to do and what your people make of it. Where your year turns on what a power did, say so in your report and the Keeper will settle it.
- Anything about another people or about land beyond what your record gives. Give no distance or travel time that is not in your record or in this file; say where a party set out for and by what way, and the Keeper prices the trip.
- The name of your people or their home, and the name of any office holder your record leaves unnamed: these stay unnamed unless a moment in your year has a mortal say a name aloud, and then you write who said it, when and to whom.
