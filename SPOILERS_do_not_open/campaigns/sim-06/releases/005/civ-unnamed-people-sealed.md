## What they know that your god does not
- The Tally-keeper, the Stallreeve, the Assay-master, the head of clay and the eleven: what the Year 4 night's note gives each of them of the seventh weight, unchanged. Nobody in the town knows where it is or what it weighed. Each speaks of it only to a plain asking, as written there.
- The ship owner of the open water, the carrier. He took his coasters out past the Horn in the first days of the tenth month of Year 3 and lay at Corrith for some weeks, trading as he does every summer. He said the town's word there as cut, on the quay, to the masters of the houses his coasters trade with, with the half-stick in his hand, and to nobody as a price. He knows the house of the southern coaster's master by its name. He looked for a hull or a man of that house at Corrith, to put the word to it by name as the Weightward asked, and found none in the weeks he lay there: its port is another town, farther along that coast, where his coasters do not go. So the word was not put to her house by name. The council's people at Corrith did not ask him for it, and he did not go to them. Nobody gave him an answer, a stick or a token to carry home.
- What he heard on the quay at Corrith, which is his to tell or keep, as his temper gives: that most of it was old news there. The quays had had it since the winter, sold as news by a coaster's master, that the delta market has a reader of scales who takes barley and no silver, who was seated as keeper of the hall's weights, and who says before the yard that it is a god; that the hall's clay pieces are a twentieth heavy; and that the old weights are six of seven. What was new to them was the figures and the ring's day. Two masters said they will reckon delta salt and pots by the old six once the market itself does and not a day before. One asked what the Weightward is; he said it is the Weightward of the hall, and gave no more, as he had told it he would not promise more. One asked whether a southern scale may be read against the hall's; he said a scale is read at the gate, like any. The saying he heard most there, of a god in a market: that a god ashore is a landlord. Nobody there asked him about the fifth old weight; nobody there knows of it.
- The eleven new pieces of the third nest, rubbed down. If they are set in the Weightward's pans, they read, in its ones: 12, 24, 36, 48, 72, 84, 96, 120, 144, 168 and 180, each to a hair. Nobody in the town knows these figures unless the Weightward calls them. Use them only if the pieces are set in its pans in your telling.
- The master of the potters. From the two sticks she has worked out for herself what her clay loses in the fire: between one part in twelve and one part in fourteen of a piece's dry weight, piece by piece, about one part in thirteen over the load, and that pieces weighed alike before the fire do not come out alike. Whether and to whom she says it is yours. The cracking of the downs clay worries her more each year; she says so only to a plain asking about the clay.
- Orrin of the east kilns (added at the check, with the pass-two release): his stick of tile weights shows him nothing that tells one load from another. His tiles lose in the fire alike, whichever loads go on to crack. That holds for the months you have written and after. He has told nobody what it shows, and it goes only in your sealed files.
- The southern coaster's master, at the town from the twelfth day of the tenth month of Year 4. Nobody in the town knows why she has come or what she will ask. The carrier knows her house's name and has not said it to the Weightward. What she is, wants and says is the Keeper's and is not in this file.
- Names, the Keeper's to give, none yet said in the Weightward's hearing: the Stallreeve, Tam Wherry; the Tally-keeper, Annis Furrow; the Assay-master, Osric Marl; the ship owner of the open water, Davin Sculler; the master of the potters, Wynne Daub; the Tally-keeper's apprentice, Wat; the fish seller, Hob Sedge. A name goes in a public file only when a mortal says it aloud in the Weightward's hearing, and then you say who, when and to whom. The Assay-master keeps his word that he will not say his until the Weightward has one of its own to put in the other pan.
