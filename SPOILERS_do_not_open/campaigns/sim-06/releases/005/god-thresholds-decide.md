rule, and to spread. That is what the world below expects of you.

## What your people tell you
Copied whole from your people's own report of Year 4 (`SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/sim-06/civs/sarrow/years/004-report.md`, the section "What the people tell their god"). It is their telling, in their words, and they may be wrong.

We are thin, and we are still here. We counted twice before the first snow, the Steward and the hearth mothers, and the mothers' count was the lower, and we set the share by it and cut it by a quarter from the first snow, and told every hearth why before it was done. We sealed the seed under two marks, and the Mother holds her half. We said the winter's dead aloud at the fire, three hundred, and you stood through it. The band ate last a third winter, and the youngest captain said the thanks were the first they had had.

The Marshal sent riders to the far houses for the loan, as you said, in the open, and did not accuse any. [Branch A: Both houses sent, and the Steward counted it as a week of the ration, and it stands on a separate cord. Branch B: The nearer sent a little, the farthest sent words, and the Marshal told you which.] The stores held [Branch A: to the last days before the thaw, and the seed seal was whole when we broke it at the sowing] [Branch B: some weeks short; we ate the herd and the dried meat, and in the last week the Steward and the Mother broke the seal on a part of the seed, as you said it would be the last of all, and the sowing is short again]. [Branch A: A hundred or two more of the old and the youngest died than in an ordinary year. Branch B: Some hundreds more of the old and the under fives died than in an ordinary year.]

You walked to the farthest door and laid salt and the oath on it before the keeper's eyes, and told him first. He said his cord had nothing in it the knots did not say. We saw the same roofs, the same horses and the same full smokehouses. The watchmen said the river's trains complained loudly and paid, and none turned back at their door.

Kessan, Idris and you sat with the cords of the first trains and the trains were fewer by a third. Kessan said the raise had bought nothing this spring that the old toll would not have, and Idris said the sill takes one toll and not two, and the two of them gave back half of the raise, to a knot and a quarter the beast, from the ninth month, and the grain first order stands. We do not know what you said at the table, and you have not told us.

The older watcher at the ford gave the word you sent to a master of the river's trains, after one would not take it, and waived his toll in your name, and the train left the ford for the river kingdom by the road north-east and the river. After that we do not know. No one of the king's came. And then in the summer no train of the river kingdom came over the road at all, and the trains of other peoples came as before. We do not know why. The Marshal says a king who sends a letter in the month the toll is raised and keeps his trains home in the summer the toll is lowered has watched the ford. Kessan asked you by a rider on the grass whether he might send riders beyond the houses to learn why, and the Steward asked what to do for salt if none comes before the snow, and the Mother asked whether to tell the households the salt will not last. You did not answer on the grass. We were not answered, and we waited. Salt is short.

The herders cut a notch at the cairns at the old cutting and the other cutting, and the second at the first snow of the heights, and the line stood where the oldest say it stood last year, no higher. The flower did not come. The herders say a stone cut to count a line does not hold it, and the households do not hear them.

The star dimmed seven nights, the most yet. Some say it dimmed for the toll and some for the grass and some for the dead. The cranes went over in the third month. The counted road did not come.

We have set the seat and left the door unlatched at the first snow, and we will again.
