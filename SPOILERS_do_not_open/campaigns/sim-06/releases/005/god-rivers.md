# Release: god-rivers, Year 5, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 5, and what your people report. It is your go for nothing by itself.

## What you did and what came of it
- At the council you answered what had stood a year: the star, and what you want. The weirs' people and the head of the oldest kin house took it as plain and partial.
- At the flood's fall the weir hands measured with their own pole: behind the oldest weir, where the gap was opened, the bed lies lower by about a hand along the line of the gap; behind every other weir it has shoaled a little more. The deep water lay a little farther from the ford stones again.
- You walked the pasture at the dark of the moon in the third month with the head herd keeper and three hands. The counts agreed. In the fifth month, while you were at the ford, a pasture hand left the head herd keeper's question with the Seat Warden: whether the offer stands for the dark of the moon in the first month of the harvest. It met silence and waited. When you came home the Warden gave it to you and you said yes. You walked the pasture a second night, in the seventh month. Nothing strayed and nothing came either night, and the head herd keeper put in the cord that you do not tire.
- The council gave leave for the salt road, eight days out. At the ford the carrier family the keepers know best ate with you and would not have you on its road: what a carrier pays at each stage is its trade, they said, and not for a town's counting. You waited to the fifth day. No other family came. You came home by the Brook Path.
- At the ford, at dusk and dawn, you stood in the larger river to the knee. No pull.
- At the low water you brought the measures to the council.
- In the eleventh month, in the last of the ice, a great run of fish came up and stood at the weirs four days, thickest at the opened gap. You stood on the bank with the Ferry Elder. A weir hand's child asked whether you had sent them.
- No one asked for warm hands this year. You did not listen.

## Asked of you and not answered
- A weir hand's child, on the bank in the eleventh month: whether you sent the fish.

## What the Keeper ruled
- Your three readings are right as you state them. The water you stood in at the ford is the larger river's, and no pull was there.

## The sky
The star dimmed on three nights of your year, fewer than last year: twice in the second month and once in the seventh. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people count twenty-four such nights since you came.

## Your people at the year's turn
About as last year, and better fed at the winter's end than in any year since you came.
