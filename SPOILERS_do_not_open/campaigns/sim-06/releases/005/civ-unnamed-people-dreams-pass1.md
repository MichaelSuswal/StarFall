## What your people meet, by month
- The weather. An ordinary year. The grain came in as in most years. Winter was cold in the ordinary way. The river was high in spring and low in the dry months.
- The third barrow song. Settled: no singer in any hamlet holds the third part. The runners, if they are sent, come back with old people who remember that there was a third part and cannot give its words. The two young households hold what they held.
- The salt. The salt carriers came as before, at last year's price. The town goes into a fifth lean salting season unless the court's own bargain fills it.
- The stone that lay at the river's mouth. Nobody in your town knows what became of it. Whoever of your people next comes to the river's mouth finds the stack gone from above the high water, the hides folded and weighted with a stone, and the marks of a ship's boat on the bank. What the harbor says of it is the Keeper's.
- The harbor house's order and the stone party, if the court sends one. By the river from the town to its mouth is 112.4 miles, 4 days by boat going down and 10 days coming back up. From the mouth the harbor lies west along the coast: 97.9 miles on foot round the shore, 7 days, or by the house's ship. Write whether the court sends it, what stone the Steward and the old cutter spare against the wall's own need and the worn picks, who leads, when they set out, and what leave the leader has. What they meet beyond the river's mouth, what the factor answers, and whether anyone outside his house takes a word on, are the Keeper's: write the party's errand as attempts and write your year both ways where it turns on them, briefly.
- Iron came down from the mining town grey and soft, as for two winters now.
- The Guest's walk to the harbor in the dry months, if the throne and the Warden of the Wall give leave. Write whether leave is given, who goes, the day they set out and the day named for the return, and how the town keeps its nights while it is gone. Give no days for the road that this file does not give; the Keeper prices the walk at the Guest's own pace. What it meets on the road and at the harbor is the Keeper's.
- On the nights the wall's men sit its body: they see what they have seen before, no more and no less. Write what they write down.
- No stranger came to the gate this year but the ordinary carriers and traders.

## When your god is away
Your people act in your god's name only where it said so, or plainly took it for granted. Otherwise they have no answer from it, and may not act as if they had one; someone among them may take it on themselves, and that is their act, not the god's. A question put to your god while it is away meets silence and stays pending, unless its standing words answer it. Your people know only that they were not answered.

## What is yours to decide, and what is not
Yours: everything your people do, think, say and feel, every mortal among them, how each office holder meets the god and whether each takes its word, their work, stores, births and deaths, quarrels and stories. Mortals have their own will. They do not know the god's plans; they meet what it does, as it does it.
Not yours:
- An outcome that is uncertain and contested (a fight, a search, a persuasion of someone outside your people, a boat in a swell, a craft tried beyond what your people know). Do not settle it. Write it in your report as an attempt: who tries what, when, what success and failure would each mean, and every circumstance from your record that bears on it. The Keeper gives the odds and rolls. Write the rest of your year both ways where it turns on that outcome, briefly.
- What a god's power does or shows. Say what the god is seen to do and what your people make of it. Where your year turns on what a power did, say so in your report and the Keeper will settle it.
- Anything about another people or about land beyond what your record gives. Give no distance or travel time that is not in your record or in this file; say where a party set out for and by what way, and the Keeper prices the trip.
- The name of your people or their home, and the name of any office holder your record leaves unnamed: these stay unnamed unless a moment in your year has a mortal say a name aloud, and then you write who said it, when and to whom.
