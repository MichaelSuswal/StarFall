# Decide: god-dreams, Year 5

## Why you come down
Gods come down here to lead a people: to find or gather them, to build, to
rule, and to spread. That is what the world below expects of you.

## The year
Year 5 begins at the turn of the year, in your waking season, autumn, and runs round the four seasons to the same season again. A year here is twelve months. Your fourth year on the ground is over. The whole of Year 5 is yours to decide tonight. You will be told what came of it in your next release.

## What your people tell you
Copied whole from your people's own report of Year 4 (`SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/sim-06/civs/unnamed-people-dreams/years/004-report.md`, the section "What the people tell their god"). It is their telling, in their words, and they may be wrong.

The seat, from the wall, in her own words: "The guest asked what we would do if she were away. We told her. She asked in time, and a keeper who asks that is a keeper who means to be back. The stone is gone down to the sea for the second time and the nine with it and your word. We hold the bargain as ours. We have not spent the iron's worth of it yet."
The Warden of the Door: "I carry what I am given and I have said I take you for the one awaited. You said at a father's door that you were wrong, and asked whose word it was. I found it was the roll at the quay that nobody struck. I think a runner read it to you. I cannot swear it. The Steward has had the roll struck. I asked you whether you meant four days down the river from the mouth, for the town lies four days up. You gave me nothing I can say. The leader will say it as you gave it and will say up if he is asked."
The Warden of the Wall: "The wall holds your door and has held it a third winter. You told the four what they sit against, and told them to move you by the cord if fire comes. The captain has staked the way to the gate. The wall has seen you lie still on the lot nights and has called it asleep, and you say a lying hind is not you in a dream. I have not been told what the wall has been sitting. The iron is soft, and the old heads are fewer than last year. The wall has stopped being surprised at the dawns, which is not the same as not minding. I have not taken you for the awaited, and I have not refused you."
The Steward of the Store: "Grain will reach the harvest with a thin margin, as it has three years. Fuel and oil are enough. Salt is as the party made it. The stone costs the town more in picks than it knew. The old iron is a third down. I lent a third boat and counted the winter aloud without being asked. I took your word grudgingly and I have not changed."
The Keeper of the Count: "Seven nights the star dimmed, twenty-one in all, and a dying in the town on seven. The roll of kept households is about five hundred and ninety. Five of this year's seven you were awake, and two you were lying. I have written lying beside the old tag. The cord does not say why. I have your list beside the cord. The seventh place is empty."
The old cutter: "The picks wear twice as fast. We cut the fifth to the cord and the wall had four parts of the five. The three cuts are as they were, with the stones round them. No one told us."
The Night Sitter: "I sleep more. The hamlet girl found she could. A sitter is not taught. You are good with a kettle."
The barrow singers' households: "The oldest of us died in month 3 and did not die alone. She gave us what she could and her voice would not hold the last. We have two parts in three of the third song and sing it to the gap. We asked if you would hear it, and have had no answer. Some say you could have taken it from her sleep. Her house did not ask."
The town: "A star that dims seven times in a year is a star that dims. The iron is soft. The salt came or did not. There is a stranger in the delta who reads scales and says he is a god, and we do not know whether he is. We lit our lamps. We counted the children at dusk."
The father of the second house: "My son came home two autumns past and not last year. A plain thing was plainly said. Thank you for the bread."
The hamlet girl: "I can sit. I will sit two nights in ten. My hands shake."
