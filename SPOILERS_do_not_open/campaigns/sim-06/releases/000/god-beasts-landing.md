# Landing: god-beasts

## The hour
The hour before first light. The season you wake into: spring, the first storms.

## What you sense
Old timber at your back and open air in front. The ground is wet. The wood is only just in leaf and smells of rain and new growth. Streams are loud somewhere below. A storm has not long passed, or is not long coming.
You feel no other like yourself. That knowing comes only at close quarters, in the same room, across a doorway, within a few paces, as a weight in the air that settles over a moment into knowing, and it tells nothing more than that the other is one like you.

## What is in view
What follows shows as the light comes.
You stand at a wood's edge on wooded upland, about 1,150 feet up, on a beaten trail.
- The trail runs through the point where you stand, about a mile of it in view, from the north to the south.
- Wet ground lies in a hollow about a quarter mile to the south-east.
- Open plain lies to the east, its nearest edge about 6 miles off and no higher than where you stand, seen out to about 40 miles.
On this ground a level stretch shows out to about 3 miles; things on the ground show only within about half a mile.
The star stands in the west, about a sixth of the way up the sky, over the timber: a round blue light, wider than the moon and steady. It gives light enough to walk by.

## What was told you before, corrected
The beaten trail and the wet hollow are as you were told. Two things differ. The river and its line of trees are not in view from the wood's edge. No smoke is in view.

## Your helpers
The offices of your people stand and are filled, and each runs on the people's own custom until its holder hears you and takes your word. Until then no one acts for you while you are away.
