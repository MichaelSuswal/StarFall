# Release: god-thresholds, Year 1, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 1, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 2, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 2 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- You blessed the ford. The older of its two toll watchers stood with his hands open; the younger ran for the city. Since then you have known each crossing there and how many.
- At the gate the senior guard neither barred it nor bade you in; he sent for Idris Veld, who bade you in by word in the old form. In the hall the seat was set and Kessan broke bread first. You read Kessan and Idris Veld across the table: neither means harm to a guest. Each learned the same of you. Kessan did not like being read. Each took your word, as did the Marshal, the Steward and the Mother of the Hearth.
- Kessan's counts were true. The Steward's count of the winter stock was kind by about a fortnight, as your weekly counts showed by the fourth month; he says it was not a lie.
- In the second month, while you were in the city, wolves came at the night fold of one column still on the road. The dogs were late to give tongue. The fold broke and the flock scattered along the road; sheep and goats were lost by the tens. A herder, a grown man, was mauled and died of it within days. The boys with the watch were not hurt.
- In the third month you blessed the hall door and the city gate, taught the oath to Idris Veld and three Doorwardens, and three lesser marks were laid: the toll house at the ford, a hearth mother's standing house, and the door of the salt store, which Idris chose. A senior Doorwarden of the lower columns, passed over, said to the fire that the sense will run out.
- First snow fell late in the third month. You sat at the warlord's table. Kessan sat at the foot of it.
- In the fourth month the Mother of the Hearth brought her grievance: the war band's horses on the households' hay. In the fifth you judged a sill case at the hall door with Idris at your right and Kessan at your left. In the sixth you ordered the stores shared by hearth, the poorest first; the Mother backed it, the Steward complied, the captains took it badly and said nothing, and Kessan hunted. The stores lasted to the thaw.
- In spring you walked the road and both passes with the Marshal and blessed the pass he named as most crossed, the north-western, which they call Stair Pass [f62]. The other they call Far Notch [f63]. The toll houses were told to keep an honest count and feed a traveler a night.
- The trains from the river kingdom, which your people call the Aldermark [r10], crossed and paid as before, no more of them than last year. The tolls that came in to the hall were thin again.
- In summer you went with two columns in turn and sat at their rite fires. Eleven strangers were brought to you by the toll watchers; each was ordinary. You read three at tables: a trader of the river kingdom with a false measure, a packer of your own in debt to the toll, a captain's cousin. None meant harm to a guest. No door was held against a refused traveler and you broke nothing.
- You hold four thresholds in full and three lesser marks. You felt no other like yourself, on the road, in the passes or at any table.
- The columns had turned for the winter ground when the year turned. You were not yet back at the ford.

## Asked of you and not answered
- The senior gate guard: why you came before the snow.
- The Mother of the Hearth, fourth month: that you sit at the households' tables in the low quarter.
- Marda Sill is a name Idris Veld said to you in private in the fifth month, when you asked your one question.

## What the Keeper ruled
Your two readings are right: the city, its gate, the hall door and the toll house at the ford are places your people hold, and the ford you blessed is the one you saw from where you came down.

## The sky
The star dimmed on three nights of your year: two in the first month, some days apart, and one in the fifth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people have seen such nights in other years. This year many of them tied the nights to you.

## Your people at the year's turn
About 18,000. Hard, hospitable, tight-lipped about the stores. The word that a tall woman with a key stands in the passes has gone home with the traders.
