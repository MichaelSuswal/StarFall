# Release: god-dreams, Year 1, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 1, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 2, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 2 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- The watch at the gate sent for the Warden of the Door. You waited most of a morning. He gave you the custom's words and carried his own table out to the guest house yard, since you cannot pass a low door. The gate takes your width.
- You kept the three nights and answered on the third as you meant to. That night he took you for the one the place is kept for, and told the seat alone. He asked whether you are that guest; you said neither yes nor no.
- The seat let you stay on the court's bread and spoke your custom from the wall. Late in autumn the court's hearth was lit from your lamp and the seat said aloud that she takes you for the one awaited. Households began to ask from that day: about 330 by the year's end.
- Keeping Sleep, two households a night by the Keeper of the Count's lot: those households slept whole, and their night's fear came to you each dawn as your card gives. The flames near you dipped when you were afraid, and you said so when asked.
- In winter the Steward counted grain, fuel and salt aloud before the council at your asking. You sat a night beside the Night Sitter in her work yard with a child in fever and a very sick old man, and did not work; the child lived, as most do. In kept houses you walked the last sleep of about a dozen dying. None woke for it and none was cured. Their households say the dying were quiet, and some speak of a hind or a lamp.
- In spring you walked the nearest hamlets with a score of the wall watch. Some headmen took the flame. At two hamlets the lane was too narrow for you and the headman came out. One hamlet in three or four has a Night Sitter.
- In summer you sat with the stone cutters and walked the wall with the Warden of the Wall, who named what it lacks. At your asking the seat sent out two parties of four runners in the second month of summer, with linen and pots as gifts.
- The river party came home in the last month. At the river's mouth they found the crew of a coasting ship beached for the night, were fed, and traded their gifts for salt. The sailors told them the salt is boiled at a harbor town west along that coast, and what it fetches there: far less than the carriers ask at your gate. The mouth is four days down by boat and ten back.
- The western party had not come home when the year turned.
- Where the offices stand: the Warden of the Door, the seat and the Keeper of the Count take your word; the Steward takes it grudgingly; the Warden of the Wall does not, and keeps the wall as it was; the Gate Bench is two for and one against, and has ruled that no house is to be shamed for not keeping the flame.
- You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- The stone cutters: "Do you know who made the lowest cuts?" and "Are you a giant's kin?"
- Households, after the third dim night: "Does the star dim for the dying?"

## What the Keeper ruled
- A flame lit from your lamp makes a shrine kept for you; it is not Keeping Sleep. The court's hearth is such a shrine, and is one of your seven only on a night you light your lamp there at dusk to keep that household's sleep.
- What arrives at dawn is the fear that would have broken that household's sleep that night. Grief is not fear and does not arrive. Fear for a dying one, where it is fear, does.
- A small thing of your own showed itself in the night beside the Night Sitter: you stayed attentive through the whole watch and marked each change in a sleeper's breathing. It is written on your card as a knack. It is no power.

## The sky
The star dimmed on three nights of your year: two in the first month, some days apart, and one in the fifth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people have seen such nights in other years. This year many of them tied the nights to you.

## Your people at the year's turn
About 5,000, settled, anxious over salt. The salt carriers saw you twice and took word of you to the harbor towns, who asked through them what you are.
