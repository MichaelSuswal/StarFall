# Release: god-sea, Year 1, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 1, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 2, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 2 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- The first night's listening gave you the coast of a day: oars and hulls at the town's shore, voices on the quay, surf on the rocks at the point, nothing moving along the bottom that was not tide. The deaf day followed.
- From the foot of the quay's lowest step you learned the town's dusk: the lamp-tenders light the harbor lamp; the one who holds the throne keeps it and lit it herself on the first dusk of the dark half; a bronze bell hangs below the lowest step and a sealed jar is hung there for leaving word, as you hoped. The gulls and seals left where you lay, and the fishers noticed the quiet.
- In the second month the Warden-Monarch came alone to the tide-door at the lowest tide at dusk. You said what you meant to say. She kept still and you showed your shape once. She asked what to call you and you said Skerr. The next dusk she left a jar with a notched stick (boats in the book, boats in the yard, the stores) and struck the bell; you answered with a stick. She took your word. In the third month she told four of the court; she did not tell the two Captains.
- The court hauled the boats about ten days early, caulked and tarred, checked the chain, counted the stores and shared them evenly through the lean. The Harbormaster did it as the court's order and does not take your word; he says the word is a convenience to her line in their quarrel over the chain. The Reeve and the Eldest Midwife wait.
- You joined one pile at the lowest step with the Master of Timber watching, and paid the day. He took your word and keeps which piece it was. Five storms came in the winter, the first late in the third month, the longest a blow of four days in the fifth. None did the quay or the boats harm.
- In the fourth month, between the second storm and the third, a decked boat of five was caught out at dusk in a rising swell. You lit your lamp by your own hand at the water's edge by the harbor mouth. The mouth and the inner water lay calm; the water outside did not. The boat came in with all five. The town says the lamp brought them in and means the court's lamp. That water would not take you again until the seventh month, as your card gives.
- The winter's second listening gave you the winter sea of a day and nothing you did not expect.
- In spring you walked some miles of strand each way from the point by night and set your marks at the tideline. You found nothing made by hands that the town does not already know. Gleaners saw a hooded old man three times and were told by the Warden-Monarch that it was nothing to fear.
- In summer you joined one keel, of the point ferryman's boat, chosen by the Master of Timber. You watched at the town's edge and spoke to no one.
- You saw no one you would call a keeper of something small that you are ready to name. You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- The Warden-Monarch: whether you are the one who spoke to Brennis, a lamp-warden of old to whom, the eldest say, the promise was made. She also offered you a bed in the lamp-house.
- The Master of Timber: "What do I owe for it?" and "Why must it be secret?"

## What the Keeper ruled
- The bell and the jar are the town's own old custom, as you found.
- Calm Under the Lamp: set at the water's edge by the harbor mouth, the lamp's light shows across the mouth and the inner water of this harbor and no farther. On your card.
- A small thing of your own showed itself that night: your lamp stayed alight in wind and spray that would have put out another hand's. It is written on your card as a knack. It is no power.

## The sky
The star dimmed on three nights of your year: two in the first month, some days apart, and one in the fifth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people have seen such nights in other years. This year many of them tied the nights to you.

## Your people at the year's turn
About 2,011. Stores enough. The town does not know of you; five of the court do. A fisher's wife told the hill shepherds that "the bell on the quay was answered".
