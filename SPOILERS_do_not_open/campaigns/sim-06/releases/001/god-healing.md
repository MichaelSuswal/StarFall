# Release: god-healing, Year 1, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 1, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 2, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 2 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- At the mounds the soil read as old bone, long quiet, nothing you know as sickness. On the river bank beside them, where newer graves lie, the ground read wet and foul with the newly dead, and the wet goes down toward the low side. The river water where it runs by read sound.
- The village is larger than a village: it is where the court of this people sits. The midwife Hedda took you in at her door and gave her name. At the wellhead the Warden of Waters heard you. The well read sound. The grain store read sound. He set part of the store apart and had the well drawn off all the same, on your word that it should be kept so.
- Through the year you mended about thirty, one at a time with rests, each as your card gives, and paid each cost: a labor gone wrong, fevers past the ninth day, wounds gone hot, the wet chest in the old and the small. In the eleventh month the court's sitters had failed three nights with the Hearth-Holder's granddaughter, a child of four, and you were sent for through Hedda. She lives. You were carried to bed with the shake. Those you mended tell Hedda they know when you are near with their eyes shut.
- In summer the Reckoner of Bodies received you and told you her counts in plain words: last winter's dead are nearly all from steadings downstream of the bank burials, and those who drew from the creek fared better than those with wells. She asked you straight whether you are the one the seat is kept for and you answered truly; she asked your name and had it. She took your word at the end of the ninth month, after her count.
- On the late summer walk and through the autumn you read the wells of each steading you reached. The wells of the low steadings downstream of the bank burials read foul, with the same foulness as the bank ground. You said so plainly. Where the elders would hear it the wells were drawn off or closed and water was boiled or carried from the creek. Two steadings stand empty since last winter.
- In the eighth month you rotted a heap of spoiled grain by the water in an afternoon and carried the smell a day and a night. The Warden of Waters took that story to the court.
- The winter was an ordinary sickness season. The wasting fever of last winter did not come as it came then.
- A faint tremor has stayed in your hands since the first grave mending. It goes at the thaw.
- You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- The Reckoner, ninth month: that you take no step toward the court without coming to her first.
- The sitters of the nearest steadings, through Hedda: that you look at the moss of the mounds. The old women say it does not bite the tongue as it did, and brews weak.
- The Hearth-Holder, in the last month, by a runner: that you sit at the seat's hearth when you are well.

## What the Keeper ruled
Reading the Rot reads soil and water as your card says, and it did. Nothing was outside your card.

## The sky
The star dimmed on three nights of your year: two in the first month, some days apart, and one in the fifth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people have seen such nights in other years. This year many of them tied the nights to you.

## Your people at the year's turn
About 5,000 in their scattered steadings, tired, less frightened than a year ago where you walked. The Judge-Mother wants you watched. The Spearward you have not met. The people say the dead are unquiet while the seat is empty.
