## The year
Year 1. Your people's year opens in autumn and runs round the four seasons to autumn again. Months are counted from that opening: month 1 is the first month of autumn.
Your god came down in the hour before first light on the first day of the year. This is the first year any of your people meet it. Its decisions file says what it means to do, in order; that is its intent, and what comes of each step among your people is yours to work out.

## The world this year, as your people met it
- The weather. An ordinary year. The columns came down in autumn as they do. The first snow fell late in the third month. The winter was ordinary in length. The thaw opened the passes at the usual time. The high grass this summer was ordinary, better than last year's.
- Wolves. In the second month, as the columns were still coming down, a pack came at the night fold of one column on the road and was among the sheep before the dogs gave tongue. How the fold's keepers met it is an uncertain outcome: write it for the Keeper as an attempt, with what the column had to hand.
- The road. The trains from the river kingdom crossed and paid as they do, no more of them than last year. The tolls that came in to the winter hall were thin again.
- Late in the third month cranes passed overhead for some days toward the plain, as they do each year.
- Your god's decisions speak of a ford a mile from where it came down and a city beyond it. They are the ford with its toll house and your winter city. Your people know the god on sight, as your record says.
- Three nights this year the star dimmed. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Two of the nights fell in the first month of your year, some days apart, and one in the fifth month. Anyone awake under a clear sky saw it. Your people have seen such nights in other years; what they make of them is theirs.

## What is yours to decide, and what is not
Yours: everything your people do, think, say and feel, every mortal among them, how each office holder meets the god and whether each takes its word, their work, stores, births and deaths, quarrels and stories. Mortals have their own will. They do not know the god's plans; they meet what it does, as it does it.
Not yours:
- An outcome that is uncertain and contested (a fight, a search, a persuasion of someone outside your people, a boat in a swell, a craft tried beyond what your people know). Do not settle it. Write it in your report as an attempt: who tries what, when, what success and failure would each mean, and every circumstance from your record that bears on it. The Keeper gives the odds and rolls. Write the rest of your year both ways where it turns on that outcome, briefly.
- What a god's power does or shows. Say what the god is seen to do and what your people make of it. Where your year turns on what a power did, say so in your report and the Keeper will settle it.
- Anything about another people or about land beyond what your record gives. Give no distance or travel time that is not in your record; say where a party set out for and by what way, and the Keeper prices the trip.
- The name of your people or their home, and the name of any office holder your record leaves unnamed: these stay unnamed unless a moment in your year has a mortal say a name aloud, and then you write who said it, when and to whom.
