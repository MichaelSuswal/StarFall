# Release: god-beasts, Year 1, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 1, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 2, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 2 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- From the wood's edge the listening gave you beasts of the timber and the near plain and no herd line within its reach. You went south-east down the beaten trail, which your people call the Herd Road [f40]. On the fourth dusk the listening read cattle, goats, sheep and dogs in a long line ahead, moving on at a herd's pace.
- At the rear camp the dogs fell silent and turned to you and the herd beasts lifted their heads. A girl ran for the Houndwarden, whose name is Tessaly. A hunter raised a spear and she told him once to put it down. You answered her as you meant to. She let you keep the night watch at the margin of the rear camp, on her own word.
- The Speaker, Orrin Haddock, came back along the line and heard you: a keeper that comes to the edge of the fire will be heard at council like any elder and no more.
- Four nights in spring wolves came at the calving herds at the rear. You knew where they lay and which way they moved, and with Tessaly's dogs and hunters they were turned each time. Little was lost. The rear camp says the herds sleep better.
- At the stopped camp on the river in the sixth month the council asked you what you are and you told it plainly. The Marchwarden asked whether you knew the song of the ford below the camp; you had learned it. You told them the boldness of beasts near the tents may be your doing where you have spoken with one, and Tessaly keeps the cord: a fox that took a kid by day, a boar that stood against a hunter. That month a pack took calves on the east flank two nights running; you kept the flank with the dogs and hunters and it was turned with no more than one calf lost after.
- In autumn the council asked you, after a short quarrel, to stand with the hunters and say where the game lay. You did, and asked that the hunt keep its season.
- You wintered at the margin of the main camp in the high wood. The cutters of the stone towns were in the winter woods farther in than last year. Three clans camped apart all year and wintered apart, farther off than before, after a quarrel over fuel wood in which a boy of the main band was cut on the forearm; the Three at the Fire judged it.
- The band's trade party went to the stone towns in the fifth month with beasts, hides and felt, and came back before the going up with what the Saltkeeper counts as two parts in three of what was sent for, and with tools of the stone towns' make. The stone towns asked more for it than last year.
- The cattle of two clans that coughed last autumn still cough, some of them. A few died in the winter.
- No office holder has taken your word. You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- The eldest head of the three clans, before you and not to you: that a keeper that asks nothing will want something later.

## What the Keeper ruled
- The Listening: your own people read as nothing at all. An empty place is not a thing the listening marks; you can only reason to it from kept beasts that move and rest as kept beasts do. A herder with a dog reads as a dog alone. A few of your people apart from any beast read as nothing. On your card.
- A small thing of your own showed itself in the spring nights: you know where the wind is without thinking and keep downwind of what you watch. It is written on your card as a knack. It is no power.

## The land you walked
By the map: the grass your people call the Farrow Grass [r22] is broad grazing plain falling to the east, with a river, the Farrow, and its side streams, and wet ground in the hollows.

## The sky
The star dimmed on three nights of your year: two in the first month, some days apart, and one in the fifth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people have seen such nights in other years. This year many of them tied the nights to you.

## Your people at the year's turn
About 5,000, unhurried, uneasy about the cattle and the cut woods. They say the star dims for the keeper.
