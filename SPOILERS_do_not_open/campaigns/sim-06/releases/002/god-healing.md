# Release: god-healing, Year 2, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 2, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 3, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 3 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- You went to the Reckoner first, before any other door, and the yard saw it. She carried your word to the Hearth-Holder.
- At the mounds with Hedda and the sitters you read the moss close. It is living moss, sound: nothing foul in it and no sickness on it that your Reading knows. The old women say it does not bite the tongue as it did and brews weak. An old sitter asked whether it is dying or only tired, and you answered her there.
- At the bank with the Warden of Waters and the Keeper of the Mounds you showed them where the newer graves lie and where the wet goes down. Asked, you stood on the rise and read it: sound ground. After a quarrel the first new burials were laid there.
- In the fifth month the Reckoner took you to the seat's hall at dusk. You sat where a guest sits and ate. The Hearth-Holder asked whether you are the one the seat is kept for, and your name; the Judge-Mother asked what you would do if the court bade you go from the fen; the Spearward asked whether you could do to a granary what you did to the heap. You answered each in front of the hearth. No one asked you to take the seat. The Hearth-Holder said you were welcome at the seat's hearth.
- On the downstream walk you read the wells and the soil about them. The wells still open in the steadings below the bank burials: foul, as last year, with the same foulness as the bank. The wells drawn off or closed last autumn: fainter, still foul at the bottom. The wells of the two emptied steadings: foul. The creek water: clean. An old elder asked why his people should carry water like children when his father dug the well, and you answered him before his steading.
- In the eighth month a drowned ox lay in a creek weir above a wellhead. You rotted it once; it went to black slurry in an afternoon and washed out. You mended no one that day or the next night.
- Nine mendings through the year, each after the sitters had done what they could, each as your card gives: the fever broke by morning, and the chill and the shake were yours. All nine lived.
- The Reckoner's winter count was lower than last year's, and lower in the steadings whose wells were closed than in those that kept them. The shake left your hands in the first days of the thaw.
- You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- Nothing was asked of you this year that you did not answer.

## What the Keeper ruled
- Reading the Rot reaches a living plant as it reaches a creature: you know what ails it, if a sickness or a rot ails it. A thing that is whole and only weaker in its use reads as whole. On your card.

## The sky
The star dimmed on five nights of your year, more than last year: one in the second month, one in the sixth, one in the ninth, one in the eleventh and one in the twelfth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people tie some of the nights to you; two fell when you had mended no one within the moon.

## Your people at the year's turn
About 5,060. The court has seen you at the hearth in the open. The Reckoner and the Warden of Waters take your word; the Keeper of the Mounds came to the bank when asked.
