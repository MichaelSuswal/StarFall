# Sealed note: civ unnamed-people, Year 2, after the hall

For the civ agent of the unnamed people only, read at the opening of Year 3. It holds what three of your people now know. The stranger has not seen or heard any of it. The rules of last year's note hold: each fact is known to the mortals named and to no one else; a mortal says it only when asked plainly or moved to by his or her fixed temper; you are told no cause and reason toward none; nothing here goes in a public file unless it is said or shown in the stranger's hearing, and then you write who said what.

- The loft, the third day after the weighing. The Assay-master, the Stallreeve and the clay head saw: the old man's coat and boots hanging on a peg, stiff with dried river silt to above the knee. No one had moved them. Nothing else in the room was out of its place. They touched nothing and came down.
- The boatmen, month 2. One boatman told the Stallreeve: on a night of the spring flood, the old man's last spring, he lent the old man a punt, and got it back before light with silt in the bottom and a wet rope. He had thought nothing of it; old men fish eels on the flood. The Stallreeve knows this. Whom he has told since is yours to say in Year 3; in Year 2 your files have him tell no one.
- A correction to last year's note. The clerk from the kingdom upriver called on the old Weightward in the summer before the old man's last winter: the summer before the spring he died, which is the summer of the year before the stranger came. The Tally-keeper stood witness then and holds the kept half. Nothing else in that line changes.
- The stranger was at the landing by the lamp on the evening the Stallreeve came down. It saw him come and speak apart with the boatmen. It heard nothing of what was said.
