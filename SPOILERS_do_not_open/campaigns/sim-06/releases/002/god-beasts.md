# Release: god-beasts, Year 2, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 2, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 3, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 3 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- At the first storm the Houndwarden said your answer in the ring as you gave it. The eldest head of the three clans said it was a small thing to want and that small things are the first things. The Speaker said the council would not send you from the edge and would not take your word, and that the Houndwarden might carry to you what she saw of your costs.
- Down the Herd Road [f40] you kept the rear margin each night and told her what you read. On the three wolf nights of the calving you told her where they lay and which way. The hunters and dogs did not turn them: about thirty calves were lost over the three nights. The rear camp says the keeper is no better than a dog, and the knot for the spring wolves is open on her cord.
- The fourth month was dry: no rain on the summer grazing, the small streams thin or stopped, the river lower and running. The band watered clan by clan by turns and moved by night. You read each herd at the water at dusk and she passed it on. The turns held. About eighty cattle, thirty goats and twenty sheep were lost, mostly calves and old cows, and about sixteen children died of the flux at fouled pools. A woman at the water said someone should ask you for rain; the Houndwarden would not carry it. Rain came back in the fifth month, three days steady, and some say it came for you.
- The eldest head gave you leave for one month at the three clans' margin, on three conditions: no coming into their ring or camp, no word carried either way, gone at the month's end. You kept them. On the twentieth night he came to the edge of the light and asked whether you ask anything of them, and whether you would carry word between them and the main camp; you answered both.
- At the river camp you gave the ledger from her cord. The Marchwarden asked whether the dimming of the star is yours and had your answer. No vote.
- What the Listening gave you of the cutters in the seventh and eighth months, toward the winter woods: about eighty, in three camps, with draught oxen, a day's walk deeper into the woods than last year. You told the Marchwarden; he answered from the songs that they have not come so far in before, and took your count to the Speaker.
- In the ninth month you told the Houndwarden of a herd that was hurt at the edge of the camp. The herders found five coughing beasts in the two clans' cattle and had them apart before the second day. Five died. The cough did not go through the herds. In winter the two clans' herds were kept on their own ground at the downwind end of the camp, on their own browse.
- The council asked you to stand with the hunt; you did, and the hunt kept its season. No share was set for you.
- The trade party came back from the stone towns with salt and iron at last year's price and about three fifths of the salt wanted. It saw some of the stone towns' own draught oxen coughing in their yards. The band now says the cough came from the stone towns.
- You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- Nothing was asked of you this year that you did not answer.

## What the Keeper ruled
- The Listening gives a gathering's state as your card says and no more. A herd with sick beasts in it reads as hurt in part, not with what and not which beasts. On your card.

## The sky
The star dimmed on five nights of your year, more than last year: one in the second month, one in the sixth, one in the ninth, one in the eleventh and one in the twelfth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people say they are the keeper's.

## Your people at the year's turn
About 5,070. The herds leaner by the calves and the dry month. The Speaker, the Houndwarden, the Marchwarden and the Saltkeeper have each heard you; none has taken your word. The three clans wintered apart again, a little nearer than last year.
