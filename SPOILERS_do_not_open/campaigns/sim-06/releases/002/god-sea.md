# Release: god-sea, Year 2, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 2, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 3, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 3 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- The Warden-Monarch read your stick and met you alone at the tide door on the third night. You told her the three things. She took your word as before and has kept the answer about Brennis from the court. She carried your two answers to the Master of Timber in her own words; he said the first was more than he expected and the second more than he would have liked, and took it.
- The first night's listening gave you the coast of a day: the town's own oars and hulls, voices on the quay, surf at the point. No hull that was not the town's, no voice that was not the town's, nothing moving along the bottom that was not tide. The deaf day followed.
- In the third month she came down alone and told the water at the tide door what the eldest remember, as you asked. You were in that water and heard it. That the promise is older than the walls: whoever keeps the lamp is kept count of. That the eldest say it was made to a lamp-warden named Brennis, by something that came up the quay steps and went down them again, and that the bell was hung so it could be called. That most of the town says Brennis made the promise herself, to the town, and nothing came up any steps. That an old net-mender tells of a town called Low Garrow which the sea took in one night, and that the tellers do not agree whether the promise is older than that town or younger. That the old woman at the east lamp had it from her grandmother that the thing on the step did not ask for a name, and Brennis did not give one.
- You joined a step-board at the quay in the second month, the keel of the stand-by boat in the eleventh, and each cost you the hand for a day. The quay held through four storms, the longest three days. The keel you joined last year was hauled in the first month and showed no worm, check or softening after a year in salt.
- The Warden-Monarch chose two tenders for your lamp, the old woman at the east lamp and the boy at the quay's west end, and showed them how to fill, trim and light it. They were told whose it is and not what it does. The lamp is in her keeping when you are not at the water.
- In the fifth month, nine days after the third storm, a decked crab boat with four aboard was caught at dusk in a long heavy swell with no wind in it. You lit your lamp by your own hand at the water's edge by the harbor mouth and stood. The mouth and the inner water lay calm; the swell broke on the skerries outside. The tenders refilled and trimmed it as shown and did not leave it. The boat came in with all four and the stand-by boat took her line. You were seen there by the Warden-Monarch, the tenders, the stand-by boat's crew and at least one of the Captains. That water would not take you for a season after, as your card gives.
- In spring you swam the skerries and the floor of the nearer bay by night. The harbor's own water, which you stilled in the fifth month, would not take you until the eighth; from then you swam the harbor bed too. Under the quay: the bell and the jar you know. On the harbor bed inside the mouth, under the line where the chain runs when it is raised: ballast stones, and the broken lower timbers of two boats of the town's own build, not long in the water. Beyond the skerries, on the floor of the nearer bay: sand, rock and weed, and nothing made by hands. Nothing moved along the bottom that was not fish or tide. You found nothing that anyone keeps.
- In summer you watched from the town's edge and the water. The lamp tenders and the point ferryman saw a hooded figure at dusk. No one spoke to you. You saw no one you would yet name a keeper of something small.
- You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- The Eldest Midwife, in the seventh month, alone at the lowest step at dusk, to the water: "I deliver what comes and bury what goes. You are the one that keeps a count, and I would like to know what you count for. If you want to be answered, come to a bedside. I will not come to the step again."
- The Warden-Monarch, in the twelfth month: a stick left in the jar with five notches, the nights the star dimmed, and no words.

## What the Keeper ruled
- Sound Timber: three pieces in a year, each weeks apart, is within your card. On your card.
- Calm Under the Lamp: the tenders tended it right, and the mouth lay calm while it burned. Tenders keeping it after you have lit it is within your card and your trade. On your card.

## The sky
The star dimmed on five nights of your year, more than last year: one in the second month, one in the sixth, one in the ninth, one in the eleventh and one in the twelfth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people tie the nights to the sea and the step.

## Your people at the year's turn
About 2,023. Stores enough; no one went hungry in the lean. The town does not know of you; the Warden-Monarch, four of the court and the two tenders do, and the heir has been told the keeping is real. The Harbormaster gives your asks as the court's orders and does not take your word. A shepherd from the hills asked a fisher whether the sea town still rings for the sea.
