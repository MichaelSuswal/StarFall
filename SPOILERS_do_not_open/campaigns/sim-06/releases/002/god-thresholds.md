# Release: god-thresholds, Year 2, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 2, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 3, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 3 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- At the ford the older toll watcher gave you the count from his cord: crossings down a little on last year, the trains as many or near it but shorter. It agrees with what you know by the blessing. He puts it down to a poor year on the river, which a trader told him.
- The senior gate guard had your answer and took it as weather. The gate is kept at night by the sworn band in pairs with a Doorwarden's boy at each post. Your answer went round the city: the Guest does not know.
- In the hall you asked your four things. The watch at that fold was a captain's and so Kessan's, and he said the failure was his. The dogs were late because the marches were hard and the dogs fed thin. Four columns had set boys on the night watch with one grown man among them on some nights; none had boys alone. The dead man's household is at a low quarter hearth that keeps it. Idris Veld fixed the price by the old sill table, paid from the captain's column's herd. Your orders went out by the Marshal's riders. The Steward entered the household's winter keep on your account, on a separate cord, and does not know how it is to be paid.
- The two counts: the Steward's gave a week to spare at the thaw; the hearth mothers' gave about eleven days short. He explained most of the eighteen days and could not explain some. You ordered sharing by hearth from the first snow, the poorest first, the war band last, and told the captains why yourself. The stores lasted to the thaw.
- You sat at about a dozen low quarter tables through the cold. The households say many things of the star; you told each you do not know whose it is. Kessan sits at your right; the Doorwardens took it as a slight. He gave you the band (about three hundred and sixty), the passes, and the toll cords: thin, near half of the old cords for a generation back. He has never counted the far toll houses, which are the Marshal's.
- The senior Doorwarden of the lower columns came to the hall door and was heard. He said a rite held by four is held by four, and asked that the oath be taught to him or one of his. He took your answer standing.
- At Idris Veld's table you asked what Marda Sill is. What she knows: a person in the recital, the first to take the toll at the ford for the Sarrow themselves, before whom the Sarrow guided for the wardens of a league in the lowlands; also a word in the oath's old form, said once at its close: as Marda held it. The Doorwardens of the high rite say it is a woman's name; the keepers of the lower columns say Marda is older than the Sarrow's own speech and that Sill may be what one is called who stands. She did not ask why you asked.
- At the thaw you blessed the far pass, 2.4 miles from the winter city. It holds: five of seven, and you know who crosses both passes.
- You walked the road to the north-east with the Marshal, house by house, to its far end, eight days out. He named the ford and the two nearest houses as thin and poor, and the two farthest as honest and rich. At the two farthest you saw new roofs, good horses and full smokehouses. The watchmen gave one story for all three and showed cords that agree with the riders' tally and the hall's. You left with their word and the cords as shown. The Marshal had not walked those houses in some years, and went quiet. Seven strangers were brought to you in spring, all ordinary.
- Summer with the lower columns. On the high grass in the tenth month the blue pass flower was not on the old cutting, where there had been fewer each summer.
- You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- A herder at a rite fire, in the tenth month: whether the cold is coming down, since the blue flower was not on the cutting.
- A younger Doorwarden of the lower columns, in the last month: that you judge a matter of grass between two households of his column. Your decisions gave no verdict, so none stands; the two households wait.
- The Steward has not asked you, but has asked Idris Veld, how your account is to be paid.

## What the Keeper ruled
- Nothing was put to the Keeper.

## The sky
The star dimmed on five nights of your year, more than last year: one in the second month, one in the sixth, one in the ninth, one in the eleventh and one in the twelfth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. The Doorwardens now count five dimmings against five blessed thresholds.

## Your people at the year's turn
About 18,150. The war band ate last and lean and held. Each office holder took your word this year.
