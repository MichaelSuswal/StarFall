# Release: god-dreams, Year 2, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 2, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 3, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 3 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- On the sixth day the seat spoke your answer from the wall at dusk. The Keeper of the Count took up the tally of dim nights that night. The headmen and the Night Sitter called it honest. Some kept households were let down. In the lowest streets it was said that you did not say no.
- At the scar the cutters showed you three of the lowest cuts where you could reach them. You looked long, touched none and claimed none. The old cutter said, "Then we are no wiser than we were." They have since ringed the three cuts with stones, unasked.
- The Warden of the Wall told you to your face what he distrusts: the flames that dip, a watch split between two lamps, and asking that is done without an order. He would have you bring no flame within the watch's yards, light none for a man on duty, ask the wall nothing but through him, and tell him the days you walk beyond the gate. Next morning his captain brought his answer on the honest thing: let the wall hold your door, and let the men who sit it be told by you what they sit against. The seat gave leave for the first, and four of the wall sit your door. He has not taken your word and has not refused it.
- The western party came home in the first month, all four alive and thin. They found no one and no smoke in the hills.
- The court sent six for salt in the third week of the second month, in a planked river boat, with twenty bolts of linen, a hundred pots and fifteen jars of flax oil, no flame and no lamp. They did not come home before the cold, and no word came all winter. The town had a second lean winter for salt. They came up the river at high water in the eighth month, all six, with salt. What they tell: no ship lay at the river's mouth; they went round the shore on foot, laden, in bad weather, to the harbor town where the salt is boiled; they were given bread and three nights; on the third night the leader spoke your words to the factor of the house that sets the price of salt for the bend, and he heard them through. They bought salt at the harbor's price from another house's pans. The gales shut the coast road and they wintered at the harbor. The answer they were given to carry: that house will set a fair standing price by the year, for stone of your scar cut to its order. The leader says the harbor people talk freely now of the guest at the bend who keeps a lamp. He told them nothing of what the lamp does.
- Keeping Sleep went to two households a night, by lot, and to three on three nights of deep winter that you named the day before. On those three mornings the wall's men at your door saw what your card says of you after three, and no more.
- In the fifth month you sat a night in the Night Sitter's work yard with an old woman, very sick, and a boy in fever. The old woman died near dawn; the boy lived. The Night Sitter said her work takes three nights' sleep and her hands, and that she would not ask for what she cannot see the bottom of. In the sixth month her pupil asked, and her household was kept on the thirteenth night. The household slept. The Night Sitter did not: she said so, flatly, and thanked the household for the trouble. What arrived in you at dawn was that household's fear and nothing else.
- The old Warden of the Door named his successor in the third month, a woman of the lower town who keeps both lamps. She carries your asks faithfully and has not said she takes you for the one awaited.
- In spring you walked six more hamlets with a half score of the wall watch and lit a flame only where asked. A headman asked, at dawn after the dim night of the ninth month, "Was it you?", and you answered as you had from the wall.
- You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- The Warden of the Wall, by his captain: that the men who sit your door be told by you what they sit against.
- The Keeper of the Count, through the new Warden of the Door: whether a household that lit its own niche at night from its kept flame was a third household kept. (Ruled below.)

## What the Keeper ruled
- Keeping Sleep keeps a household's sleep from fear. What a worker's own work takes from her is hers to pay and is not fear; your gift does not reach it. On your card.
- Three households on a named night carry what your card says and no more; the town's winter changes nothing.
- A flame a household lights from its own kept flame is a shrine kept for you, as last year's ruling gives, and not a household kept that night. The cap of two was kept.

## The sky
The star dimmed on five nights of your year, more than last year: one in the second month, one in the sixth, one in the ninth, one in the eleventh and one in the twelfth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. The Keeper of the Count's cord has them, with the dyings: on two of the five an old man and a woman in childbed died in the town, and on three no one did. Whether any dimming is yours is not known to anyone.

## Your people at the year's turn
About 5,040. Salt short through the winter; stores otherwise enough. The seat, the Keeper of the Count and the old Warden of the Door take your word; the Steward grudgingly; the Warden of the Wall has not.
