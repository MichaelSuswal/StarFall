## The year
Year 2. Your people's year opens in spring and runs round the four seasons to spring again. Months are counted from that opening: month 1 is the first month of spring.
Your god has been among your people for a year, as your Year 1 files tell it. Its decisions file says what it means to do this year, in order; that is its intent, and what comes of each step among your people is yours to work out. Where it answers something a mortal asked last year, the mortal hears the answer when and how the file says it is given.

## The world this year, as your people met it
- The weather. An ordinary year. The spring flood was ordinary in height. After it the deep water lay a little farther from the ford stones again, on the side away from the weirs; the ferry keepers saw it, as anyone watching a flood would. Summer was ordinary and the low water came when it does. Winter froze the channels as it does.
- The ford. From the town to the ford where the brook meets the larger river is 25.1 miles: one day by barge down the brook, two days on foot by the Brook Path [f46]. The salt carriers came less often again. Those who came, asked, say that others now go a southern way.
- In the seventh month, the first of autumn, a herd of the town's goats with a few cattle strayed from the hill pasture, down into the reed and the channels. How it was noticed, who went after it and what came of the search is an attempt for the Keeper: write who goes, how many, for how long, and what your record gives that bears on it, and write the year both ways, briefly.
- No stranger came this year but the carriers and the ordinary travelers at the ford.
- Five nights this year the star dimmed, more than last year. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. One fell in the second month of your year, one in the sixth, one in the ninth, one in the eleventh and one in the twelfth. Anyone awake under a clear sky saw it. What your people make of such nights, and of their coming oftener, is theirs.

## What is yours to decide, and what is not
Yours: everything your people do, think, say and feel, every mortal among them, how each office holder meets the god and whether each takes its word, their work, stores, births and deaths, quarrels and stories. Mortals have their own will. They do not know the god's plans; they meet what it does, as it does it.
Not yours:
- An outcome that is uncertain and contested (a fight, a search, a persuasion of someone outside your people, a boat in a swell, a craft tried beyond what your people know). Do not settle it. Write it in your report as an attempt: who tries what, when, what success and failure would each mean, and every circumstance from your record that bears on it. The Keeper gives the odds and rolls. Write the rest of your year both ways where it turns on that outcome, briefly.
- What a god's power does or shows. Say what the god is seen to do and what your people make of it. Where your year turns on what a power did, say so in your report and the Keeper will settle it.
- Anything about another people or about land beyond what your record gives. Give no distance or travel time that is not in your record or in this file; say where a party set out for and by what way, and the Keeper prices the trip.
- The name of your people or their home, and the name of any office holder your record leaves unnamed: these stay unnamed unless a moment in your year has a mortal say a name aloud, and then you write who said it, when and to whom.
