# Release: god-secrets, Year 2, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 2, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 3, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 3 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- The Granary Warden asked what you wanted for it and why a guest of a year would hand him the credit, and took the count as his own. The harvest was counted in at the door, cried and put to stick.
- The closing count of the whole store, on the twenty-seventh day of the first month, did not reach the far wall. It could not be squared with the Warden's book, and the cry at market was held for a sitting of the council.
- At the council table a boat headman asked who the man is who will not give a name, and you answered for yourself.
- In winter the Gate Warden took the cup and put his questions; you answered what you could and said so where you could not. You gave him the watch's rations in one figure. He said he owed no man a favor. You still hold the one kept thing. A reed cutter began a telling and, at your word, did not finish it.
- In spring a loaded boat grounded once at the river gate. You put the cost of the groundings in one figure beside the cost of dredging and gave both at the table when the new Speaker asked. The crews sounded the channel and the Clerk has the depths on sticks.
- On the ninth day of the tenth month you went down the river on the first of three boats, as a weigher: 31.9 miles, two days. The string lay about a fortnight at the boat families' landing at the mouth, sold its cargo, took on salt and wool, and came up in three days. At the mouth: the boat families, crews of coasting boats, and the ordinary talk of trade. The coasters work the shore both ways and name a harbor town along the coast to the west, as your people already tell it. You heard of no one who keeps the water below the town, and of nothing strange on it. No one there looked for you. You turned the key on nothing.
- Back on the twenty-eighth day, you asked to walk the length of the granary, and the Warden agreed. The bins nearest the door are full to the mark. The far bins are hollow: faced with sacks and empty behind. The Warden saw it with you and sent for the Clerk. The two families whose people keep that end say the roll is wrong.
- You asked the Clerk for an evening with the old league's tallies. He asked why, heard you, and said he will not answer until the harvest is in.
- You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- The old woman at the tanners' end, in the sixth month: "Is it you that does it, Counter?" (You answered that you do not know.) Nothing asked of you went unanswered.

## What the Keeper ruled
- Nothing was put to the Keeper. Your word on the dim nights is noted.

## The sky
The star dimmed on five nights of your year, more than last year: one in the second month, one in the sixth, one in the ninth, one in the eleventh and one in the twelfth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. The town counts eight such nights since you came and many tie them to you. You were in the town for all five.

## Your people at the year's turn
About 5,085. The harvest ordinary. What the store holds against the roll is now known to the Warden, the Clerk and you, and will be known to the council.
