# Release: god-rivers, Year 2, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 2, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 3, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 3 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- The council heard you on the ford. The Ferry Elder had the keepers cut the flood marks and a second cord of low-water marks at the same stone, as the town's own count. The Speaker asked why a guest cares for the ford and had your answer.
- You told the children plainly where you woke. The Seat Warden said the cup would be filled in the morning.
- In summer you went down the brook by barge with two ferry keepers and the Reckoner's cord walker, stopping at the four reed hamlets, to the ford where the brook meets the larger river: 25.1 miles, a day by barge without the stops. You were at the ford about six days and came home on foot by the Brook Path [f46] in two.
- At the ford: the deep water lies farther from the ford stones than the keepers' oldest marks, on the side away from the weirs. The salt carriers who crossed while you were there said that others now go a southern way; they named no place for it.
- On the fifth morning you stood in the larger river to the knee until the sun cleared the reeds. You felt no pull.
- Warm hands twice: a hamlet man with a fevered foot, and a young mother in the town after a hard birth. Both recovered. The cold was yours for days each time.
- In late summer you knelt in the running water below the gate for about an hour, with the Seat Warden, a ferry keeper and a well keeper watching, and slept about two hours after. Nothing came against you. What the water told you of the day: the deep water lying off the ford on the side away from the weirs; fewer crossings by the carriers than the keepers' cords show for other years; the ordinary muddle of weirs, ferry and washing; nothing strange coming down from the hills; no pull in this river.
- In autumn you told the council in one sitting what was measured, said and heard. The council had the ferry keepers set a mark to read by at the autumn low water and put the rest off to the thaw.
- In the first month of autumn a herd of about sixty goats and five cattle strayed from the hill pasture into the reed. Eight searched four days and brought home about thirty-five goats and three cattle. The rest were lost. You were not asked and did not go.
- You felt no other like yourself.

## Asked of you and not answered
- The ferry child, in spring: "Can I go with you to the ford?" (The Ferry Elder refused for you.)
- The ferry child, at the fire: "Then you are a spring's?"
- A ford keeper, at the ford: "What are you looking for in the river?"

## What the Keeper ruled
- Your readings of the listening, the pull and what you told the children are right as you stated them. Nothing is added to your card.

## The sky
The star dimmed on five nights of your year, more than last year: one in the second month, one in the sixth, one in the ninth, one in the eleventh and one in the twelfth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Some of your people tie the nights to you.

## Your people at the year's turn
About 2,030, with about 155 of them in the four reed hamlets. An ordinary year. No one takes you for more than a guest who counts and whose hands are good at a sickbed.
