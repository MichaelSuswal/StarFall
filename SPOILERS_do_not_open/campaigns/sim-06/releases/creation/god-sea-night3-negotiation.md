# Negotiation: god-sea, Night 3

## Round 1
### What was read and not priced
- You proposed no edge, so nothing is priced against a weakness and no weakness is owed.
- The habits and prides you wrote are taken as tempers: who your people are, able to fail. None is a guarantee.
- What you said of the land and its past is written as your wish or your people's belief. The world decides.
- Who each office holder is stands as who they are, never what they will do.
- Everything under Skills and culture is ordinary and free.

### Your landing: the helpers and the seat
Draft text: You come to a people that does not know you. The harbor town is the town's, the line of lamp-wardens holds the throne for a promise whose maker your people do not know, and the court serves the town under its own offices. So the form is "designed, not yet serving": you land with no helpers in fact and no seat that is yours in fact. Until each office is won in play, nobody acts for you while you are away, your unplayed time runs on your standing conduct alone, and your people go their own way under their own court. That is the cost, the same for any god who lands so. It is not a refusal of a default, it bears on no edge, and it buys nothing. Where your people keep a seat or a place for one they await, they keep it for that one and do not know it is you: an office is won when its holder takes you for the one awaited and takes your word, and not before.
Choices: (a) Keep the meeting as you wrote it, at the cost above. The offices stand as you described them, each under your people's own custom, and each becomes your helper when its holder hears you and takes your word, in play. (b) Say that your people already know you when you land: that you have been among them, or that they know you on sight for whose they are. Then the offices you described serve you from landing, your word is carried while you are away, and the meeting is rewritten so, in your words. Neither way is priced and neither buys anything.
The Keeper's reasons: an office can carry the word only of one its holder knows. What you wrote has your people holding their offices under their own law, or for one they have not met. The cost is said here plainly so that you choose knowing it.
Accept, refuse or counter, and give your reasons.

### The spears
Draft text: About one hundred and fifty who can carry a spear, fishers with a boat-hook and a short spear, mustered and drilled by the Captains each spring. Ordinary, not priced.
The Keeper's reasons: a force that is kept as a body, paid or fed for it and drilled, is ordinary up to about one in fifty of a people, and an edge above that. A muster of working people drilled for some days a year is a levy and not such a body, at any number a people could call.
### Their magic
Draft text: You asked for none and asked to be told if the fitting puts one among them. It does: in a town of this size there are one or two who work a small thing, as in most towns. Who they are and what they do is your people's own, written in their record, and each bears her own cost. They are ordinary, and no edge.
The Keeper's reasons: how much of it there is comes from the world and is the same for every people.

Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the default.
