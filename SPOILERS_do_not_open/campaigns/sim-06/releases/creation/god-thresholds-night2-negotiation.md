### What holds on every card
These are how this table prices, the same on any card, and are not argued item by item:
- A body held is not broken. A god that is bound, caged, walled in or shut out stays so until it is freed or its body is broken; only a broken body returns.
- A return's price is taken by a logged roll from your own record (your record of gods and your goals). You are not told which item went.
- A thing that is the instrument of a power: in any other hand it is only the ordinary thing, and no one else gains the power by holding it. If it is destroyed, the power is gone until your next return, when the thing comes back with your body. If another holds it when your body breaks, it stays where it is.
- Anything a power does to another god, or to a thing in another god's keeping, is unruled until it happens.
- One weakness buys one step: one improvement elsewhere, or one notch on one power.

### The Latch
Draft text:
Description: With the key in her hand, the god blesses a threshold by standing in it, laying salt on the sill and speaking the guest oath over it: a door, a gate, a tent flap, a ford or a pass, any one crossing a body goes through. She holds at most seven; an eighth releases the oldest. While a threshold is blessed she knows, wherever she is, when anyone crosses it and how many, and who if she has seen their face; nothing of what is said or done there. By a word spoken to the key she may shut a blessed threshold that has a frame and a leaf (a door, a gate, a flap): it opens to no hand, ram or tool until she lifts it or its frame is broken, and it admits anyone she has named guest by speaking the oath over them.
Drawback: to be chosen, below.
Limits: A ford or a pass can be blessed and watched, never shut: it has no frame to hold. A ford is blessed for a spear's length either side of where she stood. A door moved elsewhere is not the threshold; a frame rebuilt in place is. Each blessing uses a handful of salt, which is spent. One who crosses is a body to her unless she has seen the face; she does not know a god from a mortal by a crossing. A host's death leaves the mark. Guests may be named in a body only when they stand before her. The oath: a guest she has named, and anyone who has eaten under a blessed roof with her leave, she may not harm under that roof or within a day's walk after; if she does, that threshold shuts against her and every other goes dark for a season. That is a condition of the power and a penalty for breaking her own word, so it is a Limit and buys nothing. A mind: none. A scarcity: it spends salt. More than ordinary craft: a door made or held by more than craft is unruled. Use across a year: bounded by the seven. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, water, being watched.
Choices, for the drawback, each in full:
(a) The guest's law on herself: she may not cross any threshold she has not blessed unless one who keeps it bids her in. A door she has broken is still a threshold.
(b) The shut door holds her too: while any threshold stands shut by her word, no door anywhere opens to her own hand, and she passes only where another opens for her; and a shutting lasts a night and a day unless she renews it at the threshold in person.
The Keeper's reasons: a knowing from any distance and a door no ram opens is the largest thing on your card, and the oath as you wrote it costs only if you break your word. A drawback is something you would notice losing every day you hold the power.

### The Step
Draft text:
Description: From inside a threshold she holds blessed in full, the god steps through and arrives in the same moment in another she holds blessed in full, with what she holds and wears, and with one person who holds her hand and has been named guest.
Drawback: to be chosen, below.
Limits: Not to a threshold she has not blessed, one gone dark, a broken one, or a lesser mark. Not while bound or held, nor without the key in hand. If the arrival is blocked she arrives within a pace of it. Other gods as the one led: unruled. Use across a year: bounded by the drawback. A mind: none. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, water, being watched, a scarcity.
Choices, each in full:
(a) The door she left stands open for a night: nothing of the oath holds there, it will not shut, and it tells her nothing of who crossed. And she is the guest of the door she arrived in: until the dawn after a full day she may not go beyond sight of it, nor shut or lift it.
(b) The door she left stands open for a night, as in (a), and for a day she cannot shut or lift the door she arrived in (both as you wrote them); and she may step once between one new moon and the next.
The Keeper's reasons: a step across any distance is a great thing, and your two costs fall on doors, not on you. One of these puts a cost where you would feel it.

### The Breaker's Hand
Draft text:
Description: The god lays a hand on a thing held shut (a bar, a lock, a gate, the wall of a house, a seal, a weir, a chain) and it gives where she touched: wood splits, iron parts, mortar fails. One stroke does what a very strong person with a ram could do in a day, and no more. It does nothing to a living body or to a thing that is not shut.
Drawback: to be chosen, below. And as you wrote: she can mend nothing she has broken, a blessed threshold she breaks loses its blessing for good, and her people may not mend it at her order for a season.
Limits: Broken only where she touched. A thing broken by another's hand with something she gave them is not her breaking. A thing sealed or held by another god or by more than craft: unruled. Being watched: it is seen. A mind: none. Not applicable: light and dark, distance, falling, water, a scarcity.
Choices, each in full:
(a) The arm pays: the arm that broke hangs useless until the next dawn, and that hand cannot hold the key.
(b) The breaking unbars her: for a day after, none of her blessed thresholds will shut.
The Keeper's reasons: not mending what she breaks restates what a breaker is, and a drawback that restates the god costs nothing. One of these is a cost.

### The Reading of the Host
Draft text:
Description: Of one person seated across a table or standing across a threshold from her, the god may know one thing, yes or no: does this one mean harm to a guest at this table or this crossing. Not why, and not to whom. It holds for the one meal or the one crossing.
Drawback: Whoever she reads learns that she has read them, and learns the same of her. (Chosen by you from the pair.)
Limits: One person at a time. Not one who is asleep or insensible, not a beast, not a god. It reads the meaning to harm at this meeting, never the mind beyond it, and compels no one. The god's own people: read as anyone. Distance: across a table or a threshold. Use across a year: no limit; each reading shows her. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water, being watched, a scarcity, more than ordinary craft.
Choices: none; you chose.
The Keeper's reasons: it is near to minds, so it is kept to one question at one meeting.

### The Return Home
Draft text:
Description: When the god's body is broken, she returns whole after a month at the nearest ground of her kind: the nearest standing doorway of a house where people live, or gate of a wall. What she carries comes back with her, the key too if it was on her.
Drawback: Two items of memory or habit at each return.
Limits: The standing scale's month rung; the faster return costs more. The month runs from the breaking. A body held is not broken. The walk is added.
Choices: none; you named the rung. The ground is written as the plainest reading of "her kind"; say if it is wrong.
The Keeper's reasons: the standing scale.

### The trade: the key as the instrument
Draft text (weakness): The Latch comes only through the key of dark iron, and The Step, which stands on it, goes with it. If the key is out of her hand and her person and held or kept by another, both are gone until she holds it again: the marks stand, dark to her, and she can neither shut, lift, hear nor step. In any other hand it is only an iron key that fits no lock. A copy is a key. In her own keeping out of arm's reach it is not taken. If it is destroyed, both are gone until her next return.
What it buys: one of her priesthood who has taken the oath from her in person may, at her order, lay a lesser mark on a doorway of a place her people hold, by salt, the oath and the rite. A lesser mark counts among her seven and is known to her as any is; it cannot be shut and cannot be stepped to. If the key is taken, lesser marks go dark with the rest.
Choices, each in full:
(a) as written above;
(b) a different weakness of your own naming in place of the key, priced by the same rule, for the same step;
(c) no trade: the key is the power's tool as an ordinary possession would be, any key or none serves, and only her own hand marks a threshold.
The Keeper's reasons: one weakness, one step. The weakness is a heavy one, since two powers hang on it, and the step is priced as you asked it.
