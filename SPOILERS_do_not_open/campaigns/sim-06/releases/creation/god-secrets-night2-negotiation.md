# Negotiation: god-secrets, Night 2

## Round 1

Your self is read. Below, each thing you proposed is written as draft card text (Description, Drawback, Limits), with a choice wherever the price could go two ways, and the Keeper's reasons. Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the default.

### What holds on every card
These are how this table prices, the same on any card, and are not argued item by item:
- A body held is not broken. A god that is bound, caged, walled in or shut out stays so until it is freed or its body is broken; only a broken body returns.
- A return's price is taken by a logged roll from your own record (your record of gods and your goals). You are not told which item went.
- A thing that is the instrument of a power: in any other hand it is only the ordinary thing, and no one else gains the power by holding it. If it is destroyed, the power is gone until your next return, when the thing comes back with your body. If another holds it when your body breaks, it stays where it is.
- Anything a power does to another god, or to a thing in another god's keeping, is unruled until it happens.
- One weakness buys one step: one improvement elsewhere, or one notch on one power.

### Second Face
Offered both ways.
(a) As form, at no price: your form gives you two shapes. The shift is written under Form with the limits you gave: a long breath; seen if anyone is looking; what you wear and carry falls where you stood, but for what a dog can hold in its mouth; the dog cannot speak, is no tougher than a dog, and a body broken as the dog returns as the man.
(b) As you asked, with a price heavier than it needs.
Description: The god changes between the man and the old dog, in about a long breath, wherever it stands, seen if anyone is looking. What it wears and carries falls where it stood, but for what a dog can hold in its mouth. One body at a time. As the dog it understands speech and makes none.
Drawback: Each change, either way, leaves it slow for a day, as a man after a fever, and takes one ordinary word from its tongue until it hears the word spoken and learns it again. Changes close together lengthen the slowness and cost more words.
Limits (either way): The dog's body is a dog's. Broken as the dog, it returns as the man. No limit on how long it stays a dog. Other gods: a god at close quarters knows it in either shape. A mind: none. Not applicable: light and dark, distance, falling, water, a scarcity, more than ordinary craft.
The Keeper's reasons: a rolled form of two shapes needs no price beyond its own limits. The drawback in (b) is heavier than the shift needs; it is written as you ask if you choose it, and nothing is given for it.

### Kept Word
Draft text:
Description: When a mortal of the god's choosing tells it a true thing in trust and it agrees to keep it, the thing is kept. While it is kept, no mortal magic can read it out of the god, nor out of the teller while the teller thinks of it as told to the god. The god feels a small tug in the chest if the teller speaks the thing aloud to another in the same town, and does not learn to whom. It holds at most seven kept things.
Drawback: A kept thing binds the god absolutely. It may never repeat it, act where the act would reveal it, or bargain with it, not to save itself or its people, until the teller releases it. An eighth thing displaces the oldest, which the god forgets entirely, and the teller is not told.
Limits: Only what is told in trust, not what is overheard. The god may refuse before the telling is finished. A teller's death releases nothing. A thing already known to many takes no place. More than ordinary craft, another god's reading: unruled until it happens. A mind: it rules none. The god's own people: as anyone. Use across a year: bounded by the seven. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water, being watched, a scarcity.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: it binds the god more than it arms it, and is priced as written.

### Finding What Was Hidden
Draft text:
Description: Holding its bronze key, and standing in a room, a yard, a cellar or a stretch of road no wider than a market square, the god feels where something has been put on purpose so that it will not be found: a false bottom, a sealed door, a buried cache, a ledger behind a wall, a cellar under a cellar. It learns where the hiding place is and roughly how large, never what is in it, who hid it or why.
Drawback: Each finding leaves its voice rough for a day. It may never search the hidings of its own people, the council and its helpers among them. (Chosen by you from the pair.)
Limits: Hiding places, not secrets. A thing lost, or buried by time and not by intent, is not found. A place hidden by a god or by magic may or may not be found; unruled until it happens. Not in the dog's shape. Distance: where it stands, no further. Being watched: nothing marks it. Use across a year: no limit beyond the voice; it goes where the god goes. A scarcity: none. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, water.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: a finding power gives the place and never the cause. The blind spot for its own people is the real price; the voice is small.

### Return
Draft text:
Description: When the god's body is broken, it returns whole after a season, in the man's shape, at the nearest ground of its kind. What it carries comes back with it; a thing taken from it before the breaking does not.
Drawback: One item of memory or habit at each return.
Limits: The standing scale's season rung. A body held is not broken. The walk is added.
Choices: "ground of its kind" must be written. Each in full:
(a) the nearest market street, anyone's, where strangers pass unremarked;
(b) the nearest door that has been sealed or forgotten.
Or name another, if it gives no advantage the nearest of these would not.
The Keeper's reasons: the standing scale. The ground must be one the Keeper can find on any day.

### The trade: the key as the instrument
Draft text (weakness): Finding What Was Hidden comes only through the bronze key. While another holds it on their person or in a place they alone can reach, the power is gone until the god holds it again. In any other hand it is only a small bronze key. (Your draft said whoever holds it "holds the power"; no one else gains it, as your next sentence says.) A copy is a copy. Lost, buried or sunk, it lies dormant until the god takes it up, and the god has no sense of where. Left on the ground when the god takes the dog's shape, it is anyone's to pick up. If it is destroyed, the power is gone until the god's next return.
What you asked for it: Kept Word holding twelve things, not seven.
Choices, each in full:
(a) as you asked: twelve kept things;
(b) another improvement of one step's size in its place, named by you, since twelve in place of seven is a small step for this weakness;
(c) a different weakness of your own naming in place of the key, priced by the same rule;
(d) no trade: the key is the power's tool as an ordinary possession would be, any hand of the god's serves without it, and Kept Word stays at seven.
The Keeper's reasons: one weakness, one step. The Keeper says plainly when a step asked is smaller than its weakness would cover, as it says when one is larger.

### Read, not priced
The coat, boots, knife, purse and weights are ordinary possessions. So is the tally stick, which does nothing a tally stick does not. Your caution and your wish to be unremarkable are conduct and buy nothing.
