# Negotiation: god-beasts, Night 2

## Round 1

Your self is read. Below, each thing you proposed is written as draft card text (Description, Drawback, Limits), with a choice wherever the price could go two ways, and the Keeper's reasons. Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the default.

### What holds on every card
These are how this table prices, the same on any card, and are not argued item by item:
- A body held is not broken. A god that is bound, caged, walled in or shut out stays so until it is freed or its body is broken; only a broken body returns.
- A return's price is taken by a logged roll from your own record (your record of gods and your goals). You are not told which item went.
- A thing that is the instrument of a power: in any other hand it is only the ordinary thing, and no one else gains the power by holding it. If it is destroyed, the power is gone until your next return, when the thing comes back with your body. If another holds it when your body breaks, it stays where it is.
- Anything a power does to another god, or to a thing in another god's keeping, is unruled until it happens.
- One weakness buys one step: one improvement elsewhere, or one notch on one power.

### The Listening
Draft text:
Description: Standing still with its attention set outward, the god knows where living things are within about a mile of its body: where, how large, which way they move, and whether each is hunting, grazing, resting, afraid or hurt. It knows beast from person. It does not know a thought, a name, or what a thing intends beyond its present state. It holds for as long as the god stands still.
Drawback: It never reads the god's own people: they are an empty place in the listening. (Chosen by you from the pair.) While it listens the body is still and deaf as a stone, hearing no speech, step or shout until it breaks the listening with a breath of effort. Every kill or death within its reach is felt as a small cold weight, and it cannot choose not to feel it.
Limits: Through trees, hills and walls; not through deep water or thick earth. It does not tell two persons of a size apart. One who stands as still as the god is not found, and one very still among beasts reads as a beast. Another god reads as a living thing of a size, no more. A mind: none. Use across a year: as often as it stands still. A scarcity: none. More than ordinary craft: a thing hidden by more than craft is unruled until it happens. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, being watched.
Choices: none here; see the trade.
The Keeper's reasons: it is a knowing of things out of sight, so it gives where and what state, never who or why.

### Beast Tongue
Draft text:
Description: The god understands the voice and bearing of any beast it speaks to and is understood by it, in a beast's plain terms: hunger, fear, ground, young, water, a thing that hunts, a thing that is safe. It can ask where, how many, how long ago, and what the beast fears. It commands no beast, and no beast tells what it does not know.
Drawback: A beast it speaks with loses some of its fear of the god and, over days of company, of people near it. Such beasts come close, linger and grow bold, and stay so when the god is gone.
Limits: Beasts only: not people, and not things that wear a beast's shape wrongly, which the god knows by their not answering. It does not speak through a beast to another place. Use across a year: no limit; each speaking leaves its boldness. A mind: it rules none. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water, being watched, a scarcity, more than ordinary craft.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: the boldness is a price that falls on the people the god keeps, which it would notice.

### The Trail
Draft text:
Description: Holding a thing that was of a wrong done (blood, a scrap, a thing the doer held, a footprint), the god can follow the doer by that trail across any ground, at the pace of its form, for as long as the trail holds, up to about a season after the wrong. It finds the doer. It does not learn the doer's name or reason.
Drawback: Once it takes a trail it cannot put it down until it finds the doer or the trail goes cold. It turns for nothing: no entreaty, no better trail, no need of its own people. And it cannot decide afterward that the wrong was less than it thought.
Limits: Only what is a wrong in the god's own judgment. Deep water, a long burn or hard rain makes a trail go cold; a long stretch by cart or boat breaks it. One trail at a time. It may refuse a trail and may not give one back. A doer that is a god: unruled until it happens. A mind: none. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, being watched, a scarcity.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: the trail it cannot leave is a heavy price, and the power is written as asked.

### The Horn Call
Draft text:
Description: When the god sounds its horn, every beast of the wild within hearing, about two miles on open ground and a mile in timber, turns and comes: herds, packs and singles, hunter and hunted together. They gather without attacking one another or the god, stay while it holds the note and about a quarter of an hour after, and then scatter. They are called, not commanded.
Drawback: The god cannot choose who comes. Those that answer are angered for about a day, as beasts pulled off their own ground, and they remember it. The call tells every person in earshot where the god is.
Limits: Herd beasts of a people within hearing answer too and may break pens. Not through a mountain. Things no one has a name for do not answer. A mind: beasts are called, never ruled. Use across a year: no limit; the anger is the cost. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, water, a scarcity.
Choices: none here; see the trade.
The Keeper's reasons: fairly priced as written.

### The Fed Storm
Draft text:
Description: When the god is angry in earnest at a wrong it has judged and not yet answered, a storm gathers within about three days over the country a day's walk around it, and breaks. It waters the ground and fills the low places. It comes of the god's anger and not its will: it cannot be called for rain's sake or sent away.
Drawback: The storm falls on innocent and guilty alike, and on ground the god does not choose; it floods the low places and can drown the young of the herds. It does not end until the anger is answered. And, heavier than the power needs and written as asked: at the storm's height the god feels the dead of that flood as it feels a kill in The Listening, listening or not.
Limits: Temper or vexation brings none; only a judged wrong. It strikes no one by name. Being watched: it is weather to any who do not know. The god's own people are not spared. Use across a year: only as often as the god is so angered. A mind: none. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, a scarcity.
Choices: the feeling of the flood's dead is heavier than this power needs. It is written as you ask; say if you would rather it were not.
The Keeper's reasons: a power that comes unbidden is half a price already. The Keeper adds nothing and takes nothing for the heavier part.

### Return by the Herd
Draft text:
Description: When the god's body is broken, it stands up whole after a season among beasts at the nearest wild ground where game lives. What it carries comes back with it, the horn too if it was on the god when the body broke.
Drawback: One item of memory or habit at each return.
Limits: The standing scale's season rung. A body held is not broken. The walk is added. If another holds the horn when the body breaks, it stays where it is, and in that hand it is only a horn (your draft had the power "held by that hand"; no one else gains it).
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: the standing scale.

### The trade: the horn as the instrument
Draft text (weakness): The Horn Call comes only through the god's horn. If it is held or carried away by another, the power is gone until the god holds it again. In any other hand it is only a horn. A copy, or any other horn, does nothing. If it is destroyed, the power is gone until the god's next return. (Your draft had a new horn made from a beast freely given; see what holds on every card.)
What you asked for it: The Listening reaching about ten miles in open country.
Draft text (what it buys): In open country The Listening reaches about ten miles, and in timber about three. Within a mile it is as the power's text. Beyond a mile it knows only gatherings: a herd, a pack, a band of a dozen or more, where, which way they move and their state, and not a single beast or person.
Choices, each in full:
(a) as written above;
(b) the reach in full, every single thing to ten miles, for a second weakness of your naming beside the horn;
(c) a different weakness of your own naming in place of the horn, priced by the same rule, for the step in (a);
(d) no trade: the horn is the power's tool as an ordinary possession would be, any horn the god sounds serves, and The Listening stays at a mile.
The Keeper's reasons: ten times the reach is more than one step if every hidden walker in ten miles is known; narrowed to gatherings past the first mile, one weakness covers it.
