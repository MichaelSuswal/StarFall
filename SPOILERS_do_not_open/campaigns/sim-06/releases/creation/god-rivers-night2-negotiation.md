# Negotiation: god-rivers, Night 2

## Round 1

Your self is read. Below, each thing you proposed is written as draft card text (Description, Drawback, Limits), with a choice wherever the price could go two ways, and the Keeper's reasons. Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the default.

### What holds on every card
These are how this table prices, the same on any card, and are not argued item by item:
- A body held is not broken. A god that is bound, caged, walled in or shut out stays so until it is freed or its body is broken; only a broken body returns.
- A return's price is taken by a logged roll from your own record (your record of gods and your goals). You are not told which item went.
- A thing that is the instrument of a power: in any other hand it is only the ordinary thing, and no one else gains the power by holding it. If it is destroyed, the power is gone until your next return, when the thing comes back with your body. If another holds it when your body breaks, it stays where it is.
- Anything a power does to another god, or to a thing in another god's keeping, is unruled until it happens.
- One weakness buys one step: one improvement elsewhere, or one notch on one power.

### The two shapes
Not a power. Your form gives you two shapes, and the shift is written under Form with the limits you gave: three slow breaths; the water shape cannot speak, hold a thing or carry the stone; a held body cannot shift. No price.

### Listening water
Draft text, two ways. The Keeper will not write it without an end to its reach: a hearing of every word by every bank of a whole country's waters is over the top at any price, and no god has that.
Choices, each in full:
(a) Near and full.
Description: Standing in, kneeling at or laying a hand on running water or a spring, the god holds still and hears what the water carries within a day's walk (twenty miles) of water up and down from that place, feeders included: words spoken on or near the banks within about a day, that someone crossed or drank and how many, what sickness runs in it, what the weather is doing upstream, what lies in a pool or under a bank. It comes as the water's own muddled telling, old and new together.
(b) Far and plain.
Description: Standing in, kneeling at or laying a hand on running water or a spring, the god holds still and hears the water's own state through all the water joined to it, as far as it runs: flood and drought, ice, where it is held or turned, what fouls it and what sickness runs in it. No words, and nothing of who is on its banks.
Drawback (either way, as you asked it): While listening the god is still and helpless, deaf and blind to all that is not water, and cannot shift or stand. Afterward it sleeps twice as long as it listened, and no one can wake it.
Limits (either way): Water truly joined and running or rising; a sealed jar, a cistern with no inflow and a lake with no outflow tell only what is in them. Frozen water is silent. Ice, a dam or a long dry reach ends it there. It tells what is, never why or who caused it. Other gods: a god in the same water is not known by this power. The god's own people: heard as anyone. A mind: none. Use across a year: bounded by the sleep. A scarcity: none. More than ordinary craft: water changed by more than craft may tell false or nothing; unruled until it happens. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, being watched.
The Keeper's reasons: your sleep is a fair price for either way, and is taken as you offered it. It does not buy a reach with no end.

### Calling the water
Draft text, two ways.
(a) Within sight.
Description: With the stone in hand, the god raises or lowers, speeds or stills, turns or parts running or standing water within its sight: swells a stream, drops a ford to a trickle, holds a current, pushes it against its fall for a short time, shifts a spring's course by a few paces, opens a spring stopped with a stone where water lies behind it. It moves what water is there and makes none.
Drawback: Each working draws the water from the god's own body. It goes dry, brittle and slow, and cannot shift or work any power again until it has lain in running water a day for a small working and up to a month for the largest within sight. Worked in anger, the water goes where the god feels and not where it aims.
(b) To the size of a valley.
Description: As (a), and beyond sight: up to the whole of one river in one valley, to the height of that river's own greatest flood and no higher. Never a sea, and never a river turned into a new bed for good.
Drawback: As (a), and for a working beyond sight the god lies in running water a full season, unable to shift or to work any power, and one item of memory or habit is taken from its record as a return's is.
Limits (either way): Not water in a sealed vessel, in a living body, falling as rain or standing as ice. Water held by a dam or built channel only once the hold is broken. No more water than is in reach. Being watched: it is seen as flood or ebb. The god's own people: the water does not know them and spares no one. Use across a year: bounded by the lying in water. A mind: none. A scarcity: none. Not applicable: light and dark, falling.
The Keeper's reasons: you asked for no cap and a heavy price. A flood that drowns a valley falls on a whole people in a day, so the price for it is a season and an item; a top is still written, because nothing is over the top. Way (b) weighs heavily when the Keeper sets your card beside what a god here may carry.

### Warm hands
Draft text:
Description: With its hands on a hurt or fevered person, and water it has touched to drink or wash with, the god mends broken flesh, brings down fevers, and clears the sicknesses that move in water and the sicknesses of childbirth, one person at a time, giving of its own warmth.
Drawback: The god takes the chill. Its hands and then its whole body go cold, slow and clumsy for as many days as the healing would have taken in nature. It cannot heal itself.
Limits: It does not raise the dead, restore a limb, undo age or cure what is not hurt. It cannot be sold or bought with another's suffering. The warmth will not come for anyone the god has deceived or knowingly wronged (a condition, not a price). Distance: touch. Use across a year: bounded by the cold. A mind: none. Other gods: unruled. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, being watched, a scarcity.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: the cold, counted in the days nature would have taken, is a full price.

### Unsigned
Offered both ways, because part of it describes no act only a god can do.
(a) As no power, at no price: the god's water workings look like weather and water, and carry no sign of a hand. Whoever sees it work, or reasons it out, knows.
(b) As a power.
Description: The god's workings through water leave no sign that leads to it. A mortal who hears of one, or sees only its result, finds nothing in it that points to a hand.
Drawback: It is never off. The god cannot make a working show its hand, even when it wants to be known, and cannot claim one in words: when it tries to say "we did this", what it says is that the water did. The thanks for what it does go to the water, the ferryman or chance.
Limits: It hides works, not the god's nature: a god at close quarters still knows it. It hides nothing from one who watched it work, or was told by one who watched. It hides no deed with no water in it. A mind: it rules none. No one is made unable to believe; they are given nothing to go on. (Your draft had mortals unable to believe when told. That would rule minds across a whole people, and it is not written.)
The Keeper's reasons: a drawback that only restates what the power does costs nothing, so (b) carries the tongue that cannot claim.

### Rise at the spring
Draft text:
Description: When the god's body is broken or scattered, it gathers again after a season at the nearest spring or head of running water, in the woman's shape. What it carries comes back with it.
Drawback: One item of memory or habit at each return.
Limits: The standing scale's season rung. A body held is not broken. The walk is added.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: the standing scale.

### The trade: the stone as the instrument
Draft text (weakness): Calling the water comes only through the flat grey stone. If it is carried off, won, lost or given, the power is gone until the god holds it again. In any other hand it is only a stone. (Your draft had a free gift pass the power to the other hand; no one else gains it.) It is not taken while it lies in the god's pouch or within its reach on the bank. If it is destroyed, the power is gone until the god's next return. The god does not feel where the stone lies (your draft's "faint cold in the bones" is a sense at a distance and would be a second step).
What it buys: a far sense.
Description: While the god stands in or touches running water, it feels another god that is standing in or on water of the same river (its feeders and its outflow included) as a pull, upstream or down. Nothing of who, what it can do, how far, or whether it means well. A pull is a pull: it does not tell one from several.
Choices, each in full:
(a) as written above;
(b) a different weakness of your own naming in place of the stone, priced by the same rule, for the same step;
(c) no trade: the stone is an ordinary possession, Calling the water needs only the god's hand, and no far sense is had.
The Keeper's reasons: one weakness, one step. The sense is kept to one river and to a pull, because knowing another god from far off is no part of any god here without a price.
