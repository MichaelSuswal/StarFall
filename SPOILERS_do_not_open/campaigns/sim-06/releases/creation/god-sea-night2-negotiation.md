# Negotiation: god-sea, Night 2

## Round 1

Your self is read. Below, each thing you proposed is written as draft card text (Description, Drawback, Limits), with a choice wherever the price could go two ways, and the Keeper's reasons. Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the default.

### What holds on every card
These are how this table prices, the same on any card, and are not argued item by item:
- A body held is not broken. A god that is bound, caged, walled in or shut out stays so until it is freed or its body is broken; only a broken body returns.
- A return's price is taken by a logged roll from your own record (your record of gods and your goals). You are not told which item went.
- A thing that is the instrument of a power: in any other hand it is only the ordinary thing, and no one else gains the power by holding it. If it is destroyed, the power is gone until your next return, when the thing comes back with your body. If another holds it when your body breaks, it stays where it is.
- Anything a power does to another god, or to a thing in another god's keeping, is unruled until it happens.
- One weakness buys one step: one improvement elsewhere, or one notch on one power.

### Water's Hearing
Draft text:
Description: With its right hand in salt water and a night given to listening, the god hears what the sea has taken up in the last day along that coast, within about a day's walk (twenty miles) of where it listens: voices at the shore, hulls, oars, bodies falling in, things moving along the bottom. Sounds only, never faces or names, and never why a sound was made.
Drawback: For a day after a listening, the god hears nothing spoken on land. Mortal speech is a hum to it.
Limits: Salt water only; nothing through fresh water or inland. Nothing from past the shelf. It cannot choose which sounds come. A storm drowns everything but the storm. Distance: twenty miles along the coast, no further. Nothing a watcher sees marks it. The god's own people: their sounds come as anyone's. A mind: it rules none. Use across a year: one night for one listening, and a deaf day after each. A scarcity: it touches none. More than ordinary craft: a sound made or hidden by more than craft may not come true; unruled until it happens. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying.
Choices: none; the price stands one way.
The Keeper's reasons: it is a power that tells of things far off, so it gives effects (sounds) and never causes. The night it takes and the deaf day are a real price for a god that must hear its court.

### Calm Under the Lamp
Draft text:
Description: With the god's lamp lit and set at the water's edge or on a boat's bow, the water of one bay or harbor lies calm as far as the lamp's light would show on a still night: no swell, no chop, no breaking surf. It lasts while the lamp burns on one filling of oil and ends when the lamp goes out for any reason.
Drawback: The water it stilled will not take the god for a season after. It cannot swim there, and its beast half goes stiff and heavy if it touches that water.
Limits: It stops no storm, stills no open sea, and does nothing to what lives in the water. Anyone may snuff the lamp. Being watched: the lamp is seen; the calm reads as chance to those who do not know. Carrying: the lamp must be lit by the god's own hand. The god's own people: the calm serves anyone on that water. A mind: none. A scarcity: it makes a harbor safe for a night and makes no harbor. Use across a year: each stretch of water stilled is closed to the god for a season. Not applicable: falling.
Choices: none here; see the trade.
The Keeper's reasons: the season's shutting-out is a price this god would feel in its own harbor, and it stands as written.

### Sound Timber
Draft text:
Description: Timber the god shapes and joins with its own right hand (a keel, a strake, a pile, a door-post) does not rot, split or take worm in salt water for a generation of ordinary use. The work goes at the pace of the craft, one piece at a time, and is ordinary craft in every other way.
Drawback: Each piece costs the god its right hand's strength for a day: it cannot hold a hammer or grip a rope, and the burned left hand cannot do the work.
Limits: Only timber the god itself shaped. Not stone or iron. Lasting, not stronger; fire burns it. It looks well made and nothing more. Use across a year: one piece, then a day without the hand. A scarcity: sound timber may be short where the god lands; the power makes timber last and makes none. More than ordinary craft: none. Not applicable: light and dark, distance, falling, water, being watched, other gods, a mind.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: the hand is the price, and the pace keeps it from fitting out a fleet in a year.

### Return from the Deep
Draft text:
Description: When the god's body is broken, it returns whole after a season at the nearest shore of the sea. What it carries comes back with it.
Drawback: One item of memory or habit at each return.
Limits: The standing scale's season rung. It returns to the sea's edge, not to its people; the walk is added. A body held is not broken (your draft had "held, scattered or shut out"; held and shut out do not bring a return).
Choices: none; you named the rung.
The Keeper's reasons: the standing scale, as every god's return is priced.

### The trade: the lamp as the instrument
Draft text (weakness): Calm Under the Lamp comes only through the god's lamp. If it is taken, lost or given, the power is gone until the god holds it again. In any other hand it is only a lamp. (Your draft said whoever takes it "has that power"; no one else gains it.) If it is destroyed, the power is gone until the god's next return. (Your draft had a new lamp made over a season; see what holds on every card.)
What you asked for it: three things, a wider reach (a whole bay, or a harbor mouth and its roadstead), a longer hold (refilled and tended, a night and a day), and helpers tending it in your name. One weakness buys one of them.
Choices, each in full:
(a) The longer hold: the lamp may be refilled and tended, and the calm holds while it burns, up to a night and a day; then it must go out.
(b) The wider reach: the calm covers the whole of one bay, or one harbor mouth and its roadstead, for one filling of oil.
(c) Helpers: a keeper you have named face to face may light and tend the lamp in your name while you are elsewhere; the calm is as in the power's text, and the water's refusal still falls on you.
(d) A different weakness of your own naming in place of the lamp, priced by the same rule, for one of the three.
(e) No trade: the lamp is the power's tool as an ordinary possession would be, any lamp you light serves, and nothing is bought.
A second of the three would need a second weakness.
The Keeper's reasons: one weakness, one step, the rule for every card.
