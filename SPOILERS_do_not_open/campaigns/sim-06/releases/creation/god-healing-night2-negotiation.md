# Negotiation: god-healing, Night 2

## Round 1

Your self is read. Below, each thing you proposed is written as draft card text (Description, Drawback, Limits), with a choice wherever the price could go two ways, and the Keeper's reasons. Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the default.

### What holds on every card
These are how this table prices, the same on any card, and are not argued item by item:
- A body held is not broken. A god that is bound, caged, walled in or shut out stays so until it is freed or its body is broken; only a broken body returns.
- A return's price is taken by a logged roll from your own record (your record of gods and your goals). You are not told which item went.
- A thing that is the instrument of a power: in any other hand it is only the ordinary thing, and no one else gains the power by holding it. If it is destroyed, the power is gone until your next return, when the thing comes back with your body. If another holds it when your body breaks, it stays where it is.
- Anything a power does to another god, or to a thing in another god's keeping, is unruled until it happens.
- One weakness buys one step: one improvement elsewhere, or one notch on one power.

### Mending
Draft text:
Description: By touch, one living person or creature at a time, the god mends a wound, a sickness or a failing body toward health: bones knit, a festering wound clears, a fever breaks, a labor is eased. It takes as long as the harm needs, minutes for a cut, an hour or a night for a deep wound or a long illness. It mends what is alive and still near mending, and one whose breath stopped no longer ago than a held breath. It does not raise the dead, undo age, regrow a limb or mend the mind. What is mended can be hurt or sicken again.
Drawback: The god takes a share of what it mends into its own body. A mild harm costs it a cold and a stiffness for a day. A grave one costs it fever, cold in the bones and a tremor in the hands for days, longer the more it has mended in a season, and it cannot mend again while the fever is on it without the tremor settling in. And, heavier than the power needs and written as asked: after a grave mending a faint tremor stays in the hands until the next spring thaw.
Limits: Touch, skin or cloth over skin. It cannot mend itself or put the share on another. A poison in the blood is mended as a sickness. The god's own people: mended as anyone. Other gods: unruled. A mind: none. Use across a year: bounded by the fever and the tremor. A scarcity: the knowing of medicine may be short where it lands; the power mends and teaches no one. More than ordinary craft: a harm done by more than craft is unruled until it happens. Not applicable: light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, water, being watched.
Choices: the tremor until the thaw is heavier than this power needs. It is written as you ask; say if you would rather it were not.
The Keeper's reasons: the share taken is a full price. The Keeper adds nothing and takes nothing for the heavier part.

### Reading the Rot
Draft text:
Description: By touch, or by looking closely from within a few paces, the god knows what ails a person or creature: the kind of sickness, how far gone, where it sits, what is likely to follow. Standing at a spring, a well, a store of grain or a stretch of soil, it knows whether the water, grain or ground is fouled, and with what.
Drawback: It cannot read its own people. Whoever is of its people is closed to it: it can mend them and cannot know what ails them without asking. (Chosen by you from the pair.)
Limits: One body at a time, a breath or two. It tells what is wrong now and its likely course, never who or what brought it about. A wrongness that is not sickness, rot or poison reads only as nothing it knows. Not minds, intent or lies. Another god's body reads no better than a mortal's; unruled beyond that. "Its own people": those of the line, the court and the shrines settled on Night 3 and their children; a stranger who joins later counts once taken in. Use across a year: no limit on how often; it goes where the god is. A scarcity: none. Not applicable: falling, carrying, water, being watched.
Choices: none; you chose.
The Keeper's reasons: a reading power gives effects and never causes. The blind spot is a real price for a healer.

### Hasten the Rot
Draft text:
Description: By touch, the god hastens the decay of dead matter within arm's reach: a carcass, a corpse, a mound of grain, a plank, a rope, a heap of straw, up to about a cartload at a time, in moments what would take a season. It does nothing to living flesh, stone or metal, or to anything it is not touching.
Drawback: The hands that rot a thing smell of it for a day and a night, so that anyone near knows, and the god cannot mend in that time.
Limits: A tree or a living field is not dead matter; nor is a body in its first breaths of dying. A thing sealed from the air does not rot. A thing cured in salt rots no faster than it would. Being watched: it is seen, and smelled after. The god's own people's stores rot like any. More than ordinary craft: unruled. A mind: none. Not applicable: light and dark, distance, falling, water.
Choices, each in full, on how often:
(a) one use between one dawn and the next, a cartload, with the drawback as written;
(b) as often as the god will, and each use after the first in a day adds a day and a night to the smell and to the time it cannot mend.
The Keeper's reasons: without one of these a town's whole granary, or a bridge's timbers, could go in a night, which falls on a whole people at once. Salt is left its hold because the power is about rot, not about undoing what keeps rot off.

### Return from Ground
Draft text:
Description: When the god's body is broken, it returns whole after a season at the nearest ground of its kind: a marsh edge, a burial field, a midden or slow river-mud. What it carries comes back with it.
Drawback: One item of memory or habit at each return.
Limits: The standing scale's season rung. A body held, sealed in or shut out is not broken, and nothing returns until the body is broken (your draft counted being held, unheard or shut out as broken; see what holds on every card). If the nearest ground is where it was confined, the next nearest. The walk is added.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: the standing scale, and the one rule on held bodies for every god.
