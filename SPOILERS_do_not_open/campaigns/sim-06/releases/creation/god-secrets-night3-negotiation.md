# Negotiation: god-secrets, Night 3

## Round 1
### What was read and not priced
- You proposed no edge, so nothing is priced against a weakness and no weakness is owed.
- The habits and prides you wrote are taken as tempers: who your people are, able to fail. None is a guarantee.
- What you said of the land and its past is written as your wish or your people's belief. The world decides.
- Who each office holder is stands as who they are, never what they will do.
- Everything under Skills and culture is ordinary and free.

### Your landing: the helpers and the seat
Draft text: You come to a people that does not know you. The walled town is the town's, the council is the town's council, and the Clerk of the Tally, the Granary Warden, the Gate Warden, the Boat Master and the Crier serve the council under its own law. You wrote that the Clerk of the Tally keeps your place at the table when you are away: at landing there is no place of yours to keep. So the form is "designed, not yet serving": you land with no helpers in fact and no seat that is yours in fact. Until each office is won in play, nobody acts for you while you are away, your unplayed time runs on your standing conduct alone, and your people go their own way under their own council. That is the cost, the same for any god who lands so. It is not a refusal of a default, it bears on no edge, and it buys nothing.
Choices: (a) Keep the meeting as you wrote it, at the cost above. The offices stand as you described them, each under your people's own custom, and each becomes your helper when its holder hears you and takes your word, in play. (b) Say that your people already know you when you land: that you have been among them, or that they know you on sight for whose they are. Then the offices you described serve you from landing, your word is carried while you are away, and the meeting is rewritten so, in your words. Neither way is priced and neither buys anything.
The Keeper's reasons: an office can carry the word only of one its holder knows. What you wrote has your people holding their offices under their own law, or for one they have not met. The cost is said here plainly so that you choose knowing it.
Accept, refuse or counter, and give your reasons.

### The spears
Draft text: About six hundred who can carry a spear, of whom about a hundred are the standing watch under the Gate Warden, trained and fed from the common store. Ordinary, not priced.
The Keeper's reasons: a force that is kept as a body, paid or fed for it and drilled, is ordinary up to about one in fifty of a people, and an edge above that. A hundred in five thousand is at that line and not over it. The rest are a call-up.
### Their magic
Draft text: Five or six known by name in a town of this size, each working one small thing at her own cost, none every day. Fitted as you wrote it: ordinary in this world, and no edge. The cost each bears is the whole price. You cannot make another by telling; such a one comes as the world gives her.
The Keeper's reasons: how much of it there is comes from the world and is the same for every people.

Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the default.
