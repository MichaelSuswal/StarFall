# Negotiation: god-dreams, Night 2

## Round 1

Your self is read. Below, each thing you proposed is written as draft card text (Description, Drawback, Limits), with a choice wherever the price could go two ways, and the Keeper's reasons. Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the default.

### What holds on every card
These are how this table prices, the same on any card, and are not argued item by item:
- A body held is not broken. A god that is bound, caged, walled in or shut out stays so until it is freed or its body is broken; only a broken body returns.
- A return's price is taken by a logged roll from your own record (your record of gods and your goals). You are not told which item went.
- A thing that is the instrument of a power: in any other hand it is only the ordinary thing, and no one else gains the power by holding it. If it is destroyed, the power is gone until your next return, when the thing comes back with your body. If another holds it when your body breaks, it stays where it is.
- Anything a power does to another god, or to a thing in another god's keeping, is unruled until it happens.
- One weakness buys one step: one improvement elsewhere, or one notch on one power.

### Walk the Dream
Draft text:
Description: While one of her own people sleeps, the god may enter that sleeper's dream and walk in it: speak with the sleeper, hear them, comfort them, show them a place, and stay until they wake. One dream at a time. She may enter the last sleep of one who is dying, if they are asleep. She is a presence in the dream and cannot rewrite it, wake the sleeper, make them remember or compel them to anything. A sleeper may push her out by fear or refusal. What is said is remembered as a dream is, in pieces, and fades unless told.
Drawback: While she is in a dream her body stands where it was, dim, slow and nearly voiceless: it cannot walk more than a step, speak above a murmur, lift or carry, and is easy to overlook, move or hold. Harm that lands on it is felt and not resisted. If the body is broken she is thrown out of the dream and the break stands. The sleeper's fear in a nightmare reaches her as her own while she is there.
Limits: Her own people: anyone in a household that keeps her shrine, wherever they sleep. Distance: any, among those. Not those who keep no shrine of hers, and not the unsleeping. A sleeper under Keeping Sleep that night has no dream to enter. She learns only what the sleeper says or shows; the power shows her nothing else that may be in a dream, and what she meets there that is not the sleeper is unruled until it happens. A mind: it rules none. Use across a year: one dream at a time, her body thin for as long. A scarcity: none. More than ordinary craft: a sleep made or kept by more than craft is unruled until it happens. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water, being watched.
Choices: none; the price stands one way.
The Keeper's reasons: it reaches any distance, so it is priced by the body left thin and by one dream at a time. It tells her what a sleeper knows and no more.

### Keeping Sleep
Draft text:
Description: At dusk the god lights her lamp at a household shrine kept for her. That household sleeps the night through, unbroken: no waking fear, no night terror, and the rest is whole. It heals no wound and no illness. It holds no one against real danger: a broken door, a shout, a fire or a blow wakes them as it would anyone. The household has no dreams that night. Up to seven households at once, each lit separately and renewed each dusk.
Drawback: For each household kept, the fear of its night arrives in the god at dawn as her own, wherever she is, and stays until dusk of the following day. One or two, she is shaken. Three or more, she acts on first impulse, cannot hold a plan across an hour, and cannot enter a room that is shut or crowded. All seven, she cannot stand still and flees what frightens her if it comes near.
Limits: Only a household that keeps her shrine. One night per lighting; it does not stack. A household may refuse it by putting out the shrine flame. It takes only the fear of the night's sleep, never the fear of a present danger. Carrying: the lamp. Other gods' peoples: it does not reach them. Use across a year: seven households a night at most. A scarcity: safe sleep is a thing people may want badly; the power gives seven households a night and no more. A mind: it quiets sleep and compels nothing. Not applicable: falling, water, being watched.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: the fear is a price she would feel every day she uses it, and it rises with the use.

### Rising
Draft text:
Description: When the god's body is broken, she returns whole after a season at the nearest ground of her kind. What she carries comes back with her, the lamp too if it was on her.
Drawback: One item of memory or habit at each return.
Limits: The standing scale's season rung. The season runs from the breaking. A body held or shut away is not broken, and no season starts until the body is broken. The walk from the ground is added.
Choices: "ground of her kind" must be written. Each in full:
(a) the nearest house, anyone's, where a lamp is kept burning through the night;
(b) the nearest place where someone has died within the year and been mourned: a deathbed, a fresh grave.
Or name another, if it gives no advantage the nearest of these would not.
The Keeper's reasons: the standing scale. The ground must be one the Keeper can find on any day.

### The trade: the lamp as the instrument
Draft text (weakness): Keeping Sleep comes only through the god's lamp. If it is out of her hand and away from her person, by theft, trade, loss, force or her own free gift, the power is gone until she holds it again, and the shrine flames lit from it are only fire while it is away. In any other hand it is only a clay lamp. A copy does nothing. If it is destroyed, the power is gone until her next return.
What it buys: a keeper of a household shrine may light and tend that shrine's flame at dusk in her name, from a flame first lit from the lamp, and Keeping Sleep holds for that household when she is not there. It counts among the seven. The fear still reaches her at dawn.
Your question, answered: the lamp comes back with the body if it was on her when the body broke. If another held it then, it stays where it is.
Choices, each in full:
(a) as written above;
(b) a different weakness of your own naming in place of the lamp, priced by the same rule, for the same step;
(c) no trade: the lamp is an ordinary possession, any lamp she lights serves, and only her own hand lights a shrine.
The Keeper's reasons: one weakness, one step. The step asked is one step.
