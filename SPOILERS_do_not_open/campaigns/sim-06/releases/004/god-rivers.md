# Release: god-rivers, Year 4, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 4, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 5, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 5 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- You answered the small boy at the fire and the head herd keeper at the council. The Ferry Elder read the marks with you: the deep water moved again.
- The weir hands measured at the low water: the bed is shoaled behind every weir, more behind the older ones. The council opened a gap in the oldest weir for a season. What the water does there shows at next spring's flood.
- In summer you went down the larger river by barge with the two ferry keepers and the cord walker: five days down, 133.6 miles from the ford, through the hills and the country of many channels to where the river meets the sea in a shifting delta of reed and sand. The water turned salt on the fifth day. You met reed cutters at their staithes all the way down and a band of the boat people at the delta. No salt comes up that river. The reed cutters of the lower channels say the salt for the stone towns is landed at a harbor far to the west and goes up to the stone towns' south town by land.
- At the ford you answered the ford keeper.
- You stood in the larger river to the knee each dusk and each dawn. You felt no pull. No other like yourself stands in or on that river.
- The one listening, at the delta: within twenty miles, salt water and fresh meeting, the channels' ordinary life, boats of the boat people, nothing like yourself. Nothing came against your sleep. About twelve days home.
- In the tenth month you laid Warm hands on a child of the lower town. The fever came down and you took the chill.
- In autumn you told the council all of it.

## Asked of you and not answered
- Some of the weirs' people, to the Seat Warden, while you were on the water: "Does the star dim for her?" It was put again at the autumn sitting by the head of the oldest kin house: "Is the star yours?" Your decisions gave no words for it.

## What the Keeper ruled
- Your three readings of your card stand as you stated them.
- You asked to be told: you felt no pull.

## The sky
The star dimmed on seven nights of your year, more again than last year: in the second, third, fourth, eighth, tenth, eleventh and twelfth months. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people count twenty-one such nights since you came.

## Your people at the year's turn
About as last year. The council has a gap in its oldest weir and a ford it now knows the salt does not need.
