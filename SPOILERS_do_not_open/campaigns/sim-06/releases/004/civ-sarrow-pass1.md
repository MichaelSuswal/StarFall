## What your people meet, by month
- The weather. An ordinary year. The first snow came at its usual time and lay as it does. The passes opened at the thaw in the ordinary way.
- The stores. What was laid up is what your own state gives, with the raised toll taken since last thaw. Whether it lasts to the thaw this winter, at the share your people set, is an attempt for the Keeper: write what was counted, by whom, how it was shared, whether the seed was set apart under seal and kept, whether grain came from the far houses, and write the year both ways, briefly.
- The grain asked of the far houses as a loan. Whether the riders go, what each house answers and what it sends is an attempt for the Keeper: write who rides, when, what is said at each door, and what your record gives each house to hold.
- The crossings. In autumn the trains crossed as last autumn. From the thaw, in the seventh month, fewer trains came to the far toll houses than last spring, by about a third, and those of the river kingdom paid the raised toll with open complaint. Settled: in the summer, months 10 to 12, no train of the river kingdom came over the road at all. Trains of other peoples came as before. What the watchmen, the Marshal and the hall say of it is yours. What the three at the table do about the toll when they sit with the cords after the first trains, as your god's decisions give, is yours for the mortals and your god's for its own word.
- The word for the river king. It is in your god's decisions, under Messages. Whether the older watcher of the ford finds a train master leaving for the river kingdom this autumn who will take it for a waived toll is yours to write up to the moment it leaves the ford; trains leave the ford for the river kingdom in autumn as every year. What becomes of it on the road and at the chancery is the Keeper's.
- No envoy of the river king came this year.
- The farthest toll house. From the winter city to the far end of the road is 68.2 miles on foot, 5 days. What your god's own marking of that door tells it is the Keeper's; write only what the mortals there saw it do and said. What anyone sees at the two farthest houses is what was there before: new roofs, good horses and full smokehouses.
- The high grass. The blue pass flower did not come up again. If herders set a cairn and cut a notch at the line of the grass, the line stands this year where the oldest say it stood last year.
- No stranger out of the ordinary came by the road this year.

## When your god is away
Your people act in your god's name only where it said so, or plainly took it for granted. Otherwise they have no answer from it, and may not act as if they had one; someone among them may take it on themselves, and that is their act, not the god's. A question put to your god while it is away meets silence and stays pending, unless its standing words answer it. Your people know only that they were not answered.

## What is yours to decide, and what is not
Yours: everything your people do, think, say and feel, every mortal among them, how each office holder meets the god and whether each takes its word, their work, stores, births and deaths, quarrels and stories. Mortals have their own will. They do not know the god's plans; they meet what it does, as it does it.
Not yours:
- An outcome that is uncertain and contested (a fight, a search, a persuasion of someone outside your people, a boat in a swell, a craft tried beyond what your people know). Do not settle it. Write it in your report as an attempt: who tries what, when, what success and failure would each mean, and every circumstance from your record that bears on it. The Keeper gives the odds and rolls. Write the rest of your year both ways where it turns on that outcome, briefly.
- What a god's power does or shows. Say what the god is seen to do and what your people make of it. Where your year turns on what a power did, say so in your report and the Keeper will settle it.
- Anything about another people or about land beyond what your record gives. Give no distance or travel time that is not in your record or in this file; say where a party set out for and by what way, and the Keeper prices the trip.
- The name of your people or their home, and the name of any office holder your record leaves unnamed: these stay unnamed unless a moment in your year has a mortal say a name aloud, and then you write who said it, when and to whom.
