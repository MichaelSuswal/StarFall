Read by your people's agent only. Never by the DM.

Each fact is known to the mortals named and to no one else. You are told no cause and reason toward none.

## What they know that your god does not
- The Tally-keeper: in the summer before the old Weightward's last winter a clerk from the kingdom upriver called on him and asked to see the old weights; he showed six and said the seventh, the largest, was out for cleaning; she stood witness and holds the kept half. Asked plainly whether she knows anything of the seventh weight, or for that year's witnessings, she answers plainly. She does not volunteer. A search for more stops with her: she knows no more than that.
- The Stallreeve: a boatman lent the old man a punt on a night of the spring flood, his last spring, and got it back before light with silt in the bottom and a wet rope. Asked plainly and away from the yard, he tells it. He gives no reading of it. A search for more stops with the boatman, who knows what he lent and what came back.
- The Assay-master, the Stallreeve and the head of clay: the coat and boots in the loft, stiff with dried river silt to above the knee. The Assay-master has told the Weightward this, and that alone.
- The eleven: all of the above, since the winter sitting of Year 2, behind the shut door. They had the hall, the house, the landings and the channel looked in, and gave the looking up. Where the weight is, nobody in the town knows.
- The eleven, on the second stick: the things on it came before them as the public release gives, and the votes fell as it gives. Why each of them voted as they did is yours, from what each knows. Those who know of the clerk may weigh that when the empty seat is put to them; they still took it. What they said to one another of it is said behind the shut door and stays in your sealed files.
- The door-stone of the fourth month: its owner knows where it has lain and for how long, and that is yours to set. It is a door-stone. No one in the town knows what the seventh weighed.
- Names, the Keeper's to give, none yet said in the Weightward's hearing: the Stallreeve, Tam Wherry; the Tally-keeper, Annis Furrow; the Assay-master, Osric Marl; the ship owner of the open water, Davin Sculler; the Tally-keeper's apprentice, Wat; the fish seller of the fifth day, Hob Sedge. A name goes in a public file only when a mortal says it aloud in the Weightward's hearing, and then you say who, when and to whom. The Assay-master keeps his word that he will not say his until the Weightward has one of its own to put in the other pan.
- The ship owner of the open water knows the house of the southern coaster's master by its name. He has not said so to the Weightward, who did not make him answer.
