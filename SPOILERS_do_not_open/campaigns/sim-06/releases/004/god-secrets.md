# Release: god-secrets, Year 4, the year resolved

What you saw, heard, were told and felt in Year 4, and what your people report. You read this at the opening of Year 5, in place of a last year's release. It is your go for nothing by itself; your Year 5 release names your files.

## What you did and what came of it
- The harvest was counted aloud at the door from the first sack, the far run and the near bins alike, and the store squared. The closing count was cried at market as the Warden's.
- In the winter you asked the Clerk of the Tally whether he wished to hear what the old stick says. He did. You gave him the figures. He has told no one.
- The channel at the river gate was shallower again a third spring. The council's own rule fired, and the bar was dredged in the tenth and eleventh months.
- In summer you went up the river with the Boat Master's cousin's crew from the twelfth day of the tenth month, four days to the landing at the river's head, and on alone by the drovers' track, 17.3 miles to the plain's edge and then out onto the plain with drovers at their pace, about twelve days out and twelve back. You saw the plain: open grass, herds, drove roads, wells. You did not come within many days of the pass. The drovers you met tell the same tale of the pass herders' Guest that you had last year, and no one among them had stood nearer.
- The star dimmed twice while you were on the plain. You felt no weight in the air.
- You were back in the town on the twentieth day of the eleventh month, before the day you named to the Clerk.

## Asked of you and not answered
- Nothing new was put to you and left unanswered.

## What the Keeper ruled
- Saying what you once read by Tally-Reader is speech and no new use of it.

## The sky
The star dimmed on seven nights of your year, more again than last year: in the second, third, fourth, eighth, tenth, eleventh and twelfth months. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Your people count twenty-one such nights since you came.

## Your people at the year's turn
About as last year. The count at the door has been made the same way twice.
