# Report to the Keeper: the Longwalk, Year 1

## How the god was met

Spring, month 1, the dusk of the fourth day of the year, at the rear camp. The dogs at the rear fell silent together and turned back toward the wood. The herd beasts there lifted their heads. A girl of about twelve minding calves ran for Tessaly, the Houndwarden. Tessaly came out beyond the last fire with two dogs, who would not go to the end of their cords and sat. She saw the god standing low and still at the edge of the light. A hunter of the rear raised a spear; Tessaly told him once to put it down and he did. The god spoke when she spoke, in a low even voice, and told her it was not of the band, that it was one of the keepers they speak of, and that it would keep the herds if the band wished it. She asked what it wanted and whether it would harm the herds. It said nothing, and no. She told it to stay at the edge of the light and said she would carry its word to the Speaker. She did not take its word as an order. She told only the two hunters who saw, to hold their tongues.

Some days later, month 1, the Speaker Orrin Haddock came back along the line over two days and met the god at dusk with Tessaly standing by. He heard it. He said a keeper that came to the edge of the fire would be heard at council like any elder and no more. He did not take its word as an order. He told Tessaly the night watch was hers to say.

Month 1 onward: Tessaly let the god keep the night watch at the margin of the rear camp on her own word as Houndwarden. That is the whole of what any office holder took of it, and it is not the taking of the god's word as a helper.

Month 6, at the stopped camp on the river, the council sat at dusk and asked the god, which stood at the edge of the firelight and would not sit in the ring. The Speaker asked what it was and was told plainly. The Marchwarden asked whether it knew the song of the ford below the camp, and it said it had learned it in the nights. The council asked whether the bold beasts near the tents were its doing, and it said where it had spoken with a beast the boldness might be its doing, and asked Tessaly to keep a cord count. The eldest head of the three clans that want to winter apart said a keeper that asks nothing will want something later. No vote. The council neither told it to go nor to stay.

Autumn: the council asked it, after a short quarrel, to stand with the hunters and tell Tessaly where the game lay, and it did, and asked the hunt to keep its season. Winter: it kept the margin of the main winter camp.

The Saltkeeper and the Marchwarden have each heard the god speak at council and neither has taken its word. The Runners carried nothing for it.

## What was done in the god's name

Nothing. No office holder acted for the god. The helpers are not serving, as the record says. The night watch and the cord count are Tessaly's own arrangements with a keeper at the margin, not done in its name.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper

1. The wolves of the rear herds in spring, four nights, month 1 to 3. Attempt: the god keeps the night watch at the margin of the rear camp with Tessaly's dogs. Success would mean the wolves are turned from the herds and little or nothing is lost, and the rear camp says the herds sleep better; failure would mean calves are lost as in any spring and the rear camp says the keeper is no better than a dog. Circumstances from my record: the god stands at the margin and asked for no share; a bold wild beast near the tents is something the god's own words say may be its doing; the dogs sat and would not approach it. I wrote the year both ways in `years/001.md`.
2. The wolves of the east flank in summer, two successive nights, month 6. Attempt: a pack takes calves from the east flank; Tessaly asks the god, and the keeper keeps the flank at night with the dogs and the Houndwarden's hunters. Success: the pack is turned off and takes no more than a calf or none. Failure: two calves lost, a third knot on the cord, and the council says it saw nothing for its trouble. Circumstances: the herds are fattening on the river grass, wolves follow the calving, the cord of bold beasts is read at council in the sixth month. The cord has a knot left open for this.
3. The trade party to the stone towns, month 5, set out by the band's usual way with beasts, hides and felt. In the plain case it returned before the going up with about two thirds of the salt the band wanted, at a higher price, and with some iron. Whether the way was clear, and whether the party was met or hindered, is for the Keeper; I wrote only the plain case.

## What turns on a power

1. The dogs fell silent and turned to face the god, and the herd beasts lifted their heads, on the dusk of the fourth day of the year. I wrote only what Tessaly saw. Whether this is the god's doing or what the beasts do for any keeper is the Keeper's to say.
2. The god reads, I am told, only what its Listening reads. What my people's herders read by it when a few stand apart from the herd, and whether a single herder with a dog reads as a dog alone, is the god's own request for a ruling and I cannot settle it for them.
3. The boldness of beasts near the tents. The cord holds a fox that took a kid by day and a boar that stood against a hunter, and a third knot left open for the wolves. Whether any of these are the god's doing is for the Keeper.
4. The three dimmed nights of the star, two in month 1 and one in month 5. My people say they dim for the keeper. I wrote only that they saw them and what they said.
5. The Still One reading of the herd on the first dimmed night: that it was listening to something and was not afraid. This is her ordinary gift and I did not decide what the herd was listening to.

## What touches anyone else

- One trade party of the band set out in month 5 for the stone towns of the Hask Ridge [r20] with beasts, hides and felt, by the way the band has always gone, and returned before the going up with salt and iron at the holds' price, dearer than last year. No traveler came to the band. No one was sent anywhere else. The cutters of the holds were in the winter woods farther in than last year and the band took note of it.
- The Saltkeeper and the Marchwarden said before the council that the holds' cutting is thinning the winter woods; that is a worry carried to the council, not an act.
- The three clans that want to winter apart stayed apart in the winter woods, farther than last year, after a quarrel over fuel wood in month 11 that ended in a boy of the main band being cut across the forearm by a young man of the three clans. The Three at the Fire judged the knife man to owe a share of meat to the boy's hearth for three generations and to walk behind the main camp unfed from the common pot for the rest of the winter. The head of the three clans is angry. No violence beyond the one cut.

## Asked of the god

- Tessaly, month 1, the first dusk: "What do you want?" The god answered, in front of her, that it asked nothing. "Will you harm the herds?" It answered that it would not.
- The Speaker, month 1: "What are you?" It answered that it was not of the band and had come to the margin of the fire.
- The council, month 6, by the Speaker: "What are you?" Answered in front of them: one of the keepers they speak of, come to lead as it was able, to be heard at council and no more.
- The Marchwarden, month 6: "Do you know the song of the ford below the camp?" Answered: it had learned it in the nights.
- The council, month 6: asked whether the wild beasts grown bold near the tents were its doing. Answered: where it had spoken with a beast the boldness might be its doing; it asked Tessaly to keep a cord count of every bold beast, where, and what it took, and said it would answer for the count.
- The eldest head of the three clans that want to winter apart, month 6, not to the god but before it: that a keeper that asks nothing will want something later. Not answered by the god.
- The council, autumn: whether it would stand with the hunters and tell the Houndwarden where the game lay. Answered: it would, and asked that the hunt keep its season.

## Names said aloud

None new. Tessaly and Orrin Haddock are names already in my record. No mortal said a name for the people, for their home or camping grounds, or for an office holder my record leaves unnamed.
