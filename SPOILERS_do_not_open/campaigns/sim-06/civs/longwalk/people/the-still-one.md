# The Still One

Of the Longwalk, born to one of its clans, which calls her by that name. An old woman, past seventy, small, slow among her own, seldom spoken to. By her small gift, at a cost she pays, she can sit by a herd at dusk and tell its mood and its trouble. On the day after, she does not eat, is slow for some days, and the young are wary of her. She trains no one and the gift ends with her.

History fixed in Year 1: on the first of the two dimmed nights of the first month she sat by her clan's cattle and said afterward only that the herd was listening to something and was not afraid. She has not met the keeper to speak with. She watches it from the edge of the firelight on some nights, and has said to one granddaughter that a keeper at the margin is not the thing she feared when she was a girl. She has told no one what she thinks it is.
