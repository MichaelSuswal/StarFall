# The Saltkeeper

Of the Longwalk. Keeps the salt and the dried meat of the band by cord, counts the goods of the trade party out and in, and sits in the council. Their name is not in my record and none has been said aloud. Their years and kin are not in my record. Careful with the cord, slow to say a price is fair.

History fixed in Years 1 and 2: told the Speaker in the first spring that the cord would run out before midsummer; in Year 2 kept back from the trade party every beast of the two clans that had coughed and every beast thin from the dry month, and with the Houndwarden said that the next party would take no cattle into the towns' yards. Has heard the keeper speak at council and has not taken its word.

Year 3 (a dated line): sent the trade party in the fifth month with goats, sheep, hides and felt and no cattle; it came home with about half the salt the band wanted, at no better price, and with greyer and softer iron. Had meant to keep the party's beasts apart a month on their return and said so when the keeper's asking was carried to them, and did. Bought no beast. Has heard it said in the band that the sickness is in the iron and has not said it is not.

Year 4 (a dated line): sent the trade party in the fifth month with goats, sheep, hides, felt and pack goats and no cattle, and a cord that said salt first and iron only for the edges the band could not do without. It came home in the ninth month with about half the salt wanted at no better price and iron in about two thirds the pieces, greyer and softer. Kept the party's pack goats and sheep apart six weeks, which was already meant, said so when the keeper's asking was carried to it, and asked for the keeper's reading of them at the end as one asks a dog. Bought no beast.

Year 5 (a dated line): sent the trade party in the fifth month with goats, sheep, hides, felt and pack goats and no cattle, and a cord that said salt first and iron only for the edges. It came home in the ninth month with about half the salt wanted, dear, and iron in few pieces, grey and soft. Refused a clan head's asking to send three sound bulls into the yards where the towns' oxen coughed. Kept the party's pack goats and sheep apart six weeks, which was already meant, said so when the keeper's asking was carried to it, and asked for the keeper's reading of them at the end as one asks a dog. Had the lean meat smoked and the fat salted to spare the salt. Bought no beast.
