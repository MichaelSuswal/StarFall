# What the Longwalk say, end of Year 2

Their own telling. Where they explain a cause, they are wrong about something that matters.

About the keeper at the margin
- They say a keeper that asks once and asks small is the worse kind, since a small asking is the first. The eldest head's people say this. The herders and the dog handlers say the opposite: that a keeper that says what it wants and then wants no more is the only kind worth having at the edge.
- They say the keeper asked its thing before the fourth dimming and so the saying was kept, and that the star dimmed after only to be sure it was heard. This is wrong about the order of the star and the asking, and it matters because it ties the star to the keeper's wants, so that a dimmed night is taken for a sign that the keeper wants something again.
- They say it asked to be told when its doing costs the band, and the dog handlers now bring Tessaly small costs to carry to it, and she carries them. They say, wrongly, that a keeper that is told its costs will pay them. Nothing the keeper has said says it will.
- They say the keeper reads a herd as a dog does, by what the herd does, and not as the Still One does, by what it feels. This is the dog handlers' saying, and they are wrong about it or right about it for reasons they do not have.
- They say it does not eat, does not sleep and does not lie down, and that it stood a month at the apart clans' fire and did nothing. The apart clans say this is the best thing a keeper can do.
- They say the keeper "knew the water". The herds were watered by turns and moved by night in the dry month, and the herders say the keeper told Tessaly which herds were afraid. They say, wrongly, that the keeper brought the rain back, because it stood at the margin for the whole of the dry month and the rain came in the month after. Some of the old say that the keeper asked the storms for it, and that this is what a keeper is. Tessaly says it was not asked. The old say that is why it came.

About the star
- Eight nights since the keeper came, three the first year and five the second. They say the star dims oftener since the keeper came, and will go on dimming oftener until the keeper does what it came to do. Nobody can say what that is. The Marchwarden notches the nights and says it is five against three and that the old have no word for it. The apart clans say the star dims when the band grows too large to walk as one. They are wrong about what it is, and it matters, because they have begun to count the band's numbers by the nights.
- They say the keeper does not say it is not its own, and that a keeper which cannot say whose the dark is, is either honest or hiding. Both are said.

About their own rulers
- Of the Speaker: that Orrin Haddock gave the dawn water to the clans that came last and so held the band by its middle, and that his chest fever is his age and not the keeper's.
- Of the Houndwarden: that Tessaly will not ask the keeper for rain, and that she is right, or that she is proud.
- Of the Marchwarden: that he has heard the keeper at his own fire and learned from it that the cutters have come past the first stand of the winter woods, which the songs say never happened, and that he has said it in council.
- Of the Still One: that she sat the three nights at the water and paid for it, and the young are wary of her again.

About their neighbors
- The miners of the Hask Ridge [r20] cut the winter woods, sell salt and iron dear, and this year their oxen cough. The band says the cough came to the clans' cattle from the miners, with the iron or with the beasts the holds sold, and that the miners have given the band a plague as well as a price. They are wrong about the order of it, since the clans' cattle coughed first, and it matters, because next year's trade party takes no cattle to the towns' yards and the salt will be less.
- The song-thieves are not spoken of this year.
- The three clans that want to winter apart say the keeper kept their margin well and that this proves it is a keeper of margins and not of the band. They have begun to say the band is too big for one road, and that the keeper will keep each part's margin if the band is parted. This is the thin edge of the quarrel to come.

About the strange things of the year
- The dry month. They say the grass is thin because the keeper stood at the edge of the grass. They say the rain came back because of what the Houndwarden did not do. These are said by those who lost children.
- The flux. They say it came from the fouled pools. They are right.
- The cord of bold beasts is read as a small thing, and people now say that the beasts were bold at the water because of the dry and not because of the keeper, because the keeper says it spoke with none. Some hold that the keeper says so because it has to.
