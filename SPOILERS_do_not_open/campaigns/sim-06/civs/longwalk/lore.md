# What the Longwalk say, end of Year 3

Their own telling. Where they explain a cause, they are wrong about something that matters.

About the keeper at the margin
- They say the council gave the keeper its first thing at the first storm, and that a keeper that is given a first thing will be given a second. The eldest head's people say this and say they were right to refuse it his camp. They are wrong that the council gave the keeper anything: it gave the Houndwarden leave to send her hunters at dusk, and the keeper has had nothing. It matters, because they have begun to count the council's leaves as the keeper's winnings.
- They say the keeper's word turned the wolves, or, if some calves were lost, that it was the hunters' late going and not the word. The herders say the first. The old hunters say the dogs knew the wolves before the keeper said, and that the keeper only said it aloud. The hunters are wrong about which came first, since the dogs turn to the keeper and not to the wolves, and it matters because the dog handlers are beginning to trust their dogs less than the keeper.
- They say the keeper that told of the pool below the second ford "knew the water". The Marchwarden says it knew a herd, and the camp moved because of a verse and a herd, and that a child is worth a night. The mothers of the three who died say the keeper should have said sooner. They are wrong that the keeper could have known sooner, and it matters, because they say a keeper that is blamed for a thing it could not know will stop saying what it does.
- They say the keeper "knows what ails a beast and will not say". The two clans say this, having lost a sixth of their cattle and been told nothing. They are wrong, since it reads a herd as hurt and not why, and it matters because they have begun to ask the Houndwarden to ask it for more than it has.
- They say it did not come to the three clans' fire this year because it was sent away. It was not sent. The eldest head refused it.
- They say that it asked the Marchwarden what it should do, and he asked it what the council should do, and it said that was the council's. The old say this is what a keeper is: that it says what it reads and will not say what to do. The herders say it is a coward's way. Both are said.

About the star
- Fourteen nights since the keeper came: three, five, six. They say the star dims oftener each year since the keeper came, and will dim oftener each year until the keeper does what it came to do. The Marchwarden notches the nights and says there is a pattern in the numbers and that he cannot read it.
- The apart clans say the star dims when the band grows too large to walk as one, and count heads against the nights. They are wrong, and it matters, because they have begun to say that the band's heads were counted too many in the year the star dimmed twice in a month, and a few say the thing to do is to part.
- They say the star did not dim in the winter because the keeper stood at the margin of the winter camp all that winter. The apart clans say it did not dim because the clans were wintered apart and the band was small. Both are wrong about why.

About their own rulers
- Of the Speaker: that Orrin Haddock took no vote on the keeper's leave and let it stand, and held the band by his middle again; that his second chest fever was not the keeper's doing but his age, and that the band will soon be asked who comes after. The clans say a name of an office and not a person, and some say of the Marchwarden, some of the Houndwarden, and the Houndwarden has said she has dogs enough.
- Of the Houndwarden: that Tessaly took the keeper's word in the spring and she is a dog handler with a keeper at her ear. She says she took the council's leave and not the keeper's word. She is right and the band does not listen.
- Of the Marchwarden: that the verse for the third stand is an old lie now, and that he has said so in council. He says the verse should be sung differently, and some of the old say that a verse sung differently is not the verse.
- Of the eldest head of the three clans: that he refused the keeper his margin and is proud, or wise. His clans' young herders say it was a pity.
- Of the Saltkeeper: that the Saltkeeper had meant to keep the party's beasts apart before the keeper asked and said so. They say a keeper that is told what it meant is not contradicted.
- Of the Still One: that she sat two nights at the two clans' herds, did not eat, and said the beasts were tired in the chest. The two clans say it was the only true thing said of the cough all winter.

About their neighbors
- The miners of the Hask Ridge [r20] cut the winter woods a little farther in, sell salt and iron dear, and send iron greyer and softer than before. The band says the sickness of the stone towns' oxen is in the iron, and that the iron is soft because the sickness is in it. They are wrong: the smiths of the stone towns say it is their ore. It matters, because the band is now saying the party should buy salt and no iron, and the band's tools will then run short.
- The band says the cough came to their cattle from the stone towns and that the party's word about the first stands was a prayer to a people who do not pray. They are wrong that it was a prayer, and it matters, because the Speaker had the party carry the word as the band's own claim and not as a plea, and the band now says it was a begging.
- The song-thieves are not spoken of this year.
- The three clans that want to winter apart say the keeper kept their margin well, but that a keeper which keeps the margin of the main camp by the council's leave is no keeper of theirs. They say the band is too big for one road. This is the thin edge of the quarrel to come.

About the strange things of the year
- The pool below the second ford. They say the pool went bad because the herds fouled it in a wet year, and that the verse of the ford is wrong and the Marchwarden should sing it differently. The Marchwarden says the verse is right.
- The flux. They say it came from the pool. They are right.
- The cord of bold beasts is read as a small thing, three knots, none of them the keeper's, and people say the keeper is a keeper of herds and not of beasts at all.
