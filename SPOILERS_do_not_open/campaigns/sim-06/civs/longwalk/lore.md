# What the Longwalk say, end of Year 4

Their own telling. Where they explain a cause, they are wrong about something that matters.

About the keeper at the margin
- They say the council gave the keeper its first thing three springs ago and its second this spring, and that a keeper that is given a thing twice has been given a custom. The eldest head's people say this, and the eldest head said it in the ring. They are wrong that the council gave the keeper anything: it gave the Houndwarden leave to send her hunters at dusk, twice, and the keeper has had nothing. It matters, because the band counts the council's leaves as the keeper's winnings and the habit is set.
- They say the keeper's word turned the wolves again, or that the pens by the fires did, or that the dogs did. The herders say the first, the old herders say the pens, and the old hunters say the dogs. The old herders are right that the pens were wanted, and wrong about what that shows, since the wolves were turned before they came to the pens. It matters, because the herders are beginning to say the calving needs a keeper's reading to be safe, and the dog handlers to say a dog does not know a wolf till the keeper says.
- They say the keeper "cried at a herd at the first dusk" at the pool below the third camp, and the camp did not move, and nothing came of it. Some say it was right to cry and the herd was hurt by the pool and eased because the Marchwarden kept it off. Some say the keeper cries at a cough. Both are said, and both are wrong that they know the herd was hurt by the water, since the keeper said it did not know and the herd was resting the next dusk. It matters, because the mothers of last year's three have begun to say a keeper that speaks early will be believed less for it, and the band is beginning to say nothing at all of what it does not understand.
- They say the keeper "cannot say what the cough is" and so it is a small keeper. The two clans say it. They also say it told them the cough came hard to them because they wintered apart. It did not say the cough came because they wintered apart; it said it came hard to the clans that wintered apart, and that no other herd took it. It matters, because some of the two clans have begun to say they should winter among the herds and take the cough to everyone, and the old herders cannot persuade them.
- They say the keeper is "a dog that points at a flock", the herder's saying. The herder means it well. The band has taken it up as a thing to say of anything that tells a danger and not a place.
- They say the keeper went along the timber on the nights it was not at the margin, and the star dimmed one of those nights, and that it went to see to the star. The Marchwarden says the keeper told him of the cutters, and that is where it went. They are wrong, and it matters, because the apart clans have begun to say the keeper is gone from the camp when the star dims.
- They say a young herder of the apart clans asked the keeper whether the star dimmed for the band's numbers, and the keeper did not answer, and when it did, it did not say no. His clan says that is a yes. They are wrong that it was not an answer: it was asked while it was away, and when it was asked again it said what it said. It matters, because the apart clans' talk of parting at the next going down now has a keeper's silence under it.

About the star
- Twenty one nights since the keeper came: three, five, six, seven. The Marchwarden notches them and says the numbers climb by one or two and not by a rule he can find. The band says they will climb till the keeper does what it came to do.
- The apart clans say the star dims when the band grows too large to walk as one, and count heads. The band was five thousand and a hundred and more this spring and they say it was four thousand and a few hundred when it dimmed less. They are wrong about the count of the first years, and it matters because the quarrel rests on it.
- The band says the star dimmed in the winter this year though the keeper stood at the margin, and that the saying that the keeper's margin held it in the winter was wrong. They are right that the saying was wrong. They say instead that the star dims more when the keeper is nearer, which is not what the nights show.

About their own rulers
- Of the Speaker: that Orrin Haddock took no vote on the leave for the wolves the second time and held the band by his middle, and that he is slower and sleeps through part of a long ring. The clans say a name of an office and not a person; some say the Marchwarden, some the Houndwarden, and the Houndwarden says she has dogs enough.
- Of the Houndwarden: that Tessaly told the keeper to its face that the council had not taken its word, and that she is a dog handler who has put the keeper in its place. She says she told it so because she did not wish it to believe a thing the band did not believe. She is right, and the band hears it as a snub, and the herders are cooler to her for it.
- Of the Marchwarden: that he would sing the third stand differently and has not, that he kept the herd off the pool one night and would not move the camp, and that he was cautious and also wise. He says he weighs a night's herd against a child. Some of the old say a verse sung differently is not the verse.
- Of the eldest head of the three clans: that he said a thing given twice is a custom, and that he may be right. His clans' young herders say he is old and the band is not.
- Of the Saltkeeper: that the Saltkeeper had meant six weeks before the keeper asked and said so, and asked for the keeper's reading all the same. They say a keeper that is told what it meant is not contradicted, and that the Saltkeeper has begun to lean on the keeper's reading. The Saltkeeper says it was one more dog.
- Of the Still One: that she sat one night by the herds of the two clans and said the herd was tired in the chest still and less. The two clans say it is the second true thing said of the cough.

About their neighbors
- The miners of the Hask Ridge [r20] cut the winter woods a little farther in again, sell salt and iron dear, and send iron greyer and softer than before. The band says the iron is soft because the sickness of the towns' oxen is in it, and that the party should buy salt and iron only for the edges it cannot do without. They are wrong: the smiths of the stone towns say it is their ore. It matters, because the band's tools are running short and the smiths mend what they can.
- The band says the council sent no word this year because the last word was a begging, and that a begging is not said twice. They are wrong that it was a begging; it was carried as a claim. It matters, because the band believes the holds have heard its asking and refused it, and the holds have said nothing.
- The band says the cough came to its cattle from the stone towns and that the towns' oxen cough still. They are right that the oxen cough. They have not shown the cough came from them.
- The song-thieves are not spoken of this year.
- The three clans that want to winter apart say the band is too big for one road, and some say it should part at the next going down. This is the quarrel to come.

About the strange things of the year
- The pool below the third camp. They say the pool was good, or was hurt and the Marchwarden cured it by keeping the herd off. Neither is known.
- The three children of last year's flux. The mothers say they would sooner a keeper that speaks first than one that speaks sure. The Marchwarden says the same, in the verse of a ford.
- The cord of bold beasts is read as a small thing, two knots, none of them the keeper's. People say the keeper is a keeper of herds and not of beasts at all, and are surer of it than last year.
