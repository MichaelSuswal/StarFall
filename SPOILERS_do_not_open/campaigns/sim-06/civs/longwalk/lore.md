# What the Longwalk say, end of Year 5

Their own telling. Where they explain a cause, they are wrong about something that matters.

About the keeper at the margin
- They say the council has now given the keeper its third thing, the leave for the wolves, and that a thing given three times is a law. The eldest head's people say this, and the eldest head said it in the ring at the river camp. They are wrong that the council gave the keeper anything: it gave the Houndwarden leave to send her hunters at dusk, three times, and the keeper has had nothing. It matters, because the band counts the council's leaves as the keeper's winnings and the habit is now set past mending.
- They say the keeper's word turned the wolves, or the pens by the fires, or the dogs, or that the keeper's word cost thirty calves and the leave is worth no more than waiting. The herders who lost the most say the last. The old herders say the pens. The old hunters say the dogs. The Houndwarden says only that the first night's count was six and the whole was thirty, and that she told the keeper so. They are wrong that any of it is known. It matters, because the band has begun to say the calving is a gamble the keeper's reading does not change, and some herders, having heard six said and then thirty, say a count given early is worth nothing.
- They say the keeper "took back" what it had said of the star when it said at the first storm that it had not said yes. The apart clans say it. They are wrong: it said nothing of yes before, and the yes was their own reading of its silence. It matters, because a part of the apart clans now feels cheated by a keeper that did nothing but say what it said, and a part is relieved, and the two parts do not agree on what they were promised.
- They say "a body keeps one margin and not two" means that if the clans part the keeper keeps the main camp and not them, that the apart clans are the part left unkept. The eldest head said so. The old of the main band say it meant only that it keeps the main camp's margin, as it has said. The keeper said it was not by its choosing. It matters, because the eldest head hears a threat and the main band hears a plain thing, and each says the other has heard it wrong.
- They say the keeper "cannot say what the cough is" and so it is a small keeper, and that it points at a flock and does not name a beast. The herder's saying has become the two clans' rule: a clan with a dog that points at a flock goes and looks. They are right that it does not name a beast. They are wrong that this makes it small, since the herder found his coughing beasts sooner by going where it pointed. It matters, because the clan has learned to look without it, and the band says the keeper is only a dog and a dog is not asked.
- They say the keeper "did not blame the two clans for wintering apart", and the old woman of the two clans says it said the same thing it said before. They are right. They say, the young of the two clans, that the cough chose them. They are wrong that it chose; no one knows why it came to them and to no other herd. It matters, because some of the two clans still think they should winter among the herds and take the cough to everyone, and say the keeper's silence on why is a leave.
- They say the keeper was along the timber the night the star dimmed in the seventh month, and that the star dims when the keeper is gone. The apart clans say it. The main band says the keeper stood at the margin on two of the three. Tessaly says she cannot say of the third. They are wrong to make a rule of one night and wrong to make none of the other two. It matters, because the quarrel of last year, that the keeper is gone when the star dims, has a night under it again.

About the star
- Twenty four nights since the keeper came: three, five, six, seven, three. The Marchwarden notches them and says the numbers fell as they climbed, and he can find no rule in either.
- The band says the star dimmed less this year because the keeper told the ring at the first storm what it had not said, and that the star had waited to be answered. They are wrong about the order of it: the star dimmed twice in the second month, in the month after the ring and not before. It matters, because the band has begun to say a keeper's word moves the star, and will ask it for the star the next time it dims.
- The apart clans say the star dims when the band grows too large to walk as one, and the band was five thousand and two hundred this year and the star dimmed three times, and their count is broken. Some say the star dims when the apart clans speak of parting, and that it eased because they did. Some say the band was never too many. The eldest head says nothing is settled. They are wrong that any count settles it, and wrong about the count of the first years, as they were last year, and it matters because the quarrel rests on it.
- The band says the star did not dim in the winter, as in the first winter and the third, and the keeper stood at the margin. It says the star dims less when the keeper stands, which is not what the winters show.

About their own rulers
- Of the Speaker: that Orrin Haddock took no vote on the leave a third time and held the band by his middle, and that he slept through the closing of a long ring in winter and the Marchwarden spoke it for him. The clans say a name of an office and not a person; some say the Marchwarden, some the Houndwarden, and the Houndwarden says she has dogs enough. They are wrong that the Marchwarden wishes it: he said only that an old man closes for an old man. It matters, because the clans will have a name for the Speaker's place before the Speaker has one.
- Of the Houndwarden: that Tessaly stood in the ring and said the count had been thirty and not six, and that she is a dog handler who tells the truth against herself and so is to be believed, or that she gave the ring a bad count at the river camp and was brought to it by the herders. She says she gave the first night's count when it was the first night's count. She is right, and the band says both.
- Of the Marchwarden: that he has said the verse of the third stand is an old lie still and not a worse one, since the cutters came no farther this year. He says he weighs a night's herd against a child, and that he notched three nights where he notched seven. Some of the old say a verse sung as it was when the axe was at the first stand is not a verse for the third.
- Of the eldest head of the three clans: that he said a thing given three times is a law, and that he said the band would be counted again at the next going down, and that he will speak of parting in the ring if the star dims as before and will not ask the keeper's leave. His clans' young herders say he is old and the band is not, and that the keeper never said yes.
- Of the Saltkeeper: that the Saltkeeper had meant six weeks before the keeper asked and said so, and asked for its reading all the same, as last year. They say a keeper that is told what it meant is not contradicted, and that the Saltkeeper smoked the lean meat and salted the fat, and that this is the Saltkeeper's own and not the keeper's.
- Of the Still One: that she sat one night by the herds of the two clans and said the herd was tired in the chest still, and as last year. The two clans say it is the third true thing said of the cough, and that it is the same thing.
- Of the herder of the two clans: that he walks his herd at dusk when the keeper is away and finds the sick as soon as it does, and that a man who has a dog that points and has learned to point himself is the more to be trusted. He says it is the clan's.

About their neighbors
- The miners of the Hask Ridge [r20] cut the winter woods about as far in as last winter, sell salt and iron dear, and send iron grey and soft. The band says the iron is soft because the sickness of the towns' oxen is in it, and that the party should buy salt and iron only for the edges it cannot do without. They are wrong: the smiths of the stone towns say it is their ore. It matters, because the band's tools are running short and the smiths mend what they can.
- The band says the council sent no word this year because the last word was a begging and a begging is not said twice, and some say the towns have stopped asking after it. They are wrong that it was a begging; it was carried as a claim. It matters, because the band believes the holds have heard its asking and refused it, and the holds have said nothing.
- The band says the cough came to its cattle from the stone towns and that the towns' oxen cough still. They are right that the oxen cough. They have not shown the cough came from them. A clan head of the herds that lost most said three sound bulls might go to the yards for salt; the Saltkeeper said no beast of the main herds would go where the oxen coughed, and the band says the Saltkeeper is right and the salt is half.
- The song-thieves are not spoken of this year.
- The three clans that want to winter apart say the band is too big for one road, and some say it should part at the next going down. This is the quarrel to come, and it has a keeper's words under it: that it is the council's and the clans' and not the star's.

About the strange things of the year
- The pools. They say no pool went bad because the keeper stood at them. The Marchwarden says no pool went bad in a wet year and the verses do not say one could. Neither is known.
- The cord of bold beasts is read as a small thing, one knot, a boar. People say the keeper is a keeper of herds and not of beasts at all, and are surer of it than last year.
