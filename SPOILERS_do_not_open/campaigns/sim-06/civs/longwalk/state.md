# The Longwalk people of god-beasts: state at the end of Year 5

Rough figures from the people's own cords, not a census. Where a number turns on a ruling the Keeper holds (the spring wolves, and the reading of the party's beasts, see years/005-report.md), it is given for the plain case, and the other branch is given after it. The start of the year is taken from the Keeper's line on Year 4: the calving of Year 4 lost about thirty calves, so the herds began Year 5 at about 4,756 cattle.

population: about 5,200, one band on the move, now coming down from the winter woods toward spring. Births about 215, deaths about 165 (the old in winter, three infants of a belly sickness in the sixth month, the ordinary). The band grew by about 50, a little more than last year, since no pool went bad, no flux took children and no blow was struck.

stores: dried and salted meat as last year, not full; the autumn hunt found game as in most years and the herds were fat from the summer. The women smoked the lean meat and salted only the fat, to spare the salt. Salt about half of the year's wanting, as the last three years, because no cattle went to the stone towns to pay for it and the price did not fall. Iron about two thirds of the pieces of the years before last, as last year, grey and soft, because the Saltkeeper bought only the edges the band could not do without; edges turn and the smiths of the band mend what they can.

herds: about 4,950 cattle, 5,950 goats, 985 sheep, about 740 dogs (rough cords). Cattle gained by the calving and the ordinary summer and lost by the cough: about ten beasts of the two clans by the ninth month and about one in fifteen of theirs, some forty, in the winter. The cough stayed in their herds, which wintered apart on their own ground and browse. The wolves of the calving took no more than about six calves in the plain case. Other branch (the wolves not turned): about thirty calves lost, cattle about 4,926.

treasure: the herds and the dogs; little metal; salt and iron bought at the stone towns' price, no cheaper than last year, and the iron poorer.

trade: out, sound goats and sheep, hides and felt, pack goats, no cattle, by one trade party to the stone towns in the fifth month, carrying no word from the council; in, salt about half of the wanting and iron in few pieces, grey and soft, at last year's price. The party's pack goats and sheep were kept apart six weeks on its return, then read at dusk by the keeper and joined the herds in the plain case. No beast was bought. No other trade. No stranger came to the fires.

strength: about 900 who can carry a spear, about 300 of them hunters by trade; no walls. No one gravely hurt in the autumn hunt. No blow was struck in the winter camp.

mood: steadier than any year since the keeper came, with the surface unhurried. The star dimmed three times, to seven last year, and none in the winter; the apart clans' count of heads against the nights is broken, since the band is larger and the star dimmed less, and the keeper has said in the ring that it did not say yes. Some of the apart clans say it was never a yes, some say the band should still part at the next going down, and their eldest head says nothing is settled and that he will speak of parting in the ring without the keeper's leave if the star dims as before. The three clans wintered apart, a little nearer than last year, and are not reconciled. The band says the star eased because the keeper spoke at the first storm. The council held the Houndwarden's leave for the wolves a third time and the eldest head says a thing given three times is a law; the band counts the leave as the keeper's winnings and the herders are split on whether it earns its calves. The two clans lost about forty cattle again and have made a rule of the herder's saying, that a clan with a dog that points at a flock goes and looks; some of them spoke of wintering among the herds and did not. The cutters are no farther in than last winter, the salt is half and the iron poor, and the council sent the towns no word again. The Speaker had no chest fever and is slower; he slept through the closing of a ring in winter and the Marchwarden spoke it, and the clans' talk of who follows him is a little louder, and no name has been said aloud.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The wolves of the calving were not turned on the three nights [r-0291]: about thirty calves lost; the numbers take that branch.
- The dry month: the turns at the river held [r-0292]; the numbers take the lighter branch (about eighty cattle, thirty goats, twenty sheep; the children of the flux as written).
- The cutters as the keeper told them: three gatherings with draught beasts, deeper in than the songs put them; no number.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- The wolves of the calving were turned on all three nights. About six calves were lost. Your numbers take that branch.
- The council's word to the stone towns was heard and nothing was promised. The cutters were a little farther in again.
- What the keeper told the Marchwarden of the cutters: three gatherings of people with draught beasts, working and unafraid, a little deeper. No number.
- The keeper's readings give where a gathering is and whether it is hurt, afraid or resting. That a herd was drinking is its own guess, and it said so.
- The herder's question, what the cough is, had no answer.

## Keeper, Year 4, pass two
- The wolves of the calving got in among the calves on the second and third nights, though the Houndwarden sent hunters and dogs at the dusk of each reading and the calves were penned. About thirty calves were lost. Your numbers take that branch.
- The trade party's beasts had not taken the cough; read at six weeks they showed no hurt and joined the herds.
- At the pool below the third camp the herd read resting at the second dusk; the hurt of the first was a pair of beasts horned in a fight. The camp stayed.
- The cough took about forty of the two clans' cattle in the winter and no other herd's.
