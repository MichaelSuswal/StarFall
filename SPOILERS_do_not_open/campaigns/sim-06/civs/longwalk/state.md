# The Longwalk people of god-beasts: state at the end of Year 4

Rough figures from the people's own cords, not a census. Where a number turns on a ruling the Keeper holds (the spring wolves, and the reading of the party's beasts, see years/004-report.md), it is given for the plain case, and the other branch is given after it.

population: about 5,150, one band on the move, now coming down from the winter woods toward spring. Births about 210, deaths about 165 (the old in winter, a few infants, two small children of a belly sickness in the sixth month). The band grew by about 45, a little more than last year, since no pool went bad and no flux took children.

stores: dried and salted meat as last year, better than the two years before and not full; the autumn hunt found game as in most years and the herds were fat from the summer. Salt about half of the year's wanting, as last year, because no cattle went to the stone towns to pay for it and the price did not fall. Iron fewer pieces than last year, about two thirds, and greyer and softer, because the Saltkeeper bought only the edges the band could not do without; edges turn and the smiths of the band mend what they can.

herds: about 4,780 cattle, 5,800 goats, 970 sheep, about 735 dogs (rough cords). Cattle gained by the calving and the ordinary summer and lost by the cough: about ten beasts of the two clans by the ninth month and about one in fifteen of theirs, some forty, in the winter. The cough stayed in their herds, which wintered apart on their own ground and browse. The wolves of the calving took no more than about six calves in the plain case. Other branch (the wolves not turned): about thirty calves lost, cattle about 4,756.

treasure: the herds and the dogs; little metal; salt and iron bought at the stone towns' price, no cheaper than last year, and the iron poorer.

trade: out, sound goats and sheep, hides and felt, pack goats, no cattle, by one trade party to the stone towns in the fifth month, carrying no word from the council; in, salt about half of the wanting and iron in fewer pieces, greyer and softer, at last year's price. The party's pack goats and sheep were kept apart six weeks on its return, then read at dusk by the keeper and joined the herds in the plain case. No beast was bought. No other trade. No stranger came to the fires.

strength: about 900 who can carry a spear, about 300 of them hunters by trade; no walls. One hunter hurt in the autumn hunt by a fall, not gravely, fit by winter. No blow was struck in the winter camp.

mood: tired and a little steadier than last year, with the surface still unhurried. The two clans lost far fewer cattle than last winter and the herder who asked what the cough was has had a smaller answer than he wanted and has begun to use it. The cutters are a little farther in again, the iron is poorer, and the council sent the towns no word this year, saying it had been a claim and would not be said twice; the band's talk is that it was a begging. The star dimmed seven times, the most yet, three of them in the winter when the keeper stood at the margin, and the saying that it did not dim last winter for the keeper's margin is gone; the apart clans count heads against the nights and some say the band should part at the next going down. The council gave its Houndwarden leave for the wolves a second time, and the eldest head of the three clans said a thing given twice is a custom. The three clans still winter apart, no nearer and no farther than last year, and are not reconciled. The Speaker had no chest fever this winter and is slower; the clans' talk of who follows him is louder and no name has been said aloud.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The wolves of the calving were not turned on the three nights [r-0291]: about thirty calves lost; the numbers take that branch.
- The dry month: the turns at the river held [r-0292]; the numbers take the lighter branch (about eighty cattle, thirty goats, twenty sheep; the children of the flux as written).
- The cutters as the keeper told them: three gatherings with draught beasts, deeper in than the songs put them; no number.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- The wolves of the calving were turned on all three nights. About six calves were lost. Your numbers take that branch.
- The council's word to the stone towns was heard and nothing was promised. The cutters were a little farther in again.
- What the keeper told the Marchwarden of the cutters: three gatherings of people with draught beasts, working and unafraid, a little deeper. No number.
- The keeper's readings give where a gathering is and whether it is hurt, afraid or resting. That a herd was drinking is its own guess, and it said so.
- The herder's question, what the cough is, had no answer.

## Keeper, Year 4, pass two
- The wolves of the calving got in among the calves on the second and third nights, though the Houndwarden sent hunters and dogs at the dusk of each reading and the calves were penned. About thirty calves were lost. Your numbers take that branch.
- The trade party's beasts had not taken the cough; read at six weeks they showed no hurt and joined the herds.
- At the pool below the third camp the herd read resting at the second dusk; the hurt of the first was a pair of beasts horned in a fight. The camp stayed.
- The cough took about forty of the two clans' cattle in the winter and no other herd's.
