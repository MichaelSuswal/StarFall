# Report to the Keeper: the Longwalk, Year 2

## How the god was met

Spring, month 1, the dusk of the first storm, the council ring at the winter camp in the high wood. The god stood at the edge of the firelight and gave Tessaly its answer to be carried in. She said it in the ring as given: "It is true, and I will say what I want once. I want this band many and unhurried, and I want to be told when what I do costs it. I ask no share, no seat and no rite. The council may keep me at the edge or send me from it." The eldest head of the three clans heard it, and said it was a small thing to want and that small things are the first things. The Speaker, Orrin Haddock, said the council had heard it, that a thing which asks no share, no seat and no rite is a margin keeper and the margin is the Houndwarden's, that the council would not send it from the edge and would not take its word, and that Tessaly might carry to it what she saw of its costs. No vote. Tessaly gave this back at the margin. Nobody took its word.

Spring, months 1 to 3, down the Herd Road: Tessaly let it keep the margin of each night's camp at the rear with her dogs, on her own word as Houndwarden. Each dusk and before light she took from it what it read of the herds (hurt, afraid, resting) and where wolves lay, and told the herders. She did not take it as an order. The herders had already put apart coughing beasts; they kept doing it.

Spring, month 2: the Speaker put the god's request for leave at the three clans' margin to their eldest head. He said he would answer when the summer water was known.

Summer, month 4, the dry month: the god stood at the margin each dusk and before light as in spring and told Tessaly each dusk what it read of each herd at the water. Tessaly told the herders that the keeper read it so and that she took it as one takes a dog's word, and they moved herds or kept them as they judged. No one took its word as an order.

Summer, month 5, first days: the eldest head gave leave for one month, no ring, no word carried either way, gone at the month's end, through the Speaker and Tessaly. The god went by the wood's edge, came to their dogs first, kept their night watch for the month, and said to their head that it asked nothing. The head came to the edge of the light on the twentieth night and put two questions (below). It went back to the main camp at the month's end.

Summer, month 6, the first days, the river camp: the council sat at dusk. The god gave the ledger from Tessaly's cord and said it had spoken with neither the fox nor the boar of last summer, and with no beast this year. The Marchwarden asked whether the dimming of the star was its own and was answered that it knew nothing of it, had done nothing at those hours that it knew of, and did not say it was not its own because it could not say what it did not know. The council spoke at length. No vote.

Autumn, month 8: it came to the Marchwarden's fire at dusk and told him what it had counted of the stone towns' cutters in the winter woods. It asked whether the songs said they had come so far in before. He answered from the songs and took what it counted to the Speaker. He did not take its word as an order.

Autumn, month 8, the first cold: the council asked it to stand with the hunters. It said it would and asked the hunt to keep its season. The hunt kept it. No share was set for it.

Winter: it kept the margin of the winter camp. The Speaker, the Houndwarden, the Marchwarden and the Saltkeeper have each heard it speak and none has taken its word. The Saltkeeper was told nothing of trade. The Runners carried nothing for it.

## What was done in the god's name

Nothing. The Houndwarden carried its words and its readings and acted on them by her own judgement as Houndwarden; she said "the keeper reads", not "the keeper bids". The watch, the cord and the keeping apart of coughing beasts are her arrangements and the clans' own. No helper is serving.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper

1. The wolves of the calving, three nights, months 1 to 3, about the rear herds and one flank. Attempt: the god stands the margin and tells Tessaly where the wolves lie and which way; Tessaly and the hunters with the dogs turn them. Success: the wolves are turned each night and no more than about six calves are lost over the three nights; the rear camp says the herds sleep better. Failure: about thirty calves are lost, the rear camp says the keeper is no better than a dog, and the knot for the spring wolves stays open on the cord. Circumstances from my record: last year the Keeper turned the wolves on the four spring nights and the east flank; the dogs fall silent and turn to the keeper because of what it is; the god's reading of a gathering as hurt, afraid or resting is its Listening; the herds are thin from winter and the calves newly dropped. I wrote the year with the success branch and the failure branch after it in `years/002.md`.

2. The watering of the herds in the dry month, month 4, the first of summer. The weather release is no rain on the summer grazing for the month, the grass browned at the edges of the range, the small streams thin and some stopped, the river lower and running. Attempt: the band waters its herds clan by clan by turns laid out by cord, at dawn and dusk, rests them in the trees through the heat and moves them by night onto the browned edge in short moves; drinking water is drawn above the camps; the Still One sits by the herds at the thinnest water on three nights; the god reads each herd at dusk and Tessaly passes it to the herders. Success: the herds are watered by turns, the band loses about eighty cattle (mostly calves and old cows), thirty goats and twenty sheep, the quarrel at the upper bank is settled by the Speaker and ends without blows. Failure: the press at the banks breaks the turns by night, calves are trampled, about three hundred and fifty cattle and about a hundred and fifty goats and sheep are lost, the quarrel at the upper bank comes to blows, the band goes into autumn much leaner and the three clans winter far apart. Circumstances from my record: about 5,000 people, about 4,300 cattle, 5,400 goats, 900 sheep and 700 dogs strung along the river; the herds thin from winter and the calves young; the cattle of two clans still cough; the three clans that camp apart had their own streams stop and came to the river late; the river was running, only lower; no fire is to be lit on the dry grass; the god spoke with no beast that anyone saw. The flux among the children at the fouled pools I took as mine (about sixteen dead) and wrote in both branches.

If a herd comes into danger in that month that no dog or hunter can serve, the god's own decisions leave it the one exception of speaking with a single beast; I wrote the year without it and left it to the god.

## What turns on a power

1. The god's dusk readings of the herds at the water, and in spring and autumn of the herds that cough. I wrote only that it told Tessaly each dusk which herds it read as hurt, afraid or resting and that Tessaly told the herders. What the Listening actually showed, and whether it lets the god tell a herd that coughs from a herd that does not, is the Keeper's to rule (the god's own question 1). In autumn the god told Tessaly of "the herd that was hurt at the edge of the camp" and the clans had the five coughing beasts apart before the second day; whether the reading did that is for the Keeper.
2. The dogs of the three clans fell silent and turned to the god on its coming, as the main camp's do. I took the Keeper's year 1 ruling, because of what it is, and did not write any doing.
3. The five dimmed nights of the star: months 2, 6, 9, 11 and 12, an hour each, the blue deepening and the star dim in the middle of the night, anyone awake under a clear sky seeing it. My people say it is the keeper's. The god said it knew nothing of it. I wrote only what they saw and said.
4. What the god counted of the stone towns' cutters in the winter woods, which the Listening read in months 7 and 8. I did not write the count, the distance in, or what the cutters take. The god tells the Marchwarden, and the Marchwarden's taking of it to the Speaker and the Saltkeeper turns on it. The Keeper or the god is to give it.
5. The Still One's reading of the first herd she sat by in the dry month: that it was afraid of the others and not of the thirst. This is her ordinary gift.
6. The rain's return in month 5, three days steady. My people say it came for the keeper, or because Tessaly would not ask. I wrote only that they say so.

## What touches anyone else

- The trade party. Set out in month 5 for the stone towns of the Hask Ridge [r20] by the way the band has always gone, with sound sheep and goats, hides and felt, and fewer cattle than last year, every beast that had coughed or was thin from the dry month being kept back. It came back before the going up with salt and iron at last year's price, no cheaper, about three fifths of the salt the band wanted. It reported the cutters in the winter woods again, farther in, and some of the towns' own draught oxen coughing in their yards. The Saltkeeper and the Houndwarden have said next year's party will take no cattle into the towns' yards. The band says the cough came to its cattle from the stone towns. I do not know that, and wrote it as their belief.
- The cutters. The god counted them by the Listening and told the Marchwarden in month 8. The Marchwarden took it to the Speaker and the Saltkeeper.
- The three clans that camp apart. The god kept their margin for a month in month 5 on their leave, and wintered with the main band apart as before, nearer than last year. No word was carried between them and the main council by the god.
- No stranger came to the fires. No one was sent anywhere but the trade party. No message was carried by or for the god.

## Asked of the god

- The eldest head of the three clans, month 6 of Year 1, before the god and not to it: "a keeper that asks nothing will want something later". Answered this year, month 1, in the ring, by Tessaly, from the god's words, as above.
- The Speaker, month 2, asked of the eldest head the leave the god asked of him, to keep the margin of the three clans' camp for a month. Not a question put to the god.
- The eldest head, month 5, by the Speaker and Tessaly to the god, three conditions on his leave: "it will not come into the ring or the camp, it will bring no word from the main council and carry none back, and at the month's end it will go." Accepted by the god's going.
- The eldest head, month 5, at the edge of the light, the twentieth night, two things to the god: "Do you ask anything of us?" Answered in front of him: it asked nothing. "Will you carry word between us and the main camp?" Answered in front of him: it carried no word between the clans on its own.
- The Marchwarden, month 6, at the river camp, before the council: "Is the dimming of the star yours?" Answered in front of the council: it knew nothing of it, it had done nothing at those hours that it knew of, and it did not say it was not its own because it could not say what it did not know.
- The council, month 8, by the Speaker, at the first cold: "Would you stand with the hunters at the hunt's edge and say where the game lay?" Answered in front of the Speaker: it would, and asked the hunt to keep its season.
- A woman of the east flank herds, month 4, to Tessaly at the water, not to the god: "Someone should ask it for rain." Tessaly would not carry it. Not put to the god. The god's decisions have it call no storm this year.
- The Houndwarden carried small costs from the dog handlers in the winter months, at the god's asking to be told them, and the god heard them. These were not questions.

## Names said aloud

None. Tessaly and Orrin Haddock are names already in my record. No mortal said a name for the people, their home, a camping ground, or an office holder my record leaves unnamed. The Marchwarden, the Saltkeeper, the eldest head of the three clans, the east flank woman and the Still One's granddaughter remain unnamed.
