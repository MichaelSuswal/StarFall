# Report to the Keeper: the Longwalk, Year 5

## How the god was met

Spring, month 1, the dusk of the first storm, the council ring at the winter camp in the high wood. The god stood at the edge of the firelight and gave Tessaly its answer to what the apart clans took from its silence, in the words of its decisions, and Tessaly said it whole in the ring. The young herder of the three clans that camp apart was there, at the back of his eldest head's men, brought by a runner Tessaly sent; the herder of the two clans stood at the edge among his own.
- The young herder said he had asked twice and been given at the second asking an answer that was not a no, that his clan had taken it for a yes, and that now it said it had not said yes. Part of his clan, behind him, were angry that the keeper was taking back what it had let them hold; part said it was the first plain thing it had said of the star and that a clan does not part for a thing that was not said. He did not take its word.
- The eldest head of the three clans said it had now said twice that it wanted the band many, that a thing said twice is said, and that "a body keeps one margin and not two" was said to him because he had refused it a second month, so that a part that left would be the part left unkept. He did not take its word.
- The Speaker, Orrin Haddock, said the council had heard it, that the parting of a band is the council's and the clans' and no keeper's, as it said itself, and that the council would not take its word on the star nor hold it to one. No vote. He did not take its word.
- Tessaly gave the ring's words back at the margin as they were said and told it besides that the council had taken none of its word and given none, that she held the leave and acted on the leave, and that the last calving's loss was about thirty calves and not six: the six was the first night's count, said before the second and third nights were reckoned at the river. She said the herders had penned at the dusk it spoke as it asked, that they said afterward they should have done it sooner, and that the second and third nights were the same dusk and the same pens. It heard her and said nothing more that night.
- The herder of the two clans came to the margin after the ring and asked it, through Tessaly, to read his herd at dusk each few nights. It said it would.

Spring, month 1, in the same ring, the council on the leave for the wolves (below, in the god's name). Heads of the clans that lost the most said the leave was worth no more than waiting. The eldest head said the custom was thirty calves. The Speaker said the leave stood for the calving only and took no vote. None took the god's word.

Spring, months 1 to 3: Tessaly let it keep the margin of each night's camp and took its dusk and dawn readings as one takes a dog's word. On three nights of the calving she sent hunters and dogs on its wolf reading the same dusk by the council's leave (below). In month 3 she passed its reading of a gathering hurt in part at the far end of the two clans' herds; the herders found four coughing beasts and put them apart.

Spring, month 2, two dimmed nights: it stood at the margin, dusk before and again after midnight, and told the Marchwarden at his fire after the second night what it read of the herds under the star, and that it read the beasts and not the sky. He put it with his notches and said it was a mark that cost no one and claimed nothing. He did not take its word as an answer to anything.

Summer, months 4 to 6: it stood at the stopped camp and at each pool the Marchwarden named. No pool went bad and no camp moved. Month 6, the river camp: the council sat at dusk, Tessaly gave the cord (one knot, a boar) and the whole count of the calving. No vote on anything put to it. Nobody asked it for rain.

Autumn, month 7: it was along the timber some nights. The herder of the two clans came for his reading on one such night and met Tessaly and silence; it read his herd at the next dusk. Month 8: it came to the Marchwarden's fire at dusk and told him what it read of the cutters. He took it to the Speaker and the Saltkeeper. He did not take its word as an order. Month 8, the first cold: the council asked it to stand at the hunt's edge. It said it would and asked the hunt to keep its season. The hunt kept it. No share was set for it.

Autumn, month 9: Tessaly carried its asking to the Saltkeeper that the party's beasts be kept apart until a dusk's reading showed no hurt in part and that the two clans' cattle winter apart. The Saltkeeper said six weeks and the apart wintering were already meant, and asked for its reading at the end as one asks a dog. That is not a taking of its word. The old woman of the two clans came to the margin and put a question to it (below) and took the answer as the same thing it had said before.

Winter: it kept the margin of the winter camp. The herder of the two clans came each few nights and it read his herd. In month 12 the Marchwarden asked it about the star (below). The Speaker, the Houndwarden, the Marchwarden and the Saltkeeper have each heard it speak. None has taken its word as an order. The Still One has not spoken with it. The Runners carried nothing for it.

## What was done in the god's name

Nothing, in the sense of an office carrying out its wishes. Tessaly sent hunters and dogs on its wolf readings on three nights by the council's leave, as in other years, and told the herders it was her word and the leave and not its order; she kept a third of her force at the pens by her own judgment. The herders' penning at the dusk of each reading was at her word. The Marchwarden's walking of the pools, the Saltkeeper's six weeks, the two clans' putting apart of coughing beasts and wintering apart, and the Houndwarden's cord are the band's own arrangements. A mortal took on himself the herder's walking of his gatherings at dusk when the keeper was away; that is his act. No helper is serving.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper

1. The wolves of the calving, three nights, months 1 to 3. The number of nights is mine from the cord; the release gave none. Attempt: on each night the god tells Tessaly how many gatherings of wolves lie near the herds, where and which way, and she sends hunters and dogs to the place the same dusk, by the council's leave, without waiting to see a wolf. She keeps back a third of the dogs and four hunters at the pens by the fires with the herders and sends the rest. The herders draw the calves from the outer edge of every herd along that stretch of the line to the thorn pens at that dusk, not only the herd named, and set the dogs at the pen edges. Nights: first the rear herds, second the east flank, third the head of the line near the edge of the Farrow Grass. Success: the wolves are turned before they reach the calves and about six calves are lost over the three nights. Failure: about thirty calves are lost again, the herders say the leave has cost thirty twice, and the eldest head says a custom given three times has been shown. Circumstances from my record: last year the same dusk plan with the calves penned at the dusk of the reading failed on the second and third nights and about thirty were lost, though the hunters went the same dusk to the places named; the year before, the same dusk plan without pens turned them and about six were lost; two years before, the hunt waited to see a wolf and about thirty were lost; the calves are newly dropped and the herds thin from winter; the dogs turn to the god and not the wolves; the hunters are told where and which way; Tessaly is slow and sure with the dogs; this year a third of the force stays at the pens and every herd along the stretch pens, which are changes of mine on the Houndwarden's own judgment after the thirty. I wrote the year with the success branch and the failure branch briefly after it in `years/005.md`. State carries both.

2. The reading of the party's beasts at the end of six weeks, months 9 to 10. Attempt: the pack goats and the sheep the party brought back are kept apart on the downwind end; at the end of six weeks the Saltkeeper asks Tessaly for the god's dusk reading of them, and the god reads whether any stand hurt in part. Success: no hurt in part is read and they join the herds. Failure: a beast reads hurt in part, they are kept apart another month and the beast is killed and burned by the old rule. Circumstances: the party was in the stone towns' yards and saw the towns' draught oxen coughing from the fence and did not go in among them; it bought no beast; the cough has not been known in the band's goats or sheep; your release says the cough stayed in the two clans' beasts through the year; last year the same reading showed no hurt. I wrote the plain case (no hurt read) and the failure briefly after it.

3. The first dusk reading at any pool, months 4 to 6, if the god read a herd hurt. Your release says no pool went bad beyond the ordinary, and I wrote no reading of hurt at any pool. If it read one, the conduct is the Marchwarden's of last year: Tessaly carries it with how little one dusk is and that the water is its guess, he keeps the herd off the pool one night, the camps draw and boil their water above, he asks for the next dusk's reading and does not move the camp unless the second dusk reads hurt too. Success: the second dusk reads resting and the camp stays. Failure: it still reads hurt, he moves the camps up the stream. No flux in either case.

The wintering of the two clans among the gathered herds did not arise: some of the two clans spoke of it in month 9 and after some quarrel they wintered apart on their own ground, as your release's first case has it. I wrote one branch and carried about forty head lost. The autumn deaths of the two clans' cattle before the winter (about ten by month 9) are my own count of a cough that stayed in their herds through the year.

## What turns on a power

1. The god's readings of wolves near the herds at the calving: where, how many gatherings, which way. I wrote only that it told Tessaly and what she and the herders did. Whether the readings were as it said is the Keeper's.
2. The god's reading in month 3 of a gathering at the far end of the two clans' herds as hurt in part, the herders finding four beasts coughing there, and its readings each few nights at the herder's asking, and the herder finding coughing beasts in the gatherings it named. Whether its reading let it name gatherings with the cough, and whether it did so correctly, is for the Keeper, as last year. Where it was away the herder walked his gatherings himself and found two.
3. The god's readings at the pools in months 4 to 6 (see uncertain outcome 3). I wrote no reading of hurt.
4. The god's readings of the herds on the nights the star dimmed. Month 2, twice, it was at the margin at dusk before and after midnight and told the Marchwarden what it read of the herds under the star. I wrote only what the herders saw: the cattle standing and chewing, the calves at the cows, nothing fretting, the dogs lying down. What its own reading gave is the Keeper's to say, as your release has it. On the month 7 night it was along the timber some nights of the month and Tessaly could not say whether it was at the margin that night; I wrote that as hers.
5. The god's reading of the cutters in months 7 and 8: gatherings with draught beasts, working and unafraid. I wrote no count or distance. The winter showed the band that the cutters were about as far in as last winter, from your release. If the god's reading should differ, the Keeper settles it.
6. The three dimmed nights of the star, months 2, 2 and 7, an hour each, the blue deepening and the star dim in the middle of the night; twenty four since the keeper came. Your Year 4 line says they are not known to be anyone's doing. The band ties it to the keeper's words at the first storm, the apart clans to the keeper being gone from the margin, and nobody to anything known. I wrote only what they saw and said.
7. The dogs fell silent and turned to the god on its coming to the margin each year, and lay down on the dimmed nights. I took the Keeper's earlier rulings and did not write any doing.
8. The Still One's reading in month 11 of the two clans' herd: tired in the chest still, about as last year. This is her ordinary gift and I did not decide what the beasts were suffering.
9. Its answer in the ring in month 1 and to the old woman in month 9, in the words of its decisions. I wrote only that Tessaly said them whole and what the people made of them.

## What touches anyone else

- The trade party. Set out in month 5 for the stone towns of the Hask Ridge [r20] by the way the band has always gone, with sound goats and sheep, hides and felt and pack goats, and no cattle, carrying no word from the council and the Saltkeeper's cord (salt first, iron only for the edges the band could not do without). It came home in month 9 with about half the salt the band wanted, dear, at no better price, and iron in about as few pieces as last year, grey and soft, having seen the towns' draught oxen coughing still from the fence and the cutters in the winter woods again, about as far in as last winter. It bought no beast. Its pack goats and the sheep it brought back were kept apart six weeks on the downwind end and read at the end (uncertain outcome 2). I wrote nothing of the towns or the road beyond what it reported.
- The cutters. The god read them in months 7 and 8 and told the Marchwarden, who took it to the Speaker and the Saltkeeper. The council sent no word this year. The god took no trail on them.
- The three clans that camp apart. The eldest head and his two men and the young herder heard the god's answer in the ring in month 1. The keeper's margin at their camp was not asked of the eldest head this year and the keeper did not go. They came down the Herd Road behind the band, camped on the river's upper bank apart as in other years, and wintered apart again, a little nearer than last year, within sight of the main camp's smoke at dusk, and took the Speaker's cord for fuel wood. They counted the band's heads against the nights again; the count has weakened, since the star dimmed three times with the band larger. The eldest head says nothing is settled, that the band will be counted again at the next going down, and that if the star dims as in the years before he will speak of parting in the ring and will not ask the keeper's leave. No word was carried between the clans and the god.
- The two clans whose cattle cough. They wintered apart on their own ground at the downwind end of the camp, on their own browse, with the coughing beasts in a brush pen: about ten died by month 9 and about one in fifteen of theirs, some forty, in the winter. They burned the dead.
- No stranger came to the fires. No one was sent anywhere but the trade party. No message was carried by or for the god.

## Asked of the god

- The young herder of the three clans, month 8 and month 10 of Year 4, at the margin: whether the star dimmed because the band was too many to walk as one. Answered in the first month of this year, in the ring, by Tessaly in the god's own words, in front of him, as above.
- The young herder, month 1, in the ring after the answer, to the ring and to Tessaly and not to the god: that his clan had taken the second answer for a yes and that now it said it had not said yes. Not put to the god. The Speaker held it.
- The eldest head, month 1, in the ring, not to the god: that a thing said twice is said, and that one margin and not two was said to him. Not put to the god.
- The herder of the two clans, month 1, through Tessaly at the margin, after the ring: "Read my herd at dusk each few nights." Answered: it would, and did, each few nights through the year.
- The herder of the two clans, month 7, at the margin at dusk, on a night the god was along the timber: his reading. It was away and met silence; he waited and it read his herd at the next dusk. Nothing was put to it that stood pending longer than that.
- The Marchwarden, month 8, at his fire at dusk: whether the cutters had come in since the last count. Answered in front of him: what it read, and no more.
- The council, month 8, by the Speaker at the first cold: "Would you stand at the hunt's edge and say where the game lay?" Answered in front of the Speaker: it would, and asked the hunt to keep its season.
- The Saltkeeper, month 10, through Tessaly: the god's reading of the party's beasts at dusk at the end of six weeks. Answered by the reading (uncertain outcome 2).
- The old woman of the two clans, month 9, at the margin with the herder, through Tessaly: whether its words that the cough came hard to those that wintered apart meant the two clans were blamed. Answered in front of her: the cough came hard to them and no other herd took it, it did not say it was their fault, and it did not know why it had chosen them.
- The Marchwarden, month 12, at the margin at dusk, through Tessaly: whether the star dimming three times and not seven was its doing. Answered in front of them: it knew nothing of it, it had done nothing at those hours that it knew of, and it did not say it was not any god's, because it could not say what it did not know.
- The clan heads that lost the most calves, month 1, in the ring, not to the god: that the leave was worth no more than waiting. Not put to the god.

## Names said aloud

None. Tessaly and Orrin Haddock are names already in my record. No mortal said a name for the people, their home, a camping ground, or an office holder my record leaves unnamed. The Marchwarden, the Saltkeeper, the eldest head of the three clans, the herder of the two clans, the young herder of the three clans, the old woman of the two clans, and the Still One's granddaughter remain unnamed. In the winter the clans said an office when they spoke of who follows the Speaker, and no name.

## What the people tell their god

What they would tell it of their year, in their own words. Of the calving and the party's beasts they say only that it was tried.
- The Houndwarden: "It was thirty last year and not six, and I told you so at the margin. The council took none of your word and gave you none, and I held the leave a third time. This year I sent the hunters and dogs the same dusk you spoke, and I kept a third of them at the pens with the herders, and the herders penned every herd along the stretch and not only the one you named. We tried it on three nights. The eldest head says a thing given three times is a law. I bring the whole count to the river camp after the third night and not before."
- The herder of the two clans: "You read my herd at dusk and I found coughing beasts in the gatherings you named, and where you were away I walked them myself and found two. We lost about forty this winter, one in fifteen, and we kept them on our own ground. The old woman asked whether your words blame us and you said they do not. My clan has made the rule that a clan with a dog that points at a flock goes and looks."
- The Marchwarden: "I notched three nights of the star this year, to seven last year, and twenty four since you came. The numbers fell as they climbed, without a rule I can find. You told me at my fire what the herds did under it and said you read the beasts and not the sky. The cutters are about where they were last winter and no farther. The verse for the third stand is an old lie still, but not a worse one."
- The Saltkeeper: "We kept the party's beasts apart six weeks, as we meant to, and I asked for your reading of them at the end. We bought salt about half of what we wanted, dear, and iron for the edges only, grey and soft. The towns' oxen still cough. We sent no word this year and no cattle."
- The Speaker: "I took no vote and I closed nothing. The council took no word from you and gave you none. You said the parting is the council's and the clans', and I said the same. I am slower. The Marchwarden spoke the closing for me one night and the ring let him. The clans talk of who follows me and have not said a name. The three clans wintered apart, a little nearer."
- The young herder of the three clans: "You said you did not say yes. My clan took your silence for a yes and counted the band's heads against the nights. The star dimmed three times this year and not seven, and the band is five thousand two hundred, and our count is broken. Some of us still say the band should part at the next going down. Some say it was never a yes."
- The eldest head of the three clans: "You said one margin and not two. I heard it said to me. Nothing is settled. If the star dims as it did, I will speak of parting in the ring, and I will not ask your leave for it."
- The band: "The star dimmed three times and not seven. We do not say you hold it, and we do not say the star eased because you spoke at the first storm, though some do. The cutters are where they were. The salt is half. We keep you at the margin."
