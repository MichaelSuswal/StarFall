# Report to the Keeper: the Longwalk, Year 4

## How the god was met

Spring, month 1, the dusk of the first storm, the council ring at the winter camp in the high wood. The god stood at the edge of the firelight and gave Tessaly its answer to the herder's question of last year, in the words of its decisions, and Tessaly said it whole in the ring with the herder of the two clans standing at the edge among his own people.
- The herder said he had asked for a name and been given a smaller thing, and that a keeper that listens over a herd and cannot say which beast is hurt is a dog that points at the flock. Tessaly said a dog that points has still told you to look. He did not take the answer as a word to act on and later used its readings (below).
- An old woman of the two clans said a keeper that says it does not know is better than one that says what it does not know. Part of the two clans heard blame in "wintered apart" and were angry; others said it was only what happened.
- The eldest head of the three clans, asked nothing, said of the leave for the wolves that a thing given once is a leave and a thing given twice is a custom, and that he did not stand against it. He did not take its word.
- The Speaker, Orrin Haddock, said the leave had been given for the calving only and the calving came again, that the Houndwarden might send her hunters on a reading she judged good, and that he would hear her cord at the river camp. No vote. No clan head stood against it. He did not take its word.
- Tessaly gave this back at the margin in the same words and told it besides that the council had not taken its word for the wolves, that it had given her leave, and that she had acted on the leave and not on a word, so that it should not go about believing otherwise. It heard that and asked her to carry that it would read the herder's herd at dusk as it had said.

Spring, months 1 to 3: Tessaly let it keep the margin of each night's camp and took its dusk and dawn readings as one takes a dog's word. On three nights she sent the hunters and dogs on its reading the same dusk by the council's leave and had the herders draw the calves to the pens by the fires. In month 3 she passed its reading of a gathering hurt in part at the far end of the two clans' herds; they found three beasts coughing and put them apart.

Summer, month 5, the first dusk at the pool below the third camp: it told Tessaly a herd stood hurt in part, that one dusk was little and might be nothing, and that whether it drank and anything of the water was its own guess. Tessaly took it to the Marchwarden. He kept the herd from the pool for a night, had the camps draw and boil their drinking water above, asked for the next dusk's reading, and did not move the camp. That is a taking of its reading for one night's measure by his own judgment, and not of its word as an order.

Summer, month 6, the river camp: the council sat at dusk, Tessaly gave the cord, two knots, none the god's, and the number of calves lost. No vote on anything put to it. Nobody asked it for rain.

Autumn, months 7 and 8: it went some nights along the timber and was not at the margin. In month 8 it came to the Marchwarden's fire at dusk and told him what it read of the cutters, saying that past a mile it reads gatherings and their state and not the number of people. He asked whether the axe had passed the third stand. He did not take its word as an order and took what it read to the Speaker and the Saltkeeper.

Autumn, month 8, the first cold: the council asked it to stand at the hunt's edge. It said it would and asked the hunt to keep its season. The hunt kept it. No share was set for it.

Autumn, month 9: Tessaly carried its asking to the Saltkeeper that the party's beasts be kept apart longer, until a dusk's reading showed no hurt in part, and that the two clans' cattle stay apart on their own ground through the winter. The Saltkeeper said six weeks was already meant and so was the two clans' wintering apart, and asked for the keeper's reading at the end as one asks a dog. That is not a taking of its word.

Winter: it kept the margin of the winter camp. In month 10 the herder of the two clans asked it, through Tessaly, to read his herd at dusk each few nights and say which gathering stood hurt in part; it said it would and did, and he found coughing beasts in the gatherings it named. The Speaker, the Houndwarden, the Marchwarden and the Saltkeeper have each heard it speak. None has taken its word as an order. The Still One has not spoken with it. The Runners carried nothing for it.

## What was done in the god's name

Nothing, in the sense of an office carrying out its wishes. Tessaly sent hunters on its wolf readings on three nights by the council's leave and told the herders it was her word and the council's leave. The Marchwarden's night off the pool, the Saltkeeper's six weeks, the two clans' keeping of their cattle apart and their putting apart of coughing beasts, and the Houndwarden's cord are the band's own arrangements. No helper is serving.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper

1. The wolves of the calving, three nights, months 1 to 3. The number of nights is mine from the cord; the release gave none. Attempt: on each night the god tells Tessaly how many gatherings of wolves lie near the herds, where and which way, and at once. Tessaly sends the hunters and dogs to the place the same dusk, by the council's leave, without waiting to see a wolf, and the herders draw the calves in from the outer edge of each herd to the thorn pens by the fires and set the dogs at the pen edges for the night (the herders' custom is to pen calves after a wolf is seen; this year they pen at the dusk of the reading). Nights: first the east flank, second the head of the line near the edge of the Farrow Grass, third the rear herds. Success: the wolves are turned before they reach the calves and about six calves are lost over the three nights. Failure: about thirty calves are lost, the leave is looked on as costing calves, the eldest head says he had said so. Circumstances from my record: last year the same dusk plan turned them and about six were lost; two years ago the hunt waited and about thirty were lost; the calves are newly dropped and the herds thin from winter; the dogs turn to the god and not the wolves; the hunters are told where and which way; Tessaly is slow and sure with the dogs; the pens by the fires are an addition this year. I wrote the year with the success branch and the failure branch briefly after it in `years/004.md`. State carries both.

2. The reading of the party's beasts at the end of six weeks, month 9 to 10. Attempt: the pack goats and the sheep the party brought back are kept apart on the downwind end; at the end of six weeks the Saltkeeper asks Tessaly for the god's dusk reading of them, and the god reads whether any stand hurt in part. Success: no hurt in part is read and they join the herds. Failure: a beast reads hurt in part, they are kept apart another month and the beast is killed and burned by the old rule. Circumstances: the party was in the stone towns' yards this year and saw the towns' draught oxen coughing from the fence and did not go in among them; it bought no beast; the cough has not been known in the band's goats or sheep; the god's reading has given a gathering as hurt, afraid or resting and has never been tried on a gathering of pack goats. I wrote the plain case (no hurt read) and the failure briefly after it.

3. The first dusk reading at the pool below the third camp, month 5, and the next dusk's reading. Attempt: the god tells Tessaly a herd stands hurt in part, that one dusk is little, that the water is its guess. The Marchwarden keeps the herd off the pool one night and has the camps draw and boil their water above, and asks for the next dusk's reading. Success: the next dusk the herd reads resting, the camp stays its three days and goes on in the round. Failure: it still reads hurt, the Marchwarden moves the camps up the stream as last year. Your release has it that no pool went bad beyond the ordinary, so no flux comes in either branch; the branches differ only in whether the camp moved and what the band says. I wrote the plain case (resting) and the other branch briefly after it.

The wintering of the two clans among the gathered herds did not arise: they wintered apart on their own ground, as the release's first case has it, and I wrote one branch and carried about 40 head lost.

## What turns on a power

1. The god's readings of wolves near the herds at the calving: where, how many gatherings, which way. I wrote only that it told Tessaly and what she and the herders did. Whether the readings were as it said is the Keeper's.
2. The god's reading in month 3 of a gathering at the far end of the two clans' herds as hurt in part, and the herders finding three coughing beasts there; and its readings in month 10 on, at the herder's asking, of which gathering of his herd stood hurt in part, and the herder finding coughing beasts in the gatherings it named. I wrote that it told Tessaly and the herder and that he found beasts. Whether its reading let it name gatherings with the cough, and whether it did so correctly, is for the Keeper, as last year.
3. The first dusk reading in month 5 of a herd hurt in part, and the next dusk's reading of it as resting. I took the Keeper's line that no pool went bad beyond the ordinary and wrote the reading as the god gave it. What the herd's hurt was is not mine; the band says it was the pool or a cough.
4. The god's reading of the cutters in months 7 and 8: gatherings with draught beasts, working, unafraid, a little farther in than the last count. I wrote this from your release (the cutters a little farther in again) and from last year's ruling that it gives gatherings and their state and not a number. I wrote no count or distance. If the god's reading should differ from the release, the Keeper settles it.
5. The seven dimmed nights of the star, months 2, 3, 4, 8, 10, 11, 12, an hour each, the blue deepening and the star dim in the middle of the night. Twenty one since the keeper came. Three fell in the winter with the god at the margin. The band ties it to the keeper or to its numbers. I wrote only what they saw and said. The god was away along the timber on the night in month 8; Tessaly could say so for that one.
6. The dogs fell silent and turned to the god on its coming to the margin each year. I took the Keeper's earlier ruling and did not write any doing.
7. The Still One's reading in month 11 of the two clans' herd: tired in the chest still, and less than last year. This is her ordinary gift and I did not decide what the beasts were suffering.
8. Its answer to the herder's question, in the words of its decisions. I wrote only that Tessaly said them whole and what the people made of them.

## What touches anyone else

- The trade party. Set out in month 5 for the stone towns of the Hask Ridge [r20] by the way the band has always gone, with sound goats and sheep, hides and felt and pack goats, and no cattle. It carried no word from the council. It carried the Saltkeeper's cord: salt first, iron only for the edges the band could not do without. It came home in month 9 with about half the salt the band wanted at no better price, with iron in about two thirds the pieces of last year, greyer and softer, having been told by the towns' smiths that their ore was bad, and having seen the towns' draught oxen coughing still from the fence and the cutters in the winter woods again, farther in. A smith said at the salt scale that the band had been quiet this year; the party's man said the band had said what it had to say. It bought no beast. Its pack goats and the sheep it brought back were kept apart six weeks on the downwind end and read at the end (uncertain outcome 2). I wrote nothing of the towns or the road beyond what it reported.
- The cutters. The god read them in months 7 and 8 and told the Marchwarden, who took it to the Speaker and the Saltkeeper. The council sent no word this year. The god took no trail on them.
- The three clans that camp apart. The keeper's margin at their camp was not asked of the eldest head this year. They wintered apart again, no nearer and no farther than last year, and took the Speaker's cord for fuel wood. A young herder of theirs, sent by his clan and not by his eldest head, came to the main camp's margin twice, in months 8 and 10, to ask the god about the star (below). No word was carried between the clans and the god.
- The two clans whose cattle cough. They wintered apart on their own ground at the downwind end of the camp, on their own browse, with the coughing beasts in a brush pen, so that it stayed in their beasts: about ten died by month 9 and about one in fifteen of theirs, some forty, in the winter. They burned the dead.
- No stranger came to the fires. No one was sent anywhere but the trade party. No message was carried by or for the god. The god asked the Houndwarden and the Saltkeeper for nothing beyond the requests listed.

## Asked of the god

- A herder of the two clans, month 10 of Year 3, to Tessaly at the margin, put by her to the god: whether it could say what the cough was. Answered this year, month 1, in the ring, by Tessaly in the god's own words, in front of him, as above.
- The herder of the two clans, month 1, in the ring, after the answer, to the ring and to Tessaly and not to the god: that he had asked for a name and been given a smaller thing, and that a keeper that cannot say which beast is hurt is a dog that points at the flock. Not put to the god. Tessaly answered him.
- The herder, month 10, through Tessaly at the margin: "Read my herd at dusk each few nights and say which gathering of it stands hurt in part." Answered: it would, and did.
- The eldest head, month 1, in the ring, not to the god: that a thing given twice is a custom. Not answered by the god.
- The Marchwarden, month 8, at his fire at dusk: "Has the axe passed the third stand?" Answered in front of him: what it read, a little deeper than the last count, and no more.
- The Saltkeeper, month 10, through Tessaly: the god's reading of the party's beasts at dusk at the end of six weeks. Answered by the reading (uncertain outcome 2).
- A young herder of the three clans that camp apart, sent by his own clan, month 8, to Tessaly at the margin for the god: whether the star dimmed because the band was too many to walk as one. The god was away along the timber on the nights he waited and he went home on the third morning with no answer; the question met silence and Tessaly said it would be put when it came back.
- The same young herder, month 10, at the margin before the god with a man of the main band beside him: the same question. Answered in front of him: it knew nothing of the dimming, it had done nothing at those hours that it knew of, and it did not say it was not any god's because it could not say what it did not know.
- The council, month 8, by the Speaker at the first cold: "Would you stand at the hunt's edge and say where the game lay?" Answered in front of the Speaker: it would, and asked the hunt to keep its season.
- The mothers of the three children who died of the flux last year, month 5, to the old women and Tessaly, not to the god: that a keeper that speaks first is better than one that speaks sure. Not put to the god.
- The Houndwarden carried small costs from the dog handlers in the winter months at the god's asking to be told them, and the god heard them. These were not questions.

## Names said aloud

None. Tessaly and Orrin Haddock are names already in my record. No mortal said a name for the people, their home, a camping ground, or an office holder my record leaves unnamed. The Marchwarden, the Saltkeeper, the eldest head of the three clans, the herder of the two clans, the young herder of the three clans, and the Still One's granddaughter remain unnamed.

## What the people tell their god

What they would tell it of their year, in their own words.
- The Houndwarden: "The council did not take your word for the wolves. It gave me leave to send my hunters, twice now, and I acted on the leave. We lost about six calves. The herders drew the calves to the pens by the fires at the dusk you spoke and not after, and they say they should have done it sooner. The eldest head says a thing given twice is a custom. I told you the council took no word of yours so that you would not go about believing it."
- The herder of the two clans: "You said you do not know what the cough is. I asked for a name and you gave me a smaller thing, and I have used it. You named gatherings of my herd and I found coughing beasts in them. We lost about forty this winter, not a hundred. We kept them on our own ground. Some of my clan heard in your words that we are blamed for wintering apart. You said it came hard to us and no other herd took it, and that is so."
- The Marchwarden: "You told me at the first dusk that a herd was hurt and that it was little, and that the water was your guess. I kept the herd off the pool one night and did not move the camp. The next dusk it read resting. The mothers say you were right to say it early. I have notched seven nights of the star this year and cannot find a rule in the numbers. The cutters are a little farther in. The verse for the third stand is still an old lie."
- The Saltkeeper: "We kept the party's beasts apart six weeks, as we meant to, and I asked for your reading of them at the end. We bought salt about half of what we wanted and iron for the edges only, greyer and softer. The towns' oxen still cough. We sent no word this year."
- The Speaker: "I took no vote and I closed nothing. The council took no word from you and gave you none. I am slower. The clans talk of who follows me and have not said a name. The three clans winter apart as they did, within sight of our smoke."
- The apart clans' young herder: "I asked you twice whether the star dims because the band is too many to walk as one. The first time you were not there. The second time you said you knew nothing of it and did not say no. My clan says that is a yes."
- The band: "The star dimmed seven times this year, three of them in the winter while you stood at the margin. We no longer say you hold it off. The apart clans say the band should part at the next going down."
