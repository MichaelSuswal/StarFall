# Year 3 of the Longwalk

Months are counted from the opening of spring: month 1 is the first month of spring. Where a number is mine it is rough, a people's own cord count and not a census. Where the year turns on something the Keeper holds, I write the plain case here and the other branch briefly at the end.

## Spring (months 1 to 3)

The first storm of the year broke over the winter camp in the high wood at the end of the afternoon, and the council sat at dusk in the ring at the main camp before the band went down the Herd Road. The Speaker, the Marchwarden, the Houndwarden, the Saltkeeper and the clan heads sat wet in the ring, and the eldest head of the three clans that camp apart sat among them with two of his men behind him. The keeper stood at the edge of the firelight. Tessaly came into the ring from it and said the keeper's asking slowly and whole, as she said it last year, since she wanted it kept as given: "Last year I told you where the wolves lay on three nights and the hunt waited to see them, and about thirty calves were lost. I ask no seat and no share. I ask that the Houndwarden may act on my word at once for this one thing, the wolves at the calving, and I ask that the council say whether she may. If it says no, I tell her what I read all the same and the choice stays hers."

The ring was quiet longer than it is for most things. The thirty calves were still in every herder's mouth. The heads of the clans whose herds had lost the most said it was a plain thing: the hunters had waited to see the wolves because that is the habit of the hunt, and the habit had cost calves. The eldest head said he did not stand against it. He said it was the first thing the council had given the keeper, that a keeper which asks once and asks small is asking the first of many, and that he wished it written in the ring that he had said so. The Speaker, slow to answer, as he has been since his chest fever, said it was the Houndwarden's to say how the dogs and hunters were sent, that the council took no word from the keeper and gave it none, and that if Tessaly judged a reading good enough to send hunters on at dusk without waiting, the council would not tell her she might not, for the calving only, and would hear her cord of it at the river camp. He took no vote. No clan head stood against it but the eldest head, who had said he did not. Tessaly went out and gave this back to the keeper at the margin in the same words. It said nothing more that night.

The band went down the Herd Road behind the storms, and the calving came behind them. The keeper walked at the rear with Tessaly's dogs and kept the margin of each night's camp, standing still at dusk and before light for a long time and then going to Tessaly. On three nights of the calving it told her wolves lay near the herds, how many gatherings, where and which way. On each of those nights she sent the hunters and dogs to the place the same dusk and did not wait to see a wolf, and told the herders it was her word and the council's leave and not the keeper's order. The first night was the rear herds, the second the east flank, the third a gathering moving along the stream below the stopped camp. In the plain case the wolves were turned each night before they reached the calves and no more than six calves were lost in the whole calving. The herders at the rear said the herds slept better again. Through Tessaly the keeper asked the herders to put apart any coughing beast, as the two clans do. The two clans did, without being asked twice; a few beasts coughed in spring and the cough did not go through the herds.

In the first month the star dimmed, one night, the blue deepening for about an hour and the star standing dim in the middle of the night. The dogs lay down and would not be called. The Marchwarden notched it.

In the second month at the council on the Farrow Grass the Speaker told the ring he would not lean on the keeper and would not be seen to. The talk in the clans after the spring was that the keeper had been given a thing and had asked for it only once. The three clans' young herders said so more warmly than their eldest head would have.

In the third month the salt ran to the bottom of the cord, as it does every spring. The Saltkeeper told the Speaker the trade party must go in the fifth month and take no cattle into the towns' yards, as the Houndwarden and the Saltkeeper had said last autumn. The Speaker said that before it went the council would put a word to it to carry, about the cutters.

## Summer (months 4 to 6)

The band came down to the river under its line of trees, as every summer. There was rain in the summer as in most years, and no month was dry. The pools stood and the river ran. The keeper stood at the stopped camp at dusk and at each pool and stream the Marchwarden named, and read the herds that came down to drink. It told Tessaly each dusk which herds stood hurt after drinking and which wild gatherings, deer and boar and the rest, kept to which water, and it said each time that the water was an inference and not a reading: it read gatherings and not the water. Tessaly passed this to the Marchwarden as it was said, and the Marchwarden, who keeps the songs of the fords, weighed it against them. The song of the ford below the second camp has a verse that says the pool there is sweet in rain and sour when it is stood; he said so in council and that the verse did not say a pool could go bad in a wet year. In the fifth month the keeper told Tessaly that a herd that watered at the pool below the second ford stood hurt after drinking, two dusks running, and that the deer had left it. Tessaly asked the Marchwarden. He said the verse was the verse, and that he would sooner lose a camp's night than a child. The band moved off that pool and the camps moved up the stream. A flux had already taken a few children at that pool in the days before the move. About nine children fell sick and three died, and the band burned the bedding of the camp and boiled the drinking water above the camps by the old women's custom. The band says the keeper's word moved the camp, and Tessaly says the keeper told her a herd was hurt and she and the Marchwarden moved the camp. No one asked the keeper for rain, as none was wanted.

In the fifth month, the first days, the trade party set out for the stone towns of the Hask Ridge by the way the band has always gone, with sound goats and sheep, hides and felt, and no cattle, as the Saltkeeper and the Houndwarden had said. The Speaker had the leader of the party carry a word from the council to whoever of the stone towns it was given to: that the songs of the band hold the axe at the first stands of the winter woods and no farther, that the band had seen the cutters in the winter woods farther in each year, and that the band asked the holds to keep to the first stands. The party did not know who in the towns would hear it.

In the fourth month, at the river camp, the Speaker put to the eldest head, at the keeper's asking carried by Tessaly, the leave for the keeper to keep the margin of the three clans' camp again for a month, on the same three conditions. The eldest head answered in the fifth month, after the party had gone. He said no. He said the keeper had been given a thing by the council in the spring that his people had not given it, that a keeper which keeps the margin of the main camp by the council's leave and then asks to keep his is a keeper being taken in by pieces, and that last year's month had shown nothing but that a keeper may stand at a fire. A woman of his clan, who is not named in my record, said in his hearing that it had kept their dogs quiet and their flocks safe for the month, and he said the flocks had been safe before. The Speaker did not press him. The keeper was told, by Tessaly, and did not go.

In the fifth month the star dimmed twice, some nights apart. The party was on the road for one of them and saw it from the road. The keeper was not at the three clans' fire; whether it stood at the main camp's margin both nights Tessaly could say of one and not the other.

In the sixth month the council sat at dusk at the river camp. The keeper stood at the edge of the firelight, and Tessaly gave the ledger of her cord. The cord for the year held three knots: a boar at the second ford by day, wolves seen at the river by day and not touched, and the pack at the calving, turned. No knot was tied for a thing the keeper did. The council spoke of the calving, and of the pool, and of the leave. The eldest head said again that the first thing was given and that the second had been asked of him and refused. The council made nothing of it and took no vote.

## Autumn (months 7 to 9)

The star dimmed twice in the seventh month, some nights apart, and once in the eighth. The Marchwarden notched them and said it was three the first year, five the second and six this year, fourteen in all, and that the old have no word for it. The apart clans said the star dims when the band grows too large to walk as one, and began to count the band's heads against the nights.

In the seventh and eighth months the keeper went on some nights through the timber along the band's ground toward the winter woods, and was not at the margin. Tessaly marked it and said nothing. In the eighth month it came to the Marchwarden's fire at dusk and told him what it read of the cutters of the stone towns in the winter woods: the gatherings with draught beasts that it read, where, and whether they had moved in since it last counted. It said plainly that past a mile it read gatherings and what state they stood in and not the number of people, and that it kept more than a mile from them. The Marchwarden took what it read to the Speaker, and the Speaker and the Marchwarden took it to the Saltkeeper's fire. The Marchwarden asked it whether it would have the council send men to turn them. It answered that what the council wanted of the cutters was the council's to say, and that it took no trail on a people who had done no wrong. He said he had thought it would say so, and that it was a hard answer for a keeper. The cutters were a little farther in than last year, as the party's word agreed. The Marchwarden said in council that the verse for the third stand was now an old lie and would need to be sung differently, and that he had not wished to live to see it.

The hunt in the timber came with the first cold. The council asked it in few words to stand with the hunters at the hunt's edge and say where the game lay, and it said it would, and asked the hunt to keep its season: no doe with young, and no beast for its hide alone. The hunt kept it, as it always does. Game was found as in most years. The larders were filled better than in the two years before, with the summer's fat on the herds, and not fully. Two hunters were hurt, one by a boar, neither gravely. No share was set on the flat stone for the keeper, and the keeper set none. The share of the first kill was left on the stone and was gone in the morning.

The cough came back in the cattle of the two clans from the seventh month, and more beasts coughed than last year: about fifteen died by the ninth month. The two clans had them apart in a brush pen within two days, as they have learned to, and the keeper told Tessaly of a herd that stood hurt at the far end of the camp, and the clans said they had known.

The trade party came home in the ninth month. It brought salt, about half of what the band wanted for the winter, at no better price than last year, since no cattle had paid for it. It brought iron, and the iron was greyer and softer than the iron of other years: the smiths of the stone towns were complaining of their ore, and said so to the party. The party reported the towns' draught oxen coughing still, and the cutters in the winter woods again, farther in. The party's own beasts, the pack goats and the sheep it brought back, were kept apart on the downwind end of the camp for a month before they joined the herds, which the Saltkeeper had meant to do and said so when Tessaly carried the keeper's asking to it. No beast was bought. The party said the word of the council had been given and that the person it was given to had listened and promised nothing. The band said the sickness in the cattle was in the iron, and that the softness of the iron was the sickness's work.

## Winter (months 10 to 12)

The band went up to the high wood for the lean of winter. The two clans wintered on their own ground at the downwind end of the camp, fed from their own cut browse, as they did last winter, with the coughing beasts in a brush pen at the far end. The Speaker named browse stands to them by cord, which cost the others little. The three clans that want to winter apart wintered apart again, no nearer and no farther than last year, within sight of the main camp's smoke at dusk. The fuel wood was scarcer again, and in the tenth month the Speaker named the stands for each clan by cord, before there could be a quarrel, and the three clans took their cord. No blow was struck this winter.

In the tenth and eleventh months the sickness came back hard in the two clans' cattle. About one beast in six of theirs died. Their own rule is to burn the dead at the far end and not eat them, and they burned them. It stayed in their beasts and no other herd took it, since they had been kept apart on their own ground. A herder of the two clans asked Tessaly, at the margin, whether the keeper could say what the cough was. Tessaly put it to the keeper in the tenth month. The answer is not in my record. By the end of the year it had not gone to the clans.

The Still One sat by the herds of the two clans on two nights in the eleventh month at the two clans' asking, and did not eat the day after each, and was slow among her own for some days after. She said of the herd she sat by that it was not afraid and not listening. It was tired in the chest, as a beast is tired that has been walking uphill a long time. The young are wary of her again.

The Speaker had another chest fever in the twelfth month, shorter, a week. He lived, slower again, and the clans' quiet talk of who follows him has grown louder and no name has been said aloud.

The star did not dim in the winter. The keeper stood at the margin of the winter camp with the horn at its side and a way out behind it, as it has stood the two winters before, and listened at dusk over the herds for the cough and over the winter woods for the cutters. The dog handlers carried Tessaly the small costs of its doing and she carried them to it.

The year ended with the band at the edge of spring on the way down from the winter woods, with the keeper at the margin of the camp.

## If the Keeper rules the other way

The wolves of the calving, if the hunters sent at once did not turn them: about thirty calves lost over the three nights, the rear camp saying that the keeper's word is worth no more at dusk than after, the leave the council gave looked on as a thing that cost thirty calves and not saved them, and the eldest head saying he had said so. Cattle in the state numbers fall by about twenty-four from the plain case.

The word the Speaker had the party carry about the first stands: in the plain case it was heard and nothing was promised. If it was heeded, the cutters would stand no farther in than they stood the year before. If it was not, nothing changes from what is written, which is the cutters a little farther in.

## Causes, in short

- The council gave the Houndwarden leave for the one thing because thirty calves were lost last spring by waiting, and the clans that lost them spoke for it.
- The eldest head refused the margin because the council had given the keeper its first thing, and he reads small asking as the first of more.
- The band moved off the pool because the keeper told Tessaly a herd stood hurt after drinking, and the Marchwarden would sooner lose a night than a child; the flux had already taken three.
- The salt was about half the wanting because no cattle went to pay for it and the price did not fall.
- The cough was worse in the winter than in any year because the two clans' cattle were already weak with it from the autumn. It did not go through the herds because the two clans wintered apart on their own ground.
- The star dimmed six times because the star dimmed six times. Nothing in my record says why.
