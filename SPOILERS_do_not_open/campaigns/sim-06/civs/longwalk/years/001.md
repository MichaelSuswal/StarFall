# Year 1 of the Longwalk

Months are counted from the opening of spring: month 1 is the first month of spring. Where a number is mine it is rough, a people's own cord count and not a census.

## Spring (months 1 to 3)

The band left the winter woods some days before the first morning of the year and came down the Herd Road [f40] behind the first storms, as it does. The calving came behind the storms. The herds were thin from the winter, the dried meat was low, and the salt was short, so the first weeks were lean and quick: the cattle fed on the new grass, the women and hunters took what the road gave, and the Saltkeeper counted the last of the salt by cord and told the Speaker that the cord would run out before midsummer unless the stone towns were met. This is a worry every spring and is felt a little harder this year because the woods near the towns were cut farther in.

The band, stretched out along the road, moved at a herd's pace. The head was a day or more ahead of the rear, the clans strung between.

Two of the nights in the first month the star dimmed, some days apart. Anyone awake under a clear sky saw the blue deepen for about an hour and the star stand dim in the middle of the night, then come back. The old among the clans have seen such nights before and say little. The dogs on those nights lay down and would not be called. In the first of the two nights the Still One did not sleep and sat by the cattle of her own clan and said afterward only that the herd was listening to something and was not afraid.

On the dusk of the fourth day of the year the dogs at the rear of the band fell silent all at once and turned to face back along the trail, toward the wood. The herd beasts at the rear lifted their heads. A girl of about twelve who was minding calves at the margin ran for the Houndwarden. Tessaly came out beyond the last fire with two dogs, who would not go to the end of their cords and sat down. She saw something standing where the firelight ended, low and still, by the first trees of the road's edge. A young herder named for no one in this telling, one of the hunters of the rear, came up with a spear and raised it. Tessaly told him to put it down, once, in a low voice, and he did. The thing at the edge of the light spoke first when she did, in a low even voice, and answered the questions she asked of it plainly. It said it was not of the band, that it came to the margin of the fire, that it was one of the keepers the clans speak of, and that it would keep the herds if the band wished it. She asked what it wanted. It said nothing. She asked whether it would harm the herds. It said it would not. She told it to stay at the edge of the light and that she would carry its word to the Speaker, and it said it would stand where the light ended. She went back, and the dogs got up and followed her, and she told no one but the two hunters who saw, and told them to hold their tongues until she had spoken to the Speaker.

Tessaly sent a runner up the road to Orrin Haddock, the Speaker, that night. The Speaker came back along the line over two days, since he walks slowly and the road was crowded, and met the keeper at dusk at the margin, with Tessaly standing by. He listened, asked what it was, was told, and said that a keeper that came to the edge of the fire would be heard at council like any elder and no more, which is what the clans say. He did not say yes or no to anything else. He told Tessaly that she might let it keep the night watch at the edge of the camp if she wished, since that is the Houndwarden's to say, and that he would put it to the council when the council sat at the stopped camp.

The keeper was therefore let to stand at the margin of the rear camp for the rest of the spring and to keep the night watch with the dogs, on Tessaly's word and not the council's. Wolves came about the rear herds on four nights in the spring. What the keeper's watch did to them, and what they took or did not take on each night, is held for the Keeper's ruling (see the report, uncertain outcomes). If the watch held the wolves off, the rear camp began, quietly, to say that the herds slept better, and no one at the head knew yet. If it did not, a few calves were lost as in any spring and the rear camp said the keeper was no better than a dog.

## Summer (months 4 to 6)

The band came down to the river under its line of trees, as every summer, for water and for fattening. The beasts took the grass. The calves grew. The Saltkeeper's cord ran out in the fourth month, as expected, and the trade party for the stone towns made ready.

A third night of the dimming star fell in the fifth month. The keeper was at the edge of the camp, by Tessaly's report, and she took no note of it. The camp took note of it. By then the whole band had the word that a keeper stood at the rear. The talk was that the dim nights and the keeper went together: the old said a keeper had come on the first night of the dimming, and had been waiting at the wood's edge until the band passed. This is wrong about the order of it, since the keeper came on the first morning of the year, before either dimming, but nobody in the band could have known that, and it has become the way the band tells it.

In the sixth month, at the stopped camp on the river, the council sat at dusk and put the matter to the keeper. Twelve to fifteen of them sat in the ring, and the keeper stood at the edge of the firelight where it had said it would, and would not sit in the ring. The Speaker asked what it was. It said it was one of the keepers they speak of, that it had come to lead them as it was able, and that it would be heard at council and no more. The Marchwarden asked whether it knew the song of the ford below the camp. It said it had learned it in the nights. The council asked whether the wild beasts grown bold near the tents were its doing. It said that where it had spoken with beasts, the boldness might be its doing, and it asked Tessaly to keep a count by cord of every bold beast, where it was and what it took, and said it would answer for that count. The council spoke at length about this. The eldest head of the three clans that want to winter apart said a keeper that asks nothing is a keeper that will want something later, and that the band had done well enough for generations without one. Orrin Haddock said that a guest who has eaten at a fire is safe under it and the keeper had not eaten and was not a guest but a margin keeper, and that the council would hear it when it spoke and not otherwise. No vote was taken. The council did not tell it to go. It did not tell it to stay. The keeper was answered by silence, which in the council is neither yes nor no.

The Houndwarden's cord for bold beasts, by the end of summer, held two knots and a third left open: a fox that took a goat kid from the middle of the herd by day, and a boar that stood its ground against a hunter at the river's edge and then ran. The third knot is for the wolves of the east flank, whose nights are for the Keeper's ruling. Whether these were the keeper's doing or the ordinary doings of the summer, the cord does not say.

Salt: the trade party for the stone towns set out in the fifth month with beasts, hides and felt, by the way the band has always gone. It was away through the high summer.

## Autumn (months 7 to 9)

The hunt in the timber came with the first cold. The council asked, at the Speaker's word and after a short quarrel, whether the keeper would stand with the hunters and tell the Houndwarden where the game lay, and the keeper said it would, and asked the hunt to keep its season. The hunt kept it, as it always does, and there was no more argument about that than there is in any year. Game was found as in most years. The larders were filled enough, not well. Two hunters were wounded, one gravely, who lived. No share was set on the flat stone for the keeper, and the keeper set none and did not look to see who left it; the share of the first autumn kill was left on the stone, as every year, for whom no one says, and was gone in the morning, as every year.

The trade party came back before the going up with salt and iron, at the stone towns' price. The salt was about two thirds of what the band wanted for the winter, and the iron was dear. The Saltkeeper said the price was higher than last year and that the cutting of the upland timber had not stopped. The Marchwarden said the holds would cut the winter woods to stumps within the lives of children now walking.

The coughing sickness in the cattle of the two clans that had it last autumn came back in a small way in the ninth month, a few beasts only. The two clans separated those beasts from the herd, as they had learned to, and lost four.

## Winter (months 10 to 12)

The band went up to the high wood for the lean of winter, to the camps in the timber, for fuel and shelter. The three clans that want to winter apart camped a little apart again, farther than last year. In the eleventh month a quarrel over a stand of fuel wood between one of them and the main band came to blows between two young men, and a boy of the main band was cut across the forearm with a knife. The Three at the Fire named for the matter judged that the knife man owed a share of meat to the boy's hearth for three generations, and that he should walk behind the main band's camp unfed from the common pot for the rest of the winter, as the code requires. He did. The head of the three clans was angry that it was judged against his clan. The three clans did not leave the winter woods and did not rejoin the main camp. The keeper kept the margin of the main winter camp and had no share in the matter; it did not come to the three clans' fire, and no one from the three clans came to it.

The winter was ordinary, with about the usual deaths among the old and among the very young. The herds came through thin but alive. The keeper stood at the margin of the winter camp with its horn still at its side, as Tessaly noted, and with a way out behind it, and the camp has got used to it standing there the way it has got used to a post.

At the end of the year the band stands at the edge of spring again, on the way down from the winter woods, as every year, with a keeper at the margin of the camp that has asked for nothing.

## Causes, in short

- The salt ran short in the fourth month because the winter stores were low and the Saltkeeper's cord was drawn down by the lean spring; the trade party in the fifth month was a day's work late because the cutters were farther into the woods and the way was less certain.
- The three clans' winter apart grew because the wood was thinner, the clans were each more sure of their own need, and the keeper's coming gave their eldest head something to say that the council would hear.
- The keeper was let to stand at the margin because the Houndwarden said so and the Speaker did not forbid it, and the code gives the Houndwarden the dogs, the herds and the signs.
