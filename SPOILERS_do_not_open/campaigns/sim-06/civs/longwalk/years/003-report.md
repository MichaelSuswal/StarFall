# Report to the Keeper: the Longwalk, Year 3

## How the god was met

Spring, month 1, the dusk of the first storm, the council ring at the winter camp in the high wood. The god stood at the edge of the firelight and gave Tessaly its request to be carried into the ring and said by her, in its words: that the Houndwarden may act on its word at once for the wolves at the calving, that the council say whether she may, and that if it says no the god tells her what it reads all the same and the choice stays hers. Tessaly said it whole.
- The eldest head of the three clans said he did not stand against it, that it was the first thing the council had given the keeper, that a keeper which asks once and asks small is asking the first of many, and asked that it be written that he said so. He did not take its word.
- The Speaker, Orrin Haddock, said the council took no word from the keeper and gave it none, that the Houndwarden might send her hunters and dogs on a reading she judged good, at dusk and without waiting to see a wolf, for the calving only, and that he would hear her cord of it at the river camp. No vote. No clan head stood against it. He did not take its word.
- Tessaly gave this back at the margin in the same words. Her taking is limited: for the wolves at the calving, on three nights, she acted on its reading the same dusk by the council's leave and told the herders it was her word and the council's leave. I read this as a taking of its word for that one thing and not as the god's helper being set serving; the Keeper may read it otherwise.

Spring, months 1 to 3, down the Herd Road: Tessaly let it keep the margin of each night's camp at the rear and took its dusk and dawn readings of the herds (hurt, afraid, resting) as one takes a dog's word, not as an order. She passed its asking that coughing beasts be put apart; the two clans did it unasked twice.

Summer, months 4 to 6: it stood at the stopped camp and at each pool and stream the Marchwarden named, told Tessaly each dusk which herds stood hurt after drinking and which wild gatherings kept to which water, and said each time that the water was inference. Tessaly passed it as given. The Marchwarden weighed it against the songs of the fords and, in month 5, moved the camps off the pool below the second ford on its word of a hurt herd. That is a taking of its reading for a night's move, by his own judgment, and not of its word as an order. Nobody asked it for rain.

Summer, month 4, the Speaker put its asking for a second month at the three clans' margin to the eldest head. In month 5, after the party had gone, he refused through the Speaker and Tessaly. The god did not go.

Summer, month 6, the river camp: the council sat at dusk, Tessaly gave the cord, three knots, none the god's. No vote on anything put to it.

Autumn, month 8: it came to the Marchwarden's fire at dusk and told him what it read of the cutters, saying plainly that past a mile it reads gatherings and their state and not the number of people. The Marchwarden took it to the Speaker and the Saltkeeper. He did not take its word as an order.

Autumn, month 8, the first cold: the council asked it to stand with the hunters. It said it would and asked the hunt to keep its season. The hunt kept it. No share was set for it.

Autumn, month 9: the Saltkeeper heard the keeper's asking, carried by Tessaly, that the party's beasts be kept apart a month downwind, said they had meant it already, and did it. That is not a taking of its word.

Winter: it kept the margin of the winter camp. The Speaker, the Houndwarden, the Marchwarden and the Saltkeeper have each heard it speak. None has taken its word as an order. The Still One has not spoken with it. The Runners carried nothing for it.

## What was done in the god's name

Nothing, in the sense of an office carrying out its wishes. Tessaly sent the hunters on its wolf readings on three nights by the council's leave, as above. The Marchwarden moved the camps in month 5 on his own weighing. The Saltkeeper's keeping apart of the party's beasts, the clans' putting apart of coughing beasts, and the Houndwarden's cord are the band's own arrangements. No helper is serving.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper

1. The wolves of the calving, three nights, months 1 to 3. The number of nights is mine from the cord; the release gave no count. Attempt: on each night the god tells Tessaly how many gatherings of wolves lie near the herds, where and which way; Tessaly sends the hunters and dogs to the place the same dusk, by the council's leave, without waiting to see a wolf. First night the rear herds, second the east flank, third a gathering moving along the stream below the stopped camp. Success: the wolves are turned before they reach the calves and no more than about six calves are lost over the three nights; the herders say the herds slept better. Failure: about thirty calves are lost, the leave is looked on as a thing that cost calves, the eldest head says he had said so. Circumstances from my record: last year the watch did not turn them and about thirty calves were lost; last year the hunt waited to see the wolves, and this year it goes at the dusk of the reading; the dogs fall silent and turn to the god for what it is; the herds are thin from winter and the calves newly dropped; the hunters are told where and which way and go the same dusk; Tessaly is slow and sure with the dogs. I wrote the year with the success branch and the failure branch briefly after it in `years/003.md`. State carries both.

2. The council's word to the stone towns, carried by the trade party in month 5. Attempt: the party's leader says to whoever in the towns it is given to that the band's songs hold the axe at the first stands of the winter woods and no farther, that the band has seen the cutters farther in each year, and that the band asks the holds to keep to the first stands. Success: it is heard and heeded, and the cutters come no farther in than they stood the year before. Failure: it is heard and nothing is promised, or it is not heard. Circumstances from my record: the band is a trading band whose party has gone every year with goods, hides and felt; the holds sell salt and iron dear and cut the winter woods; the band believes the miners were once bondsmen who walked out on a master and owe everyone a fair price and give no one one; the party was met and returned as every year. Your release has the cutters a little farther in again, and salt no cheaper; I wrote the plain case, the word heard and nothing promised, and the failure line in `years/003.md`.

## What turns on a power

1. The god's readings of wolves near the herds at the calving: where, how many gatherings, which way. I wrote only that it told Tessaly and what she did. Whether the readings were as it said is the Keeper's.
2. The god's reading in month 5 of a herd hurt after drinking at the pool below the second ford, two dusks running, and the deer having left it. I wrote that it told Tessaly and that the camps moved. Whether a gathering at a pool reads to it as drinking or as grazing, and whether the reading marked a fouled pool, is for the Keeper (the god's own question 1 in its decisions). Three children died of the flux there before the move; I took the flux and the numbers as mine. The band says the keeper's word moved the camp.
3. The god's reading of the cutters in months 7 and 8: the gatherings with draught beasts, where, and whether they had moved in. I did not write a count, a distance in or what they take; the god or the Keeper is to give it. The Marchwarden took it to the Speaker and the Saltkeeper.
4. The reading of coughing beasts in months 7 and 10: the god told Tessaly of a herd hurt at the far end of the camp in the autumn and the clans said they had known. Whether its reading let it tell a herd that coughs from one that does not is the Keeper's, as last year.
5. The six dimmed nights of the star, one in month 1, a pair in month 5, a pair in month 7 and one in month 8, an hour each, the blue deepening and the star dim in the middle of the night. Fourteen since the keeper came. The band ties it to the keeper or to the band's numbers. I wrote only what they saw and said. Where the god stood on each of those nights, Tessaly could say on some and not on others; I wrote no more than that.
6. The dogs fell silent and turned to the god, as before, on its coming to the margin each year. I took the Keeper's earlier ruling and did not write any doing.
7. The Still One's reading in month 11 of the two clans' herd: not afraid, not listening, tired in the chest. This is her ordinary gift and I did not decide what the beasts were suffering.

## What touches anyone else

- The trade party. Set out in month 5 for the stone towns of the Hask Ridge [r20] by the way the band has always gone, with sound goats and sheep, hides and felt and pack goats, and no cattle. It carried the council's word about the first stands (uncertain outcome 2). It came home in month 9 with about half the salt the band wanted at no better price, with greyer and softer iron, having been told by the towns' smiths that their ore was bad, and having seen the towns' own draught oxen coughing still and the cutters in the winter woods again, farther in. It bought no beast. Its pack goats and the sheep it brought back were kept apart a month on the downwind end of the camp, as the Saltkeeper meant, and the god's asking was carried by Tessaly. It saw one of the pair of dimmed nights of month 5 from the road. I wrote nothing of the towns or of the road beyond what it reported.
- The cutters. The god read them in months 7 and 8 and told the Marchwarden, who took it to the Speaker and the Saltkeeper. The council had its own word carried by the party. The god took no trail on them.
- The three clans that camp apart. The eldest head refused the god a second month at his margin in month 5. They wintered apart again, no nearer and no farther than last year, and took the Speaker's cord for fuel wood. No word was carried between them and the god.
- The two clans whose cattle cough. They wintered apart on their own ground at the downwind end of the camp, on their own browse, with the coughing beasts in a brush pen, so that it stayed in their beasts: about fifteen died by month 9 and about one in six of theirs, about a hundred, in the winter. I took their herds at about six hundred head by their own cord. They burned the dead. The wintering among the gathered herds did not arise, and I have no second branch for it.
- No stranger came to the fires. No one was sent anywhere but the trade party. No message was carried by or for the god. The god asked the Houndwarden, the Speaker and the Saltkeeper for nothing beyond the requests listed.

## Asked of the god

- The council, month 1, by the Speaker through Tessaly, in answer to the god's request: leave for the Houndwarden to send hunters at once on its wolf reading, for the calving only. This is the council's answer and not a question. The god heard it at the margin from Tessaly the same dusk and said nothing more.
- The eldest head of the three clans, month 1, in the ring, not to the god: that the leave was the first thing given and that a keeper that asks small is asking the first of many. Not answered by the god.
- The eldest head, month 5, by the Speaker and Tessaly to the god: no, to a second month at his margin. The god did not go.
- The Marchwarden, month 8, at his fire at dusk: "Would you have the council send men to turn them?" Answered in front of him: what the council wants of the cutters is the council's to say, and the god takes no trail on a people who have done no wrong.
- The council, month 8, by the Speaker at the first cold: "Would you stand with the hunters at the hunt's edge and say where the game lay?" Answered in front of the Speaker: it would, and asked the hunt to keep its season.
- A herder of the two clans, month 10, to Tessaly at the margin, put by her to the god: whether it could say what the cough was. The answer is not in my record. It had not gone to the clans by the end of the year.
- The mothers of the three children who died of the flux, month 5, to Tessaly, not to the god: that the keeper should have said sooner. Tessaly said it could not have known sooner and did not carry it. Not put to the god.
- The Houndwarden carried small costs from the dog handlers in the winter months at the god's asking to be told them, and the god heard them. These were not questions.

## Names said aloud

None. Tessaly and Orrin Haddock are names already in my record. No mortal said a name for the people, their home, a camping ground, or an office holder my record leaves unnamed. The Marchwarden, the Saltkeeper, the eldest head of the three clans, the woman of the eldest head's clan, and the Still One's granddaughter remain unnamed.
