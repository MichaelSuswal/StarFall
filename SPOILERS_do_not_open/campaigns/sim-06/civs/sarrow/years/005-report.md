# Report to the Keeper: the Sarrow, Year 5

## How the god was met

Autumn, month 1, first days. At Marle Ford [f61] the older toll watcher gave her the count from the knotted cord: trains of other peoples as last autumn, shorter, fewer beasts; no river train since the tenth month of last year; no salt train yet. He said the word she sent the king was given to a train master and nothing had come back. He took her word and was not scolded. At the toll house table she ate with a master of another people's train and asked him over bread why the river's trains did not come and what his people say of the star, saying she asked for his telling and no more. He answered at length and slowly, looked at the key, and stopped twice; the watchers have none of it. She did not use the Reading.

Same days, at the gate and the hall. The gate guard said a single word and stood aside. At the hall, with Kessan, Idris Veld, the Steward, the Marshal and the Mother of the Hearth, she said first that three had asked her in the summer and not been answered and that it was her doing and not theirs. She answered each. Kessan, riders beyond the houses: yes, on terms, his riders and his choosing, held to the terms; he said he was glad to be asked to choose and took the terms. The Steward, salt: the order for the whole winter, the price his to name; he asked what to do if a master asked more and was told to name it; he took the order and carried it out. The Mother, the households: yes, tell them, at the hall fire on the first night of the cold, with her standing beside; the Mother took it and said it was more than she had asked. At the same sitting she left the standing answers (Idris for the rite, the households and the stores; Kessan for the road, the passes and the riders; both-touching waits). Idris and Kessan said they would hold to it; the Steward wrote it on the cord.

Month 1, the next days. She gave the captain her word for the king aloud, and the terms to Kessan before Idris. She gave the Steward her bag of salt, all but one handful, in the open before Idris; he weighed it, said it was less than a poor household's, and entered it. The Marshal told the order in the open at the ford and sent it to every house; Kessan, Idris and the Marshal each took her word. The Steward gave way once on a beast of salt at the first train and said so; the second train took his price.

Month 3. The two counts, the ration, the seed sealed under two marks with Idris witness. She told the eight captains herself before the snow, thanked them for three winters and asked for a fourth. Four had no argument; one said a band that eats last four winters will ask something one day; the youngest said nothing; Kessan said the band would hold the passes. First snow, the names of the winter's dead said by Idris, about a hundred and fifty, the Guest standing through them. By the decisions she set the seat and unlatched the warlord's door beside Kessan's household; I write nothing of whether she came to the table. At the hall fire the Mother told the households and the Guest stood beside her and said the part about the grass; the old women argued it all night; nobody said she lied.

Month 4. She sat at the low quarter tables, one hearth a week chosen by the Mother. She told the hall she did not know what the herders' words meant, in front of Kessan, Idris, the Steward and the Marshal, when two herders of the upper columns came down and said the line stood, the flower did not come, and a stone cut to count a line does not hold it. The Steward asked what a hall does with a line it cannot hold; Kessan said the hall had heard it, which was more than it had done in forty summers; the Doorwardens said it was the toll. No envoy came to be read.

Months 5 and 6. No word of hers in my record on the stores. A sill matter on salt at the hall door in month 5, heard by Idris and Kessan with her sitting by; their judgment is theirs.

Month 7. At the hall, in the open, she told Kessan, Idris and the Marshal that she may stand at a toll house door on any train day without word and why. Kessan said the far houses were the Marshal's trade and he had never counted them and said so now. Idris would carry it to the Doorwardens. The Marshal said it was one more thing he had not been asked, and then sent the word to every house by rider in the same hour, as his own act. At the ford the Marshal asked her in front of the older watcher whether the river's coughing beasts were to be penned apart from the herds; the decisions give her no word and I have none; he penned them apart on his own order and said so.

Month 8. The sitting over the cords of the first trains with Kessan, Idris and the Guest, the Steward, the Marshal and the Mother called in. Kessan and Idris settled that the toll stands at a knot and a quarter the beast and that salt trains pass free to first snow. She spoke at the table; her word is hers. On the nineteenth day, at first light, she went into the ford and was not on the far bank (the older watcher saw it, the younger ran for the hall, the hall said it was what she had said she might do). Kessan sent two spears to the ford house for the night. At the farthest house, see the next paragraph.

The farthest door, month 8, the nineteenth day, the hour after first light. A train stood at the door. The keeper had been told by the Marshal's riders in month 7 what every keeper was told, that she may come on any train day without word, and had been told no more. She stood in his threshold where there had been nothing but road; his wife set the pot down on the step; the younger watchman stepped back and the older did not. She told him to his face what she did and why, that she accused no man, that she would stay the day, and that if he bade her to his table she would eat knowing she could not lie until she rose. He kept his hands open and said it was a house of the Sarrow and a guest might stand in its door unasked, that his cord had nothing in it the knots did not say, that she might count against it, that the house would keep the day as any train day, and that he would not hurry a master for her. He bade her to his table at midday with his wife, his younger son's wife and her two children, and the older watchman, broke bread first and ate first. A girl of about six asked her what she had had for her dinner; the answer is hers. Asked whose goods were carried in and how long they stay, he said the goods are the trains' own, a night's feeding is given and the smokehouse is for that feeding, the cord holds the toll by the beast, none turned back at his door, none spoke of another road, and a toll house cannot count what it does not see. She stayed the day in sight of the door and touched nothing; the household say her hand moved once and she said aloud that it was moving and put it down. At dusk they set her a bed by the fire and she did not lie in it. At first light after the full day she was in the threshold, thanked him for the bread, and was not in it. She told what she found to Kessan, Idris and the Marshal together before anyone else; what she said is hers. They took her word and the rest depends on the Keeper.

Months 9 to 12. She stayed within two days of the hall, at the ford, the gate, Stair Pass and Far Notch. In month 11 she went up once to the cairns, left the standing answers again aloud before the five, was gone a week, saw the line at both cairns and the bare cutting, told the herders she did not know what it meant. A herder said it was the first time a god had come up twice to a stone and said she did not know. Three questions came to the hall while she was up; see Asked of the god. She came down, was at the ford before the snow, laid no seventh blessing and did not step again.

## What was done in the god's name

- The Steward, with Idris witness, carried out the salt order: the two counts at the lower, the ration cut by an eighth, the salt first to the meat put by, the pot and bread next, the rite last; the offerings to the common stock on the separate cord; the Guest's bag entered; two small salt trains bought from at the price he named, one beast at the master's.
- The Marshal, by the Guest's order, told the masters at the ford in the open that salt trains pass free, and sent it to every house with the order to send word of any train that carries salt or grain; the grain first order stood.
- The seed sealed under two marks, with Idris witness; the seal broken at the sowing in the open.
- Kessan, Idris and the Marshal named the party beyond the houses, as the Guest said, and the Steward gave the goods. They carry the Guest's word and her terms.
- The Mother told the households at the hall fire and in the low quarter, with the Guest present at the first and by Idris's word at the second.
- Idris said the names of the winter's dead at the first snow.
- The herders were brought to the hall in month 4 by the Mother and the keepers of rite.
- Under the standing answers, month 11: Idris answered in the Guest's name that the Mother was to tell the households the salt would not reach first snow, plainly, with the Steward's number; Kessan answered that the herds of the upper columns might stay a week at the high grass to the first snow of the heights.
- Not in her name: the Marshal sent the word that she may stand at a toll house door on any day to every house alike, as his own act; the Marshal penned the river's coughing beasts apart below the ford on his own order, and took on himself the master of the river with the coughing beasts when Kessan and Idris answered differently. Kessan and Idris at the sitting settled the toll and the free passage of salt trains to first snow, as their own acts.
- Orders for the year's end by horn and smoke, the Marshal's riders and the keepers of rite.
- Nothing else was done in her name by the helpers.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper

1. The winter stores to the thaw, months 3 to 7. Attempt: the people stretch the stores to the thaw by the ration cut by about an eighth from the first snow, set at the lower count, the poorest hearths cut by about a tenth and the band's houses by about a fifth, the seed grain and barley kept whole under the two marks, a small cull and hunt, and the salt, which went first to the meat put by, with the passes shut and no train to buy from and the thaw at its ordinary time. Success (Branch A): the stores last to within a few days of the thaw, the seal is whole at the sowing, the sowing is full, and about a hundred and fifty to two hundred die over the ordinary, the old and the youngest. Failure (Branch B): the stores fail some weeks short, the herd and the dried meat go, the seal is broken in the last week on part of the seed, the dead run some hundreds over, the sowing is short. Circumstances from my record: the grain, barley and dried meat are about ten days short at the full ration by the Steward's count and about sixteen by the mothers'; the cut began at the first snow and covers the lower count's gap by my arithmetic and not more; last summer's harvest was ordinary and last spring's sowing was full, so the grain is better than the winter before, when the gap was twenty and twenty five and the same kind of cut lasted to within days; the herd is small after three winters of culling; the salt in store is about half a winter's at the old measure, the meat that cannot be cured is the weak point of the mothers' count, and the Steward's count assumed salt at about the old measure; the far houses' loan is owed, the nearer's two days, repaid in the thaw; the cold is an ordinary winter; the old and the youngest go first. If the riders bring salt (item 2) it adds to the stores and the Keeper should move the branch. Written both ways in the year file and state.

2. The riders beyond the houses, from the farthest house. Who: a captain of the middle of the eight, about forty winters, hard to anger, not at the far houses (named by Kessan); a keeper of rite with the river speech, the one who read the king's letter (named by Idris); four of the Marshal's riders, the senior first (the one who carried the far cords and the loan), and a guide, an old packer of the lower columns, past sixty, who went with trains down the road north-east and along the river as a young man (named by the Marshal). Seven in all, on horse, with four laden beasts: felt on two, bar iron on one, toll cloth on one, and fodder. They carry the Guest's word to the king, in the words given in the year file, to be given into the hand of the first officer of the king or clerk of the chancery who will take it, and to wait for an answer as long as a guest may be asked to wait. Their leave: the Guest's terms, no blood, no seizing of any train or house, no taking of any thing unasked, no oath sworn in her name beyond the guest oath of the table; they say whose riders they are if asked, that they keep the sill and came as guests, and do not say her size, her place, her need or what she can do; if turned away they do not press; if held they ask for a guest's meal and a hearing at the sill and send word by anyone who will carry it; they are home before the deep snow or winter where they are given guest right and send word by the first road open in the thaw; if they find a house that sells salt they buy only what they can carry and bring it to the Steward, and they do not sell her toll to get it. They set out from the winter city in the first days after the goods were tied, by the road north-east and the river, for the river kingdom if the road runs there; I give no days for the ride. They left the farthest toll house on Pass Road on the morning of the twelfth day of month 1, fed by the keeper's household, and passed beyond. Success and failure are the Keeper's to define: success would be the word given into an officer's or clerk's hand, an answer or a telling brought home, the three things learned (why no river train crossed in months 10 to 12, where salt may be had and at what price, and what is said of the star), and the party back before the deep snow or word sent; failure would be the party turned back, held, lost or silent. Circumstances from my record: the Sarrow have never sent riders beyond the houses in my record; the river kingdom's trains stopped for three months and came back at half with sick beasts; the king's letter claims a share and was answered with a refusal and a promise of a hearing; the Guest's folded word went in Year 4 into a clerk's hand and drew no answer; the road is the Pass Road and then the road north-east and the river; the guide knows it from his youth, not recently; the party carries little to trade and the hall cannot spare more; salt is short on every road. Written both ways, briefly, in the year file: Branch R1, home before the deep snow with news; Branch R2, not home, word at the thaw or none. The whole of what they meet and are answered is the Keeper's.

3. The day at the farthest door, month 8, the nineteenth day. The people's side is written in full; what the Guest sees, what the masters of the train do, whose train and what it carries, what the cord for the day holds against what she counted, and what her powers do are the Keeper's. The hall's spring and the Marshal's standing turn on it. Branch D1: nothing for the sill. Branch D2: a second trade, the sill sits at the hall door with the keeper heard and her view last, Idris and Kessan judging together; the size of what was found sets the remedy, written in the year file as small or large. Circumstances from my record: the keeper is courteous, unhurried, with his hands open; his house has new roofs of split timber, good horses in the pens and full smokehouses, and has been walked three times and marked once; his cord had nothing in it the knots did not say; he sent words only for the loan; the nearer house sent two days; his watchmen have told one story each time (roofs mended by the carpenters of the trains, horses toll in kind for the riders and the hall, smokehouses full for the night's feeding, no train turned back, none spoke of another road); the Marshal stands under question over the farthest house and walked it himself last year with his cap in his hand; every house had word that she may come on any train day without word; Kessan said he never counted the far houses.

4. The second step. The Guest put the question to the Keeper in her decisions: whether the second step, from the farthest door back to the ford, is a step from a blessed threshold she holds in full, so that the ford stands open a second night. I record only that she was in the door at first light after a full day and was not, and that the older watcher at the ford kept the ford the night she was gone as every night and said in the morning that nothing came by it that he did not count. Whether the ford stood open that night, and whether it stands open a second night, is the Keeper's.

## What turns on a power

- The ford and the first step, month 8, the nineteenth day: what the Step did and what it showed the older watcher (he saw her in the crossing and not on the far bank), and whether the ford stood open that night.
- The farthest door: everything above, and the sixth blessing's knowledge of when a train stands there.
- The table's bar on lying: at the farthest keeper's table, at the low quarter tables in winter, at the hall tables. The households and a child tested it.
- The Reading: not used. No envoy came and no hand frightened her.
- The Breaker's Hand: not used. The household say her hand moved once at the door and she said so aloud and put it down.
- The Latch and the blessings: she laid no blessing this year, gave the Steward her bag but for a handful, and laid no seventh blessing.
- The star dimmed three nights (months 2, 2 and 7). The people tie them to the toll, the grass, the dead, the seat she set, and the lack of a blessing. The Keeper's line stands: not known to be anyone's doing.
- The blue flower, bare again, and the line standing at the first notch a second year. Whether the stones or her coming had any part is the Keeper's. The herders say a stone cut to count a line does not hold it.
- The cough of the river's beasts: not a power in my record, and not mine to cause. I record that no cough was brought to the Marshal from the Sarrow's own herds by the end of month 11. If the Keeper means it to reach them, it is the Keeper's.
- The seat at first snow: by the decisions she set it herself and unlatched the door; I write nothing of whether she came to the table.

## What touches anyone else

- The party beyond the houses (item 2): seven riders and a keeper of rite, with the Guest's word, four laden beasts, and the terms, to the river kingdom by the road north-east and the river.
- The river kingdom. Trains came back from the thaw at about half the number before the toll was raised, paying a knot and a quarter the beast, grain first. Their masters said the toll was not what kept them; some beasts coughed; two trains came short of beasts and loads and hired Sarrow carriers from the ford to take the rest over Stair Pass for a share in kind, taking no toll on loads not carried, by the old sill custom. A master of one of the first trains told the older watcher that the Guest's folded word of Year 4 was given into a clerk's hand at the chancery in month 2 of that year and that no answer was given him. Their coughing beasts are penned apart below the ford, by the Marshal's order; a master with coughing beasts was fed at the toll house and sent on without them in month 11, by the Marshal's order, while the question waited.
- Salt trains: two small trains of other peoples came before the snow and sold salt to the hall at a price the Steward named, one beast at the master's price; they crossed without toll. The masters told it on the road. The hall bought no salt after the snow; the order stands to first snow of this year by Kessan's and Idris's word at the sitting.
- Trains of other peoples crossed as last year.
- No envoy of the river king came. No stranger out of the ordinary came. Ordinary travelers brought to the Guest, none placed: a boy walking to kin with a potter's tools, a woman carrying a loom, an old man who sang at the sill, two carters from the plain.
- The farthest toll house: the party passed it, the Guest stood in its door, and its keeper's household saw it.
- The Marshal's word that the Guest may stand at any toll house door on any train day went to every house by rider in the same hour.

## Asked of the god

- Kessan, in the summer of Year 4, by the Marshal's rider on the grass: "May I send my riders beyond the houses to learn why the river's trains do not come?" It had met silence. Answered in front of him in month 1, at the hall: yes, on terms, held to them.
- The Steward, in the summer of Year 4, by the same rider: "What is the hall to do if no river train comes before the first snow and the salt runs short?" It had met silence. Answered in front of him in month 1: the order for the whole winter, and he carried it out.
- The Mother of the Hearth, month 11 of Year 4, by a keeper of rite: whether the households were to be told the salt would not last the winter at the ration. It had met silence. Answered in front of her in month 1: yes, tell them, at the hall fire on the first night of the cold, with the Guest beside her.
- The Steward, month 1, in substance: what to do if a master asks more than the hall will pay. Answered in front of him: he names the price, the hall buys only at it.
- Two herders of the upper columns, month 4, at the hall, said what the line did; the Steward asked, in substance: what is a hall to do with a line it cannot hold. Not answered in my record; the Guest told the hall she did not know what it meant.
- The Marshal, month 7, at the ford, in front of the older watcher, in substance: whether the river's coughing beasts were to be penned apart from the herds. The decisions give her no word and I have none; he did it on his own order.
- The hearth mothers, at the hall fire, the first night of the cold, month 3: nothing put to her; she said the part about the grass.
- The keeper of the farthest toll house, month 8: nothing put to her; she told him what she would do, and he said a guest may stand in its door unasked. A girl of about six at his table asked her, in play, what she had had for her dinner. Answered in front of the table, true or silent; the answer is hers.
- While she was up at the cairns in month 11, three questions came to the hall and met her absence: (1) the Mother, by a keeper of rite, whether the households were to be told the salt would not reach the first snow, which Idris answered in the Guest's name under the standing answers; (2) the Marshal's rider, by Kessan, whether the herds of the upper columns might stay a week at the high grass, which Kessan answered under the standing answers; (3) the ford's watchers, by the Marshal, whether a master of a river train with coughing beasts, who would not leave them penned apart, was to be turned from the sill. Kessan and Idris answered it differently, said the question was for the Guest, and it waited; the Marshal took it on himself. It stays pending until she answers it. The Guest was told when she came down; what she answered is hers.
- A herder at the cairns, month 11, in substance: whether he was to be glad that a god comes up twice to a stone and says she does not know. Not answered in my record.
- The hearth mothers' old women: whether a Guest who has come once must be bidden each winter, argued among themselves, not put to her.

## Names said aloud

- "Mother of the Door": said by the Guest aloud in the hall in month 1 in the words of the folded word for the king, to Kessan, Idris Veld, the Steward, the Marshal and the Mother of the Hearth, and by the Guest herself in giving it to the captain. It is the name she gave in her own word to the king, with "Guest": "I have no name to sign. They call me Guest, and Mother of the Door."
- "Guest": said by the people of her.
- Kessan, Idris Veld, Marda: already in the record.
- The Steward, the Marshal of the Road, the Mother of the Hearth, the senior and younger Doorwardens of the lower columns, the first captain, the captain of the riders beyond the houses, the guide, the keeper of rite who went with the riders, the older and younger toll watchers, the keeper of the farthest toll house and his household and watchmen, the senior of the Marshal's riders, the two herders who came to the hall, the oldest herders: not named aloud. They remain unnamed.
- The river kingdom's king and chancery: not named aloud, except as "the king" and "the chancery".
- No name for the winter city or for the people's home was said.

## What the people tell their god

We are thin, and we are still here. We counted twice before the first snow, the Steward and the hearth mothers, after the two small trains of salt had come over. The Steward's count was ten days short of the thaw at the full ration and the mothers' sixteen, and we set the share by the mothers', cut by an eighth, the poorest by a tenth, the band by a fifth, and the Mother told every hearth why before it was done. We sealed the seed under two marks, and the Mother holds her half. We said the winter's dead aloud at the fire, a hundred and fifty, fewer than the winter before, and you stood through it. The band ate last a fourth winter, and one captain said it will ask something one day, and the youngest said nothing. We stretched the stores toward the thaw as we could. We tried it, and we have written no number for the end of it.

Salt is short on every road. Two small trains came over before the snow. The Steward named a price and held it, and gave way once on a beast of the first, and he does not like it. We gave your bag to the stock and the Steward says it was all but a handful. We gave the salt offerings to the common stock, as you said. The pot was thin of salt all winter and the bread thinner. At midsummer the Steward's tally says the salt will not reach the first snow at the ration, and the Mother has told the households.

You told us at the hall and at the hearth mothers' fire that you were on the grass when we asked and that it was your doing and not ours. The old women argued it all night, and nobody said you lied. We had the Mother tell the households before the pot did, and we heard you say it beside her.

Kessan sent riders beyond the houses as guests, with your word for the king. The captain, a keeper of rite, a guide and four of the Marshal's riders went by the road north-east and the river, with felt, iron and toll cloth for salt. They left the farthest toll house on the twelfth day of the first month and went beyond it. We tried it. We tell you only that they went.

The river's trains came back at the thaw, about half as many as before the raise, and paid the toll as it stands. Their masters said the toll was not what kept them. Some of their beasts coughed, a dry cough, and two trains came short of beasts and hired our carriers from the ford. A master told the older watcher your word was given into a clerk's hand at the chancery in the second month of last year and that no answer was given him. The Marshal penned the coughing beasts apart below the ford on his own order, and we have had no cough in our own herds by the end of the eleventh month. No envoy came.

The herders came down to the hall in the cold and said the line stood where it stood last year, the flower did not come, and a stone cut to count a line does not hold it. You said you did not know what it meant. The herders cut their notch at the first snow of the heights at both cairns, and the line stood the second year. You went up to the cairns in the eleventh month for a week and saw it and said you did not know.

You went into the ford at first light on a train day in the eighth month and the older watcher saw you not come out the far bank, and the hall said it was what you had said you might do. The keeper of the farthest house found you in his door, with his wife's pot on the step, and kept his hands open, and bade you to his table with his house. He said his cord has nothing in it the knots do not say, and that a guest may stand in its door unasked, and that a toll house cannot count what it does not see. We tell you what he did and said and no more. You told Kessan, Idris and the Marshal what you found before you told anyone, and you did not tell us.

The star dimmed three nights, fewer than last year, and none in the winter. Some say it is resting and some say it was the seat you set. The cranes went over in the third month. We have not spoken of the counted road this year.

We set the seat and left the door unlatched at the first snow, and we will again.
