# Report to the Keeper: the Sarrow, Year 2

## How the god was met

Autumn, month 1. At Marle Ford [f61] the older toll watcher gave her the count of who had crossed since she was last there, from the knotted cord: down a little on last year, the trains as many or near it but short, fewer beasts to the train. He put it down to a poor year on the river, which a trader told him and which he cannot check. She asked how the trains came and did not scold. He took her word. Two travelers were fed a night at the toll house and she saw it done. What the count showed against what she knew by the blessing is hers.

The same day, at the city gate. The senior of the sworn guard heard her answer in few words (that she woke by the road in the dark before first light and went to the nearest door, and did not know why that was the hour). He took it as weather. Asked who kept the gate at night and whether it had changed, he said the sworn band in pairs with a Doorwarden's boy at each post, changed only in that nobody on the gate sleeps now. The answer went round the city: the Guest does not know. The households liked it, the Doorwardens called it a Guest's way of speaking, some captains said a god who does not know can be argued with.

Month 1, at the hall. The Steward was told to his face, with Idris Veld present, that she would rather a short true count than a kind one and did not call last year's a lie. He took it, said he would give a count he could stand behind and that the hearth mothers should have their own, and carried out the two counts. He gave a salt measure smaller than last year's and said why before she asked. She took it.

Month 2, at the hall, with Kessan, Idris Veld, the Marshal and the Mother of the Hearth. Her four questions on the herder who died of wolves. Kessan answered the first himself: the watch was his captain's and so his, the failure his. The captain said the dogs were late because the marches were hard and the dogs fed thin. The Mother said the dogs eat from the same stores as the people. Kessan asked his eight captains: four columns had set boys on the night watch with one grown man among them on some nights, none with boys alone. The Marshal said his riders saw it and did not think to say. The dead man's household (widow, two children, his mother) was put by the Mother at a low quarter hearth that keeps it. Idris fixed the price of his death by the old sill table, paid from the captain's column's herd. The Steward was ordered to give the winter keep on the Guest's account and entered it on a separate cord, not knowing how it is to be paid. Kessan took the orders against boys alone on the watch and the Marshal's riders carried them. The captains complied and grumbled to each other that the grown men for the night watch come out of the spears. Each office holder took her word.

Month 3. The two counts came to the Mother of the Hearth. The Steward's: a week to spare at the thaw at full ration. The hearth mothers': about eleven days short. Gap about eighteen days. The Steward explained most (seed barley counted as stock, damp-spoiled meat in the low granary counted by tally, the band's hay counted as fuel) and could not explain some days. The sharing by hearth from the first snow was ordered, the poorest first, the war band's houses last. She told the captains the reason herself. Two of eight said it was better than a late blow. The captain who lost the herder said nothing. Kessan said the band could eat last for one winter. The Steward said in private it would be worse, and carried it out.

First snow. The seat was set, the door unlatched, the bowl filled, by custom. The decisions give her no step for that night and I write nothing of whether she came to the seat.

Winter, month 4. The Mother of the Hearth chose the low quarter tables, about a dozen through the cold, the first the widow's household, one in each quarter a captain's. She was told the terms beforehand (eats, cannot lie at a table where she has eaten until she rises, no Reading) and the whole city knew them within the week. Households say of the star: it dims when a door is blessed; it is a lamp and her coat passes across it; it dims when someone dies who should not have; it blinks and sleeps in the cold; something passes before it. She told each table she does not know whose it is. Some took it as true and were more afraid, some as a silence kept. Kessan sat at her right at the hall table, was told he would not be read again unless he asked it or crossed the sill with a hand that frightened her. He gave the band (about three hundred and sixty), the passes (shut by snow as usual) and the toll cords, thin, near half of the old cords for a generation back, and said he never counted the far toll houses, which are the Marshal's. He took her word and did not accuse anyone. The Doorwardens took his seat at her right as a slight. Idris, at her left at the table, said the right hand of the table is the host's and of the sill the rite's, and that she never supposed otherwise.

Month 5. The senior Doorwarden of the lower columns came to the hall door, heard with Idris at her side. He said a rite held by four is held by four, the lower columns have no one with the oath from her mouth, rank spreads a rite, sense ends when the one who has it ends. He asked that it be taught to him or one of his. He took the answer (no one new this year) standing, and said he would see her at the lower columns' fires in summer. Idris said he was right about half and proud about all. In the deep snow she ate at Idris's table and asked what Marda Sill is, a place, a person, a rite or a word. Idris answered what she knew and did not ask why. She did not ask whether it was hers. Nobody was sent to find out.

Month 6. The sharing held to the thaw. The war band ate last and lean, from the beginning, and grumbled and held.

Spring, months 7 and 8. At the thaw she went to Far Notch [f63] with the Marshal and a few riders, on foot, laid salt and spoke the oath. The riders were uneasy and quiet. Asked which toll houses were thin and which honest, the Marshal named the ford and the two nearest as thin and poor, and the two farthest as honest and rich. They walked the road to the northeast house by house. At the two farthest, the Marshal and the Guest saw new roofs, good horses and full smokehouses. The watchmen, courteous, told it as one story: roofs mended by the carpenters of the trains in payment of lodging; horses toll in kind from the Aldermark traders, kept for the riders and the hall; smokehouses full for the night's feeding the order requires. Their cords agree with the riders' tally and with the hall's cords, as they showed them. The Marshal had not walked those houses in some years, said a Marshal has riders for that, stopped saying it, and went quiet. He brought her seven strangers in the spring, all ordinary.

Month 9. The columns went up. The Guest did not go with Kessan. The star dimmed.

Summer. She travelled with the lower columns and sat at their fires. The senior Doorwarden was courteous and no more, and did not speak of the oath. In the tenth month a herder at a rite fire asked her, in front of the fire, whether the cold is coming down, since the blue flower was not on the cutting. The oldest woman of the column said no flower means no summer's height. In the last month a younger Doorwarden of the lower columns asked her to judge a matter of grass between two households, and the senior did not stop him. I wrote no one for the Reading.

Month 12. The orders for the year went out by horn and smoke, the Marshal's riders and the keepers of rite: every column down by first snow, the Marshal to confirm each by rider.

## What was done in the god's name

- The captains' orders against boys alone on the night watch, given by Kessan and carried to the columns by the Marshal's riders by the first relay.
- The sill price for the dead herder, fixed by Idris Veld and paid from the captain's column's herd.
- The Steward's winter keep to the dead man's household, entered on a separate cord on the Guest's account. How it is to be paid is not known to him.
- The two counts, the Steward's and the hearth mothers', and the sharing by hearth from the first snow, carried out by the Steward and the hearth mothers, with the Mother of the Hearth at the head of it.
- The tables of the low quarter, chosen by the Mother of the Hearth.
- The toll houses' feeding of any traveler a night and honest counts, kept by the Marshal's order.
- Seven strangers brought to the Guest by the toll watchers.
- Orders for the year's end by horn and smoke, the riders and the keepers of rite.
- Nothing was done in her name by the helpers beyond her own orders.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper

1. What the Guest and the Marshal find, or make of what they find, at the two farthest toll houses. Attempt: the Guest, the Marshal and a few riders go up the road to the northeast on foot, in the spring after the thaw, to the two farthest toll houses and ask for the counts. Success would mean they learn whether the new roofs, the good horses and the full smokehouses are what the watchmen say, and where the toll goes. Failure would mean they leave with the watchmen's word and the cords as given, knowing no more. Circumstances from my record: the ford's own tally is down a little; the toll at the hall is near half of the old cords a generation back; the traders came as last year; the Marshal had not walked those houses in some years, and the near houses are the thin ones by his own telling; Kessan never counted the far houses; the watchmen show cords that agree with the riders' tally and with the hall's; the Marshal's own order to feed a traveler a night gives them a ready cause for the smokehouses. If it is learned that tolls are held back there, the hall's thinness has a cause in the Marshal's own trade, the Marshal's standing falls, and Kessan is asked what he knew. If not, the thin toll stands as the traffic, and the new roofs are a thing the Marshal's riders told in the low quarter and the Marshal keeps his office and his silence. Both are written briefly in the year file as one account, since the year does not turn on it.

2. The trip itself. The party set out for the two farthest toll houses on the road to the northeast, on foot, after Far Notch. I give no distance or time. I wrote it as done between the thaw and the columns going up in month 9. The Keeper prices it. If it takes longer than that, the walk is later or cut short, and the watchmen's tale at the far houses is told to the Marshal's riders instead.

3. The verdict of the grass dispute in the last month, which a younger Doorwarden of the lower columns asked her to judge. I did not write it. It is hers.

4. The hall verdict on the price for the dead herder's death. This is Idris Veld's, by the old sill table, not an uncertain outcome. I give it as a fact of the people and not of the god.

## What turns on a power

- The Far Notch blessing, spring: what the Latch did there and what it let her know of who crosses. Year 1 ruled that each blessing holds. Nothing in my year turns on it.
- The ford blessing and what she knew of the crossings, set beside the watcher's count in month 1. What she made of it is hers.
- The table's bar on lying, at the low quarter tables and at Idris's table. The households tested it. At Idris's table she asked and did not tell why she asked. Idris did not ask whether it was hers. If Idris had, the answer would have been that she did not know, or silence, by the decisions.
- The star dimmed five nights this year, in months 2, 6, 9, 11 and 12. The people tie them to her, to the blessings, to the dead herder, and now the Doorwardens count five dimmings against five blessed thresholds. Last year's Keeper line says they are not known to be anyone's doing. My year turns on it only in what the people say.
- The blue flower missing from the cutting at the high gates in month 10. A herder asked her at a rite fire whether the cold is coming down. Whether the flower, the dimmings and the gates are connected to any power is the Keeper's. My people say what they say, and are wrong about something.
- First snow, month 3. The decisions give her no step. If she came to the warlord's seat that night, it is in the god's file.
- The Reading of the Host: not used. At no table.
- The Breaker's Hand and the Step: not used.

## What touches anyone else

- The trains from the river kingdom crossed and paid as last year, a little down by the ford's tally. The Aldermark traders talk about a poor year on the river, or one trader did, to the older ford watcher.
- Horses taken as toll in kind at the two farthest toll houses, from the Aldermark traders, by the watchmen's word. The carpenters of the trains, who mended the roofs there by the watchmen's word.
- Seven travelers brought to the Guest: a carter with six fingers on one hand, an Aldermark clerk with oddly spoken river speech who could not eat the barley, a woman with a milk-white eye, two plainsfolk boys running from a master, a man who sang at the sill, a smith with a limp, a woman with a goat on a rope. All fed a night and sent on. None placed as what the god looks for.
- The party set out for the two farthest toll houses (see above). No other party left the Sarrow's country. No message was carried by anyone. The counted road did not come.
- The Guest returns to the ford before the snow, after the year's close. The columns are turning toward the winter ground.

## Asked of the god

- The senior gate guard, last year: "Why do you come before the snow?" Answered in front of him in month 1: she woke by the road in the dark before first light and went to the nearest door, and does not know why that was the hour.
- The Mother of the Hearth, last year: that the Guest sit at the households' tables in the low quarter. Answered in the winter in front of her: yes, one hearth a week, chosen by the Mother and not by Idris Veld or the Steward.
- The senior Doorwarden of the lower columns, last year, to the rite fire: why he was passed over for sense. Heard at the hall door in month 5, in front of the god.
- The senior Doorwarden of the lower columns, month 5, at the hall door: that the oath be taught to him or to one of his. The decisions say she chooses no one new this year. The words she gave are not in my hand.
- A household at the low quarter, month 4, in their own words, in substance: whether she was hungry. Answered in front of them, true or silent. What the answer was is hers.
- A man at another low quarter table, in substance: whether the bowl at first snow was hers. Answered in front of him, true or silent. What the answer was is hers.
- Households at the low quarter tables: whose the star is. Answered in front of them: she does not know.
- A herder of the lower columns at a rite fire in month 10, in substance: "Is the cold coming down?" The decisions give her no answer for it. Not answered in front of them in my record. She listened and he did not ask a second time.
- A younger Doorwarden of the lower columns in the last month: that she judge a matter of grass between two households of his column. The decisions provide for it. The verdict is hers and not written here.
- The Steward, privately to Idris, in substance: what the Guest's account is and how it is to be paid. Not asked of the god. Not put to her.

## Names said aloud

- "Guest": by the people, of her, and by her of herself last year.
- "Marda Sill": said by Idris to the Guest at her own table in the deep snow, month 5, in the telling of what she knew. Already in the record.
- Kessan and Idris Veld: said in the hall in month 2. Already in the record.
- The Steward, the Marshal of the Road, the Mother of the Hearth, the senior Doorwarden of the lower columns, the senior gate guard, the older toll watcher at the ford, the captain of the column that lost the herder, the dead herder and his household, the watchmen at the two farthest toll houses and the younger Doorwarden who asked her for a judgment: not named aloud. They remain unnamed.
- No name for the winter city or for the people's home was said.
