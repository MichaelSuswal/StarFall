# Report to the Keeper: the Sarrow, Year 4

## How the god was met

Autumn, month 1, first days. At Marle Ford [f61] the older toll watcher gave her the count from the knotted cord: trains as many as last year or near it, shorter, fewer beasts. She asked how the toll was reckoned in his father's time, by the beast, the load or the head, and for a train of today's size, and said she did not accuse the toll house; she wrote his answer on a cord of her own and held it as his answer and no more. He said by the beast, a knot a beast, a short knot for a man carrying, the head never tolled, only fed; the knot unchanged until the raise made it a knot and a half; he could not price a train of today's size since his father's were larger. The younger watcher said by the load, from his grandfather, a half laden mule paying by half, and that he did not know which was older. Both said the head was fed and not tolled. Asked whether any train this season was met at the ford by someone other than a watcher, both said no. They took her word and were not scolded.

The same day, at the gate, the guard bade her in with no held breath. That evening she sat alone with Kessan at the hall table, with bread, the door shut, Idris Veld outside it with a Doorwarden's boy. Kessan came out and said nothing, and at his own fire told his captains only that she had told him first what she would say, had not called the raise a theft, meant to keep his road and his spears and would not read him unless he asked. He took her word, and said it was a courtesy.

The next day at the hall, before Kessan, Idris Veld, the Steward, the Marshal and the Mother of the Hearth, she answered three asked things. The Steward: the shortfall of the offerings is drawn from the hall stores and entered as the Guest's debt, cleared first from the next offerings; she takes nothing for her own table; she will give him a number at first snow. He said it was a debt he could write and wrote it. The Mother of the Hearth: the toll is the sill's to keep and the host's to take; a raise is the host's to propose and the hall's to hear, with Kessan, Idris and her at one table, not made by one mouth; Kessan's raise stands this year; after the first trains of the thaw the three sit, and if the trains have fallen by more than the raise bought, the toll comes to where it was or to what the cords bear. The Mother said it was the first time she had heard a host's word held to a count, and carried it to the households. Kessan, told the evening before, kept his face. The keeper of rite who asked about the letter, by Idris's carrying: yes, and not by a clerk, and the sill hears a claim made in person; she told the five the words of the folded word aloud, with Mother of the Door at its end. Kessan said it was what he had said with more door in it. Idris said the sill is a door. The Mother said the name low and was heard. The Marshal and the Steward said nothing. The Marshal asked what he was to do if a house did not send; she said tell her which, she accuses none.

Month 3. The two counts. The Steward gave a hard number first: about twenty days short at the full ration; the mothers about twenty five. He explained three of five days and said he could not explain two. The Mother said it did not matter whose was nearer. The ration was set at the lower: cut by a quarter from first snow, the poorest by a fifth, the band by a third. The seed was counted apart, sealed under the Steward's mark and the Mother's with Idris witness; the Steward said the order of failing, herd, dried meat, seed last, aloud; the Mother said she would not give up her half of the seal. She told the eight captains herself, before they heard it, the sharing and the third winter, and thanked them. Three said they had no argument, one said three winters would leave the band thin at the passes, the youngest said the thanks were the first the band had had and he would not call it nothing. Kessan said the band would hold the passes on it. The Marshal told her which far houses sent and which did not. First snow: Idris said the names of last winter's dead, about three hundred, at the rite fire with the seat set; she stood through all of it, did not sit or eat; she gave the Steward the number for the Guest's debt at the fire. Nothing is written of whether she came to the warlord's table that night.

Month 4. She stayed in the winter city and did not step, sat at the low quarter tables one a week chosen by the Mother, and Kessan sat at her right. Each took her word. No envoy came, so the Reading was not used.

Months 5 and 6. No word of hers in my record on the stores.

Month 7. At the hall she told Idris why she would lay a sixth blessing on the farthest door; Idris carried it to the Doorwardens; the Marshal said at his own fire it was one more thing he had not been asked. She walked with the Marshal and a few riders to the farthest toll house. At its door she told the keeper what she would do before she did it: salt on the threshold, the oath with the key in her hand, no shutting, no step, to know by her own marking how many go in and out and not to accuse him. He said it was a house of the Sarrow and a guest might stand in its door, and that his cord had nothing the knots did not say. He, the watchmen and the Marshal stood with hands open. She laid the salt and spoke the oath. She asked the watchmen what the trains said of the toll and whether any turned back or took another road; they said the river's trains complained openly and paid, none turned back at their door, none spoke to them of another road, the faces missing were the river's, trains of other peoples came as before. She looked at what the house held of grain and said no word of it. The Marshal said nothing at all.

Month 8. The three sat with the cords of the first trains: the far houses' trains down by a third from last spring, the river's trains paying with open complaint. Kessan said that a half more on two thirds of the trains came to no more than the old toll on all of them, and that the raise had bought nothing this spring that the old toll would not have. Idris said the cords did not say why the trains fell and that the sill takes one toll and not two. Between them the two mortals settled half the raise given back, to a knot and a quarter the beast, from the trains of month 9, the grain first order standing and a sitting again at the first trains of next spring. The Steward said the stores did not stand on half a raise. The Mother said the carriers who are Sarrow would feel it. The Guest spoke at the table, and her word is hers and not in my hand.

Month 9. She went up with the upper columns, the ones she had not sat with, and told the Marshal and Idris she would sit at their fires.

Months 10 to 12. At the old cutting and one other she asked the oldest herders about the line; told them what she found last year and that she did not know what it meant; asked, through the Mother and the keepers of rite, for a cairn and a notch at the foot of the line, a second notch at the first snow of the heights, and the line's doing told to the keeper of rite. A herder asked what the stone was for; she said to count the line and not hold it. At each new fire she asked once what is meant by "as Marda held it" and Sill and held each answer as that fire's. The hall's questions to her met silence. Month 12, she came down with the upper columns and stood at Marle Ford before the snow. I write nothing of her words.

## What was done in the god's name

- Idris Veld carried the folded word for the king down to the ford by a Doorwarden's boy with the Guest's terms (waived toll, give to the first master going home, keep it dry through the snow, bring it back if refused, say nothing of her or the host). The decisions give no step for how it reached the watcher; I wrote it by Idris.
- The older toll watcher gave the folded word to a master of a river train and waived that train's toll in her name.
- The Steward and the hearth mothers' two counts, the ration at the lower count, the sharing by hearth from first snow, carried by the Mother of the Hearth.
- The seed sealed under two marks, with Idris witness.
- The Marshal's order: toll in grain and seed barley first, word of every grain train; and his riders to the two farthest houses with the loan, asked in her name.
- The names of the dead said at the first snow by Idris, from the Mother's list.
- The low quarter tables one a week, chosen by the Mother.
- The cairns and the notches at the line of the grass, cut by the herders of the upper columns through the keepers of rite and the Mother.
- The Marshal's standing order that odd strangers be brought to her: three ordinary travelers, none placed.
- Orders for the year's end by horn and smoke, riders and keepers of rite.
- Kessan and Idris Veld at the toll table acted for themselves; the toll settlement is the mortals' act, not the god's.
- Nothing else was done in her name by the helpers.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper

1. The winter stores to the thaw, months 3 to 7. Attempt: the people stretch the stock to the thaw by the ration cut by about a quarter from first snow set at the lower count (poorest hearths by a fifth, band by a third), the seed kept whole under seal, a smaller cull and hunt, and the loan grain of the far houses, with the passes shut and the thaw at its ordinary time. Success: the stock lasts to within a few days of the thaw, the seed seal is whole at the sowing, and the dead run a hundred or two over the ordinary, the old and the under fives. Failure: the stock fails some weeks short, the herd and then the dried meat go, the seal is broken in the last week on part of the seed, the dead run some hundreds over and the sowing is short again. Circumstances from my record: the stock is about twenty days short at the full ration by the Steward and about twenty five by the mothers; the cut begins at first snow, not in month 5 as last year, and covers by my arithmetic the lower count's gap and not more; last year the cut began in month 5, covered about half the gap and the stores failed some weeks short; the herd is smaller after two winters of culling so less is left to eat first; last spring's sowing was short and the seed drawn by a third, so this autumn's harvest was thin; the toll in kind is grain first from the autumn, though the trains thinned from the thaw by a third and none of the river's crossed in the summer; the loan is in or not by item 2; the cold is an ordinary winter; the old and the under fives go first. Written both ways in the year file and state.

2. The loan from the two farthest toll houses, month 1 to month 3. Attempt: the Marshal sends the senior of his riders and two others to the two farthest houses on the road to the northeast, the hall asking in the open a share of grain and seed from the stores and smokehouses as a loan to be returned from next year's toll, accusing no house. The riders set out in month 1 and are back by month 3; the Keeper prices the ride. Success: both houses send a share of grain and seed the Steward counts as about a week of the full ration, and the loan is entered. Failure: the nearer house sends a little, about two days' worth, and the farthest sends words only, that its smokehouse is full of meat for the night's feeding, that its store is the autumn toll not yet collected, and that it will send at the spring collection. Circumstances from my record: the two houses have new roofs of split timber, good horses in the pens, smokehouses full with racks hung thick, and cords that agree with the riders' tally and the hall's; the watchmen at each said as one story that the roofs were mended by the carpenters of the trains for lodging, the horses were toll in kind kept for the riders and the hall, and the smokehouses are full for the night's feeding the order requires; the Marshal called them honest and rich and went quiet when he saw them; the first captain and the Marshal found nothing they could prove last year; the toll is grain first this autumn; the autumn trains crossed as last autumn; the order accuses no house. In Branch B the Marshal's standing is under question again, and Kessan's.

3. The walk to the farthest toll house, month 7. The party (the Guest, the Marshal and a few riders) sets out on foot for the farthest house on Pass Road in the Kennoch Heights [r02], short of the road's far end. I give only the Keeper's own line: the whole road to its far end from the winter city is 68.2 miles and five days on foot, and no other figure. The Keeper prices the walk. I have them at the house in the first weeks after the thaw and back before the sitting.

4. The folded word to the river king. Not an attempt of mine up to the moment it leaves: the older watcher asks two masters; the first would not take it ("a king's business, I am a carter") and was fed and not pressed; the second, with a mixed train and a lame mule, took it for the waived toll, asked who it was for, was told only that it was a word of the Sarrow to the one who wrote to the ford, said he knew the road to the chancery and would give it to a clerk there, and put it inside his coat. The train left the ford that morning by the road north-east and the river. What becomes of it on the road and at the chancery is the Keeper's.

5. The toll sitting, month 8, as the mortals settled it. Kessan and Idris settled the half of the raise given back, to a knot and a quarter the beast, from month 9, with a new sitting at next spring's first trains. The Guest spoke at the table and her word is hers. If her word changes the rate or the grounds, the Keeper settles it; the mortals' act stands as written otherwise.

## What turns on a power

- The sixth blessing, the farthest door, month 7. What the Latch did and what it let her know of who goes in and out. The mortals there saw salt, the oath, the key, the keeper told first. Nothing among the Sarrow turns on it except the Marshal's standing in Branch B.
- Her word at the toll table in month 8.
- The first snow, month 3: the seat set at the fire and the door of the warlord's house unlatched; I write nothing of whether she came to the warlord's table.
- The star dimmed seven nights: months 2, 3, 4, 8, 10, 11, 12. The people tie them to the toll, the grass, the dead and the blessings. The Keeper's line stands: not known to be anyone's doing.
- The blue flower, bare again. The cairn and notch at the line of the grass: the second notch showed the line standing where the oldest say it stood last year, no higher. The herders say a stone cut to count a line does not hold it. Whether the Guest's presence or the stones had any part is the Keeper's.
- The table's bar on lying: at the low quarter tables in the winter; the households tested it. Nobody tested her this year to my record beyond the usual.
- The Reading: not used, since no envoy came. The Breaker's Hand and the Step: not used.
- The silence of the hall's questions to her on the grass: that she is away is the people's account, and they know only that they were not answered.

## What touches anyone else

- The river kingdom. The folded word left the ford with a train master of that kingdom in autumn (item 4). The river's trains crossed in autumn as last autumn, paid the raised toll in the spring with open complaint, and in months 10 to 12 none came at all; trains of other peoples came as before. No envoy came. The hall does not know why and has told itself several things (see lore), all of them guesses. No party was sent out beyond the houses, since Kessan's question to the Guest about sending riders met silence.
- The toll. Raised by half from the Year 3 thaw, halved at the sitting from month 9, to a knot and a quarter the beast. Taken in grain and seed barley first, before cloth, iron or coin, from the autumn, with word of every train that carries grain. The traders who carry cloth and a little barley for their own beasts found their barley taken first.
- The loan from the far houses, by branch; a loan owed to them from next year's toll.
- The farthest toll house, marked by the Guest. The watchmen and the keeper saw it. They said the river's trains complained openly, none turned back, none spoke of another road.
- Strangers: three ordinary travelers brought to the Guest in the summer, a salt peddler of the plain, two sisters walking to kin, an old man who sang at the sill. All fed a night and sent on. None placed. No stranger out of the ordinary came.
- The counted road did not come.
- The Marshal's riders carried the hall's questions up to the Guest on the high grass, and came back with no answer.

## Asked of the god

- The Steward, month 1 (asked in Year 3): "And if what is brought is less than the cord?" Answered in front of him at the hall: the shortfall is drawn from the hall stores and entered as the Guest's debt, cleared from the next offerings.
- The Mother of the Hearth, month 6 of Year 3, in the hall: whether the toll was the host's to raise or the sill's. Answered in front of her, month 1: the sill's to keep, the host's to take; a raise is the host's to propose and the hall's to hear, not by one mouth; Kessan's raise stands this year; the three sit after the first trains.
- The keeper of rite who read the letter, at the fire in Year 3, in substance whether it should be answered. Answered through Idris in month 1: yes, not by a clerk, and the sill hears a claim face to face; she will send word under her own hand.
- The Marshal, month 1, in substance: "And a house that does not send?" Answered in front of him: tell me which; I accuse none.
- The Steward, month 1: what number to draw. Answered at first snow, month 3, at the rite fire: she gave him the number. The figure is hers.
- The older toll watcher, by Idris's boy, month 1, in substance: what to do if the master will not carry it, or if none leaves. Answered by the terms she sent (bring it back; keep it dry to the thaw).
- The keeper of the farthest toll house, month 7, nothing put to her; she told him what she would do, and he said it was a house of the Sarrow and a guest might stand in its door.
- The watchmen of the farthest house, month 7, nothing; asked by her.
- A herder at the old cutting, month 10, in substance: "What is the stone for?" Answered in front of him: to count the line, not to hold it.
- At each new fire of the upper columns: she asked what Marda and Sill mean, once each. Not asked of her.
- A household at the low quarter tables, winter, in substance: whether she was hungry and whether the cold is coming down. Answered in front of them, true or silent. The answers are hers.
- Kessan, month 10, by the Marshal's rider up to the column where she was, in his words as he gave them to the rider: "May I send my riders beyond the houses to learn why the river's trains do not come?" She was away, on the high grass with the upper columns. It met silence, and stays pending.
- The Steward, month 10, by the same rider, in substance: "What is the hall to do if no river train comes before the first snow and the salt runs short?" Met silence, pending.
- The Mother of the Hearth, month 11, to a keeper of rite for the Guest, in substance: whether the households are to be told the salt will not last the winter at the ration. Met silence, pending.

## Names said aloud

- "Mother of the Door": said by the Guest in the hall in month 1, in the words of the folded word, to Kessan, Idris Veld, the Steward, the Marshal and the Mother of the Hearth; and said again, low, by the Mother. It is the name she gave in her own word to the king, with "Guest": "I have no name to sign. They call me Guest, and Mother of the Door."
- "Guest": said by the people, of her, and in her own words.
- The names of the winter's dead of Year 3, about three hundred, by hearth, said by Idris Veld at the rite fire at first snow, month 3, in the hearing of the people at the fire and of the Guest. I record none of them one by one.
- Kessan and Idris Veld: already in the record.
- Marda: said by herders at the fires of the upper columns when she asked what Marda is. Already in the record.
- The Steward, the Marshal of the Road, the Mother of the Hearth, the senior and younger Doorwardens of the lower columns, the first captain, the youngest of the eight captains, the older and younger toll watchers, the keeper of the farthest toll house and its watchmen, the senior of the Marshal's riders, the two train masters, the keeper of rite who read the letter, the herder at the cutting and the oldest herders of the upper columns: not named aloud. They remain unnamed.
- The river kingdom's king and chancery: not named aloud in my hearing, except as "the king" and "the chancery".
- No name for the winter city or for the people's home was said.

## What the people tell their god

We are thin, and we are still here. We counted twice before the first snow, the Steward and the hearth mothers, and the mothers' count was the lower, and we set the share by it and cut it by a quarter from the first snow, and told every hearth why before it was done. We sealed the seed under two marks, and the Mother holds her half. We said the winter's dead aloud at the fire, three hundred, and you stood through it. The band ate last a third winter, and the youngest captain said the thanks were the first they had had.

The Marshal sent riders to the far houses for the loan, as you said, in the open, and did not accuse any. [Branch A: Both houses sent, and the Steward counted it as a week of the ration, and it stands on a separate cord. Branch B: The nearer sent a little, the farthest sent words, and the Marshal told you which.] The stores held [Branch A: to the last days before the thaw, and the seed seal was whole when we broke it at the sowing] [Branch B: some weeks short; we ate the herd and the dried meat, and in the last week the Steward and the Mother broke the seal on a part of the seed, as you said it would be the last of all, and the sowing is short again]. [Branch A: A hundred or two more of the old and the youngest died than in an ordinary year. Branch B: Some hundreds more of the old and the under fives died than in an ordinary year.]

You walked to the farthest door and laid salt and the oath on it before the keeper's eyes, and told him first. He said his cord had nothing in it the knots did not say. We saw the same roofs, the same horses and the same full smokehouses. The watchmen said the river's trains complained loudly and paid, and none turned back at their door.

Kessan, Idris and you sat with the cords of the first trains and the trains were fewer by a third. Kessan said the raise had bought nothing this spring that the old toll would not have, and Idris said the sill takes one toll and not two, and the two of them gave back half of the raise, to a knot and a quarter the beast, from the ninth month, and the grain first order stands. We do not know what you said at the table, and you have not told us.

The older watcher at the ford gave the word you sent to a master of the river's trains, after one would not take it, and waived his toll in your name, and the train left the ford for the river kingdom by the road north-east and the river. After that we do not know. No one of the king's came. And then in the summer no train of the river kingdom came over the road at all, and the trains of other peoples came as before. We do not know why. The Marshal says a king who sends a letter in the month the toll is raised and keeps his trains home in the summer the toll is lowered has watched the ford. Kessan asked you by a rider on the grass whether he might send riders beyond the houses to learn why, and the Steward asked what to do for salt if none comes before the snow, and the Mother asked whether to tell the households the salt will not last. You did not answer on the grass. We were not answered, and we waited. Salt is short.

The herders cut a notch at the cairns at the old cutting and the other cutting, and the second at the first snow of the heights, and the line stood where the oldest say it stood last year, no higher. The flower did not come. The herders say a stone cut to count a line does not hold it, and the households do not hear them.

The star dimmed seven nights, the most yet. Some say it dimmed for the toll and some for the grass and some for the dead. The cranes went over in the third month. The counted road did not come.

We have set the seat and left the door unlatched at the first snow, and we will again.
