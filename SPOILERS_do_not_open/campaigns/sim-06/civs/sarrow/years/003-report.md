# Report to the Keeper: the Sarrow, Year 3

## How the god was met

Autumn, month 1. At Marle Ford [f61] the older toll watcher gave her the count from the knotted cord: trains as many as last year or near it, shorter, fewer beasts. He said the traders speak of another poor year on the river and he cannot check it. He took her word, was not scolded, and she saw two travelers fed. At the city gate the senior of the sworn guard did not hold a breath; he said "Guest" and stood aside. Nobody on the gate sleeps.

Month 1, at the hall, with Kessan, Idris Veld, the Steward, the Marshal and the Mother of the Hearth. She answered three things aloud. The Steward was told the dead herder's account is hers and is paid from the offerings at the hall and the rite fires, no more than he enters on the separate cord; Idris entered it and stood witness; the Steward said he was glad to be told, and asked what to do if the offerings fall short, and no answer was given in front of him. The Mother of the Hearth was told that the Guest does not know whether the cold is coming down and will go up and look; she carried it to the herder that day. The Steward was told she does not call his count a lie and would have a short true count over a kind one. Each took her word.

Month 2. The matter of grass between two households, at the sill table of the lower column's standing house, with the younger Doorwarden, both households and the captain of the column. She ate at the table. The line she set is hers and not in my hand; the captain confirmed it aloud. One household said it was fair and did not like it. The senior Doorwarden of the lower columns said it was settled as it should be.

Month 3. The two counts. The Steward gave a hard number first: about twelve days short of the thaw at the full ration. The hearth mothers: about twenty. Gap about eight; he explained six and could not explain two. The Mother said the mothers' was nearer. Both said no sharing would make the stock last, only spread it. The stores were shared by hearth from the first snow, poorest first, the war band last; she told the captains herself and thanked them for last winter. Two of eight had no argument; one said a band eating last two winters would sleep on the road; Kessan said it would hold the passes. The cords of every toll house, the far houses' too, were laid out in the hall by first snow. Sitting by: Kessan, Idris Veld, the Marshal, the Steward (who read the knots aloud), and the senior rider who carried the far cords. They agreed with one another by season and with what was laid up at the hall. The Steward said so twice. Kessan said the far cords were as neat as any. The Marshal said "As I told her." She said nothing at the table. What she made of them against her own count is hers.

Month 4. The senior Doorwarden of the lower columns came to the hall door, was told in the open that the Reading would be used on him once and that he might refuse the table, stood a long while, sat and ate. Idris sat beside her. He walked home and told no one in the hall what passed. At his own fire he said he had been read in front of the woman who keeps the high rite and that it was done as it ought to be done. Kessan sat at her right hand and was told the Doorwardens' slight at it is theirs and not his. He took it.

Month 5. The Steward read the cords aloud unasked: short of the thaw. The Mother and the hearth mothers cut the share by a fifth. No word of the god's on any of it in my record.

Month 6. The toll order, below under power and under outcomes. She was at the hall table and gave no word on the toll. Kessan gave the order and did not ask her leave. The Steward backed it, the Marshal said the traders would pay with complaint, the Mother said the small trains and the packers, who are Sarrow, carry it hardest, and asked in her hearing whether the toll was the host's or the sill's. Idris said the host's to raise and the sill's to hear. No answer from the god in my record.

Months 7 and 8. She travelled with the lower columns and did not walk the road. The letter was brought to the column where she was, by the Marshal's rider, and read aloud at the rite fire by a keeper of rite with the river speech, in front of the column. What she said is not in my record. Kessan and Idris had answered by the clerk's mouth by then or after, as below.

Months 10 and 11. At the cutting she looked and asked the oldest herders about the line of the grass; she told the herder who asked in Year 2 what she found, plainly, and that she did not know what it meant. He said it was something to be told. At each fire she asked once what is meant by "as Marda held it" and by Sill and held each answer as that fire's. Month 12: orders down, ford before the snow.

## What was done in the god's name

- The Steward's separate cord for the dead herder's household, paid from the offerings, entered by Idris Veld.
- The two counts, the sharing by hearth from the first snow, the ration cut by a fifth in month 5, carried by the Steward and the hearth mothers under the Mother of the Hearth.
- The cords of every toll house brought by the Marshal's riders by first snow.
- The walk to the far houses by the Marshal and the first captain, by her word given at the hall through the Marshal and Kessan.
- The sill table for the grass matter, set by the younger Doorwarden and confirmed by the captain.
- The oath: I have it as nothing taught or laid by any Doorwarden in her name this year, and no lesser mark laid by anyone. The senior Doorwarden's table in month 4 is as above and its upshot is hers.
- Orders for the year's end by horn and smoke, riders and keepers of rite.
- The Marshal's standing order: five strangers brought to her, all ordinary.
- Nothing else was done in her name by the helpers.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper

1. The winter stores, months 5 to 8. Attempt: the people stretch the stock to the thaw by a ration cut of about a fifth from month 5, a heavy cull of weak stock, a hunt by Kessan's riders (three ibex and a deer), and the seed barley drawn down by a third, with the passes shut and the thaw at its ordinary time. Success: the stock lasts to within a few days of the thaw, with a hundred or two deaths over the ordinary, mostly the old and the under fives. Failure: the stock fails some weeks short, the last fortnight is eaten on dried meat, the herds and seed, and deaths run some hundreds over the ordinary, the spring sowing short. Circumstances: stock about twelve days short at the full ration by the Steward, about twenty by the mothers; the cut covers about half the gap by my arithmetic; the sharing began at first snow and the band ate last; last winter ended with the last barley eaten three days after the thaw; the cold was ordinary; the old and the youngest go first; no train can come. The year is written both ways in the year file and state.

2. The far toll houses. Attempt: the Marshal and the first captain, Kessan's choice, walk to the two farthest houses on the road to the northeast and the two near ones on the way back, in the first weeks after the thaw, on foot, with two riders and no word sent. They count beasts, sacks, racks and roofs, set each cord beside the riders' tally and ask each watchman where the new horses were bought and with what. Success (they learn what is held back, if anything): the counts differ from the cords, or the watchmen's answers differ house to house. Failure (they learn nothing amiss): the counts agree with the cords and the story is the same. Circumstances: Year 2's walk found the roofs, horses and smokehouses and the Marshal went quiet; the cords agree with each other and the hall's; the hall's toll stays near half of the old cords at last year's rate; Kessan never counted the far houses; the watchmen are courteous and prepared; the Marshal did not send word because he was told not to; Kessan chose the captain for his dislike of the Marshal's riders. If found, the Marshal is asked what he knew, and Kessan. If not, the Marshal keeps his office. The year is written both ways briefly. The walk's price is the Keeper's; I give no days or distance.

3. The grass line in month 2. The verdict is the god's and not in my hand, as last year. Not an uncertain outcome of mine.

4. The Reading of the Host on the senior Doorwarden in month 4, and whether the oath was taught: see below.

5. The letter. The people's answer to the claimant stands as Kessan gave it and Idris gave it. Whether the claimant or the chancery meets it is the Keeper's.

## What turns on a power

- The ford blessing and the five thresholds: what she knows of crossings against the watcher's count and the cords laid in the hall. I write only that the cords were laid out, who sat by, and what they said. What she made of them against her own count is the Keeper's. Nothing in my year turns on it among the Sarrow, but the Marshal's standing in Branch B does.
- The Reading of the Host on the senior Doorwarden in month 4, in the open, before Idris. What it showed, and whether he was taught the oath as the decisions have it, are the Keeper's. The people know he sat, ate, was read and walked home.
- The star dimmed six nights: month 1 (one), month 5 (a pair), month 7 (a pair), month 8 (one). The people tie them to her, to the blessings, to the toll, to the grass. My year turns on it only in what they say. The Keeper's line from Year 2 stands: they are not known to be anyone's doing.
- The blue flower, missing again at the cutting. She looked and told the herder she does not know what it means. Whether it connects to any power is the Keeper's.
- The table's bar on lying: at the sill table in month 2, at the senior Doorwarden's table in month 4, and at the fires of the lower columns when she asked what Marda is. Nobody tested her this year to my record.
- The Breaker's Hand and the Step: not used. First snow, month 3: I write nothing of whether she came to the seat.

## What touches anyone else

- The trains from the river kingdom crossed and paid the raised toll after the thaw with complaint, as last year in number or near it. One trader asked at the sill whether a toll could be raised by half in a year the ford was thinner. He was heard and fed.
- The letter. A clerk of the first train after the thaw, in the seventh month, gave it to the older toll watcher at the ford; the clerk read it aloud at the sill in the river speech; no one at the ford can read. The younger watcher ran to the hall with the heart of it. Kessan answered by the clerk's mouth at the train's next crossing in month 9: that the Sarrow owed no share to a king they had not met, and that a guest of the chancery would be fed at the sill and heard like any guest. Idris Veld's word to the clerk alone: the sill would hear a claim made in person and eat with the claimant. The clerk said he would carry both. Nothing else is carried out of the Sarrow's country. No party was sent out of it.
- Five strangers brought to the Guest: a carter with one arm, an Aldermark trader's wife with the river speech and no Sarrow speech, two plainsfolk brothers with a sick ox, an old man who sang at the sill, a smith with a burn scar. All fed a night and sent on. None placed as what the god looks for.
- The far houses walk: a party of the Marshal, the first captain and two riders out and back; price it. At the two farthest houses they saw what was there last year, new roofs, good horses and full smokehouses.
- The counted road did not come.
- The toll raised by half from the thaw is the Warlord's order and falls on every train and every Sarrow carrier at the ford and the passes.

## Asked of the god

- The Steward, month 1: what to do if the offerings fall short of the cord. In substance: "And if what is brought is less than the cord?" Not answered in front of him; Idris said the cord is drawn as entered and she would hear it again.
- The Steward, month 1, answering her: that she had said the short true count last year. Not a question.
- The Mother of the Hearth, month 6, in the hall with the Guest in the room, in substance: whether the toll was the host's to raise or the sill's. Not answered in words by her in my record. Kessan answered that it was the host's and the sill's part was to hear; Idris said the sill's part was to hear.
- A trader at the sill, month 7, to Idris, in substance: whether a toll could be raised by half in a year the ford was thinner. Answered by Idris, not by the god.
- The senior Doorwarden of the lower columns, month 4: nothing put to her in my record; he was told the terms and might refuse the table.
- The herder of the lower columns who asked "Is the cold coming down?" in Year 2: answered through the Mother in month 1, and again in person at the cutting in the summer: she looked and does not know what it means. He said it was something to be told.
- Households and the younger Doorwarden, month 2: the line on the hillside, put to her at the sill table. Answered in front of them; the line is hers and not in my hand.
- At each fire of the lower columns: she asked what is meant by "as Marda held it" and by Sill, once each. Not asked of her.
- The letter: put to her, in substance, at a rite fire by the keeper of rite who read it aloud, whether it should be answered. Whether she answered is not in my record.

## Names said aloud

- Kessan and Idris Veld: said in the hall, already in the record.
- "Guest": said by the gate guard in month 1 and by the people of her.
- "Marda": said by herders at the fires of the lower columns when she asked what Marda is. Already in the record.
- The Steward, the Marshal of the Road, the Mother of the Hearth, the senior and younger Doorwardens of the lower columns, the first captain, the older and younger toll watchers at the ford, the clerk of the train, the keeper of rite who read the letter and the herder at the cutting: not named aloud. They remain unnamed.
- The river kingdom's king and chancery: not named aloud; the clerk said "the chancery" and "the king". The sender's name is not in the letter as read.
- No name for the winter city or for the people's home was said.
