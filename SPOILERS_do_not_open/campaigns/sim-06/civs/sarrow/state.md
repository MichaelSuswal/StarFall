# The Sarrow people of god-thresholds: state at the end of Year 3

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent, pass one. Numbers are rounded. This year turns on two branches of my own, the winter stores (A: they last to within days of the thaw; B: they fail some weeks short) and the far toll houses (A: as the watchmen say; B: not). Where the numbers differ the branches are given. The Keeper's pass two settles which.

population: Branch A about 18,000 (Year 2 end about 18,150). Branch B about 17,650. Why it moved: the stock fell short of the thaw at the ordinary share, the ration was cut by about a fifth from month 5 and the herds culled hard; the old and the under fives went first. Branch A is a hundred or two deaths over the ordinary, B some hundreds over, with births held in both.

stores: the winter ran short of the thaw by the Steward's count about twelve days and the mothers' about twenty. Branch A: the last barley went in the first days after the opening. Branch B: it went some weeks before, the last fortnight eaten on dried meat, the herds and part of the seed. The seed barley was drawn down by about a third in the fifth month, so the spring sowing is thin in both, thinner in B. The coming winter: not counted at the year's close. Salt is leaner again: the trains brought less. Why: the stock was short at first snow, the tolls in kind thin, the herds cut by the cull.

treasure: herds well down from the cull, the weak stock first and most of it eaten, so the coming summer's milk and wool are smaller; horses thinner; bloomery iron unchanged. Tolls laid up at the winter hall: with the raise of one half from the thaw and the trains crossing as before, about three quarters of what the old cords show for a generation back, up from near half. Why: the war band's order in month 6; the traders paid after the thaw with complaint; the ford's own tally is down a little again, so the raise did not come in full. Branch B at the far houses: more of the toll comes to the hall if the far houses are held to their cords, at the cost of a standing office.

trade: carrying, guiding and tolls as before at the raised rate. Wool, felt and iron out; grain, salt and cloth in, less of each. A letter from the chancery of the river kingdom claims a yearly share of the toll; the Warlord's answer and the First Doorwarden's answer to the train's clerk are in the year file. Five strangers brought to the god, all ordinary. Why: the raise, the poor year the traders speak of, the Marshal's standing orders.

strength: about 360 sworn spears under eight captains, unchanged; about 3,500 more when the host calls; five blessed passes and doors. The band ate last for a second winter and is tired and thin. Why: no raising this year, no losses by the cough reported, the night watches still taken from the spears.

mood: the households are thin and steady and hold to the custom of the sharing as their own. The captains are tired and loyal to the Lintel and resent the sharing less than before. The Steward is sharper and says his count out first. The Marshal is quiet; in Branch B he is under question. The Doorwardens of the high rite are quieter at the Warlord's right hand. The lower columns are less cold to the high rite since the senior Doorwarden sat at the Guest's table in the open. The herders know the line of the grass is going up and say it without grief. The old women are quieter still. Over all lies a king's letter and the Warlord's and the First Doorwarden's different answers to it.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- At the two farthest toll houses the Guest and the Marshal left with the watchmen's word and the cords as shown [r-0293]. The thin toll stands; the Marshal keeps his office. The two farthest houses stand on the road in the Kennoch Heights [r02], short of the road's end; the walk fits between the thaw and month 9.
- Far Notch holds, the fifth blessing. No verdict in the grass dispute is on the record: her decisions gave none.
