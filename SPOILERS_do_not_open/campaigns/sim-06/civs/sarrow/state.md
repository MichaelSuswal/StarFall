# The Sarrow people of god-thresholds: state at the end of Year 1

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent. Numbers are rounded and are the middle between the two branches of the wolf attempt (K); the note under each says how the branches move it.

population: about 18,050 (start about 18,000). The winter city held about 4,000 in the cold months and the rest wintered in the valley round it. Why it moved: an ordinary winter with a short stock took more of the old and the very young than an easy one, but not many more, and births held, so the net is a small rise. If the wolves broke the fold: one herder or boy fewer, a funeral and no change to the rounded figure.

stores: at the year's end the herds are fattened from an ordinary, better than last year's high grass, and the new barley is not yet in. Of the winter stock, the last barley was eaten the week the passes opened. The winter ran about ten days short at the full ration, closed by the sharing, the hunt and a cull of weak stock, and the Steward's margin at the thaw was nil. For the coming winter the stock to be laid in is the same as this year's start unless the tolls improve, and they have not. Why: the first count was favorable by about a fortnight, the columns came down thin, and the tolls in kind were thin. If the wolves broke the fold: the herd is short by some dozens of sheep and goats, which moves the coming winter's meat a few days thinner.

treasure: herds and horses (herds restored by the summer, a little below the start in the branch where the fold broke), bloomery iron unchanged, tolls in kind laid up at the winter hall thin again, about two thirds of an ordinary year's by the Steward's reckoning. Why: the trains from the river kingdom crossed and paid as before, no more of them than last year, and carrying did not recover.

trade: carrying, guiding and tolls as before. Wool, felt and iron out; grain, salt and cloth in. No new routes. The toll houses now feed any traveler a night before asking his business, which the custom already did, and keep an honest count, which the Marshal now enforces by asking. Why: the Marshal acted on the god's order; nothing else changed. The word that a tall woman with a key stands in the passes has gone out with the trains; it is in the report.

strength: about 360 sworn spears under eight captains; about 3,500 more when the host calls; the passes themselves. Unchanged. The sworn band is unhurt by the wolves. The captains are wary of the sill court and of the god's closeness to the Doorwardens.

mood: steadier at the hearth, unsettled above it. The households are warm toward the Guest, since she heard them first and fed the poorest first. The Doorwardens are exalted and proud. The captains are wary and say little. The Steward is uneasy over the mark on his salt store. Kessan keeps his own counsel and has kept the host. The people say the Guest is in their house, and the old women have a new thing to argue.
