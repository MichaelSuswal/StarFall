# The Sarrow people of god-thresholds: state at the end of Year 4

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent, pass one. Numbers are rounded. This year turns on two attempts of mine, the winter stores (A: they last to within days of the thaw; B: they fail some weeks short) and the loan asked of the two farthest toll houses (A: both send a real share; B: one sends a little and one words). The loan bears on the stores. Where the numbers differ the branches are given. The Keeper's pass two settles which.

population: Branch A about 17,550 (Year 3 end about 17,650). Branch B about 17,250. Why it moved: the ration was cut by about a quarter from first snow, set at the lower count, the herds were already culled to little, and the old and the under fives went first; births held. Branch A is a hundred or two deaths over the ordinary, B some hundreds over.

stores: the autumn counts were about twenty days short of the thaw at the full ration by the Steward and about twenty five by the hearth mothers; the share was set at the lower. The seed grain and seed barley was counted apart, sealed together by the Steward and the Mother of the Hearth with Idris Veld witness, and not drawn. Branch A: the last barley went in the last days before the opening and the seed seal stood whole to the first day of sowing, so the spring sowing is whole. Branch B: the stock failed some weeks short, the herd and the dried meat went and the seal was broken in the last week on part of the seed, so the sowing is short again and thinner than last year's. Loan: Branch A, both far houses sent a share the Steward counted as about a week of the full ration, entered as a loan to be repaid from next year's toll; Branch B, a little, about two days, from the nearer house, and words from the farthest. Salt: leaner again; no river train came in the summer and salt came with them. The coming winter: not counted at the year's close. Why: the short sowing and thin tolls of last year, the herds down, the river's trains gone in summer.

treasure: herds small, the weak stock gone in two winters, so the coming summer's milk and wool are smaller still; horses thin; bloomery iron unchanged. Tolls laid up at the winter hall: about half of what the old cords show for a generation back, down from about three quarters, since the trains fell by about a third from the thaw at the far houses and none of the river kingdom's crossed in the summer; the raise of one half stood to month 8, and was then halved at the sitting, to a knot and a quarter the beast, from month 9. Toll is taken grain first. A loan is owed to the far houses on a separate cord in Branch A (about a week of the ration) and a smaller one in B. Why: the trains, the half raise, the grain first order, the loan.

trade: carrying, guiding and tolls as before at the lowered rate. Wool, felt and iron out; grain, salt and cloth in, less of each, grain taken first as toll. No train of the river kingdom crossed in the summer; trains of other peoples came as before. A folded word of the Guest's to the king of the river kingdom left the ford with a train master of that kingdom in autumn, with the toll waived; nothing more is known. No envoy came. Three ordinary travelers brought to the Guest, none placed. Why: the raise, the river kingdom's absence, the Marshal's grain first order.

strength: about 360 sworn spears under eight captains in Branch A, about 350 in B; about 3,500 more when the host calls; six blessed passes and doors. The band ate last for a third winter and is thin and tired; the youngest captain said the thanks were the first the band had had. The night watches are still taken from the spears. Why: no raising, the third winter eating last, the cold.

mood: the households are thin and steady, hold the sharing as their own custom, and the Mother of the Hearth is the one they trust on a count. The captains are tired and mutter more than before; the youngest of the eight spoke aloud. The Steward gives a hard number first and seals the seed with the Mother's mark beside his own. The Marshal is quiet and was seen at the farthest door with his cap in his hand; in Branch B he is under question again over the farthest house. The Doorwardens of the high rite count the dimmings against six blessings and are quieter at the Warlord's right hand. The lower columns are as they were. The herders of the upper columns have cut notches at the line of the grass and say it stood, and are not eased. Over all lies a king's letter, a folded word gone after it, and a summer without the river's trains that no one at the hall can explain.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- At the two farthest toll houses the Guest and the Marshal left with the watchmen's word and the cords as shown [r-0293]. The thin toll stands; the Marshal keeps his office. The two farthest houses stand on the road in the Kennoch Heights [r02], short of the road's end; the walk fits between the thaw and month 9.
- Far Notch holds, the fifth blessing. No verdict in the grass dispute is on the record: her decisions gave none.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- The stores failed some weeks short of the thaw. The last fortnight was eaten on dried meat, herd and seed. About three hundred died over the ordinary, the old and the under fives. The sowing was short. Your numbers take that branch.
- At the far toll houses the Marshal and the first captain counted stores, beasts and roofs and left with the watchmen's word. Each house had its account of a good year's barter. Nothing was found that the captain could prove. The Marshal keeps his office.
- The Reading at the table in the fourth month: the senior Doorwarden means no harm to a guest at that table, and he learned the same of her. She taught him the oath in person that winter. No lesser mark was laid.
- She gave no word on the toll and none on the letter.
- The dimmings of the star are not known to be anyone's doing.

## Keeper, Year 4, pass two
- The far houses' loan: the nearer house sent about two days' worth; the farthest sent words only. Your numbers take that branch.
- The stores lasted to within days of the thaw. The seed seal was whole at the sowing. About a hundred and fifty died over the ordinary, the old and the youngest.
- The word for the river king was given to a clerk of the chancery in the second month. No answer and no envoy came this year.
- From month 9 the toll stands at a knot and a quarter the beast, as the table settled it. In months 10 to 12 no train of the river kingdom came.
