# The Sarrow people of god-thresholds: state at the end of Year 5

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent, pass one. Numbers are rounded. This year turns on one attempt of mine, the winter stores (A: they last to within days of the thaw; B: they fail some weeks short), and on two things that are the Keeper's: the riders beyond the houses (R1: home before the deep snow with news; R2: not home, word at the thaw or none) and the day at the farthest door (D1: nothing for the sill; D2: a second trade and a sill). Where the numbers differ the branches are given. The riders and the door move the numbers little except where I say so. The Keeper's pass two settles which.

population: Branch A about 17,600 (Year 4 end about 17,550). Branch B about 17,300. Why it moved: the ration was cut by about an eighth from first snow, set at the lower count, the old and the under fives went first and the dead named at the first snow were about a hundred and fifty, fewer than the winter before; births held. Branch A is a hundred and fifty to two hundred deaths over the ordinary, B some hundreds over. No sickness of people is in my record this year; the cough is of the river's beasts.

stores: the autumn counts were about ten days short of the thaw at the full ration by the Steward and about sixteen by the hearth mothers, taken after two small salt trains; the share was set at the lower. The seed grain and seed barley was counted apart, sealed together by the Steward and the Mother of the Hearth with Idris Veld witness, and not drawn. Branch A: the last barley went in the last week before the opening, the seal was whole at the sowing, the sowing is full, and the harvest of the summer was ordinary and is laid up at about a sound year's, so the autumn counts of Year 6 start from a sound harvest. Branch B: the stock failed some weeks short, the herd and the dried meat went and the seal was broken in the last week on part of the seed, the sowing was short and thin, and the harvest was thin. Salt: about a quarter of a winter's at the old measure at the first count; about half after two small trains, the Guest's bag all but one handful, and the offerings given to the common stock; it went first to the meat put by, then to the pot and bread in a thin measure, the rite last; the Steward's tally at midsummer shows it will not reach first snow at the ration. The far houses' loan: the nearer house's two days were repaid in kind from the first grain toll of the thaw; the farthest sent words only and is owed nothing. If the riders bring salt (R1), the Steward counts it on top and the Keeper moves this line. Why: a full sowing and an ordinary harvest after a short year, against the herds still down, the river's trains at half, and salt gone short on every road.

treasure: herds small, three winters of culling behind them, the milk and wool smaller than the old cords, horses thin, bloomery iron down by what went for salt and the riders' goods (one laden beast of bar iron, two of felt, one of toll cloth went out with the riders, and a bar of iron and a length of felt for each beast of salt the Steward bought). Tolls laid up at the winter hall: about two fifths of what the old cords show for a generation back, down from about half, since the river's trains were at about half from the thaw and none crossed at all in the last three months of the year before, salt trains passed free all winter and are passed free to the first snow, and the toll stands at a knot and a quarter the beast, grain first. In Branch D2 the good horses of the farthest toll house are the hall's. Why: the trains, the salt order, the toll as it stands.

trade: carrying, guiding and tolls as before at the lowered rate. Wool, felt and iron out; grain, salt and cloth in, less of each; salt bought from two small trains of other peoples before the snow at about the Steward's price with one beast at the master's. Trains of the river kingdom came again from the thaw, about half as many as before the raise, with some beasts coughing and two trains short of beasts, which hired Sarrow carriers from the ford for a share in kind, a small gain to the carriers. A party of seven went beyond the houses with four beasts of felt, iron and toll cloth to trade for salt and with the Guest's word for the river king; nothing is known of it past the farthest house. No envoy came. A few ordinary travelers brought to the Guest, none placed. Why: the river's absence and half return, the salt order, the cough, the party sent out.

strength: about 360 sworn spears under eight captains in Branch A, about 350 in B, less the one captain who went beyond the houses with the riders; about 3,500 more when the host calls; six blessed passes and doors. The band ate last for a fourth winter, thin and tired and a little heard. Two spears of the band stood at the ford house the night the Guest stepped. The night watches are still taken from the spears. Why: no raising, a fourth winter eating last, the cold, a captain away.

mood: the households are thin and steady, hold the sharing as their own custom, and heard the host say it was short before the pot said it; the Mother of the Hearth is the one they trust on a count. The captains are tired and said less this year, and are not forgiven nor forgiving. The Steward gives a hard number first, sealed the seed with the Mother's mark beside his own, and gave way once on a beast of salt and does not like it. The Marshal is quiet, was seen at the ford with the coughing beasts and ordered them penned apart on his own word, and is under question over the farthest house; in D1 he is eased a little, in D2 he asked before the five whether he was to be relieved. The Doorwardens of the high rite count three dimmings against six blessings and no door blessed this year, and say nothing about what follows. The lower columns are as they were; the senior Doorwarden of the lower columns says a rite named with salt and held without it is not a lie. The herders of the upper columns say the line stood a second year and are not eased, and were heard at the hall in the winter. Over all lie a king's letter, a folded word gone after it, a party gone beyond the houses, a summer of half the river's trains with sick beasts, and salt.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- At the two farthest toll houses the Guest and the Marshal left with the watchmen's word and the cords as shown [r-0293]. The thin toll stands; the Marshal keeps his office. The two farthest houses stand on the road in the Kennoch Heights [r02], short of the road's end; the walk fits between the thaw and month 9.
- Far Notch holds, the fifth blessing. No verdict in the grass dispute is on the record: her decisions gave none.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- The stores failed some weeks short of the thaw. The last fortnight was eaten on dried meat, herd and seed. About three hundred died over the ordinary, the old and the under fives. The sowing was short. Your numbers take that branch.
- At the far toll houses the Marshal and the first captain counted stores, beasts and roofs and left with the watchmen's word. Each house had its account of a good year's barter. Nothing was found that the captain could prove. The Marshal keeps his office.
- The Reading at the table in the fourth month: the senior Doorwarden means no harm to a guest at that table, and he learned the same of her. She taught him the oath in person that winter. No lesser mark was laid.
- She gave no word on the toll and none on the letter.
- The dimmings of the star are not known to be anyone's doing.

## Keeper, Year 4, pass two
- The far houses' loan: the nearer house sent about two days' worth; the farthest sent words only. Your numbers take that branch.
- The stores lasted to within days of the thaw. The seed seal was whole at the sowing. About a hundred and fifty died over the ordinary, the old and the youngest.
- The word for the river king was given to a clerk of the chancery in the second month. No answer and no envoy came this year.
- From month 9 the toll stands at a knot and a quarter the beast, as the table settled it. In months 10 to 12 no train of the river kingdom came.

## Keeper, Year 5, pass two
- The riders: thirteen days from the far end of the road to the river king's town; received as guests; the word given into a clerk's hand; a written answer given them; one beast's load of salt bought, dear; home on the twentieth day of the second month, before the snow. What they tell and what the letter says are in your god's own telling.
- The winter stores failed some weeks short of the thaw. The herd went, then the dried meat; in the last week the seal was broken before witnesses on part of the seed. About four hundred died over the ordinary. The sowing was short. Your year takes Branch B.
- The day at the farthest door: about one part in three of the toll taken that day went into the house's own store, under the name of the watchman's tenth; the cord knots only the hall's part, and knots it truly. Your year takes Branch D2, large.
- The night the Guest was at the far door, the ford stood open, as the older watcher could not know. The night after she stepped back, the far door stood open. At the ford she was guest again until the dawn after a full day.
- The river kingdom's beasts cough. None of your own herds showed it by the year's end.
