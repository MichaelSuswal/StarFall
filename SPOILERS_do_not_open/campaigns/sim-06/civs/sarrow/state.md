# The Sarrow people of god-thresholds: state at the end of Year 2

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent, pass one. Numbers are rounded. Nothing this year turned on a branch of my own, so there is one set of numbers; where the Keeper's pass two may move them, the note says how.

population: about 18,150 (Year 1 end about 18,050). Why it moved: the stores were shared by hearth from the first snow and lasted to the thaw, so fewer of the old and the very young died than in Year 1, and births held. The net is a small rise. The one grown death of Year 1 from the wolves is already in the start figure.

stores: the winter just ended ran to the thaw with the last barley eaten three days after the passes opened (Year 1: the week the passes opened, margin nil). The coming winter: the Steward's count, the hearth mothers' count and the gap between them are in the year file. The mothers' count, which I take as the nearer, has the stock about eleven days short of the thaw at the full ration, the Steward's a week to spare; the gap is about eighteen days, of which the Steward explained most (seed barley counted as stock, damp-spoiled meat in the low granary counted by tally, the war band's hay counted as fuel) and some days he could not. The new barley is not yet in at the year's close. Salt is leaner than last year: the trains brought less. Why: the same start as last year, the tolls in kind unchanged and thin, the herds a little short of the start by the Year 1 fold; the sharing from first snow is what made the difference.

treasure: herds and horses (sheep and goats a little below the start still, from the broken fold of Year 1, the cull of weak stock smaller than last year's), bloomery iron unchanged. Tolls in kind laid up at the winter hall about as thin as last year: about two thirds of what the Steward reckons an ordinary recent year, and near half of what the old cords show for a generation back. Why: the ford's own tally is down a little, the trains crossing as many or near it but shorter, and what the two farthest toll houses take in is not what the hall is shown. (K, whether anything is held back there: the report.)

trade: carrying, guiding and tolls as before. Wool, felt and iron out; grain, salt and cloth in. No new routes. The toll houses feed any traveler a night before asking his business, and keep a count; the counts at the far houses agree with the riders' tally and with the hall's cords, as the watchmen show them. Seven strangers were brought to the god this year, all ordinary. Why: the Marshal's standing orders; nothing else changed.

strength: about 360 sworn spears under eight captains (two lost to the cough, two boys sworn); about 3,500 more when the host calls; the passes themselves, five of them now blessed (the ford, the hall door, the city gate, Stair Pass, Far Notch). Unchanged in number. Night watches on the road now take grown men from the spears, which costs the band sleep. The captains are wary and tired and say little.

mood: steadier at the hearth, taut above it. The households are warm toward the Guest, who sat at their tables, and unsettled by her saying she does not know whose the star is. The Doorwardens of the high rite are proud and uneasy at the Warlord's seat at the Guest's right hand. The lower columns have had the Guest at their fires and their senior Doorwarden was heard and not given what he asked. The captains took the sharing from first snow badly and held. The Steward is used to the Guest's counts. The Marshal is silent and the Warlord says he never counted the far houses. The herders are afraid of the bare cutting and the star that dims oftener. The old women are quieter.
