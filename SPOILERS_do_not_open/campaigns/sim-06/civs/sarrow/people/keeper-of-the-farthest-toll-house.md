# The keeper of the farthest toll house

Unnamed; no one said the name aloud. A man of the age to have grandchildren who has kept the farthest toll house on Pass Road, in the Kennoch Heights [r02], a good many years, with two watchmen under him. Courteous, unhurried, with his hands open. His house has the new roofs of split timber, good horses in its pens and full smokehouses, and has been walked three times.

Year 4, dated: month 3 (by the riders' account), the house answered the hall's loan, in the words and the share recorded in the year file by branch. Month 7, was told at his own door by the Guest, before she acted, that she would lay salt on his threshold and speak the oath with the key in her hand, to know by her own marking how many go in and out and not to accuse him or any man. He said it was a house of the Sarrow and a guest might stand in its door unasked, and that the cord of the house had nothing in it the knots did not say. Stood with his hands open while she laid the salt. Told her the trains of the river kingdom had complained loudly and paid, that none had turned back from his door, that none had spoken to him of another road, and that a toll house cannot count what it does not see.
