# The Steward of Salt and Stores

The office holder is unnamed; no one said the name aloud in Year 1. A man, careful, middle aged, who keeps the granaries, salt and winter stock.

Fixed this year: he does not give a host a hard number in autumn, holding that a people given it eats worse all winter. He gave the Guest a count favorable by about a fortnight, and the weekly counts revealed it by the fourth month; he says he did not lie and counted what the granaries held. He gave her a measure of salt that would not shame a poor household and watched her while he gave it. He stood at the mark laid on his salt store and moved his ledgers out of the room beside the door. He resisted the sharing by hearth in private, then carried it out. He stretched the last barley to the day the passes opened. He is uneasy about the Guest and is, in the opinion of the Mother of the Hearth, the only one who could have kept the people fed to the thaw.

Year 2, dated: month 1, told by the Guest to his face that she would rather a short true count than a kind one; he said he would give her one he could stand behind and that the hearth mothers should have their own. Month 3, his count (a week to spare at the thaw) and the hearth mothers' (about eleven days short) differed by about eighteen days; he explained most of it (seed barley counted as stock, damp-spoiled meat in the low granary counted by tally, the band's hay counted as fuel) and could not explain some days. He carried the sharing from the first snow, saying in his own office it would be worse. He kept a separate cord marked as the Guest's for the dead herder's household's winter keep and has not been told how it is to be paid. The last barley went three days after the thaw. He is used to her counts now. Still unnamed.
