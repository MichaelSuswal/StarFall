# Kessan, called the Lintel

The Warlord of the Sarrow. Fifty winters at the opening of Year 1, fifty one at its close. Heavy, scarred, careful with his word, short in temper, eleven years over the war band. Respected, not much loved; afraid he will not be ready.

Fixed this year: he is not afraid of the Guest, only of being found unready. He gave her his counts truly and quickly, and was relieved, which he told his captains, who took it badly. He was read at the table and disliked it, and did not say so in the hall. He sat at the foot of his own table the first night of snow, from awe and not from weakness. His captains murmur that the Guest holds the sill court and his men answer at it. He judged the sill case of the toll watcher with her and thought it just, and wished his man had had a day to answer. He led the hunt in the sixth month, and led the host up in spring. He believes the host is the one thing the Guest has not asked for, and does not know what he will do if she does.

Ages one year. Acts from his own will.

Year 2, dated: month 2, he said before the hall that the fold was his watch and the failure his, flat, and had the captain brought in; he answered for his captains and ordered no column to go down the road with boys alone on the night watch. Month 3, he told his captains the band could eat last for one winter and hold the passes on it. Month 4, he sat at the Guest's right hand at the hall table, was told he would not be read again unless he asked it, and gave her the count of the band (about three hundred and sixty), the passes and the thin toll; he said he had never counted the far toll houses, which are the Marshal's trade, and did not accuse the Marshal. He led the going up in month 9, as for twelve years. Ages one year. Fifty two winters.

Year 3, dated: month 3, sat by while the cords of every toll house were laid out in the hall, and said the far houses' cords were as neat as any. Told his captains the band would eat last a second winter and hold the passes. Month 4, was told before the hall that the Doorwardens' slight at his seat at the Guest's right hand was theirs and not his. Month 6, gave the order in the hall that the toll be raised by half from the thaw; did not ask the Guest's leave. Month 7, named his first captain to walk with the Marshal to the far houses. Answered the king's letter by the clerk's mouth in month 9: that the Sarrow owed no share to a king they had not met, and that a guest of the chancery would be fed at the sill and heard. Led the going up in month 9, for thirteen years. Fifty three winters.

Year 4, dated: month 1, sat alone with the Guest at the hall table with bread before she spoke to anyone, and told his captains at his fire only that she had told him first, had not called the raise a theft, meant to keep his road and his spears and would not read him unless he asked. Month 3, heard the Guest tell the eight captains the band would eat last a third winter, and said the band would hold the passes on it. Month 8, sat at the table with Idris Veld and the Guest over the cords of the first trains, said the raise had bought nothing this spring that the old toll would not have, and gave back half of it, to a knot and a quarter the beast, saying it was not made by one mouth. Month 10, with no river train on the road, sent a rider up to the Guest with the question whether he might send riders beyond the houses to learn why; it met silence and he sent no one. Month 9, led the going up, for fourteen years. Fifty four winters.

Year 5, dated: month 1, was answered yes by the Guest on sending his riders beyond the houses and held to her terms; named a captain from the middle of the eight, one not at the far houses and hard to anger, and said to him he chose him for that. Month 3, heard the Guest tell the eight captains the band would eat last a fourth winter, and said the band would hold the passes on it. Month 8, at the sitting, kept the toll at a knot and a quarter the beast and proposed with Idris that salt trains pass free to first snow; sent two of the sworn band to stand at the ford house the night the Guest went into the ford. Heard the Marshal's order on the coughing beasts and called it sensible. Month 9, led the going up, for fifteen years. Month 11, answered under the standing answers that the herds might stay a week at the high grass; the question of the sick beasts of a river master he and Idris answered differently, and it waited. Fifty five winters.
