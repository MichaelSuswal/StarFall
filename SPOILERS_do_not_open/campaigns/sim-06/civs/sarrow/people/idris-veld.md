# Idris Veld, the First Doorwarden

An old woman, patient, shrewd, well liked, thirty years in the rite. Ages one year.

Fixed this year: she bade the Guest in at the gate by word in the old form, after a short wait while the gate guard held. She was read at the table and called it courteous. She was named a guest by the Guest, the only one named, and was taught the oath in person. She chose three Doorwardens by sense and not by rank, and passed over the senior of the lower columns, who has said the sense will run out; this is her first enemy of the year. She chose the door of the salt store for the third lesser mark, to learn how the stores stand, and the Steward knows it. She told the Guest what the recital says of a name over a sill: there is none, the sill bears the name of Marda Sill, first toll taker at Marle Ford [f61] for the Sarrow, and before her the Sarrow guided for the wardens of a league in the lowlands. She did not ask why, and did not ask again. She sits at the Guest's right hand at the sill court. Her standing is up, and she knows it will cost her.

Year 2, dated: month 5, in the deep snow, the Guest ate at her table and asked what Marda Sill is. She answered what she knew: a person in the recital, a word said once at the close of the oath, older than the Sarrow's speech by the lower columns' telling, and a woman who the lower columns say went into the ford. She did not ask why and told no one. At the hall table she sits at the Guest's left, with Kessan at the right, and at the sill court at the right; she says she never supposed otherwise. She fixed the price of the dead herder's death by the old sill table in the second month. She was asked after the senior Doorwarden of the lower columns and said he was right about half and proud about all. Ages one year.

Year 3, dated: month 1, entered the account for the dead herder's household on the cord and stood witness to the Guest's word on how it is paid. Month 4, sat beside the Guest at the hall door while the senior Doorwarden of the lower columns was heard, and said afterward that if she held the sill alone she would sleep worse. Month 6, said before the hall that the toll was the host's to raise and the sill's to hear, and that she would hear whoever came with a complaint. Month 7, said of the king's letter that the recital names the wardens of a league in the lowlands whom the Sarrow once guided for and says nothing of a share owed; her own word to the clerk was that the sill would hear a claim made in person and eat with the claimant. Ages one year.
