# Report to the Keeper: the Sarrow, Year 1

## How the god was met

Autumn, month 1, first day, before first light. The older of the two toll watchers at Marle Ford [f61] saw her come and bless it. He knew the oath, stood with his hands open, was asked nothing. The younger ran for the city.

The same morning, at the gate of the winter city. The senior of the two sworn guards did not bar it and did not bid her in. He held for a long breath, sent a Doorwarden's boy for Idris Veld, and the god said "I am a guest and I ask the sill" and waited. Idris Veld came and bade her in by word in the old form. The gate guard stood aside. The household teaching was to bid in the first who stoops, and it held out of season.

The same day, the winter hall. Idris sent for the offices. The door stood open, the seat was set, Kessan broke bread first and ate first. Kessan, Idris, the Marshal, the Mother of the Hearth and the Steward were all in the city by dark. Kessan and Idris were each read across the table. Kessan did not like it and said so only at his own fire. Idris called it courteous. Neither meant harm to a guest. Each took the god's word. Kessan gave his counts the next day, truly. The Steward gave a count favorable by about a fortnight and said it was not a lie. The Marshal gave the toll houses, the passes, the ford and the road. The Mother of the Hearth took the offer to hear the households first and brought her grievance in month 4.

Month 3. The god blessed the hall door and the city gate, taught Idris and three Doorwardens the oath in person, and had three lesser marks laid: the toll house at the ford, a hearth mother's standing house, and the door of the salt store (Idris chose it). The Steward watched it laid and said nothing. A senior Doorwarden of the lower columns, passed over, said the sense will run out. The Steward gave her a small measure of salt.

First snow, late month 3. The seat was set, the door unlatched, the bowl filled. The god sat at the warlord's table. Kessan sat at the foot of his own table.

Winter. The god walked the gates by night weekly and the sworn guards learned to be at their posts. In month 4 she heard the Mother's grievance (the war band's horses on the households' hay). Weekly counts exposed the Steward's favorable autumn figure. In month 5 she judged a sill case at the hall door with Idris at her right and Kessan at her left; Kessan thought it just and wished for a day to answer. In month 6 she ordered the stores shared by hearth, the poorest first; the Mother backed it, the Steward resisted privately and complied, the captains took it badly and said nothing, and Kessan hunted.

Spring. The Marshal walked her up the road and through both passes with a small escort of riders. They took her word to keep honest counts and feed a traveler a night. The toll watchers brought her eleven strangers over spring and summer.

Summer. She went with two columns in turn and sat at their rite fires. The keepers of rite said she ate little, said less and listened.

## What was done in the god's name

Her orders, carried by the offices: the Marshal's riders and the keepers of rite carried the count orders and the order in month 12 for all columns to be down by first snow. Three Doorwardens laid three lesser marks. The hearth mothers shared the stores by hearth. The Marshal told every toll house to keep an honest count, feed a traveler a night, and bring her any stranger who is wrong in one way. The Steward drew a measure of salt for her. The helpers did nothing in her name beyond her own orders; she was among them all year.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper

1. The wolves at the night fold of one column on the road, month 2. The god was in the city, a good many days from it, and took no part. Attempt: the night watch (six herders, two of them boys; eleven dogs; three of the sworn band at the fires; slings, staves, three spears; a late moon) tries to drive the pack from the fold. The dogs were late to give tongue. Success: the pack is driven off in the first minutes with a handful of animals lost and no person hurt. Failure: the fold broken, the flock scattered along the road, a herder or boy killed or mauled, sheep and goats lost counted in tens at least. The year is written both ways; the state numbers sit between and the note says how each branch moves them. The column's captain and the column are unnamed. (The watch's numbers are my own additions; the record gives none.)

2. Strangers brought to the god by toll watchers: eleven this year, all ordinary by my reckoning. Whether any is what the god looks for is the Keeper's. I placed none.

3. Where the god is judged by mortals: the sill case in month 5, where I wrote her verdict from the sill law as the people keep it (toll as the measure of the house stood, the rider to house the watcher three nights). The god's own file or the Keeper may overrule; the mortal reaction stands either way.

## What turns on a power

- The ford blessing (month 1), the hall door and city gate (month 3), Stair Pass [f62] (spring): what each did, and what the god knew of who crosses. The year turns little on the answer.
- The Reading of the Host on Kessan and Idris (month 1), and in summer on three others (an Aldermark [r10] trader with a false measure, a Sarrow packer in debt to the toll who sold the loading of two mules, a captain's cousin). Each was in fact harmless to a guest at the table; what the god saw is the Keeper's. The packer's and the trader's matters were settled at the sill without it.
- The three lesser marks: whether they hold.
- Whether the god read the Steward's favorable count (month 4).
- The Breaker's Hand: not used. No door was held against a refused traveler.
- The three dimmings of the star: the people have seen such nights before and tied them to her. Whether they are hers, or hers to do, is the Keeper's.

## What touches anyone else

- The trains from the river kingdom crossed and paid as before, no more than last year. The word that a tall woman with a key stands in the passes and sits at the sill goes home with them to the Aldermark [r10].
- The Marshal's order to feed a traveler a night and keep an honest count may reach traders.
- The god looked at, and in three cases read, travelers of the Aldermark [r10] and the Ansel Plain [r04] at the toll houses. One Aldermark [r10] trader paid in kind for a false measure.
- No party was sent out of the Sarrow's country. No message was carried. The counted road did not come this year.
- Columns turned toward the winter ground at the year's close. The god's return to the ford falls in Year 2.

## Asked of the god

- Gate guard: "You are the one we wait for. Why do you come before the snow?" (in substance, by the senior guard). Answered only with "I am a guest and I ask the sill."
- Idris Veld, at the table: asked what the Guest was to them. Answered in front of her: no name, call me Guest, I have come to lead those who keep the sill.
- Kessan, at the table: whether the host was still his. Answered in front of him: the host on the road and in the gates is his, and she asked for his counts.
- The Steward: what she meant to do with the salt. Answered by her asking how much she might draw without hurting the winter stock.
- The Mother of the Hearth, month 4: that the Guest sit at the households' tables in the low quarter. Not answered in words. She spent the summer at the rite fires of the columns, where the households sit.
- A senior Doorwarden of the lower columns, at the city rite fire, month 3: why he was passed over for sense. Not put to her; said to the fire.
- The Marshal: the escort she took in spring and which pass to bless. Answered by walking both and blessing the one he named.

## Names said aloud

- "Guest": the god, of herself, in month 1. No other name was given.
- Kessan and Idris Veld: said in the hall in month 1 by the offices. Already in the record.
- Marda Sill: said by Idris to the god in month 5, in private. Already in the record.
- The Steward, the Marshal, the Mother of the Hearth, the column captains and the three Doorwardens chosen: not named aloud. They remain unnamed.
- No name for the winter city or for the people's home was said.
