# Lore of the Sarrow, end of Year 2

What the people now say. Where they explain a cause, they are wrong about something that matters. The wrongness is marked after each telling for the Keeper. Year 1 tellings that still stand are kept and marked.

## About the Guest

- That she does not know why she came before the snow, and said so to the gate guard. The households say it is how a mother speaks. The Doorwardens say it is how a Guest speaks. Some captains say a god who does not know can be argued with. Wrong in all three: whatever she did or did not know, it was not said to be argued with.
- That she is a woman who eats at a table and cannot lie until she rises, and the households have tried it. A game among the low quarter's children is to ask her, in play, what she had for her dinner. Wrong in the part that matters: they take the bar on lying as a weakness to be used, and not as a thing she told them so that they might trust what she says.
- That she said she does not know whose the star is. The households are divided between taking it as true, which frightens them more than any story, and taking it as a silence she has been told to keep. Wrong in the second: nothing in her words says she was told.
- That the Guest has given the right hand of the table to the Lintel and the right hand of the sill to the First Doorwarden, and that this is a choosing of the host's heir. Wrong: it was a seat, and Idris said so.
- That she is a mother, and the mother of the host. Said still by many. She did not say it.
- That the bowl at first snow was drunk by the Guest. The old women this year argue that a Guest who has come once cannot come again, and others that she must be bidden each winter. They have not settled it, and the argument is longer than any since the first snow of the world.
- That she heard the lower columns' senior Doorwarden at the hall door and gave him no oath, and that this is why the lower columns are cold to the high rite. Wrong in the cause: they were cold to it before.

## About the strange things

- The star. Five nights this year, more than last. The households say it dims when a door is blessed, that it is a lamp at the sill of the world and the Guest's coat passes across it, that it dims when someone dies who should not have, that it is an eye that blinks and sleeps in the cold, that something passes before it. The Doorwardens say five dimmings and five blessed thresholds, the ford, the hall door, the city gate, Stair Pass and Far Notch, and say at last the count agrees. Those who count say the dimmings and the blessings do not fall on the same nights and that last year there were three dimmings and four blessings, and are told the sky keeps its own calendar. Wrong in the count: the dimmings were eight over two years and the blessings five, and the Doorwardens have set this year's five against the sum of both.
- The flower. The blue pass flower did not come up on the old cutting at the high gates. The oldest woman of a lower column says it marks the summer's height and that no flower means no height, and that the cold is walking down. A keeper of rite says the Guest's blessing at the passes passed on the flower. Another says it is the star. The old herders say it has been going for years and nobody listened. The Kennoch Gates [f64] are said by some to be cooling, and no one has gone near them. Wrong in the cause given by the Doorwardens and the households: the flower was failing before the Guest, and before the star dimmed oftener. Whether the old herders are right is not theirs to know.
- The wolves. Last year's tale is kept and has grown: that the cold's hounds ran before her and bit at the column, and this year the dead herder is remembered at the hall door and the old women say the star dimmed for him. Wrong: the star dimmed on the night after the hall had sat, and they have put the cause the wrong way round.
- The cranes. They went to tell the plain, and some now say to count the doors. Wrong, and the plainsfolk have no word of it.

## About their rulers

- Of Kessan: that he is at the Guest's right hand and the host is still his. That he said the fold was his and the failure his, flat, to the table, and the captains have not forgiven him for saying it where the Guest could hear. The Doorwardens say a man who says it so is a man who can be led. Wrong: it was said so that the captain would not be the one carrying it.
- Of Idris Veld: that she has the sill and not the table, and does not mind. Wrong in the second part, as she knows.
- Of the Steward of Salt and Stores: that his count is now a thing the hearth mothers set beside their own, and that he is, for the first time, in the same room as the Mother of the Hearth on a count. The old tale of his holding back a fortnight has hardened into a saying: a fortnight is the Steward's measure. Wrong in the saying, which makes of one year's count a habit, though the second count did differ.
- Of the Marshal of the Road: that he went quiet on the road back from the far houses and did not speak of the roofs. The riders have told it in the low quarter, and the story of the new roofs and good horses is told as a thing the Marshal's house has, with no word yet said of what it means. Wrong in the telling that makes it already a charge.
- Of the sill court: still the Guest's, say the Doorwardens, and the Warlord's men answer at it. Said less than a year ago, since the Guest has spent the summer with the lower columns.
- Of the senior Doorwarden of the lower columns: that he asked for the oath and was answered, and the Guest was right, and the Guest was wrong, according to who says it.

## About their neighbors

- The traders of the Aldermark [r10] come as last year and pay as last year, and the watchers say they are quiet at the ford. A trader told the older watcher at the ford that the river had a poor year, and the watcher tells it as the reason the trains come short. Not known to be true.
- The two farthest toll houses are said to have new roofs, good horses and full smokehouses, and the Marshal's house to have gone quiet. The watchmen at those houses say they were mended by the carpenters of the trains, that the horses are toll in kind and the smokehouses are full for the night's feeding. Not known to be true.
- The counted road did not come, as last year. The Doorwardens said they would come when the Guest had been fed a full year, and a year and more has been, and they have not come, and the Doorwardens have stopped saying it. The old say it is the other way about, and will come when the house has been judged.
- The plainsfolk have heard the cranes, the old women say.

## About themselves

- That a household that sets the seat and leaves the door unlatched is rewarded: kept from last year, and the argument whether it must be done each year is the new thing.
- That the sill law is the Guest's law. It is their own, which she kept. Said less.
- That the sharing from first snow is the Guest's law of hunger, and that a people fed by hearth in the cold are fed by the Guest. The Mother of the Hearth says it is the households' own custom, which the hearth mothers have always kept in a bad year and the host never did. Wrong in the first: the Guest said she would rather a short true count than a kind one, and the households have given it a name.
- That the old women are quieter this winter. This is true and is not lore.
