# Unnamed people of god-secrets: state at the end of Year 5

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent at the close of Year 5 (the year runs autumn to autumn). Last year's state was the civ agent's at the close of Year 4. Store figures are worked from the Keeper's Year 3 figures as before and from my own door counts since.

population: about 5,185, in the walled town [f35] and on the marsh edges and the boats. Up about 30 from 5,155. About 205 born and about 180 died; the winter fever of the fifth month was hard on the small ones (about 210 took it, 23 died). A few kin of drovers and boat families settled in and a few left; net about 5 added.

stores: the autumn harvest was ordinary: 31,760 sacks counted in at the door over twenty-three days, the far run filled first by the sack with 3,460 (its roll entry) and the near bins with 28,300, every sack stick-counted by a keeper of each of the two families at each end, with the stranger not at the door. The old stock carried in was 6,860 sacks (near bins). Closing count: near bins 35,160, far run 3,460, whole store 38,620, squared and cried as the Warden's. Spring walk of the whole store, month 7: 22,320 (near 18,860, far 3,460). By the end of the year the far run is drawn out and the old stock carried into the new harvest is 6,780, all near bins, by the count at the door. The lads' sticks at the damp corner: 810 tails in the three winter months and 40 sacks pulled spoilt since the harvest.

treasure: middling and unchanged: the granary, the council's weights, scrap silver and bronze in many private chests. Two weights of scrap silver came in from the stranger's passage to the poorest boat headman, who had lost three days' carriage on a shoal. The toll chest, kept by the Clerk, was gathered through the year at one copper bit a loaded boat or cart and stands at about 500 worker-days of hired labor by month 9, kept for the next dredging. The boat families' debts to the cooper house and the tanners' house stand at about the same as last year: the levy shares were carried off in part in carriage at the lenders' price, and two headmen borrowed again against next season.

trade: grain, reed work, salt fish and leather out; stock, wool and metal in, by river, track and coasting boat. Ordinary. Two loaded boats grounded at the shoals above and below the river gate cut in the dry spell of month 8, five boat-days lost and a fifth of a load wetted, about 70 worker-days of hired labor, against 110 last year; the Boat Master's crews now lighten loaded boats into skiffs before the shoals in a dry spell. The dredged cut held to about two feet and four inches at low water at the crown, a hand under its cut depth of two feet and eight inches; the shoals above and below it are one foot and eight inches at low water, a little shallower again than last spring. The Boat Master's string of three went down to the mouth and the cousin's family's two boats went up to the head on the ordinary runs, the stranger aboard neither. What they took on is theirs. The drovers of the plain came in as every year.

strength: a standing watch of about 100 and a call-up of about 500; earth and timber walls. Unchanged. The watch on the wall now cuts a stick of the hours on each night the star dims.

mood: steadier at the granary: the fourth autumn's count came the same way with the stranger on the market bench and not at the door, and the town speaks of it as its own custom. Sore still at the levy, a little less: the one stick of who paid what shows the boat houses bore the larger share, though most of it on credit, and the farming houses say a thing borrowed is not yet paid. Quieter about the sky: three nights this year against seven, and the town says it is because the watch counts them. Uneasy about the cellars: thirty-six households took water, five more than last winter, and the fevers of the small fell in the lowest streets. The Clerk is quiet in his house. The Warden has a fourth stick behind his lintel.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The closing count did not reach the far wall and was not squared [r-0297]; the cry was held.
- The stranger came back with the string on the ordinary run and was in the town for the month 11 night.
- On the walk through the store the far bins were found hollow [r-0299]; the Clerk was sent for. What the store holds against the roll is now known to the Warden, the Clerk and the stranger.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- The granary's figures, to be read in place of your own wherever they differ: the roll's old stock 9,950 sacks; what stood, 6,630 (the near bins 6,490 and the 140 facing sacks); the gap, 3,320, a third of the roll; the far run by the roll's entry 3,460, filled with 3,320 new sacks and the 140 facing sacks. The count squared and was cried as the Warden's.
- The evening with the league's tallies: the Clerk put the sticks in the stranger's hand to count. His count was right where the Clerk's was out by one. He said nothing of the stick he paused at.
- The journey up the river went as the crew's ordinary run and he came back with it on the thirteenth day of month 11.

## Keeper, Year 4, pass two
- The Counter went on from the landing at the river's head by the drovers' track onto the plain, and was back on the twentieth day of the eleventh month, before the day he named. Your numbers take the branch: later than the crew, on or before the twenty-second.
- The dim nights of the tenth and eleventh months found him on the plain, away from the town.
- The dredging stands as you wrote it.
