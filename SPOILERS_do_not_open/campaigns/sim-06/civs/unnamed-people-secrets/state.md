# Unnamed people of god-secrets: state at the end of Year 4

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent at the close of Year 4 (the year runs autumn to autumn). Last year's state was the civ agent's at the close of Year 3. Store figures are worked from the Keeper's Year 3 figures (see below).

population: about 5,155, in the walled town [f35] and on the marsh edges and the boats. Up about 35 from 5,120. About 205 born and about 175 died; the winter fever of the fifth month was lighter among the old and heavier among the small. A few dozen kin of drovers and boat families settled in, a few left; net about 5 added.

stores: the autumn harvest was ordinary: 31,640 sacks counted in at the door over twenty-three days, the far run filled first by the sack with 3,460 (its roll entry) and the near bins with 28,180, every sack stick-counted by a keeper of each of the two families at each end. The old stock carried in was 6,860 sacks (near bins). Closing count: near bins 35,040, far run 3,460, whole store 38,500, squared and cried as the Warden's. Spring walk of the whole store, month 7: 22,360 (near 18,900, far 3,460). By the end of the year the far run is drawn out and the old stock carried into the new harvest is 6,860, all near bins, by the count at the door. This is read from the Keeper's Year 3 figures (the roll's old stock 9,950, what stood 6,630, the gap 3,320, the far run 3,460) with the winter's draw as the Warden's sticks had it. The lads' sticks at the damp corner: 790 tails in the three winter months and 41 sacks pulled spoilt since the harvest.

treasure: middling and unchanged: the granary, the council's weights, scrap silver and bronze in many private chests. Three weights of scrap silver came in from the stranger's passage and lodging. The toll chest, kept by the Clerk, was gathered through the year and by his reckoning stood at about 520 worker-days of hired labor by month 9; it was spent on the dredging with the levy. The boat families' debts to the cooper house and the tanners' house are larger again, since two boat headmen borrowed their levy share from them against next season's carriage.

trade: grain, reed work, salt fish and leather out; stock, wool and metal in, by river, track and coasting boat. Ordinary. Three loaded boats grounded at the river gate bar in the dry spell of month 8, eleven boat-days lost and a third of a load wetted. The dredging cut the bar to about two feet and eight inches at low water at the crown. The Boat Master's string of three went down to the mouth in summer on the ordinary run. The cousin's family's two boats went up to the head of the river in month 10, with the stranger aboard the first: goods to the head were salt, salted fish, beer and reed work; what they took on there is the Keeper's.

strength: a standing watch of about 100 and a call-up of about 500; earth and timber walls. Unchanged.

mood: steadier at the granary: the third autumn's count came the same way and was cried, and the town speaks of it as its own custom. Sore at the levy: the farming houses say they paid for the boat families' river, and the boat houses are in debt to two houses that sit at the table. The two families of the far end are quiet. The Clerk is quiet in his house. More uneasy about the sky: the star dimmed seven times this year and twenty-one times since the stranger came, and the cups set on the river steps after such nights are more than any year before.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The closing count did not reach the far wall and was not squared [r-0297]; the cry was held.
- The stranger came back with the string on the ordinary run and was in the town for the month 11 night.
- On the walk through the store the far bins were found hollow [r-0299]; the Clerk was sent for. What the store holds against the roll is now known to the Warden, the Clerk and the stranger.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- The granary's figures, to be read in place of your own wherever they differ: the roll's old stock 9,950 sacks; what stood, 6,630 (the near bins 6,490 and the 140 facing sacks); the gap, 3,320, a third of the roll; the far run by the roll's entry 3,460, filled with 3,320 new sacks and the 140 facing sacks. The count squared and was cried as the Warden's.
- The evening with the league's tallies: the Clerk put the sticks in the stranger's hand to count. His count was right where the Clerk's was out by one. He said nothing of the stick he paused at.
- The journey up the river went as the crew's ordinary run and he came back with it on the thirteenth day of month 11.
