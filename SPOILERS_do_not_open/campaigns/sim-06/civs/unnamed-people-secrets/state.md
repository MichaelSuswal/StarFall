# Unnamed people of god-secrets: state at the end of Year 3

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent at the close of Year 3 (the year runs autumn to autumn). Last year's state was the civ agent's at the close of Year 2.

population: about 5,120, in the walled town [f35] and on the marsh edges and the boats. Up about 35 from 5,085. About 205 born and about 180 died; the winter fever of the fifth month was heavier among the old than last year's and took the very young less. A few dozen kin of drovers and boat families settled in, a few left; net about 10 added.

stores: the autumn harvest was ordinary. The sacks counted in at the granary door over twenty-three days came to 31,520 (my figure). The far run of twelve bins, found hollow behind its facing, was filled first by the sack before witnesses: 5,860 new sacks and the 140 facing sacks, 6,000 standing. The closing count reached the far wall and squared: near bins 29,670, far run 6,000, whole store 35,670. The old stock that stood was 4,150 sacks against 9,950 in the roll, a gap of 5,800 by the Warden's own count; what became of that grain is not known to the people as a body. Spring count of the whole store, month 7: 19,880. The near bins were drawn first and the far run last, by the sack, with both keepers cutting sticks; the old stock carried into the new harvest is 4,380 sacks by the count at the door. The lads' sticks at the damp corner: 810 tails in the three winter months and 38 sacks pulled spoilt since the harvest. The new harvest is coming in at the year's end and looks ordinary.

treasure: middling and unchanged: the granary, the council's weights, scrap silver and bronze in many private chests. Three weights of scrap silver came in from the stranger's passage up the river and his lodging. A toll of one copper bit on every loaded boat and cart through the river and land gates is ordered from the start of next year for a dredging chest kept by the Clerk; none gathered yet. The boat families' debts to two large houses are larger than the council knows; one keeper of the second tally knows more of it than before.

trade: grain, reed work, salt fish and leather out; stock, wool and metal in, by river, track and coasting boat. Ordinary. Two loaded boats grounded at the river gate bar in the dry spell of month 8, six boat-days lost and about a fourth of a load wetted. The Boat Master's string of three went down to the mouth in summer on the ordinary run. The cousin's family's two boats went up the river to its head in month 10 and came down on the ordinary run, with the stranger aboard the first: their goods to the head were salt, salted fish, beer and reed work; what they took on there is the Keeper's.

strength: a standing watch of about 100 and a call-up of about 500; earth and timber walls. Unchanged.

mood: shaken at the granary and then steadier. The town learned the far bins were hollow behind a facing, a gap of 5,800 sacks, from the Warden's own mouth, and has not turned on him; it credits him with having named it. The two families who keep the far end are sore and not charged. Busy and wary through the winter. The Clerk let the stranger sit one evening with the league tallies, which the market makes much of. The dredging is argued from two springs and is not settled; a rule turns it on next spring's count. The town is more uneasy about the sky: the star dimmed six times this year and fourteen since the stranger came, and the cups set on the river steps after such nights are more than any year before.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The closing count did not reach the far wall and was not squared [r-0297]; the cry was held.
- The stranger came back with the string on the ordinary run and was in the town for the month 11 night.
- On the walk through the store the far bins were found hollow [r-0299]; the Clerk was sent for. What the store holds against the roll is now known to the Warden, the Clerk and the stranger.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- The granary's figures, to be read in place of your own wherever they differ: the roll's old stock 9,950 sacks; what stood, 6,630 (the near bins 6,490 and the 140 facing sacks); the gap, 3,320, a third of the roll; the far run by the roll's entry 3,460, filled with 3,320 new sacks and the 140 facing sacks. The count squared and was cried as the Warden's.
- The evening with the league's tallies: the Clerk put the sticks in the stranger's hand to count. His count was right where the Clerk's was out by one. He said nothing of the stick he paused at.
- The journey up the river went as the crew's ordinary run and he came back with it on the thirteenth day of month 11.
