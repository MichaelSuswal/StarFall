# Report to the Keeper: civ unnamed-people-secrets, Year 3

The god's words in the year file are my rendering from its decisions file, which gives their shape and in several places their exact sentence (the proposal to the Warden, "I do not ask where the grain went and I will not guess", the line on a town that hangs a man for a short bin, "I do not know"). Where the year file puts other words in the stranger's mouth, they are plain and kept to what the decisions say he would say. Where the Keeper's line on Year 2 differs from my Year 2 file, I took the Keeper's line: the cry of the closing count was held, not made.

Figures of mine that the Keeper may wish to square, since the release leaves the hollow's size to me through the counting: the roll's old stock 9,950 sacks, the old stock that stood 4,150 (near bins 4,010 and 140 facing sacks), the gap 5,800, the far run of twelve bins at 6,000 by the roll's entry and filled with 5,860 new sacks and the 140 facing sacks. These are what the Warden counted and says. What became of the grain is not claimed by anyone as a body.

## How the god was met

Autumn, month 1, second day. The stranger went to the Granary Warden in the granary yard, the Clerk of the Tally standing by at the Warden's wish, and put the proposal: carry the harvest in and count it aloud at the door, fill the far bins first by the sack with a keeper from each of the two families who keep that end cutting a stick, set the sticks beside the roll, and bring the gap to the council in his own words before the Clerk and before the stranger. He said he would carry no figure of his own.
- Granary Warden: stiff and slow. Asked whether the stranger knew where the grain went, and why he would not take the figure to the council himself. Took the proposal on his own terms (told the two heads himself, took the facing down himself, the stranger on the bench by the door and not near the far run, the gap in his own mouth) and added that the roll was his to walk. Took the counsel as a trader's, not a god's word.
- Clerk of the Tally: stood by, then set the sticks beside the roll as his own office. Took no word.

Month 1, third day. The Warden told the heads of the two families who keep the far end, the cooper house and the old farming family of the east fields. Both said the roll is wrong. Neither said he knew where the grain went. Neither took any word of the stranger's; neither had it from him.

Month 2, fourth day, council. The Speaker (head of a large boat family), asked through the Clerk, agreed that the two families be heard before any word of blame. The Warden spoke first, the heads second, the Clerk laid the sticks. The east fields head moved a search of the Warden's house, his uncle's old house and the carters' yards; no one seconded. A headman of a boat family asked the stranger what he made of the gap; he said what the sticks say and whose they are. A farming head said a Warden who walked nothing for nineteen years should stand down; the stranger said a town that hangs a man for a short bin will not be told the next one is short, and that if the table turned to blame he would leave. The Speaker held the table. The council ordered the Warden to walk the roll each spring and autumn and the closing count to be cried each harvest with the far run's figure by itself. Council and Speaker took no word.

Month 2, sixth day. The Crier cried the closing count at the Warden's stone, the far run by itself, as the Warden's. Took no word.

Winter. Month 4: the Gate Warden asked the stranger at the watch's fire where the grain went; he said he does not ask and will not guess. The Gate Warden said the answer sounds the same from a man who knows and a man who does not. Took no word. Month 5, fourteenth day: the Clerk, asked over a cup once more for an evening with the league tallies on his terms, said yes, and on the twenty-second night opened the chest himself and counted three tallies with the stranger. Took no word; says the man counts better than he does. Month 6: the daughter of the dead reed cutter sat by the stranger on the bench and asked whether he would hear what her father began; he said he would rather carry nothing he would not be glad to carry and asked whether it was hers to give and whether she means someone to carry it; she did not know; he said she should keep it until she does. No new telling is held, by my count. How many he holds is the god's to say. In the same month a girl of nine asked him on the quay whether he makes the star go dim; he said he does not know.

Spring. Month 7: the Boat Master gave the stranger the first loaded boat at the gate (a boat of the cousin's family) and had his crews sound the bar at the spring high water. Took his counsel as a trader's. Month 8: the Clerk set the two springs' sticks and the two groundings' costs beside the dredging's; asked by a farming head if he had anything to add, the stranger said the sticks say what they say, whose they are, and said nothing of what the council should do. The council made a rule from the count. Took the counsel, not a god's word.

Summer. Month 9: the Gate Warden asked the stranger what he meant to do; told he would go up the river on a crew for about a month; said it was the third sensible thing the man had done. Month 10: the Boat Master gave a berth on the upriver crew of his cousin's family for three weights of scrap silver, and asked why; told the stranger means to see what lies on the water above the town. The Clerk was told only that he means to be back before the harvest.

No office holder has taken the stranger's word as a god's. The Granary Warden, the Clerk and the Boat Master took his counsel as a trader's. The Gate Warden, the Crier and the Speakers did not. No office is the god's helper as of Year 3. If the Keeper holds that taking a counsel in the course of trade, or a proposal put in the Warden's own terms, is taking the word, that is the Keeper's ruling, not mine.

## What was done in the god's name

Nothing. The far bins were filled and counted as the Warden's act; the Warden said the roll was his to walk. The Crier cried the count as the Warden's. The Clerk marked the sticks as his own office. The soundings were done as the Boat Master's. The council's rule and toll are the council's.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper

1. The journey up the river. Who: the stranger, as a weigher and counter, aboard the first of two planked boats of the Boat Master's cousin's family, the steersman the cousin's eldest son (about thirty-one, eight runs up before), a crew of four to each boat. When: they set out from the town quay at first light on the sixteenth day of month 10. By what way: up the River Iss [f11], from the town to its head, 39.7 miles, by pole and tow. The ordinary run: four days up with a day lost to the current, about three weeks lying at the families' landing at the head to sell salt, salted fish, beer and reed work and take on what the landing offers, then down in two days; about twenty-eight days, back on or about the thirteenth day of month 11. Success would mean the stranger sees what the Keeper has at the head and the crew's ordinary trade goes as it goes; failure would mean the run is changed, the stranger or the crew is delayed, held or harmed, or the stranger does not come back before the harvest. What lies at the head, who keeps the landing, who is met there beyond the boat families, what is bought and what the stranger sees and does there, and whether he returns with the crew, later but before the harvest, or not at all, is the Keeper's. Circumstances from my record: the people's knowledge file gives the river's mouth and nothing up the river; the boat families go up each summer; the river's depths are about two feet at the crown of the bar at the gate in summer low water and a loaded boat draws about two feet; the Keeper's own line is that the stranger came back from the mouth on the ordinary run last year. The year file writes the three outcomes briefly. No star night falls in the summer, so the question of where he was on a night does not arise this year.

2. The Clerk's evening with the league tallies, month 5, the twenty-second night. Not an attempt in the sense of a contested outcome: the Clerk said yes and the evening was held on his terms. What the stranger learned or saw beyond counting notches is the Keeper's. The one thing the Clerk saw: the stranger's count and the Clerk's differed by one notch on the third tally and the stranger was right on recount; and he paused longer at one stick than at the others and did not say why.

3. No search, charge or spoiled count came of the far bins. The east fields head's motion to search the Warden's house, the uncle's old house and the carters' yards had no second and fell at the table. The count of the whole store squared, the far run was counted in by the sack before witnesses, and no one tried to spoil it. I record this so the Keeper knows I wrote none.

## What turns on a power

- The six nights of the dimmed star: month 1 (one), month 5 (a pair, the eighth and the thirteenth), month 7 (a pair, the fifteenth and the seventeenth), month 8 (one, the twenty-fourth). The town counts fourteen since the stranger came. The stranger was in the town on every one of them. The people have only talk: the stranger brings it, it comes oftener, it dims for a short bin, the marsh folk block it. What the stranger did or did not do on them is the Keeper's. The decisions file says he did nothing he knows of.
- A bone-setter at the marsh edge and a woman who finds strayed beasts said their hands were dull for a day after the second night in month 5. No one else said it. I treat it as talk. If hedge-working costs have to do with the star, that is the Keeper's.
- The stranger's count of the notches on the Clerk's third tally, right where the Clerk's was wrong by one. I treat it as skill with figures. If it turns on a power, that is the Keeper's.
- The people's account of the soundings and the far bins does not turn on a power.

## What touches anyone else

- Trade: unchanged in kind. Drovers from the Ansel Plain [r04] came to market as every year; coasting boats came up in the summer months and went down again. Two loaded boats grounded at the river gate bar in month 8, in a dry spell, at one foot and seven inches at the crown.
- The string to the mouth: the Boat Master's three boats went down in summer on the ordinary run, as last year. Nothing of note is recorded.
- The upriver crew: two planked boats of the cousin's family, carrying salt, salted fish, beer and reed work, with the stranger aboard the first, paid three weights of scrap silver for passage and keep. No message carried by anyone, to anyone. What is met at the head beyond the boat families is the Keeper's.
- Travelers: the stranger only. No stranger came to the gates but the ordinary traders and drovers.
- Parties sent out: the upriver crew, as the boat families' ordinary run.
- Nothing carried to any other people. No migrants of note.

## Asked of the god

The questions and requests mortals put to the stranger, in their words as near as I can set them, and whether he answered in front of them.
1. Granary Warden, month 1, second day: "Do you know where it went?" Answered: no; he does not ask where it went and will not guess.
2. Granary Warden, same: "Why will you not take the figure to the council yourself, since you have it?" Answered: that a loss a man is allowed to name and mend is a loss he will go on finding, and a loss another names for him is one he will hide.
3. Boat headman, month 2, council table: "What do you make of the gap?" Answered in front of the council: what the sticks say, and whose sticks they are.
4. Farming head, month 2, council table (put to the table, not to him): that a Warden who walked nothing for nineteen years should stand down. Answered by the stranger, in front of the council: that a town that hangs a man for a short bin will not be told the next one is short, and that if the table turned to blame he would leave. The table did not turn and he did not leave.
5. Gate Warden, month 4, the watch's fire: "Where did the grain go?" Answered in front of the watch captain: he does not ask where it went and will not guess.
6. Clerk, month 5, over a cup: "Why did you put the figure to the Warden and not to the council?" Answered as to the Warden. The Clerk then said yes to the evening, on his terms.
7. Reed cutter's daughter, month 6, the granary bench: "My father began a thing to you and did not finish it. Will you hear it from me?" Answered: he would rather carry nothing he would not be glad to carry; he asked whether it was hers to give and whether she means someone to carry it. She did not know. He said she should keep it until she does.
8. A girl of nine of the boat families, month 6, the quay: "Are you the one who makes the star go dim?" Answered: "I do not know." She said her grandmother says he does and her uncle says he does not; he said they may both be right.
9. Gate Warden, month 9, the market bench: "What do you mean to do this summer?" and "Why go where there is nothing to count?" Answered: he will go up the river on a crew for about a month; he does not know that there is nothing.
10. Boat Master, month 10: "Why do you want to go up the river?" Answered: he may take the berth or leave it; he means to see what lies on the water above the town, who keeps it and who trades on it.
11. Granary Warden, month 11, if the stranger is back: no question put; the stranger asked to look at the store as it stands and said it is well kept.

Offers and requests the stranger put, for the record: a proposal to the Warden (count aloud, far bins first by the sack with a keeper from each of the two families, sticks beside the roll, the gap in the Warden's own words); a request through the Clerk that the two families be heard before blame and the count cried with the far run's figure by itself; an evening with the league tallies on the Clerk's terms; the first loaded boat at the gate and soundings at high and low water; the two springs' depths and costs beside the dredging's; a berth upriver as a weigher.

## Names said aloud

- "The Counter" (a nickname for the stranger), said at the council table, in the market and at the stalls through the year. Not a name the stranger gave.
- "Ivo Strand", said by the steersman of the first upriver boat at the town quay, month 10, the sixteenth day, at first light, to the water, in the boat families' words "Ivo Strand's water, Ivo Strand's fortune", with the stranger aboard, the crew and the dockmen present.
- A name of the Warden's uncle was said under his breath by the east fields head's grandson at the far run on the fourth day of month 1, in the hearing of the keepers and the boat headman who told him to hold his tongue. I did not set the name down, and no name is set down for the uncle.
- No name has been said aloud for my people, the town, or any office holder. The Clerk of the Tally, the Granary Warden, the Gate Warden, the Boat Master, the Crier, the Speakers, the steersman, the heads of the cooper house and of the east fields family, the second tally keeper of the cooper family, the reed cutter and his daughter stay unnamed.
