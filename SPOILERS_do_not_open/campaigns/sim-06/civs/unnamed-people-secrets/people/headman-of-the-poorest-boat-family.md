# The headman of the poorest boat family (unnamed)

Filed by office; no name has been said aloud. Fixed in Year 5.

## Nature
About fifty years old. Plain, proud and in debt, a headman who said across the council's table in Year 2 that his house owes more than the open books show to houses that sit at it, and who has said little since. A decent table.

## History
Has holdings on the marsh edge. Borrowed his levy share in Year 4 from the tanners' house or the cooper house, against next season's carriage.

## Year 5
- Month 8: his boat grounded at the shoal above the cut in the dry spell and sat three days, losing the carriage.
- Month 11, third day: took two weights of scrap silver from the stranger for carrying him down to the marsh edge, and asked why the man wanted to sit at the boat houses' tables. Was told it was to hear their side in their own words.
- Month 11, fifth day: said the old words to the water at the town quay with the stranger aboard.
- At his own table said the count the stranger began is the first book that has the boat houses in it, and has them in it as the ones who owe, and that his people do not know whether to thank the man for it.
- Does not take the stranger as a god; he does not know of one.

## Standing
Not on the council's inner ring. Sits at the long table by custom. Liked on the marsh edge.
