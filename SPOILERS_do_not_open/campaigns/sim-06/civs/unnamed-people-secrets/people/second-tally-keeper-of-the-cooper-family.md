# The second tally keeper of the cooper family (unnamed)

Filed by office of her own making; no name has been said aloud. Fixed in Year 1.

## Nature
A woman of about forty, the widow of a cooper's second son. Quick, silent among people, and honest with figures in a house where the figures are not. She keeps the books of the large family that coopers and brews. She does not trust the Clerk, whom she thinks would enter her family's second tally in the rolls and shame it. She does not trust her own family's head, who set her the task. She keeps both the open tally and the second.

## What she told the stranger
Told in the deep of the winter, on the granary yard bench, unasked, and not repeated by her to anyone else.
- That her family keeps a second set of tallies, as does one other large family.
- That the second tally holds what the family has lent out in grain, beer and barrels to the boat families against the next season's carriage, at a price that is not the market's price and which neither the council nor the Clerk knows.
- That the other large family, the tanners and leather dealers, holds a similar second tally against the same boat families, and the two families have each begun to hold the boat families in debt at the same time, each unaware of the other's amount.
- That the sum does not close: the second tally and the open tally together lack about a sixth of what the barrels and the grain should have brought in. She does not know whether the loss is hers, her family head's, or a boat family's.
- That the dredging quarrel is tied to this, since the boat families cannot pay a levy while they are in the two families' debt, and the two families will not forgive it while the river grounds the boats that earn the debt back.

She asked the stranger whether the sum closed. He told her it did not.

## Standing and risk
Nobody in her family knows she spoke. If it were known she would lose her place and perhaps more. She does not come to the bench again that winter. In the spring she nods to him in the market street and does not stop.

## Year 2 (added by the civ agent)
- Winter, month 5: nodded to the stranger at the granary yard bench and went by. Did not sit.
- Month 8: heard, from the gallery, a boat headman say at the council table that his family owes more than the open books show to houses at that table. She did not look at her family's head, nor he at her. She has told no one. Nobody in her family knows she spoke to the stranger last winter.

## Year 3 (added by the civ agent)
- Month 1: sent by her head to the far run as her house's keeper, cutting a stick at each bin for the twenty-three days. Her stick agreed with the Warden's to the sack every day. Saw the facing come down.
- Month 6: went at night to the Granary Warden's door and told him, standing in the doorway, that the second tally of her house has lines for barley lent out against the next season's carriage, that she cannot say they account for the far run, and that she will not say it at the table. Asked why she should be believed; was told he had not asked her to be.
- Did not go to the stranger. Nobody in her family knows she went to the Warden.

## Year 4 (added by the civ agent)
- Month 1: sent by her head to the far run again; her stick agreed with the Warden's to the sack on every day.
- Month 6: went at night to the Warden's door a second time and told him her house has lent a third more than the open tally shows, to the boat families. Did not go to the stranger.
