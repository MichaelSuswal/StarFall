# The Granary Warden (unnamed)

Filed by office; no name has been said aloud. Fixed in Year 2.

## Nature
About fifty-two years old in Year 2. Stiff, civil and proud of his book, a man who took the office from an uncle and has kept the bins nineteen years. Dislikes being handed anything and suspects a kindness of carrying a price. Slow to decide, and once decided he does it his own way and does not let it be done for him. Shamed in front of witnesses last year when the count ran short, and easier for it.

## History
Born in the town to a granary family. Keeps the tally sticks and the clay and wax books of the harvest and the bins. Has a sister whose son, fourteen, is in his household, and a ward of twelve.

## Year 2
- Month 1, second day: heard the stranger's bargain in the granary yard, with the Clerk standing by at his own wish. Took it on his own terms: his own crew, his own sticks, the count cried as his, the stranger on the bench by the door and not on the floor. Did not take the stranger as a god; he does not know of one.
- Month 1: ran the carrying in (31,400 sacks counted in over twenty-three days) and the closing count, and stood at the Crier's stone for the cry in month 2. The council ordered the closing count cried every harvest as his.
- Set two lads on the damp corner and the rats. Holds that the rats take more than the tails show.
- At the end of the summer let the stranger walk the store with him. See the year file.

## Standing
Respected, now a little warmer to the stranger than the Gate Warden is, though not his friend. Sits at the council's long table by custom.
