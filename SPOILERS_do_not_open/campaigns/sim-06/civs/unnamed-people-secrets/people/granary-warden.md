# The Granary Warden (unnamed)

Filed by office; no name has been said aloud. Fixed in Year 2.

## Nature
About fifty-two years old in Year 2. Stiff, civil and proud of his book, a man who took the office from an uncle and has kept the bins nineteen years. Dislikes being handed anything and suspects a kindness of carrying a price. Slow to decide, and once decided he does it his own way and does not let it be done for him. Shamed in front of witnesses last year when the count ran short, and easier for it.

## History
Born in the town to a granary family. Keeps the tally sticks and the clay and wax books of the harvest and the bins. Has a sister whose son, fourteen, is in his household, and a ward of twelve.

## Year 2
- Month 1, second day: heard the stranger's bargain in the granary yard, with the Clerk standing by at his own wish. Took it on his own terms: his own crew, his own sticks, the count cried as his, the stranger on the bench by the door and not on the floor. Did not take the stranger as a god; he does not know of one.
- Month 1: ran the carrying in (31,400 sacks counted in over twenty-three days) and the closing count, and stood at the Crier's stone for the cry in month 2. The council ordered the closing count cried every harvest as his.
- Set two lads on the damp corner and the rats. Holds that the rats take more than the tails show.
- At the end of the summer let the stranger walk the store with him. See the year file.

## Standing
Respected, now a little warmer to the stranger than the Gate Warden is, though not his friend. Sits at the council's long table by custom.

## Year 3 (added by the civ agent)
- Month 1, second day: heard the stranger's proposal in the granary yard with the Clerk standing by at his own wish. Took it on his own terms: told the two heads himself, took the facing down himself, carried the gap to the council in his own mouth. Asked whether the stranger knew where the grain went, and was told he did not ask and would not guess. Does not take the stranger as a god; he does not know of one.
- Month 1, fourth day: took down the 140 facing sacks of the far run of twelve bins before both families' keepers and found bare floor behind. Filled the far run first by the sack, 5,860 new sacks, 6,000 standing. The closing count squared: whole store 35,670, the gap 5,800 sacks against his roll by his own count.
- Month 2, fourth day: stood at the council and gave the gap, and said the roll was his to walk and he had carried it forward without walking it. The council ordered him to walk the roll every spring and every autumn. Stood at the Crier's stone for the cry on the sixth.
- Month 6: was told at his own door by the cooper house's second tally keeper that her house's second tally has lines for barley lent out. Said he would carry only what he had counted. Keeps a stick of it behind his lintel.
- Month 11: walked the store with the stranger on his return, if he returned. The far run stood behind its four marks.
- Standing: has not been turned on. A man who named his own floor. Does not like to be spoken of.

## Year 4 (added by the civ agent)
- Month 1, second day: heard the stranger ask for the count the council had already ordered. Said so. Did it in his own way.
- Month 1, fourth to twenty-seventh day: carried in 31,640 sacks, the far run first (3,460) and both ends stick-counted by keepers of both families. Whole store 38,500. Gave the count to the council himself on the fourth day of month 2 and stood at the Crier's stone on the sixth.
- Month 6: was told once more at his own door by the cooper house's second tally keeper that her house has lent a third more than the open tally shows. Put a third stick behind the lintel.
- Month 7, eighth day: walked the store, 22,360.
- Still does not take the stranger as a god; he does not know of one.

## Year 5 (added by the civ agent)
- Month 1, third day, evening: heard the stranger say the count was his this year and that he would not stand at the door. Said the man was asking to be shown he was not needed, and that it was not a trader's reason. Sent his sister's son to the two heads' doors with a word and not himself.
- Month 1, fourth to twenty-sixth day: stood at the door himself and carried in 31,760 sacks, the far run first (3,460) and both ends stick-counted by keepers of both families. Whole store 38,620. Gave the count to the council himself on the fourth day of month 2 and stood at the Crier's stone on the sixth.
- Month 6: was told at his own door a third time by the cooper house's second tally keeper. Told her she might carry her own to the Clerk. Put a fourth stick behind the lintel.
- Month 7, sixth day: walked the store, 22,320.
- Still does not take the stranger as a god; he does not know of one.
