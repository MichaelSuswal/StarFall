# What the unnamed people now say: Year 1

Written by the civ agent. This is the people's own telling, and it is wrong about things that matter. No god is named by the people, because none is known to them.

## The stranger
- They call him the Counter. A watchman on the land gate said it on his first day and the market took it up. Many do not know he has no name in the book, only a mark.
- They say he came down the track from the south-west with scales on his belt and no goods, paid his way, kept to himself, and never asked for anything the town would not give a guest. That is true, and they think it dull, which is why they find it odd.
- They say the books come right behind him. This began as a market joke after the count was cried. An old woman at the tanners' end said that the old people said a god would come as a stranger who counts, and that you would know him by the way the books come right. Most laugh. Some do not. Her son hushes her. What the people do not see is that the count was cried and made right because the Clerk and the Warden did it aloud before witnesses, and that the stranger was on a bench at the door saying nothing. They credit his arithmetic, not their own count.
- The Clerk says a book comes right when a man counts it. The Gate Warden says a man with no name is a man to be watched, and that the town does not need a god to count its own bins.
- The stranger gave no name and was a quiet man. They say a man who will not give his name has something to be quiet about, a debt, a crime or a family, and they argue which. This is wrong about what matters.

## The dim star
- The star dimmed on three nights, two close together in the first month and one in the fifth. The town has seen such nights in other years and does not trouble over them.
- This year the market talked about it more because the stranger came the same month. Some say the stranger brings the dimming. Some say the dimming brought the stranger. Some say the marsh folk walk across the face of the star on their night errands and block it a while. The old say the star dims when the river is low. None of these is right, and none has anything to do with the river.

## The dog
- The river quay watchman and a bone-setter at the marsh edge both tell of a brown dog the town's dogs did not bark at, in the dead of the winter. A boy tells of it a month late. They tell it as a dog story and not in the same place. The talk is that it was a marsh dog, or a ghost of one, or a messenger of the odd folk of the Iss Marsh [f36]. Nobody connects it to the stranger.

## Their own rulers
- The council: the stool at the foot of the table, given by five to four, is the council's own business and a mark of the Clerk's judgment, they say. The four who were against say it will end badly. The five say it ended the argument over the store, which is true and not the cause they think.
- The count cried at market is now talked of as a custom, as if it had always been done. It was done for the first time in living memory. People say the old Clerks did it, and are wrong about that.
- The dredging: the farming families say the boat families should pay, because the count shows the boat families send the most grain down the river. The boat families say the count is a stranger's count and no friend of theirs. Both are partly right and both blame the Counter for it.

## Their neighbors
- Drovers from the plain bring stock as always and say nothing new. Coasting boats came in summer and went down again, and spoke of the harbor town on the Garrow Shore [r01] as they do.
- Word has gone down the river on the boats that a counter sits at the foot of the council's table in the town on the bend. It is told as a curiosity.

## The old things
- The Clerk still keeps the league tallies in a chest and the walkers' names in a private book. The clerks argue over both this winter as they do every winter. The stranger asked about them, and word of that went round the clerks and nowhere else. Some clerks say he asked because he can read them. He said he cannot read at all.

## The odd folk of the marsh
- They say the odd folk deal by night, and that they were here before any of the town's people. Nothing new this year but the dog.
