# Unnamed people of god-sea: state at the end of Year 3

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent. The year closes at the opening of autumn, before the last catch is counted. Where a figure turns on the boat in the swell (month 5) it is given both ways, [BOAT CAME IN] and [BOAT LOST]; where it turns on the Harbormaster's lead along the chain's line (month 8) it is given both ways, [LEAD FOUND] and [LEAD NOTHING]. The Keeper's pass two says which stands.

population: [BOAT CAME IN] about 2,031. [BOAT LOST] about 2,029. Births about 73 (a usual year for 2,000 on a lean coast), deaths about 65 (chest fevers among the old in winter, a cold in the sixth month, a thin spring among the old and the very young, the eldest of the smokers of his wasting in the second month, and in the lost branch two drowned, making about 67). Why: from 2,023 at the end of Year 2, an ordinary year with no hunger death and no boat lost to a storm.

stores: the racks and the salt loft hold ordinary stock for the winter ahead; the shared store of the lean time was spent and is refilling. Grain is thin: ten loads came in from the coasting boats. Sound oak for one hull's keel and some planking were bought; the new hull is on the blocks in the yard on a keel of sound timber [KEEPER]. Why: the last catch was cut by about ten days of early hauling a third year, a third of the households' own fish went into the shared store again and was shared evenly, and the summer fishing was fair. The hauling and the sharing are now the custom of the quay and the court's, not the Warden-Monarch's word.

treasure: bronze in the court's chest unchanged; the old silver a few pieces lighter, paid for oak. Nets and boats as before, with the Captains' stand-by boat launched on her new keel in the first month and one new hull building in the yard. Why: trade was by barter for the most part, and the only coin out was for oak.

trade: salt fish, salt and rope out; wool, mutton, a little grain and sound oak in. The shepherds came twice (the start and the end of summer), and three coasting boats from the Iss Levels [r07] in the eleventh month. Why: an ordinary year, with the oak bought because the Master of Timber has reasons to buy it.

strength: about 150 in the spring-drilled levy, drilled in the eighth month; the walls and the harbor chain as before, with the chain's post renewed at its foot. The chain truce held: the Harbormaster answers for raising and lowering, and a lamp-tender of the line holds its key and writes each use in the harbor-book (twelve uses). The senior Captain now knows whose the lamp is. Why: no fighting and no loss to the levy.
- [LEAD FOUND] The Captains took timber from under the line and know it; the levy's two Captains are less easy with the quay and the line each than they were.
- [LEAD NOTHING] Nothing was found and the Captains say little of it.

mood: patient and watchful as before, and divided in the court, now more openly. The Warden-Monarch and the Master of Timber are quietly hopeful. The Eldest Midwife has met the keeping and says it is honest, and she says so to the court; the Harbormaster does not take the word, has begun to keep a tally of his own that has three entries, and put the question of the two lost boats to a lead and grapnel himself. Among the people at large, curious and a little afraid: a figure at the water's edge with the lamp a third year, the quiet at the point through the lean, and six nights of the star dimmed in one year against five the year before and three the year before that.
- [BOAT CAME IN] Why: the third year running a boat came in under the lamp. The east cove is grateful, the town more at ease with the keeping, and a little more at odds with the Harbormaster, who says the crew did it.
- [BOAT LOST] Why: two drowned in the lamp's light. The east cove is bitter and quiet, the line is held against it in private, the Harbormaster's doubt is louder, and the Warden-Monarch's standing is thinner outside the court than at the end of Year 2.
- [LEAD FOUND] Why: the Harbormaster lifted timber from under the chain's line, tells the Warden-Monarch the keeping was right, and asks what else it knows. The quarrel over the chain is held and not mended, each side says the other knew, and the town talks of the boats and the chain for the first time in three winters. He does not say he takes the word.
- [LEAD NOTHING] Why: the Harbormaster found nothing, told the Warden-Monarch so and that the keeping may say where, and the quarrel over the chain stands as it was.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The crab boat came in with all four [r-0296]: population 2,023.
- The keeping's lamp and the harbor lamp are two lamps, as written. The tenders tended it right. The quay board and both keels are sound, the first keel clean after a year.
