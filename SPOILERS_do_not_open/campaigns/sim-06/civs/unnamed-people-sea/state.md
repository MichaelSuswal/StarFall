# Unnamed people of god-sea: state at the end of Year 5

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent. The year closes at the opening of autumn, before the last catch is counted. Where a figure turns on the boat in the swell (month 11) it is given both ways, [BOAT CAME IN] and [BOAT LOST]. The Keeper's pass two says which stands. The Year 4 outcomes are taken as the Keeper gave them (the decked boat of the point broke at the mouth, her master and his elder son drowned, the younger son and the hired hand were hauled out; the lamp lit by the tenders without the keeping's hand calmed nothing).

population: [BOAT CAME IN] about 2,045. [BOAT LOST] about 2,043. Births about 73 (a usual year for 2,000 on a lean coast), deaths about 65 (chest fevers among the old in winter, a cold in the sixth month, a thin spring among the old and the very young), and in the lost branch two drowned, making about 67. Why: from 2,037 at the end of Year 4, an ordinary year with four storms none worse than the town knows, no hunger death and no boat lost to a storm.

stores: the racks and the salt loft hold ordinary stock for the winter ahead; the shared store of the lean was spent and is refilling. Grain is thin: nine loads came in from the coasting boats. Sound oak for one keel and some planking was bought, and a second keel timber lies in the yard's loft. The point has a decked boat again, smaller than the one lost, on a keel of the yard's oak [KEEPER]; the ferryman's boat has a new sheer strake [KEEPER]. Why: the last catch was cut by about ten days of early hauling a fifth year and by a day or two more for the Skerries rule; the long swells of the winter kept boats in for four days and the summer's added swells for a few more; a third of the households' own fish went into the shared store again and was shared evenly through the lean; the summer fishing was fair.

treasure: bronze in the court's chest unchanged; the old silver a few pieces lighter, paid for oak and grain. The yard's keel and strake were given and nothing paid. Nets and boats as before, with the point's new boat and the strake. Why: trade was by barter for the most part, and the only coin out was for oak.

trade: salt fish, salt and rope out; wool, mutton, a little grain and sound oak in. The shepherds came twice (the start and the end of summer), and three coasting boats from the Iss Levels [r07] in the eleventh month. No hull came in that was not known on this coast, and no stranger came by land. Why: an ordinary year.

strength: about 150 in the spring drilled levy, drilled in the eighth month at the chain and, for the first time, at the mouth with a long line and a heaving line; the walls and the harbor chain as before. The chain truce held and was changed: every raising is written by two hands, the quay's man and the line's tender. The chain was used eleven times, one of them the boat in the swell. The long line is made fast to the quay head's outer pile when a boat is caught. Why: no fighting and no loss to the levy; the two lost boats found under the chain's line put the key on two hands.
- [BOAT CAME IN] The Captains' boat took her line at the mouth and the Captains are easier with one another and with the quay.
- [BOAT LOST] The Captains' boat hauled two out and lost two to the sea, and the senior Captain says little of it.

mood: patient and watchful as before, and divided in the court, though less sharply than a year ago. The Warden-Monarch has been told she had the right and has told the court. The Eldest Midwife says the keeping gives half answers and calls them half. The Harbormaster does not take the word, and has found the nine with his own lead where he was told to look, and has put a sixth line in his tally and taken the old question out of the jar. The Master of Timber is quietly hopeful. The point is bitter and quiet, and has a boat; the younger son has left bread on the step, and the widow has not. The line and the quay each say the other raised the chain, in the quiet, and neither says it where it can be heard. Among the people at large, curious and a little afraid: a spring with the point quiet on many nights and the star dimmed three times in the year against seven before it.
- [BOAT CAME IN] Why: a boat caught in a long swell came in under the long line, the bell and the lamp lit as a light, with the point kept ashore by the court's own rule. The town does not agree why, and the Warden-Monarch's standing outside the court is mended. The Harbormaster put a seventh line in his tally.
- [BOAT LOST] Why: two drowned at the mouth with the long line out, the bell struck twice and twice again, and the point kept ashore by the court's own rule. The quay is bitter and the point is bitter and the two together are quiet. The Harbormaster says a rule is not a lamp and not a keeper. The Warden-Monarch's standing outside the court is thinner than at the end of Year 4. He put a seventh line in his tally.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The crab boat came in with all four [r-0296]: population 2,023.
- The keeping's lamp and the harbor lamp are two lamps, as written. The tenders tended it right. The quay board and both keels are sound, the first keel clean after a year.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- The long-liner came in with all three. Your numbers take that branch: about 2,031. The tenders tended the lamp right.
- The Harbormaster's lead and grapnel took nothing that is a hull, on either morning. Your year takes that branch.
- The chain post's heel and the crab boat's keel are sound.
- At the bedside the keeping said what it counts and showed its shape at her asking. It gave no answer to her four questions.
- The keeping had the net-mender's slip from the jar. It heard the Warden's telling of the Harbormaster's answer in the ninth month. It was not in that water for the telling in the seventh.

## Keeper, Year 4, pass two
- The lamp lit by the tenders, without the keeping's hand, burned as a lamp does and calmed nothing.
- The decked boat of the point broke at the harbor mouth on the twentieth day of the eighth month. Her master and his elder son drowned. The younger son and the hired hand were hauled out by the Captains' boat. Your numbers take that branch.
- The keeping was back in the water by the town in the fourth week of the ninth month.
- The pile and the strake are sound.

## Keeper, Year 5, pass two
- The boat in the swell on the nineteenth day of the eleventh month came in, all four, on the Captains' line. The keeping came at the first pair of strokes, within a quarter of an hour: a hooded figure at the water's edge below the lamp, which put out the flame the old woman had lit and lit the lamp again with its own hand. From then the mouth lay calm as far as the lamp's light would show. Your numbers take that branch: about 2,045.
- The lamp lit by the tenders as a light was a light. The calm came only when the keeping lit it.
- The lead rang on stone and the grapnel lifted a strake and a rib, as you wrote. The three pale stones are stones.
- Both pieces of timber the keeping joined are sound.
- The Warden-Monarch's telling to the water in the second month was heard.
- The notches and the mark are the near shoal as a swimmer learns it; the Harbormaster's lead found it as the notches give.
