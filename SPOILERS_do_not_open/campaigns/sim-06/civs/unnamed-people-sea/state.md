# Unnamed people of god-sea: state at the end of Year 2

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent. The year closes at the opening of autumn, before the last catch is counted. Where a figure turns on the boat in the swell (month 5) it is given both ways, [BOAT CAME IN] and [BOAT LOST]; the Keeper's pass two says which stands.

population: [BOAT CAME IN] about 2,023. [BOAT LOST] about 2,021. Births about 74 (a usual year for 2,000 on a lean coast), deaths about 62 (chest fevers among the old in winter, a thin spring among the old and the very young, one old man dead of a chill in the long storm, and in the lost branch two drowned). Why: from 2,011 at the end of Year 1, an ordinary year with no hunger death and no boat lost to a storm.

stores: the racks and the salt loft hold ordinary stock for the winter ahead; the shared store of the lean time was spent and is refilling. Grain is thin: nine loads came in from the coasting boats. Sound oak planks for one hull's keel and some planking were bought; the keel timber in store at the start of the year went into the stand-by boat's keel. Why: the last catch was cut by about ten days of early hauling again, a third of the households' own fish went into the shared store again and was shared evenly, and the summer fishing was fair.

treasure: bronze in the court's chest unchanged; the old silver a few pieces lighter, paid for oak. Nets and boats as before, with one new hull building in the yard on a keel of sound timber [KEEPER] and planking in store. Why: trade was by barter for the most part, and the only coin out was for oak.

trade: salt fish, salt and rope out; wool, mutton, a little grain and sound oak in. The shepherds came twice (the start and the end of summer), and three coasting boats from the Iss Levels [r07] in the eleventh month. Why: an ordinary year, with the oak bought because the Master of Timber has reasons to buy it.

strength: about 150 in the spring-drilled levy, drilled in the eighth month; the walls and the harbor chain as before. The chain truce held: the Harbormaster answers for raising and lowering, and a lamp-tender of the line holds its key and writes each use in the harbor-book (eleven uses). The Captains' stand-by boat is being re-keeled. Why: no fighting and no loss to the levy.

mood: patient and watchful as before, and divided in the court as before. The Warden-Monarch and the Master of Timber are quietly hopeful. The Harbormaster does what the court orders, does not take the word, and has begun to put the thing to the test of a tide in a tally of his own. The Eldest Midwife still waits and has said so to the step. Among the people at large, curious and a little afraid: a figure at the water's edge with the lamp, the listener gone into the water through the lean, and five nights of the star dimmed in one year against three the year before.
- [BOAT CAME IN] Why: the second year running a boat came in under the lamp. The town is grateful and more at ease with the keeping than the year before, and a little more at odds with the Harbormaster, who says the crew did it.
- [BOAT LOST] Why: two drowned in the lamp's light. The town is bitter and quiet. The line is held against it in private, the Harbormaster's doubt is louder, the Warden-Monarch's standing is thinner outside the court than at the end of Year 1, and two households of the headland cove are in mourning.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The crab boat came in with all four [r-0296]: population 2,023.
- The keeping's lamp and the harbor lamp are two lamps, as written. The tenders tended it right. The quay board and both keels are sound, the first keel clean after a year.
