# Unnamed people of god-sea: state at the end of Year 4

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent. The year closes at the opening of autumn, before the last catch is counted. Where a figure turns on the boat in the swell (month 8, while the god was away) it is given both ways, [BOAT CAME IN] and [BOAT LOST]. The Keeper's pass two says which stands. The Year 3 outcomes are taken as the Keeper gave them (the long-liner came in; the Harbormaster's lead took nothing that is a hull).

population: [BOAT CAME IN] about 2,039. [BOAT LOST] about 2,037. Births about 74 (a usual year for 2,000 on a lean coast), deaths about 66 (chest fevers among the old in winter, a cold in the sixth month, a thin spring among the old and the very young, a widow of the east cove of her wasting in the tenth month), and in the lost branch two drowned, making about 68. Why: from 2,031 at the end of Year 3, an ordinary year with no hunger death and no boat lost to a storm.

stores: the racks and the salt loft hold ordinary stock for the winter ahead; the shared store of the lean time was spent and is refilling. Grain is thin: eleven loads came in from the coasting boats. Sound oak for one keel and some planking was bought. No new hull is on the blocks; the east cove's long-liner has a new garboard strake of sound timber [KEEPER]. Why: the last catch was cut by about ten days of early hauling a fourth year and by a few days more for the Skerries rule; a third of the households' own fish went into the shared store again and was shared evenly through the lean; the winter fishing was dry for the six days after the fourth storm and the summer fishing was fair.

treasure: bronze in the court's chest unchanged; the old silver a few pieces lighter, paid for oak. Nets and boats as before, with the headland cove's crab boat launched on her new keel in the first month [KEEPER]. Why: trade was by barter for the most part, and the only coin out was for oak.

trade: salt fish, salt and rope out; wool, mutton, a little grain and sound oak in. The shepherds came twice (the start and the end of summer), and three coasting boats from the Iss Levels [r07] in the eleventh month. No hull came in that was not known on this coast, and no stranger came by land. Why: an ordinary year.

strength: about 150 in the spring drilled levy, drilled in the eighth month; the walls and the harbor chain as before, with the chain's post and the quay head's outer pile renewed [KEEPER]. The chain truce held: the Harbormaster answers for raising and lowering, and a lamp-tender of the line holds its key and writes each use in the harbor-book (thirteen uses, one of them the boat in the swell). The senior Captain has seen the keeping's lamp lit with nobody at the water's edge. Why: no fighting and no loss to the levy.
- [BOAT CAME IN] The Captains' boat took her line at the mouth, and the Captains are easier with one another and with the quay.
- [BOAT LOST] The Captains' boat hauled two out and lost two to the sea, and the senior Captain says little of it.

mood: patient and watchful as before, and divided in the court, though less sharply than a year ago. The Warden-Monarch and the Master of Timber are quietly hopeful. The Eldest Midwife has met the keeping a second time and says it is honest and that it does not know it was Low Garrow; she says so to the court. The Harbormaster does not take the word, but he has heard that it thanked him and asks nothing more of his chain, and he keeps a tally of his own with five lines and a question standing in the jar. Among the people at large, curious and a little afraid: a spring with no listener on the step, and seven nights of the star dimmed in one year against six, five and three before it, one of them with the keeping away.
- [BOAT CAME IN] Why: the Warden-Monarch lit the keeping's lamp on her own word with the keeping away, and four came home. The point is grateful and the town does not agree why: the lamp, the crew, the chain and the Captains' boat all have their tellers. The Warden-Monarch's standing has held, and the part of the court that said she should have asked first has gone quiet.
- [BOAT LOST] Why: two drowned in the lamp's light, with the keeping away. The point is bitter and quiet, the widow leaves no bread, and the Harbormaster says in the harbor-court that the lamp was the Warden's and the keeping was not there to fail. The Warden-Monarch's standing outside the court is thinner than at the end of Year 3, and nobody blames the keeping.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The crab boat came in with all four [r-0296]: population 2,023.
- The keeping's lamp and the harbor lamp are two lamps, as written. The tenders tended it right. The quay board and both keels are sound, the first keel clean after a year.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- The long-liner came in with all three. Your numbers take that branch: about 2,031. The tenders tended the lamp right.
- The Harbormaster's lead and grapnel took nothing that is a hull, on either morning. Your year takes that branch.
- The chain post's heel and the crab boat's keel are sound.
- At the bedside the keeping said what it counts and showed its shape at her asking. It gave no answer to her four questions.
- The keeping had the net-mender's slip from the jar. It heard the Warden's telling of the Harbormaster's answer in the ninth month. It was not in that water for the telling in the seventh.
