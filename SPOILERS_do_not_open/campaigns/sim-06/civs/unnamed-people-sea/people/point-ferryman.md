# The point ferryman

Unnamed. The ferryman of the point, whose boat goes out latest and comes in last. Filed from Year 4 because the god means to watch him from the water in summer.

## History (fixed)
- Year 1: the Master of Timber chose his boat for the first keel of the keeping's. He did not know why his boat had the first sound keel of the yard's year; he thought the yard was repaying a debt.
- Year 2: seen twice at dusk the figure at the town's edge from his boat; told his wife and no one else. Went back out in fair weather for a late boat with a fouled sail and towed her in.
- Year 3: said the water off his bow lay strangely quiet on some evenings, and did not like to say why. Went back out in fair weather for a late boat with a fouled sail and towed her in, as he has done all his life.
- Year 4: the same, in the third week of the twelfth month, in fair weather. Said the water off his bow lay strangely quiet on some evenings in summer. Called the tow a small thing.

## Nature
A man who goes back out for the late boat and calls it nothing.

## Year 5
- Month 3: spoke for the point's boats to the Master of Timber, that the yard's keel on his boat had stood five winters with no worm and no check.
- Month 4: heard the point named in the court's rule, and said he would keep it when he had buried his own.
- Month 10: his sheer strake was chosen for renewal; said the yard had been kind to him twice and he had begun to wonder what it was repaying.
- Month 11: stood on the quay head and did not put his boat out in the swell. Said again that the water off his bow lay strangely quiet on some evenings. See state.md for the two ways.
- Month 12: went back out in fair weather for a late boat with a fouled sail and towed her in, and called it a small thing.
