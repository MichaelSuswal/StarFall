# The Harbormaster

Unnamed. A man of fifty-eight in Year 1. Holds the quay, the boats and the tides, the town's order, and the harbor court at low water on the quay steps.

## History (fixed)
- Went to sea as a boy, captained a boat for twenty years, and took the harbor court when the one before him died. Has held it eleven years.
- His sister's son was on one of the two boats lost last autumn. He has not spoken of it in the court, and does not like it spoken of in his hearing.
- He holds that the quay and the chain are his by right of the tide, and that the line of lamp-wardens has reached into the sea's business since before his grandfather's day. This is the quarrel over the chain.

## Nature
Hard, practical, proud and fair. He does not lie. He does what the court orders and does it well, and he does not take a word that cannot be put to the test of a tide. He is quick to see a use in a story, and slow to forgive it.

## Year 1
- Heard the Warden-Monarch's word at the low-water sitting. Did not take the god's word. Did the early hauling, the chain and the lean-time rule as the court's order, "for the sake of the court's peace," and did them honestly.
- Kept the boats in the worst weather in the spring, to his credit.
- Settled the chain in spring by a truce: he answers for its raising and lowering, and a lamp-tender of the line holds the key.
- Not a helper of the god by the Keeper's record: he has not heard it.

## Aging
One year older each year.

## Year 2
- Month 1: gave the haul as the court's order, on his own count. Stood at the cairn beside the Warden-Monarch.
- Month 2: assented to the quay repair without being told.
- Month 5: on the quay himself in the swell; had the chain let down and put out the Captains' boat. Not a helper of the god by the Keeper's record. See state.md for the two ways he speaks of it.
- Month 10: took the stand-by boat's new keel at no price, for the boat's sake.
- Has begun a tally of his own of the mouth. Fifty-nine at the year's end.

## Year 3
- Month 1: gave the haul as his own order and said it was the quay's custom now.
- Month 4: assented to the chain-post repair without being told. Was asked by the Warden-Monarch whether the town lost two boats under the chain's line, and said: two boats, the autumn before the keeping came, the chain down by his order and written, nine men, his sister's son on the second, nothing found. He said the line and he each say the other's tender held the key that night. He said he would put a lead and grapnel along the line himself.
- Month 5: on the quay himself in the swell; had the chain let down and put out the Captains' boat. Did not come to the water's edge. Not a helper of the god by the Keeper's record. See state.md.
- Month 8: put a lead and grapnel along the chain's line from the Captains' boat at the lowest springs. See state.md for the two ways.
- Month 10: took the headland cove crab boat's new keel at no price, for the cove's sake.
- His tally of his own has three entries at the year's end. Sixty at the year's end.

## Year 4
- Month 1: gave the haul as his own order, and a rule of his own: no boat past the mouth while the watch on the lamp-house stair can hear the Skerries breaking from the northwest.
- Month 2: heard the god's answer at the chain-post from the Warden-Monarch's mouth, alone. Said a boat's lower timbers is a hull to him, that he dragged both sides of the line and took none, and asked where inside the mouth. Said nobody had thanked him for the lead, that "I ask nothing more of your chain" was the first thing from that water he would have put his name to, and that he takes a tide and not a word. Added a fourth line to his tally. Not a helper of the god by the Keeper's record.
- Month 4: assented to the quay head pile without being told; stood on the quay head through the longest storm.
- Month 8: on the quay himself in the swell, with the god away; had the chain let down and put out the Captains' boat. Did not come to the water's edge. Added a fifth line to his tally. See state.md for the two ways.
- Month 10: took the east cove long-liner's new strake at no price, for the cove's sake.
- Sixty-one at the year's end.
