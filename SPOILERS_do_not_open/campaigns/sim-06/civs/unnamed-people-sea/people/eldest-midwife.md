# The Eldest Midwife

Unnamed. A woman of about seventy in Year 2. Holds births, sickness and the dead, and the peace between households.

## History (fixed)
- Has delivered most of the town under fifty. Sits beside the Warden-Monarch on the quay steps.
- Year 1: heard the Warden-Monarch's word, said she would wait to hear what the keeping said to her, and asked that the lean be shared evenly.

## Nature
Blunt, tired, exact. Believes the dead and the living both want an account.

## Year 2
- Asked again for the even share and the third of the households' fish in the store.
- Month 7: went alone to the lowest step at dusk and said to the water, in the words a midwife's girl reports, that she delivers what comes and buries what goes, that the keeper of the count should say what it counts for, and that if it wanted to be answered it should come to a bedside, for she would not come to the step again. Has not met the god. Not a helper by the Keeper's record.

## Aging
One year older each year.

## Year 3
- Month 1: heard the god's words from the Warden-Monarch's own mouth, alone, in her kitchen. Said it was the first thing out of that water to tell her what it failed at. Said she would name a bed within the month.
- Month 2: sat alone at the bedside of the eldest of the smokers and met the god there, once, at the lowest tide at dusk. Put it four questions and asked to see it. Offered it the clerks' list of the year's dead each autumn, through the Warden-Monarch. Told the Warden-Monarch it was honest and that she would not take its word as the court's order. Told the inner court she had sat with it and would hear no more said of the line using it for the chain.
- Takes its word as honest and not as an order. Whether her office is now a helper is the Keeper's.
- Seventy-one at the year's end.
