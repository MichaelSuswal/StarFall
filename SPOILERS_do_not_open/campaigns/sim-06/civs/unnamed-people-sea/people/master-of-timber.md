# The Master of Timber

Unnamed. A man of sixty-one in Year 1. Holds the yard and the boatwrights.

## History (fixed)
- Son and grandson of boatwrights. Has built boats for forty-five years and held the yard for twenty. Widowed.
- Has argued with the court for years that sound timber is short on this coast and that the yard is patching old hulls. He lost no one on the two boats of last autumn, but he built one of them.

## Nature
A man of the hands: sharp-eyed, dry, unsentimental, and given to testing a thing before he says it. Quiet about what he has seen. He believes what he can put his hands on.

## Year 1
- The Warden-Monarch brought him to the quay in the dark in the third month. He met the god, heard that one piece was its own and that he should not say which, and saw it lay its right hand on a pile. He went to the pile at first light. He took the god's word, and has told no one which pile, and has told the Warden-Monarch only that he took it.
- Traded for oak with the coasting boats in the eleventh month.
- Chose the point ferryman's boat as the one that goes out latest and comes in last, in the tenth month. In the eleventh the god laid its hand on the keel. He said that was the second, and enough.
- A helper of the god by taking its word, by the Keeper's record.

## Aging
One year older each year.

## Year 2
- Month 1: heard the god's two answers from the Warden-Monarch. Found the ferryman's keel clean after a year in salt.
- Month 2: had the yard fit three step-boards so none could be told from the others; saw the god lay its hand on one.
- Month 10: chose the Captains' stand-by boat for the keel, with the Harbormaster's assent.
- Month 11: saw the god lay its hand on the keel. Said that was the fourth piece in two years and enough.
- Sixty-two at the year's end.

## Year 3
- Month 3: told by the Warden-Monarch that the keeping asks for nothing. Named the chain-post's foot as the piece.
- Month 4: had the yard cut two new heels and scarf them on by day; saw the god lay its hand on one in the dark. Said that was the fifth piece in three years.
- Month 10: chose the headland cove's crab boat for the keel, with the Harbormaster's assent.
- Month 11: saw the god lay its hand on the keel. Said that was the sixth piece in three years and enough.
- Sixty-three at the year's end.

## Year 4
- Month 3: named the outer pile at the quay head as the piece.
- Month 4: had the yard cut two piles and drive them side by side; saw the god lay its right hand on one in the dark. Said that was the seventh piece in four years.
- Month 7: told by the Warden-Monarch that the keeping would be gone some weeks.
- Month 10: chose the east cove's open long-liner, a garboard strake, with the Harbormaster's assent.
- Month 11: saw the god lay its right hand on the strake in the yard. Said that was the eighth piece in four years and enough.
- Sixty-four at the year's end.

## Year 5
- Month 2: sat in the stern of the Captains' boat in the drag; knew the strake for his own, laid nineteen years before in a decked boat of the point; knew the rib for his own master's.
- Month 3: asked by the Warden-Monarch about the point's keel; said he would ask the point himself. Went to the point and told the widow the oak and the keel were the yard's, and that he would answer her straight if she asked whether any other hand would lie on it.
- Month 4: had the yard cut two keel timbers of one length; saw the god lay its right hand on one at the scarf in the dark. Said that was the ninth piece in five years.
- Month 10: chose the point ferryman's boat, a sheer strake, with the Harbormaster's assent.
- Month 11: saw the god lay its right hand on the strake in the yard. Said that was the tenth piece in five years and enough.
- Sixty-five at the year's end.
