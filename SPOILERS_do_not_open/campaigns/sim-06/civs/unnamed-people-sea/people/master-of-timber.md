# The Master of Timber

Unnamed. A man of sixty-one in Year 1. Holds the yard and the boatwrights.

## History (fixed)
- Son and grandson of boatwrights. Has built boats for forty-five years and held the yard for twenty. Widowed.
- Has argued with the court for years that sound timber is short on this coast and that the yard is patching old hulls. He lost no one on the two boats of last autumn, but he built one of them.

## Nature
A man of the hands: sharp-eyed, dry, unsentimental, and given to testing a thing before he says it. Quiet about what he has seen. He believes what he can put his hands on.

## Year 1
- The Warden-Monarch brought him to the quay in the dark in the third month. He met the god, heard that one piece was its own and that he should not say which, and saw it lay its right hand on a pile. He went to the pile at first light. He took the god's word, and has told no one which pile, and has told the Warden-Monarch only that he took it.
- Traded for oak with the coasting boats in the eleventh month.
- Chose the point ferryman's boat as the one that goes out latest and comes in last, in the tenth month. In the eleventh the god laid its hand on the keel. He said that was the second, and enough.
- A helper of the god by taking its word, by the Keeper's record.

## Aging
One year older each year.
