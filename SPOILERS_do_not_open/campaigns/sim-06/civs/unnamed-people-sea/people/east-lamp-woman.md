# The old woman at the east lamp

Unnamed. A woman of about seventy-three in Year 2. A lamp-tender of the Warden-Monarch's household, at the east lamp.

## History (fixed)
- Has tended the east lamp for most of her life. Has a girl who brings her bread.
- By the record, she knows a day ahead when a boat will not come back, and is cold to the bone for a week after each knowing. Ordinary in this world; she bears her own cost.
- Year 1: said the lowest step "has a listener on it," and called the star's dimming a counting.

## Nature
Plain, dry, patient and cold. Says little and says it once. Does not claim the knowing and does not hide it.

## Year 2
- Month 4: chosen by the Warden-Monarch as one of two tenders of the keeping's lamp, with the boy. Told it is not to be lent, snuffed or carried off. Asked nothing.
- Month 5: kept the keeping's lamp at the water's edge through the swell and did not leave it. Had no knowing that week and had not been cold in the days before; said so.
- Her phrase "the listener" is now the town's.

## Aging
One year older each year.

## Year 3
- Month 1: said the listener had gone into the water to listen, and was cold for two days.
- Month 5: kept the keeping's lamp at the water's edge through the swell and did not leave it. Had no knowing that week and had not been cold in the days before; said so again.
- Counts the star's nights: "it counts up." Seventy-four at the year's end.
