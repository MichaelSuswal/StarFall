# Report to the Keeper: civ unnamed-people-sea, Year 2, pass one

## How the god was met

Autumn, month 1.
- The Warden-Monarch: hung the jar and struck the bell once on the first dusk of the dark half. Before dawn a stick lay across the lid, and she read it as asking her to the tide-door on the third night after. She met the god alone at the lowest tide at dusk. It told her three things: it does not know the name Brennis and cannot say it is the one who spoke to Brennis; it asks her to tell it what the eldest remember of the promise and who made it; it thanks her for the bed in the lamp-house and will not take it. She took its word as she did last year. She kept the Brennis answer from the court. She told the inner court the haul and the lean rule, which is the god's item 5, at the third-week sitting, and said nothing of Brennis.
- The Harbormaster: not met. He gave the haul as the court's order, said he had meant to haul at the turn of the month on his own count, and did not take the god's word.
- The Reeve, the Eldest Midwife: not met. The Reeve said the count wanted doing. The Midwife asked for the even share and a third of the households' fish in the store again.
- The Captains: not told.
- The Master of Timber: heard the god's two answers (nothing owed; a quay known to be a god's work will be leaned on, and a god that is known will be asked for what it cannot give) from the Warden-Monarch in the yard in the fourth week. He said the first was more than he expected and the second more than he would have liked, probably true. He took it. Not met in person this month.

Autumn, month 2.
- The Master of Timber, with the Warden-Monarch, on a moonless night at the quay in the first week: the god came up the lowest step, said nothing to either, laid its right hand on one of three renewed step-boards and went down. The Master of Timber took the word again. The Harbormaster assented to the quay repair without being told of the god; the Warden-Monarch sealed it.

Autumn, month 3.
- The Warden-Monarch told the god at the tide-door what the eldest remember, alone, aloud, to the water, in the third week. She does not say whether it answered. See What turns on a power.

Winter.
- Month 4: the Warden-Monarch read a stick as asking her to choose two lamp-tenders and show them how to fill, trim and light the god's lamp. She chose the old woman at the east lamp and the boy at the west end of the quay. They were told it is the keeping's lamp and not to be lent, snuffed or carried off, and told nothing of what it does. The boy asked whose it was and was told. She told her heir that the keeping is real and does not claim the old promise. The heir is not a helper.
- Month 5: the swell. The god was seen at the water's edge by the harbor mouth by the Warden-Monarch, the old woman, the boy, the lamp-tenders at the tide-door, the Captains' boat crew and at least one Captain. It lit the lamp and stood. It spoke to no one. The Harbormaster was on the quay and did not see it close (see Uncertain outcomes).
- Month 7: the Eldest Midwife went alone to the lowest step at dusk and spoke to the water. She did not take the god's word, and has not met it.

Spring: the god was not seen on the strand. The gulls and seals left the point on a good many nights through the lean. Nobody spoke to it.

Summer.
- Month 10: the Warden-Monarch told the Master of Timber the keeping asked for the keel of the boat that goes out latest and comes in last. He chose the Captains' stand-by boat and got the Harbormaster's assent on the ground that its old keel had checked and the yard had a timber in store. The Harbormaster does not know the keel is the keeping's.
- Month 11: the god laid its right hand on the keel in the yard, in the second week, before the Master and the Warden-Monarch, and said nothing. A hooded figure was seen at the town's edge on some evenings by the lamp-tenders, and twice at dusk by the point ferryman from his boat. No one spoke to it.

## What was done in the god's name

- By the Warden-Monarch: relayed the haul, the stores count, the even sharing (a third of the households' fish into the shared store), the lamp every dusk, no boat out in the worst weather; left the jar with the tally of storms and boats in months 1, 4 and 12; asked the eldest for the telling and told it to the water in month 3; chose the two lamp-tenders and showed them the lamp in month 4; told her heir; told the Master of Timber the god's two answers and the summer request; sealed the quay repair.
- By the Master of Timber: kept which piece is the god's, had the yard cut and fit three boards so no one could say which, chose the Captains' stand-by boat for the keel.
- By the Harbormaster: the hauling and the rule against sending boats out in the worst weather, as the court's order, and the assent to the quay repair and the stand-by boat's re-keeling without knowing. Not in the god's name.
- By the two lamp-tenders: filled and trimmed the lamp in month 5, as shown.
- Nothing was done in the god's name by the Reeve, the Eldest Midwife or the Captains, beyond doing what the court ordered.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper

1. The boat in the swell, month 5, ninth day after the third storm. A decked line-and-crab boat of the headland cove, patched twice by the yard in the last years, with four aboard: her master, a sound hand of about forty; his brother; the brother's son, fifteen; and a hired hand. They stayed on a good shoal at the outer grounds, misread the signs, and the swell came up faster than the old signs allow. At dusk a long heavy swell from the northwest, no wind in it and no storm in it. The boat was running for the harbor mouth between the Garrow Skerries [f31] and the quay, with the swell breaking on the Skerries on her beam.
   - Circumstances from the record and the year: the chain was let down by the Harbormaster, who came to the quay himself within the hour; the two Captains' boat was put out and stood by at the mouth; the Warden-Monarch's heir was in the lamp-tower with the harbor lamp; the Warden-Monarch had the keeping's lamp brought to the water's edge by the harbor mouth below the lamp; the hooded figure took it and lit it by its hand, and stood at the water's edge. Last year's boat came in with the mouth calm from the moment the lamp was lit and the water outside the mouth not calm. The boat is old and patched, not rotten. The crew know the coast and stayed too long, not for want of skill.
   - What the lamp-tenders did: the old woman at the east lamp and the boy kept the lamp, filled it and trimmed it as shown, filled it again before the first watch was out; the boy ran oil from the lamp-house, fell on the quay stones and bruised his knee, and did not stop; the old woman did not leave the lamp. They kept it until the Harbormaster told them to stop in the lost branch, or through the night and into the next day in the other. I do not know whether they tended it right; you said you would tell the god.
   - The old woman's record gives her a knowing a day ahead when a boat will not come back, with a cold to the bone after. She had no knowing that week and had not been cold in the days before. I leave what that means to you. The net-mender who frees fouled lines was ashore and her power was not in play.
   - Success: all four come in, the Captains' boat takes her line at the mouth. Failure: the boat breaks at the mouth, the master and his brother drown, the brother's son and the hired hand are hauled out by the Captains' boat.
   - The year is written both ways: population 2,023 or 2,021; the town's mood, the Warden-Monarch's standing, the Harbormaster's talk and the Eldest Midwife's visit to the step differ by branch.
2. No other contested outcome was written. The ferryman's late boat in month 11 was in fair weather and I did not make it contested.

## What turns on a power

- Water's Hearing, month 1 night and day, and again on many nights through the lean: written only as what the fishers and gleaners saw, the gulls and seals leaving the point and the skerry.
- Sound Timber, the quay board, month 2: the god's right hand on one of three renewed step-boards. What the Master found at first light, and what the board is, are not settled. The year says only that the quay held in all four storms and does not credit the board.
- Sound Timber, the keel, month 11: the god's right hand on the keel of the Captains' stand-by boat. What the keel is, and how the boat does, are not settled.
- Calm Under the Lamp, month 5: what the lamp did to the water, and whether the tenders tended it right, as the god asked you to tell it.
- The two lamps: I wrote that the Warden-Monarch had the keeping's lamp brought from the lamp-house to the water's edge by the harbor mouth, with the harbor lamp up in the tower tended by the heir. If you mean them to be the same lamp, say so and I will bring the tower into line.
- The sticks across the jar in months 1 and 4, and the Warden-Monarch's reading of them as requests in words. She reads them and says what they ask. How a stick carries a request is yours, and so is whether any stick answered her telling in month 3, her tally of dimmed nights in month 12, and her storm tally in month 4 (I wrote an answering stick in month 4 as in Year 1; correct it if wrong).
- The Warden-Monarch's telling to the water at the tide-door in month 3, and the Eldest Midwife's words to the step in month 7: whether the god heard either is yours.
- A clean keel at one year, on the point ferryman's boat at the haul in month 1: I extended last year's release to say it showed no worm, check or softening after a full year in salt. Please confirm.
- The five dimmings of the star, months 2, 6, 9, 11, 12: written as seen, with no claim of cause. The people tie them to the sea and the step in lore.

## What touches anyone else

- Trade:
  - The shepherds of the Bren Hills [r05] came twice, at the start of summer and at its end, with wool and mutton for salt fish. A shepherd at the landing asked a fisher whether the sea town still rang for the sea. The fisher said it was a custom and no more, by the Harbormaster's word.
  - Three coasting boats from the Iss Levels [r07] came in month 11. Salt, rope and fish went out. Sound oak planks (enough for one keel and some planking) and nine loads of thin grain came in. A few pieces of old silver went out.
- No party went out and no court message was sent. No stranger came by land and no hull not known on this coast came in.
- Nothing carried.

## Asked of the god

- The Warden-Monarch, month 1, last year's questions: "What do I call you?" answered last year. Whether it was the one to whom Brennis had spoken: answered in front of her this year at the tide-door (it does not know the name and cannot say it is the one). Her offer of a bed in the lamp-house: thanked, and refused, in front of her.
- The Master of Timber's two Year 1 questions, "What do I owe for it?" and "Why must it be secret?": answered through the Warden-Monarch in month 1 (nothing; because a known quay would be leaned on and a known god asked for what it cannot give). Not answered in front of him.
- The Warden-Monarch, month 3, to the water at the tide-door: she told it what the eldest remember of the promise, as it asked. Whether it was answered in front of her: not that I know. [KEEPER]
- The Warden-Monarch, month 12: a tally of the nights the star dimmed, five notches, left in the jar without words. Nothing asked in words. Not answered that I know. [KEEPER]
- The Eldest Midwife, month 7, alone at the lowest step at dusk, in her words as the midwife's girl reports them: "I deliver what comes and bury what goes. You are the one that keeps a count, and I would like to know what you count for. If you want to be answered, come to a bedside. I will not come to the step again." Not answered in front of her. In the lost branch she said it more sharply a second night.
- No other mortal put a question to it.

## Names said aloud

- "Brennis": said by the god to the Warden-Monarch at the tide-door in month 1, to say it does not know the name. Said by the Warden-Monarch to the eldest, to her heir and to the water in months 1 to 4. Already in the record.
- "Low Garrow": said by the oldest net-mender to the Warden-Monarch in months 1 to 3, in the old telling. Already in the record.
- "Skerr": said by the Warden-Monarch to her heir and to the Master of Timber. Already in the record.
- "the listener": a byname for the god, said by the old woman at the east lamp last year and now by the town; "the keeping", the court's. Neither is a name for the people, the town or an office holder.
- No name was said aloud for the people, the town or any office holder. All stay unnamed or filed by office.
