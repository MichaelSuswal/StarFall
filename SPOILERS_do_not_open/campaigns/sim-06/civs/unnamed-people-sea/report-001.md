# Report to the Keeper: civ unnamed-people-sea, Year 1, pass one

## How the god was met

Autumn, month 1: not met. The god lay in the water at the point on the first night and under the rocks the day after, then at the foot of the quay's lowest step for several dusks. Fishers noticed the gulls leaving the quay's foot and the seals leaving the skerry by the harbor mouth for the first night and the day after, and the gulls absent from the lowest step on some evenings. The gleaners at the point found the strand "quiet." The old woman at the east lamp said the lowest step "has a listener on it." Nobody linked it to a person. The lamp was lit every dusk by the lamp-tenders. The Warden-Monarch lit it herself on the first dusk of the dark half.

Autumn, month 2: the Warden-Monarch (office only), at the lowest tide at dusk at the tide-door, alone with a shuttered lantern. The god, hooded and cloaked, said it was the one who keeps the keeping. It wanted the boats hauled and the stores counted before the first storm. A word left in a sealed jar on the lowest step with the bell struck once would reach it, and it would answer with a stick and a mark before dawn. Its lamp was not to be lent or snuffed. It asked her to tell the court in her own way and to name it to no one outside. She kept still and was shown its shape once. She took its word after one night. The next dusk she set the jar with a notched stick and struck the bell. Before dawn a second stick lay across the jar's lid with a mark, and she took it as an answer. She is a helper by the Keeper's record. She told the inner court at the low-water sitting in the first week of month 3: the Harbormaster, the Master of Timber, the Reeve of the Fields and the Eldest Midwife, not the Captains. She named the god Skerr.

Autumn, month 3: the court.
- The Harbormaster did not take the word. He did the early hauling, the chain and the lean rule as an order of the court "for the sake of the court's peace". He told the sitting he thought the word a convenience to the line in the chain quarrel.
- The Master of Timber said he would believe it when he had something in his hand.
- The Reeve said the count wanted doing anyway.
- The Eldest Midwife said she would wait to hear what the keeping said to her, and asked that the lean be shared evenly.

Later in the month the Warden-Monarch brought the Master of Timber to the quay on a moonless night. The god told him one piece was its own and that he should not say which, and laid its right hand on a pile by the lowest step. He took the word and is a helper by the Keeper's record. The Captains and the other office holders have not heard the god.

Winter: the god kept out of the town. A jar with the storm and boat tally was left in month 4 and answered with a stick before dawn. In month 4 the god was seen at the water's edge below the lamp (see Uncertain outcomes).

Spring: the god was seen three times by different gleaners, walking the strand west and east of the point at the lowest tide at night, a hooded old man at an old man's pace. It was not spoken to. The Warden-Monarch told the gleaners it was nothing to fear.

Summer: the god did the keel in the boatyard in month 11. The Master of Timber had chosen the boat and took the word. A hooded figure was seen at the town's edge on some summer evenings by lamp-tenders and by one of the midwife's girls. No ferryman, midwife or lamp-tender was spoken to.

## What was done in the god's name

- By the Warden-Monarch:
  - The court was told.
  - The late boats were drawn in about ten days early in month 3, with caulking and tarring done, the chain inspected and the lamp lit every dusk of the dark half.
  - The stores were counted. A third of the households' own fish went into the shared store and was shared evenly through the lean (agreed by the Reeve and the Eldest Midwife).
  - No boat went out in the worst weather in spring.
  - She kept the jar, and the Master of Timber was brought to the quay twice.
- By the Master of Timber: he kept the secret about the pile and the keel, chose the point ferryman's boat, and bought oak.
- By the Harbormaster: he carried out the same orders as the court's, not the god's.
- The two Captains carried out the Harbormaster's orders and were not told of the god.
- The spring chain truce is the Warden-Monarch's and the Harbormaster's act, not the god's.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper

1. The late boat in the swell, month 4, between the second and third storms. A small decked boat of five, one of the late boats, was caught out at dusk with a swell rising and no storm yet in it. It was making for the harbor mouth between the Garrow Skerries [f31] and the quay. The chain was down, the lamp-tender's lamp lit, and the Captains' boat stood by at the mouth. The skerries are feared in an onshore blow, and the swell breaks on them on the boat's beam. The god planned to light the lamp by its own hand at the water's edge.
   - Success: the boat comes in with all five.
   - Failure: it breaks on the mouth. Three of the five drown and two are hauled out by the Captains' boat.
   - The year is written both ways, with population at 2,011 or 2,008.
   - The record also gives a net-mender who frees fouled lines and an old woman at the east lamp who knows a day ahead when a boat will not return. Neither was used here.
2. No other contested outcome was written.

## What turns on a power

- Water's Hearing: the quiet at the quay's foot and the skerry in month 1, written only as what the fishers saw.
- Sound Timber, the pile (month 3): the god's right hand on a pile at the lowest step, seen by the Master of Timber. What he found at first light, what the pile is, and whether it held in the five storms and the first one late in month 3 are not settled. The year says only that the storms did no harm, as the result of the whole quay and the early hauling together.
- Sound Timber, the keel (month 11): the god's right hand on the keel of the point ferryman's boat. What the keel is, and how the boat does, are not settled.
- Calm Under the Lamp (month 4): the lamp flared when the figure lifted its hand, as the lamp-tenders saw. What it did to the water is not settled.
- What the Warden-Monarch was shown of the god's shape. I wrote that she says it was old, wet and not a man. Please settle what she saw.
- The marks left on the rocks at the tideline in spring. Please settle what they are.
- The sticks and marks in the jar (months 2 and 4). The Warden-Monarch read them and will not say what they bore. Please settle it.
- The three dimmings of the star: written as seen before, with no claim of cause. If any is tied to the god, the Keeper may say so.

## What touches anyone else

- Trade:
  - The shepherds of the Bren Hills [r05] came twice, at the start of summer and at its end, with wool and mutton for salt fish.
  - Three coasting boats from the Iss Levels [r07] came in month 11. Salt, rope and fish went out. Sound oak planks (enough for the keel and some planking of two boats) and twelve loads of thin grain came in. A few pieces of old silver went out.
- A leak: a fisher's wife at the shepherds' second landing, asked why the bell was struck so often on the quay, said "the bell on the quay was answered". It went to the hills.
- No party went out and no court message was sent. No stranger came whom the town had not seen before, except what the god's coming brought.

## Asked of the god

- The Warden-Monarch, month 2: "What do I call you?" Answered in front of her: Skerr.
- The Warden-Monarch: whether it was the one to whom Brennis had spoken. Not answered in anything she has repeated, and the decisions file gives no answer.
- The Warden-Monarch offered a bed in the lamp-house. Not answered. The god went down into the water.
- The Master of Timber, month 3: "What do I owe for it?" and "Why must it be secret?" The decisions file gives no answer for either. The god's summer plan says it asks nothing in return. Please say whether it answered.
- No other mortal put a question to it.

## Names said aloud

- "Skerr": said by the god to the Warden-Monarch at the tide-door in month 2, when she asked. She then said it to the Harbormaster, the Master of Timber, the Reeve and the Eldest Midwife at the low-water sitting in the first week of month 3.
- "Brennis": said by the Warden-Monarch to the god in month 2. It is already in the record and is listed for completeness.
- No name was said aloud for the people, the town or any office holder. All stay unnamed or filed by office.
