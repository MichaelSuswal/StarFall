# Lore of the unnamed people of god-sea, after Year 3

What the people now say. It is their own telling, and where they explain a cause they are wrong about something that matters; the wrongness is marked for the Keeper in square brackets and is not known to them. Lines that turn on the boat in the swell (Year 3, month 5) are marked [BOAT CAME IN] and [BOAT LOST]; lines that turn on the Harbormaster's lead (Year 3, month 8) are marked [LEAD FOUND] and [LEAD NOTHING]. The Year 2 boat came in, as the Keeper ruled, and its lines stand without marks.

## About the god

- Within the court (the Warden-Monarch, the Master of Timber, now the Eldest Midwife; the Harbormaster and the Reeve have heard of it and not met it): "the keeping." The Warden-Monarch and the Master of Timber still say Skerr in the sitting. The Warden-Monarch has kept two things to herself and her heir: that the keeping did not claim Brennis and said so plainly, and that it did not make the star dim and does not know what does. The second she has told the inner court. She holds, privately, that the keeping and the promise may not be the same thing. The Master of Timber holds that the keeping asks nothing and gives sound work, and that is enough. [Wrong: that the keeping is bound to the line and answers only to the one who strikes the bell. It has said it asks nothing in rites and has taken nothing from the line; the line has taken the habit of thinking it is theirs.]
- The Eldest Midwife says it came to a dying man's bed at the lowest tide, asked nothing, took nothing and was honest, and that she knows a liar at a bed. She will say no more. She says it did not keep a people once and said so. [Wrong, or unsupported: she takes "a people" to be Low Garrow, because she asked and it did not turn her away.]
- Among the lamp-tenders and the Captains: that a hooded figure lights a lamp at the water's edge when a boat is out in the swell, and the lamp stands in the dark as bright as the harbor's. The Captains' boat crew say it stood at the mouth and did not move. The senior Captain has been told by the Warden-Monarch whose lamp it is and keeps it from the levy. [Wrong, or half right: they say the figure is the lamp-warden Brennis come back, because the lamp is a warden's lamp. The keeping told the Warden-Monarch it does not know the name.]
- In the town at large: "the listener on the lowest step." The old woman at the east lamp coined it in Year 1 and it has stuck. The fishers say the listener went into the water through the lean and was gone from the step for many nights together, and came back with the fishing. The old say the tally-voices went into the sea.
- Among the fishers: the heel of bread and the fish-head on the lowest step, and a word said over it; more say the word, and some leave a pinch of salt. They say the lamp brings boats in and the keeping looks to the boats.
  - [BOAT CAME IN] The east cove has begun to put a pinch of salt on the step before they put out. They say three years running is no accident.
  - [BOAT LOST] The east cove's widow leaves no bread, and fewer in the east cove say the word. They say the lamp lights the mouth and does not save a crew, and that the sea keeps what it keeps.
- In the Warden-Monarch's household: that the bed in the lamp-house is kept made against the keeping's coming, and that it is never slept in. [Wrong: the household says it will be slept in the night the line is in need. The keeping thanked her for the bed twice and said it would not take it.]
- The Master of Timber's yard says, among themselves, that the Master has a cure for timber that he got from the boatwrights of the river town on the levels, a tar or an ash or a way of drying, and that it is why the yard's oak does not check and why the chain-post's new heel took the longest storm. They say the Captains' stand-by boat's new keel is the proof. [Wrong: it is not a cure, and not from the river town.]

## About their own rulers

- The Warden-Monarch is held to have been right to haul early three years running, though the hauling is now said to be the Harbormaster's own custom, and he says so at the harbor-court. Her standing within the court has held. Outside it she is spoken of with respect and some unease, as one who has been spoken to three times.
  - [BOAT LOST] The unease has grown to blame in the quiet. It is said she has the weather and the lamp in her ear and has two more stones to answer for.
- The Harbormaster is said to have been right about the hauling, which he now does as his own order, and to have been right about little else. He is said to be sour, fair, and to keep his own tally of the mouth.
  - [BOAT CAME IN] He says the crew did it, and has begun not to say it aloud.
  - [BOAT LOST] He says the lamp did not save them, and the town hears it as a thing he has always said.
  - [LEAD FOUND] It is said in the town, a month after, that the Harbormaster went out on the chain's line at the lowest springs and came up with oak. He will not say what, and the harbor-book room has a plank and a rib in it that the clerks do not speak of. The line says the quay knew. The quay says the line knew. [Unsupported: the town holds that the chain took the two boats, and some say whoever raised it did so to keep a raid out. Nothing in the court's record says there was a raid.]
  - [LEAD NOTHING] It is said he was looking at his chain's footings and was not wrong to. A few say he was looking for his nephew's boat and the sea had it.
- The Master of Timber is said to have a good eye and a close mouth. The stand-by boat's new keel is called the yard's kindness, and so is the next, on the headland cove's crab boat, though the headland cove says the Harbormaster owed it a timber.
- The two Captains are said to have drilled the levy well. One of them says he saw the figure at the mouth twice and will not say more, and he does not say he was told.
- The Eldest Midwife is said to have sat up with the eldest of the smokers when he died and to have said afterward that she knew a liar at a bed. The granddaughter has told it twice and the town believes it.

## About their neighbors

- The shepherds of the Bren Hills [r05] still tell on the hills the tale of the sea town that rings for the sea. They asked nothing of the bell this year. The town does not know how much the shepherds know.
- The coasting boats from the Iss Levels [r07] brought oak and took salt fish, as they do. The town says they are good traders and a little close, and that the oak was sound this year.

## About the old town

- The oldest net-mender, asked by the Warden-Monarch in the spring, says Low Garrow lay at the end of the causeway where the cut stones go under at the lowest tides, that her mother and her mother's mother do not agree whether the town lay past the last stones or along the road's near end, and that the stones lie five days on foot round the shore by the old count. She says the sea took it in one night for its pride and that those it spared walked the other way along the shore and founded the harbor town. She says she does not know that it is so. [This is the people's own telling, as my record holds it; I do not know how much of it is so.]
- Among the fishers: that the stones are the road, and nobody has put a boat over them because the swell breaks there.

## About the strange things of the year

- The star's dimming, six nights this year against five last year and three the year before: the fishers say the star dims when the sea breathes in, and that it went six times because the sea breathed harder again, three, five, six, and nobody says what comes after. The old woman at the east lamp says it is a counting and that it counts up. Some of the younger fishers say the keeping tends the star as the Warden-Monarch tends the lamp, and the Warden-Monarch's household, who know it denied it, do not say so. The Harbormaster says a star is a star. The Warden-Monarch keeps the count on a stick. [Wrong, or unsupported: they tie the nights to the sea and the step. The Keeper's word is that the dimmings are not known to be anyone's doing.]
- The cranes in the third month: the oldest fisher says he has stopped reading cranes. Five storms came, the longest of two days, none worse than the town knows.
- The boat in the swell in the fifth month: the town says the lamp lit the mouth, for the third year running, and does not agree about the rest.
  - [BOAT CAME IN] The town says the lamp brought it in again, and that the third time is no accident.
  - [BOAT LOST] The town says the lamp lit the mouth and the sea took two. The old woman at the east lamp, who had no knowing that week, says no one could have known.
- The quiet at the point: the gleaners will not walk the strand on those nights, and the seals and gulls stayed away from the skerry on many nights in the lean. The old marks on the rocks are still not touched. The ferryman says the water off his bow lay strangely quiet in the summer on some evenings.
- The new keel on the headland cove's crab boat, and the post's new heel, two more of the pieces the yard has done this way and said nothing of: the yard says the oak is blessed. The Master of Timber says it is oak.
