# Lore of the unnamed people of god-sea, after Year 2

What the people now say. It is their own telling, and where they explain a cause they are wrong about something that matters; the wrongness is marked for the Keeper in square brackets and is not known to them. Lines that turn on the boat in the swell (month 5) are marked [BOAT CAME IN] and [BOAT LOST].

## About the god

- Within the court (the Warden-Monarch, the Master of Timber; the Harbormaster, the Reeve and the Eldest Midwife have heard of it and not met it): "the keeping." The Warden-Monarch and the Master of Timber still say Skerr in the sitting. The Warden-Monarch has kept one thing to herself and her heir: that the keeping did not claim Brennis and said so plainly. She holds, privately, that the keeping and the promise may not be the same thing. The Master of Timber holds that the keeping asks nothing and gives sound work, and that is enough. [Wrong: that the keeping is bound to the line and answers only to the one who strikes the bell. It has said it asks nothing in rites and has taken nothing from the line; the line has taken the habit of thinking it is theirs.]
- Among the lamp-tenders and the Captains: that a hooded figure lights a lamp at the water's edge when a boat is out in the swell, and the lamp stands in the dark as bright as the harbor's. The Captains' boat crew say it stood at the mouth and did not move. [Wrong, or half right: they say the figure is the lamp-warden Brennis come back, because the lamp is a warden's lamp. The keeping told the Warden-Monarch it does not know the name.]
- In the town at large: "the listener on the lowest step." The old woman at the east lamp coined it in Year 1 and it has stuck. The fishers say the listener went into the water through the lean, and came back with the fishing. The old say the tally-voices went into the sea.
- Among the fishers: the heel of bread and the fish-head on the lowest step, and a word said over it.
  - [BOAT CAME IN] More say the word, and some have begun to leave a pinch of salt. They say the lamp brings boats in and the keeping looks to the boats.
  - [BOAT LOST] Fewer say the word, and the oldest of the headland cove will not leave bread at all. They say the lamp lights the mouth and does not save a crew, and that the sea keeps what it keeps.
- In the Warden-Monarch's household: that the bed in the lamp-house is kept made against the keeping's coming, and that it is never slept in. [Wrong: the household says it will be slept in the night the line is in need. The keeping thanked her for the bed and said it would not take it.]
- The Master of Timber's yard says, among themselves, that the Master has a cure for timber that he got from the boatwrights of the river town on the levels, a tar or an ash or a way of drying, and that it is why the yard's oak does not check. They say the stand-by boat's new keel is the proof. [Wrong: it is not a cure, and not from the river town.]

## About their own rulers

- The Warden-Monarch is held to have been right to haul early two years running, and her standing within the court has held. Outside the court she is spoken of with respect and some unease, as one who has been spoken to twice.
  - [BOAT LOST] The unease has grown to blame in the quiet. It is said she has the weather and the lamp in her ear and has two more stones to answer for.
- The Harbormaster is said to have been right about the hauling, which he did on his own count, and to have been right about little else. He is said to be sour, fair, and to keep his own tally of the mouth.
  - [BOAT CAME IN] He says the crew did it. He has two entries in his own head, and tells no one.
  - [BOAT LOST] He says the lamp did not save them, and the town hears it as a thing he has always said.
- The Master of Timber is said to have a good eye and a close mouth. The stand-by boat's new keel is called the yard's kindness.
- The two Captains are said to have drilled the levy well. One of them says he saw the figure at the mouth and will not say more.
- The Eldest Midwife is said to have gone down to the step and told the water what she thought. The girl who carried her bag has told it twice, and the town believes it.

## About their neighbors

- The shepherds of the Bren Hills [r05] still tell on the hills the tale of the sea town that rings for the sea. A shepherd at the landing asked a fisher whether the town still rings. The fisher said it was a custom and no more, by the Harbormaster's word. The town does not know how much the shepherds know.
- The coasting boats from the Iss Levels [r07] brought oak and took salt fish, as they do. The town says they are good traders and a little close, and that the oak was sound this year.

## About the strange things of the year

- The star's dimming, five nights this year against three last: the fishers say the star dims when the sea breathes in, and that it went five times because the sea breathed harder than last year, three and then five, and nobody says what comes after. The old woman at the east lamp says it is a counting and has stopped asking what of. Some of the younger fishers say the keeping tends the star as the Warden-Monarch tends the lamp. The Harbormaster says a star is a star. [Wrong, or unsupported: they tie the nights to the sea and the step. The Keeper's word in Year 1 is that the dimmings are not known to be anyone's doing.]
- The cranes in the third month: the oldest fisher says the cranes told them a hard winter last year and the winter was not hard, and that he trusts the sea. Four storms came, the longest of three days, none worse than the town knows.
- The boat in the swell in the fifth month: the town says the lamp lit the mouth, for the second year running, and does not agree about the rest.
  - [BOAT CAME IN] The town says the lamp brought it in again, and that the second time is no accident.
  - [BOAT LOST] The town says the lamp lit the mouth and the sea took two. The old woman at the east lamp, who had no knowing that week, says no one could have known.
- The quiet at the point through the lean: the gleaners will not walk the strand on those nights, and the seals and gulls stayed away from the skerry on more nights than the year before. The old marks on the rocks are still not touched.
- The ferryman's keel, a full year in salt water with no worm and no check: the ferryman says the yard was kind. The yard says the keel is lucky.
