# Lore of the unnamed people of god-sea, after Year 5

What the people now say. It is their own telling, and where they explain a cause they are wrong about something that matters; the wrongness is marked for the Keeper in square brackets and is not known to them. The Year 4 outcomes stand as the Keeper gave them (the point's boat broke at the mouth, two drowned, the lamp lit by the tenders calmed nothing), and their lines are written without marks. Lines that turn on the boat in the swell (Year 5, month 11) are marked [BOAT CAME IN] and [BOAT LOST], and inside those, where the god's coming to the bell matters, [GOD CAME] and [GOD NOT THERE].

## About the god

- Within the court (the Warden-Monarch, the Master of Timber, the Eldest Midwife): "the keeping." The Warden-Monarch and the Master still say Skerr in the sitting. The Warden-Monarch has kept two things to herself and her heir: the last four words of what it said to her in the autumn, and that it did not make the star dim and does not know what does. She has told the inner court that it said she had the right, that its lamp is a lamp in any hand but its own, that the lamp does not still water for her, and that the boat broke because it was far. She holds, privately, that the keeping said "far" as a man says a thing he is ashamed of, and that it can be near. The Master of Timber holds that the keeping asks nothing and gives sound work, and that it said it could be near, and that is enough. [Wrong: that the keeping is bound to the line and answers only to the one who strikes the bell. It has said it asks nothing in rites and has taken nothing from the line; the line has taken the habit of thinking it is theirs, and now of thinking the bell is a leash.]
- The Eldest Midwife says it gave her a half answer and called it a half, and that she has not had that from a living thing in her trade. She says it counts the widow of the east cove among the kept, and that she told the daughters yes, it was in the house, and no more. She says it does not say Low Garrow and does not say not. She sent no house this year and says so. [Wrong, or unsupported: she holds that it keeps the dead she sends it, because it took the list and read it standing and took the two from the point at its head. It said it reads the list. It has not said it keeps them.]
- The Harbormaster, who does not take the word, says the keeping said where and the where was so, and that it said no more than where, which is how he would have asked. He says it mistook the lead for the tide and he corrected it by the Warden's mouth, and it let that stand. He says to nobody but his tally that it knows who raised the chain and will not say, because it does not want the line to know. [Unsupported: the keeping said it names no cause. It did not say it knows one. The Harbormaster has made it keep the line's secret.]
- Among the families of the nine: that the keeping set a cairn for them under the water, three pale stones in a row, and that the lead rang on the stones. Some of the families leave a pebble on the lowest step for each of the nine, and some have begun to say the word over it that the east cove says over the bread. [Wrong: the three stones were laid to mark a place for a lead, not a grave. The keeping said it does not say what broke the boats, and it did not say they were the nine.]
- Among the lamp-tenders and the Captains: that a hooded figure lights a lamp at the water's edge when a boat is out in the swell. This year the Warden-Monarch had it lit as a light, and said so aloud, and the tenders say it burned as a light does and the old woman says she lit it right. They say the bell now has two strokes, one for a word and two for a boat, and the boy says he heard the keeping said it would come at once. [Wrong, or half right: the senior Captain holds that the keeping hears the bell only when it is in the water by the quay, and that the bell is a thing the line owns. The keeping said it would come from wherever it lay within a night's swim, and asked the bell of the Warden because she keeps it.]
  - [BOAT CAME IN] The point says the bell, the long line and the lamp brought them in, and the old woman says that is three things and not one. [GOD CAME] The east cove says the keeping came to the bell and the boat came in, and puts a pinch of salt on the step and two for the bell. [GOD NOT THERE] The point says nobody came to the bell and the boat came in, and that the Captains and the long rope did it, and some say the keeping is not as near as it was said.
  - [BOAT LOST] The point says the lamp was lit as a light, the bell was struck four times, and the sea took two with the long line out. [GOD CAME] The east cove says it came and the sea took two anyway, and that is the keeping's measure. [GOD NOT THERE] The east cove says the bell was struck and nothing came to it, and the town hears it in the quiet as the keeping's failing, and nobody says it where the Warden can hear.
- In the town at large: "the listener on the lowest step." The fishers say the listener did not go off the step this spring, or went and came back the same week, and that the gulls stayed at the step's foot through most of the lean and left on some nights. They say it came back with the fishing, and then they say it never left. The old say the tally-voices have not gone farther this year, and are in the water by the point. [Wrong: they say it stayed because the east cove put salt on the step, or because the point's boat was built, or because the stones were laid. The Keeper's word on where it was is not theirs.]
- Among the fishers: the heel of bread and the fish-head on the lowest step, and a word said over it; more say the word, and some leave a pinch of salt. The point leaves no bread, except the younger son, who left one the morning the new boat first went out, and said nothing. The widow of the point leaves none.
- In the Warden-Monarch's household: that the bed in the lamp-house is kept made against the keeping's coming, and that it is never slept in. [Wrong: the household says it will be slept in the night the line is in need. The keeping thanked her for the bed twice and said it would not take it.]
- The Master of Timber's yard says, among themselves, that the yard's keels and piles and the strakes do not check and do not shake, and that the Master has a way with oak he got from the boatwrights of the river town. They say the point's new keel is the proof, and the ferryman's new strake. They say the oak is blessed. The Master says it is oak. [Wrong: it is not a cure, and not from the river town.]

## About their own rulers

- The Warden-Monarch is held to have been right to haul early five years running, though the hauling is the Harbormaster's own custom. Her standing within the court has held. Outside it she is spoken of with respect and some unease, as one who has been spoken to five times, and who said aloud at the cairn what the harbor-book said of the night the nine were lost, and read it standing.
  - [BOAT CAME IN] The unease has eased. It is said she lit it as a light and said so, and that was honest, and that the bell was hers to strike.
  - [BOAT LOST] The unease has grown thin again. It is said she struck the bell four times and the sea took two, and that she said it was a light.
- The Harbormaster is said to have been right about the hauling and about the Skerries, which he listens for from the lamp-house stair, and now right about the lead. He is said to be sour, fair, and to keep his own tally of the mouth, which is said to have seven lines. The town says he found the nine with a lead where the keeping told him, and took it badly, and took it. The nine families say he laid them on the cairn himself and asked them to let a stone fall. He says the key is on two hands now and that is the quay's. He says the rule that names the point is his and not the keeping's.
  - [BOAT CAME IN] He says the rule held and the point stayed in and the long line was used, and says it quietly.
  - [BOAT LOST] He says the rule held and two were lost, and that a rule is not a lamp and not a keeper.
- The Master of Timber is said to have a good eye and a close mouth. The point's new keel and the ferryman's strake are called the yard's kindness, and the ferryman says the yard has been kind to him twice and he has begun to wonder what it is repaying.
- The two Captains are said to have drilled the levy well, and now at the mouth with the long rope. The senior says he looked for the figure at the water's edge in the swell and does not say what he saw. The second Captain says he was told where, and that the lead rang, and nothing more.
- The Eldest Midwife is said to have sat up with the old in the winter and sent no house this year. The daughters of the east cove say she said yes, it was in the house.
- The widow of the point is said to have taken the yard's keel for her younger son and to have set no hand to it, and to have forbidden none. The point says she is hard. The younger son is said to be quiet and quick.

## About their neighbors

- The shepherds of the Bren Hills [r05] still tell on the hills the tale of the sea town that rings for the sea. They asked nothing of the bell this year, and the bell now rings in two voices. The town does not know how much the shepherds know.
- The coasting boats from the Iss Levels [r07] brought oak and grain and took salt fish, as they do. The town says they are good traders and a little close, and that the oak was sound this year.

## About the old town

- The oldest net-mender says nothing new, and says she is too old to carry the keeping a better answer. The fishers say the stones of the causeway are the road, and that nobody has put a boat over them. This year some of them say the three pale stones laid in the harbor are the same stones as the causeway's, and that the keeping carried them. [Wrong: the Keeper's word is that the stones were laid by hand as a mark and name no town.]
- The Eldest Midwife says that if the keeping does not say Low Garrow and does not say not, then the telling of Low Garrow is a telling and no more, and the town should say so. She says it at three bedsides and has stopped saying it was.

## About the strange things of the year

- The star's dimming, three nights this year against seven last year, six the year before, five, and three the first year: the fishers say the star dims when the sea breathes in, and that the sea breathed less this year, and that the keeping stayed near, and that a thing held near is held steady. The younger fishers say this proves the keeping holds the star up when it is close and lets go when it is far, as they said it had when it went off in the spring of Year 4. The old woman at the east lamp says the nights have been three, five, six, seven and three, and that it does not count up, and that she does not know what it counts. The Warden-Monarch, who knows it denied making the dimmings, keeps her stick of three notches and does not say so. The Harbormaster says a star is a star. The Master of Timber says that if the keeping does not do it, then something else does. [Wrong, or unsupported: they tie the nights to the sea and the step, and to the keeping's nearness. The Keeper's word is that the dimmings are not known to be anyone's doing.]
- The cranes in the third month: the oldest fisher says he has stopped reading cranes. Four storms came, the longest a day and a night, none worse than the town knows.
- The winter with no boat in the swell: the fishers say the Harbormaster's ear for the Skerries kept the boats in, and the east cove says the salt did, and the new rule that names the point did. The old woman at the east lamp says she had no knowing and no cold all winter.
- The two boats found under the chain's line: the quay and the line each say the other let the chain up. The Harbormaster and the Warden-Monarch have each said they will break the head of any who says it aloud, and nobody says it aloud. The nine families say it was the chain, and that nobody looked. [Wrong: nobody knows. The lead can say where the timbers lie, and it cannot say how the boats came to lie there. The town has made the chain the cause.]
- The quiet at the point: the gleaners will not walk the strand on those nights, and the seals and gulls stay away from the skerry. This year the quiet came on a good many nights through the lean, and in the summer it came under the lowest step. The old marks on the rocks are still not touched. The ferryman says the water off his bow lay strangely quiet in the summer on some evenings, and that it is the same quiet as in the years the yard was kind.
- The boat in the swell in the eleventh month, with the long line made fast to the quay head and the point kept ashore by the court's rule: the town does not agree about the rest.
  - [BOAT CAME IN] The town says the long line and the lamp and the bell brought them in, and does not say which.
  - [BOAT LOST] The town says the lamp was lit as a light and the sea took two, and that the rule that kept the point in did not keep the quay's boat out.
- The point's new boat, launched in the tenth month on a keel the yard gave, and the ferryman's new strake: two more of the pieces the yard has done this way and said nothing of. The yard says the oak is blessed. The Master of Timber says it is oak.
