# Lore of the unnamed people of god-sea, after Year 1

What the people now say. It is their own telling, and where they explain a cause they are wrong about something that matters; the wrongness is marked for the Keeper in square brackets and is not known to them.

## About the god

- Within the court (the Warden-Monarch, the Harbormaster, the Master of Timber, the Reeve of the Fields and the Eldest Midwife): "the keeping has spoken at the tide-door." They call it the keeping, and the Warden-Monarch has given them the name Skerr in the sitting. The Harbormaster says it as a name for a convenience. [Wrong: the Warden-Monarch and the Master of Timber believe the keeping is bound to the line of lamp-wardens and answers only to the one who strikes the bell. They have not asked what it wants, and the thing it asked for, a lit lamp, boats hauled, stores counted, is what it asked of them and not all of what it is.]
- Outside the court, as rumor and no more: the bell on the quay was answered; a hooded old man walks the strand at the lowest tides; the Warden-Monarch has the weather in her ear. [Wrong: that the Warden-Monarch foretold the first storm. The hauling was done on a word that named no day, and the storm was late; she did not foretell it.]
- Among the old: that the walker is one of the tally-voices come back, who sang names on the quay when they were children and wanted nothing. [Wrong, or only half right: the tally-voices asked the names of every cove and sang them back, and the walker has asked no one anything.]
- Among the fishers: that the gulls and seals leave the quay when the keeping sits on the step. They leave a heel of bread or a fish-head on the lowest step as they always did, and some of them now say a word over it.
- The old woman at the east lamp says the lowest step "has a listener on it." She says it is a counting, not a threat.
- The Master of Timber's yard says, among themselves, that the Master has found a source of sound timber. They think it is oak from the river boats. [Wrong: the piece he treasures is not from the river boats.]

## About their own rulers

- The Warden-Monarch is held to have been right to haul the boats early, and her standing within the court has grown. Outside the court she is spoken of with a mix of respect and a little unease, as one who has been spoken to.
- The Harbormaster is said to be sour, and he is, but he kept the lean fair and kept the boats in the worst weather, and the town does not hold his doubt against him. He is said to have been wrong about nothing and right about little.
- The Master of Timber is said to have had a lucky year.
- The two Captains are said to have drilled the levy well and to have asked no questions.

## About their neighbors

- The shepherds of the Bren Hills [r05] have heard something of the bell, and are said to be telling it on the hills as a tale of "the sea town that rings for the sea." The town does not know how much they know.
- The coasting boats from the river town on the levels brought oak and took salt fish, as they do. The town says they are good traders and a little close.

## About the strange things of the year

- The star's dimming, three nights: the fishers say the star dims when the sea breathes in, and that it went three times because the sea breathed hard. The clerks have written the three nights together. The old say it counts. [Wrong: the dimmings are tied by the people to the sea and to the stranger at the step, because two fell in the first month, when the shore went quiet. They were seen in other years as well, and have nothing to do with the sea.]
- The cranes in the third month: cranes going inland late means a hard winter. The winter was not hard. Five storms, none the worst. The fishers say the cranes are less to be trusted than they were.
- The boat in the swell in the fourth month: the town says the lamp brought it in or failed it, as the outcome is. The lamp has become a thing the town looks at.
- The marks on the rocks: the shepherds, the drowned's kin, the keeping: no one agrees, and the gleaners will not touch them.
