# Lore of the unnamed people of god-sea, after Year 4

What the people now say. It is their own telling, and where they explain a cause they are wrong about something that matters; the wrongness is marked for the Keeper in square brackets and is not known to them. The Year 3 outcomes stand as the Keeper gave them (the long-liner came in; the Harbormaster's lead took nothing that is a hull), and their lines are written without marks. Lines that turn on the boat in the swell (Year 4, month 8, with the god away) are marked [BOAT CAME IN] and [BOAT LOST].

## About the god

- Within the court (the Warden-Monarch, the Master of Timber, the Eldest Midwife): "the keeping." The Warden-Monarch and the Master still say Skerr in the sitting. The Warden-Monarch has kept two things to herself and her heir: that the keeping did not claim Brennis and said so plainly, and that it did not make the star dim and does not know what does. The second she has told the inner court. She holds, privately, that the keeping and the promise may not be the same thing, and she holds now that the keeping's lamp may not need the keeping to be lit. The Master of Timber holds that the keeping asks nothing and gives sound work, and that is enough. [Wrong: that the keeping is bound to the line and answers only to the one who strikes the bell. It has said it asks nothing in rites and has taken nothing from the line; the line has taken the habit of thinking it is theirs.]
- The Eldest Midwife says it came to a second bed and was honest, and that it does not remember which people it did not keep and does not say it was Low Garrow. She says she has stopped saying it was Low Garrow. She says it counts the ones it lost by the same hand as the ones it kept, and that this is the only honest count. She will say no more. [Wrong, or unsupported: she holds that it keeps the dead she sends it, because it took the list and read it standing. It said it reads the list. It has not said it keeps them.]
- The Harbormaster, who does not take the word, says the keeping asked its question straight and withdrew it when it had its answer, which is more than the line did, and that it thanked him, which nobody else did. He says he will not drag a harbor on a word. He says to nobody but his tally that it knows where the two boats lie, and will not say, because it does not want the line to know. [Unsupported: the keeping said it found broken lower timbers inside the mouth and did not say they were the two boats. The Harbormaster has made them his nephew's boat.]
- Among the lamp-tenders and the Captains: that a hooded figure lights a lamp at the water's edge when a boat is out in the swell, and the lamp stands in the dark as bright as the harbor's. This year, for the first time, the lamp was lit at the water's edge by the old woman with nobody standing there, and the Captains' boat crew say they looked and saw nobody. The senior Captain has kept this from the levy. [Wrong, or half right: they say the figure is the lamp-warden Brennis come back, because the lamp is a warden's lamp, and that she did not come this year because she was not called by the bell. The keeping told the Warden-Monarch it does not know the name.]
  - [BOAT CAME IN] The point says the lamp brought them in with nobody at it, and that the lamp does it of itself. A few say the keeping was there and unseen. The Warden-Monarch says nothing to either.
  - [BOAT LOST] The point says the lamp lit the mouth and the sea took two, with nobody at it, and that a lamp lit without the one who keeps it is only a lamp. The old woman at the east lamp says the lamp was lit right and tended right, and that was all she could do.
- In the town at large: "the listener on the lowest step." The old woman at the east lamp coined it in Year 1 and it has stuck. The fishers say the listener went off the step this spring for the first time in four years, and that the gulls came back to the step's foot and stayed. They say it came back with the fishing. The old say the tally-voices went into the sea and have gone farther. [Wrong: they say it went up the shore to the Cliff of Bones [f32] to fetch the drowned back, or to sleep through the lean. Nobody in the town knows where it went.]
- Among the fishers: the heel of bread and the fish-head on the lowest step, and a word said over it; more say the word, and some leave a pinch of salt. The east cove puts a pinch of salt on the step before they put out and says four years is no accident. They say the lamp brings boats in and the keeping looks to the boats.
  - [BOAT LOST] The point leaves no bread and says the keeping was not there to look.
- In the Warden-Monarch's household: that the bed in the lamp-house is kept made against the keeping's coming, and that it is never slept in. [Wrong: the household says it will be slept in the night the line is in need. The keeping thanked her for the bed twice and said it would not take it.]
- The Master of Timber's yard says, among themselves, that the Master has a cure for timber that he got from the boatwrights of the river town on the levels, a tar or an ash or a way of drying, and that it is why the yard's oak does not check and why the quay head's new pile took the longest storm. They say the headland cove's new keel is the proof. [Wrong: it is not a cure, and not from the river town.]

## About their own rulers

- The Warden-Monarch is held to have been right to haul early four years running, though the hauling is now said to be the Harbormaster's own custom, and he says so at the harbor-court. Her standing within the court has held. Outside it she is spoken of with respect and some unease, as one who has been spoken to four times, and who lit a lamp with nobody to answer for it.
  - [BOAT CAME IN] The unease has eased. It is said she was right, and that she is a keeper who takes a thing on herself.
  - [BOAT LOST] The unease has grown to blame in the quiet. It is said she lit it on her own word and two went to the cairn, and that she said so before the boat was in, which some count to her credit and some do not.
- The Harbormaster is said to have been right about the hauling and about the Skerries, which he now listens for from the lamp-house stair, and to have been right about little else. He is said to be sour, fair, and to keep his own tally of the mouth, which is said to have five lines and a question in the jar. The town says he was thanked by the water, and that he took it badly, and that he took it.
  - [BOAT CAME IN] He says the crew and the chain did it, and does not say it so loud.
  - [BOAT LOST] He says the lamp was the Warden's and let it be hers, and the town hears it as fair, and some as hard.
- The Master of Timber is said to have a good eye and a close mouth. The quay head's new pile and the east cove's long-liner's strake are called the yard's kindness, and the east cove says the Harbormaster owed it a timber.
- The two Captains are said to have drilled the levy well. The senior says he looked for the figure at the water's edge in the swell and saw none, and he does not say he was told.
- The Eldest Midwife is said to have sat up with a second dying woman and to have said afterward that it had not sat as the dying sit. The daughters have told it twice and the town believes it, and does not know what she meant.

## About their neighbors

- The shepherds of the Bren Hills [r05] still tell on the hills the tale of the sea town that rings for the sea. They asked nothing of the bell this year. The town does not know how much the shepherds know.
- The coasting boats from the Iss Levels [r07] brought oak and took salt fish, as they do. The town says they are good traders and a little close, and that the oak was sound this year.

## About the old town

- The oldest net-mender, asked by the Warden-Monarch two springs ago, says Low Garrow lay at the end of the causeway where the cut stones go under at the lowest tides, that her mother and her mother's mother do not agree whether the town lay past the last stones or along the road's near end, and that the stones lie five days on foot round the shore by the old count. She says the sea took it in one night for its pride and that those it spared walked the other way along the shore and founded the harbor town. She says she does not know that it is so. [This is the people's own telling, as my record holds it; I do not know how much of it is so.]
- Among the fishers: that the stones are the road, and nobody has put a boat over them because the swell breaks there. This spring some of them say the listener went to the stones, because it went the way the old say the spared ones came. [Wrong, or unsupported: nobody knows where it went.]
- The Eldest Midwife says that if the keeping does not know it was Low Garrow, then the telling of Low Garrow is a telling and no more, and the town should say so.

## About the strange things of the year

- The star's dimming, seven nights this year against six last year, five the year before and three the year before that: the fishers say the star dims when the sea breathes in, and that the sea breathed harder again, three, five, six, seven. The old woman at the east lamp says it is a counting and that it counts up, but not by the same step each time, and that she does not know what it counts. One of the nights fell while the keeping was away. The fishers on the night grounds said it dimmed with nobody on the step; some of the younger fishers say that proves the keeping holds the star up when it is near and let go while it was far, and that it dimmed on the other nights because it was tired. The Warden-Monarch, who knows it denied making the dimmings, does not say so. The Harbormaster says a star is a star. The Master of Timber says that if the keeping does not do it, then something else does. The Warden-Monarch keeps the count on a stick, seven notches this year. [Wrong, or unsupported: they tie the nights to the sea and the step, and to the keeping's nearness. The Keeper's word is that the dimmings are not known to be anyone's doing.]
- The cranes in the third month: the oldest fisher says he has stopped reading cranes. Five storms came, the longest of two days, none worse than the town knows.
- The winter with no boat in the swell: the fishers say the Harbormaster's ear for the Skerries kept the boats in, and the east cove says the salt on the step did. The old woman at the east lamp says she had no knowing and no cold all winter.
- The boat in the swell in the eighth month, with the keeping away: the town says the lamp was lit by the old woman and that nobody stood at the water's edge, and does not agree about the rest.
  - [BOAT CAME IN] The town says the lamp brought them in, with nobody at it.
  - [BOAT LOST] The town says the lamp lit the mouth and the sea took two, and that the keeping was not there, and some of the east cove say the keeping is the reason the other boats came in and this one did not.
- The quiet at the point: the gleaners will not walk the strand on those nights, and the seals and gulls stay away from the skerry. This year the quiet did not come through the lean at all, and came back late in the ninth month on a night. The old marks on the rocks are still not touched. The ferryman says the water off his bow lay strangely quiet in the summer on some evenings.
- The quay head's new pile and the east cove's long-liner's strake, two more of the pieces the yard has done this way and said nothing of: the yard says the oak is blessed. The Master of Timber says it is oak.
