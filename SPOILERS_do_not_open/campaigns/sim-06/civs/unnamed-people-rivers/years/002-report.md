# Report to the Keeper: unnamed people of god-rivers, Year 2, pass one

## How the god was met
Spring, first days after the thaw. A weir hand at dawn work saw her come down the stream path and called the gate house. The Seat Warden left the cup where it stood at the low seat, put her on the guest bench and fed her. She did not offer the cup. The Warden took her as a guest.
Spring, the day after. The sitting Speaker (Year 1) and the Ferry Elder, asked together at the gate house for a hearing about the ford. The Speaker gave her a place at the first full sitting after the flood and the choosing. The Ferry Elder said he would sit for it. Neither took her for more than a guest asking.
Spring, sixth day after the flood went down. The council chose a new Speaker, a woman of about 52. That is the council's, not the god's.
Spring, about the tenth day after the flood went down. The hearing. The Ferry Elder said yes at once to the flood marks and the low-water cord, and took her word as a guest's counsel. The Reckoner asked which stone, who cuts and whose count, and had the cord settled as the town's and his, then took her word on the cord as a thing worth doing and not as a god's. The Speaker asked why a guest cared for the ford and called the answer honest and odd; she did not take the word as more than counsel. The head of the oldest kin house asked "The water told you? Which water, and what did it say?" and said a cord of what the town already knew was no more than that, and did not stand against it. The council gave its go by the second evening. No oath either way.
Spring, month two. The Warden heard her say she would not ask again whether the cup waited for her, and answered that the cup would be filled in the morning, and that the first hearth was a small thing. She did not take her word as a god's. She kept the cord as before. The children took her plain telling hard and liked it.
Summer. The Ferry Elder named two ferry keepers; the Reckoner named his cord walker. Each took her word on the going as a guest's askings the Ferry Elder and Reckoner granted. The hamlets and the ford keepers gave her hearth and information. The eldest midwife, asked in person to tell her of any child or mother at risk, agreed to, and sent word in winter.
Summer, month six. The Warden, a ferry keeper and a well keeper sat the short listening at her asking. They took her word as a guest's.
Autumn. The council heard her in one sitting and talked three evenings. The Ferry Elder asked what she would do about the ford. The council answered by its own way, putting off the large things. The head of the oldest kin house said he would say nothing against a cord that was the town's. The well keepers and weir hands told the council at once of a cloudy well and muddy weir water, as she had asked, and told her after.
Winter. The Warden said back the cord at the autumn sitting. The eldest midwife sent word of a young mother in a fever, and the guest held her hands.
Whether each took her word as the god's helper: none did. No office holder has taken her for the one awaited or for a god. The Seat Warden holds the nearest to it, saying only that the cup has not been empty of her. The Keeper rules whether any office holder is a helper under the record's rule. I have written none as one.

## What was done in the god's name
Nothing was done in her name. Done at her asking, and kept by the people as their own: the ferry keepers cut the flood mark (the sixth) at the gate stone; the low-water cord was cut at the gate stone and at the ford stone, each season at low water, four marks each, and the Reckoner keeps it; the Ferry Elder sent a barge to the ford with the order; the Seat Warden kept the cord of her sayings and said it back at the autumn sitting; the Warden, a ferry keeper and a well keeper sat the short listening; the well keepers and weir hands told the council of the cloudy well and the muddy weir water; the eldest midwife sent word of the mother in a fever. The council set aside again one part in twenty of the barley and millet, its own habit by now; not at her asking.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper
1. The strayed herd, autumn month seven. Who tries: eight, three herders of the hill pasture, two weir hands, two ferry keepers with one reed boat, one dog handler with three dogs. Where: the reed and channel margins below the hill pasture. How long: up to four days, after which the council recalls them since the harvest needs hands and the autumn rains have begun. What: bring home a herd of about 60 goats and 5 cattle. How it is noticed is also for the Keeper: the pasture hands count at dusk by custom, but with every hand at the harvest the pasture was kept by two hands and a boy, so it may be missed that evening or found missing in the morning. The record bears on it: the weir hands and ferry keepers know the nearer channels, the boat bands trade at dusk in the Braidwater's nearer channels and may have seen strays, the Braidwater is seen only in its nearer channels, the reed is thick, the autumn rains have begun, and every hand is at the harvest at 0.9 rates. Success: most of the herd home within the days given, a few goats lost. Partial: a part home and a part lost. Failure: lost to the reed or the water, with a searcher at risk of the channels (one death at most). The god is in the town and I did not write her into the search; if the god's file sends her, the DM says. Written both ways in the year file.
2. The watch over the short listening, late summer month six. Who tries: the Seat Warden, a ferry keeper and a well keeper. What: keep the fire, the mat, and the dogs off through about two hours of sleep. What threatens: stray dogs, anything that hunts a sleeping god, and a hostile house head who stood apart on the bank. Circumstances: she lay at the water's edge below the gate in the low water of late summer, a few dozen watching from the bank, harvest opening. I wrote nothing coming but I wrote it both ways. If something came, the ferry keeper sounds the horn and the town sees what it cannot name.
Nothing else is contested: no fight, no persuasion of another people, no craft beyond what my people know.

## What turns on a power
1. The pull in the larger river. Seen: at the ford, on the fifth morning, she waded in from the ford stones up to the knee in the woman's shape, stood facing upstream with her hands loose until the sun cleared the reeds, came out and said nothing. What she felt, if anything, is for the Keeper; her file asks that the Keeper tell her in the next release. My year does not turn on it beyond the council's answer.
2. The short listening below the gate, late summer month six. Seen: she knelt in the running water about an hour, went still, did not answer when the Warden spoke to her, lay down and slept about two hours, and woke asking for water. I wrote the two hours from her file; if the Keeper rules otherwise, the town saw otherwise. What she heard is for the Keeper.
3. What she told the council in the one sitting in autumn, in part what the water told her. I did not write it and the council's course turns on it. Written both ways: if her counsel was a mark to read by, the ferry keepers set it in the autumn low water; if a new crossing or a second ferry, the council puts it off to the thaw and the winter marks. If the water told her the deep water will not return, the talk is long and hard; if the bed is quiet and the water will return, the council holds easier. The Keeper sets her actual words beside this.
4. Warm hands, asked twice. A hamlet man with a reed stump gone into his foot and fevered, month four, at the second hamlet. A young mother in the town with fever after a hard birth, month eleven. Seen: her hands went cold and white while she held them, and she warmed them and said little. What it did to each is for the Keeper. Written both ways in the year file; population differs by up to two.
5. The unsigned reading: the town reads her holding as the midwives' craft in a larger measure and she does not claim it. Nothing in my record contradicts it. Her account to the children of where she woke is read as a plain telling, not a working.
6. The star dimmed five nights (months two, six, nine, eleven, twelve). It is the Keeper's, not mine. The people tie it to her sometimes, wrongly, and I know no cause.
7. The cold in her hands after each warm hands: seen only as cold hands for days. The Keeper rules if there is anything more.

## What touches anyone else
Salt carriers: crossed fewer times than last year. A party crossed on the fourth day of the god's stay at the ford and she asked them what they had seen of the larger river's bed, its floods and its crossings, and where they go instead. They said, as the town had been told, that others go a southern way. What else they said is hers and the Keeper's; I wrote none. No distance or place for the southern way is written. Word of a barefoot woman who stood in the river may travel with the carriers and the ordinary travelers who were at the ford; I wrote none of that.
Boat bands: traded at dusk as before. They may have seen the barge go down; I wrote no meeting.
Namers: none came.
Parties sent out by my people: one, four of them, the god, two ferry keepers (an older woman of the ford landing and a younger man who cuts the marks) and the Reckoner's cord walker. Set out for Wellen Ford by barge down the brook, stopping at the four reed hamlets of the nearer channels as the way allowed, then at the ford about six days, then home on foot by the Brook Path, two days, as my record gives. The barge was left at the ford for the ford keepers. The stops at the hamlets are mine to name and the Keeper's to price. The ford keepers sent their low-water marks up the Brook Path by a runner in winter after the ice held. Searchers: eight, within the town's own land and reed, as above.
Anything carried: the low-water cord, the town's cords for the hamlets, and the order for the marks to the ford. Nothing given to anyone outside.

## Asked of the god
The Ferry Elder, at the hearing: "Will you have the keepers do the marks, or do you mean to?" Answered: she asked the ferry keepers, as her file gives.
The Reckoner, at the hearing: "Which stone, and who cuts, and whose count is it?" Answered in part: the depth at the ford in low water in each season, taken at the same stone, so the town has its own count. The settling was the council's.
The Speaker, at the hearing: "Why does a guest care for our ford?" Answered: a town that sees its own ford move decides better than one that is told, as her file gives.
The head of the oldest kin house, at the hearing: "The water told you? Which water, and what did it say?" Answered in front of him: the keepers first and the water second, as her file gives; what the water said is for the Keeper.
The ferry child, in the spring: "Can I go with you to the ford?" Not answered by the god in my files; the Ferry Elder refused.
A child at the fire: "Where do you come from?" Answered in front of them: she woke at the spring head up the stream in the thaw, before that she does not remember, she does not know why she came, as her file gives. Any further question the children put and any answer I did not write; the god's words are the DM's.
The ferry child, at the fire: "Then you are a spring's?" Not answered in my files.
The Seat Warden: no question put; the cup would be filled in the morning, said by the Warden.
A hamlet wife: "Will you hold his hands?" Answered: yes.
A ford keeper, at the ford: "What are you looking for in the river?" Not answered in my files.
The Ferry Elder, in autumn: "What would you do about the ford?" Answered in front of the council, as counsel; the content is her file's and the Keeper's.
The eldest midwife sent word in winter of a mother at risk, and the guest held her hands: answered.

## Names said aloud
The Counting Woman: said by the ferry child and the children, and by many in the town now. Said at the ford by the cord walker to the ford keepers when they asked how she was called.
Knee Deep: said by a ford keeper to another at the ford, the morning she came out of the river, and by the ferry keepers after.
Nessa Ferry: not said this year.
Names of the four reed hamlets: each said aloud when asked. I have not set them down because my record gives none.
No name for the people or their home was said. No office holder was named, the Speaker included.
