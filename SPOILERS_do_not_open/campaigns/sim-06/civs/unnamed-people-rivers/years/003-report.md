# Report to the Keeper: unnamed people of god-rivers, Year 3, pass one

## How the god was met
Spring, first days after the thaw. A weir hand at dawn work saw her come down the stream path and called the gate house. The Seat Warden left the cup where it stood at the low seat, put her on the guest bench and fed her. She did not offer the cup. The Warden took her as a guest.
Spring, the first evening. The children, the ferry child at the front. The ferry child asked whether she could go to the ford, and then whether she was a spring's. The ferry child took the first answer as what a grown person says, and did not take the second as an answer. The Seat Warden kept the cord of it at her asking, and said she would, as she kept the rest.
Spring, month one, the day after the stones showed. The Ferry Elder stood with her and the Reckoner at the gate stone at her asking and read the flood marks and the low-water cord. He said the deep water had moved again, a little, in the same direction, and did not take the cords as more than the town's own. The Reckoner said the cord said the same.
Spring, sixth day after the flood went down. The council chose a new Speaker, a man of about 46. That is the council's, not the god's.
Spring, about the tenth day. The sitting. The Speaker answered her question plainly: the council had not settled the ford; it would set a second small fish weir and move no great weir before the autumn. He did not take her asking as more than a guest's. The head herd keeper, a hill pasture woman of about fifty, corrected the loss to about 25 goats and 2 cattle (the Keeper's line agrees), said the pasture would count at dawn and dusk, keep three hands on the pasture, and send to the water gate for the boats after looking an hour, and that the guest might come if she liked. She took the guest's word as an offer, not an order. The Ferry Elder asked what he was to say to the keepers about the river, and was told nothing yet.
Summer, month four. The council sat three evenings for her leave. The head of the oldest kin house stood against it, and did not stand against it by the third evening. The Ferry Elder counted the days and set three down and the turn at dusk of the third, and took her word on the going as a guest's asking he could grant. The older ferry keeper said three and held to it. The Reckoner named his cord walker on the condition that the knots come to him first. The Speaker gave the council's go. No oath either way.
Summer, month five. The ferry child hid on the barge and was taken off it by the Ferry Elder. The ford keepers lent the barge left with them and kept the ferry. The ford keeper who asked last year what she was looking for asked again at the fire; she answered. The ford keepers took her answer as plain and strange. The two ferry keepers and the cord walker took her word on the going as the council's.
Summer, the ford, end of month five (if the party was back). The two ferry keepers and the cord walker sat the watch at her asking. They took her word as a guest's.
Autumn, month eight. The council heard her in one sitting and talked three evenings. She told the Reckoner and the council of the cord of dimmed nights with where she stood. The Reckoner said he kept one already and had added the marks. The Seat Warden said back the cord.
Winter. The ferry child asked again to go to the ford, and the Ferry Elder said when she was twelve and had a pole of her own.
Whether each took her word as the god's helper: none did. No office holder has taken her for the one awaited or for a god. The Seat Warden holds the nearest to it, saying only that the cup has not been empty of her. The Keeper rules whether any office holder is a helper under the record's rule. I have written none as one.

## What was done in the god's name
Nothing was done in her name. Done at her asking, and kept by the people as their own: the Ferry Elder read the flood marks and the low-water cord with her; the ferry keepers cut the seventh flood mark and the low-water marks at the gate stone and the ford stone, four more each; the Seat Warden kept the cord of her sayings and said it back; the Speaker and the council heard her question about the ford and set a second small weir of their own choosing; the herd keepers changed their count and their word to the water gate; the council gave leave for the going; the Ferry Elder named two ferry keepers and the Reckoner his cord walker; the ford keepers lent the barge; the Reckoner added the marks for where she stood to his cord of the dimmed nights; the well keepers and weir hands told the council of the cloudy well and the muddy water. The council set aside again one part in twenty of the barley and millet, its own habit by now; not at her asking.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper
1. The journey down the larger river, summer, month five. Who tries: the god, the older ferry keeper of the ford landing (about 41, knotted hands, has worked the ford landing most summers of her life, and went to the ford last year), the younger ferry keeper who cuts the marks (about 26), and the Reckoner's cord walker (about 23, who keeps exact counts). Four. Set out: about the middle of month five from the town by barge down the brook, one day to the ford; two days at the ford; cast off down the larger river at dawn of the fourth day of the going. How many days down: three, agreed by the council at the Ferry Elder's counsel and by the older ferry keeper; the turn is made at dusk of the third day wherever they lie and by no one's later decision. Back: your ferry keepers count two and a half days up for each day down, so about eight days up; the ford about the twenty eighth of the month; the Brook Path two days after. What they ask: where the carriers cross now (the town was told only that others go a southern way, with no place, no distance, no reason), and why the deep water moves. They stop at each hearth and landing the river gives and ask news and a count, and the cord walker knots it. The god means to stand in the larger river to the knee each evening and each dawn, and not shift in anyone's sight. What is the Keeper's: what lies below the ford, who is met, what the river tells her, and whether anything comes against her listening. Circumstances from my record that bear on it: no one of the town has been more than a day below the ford; the barge is a flat river barge that the people pole across shallows and the larger river is deeper and faster than the shallows they pole; the ferry keepers work the ford ferry on the larger river and know it at the ford; the party is three mortals and the god, who works at 1.6 of a mortal when she takes the sweep; the water is low, in the dry weeks of summer; my record's table gives the river's mouth as 164.4 miles by boat downstream and 6 days, which the keepers do not hold as a figure they have run; the Braidwater [r26] and its boat bands are known only in the nearer channels. The record gives no hostile neighbor on the larger river below the ford. Success: the party goes down three days, turns at dusk of the third, comes up in about eight, is at the ford about the twenty eighth, and is home in the first days of month six, before the cutting is well opened, with news of where the carriers cross. Partial: delayed or short of knowledge; or comes home with news that costs the town (a quarrel, a debt, a visitor following). Failure: the barge lost or harmed in the current, a keeper or the cord walker lost to the river (one death at most from the river alone, more if someone comes against them), or the party held below the ford. Written both ways in the year file: population differs by one at most.
2. The watch over the listening at the ford, end of month five (if the party was back). Who tries: the older ferry keeper, the younger ferry keeper and the cord walker, with the ford keepers keeping the ferry and the horn. What: keep a fire, a mat and the dogs and the cold off through about two hours of sleep, and wake the council's runner if anything came against her. What threatens: stray dogs, travelers on the public crossing, anything that hunts a sleeping god, and a party of carriers crossing or a stranger from downstream who has heard of a woman who stands in the river. Circumstances: she lay at the water's edge at the ford stones in the low water, a few dozen of the ford's people and some travelers watching from the bank, the ferry closed for the evening, the ford keepers' horn at hand. The ford is a crossing open to anyone, by the code, so the bank is not the town's to clear. I wrote nothing coming but I wrote it both ways. If something came, the older ferry keeper sounds the horn and the ford sees what it cannot name.
Nothing else is contested: no fight, no persuasion of another people, no craft beyond what my people know. The second small weir is ordinary work. The child hidden on the barge was found and taken off at the landing before it cast off; that is mine and settled.

## What turns on a power
1. The pull in the larger river. Seen: at the ford, on the second dusk and the third dawn, and by the party on the journey each evening and each dawn, she waded in from the bank up to the knee in the woman's shape, stood facing upstream with her hands loose, and came out. What she felt, if anything, is for the Keeper; her file asks that the Keeper tell her in the next release. My year does not turn on it beyond what the ferry keepers and the cord walker say of it, and the ford keepers' name for her.
2. The listening at the ford. Seen: she knelt in the running water of the larger river about an hour, went still, did not answer when spoken to, lay down and slept about two hours, and woke asking for water. What she heard is for the Keeper. My year turns on it at the autumn sitting: the council's course on the weirs, on a crossing and on the ferry depends on what she told it of the water.
3. What she told the council in the one sitting in month eight, in part what the water told her, and what the party saw. I did not write it and the council's course turns on it. Written both ways in the year file, in short. The Keeper sets her actual words beside this.
4. Warm hands: none asked this year. No sick call came. If the Keeper's events include one at the ford or on the river, the Keeper writes it.
5. The star dimmed six nights, in the first month, a pair in the fifth, a pair in the seventh and one in the eighth. It is the Keeper's, not mine. The Reckoner cut a mark beside each knot for where she stood: in the town five times, away once, the second of the pair in month five, when she was on the water. The people tie it to her sometimes, wrongly, and I know no cause. The Keeper's Year 2 line says the dimmings are not known to be anyone's doing; my people do not know that and say otherwise.
6. The cold in her hands after warm hands: not seen this year, since no one was held.

## What touches anyone else
Salt carriers: crossed fewer times again. A party crossed at the ford on the second day of the god's stay and ordinary travelers. She asked what they had seen of the larger river and where they go when they cross less. They said again that others go a southern way, with no place, no distance and no reason. Word of a barefoot woman who stands in the river may travel with the carriers and the ordinary travelers who were at the ford; I wrote none of that.
Boat bands: traded at dusk as before. They may have seen the barge go down the brook; I wrote no meeting.
Namers: none came.
Parties sent out by my people: one, four of them, the god, the older ferry keeper of the ford landing, the younger ferry keeper who cuts the marks, and the Reckoner's cord walker. Set out for the ford by barge down the brook, about the middle of month five, one day, two days at the ford, then down the larger river three days and back as above, then the Brook Path two days home. The stops on the larger river and who is met there are the Keeper's to price and to give. The ford keepers lent the barge left with them last summer and kept the ferry. The ford keepers sent their low-water marks up the Brook Path by a runner in winter after the ice held. Searchers: none; no stock strayed. If the party was not back by the counted day, the Ferry Elder sends runners down the Brook Path to the ford and the town's barge after, with three poles; that is written in the year file's second branch.
Anything carried: the low-water cord, a bag of the town's cords for the ford keepers, the cord walker's knots. Nothing given to anyone outside. The cord walker's knots of the landings and hearths below the ford, if any, are the Reckoner's before the town's.

## Asked of the god
The ferry child, at the fire in spring: "Can I go with you to the ford?" Answered in front of her: the Ferry Elder said no, the Elder is right to keep a child out of the larger water, the guest will tell her at the fire what she saw and what the keepers measured, and will ask again when the Elder says.
The ferry child, at the fire in spring: "Then you are a spring's?" Answered in front of her: she woke at a spring, does not know if she is any spring's, and this is all she knows.
A small boy at the fire: "A spring cannot walk." Not answered in my files.
The Seat Warden: no question put. The guest asked her to keep the cord of it; she would, as she kept the rest.
The head herd keeper, at the sitting: "Why would you search for our goats, and what would you do that a boat and a dog cannot?" Not answered in my files; she said what she asked, and the keeper said what the pasture would do.
The Ferry Elder, at the gate stone: "Has the deep water moved, or has the ford?" Not answered by the god; the Elder answered it himself. The god had asked him.
The Ferry Elder, at the sitting: "What am I to say to the keepers about the river?" Answered in part: nothing yet; she would bring it to the council in summer with a going she wished to ask leave for.
The head of the oldest kin house, at the council before the going: "What do you seek below the ford?" Answered in front of him: why the deep water moves, and where the carriers cross now. He said that was two things, and she said it was.
The Ferry Elder, at the council: "How many days down, and when do you turn?" Answered in part: she had said three or four; the council and the keepers set three and the turn at dusk of the third. The settling was the council's.
A ford keeper, at the ford fire: "What are you looking for in the river?" Answered in front of the ford keepers: she is looking for why the deep water moves, and also, if there is any, for another like herself, and has found none; and she does not know what she is looking for in the sense of why she came.
A ford keeper, after: "What is another like yourself?" Not answered in my files.
The ferry child, in winter: "Can I go to the ford now?" Answered by the Ferry Elder, not the god: when she is twelve and has a pole of her own.
Any further question the children or the keepers put on the journey and any answer I did not write; the god's words are the DM's.

## Names said aloud
The Counting Woman: still said by the children and many in the town. Said at the fire in the spring.
Knee Deep: said by the ford keepers and ferry keepers at the ford and after.
Nessa Ferry: not said this year.
Names of the four reed hamlets: not asked this year, since the party did not stop at them.
No name for the people or their home was said. No office holder was named, the Speaker included. The older ferry keeper, the younger ferry keeper and the cord walker are not named. If a name is said aloud below the ford, by the party or by anyone met, the Keeper writes who said it, when and to whom; I write none.
