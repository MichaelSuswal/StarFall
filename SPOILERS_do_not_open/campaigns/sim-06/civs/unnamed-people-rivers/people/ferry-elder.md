# The Ferry Elder (unnamed)

Office: heads the ferry keepers, the ford and the barges, and sits on the council. A man of about sixty three in Year 1, thick, loud, practical, with a ferryman's arms and a bad knee. Born in a hamlet, has counted sixty three floods, and watches the deep water leave the ford more than any other worry.

Nature: practical to the bone and fond of arguing. Distrusts anything that cannot be poled. Took to the guest for her questions about the water.

Year 1: sent two ferry keepers with the guest to the hamlets. Had the first flood marks cut on the gate stone, and had the old ferrymen recall the earlier four. Wanted the sleeper carried in. Does not take her for more than a woman with a gift and a manner. Not a helper by the Keeper's rule unless the Keeper so rules.

Year 2: heard her at the hearing about the ford and said yes at once to the flood marks and the low-water cord. Sent a barge to the ford with the order in the second month. Named two ferry keepers to go with her (an older woman of the ford landing and a younger man who cuts the marks) and stayed home with his knee. Refused the ferry child leave to go. Asked her in autumn what she would do about the ford. Still does not take her for more than a woman with a gift and a good eye for water. Not a helper by the Keeper's rule unless the Keeper so rules.

Year 3: sixty five or so. At her asking read the flood marks and the low-water cord with her and the Reckoner in the flood month, and said the deep water had moved again. At the council counted the days of the going aloud (three down, eight up) and set the turn at dusk of the third day. Named the same two ferry keepers. Stayed home with his knee. Took the ferry child by the ear at the landing. Counted the party twice when it came home, if it came. Says the larger river is not the town's. Not a helper by the Keeper's rule unless the Keeper so rules.
Year 4: about sixty six. Read the marks with her and the Reckoner at the gate stone and said the deep water had moved again, the seventh time. Counted the days of the going aloud (five down, twelve and a half up, call it thirteen) and set the turn at dusk of the fifth day, sooner at the salt or for water or storm, never later. Named the same two ferry keepers. Stayed home with his knee. Gave the ferry child her pole and sent her to the ford and no farther. Stood on the bank at the weir measure. Said two things that go together are two things. Counted the party five out at the landing post, and twice when it came in, if it came. Says the larger river is not the town's. Not a helper by the Keeper's rule unless the Keeper so rules.
