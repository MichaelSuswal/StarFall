# The head of the weir hands (unnamed)

Office: head of the mill and weir hands, who works the oldest weir with the oldest kin house's kin. A man of about 56 in Year 4, slow spoken, two fingers gone to a weir stake, proud of his pole and of the pegs of the oldest weir, which he alone knows well.

Nature: sore when blamed, fair when the cord says so. Says the deep water left the weir hands whether or not they stood there, and that the silt behind a weir is the brook's own, which was there before the weirs.

Year 4: at the spring sitting said the weir hands would measure because the council said so and because it was their pole, and that nobody in the room was to hold it for them. Poled the measure at the low water on the bank at the Elder's side, with the guest standing off, and called the figures: nine hands behind the oldest weir, seven behind the second, five behind the third, three behind the newest of the four; one behind the first small weir and a finger behind the second. Told the council what the eel are worth: the four great weirs about nine parts in ten of the town's eel, the oldest alone about one part in five. Said at the fire that if the deep water comes back to the ford stones he will eat his pole. In the ninth month pulled the stakes and stone from a third of the oldest weir with eight hands in four days, because nobody else knew where the pegs were, and told the head of the oldest kin house his first stake was a bad one. Does not take the guest for more than a guest with a guess. Not a helper by the Keeper's rule unless the Keeper so rules.
