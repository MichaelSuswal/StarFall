# The Reckoner (unnamed)

Office: boundaries, debts, marriages gone bad and quarrels over boats. A man of about fifty in Year 1, lean, precise, with a long memory and a longer cord.

Nature: careful, jealous of the town's count and of the town's debts. Was uneasy at the guest's counting from the first day, and kept the second cord himself, so the town's count stayed his.

Year 1: walked with her at every count, sat the quarrel between two old ferrymen over the third flood's mark, and sat the boat quarrel in winter. At year's end he says her count and his have never differed, which is the first thing he has said in her favor.

Year 2: kept the low-water cord, as the council set it, as the town's and his own. Asked at the hearing which stone, who cuts and whose count it is. Named his cord walker to go with the guest to the ford. Keeps a cord of his own of the dimmed star's nights: five knots for the year. Leads the Counters, who say the cords are the town's and not hers. Says her counts and his still never differ.
