# The head of the oldest kin house (unnamed)

Office: head of the oldest kin house and a council seat. A man of about sixty eight in Year 1, grey, proud of his line, who counts his house back further than the low seat.

Nature: conservative and suspicious of strangers who count. Stood against the guest's counting at the first hearing and stood down only when the Reckoner's second cord was set. Leads the Wary.

Year 1: spoke against her at the hearing, put the first whisper about the well, and was publicly shown wrong by the well keepers. He bore it, grudgingly, and has since said little. He does not take her for more than a guest, and asks what she wants.

Year 2: asked at the hearing about the ford "The water told you? Which water, and what did it say?" and said a cord of what the town already knew was a cord of nothing, and did not stand against it. Stood apart at the short listening. In autumn said the cords were the Reckoner's now and he would say nothing against a cord that was the town's. Quieter. Leads the Wary. Does not take her for more than a guest, and still asks what she wants.

Year 3: about seventy one. Stood against the going down the larger river, saying it was not the town's and that a guest who took keepers where no one comes home was owed a hard question, and asked what she sought below the ford. Did not stand against it by the third evening. Said nothing against the second small weir. Leads the Wary, who say the star dims oftener in the years she stays. Does not take her for more than a guest, and still asks what she wants.
Year 4: about seventy two. In spring said that the shoal behind each weir was a thing the water said and not a thing the town had measured, and asked "A guess, you say. Which weir?" and was told she did not know. Stood against the going, asked what she would do if the salt was some other people's, and did not stand against it by the third evening. At the autumn sitting asked whether the star was hers, and said the oldest weir was his house's, set by his house's grandmothers, and that if it was wrong it was his house that should unset it. Put his own hand to the first stake of the gap. The Fond say he has never said anything so fair; the young Wary say he gave a weir to a guest's guess. Leads the Wary, now split. Does not take her for more than a guest, and still asks what she wants.
