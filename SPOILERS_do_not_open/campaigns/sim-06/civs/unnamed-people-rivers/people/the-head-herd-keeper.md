# The head herd keeper (unnamed)

Office: head of the herd keepers of the hill pasture, with the pasture hands under her. A woman of about fifty in Year 3, hill-born, weathered, plain, who counts heads at dawn and dusk and says what the count is without heat.

Nature: exact about loss and slow to blame. Asked the guest in good faith why she would search for the goats and what she would do that a boat and a dog cannot. Does not take her for more than a guest who walks without tiring and sleeps when the river asks.

Year 3: at the sitting corrected the loss to about twenty five goats and two cattle, set three hands on the pasture and a count at dawn as well as dusk, and said the pasture would look an hour and then send to the water gate for the boats, and that the guest was welcome to come if she liked. Asked the question above and had no answer that spring.
Year 4: about 51. Had her answer before the council in spring: the guest does not tire and does not sleep, would walk the reed edge with the dogs through a night and a day, and would be one more pair of feet. Said without heat that she had watched the guest sleep at the water below the gate in front of half the town, and that a person who sleeps is not a person who does not; the guest said it was so. Put it in the cord as a woman who does not tire and who sleeps when the river asks. Nothing strayed this year; the pasture counted at dawn and dusk and lost nothing. Not a helper by the Keeper's rule unless the Keeper so rules.
Year 5: about 52. Took the guest's offer of one more pair of feet at the sitting and had her on the pasture two nights, at the dark of the moon in the third month and in the first month of the harvest, the second at her own asking left with the Warden while the guest was away. Counted at dusk and dawn beside her, and put in the cord that she walked the bounds and did not tire. Nothing strayed. Not a helper by the Keeper's rule unless the Keeper so rules.
