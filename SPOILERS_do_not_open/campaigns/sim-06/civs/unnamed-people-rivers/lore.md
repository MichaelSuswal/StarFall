# What the people now say, end of Year 5

Where they explain a cause, they are wrong about something that matters. Year 1 and Year 2 sayings still told are kept in short; what is new in Year 3, Year 4 and Year 5 is gathered at the end.

## About the woman at the water gate
- That she is the guest the cup has waited for since Nessa Ferry first set it. Said by the children, the Seat Warden's neighbors and a few old women. The Warden says only that the cup has not been empty of her, and now that it is hers and the town's. Wrong, or unproven, in taking her for what the cup is for.
- That she is a namer who forgot to sing. Wrong: the namers pay the ferry with a song and go on. Told less now.
- That she is Nessa Ferry's kin come back from the water's side. Said by the old women. Wrong in the cause of her coming.
- That she is a salt carriers' counter, sent to count the town for a master upriver. Said by the Wary. Year 2: they add that she went to the ford to count the carriers' road, since she asked the carriers where they go. Half right that she counts and asks, wrong in the master.
- That her cold hands are a woman giving her warmth to the sick and shrinking for it, a larger measure of the midwives' quieting touch. Said by the midwives and the hamlets. Year 2: after the hamlet foot and the winter mother, the midwives say they would learn it, and the eldest midwife says it has its limits as hers does. Wrong in the cause: they think it is the same craft as the midwives', only more of it.
- Year 2: that she woke at the spring head and so is a spring's, as the ferry child said, and every river is one spring come up in many places. Said by the children and the old women. Wrong in what it makes of it: she said plainly she woke there and did not know why she came; the town has filled the gap.
- Year 2: that she came for the ford, since the river sent her to mend it. Said at the weirs and the ferry. Wrong: she said she asks and does not know.
- Year 2: that she is the woman from the west who stopped the Stopped Spring and forgot her own name on the way home, come back to the water she stopped. Said by the Wary, softly. Wrong in everything but the forgetting, which she told the children herself.
- Year 2: that the ford keepers call her Knee Deep, because she stood in the larger river to ask its leave to cross. Said by the ford keepers and the ferry keepers. Wrong in the reason; she said nothing of it at the time.
- That she is the Counting Woman, the ferry child's name for her, which the children and many in the town use. Still used.

## About the water and the ford
- That the deep water leaves the ford because the ford is crossed too little, so it forgets the place. Said at the weirs. Unproven; nobody has a better cause.
- Year 2: that the weirs on the near side shoulder the deep water across to the far bank, so the ferry keepers and the weir hands quarrel at the fire, kindly. Said by some ferry keepers. Wrong or unproven; the weir hands say it left the weirs' side whether or not they stood there.
- That the water listens a little at the edge of a field and not in a market, and a long time at the water gate for a guest who asks. Year 2: and a short while there too, and the guest sleeps after it for the length of two hours, as a body sleeps after labor.
- That a flood mark on the gate stone will tell a bad flood early. Said by the ferry keepers. Wrong: a mark records only what has come. Year 2: the ferry keepers now say the low-water marks will tell it, and the Reckoner says they tell what the water is, which is not the same.
- Year 2: that a cord of marks is the town's own and is not hers. Said by the Counters. Right, and a fair pride; wrong only in forgetting whose asking it was, which they did not forget.

## About the star
- Five times this year, more than last. The old women say the star is an eye that closes for an hour when the river sleeps. The salt carriers say a sky god blinks. The midwives say the star labors and its pains come faster, and no one asks what it will bear. The weirs say it dims for the ones awake at dawn work. The Ferry Elder says it dimmed before she came and will dim after. The Reckoner keeps a cord of it, five knots for Year 2, three for Year 1, and says it has never fallen in a flood month. All wrong or unproven, and none know a cause.
- Year 2: some of the Wary say it dims oftener in the years she stays. The ferry keepers answer that it did not dim at the ford while she stood in the river, since she was there and it did not. Which is true and proves nothing.

## About their rulers and neighbors
- That the council is loud, quarrelsome, long and fair, and that this year's Speaker, a woman, hears to the end and asks the plain question.
- That the salt carriers cross less because the upriver gods are quarrelling and the roads are closed. Wrong in the cause, by their own stories.
- Year 2: that the carriers go a southern way because the southern crossing keeps a better ferry. Said by the Ferry Elder's rivals among the ferry keepers. Wrong or unproven: the carriers said only that others go that way, and gave no reason.
- That the boat bands go at the lowest tides to hold their own council in the deep water. Wrong in what they do, no one having followed.
- Year 2: that the strayed herd went to drink at the Dry Braid, where a great beast once drank a river dry. Said by the old, at the fire, whichever way the search came out. Wrong: the Dry Braid is a place in a story.

## About the well
- That the well turned last year because the stranger fouled it. Told now as a story against hasty blame. Year 2: the upper well that ran cloudy in autumn was named at once as the rain wash, and no one said the guest had fouled it.

## What they say about gods
They still hold no god. Some now say, softly, that if the river ever sent one, it would come on foot and ask for nothing and keep a cord. A few of the Fond say she is not a guest any longer and does not know what else to be called. No one has taken her for a god.

## Year 3: what is new

About the woman at the water gate
- That she goes down rivers to find her kin. Said by the ford keepers, who heard her say at the ford fire that she was looking, if there were any, for another like herself, and that she had found none. Wrong in what they make of it: she said she did not know what she was, and the ford keepers heard "like herself" and made it a sister or a people.
- That she is a spring's, and a spring does not know it is one. Said by the old women, from what she told the children at the fire this spring: that she woke at a spring, does not know whether she is any spring's, and that this is all she knows. Wrong in the cause: they take her not knowing as the spring's way, and not as hers.
- That Knee Deep stands in the larger river each evening and dawn to ask its leave to cross, and the river gave it. Said by the ford keepers and the ferry keepers who stood on the bank. Wrong in the reason; she said nothing of it, and what she felt is not told.
- That the going down the larger river was the town's going and not hers. Said by the Counters, because the council gave leave, the Elder set the days and the Reckoner's cord walker went to count. Right in the keeping, wrong in forgetting whose asking it was, which they do not.
- That a guest who takes keepers where no one comes home is owed a hard question. Said by the head of the oldest kin house, in the council, before the going. Told now as a fair thing said, whichever way the going came out.
- That the Counting Woman and Knee Deep are one woman. Said by the town, now. Right.

About the water and the ford
- That the eel follow the deep water, and the small weirs will show where it goes. Said by the weir hands, who have set a second small weir on the far side. Unproven; the first small weir took eel and the second took a little.
- That the weirs shoulder the deep water across to the far bank. Still said at the fire by some ferry keepers; the weir hands still say it left them whether or not they stood there. The sixth flood running did not settle it.
- That the larger river is not the town's, and a barge that goes down it is owed to it. Said by the Wary. Wrong or unproven; no barge was owed.
- That you learn what a river is by going up it. Said by the ferry keepers who went, as of any river, and said all the more now.

About the star
- Six nights this year, more than last: one in the flood month, a pair in the fifth, a pair in the seventh and one in the eighth. The old women say the eye closes oftener now because the river sleeps longer in the dry years. The salt carriers say a sky god blinks oftener because it is angrier. The midwives say the star labors and its pains come very fast now, and some say it will bear soon. The weirs say it dims for the ones awake at dawn work. The Ferry Elder says it dimmed before she came and will dim after. All wrong or unproven, and none know a cause.
- That it has never fallen in a flood month. Said by the Reckoner until the first night of this year, which fell in one. He says now that he would rather be shown wrong by a cord than right by a habit.
- That it dims when she is in the town, five times of six. Said by the Wary, from the Reckoner's cord with its marks for where she stood. The ferry keepers answer that the sixth fell when she was on the water and the town saw it all the same, so it does not care where she is. The Wary say a thing can be in two places. Which is true, and proves nothing.

About their rulers and neighbors
- That this year's Speaker, a man, hears a thing three times before he speaks on it, and the council is louder and longer for it. Said by the council's own, kindly.
- That the carriers go a southern way because the sky god blinks and the south is where the sky is still. Said by the old, at the fire. Wrong; the carriers gave no reason, and told the guest none.
- That the herd that strayed was thirty goats and two cattle. Said by the old and by the guest, once. The pasture hands say twenty five goats and two cattle, and the Reckoner's cord says so; the old say thirty because that is how many the Dry Braid drank.
- That the second small weir is a small answer to a large water, and that a small answer is the only kind the council has in its hand this season. Said by a weir hand and by the Speaker. Said twice and kept.

About the larger river below the ford
- What the party saw and who they met below the ford is not set down here; it is told as the Keeper gives it. What the town says of it is theirs, and where they explain a cause they are wrong about something that matters.

What they say about gods
They still hold no god. A few of the Fond say she is not a guest any longer and does not know what else to be called. Some of the Wary say the river sent a woman and the woman took keepers down the river, and that it is not the same thing as the river sending one. No one has taken her for a god.

## Year 4: what is new

About the woman at the water gate
- That the boy was right and she said so, and that a guest who tells a small boy he is right is a guest who keeps her word. Said by the children, the mill hands and the Seat Warden's neighbors. Right, and fair, and not what she meant by it: she said a spring cannot walk and she is not one, which the boy took for a win and the town took for a plain thing plainly said.
- That a spring that walks is the spring's going down, as the brook is, and that she said it the long way. Said by the old women, at the back of the fire. Wrong in the cause: she said she is not a spring and does not know why the one came with the other; the old women have made the gap into a brook.
- That she is a guest who does not tire and sleeps when the river asks. Said by the head herd keeper, from the cord, and now by the pasture hands. Right in the saying, wrong in what it makes of it: the midwives and the hamlets say the river puts her to sleep as a mother does a child, and that she is the river's child and not its guest. She said no such thing.
- That another like her would come with a weight in the air, like the weather before a storm, and that if one came she would feed it first. Said by the ford keepers, from what she said at the ford fire. Wrong in the weight: they say a storm, since that is the weight they know, and the weirs say the sky god's blink is the same weight. She did not say what the weight is. Right that she would feed it first, which the ford keepers say is the town's own code said by a stranger who has not been told it.
- That she stood in the larger river for the town's leave, as the ford keepers say Knee Deep does, and that the river gave it. Still said.
- That the ferry child, who has a pole, is the guest's own, since she went to the ford and heard the answer at the fire and came home with it. Said by the ferry keepers, kindly. Wrong: she is the Elder's, and the Elder says so.
- That the Counting Woman, Knee Deep and the guest are one woman. Said by the town. Right.

About the water and the weirs
- That the oldest weir has the most silt because it is the oldest, and that the deep water leaves the ford for the same reason: for being old. Said by the weir hands. Half right and half a hope: the silt follows the years, and the weir hands have not said why the deep water leaves.
- That the silt behind the weirs shoulders the deep water across to the far bank, and the figures show it, nine, seven, five, three. Said by the ferry keepers, now with the Reckoner's cord to point at. Wrong, or unproven: the figures say what is behind each weir and not what the water does at the ford. The Reckoner says a cord says what and not why, and the ferry keepers say that is what a cord says when it is on the other side.
- That a silt is the brook's own and was there before the weirs. Said by the head of the weir hands. Wrong in what it spares: a thing that was there before can still be made worse by what is built on it.
- That a gap in the oldest weir lets the water remember its way. Said by the old women. Wrong in the cause, whichever way it falls out.
- That the weir was unset because a guest asked and the oldest kin house owed it a weir. Said by the young Wary. Wrong: the head of the oldest kin house said it was his house's and the cord was the town's.
- That the head of the weir hands will eat his pole if the deep water comes back to the ford stones. Said at the fire, by him, and repeated with pleasure by the ferry keepers.
- That the eel know which weir is open and will go to it. Said by a weir hand's child, to laughter, and by the weir hands' wives, not to laughter.
- That the town unset a weir for the first time and the weir was the oldest. Said by everyone, correctly.

About the star
- Seven nights this year, more than last, three of them in the winter. The old women say the eye closes oftener because the river sleeps longer, and the midwives say the star's pains come faster and it has not borne, now said with less patience. The salt carriers say the sky god blinks, as before. The Reckoner says it does not count by ones, three, five, six, seven. The Ferry Elder says it dimmed before she came. All wrong or unproven, and none know a cause.
- That it dimmed in the town six nights of the seven and on the water one, so it follows her to the town more than to the river. Said by the Wary. The ferry keepers answer that she was in the town for almost every night of the year and that is what the cord says. The Reckoner says he does not think it is nothing and does not think it is something. Which is true, and proves nothing.
- That the star dimmed while she was away and the weirs' people asked the Warden whether it dimmed for her, and the Warden said she would keep it. Said at the gate house. Told now as a fair question, whichever way it was answered.

About their rulers and neighbors
- That the council called back a Speaker for the first time, because it wanted the plain question. Said by the council's own.
- That the oldest kin house gave a weir to the cord and the oldest kin house's head has never said a thing so fair. Said by the Fond, and by the Reckoner, in fewer words.
- That the salt carriers cross less because they have heard of the woman who stands in the river and go round her. Said by some of the Wary. Wrong in the cause: the carriers came less before she came, and the reed cutters say the salt for the stone towns now comes up from the sea in the south.
- That the larger river is not the town's, and a barge that goes down it is owed to it. Said by the Elder, and by the Wary, with the Elder's knee. Wrong or unproven; no barge was owed.
- That you learn what a river is by going up it. Said by the ferry keepers who went, said all the more now, and said twice.

About the larger river below the ford
- What the party saw and who they met below the ford is not set down here; it is told as the Keeper gives it. What the town says of it is theirs, and where they explain a cause they are wrong about something that matters.

What they say about gods
They still hold no god. A few of the Fond say she is not a guest any longer and does not know what else to be called. Some of the Wary say the river sent a woman and the woman took keepers further down the river than anyone had been, and that it is not the same thing as the river sending one. The children say she is the one the boy was right about. No one has taken her for a god.

## Year 5: what is new

About the woman at the water gate
- That she does not know what dims the star and said so, and that a guest who says it plainly is a guest who has nothing to hide. Said by the Fond and the old women. Wrong, or unproven: she said what she knew, and the people have made the plainness into a proof.
- That "that she knows" is a small door. Said by a weir hand, and repeated by the young Wary. Right that it is a door, wrong in what they think is behind it.
- That she wants the grandmothers not buried in a bad year, and that it was said for the old women. Said by the old women. Right in the saying, wrong in what it makes of her: they take it for a mother's wish, and she said it as a guest's.
- That she wants a council that can say no to her, and that this is what a guest says who has been told no and wants more of it. Said by some of the young Wary. Wrong: the council has said no to her and she did not press, which the Warden's cord has.
- That she looks for others like herself, and that the star's labor is the same labor: when it bears, it will bear another like her. Said by the midwives, and told to the children. Wrong in the connection: she did not tie the two, and the midwives have made the star's pains and her looking one story.
- That she does not tire and walks the pasture all night and is not tired in the morning, and that it is the herd keepers' cord and not a story. Said by the pasture hands and the head herd keeper. Right in the saying, and fair, and no one makes more of it.
- That Knee Deep walked the carriers' road and stood in the brooks it crossed. Said by the ford keepers and the ferry keepers. What was found on the road is not set down here; it is told as the Keeper gives it, and where they explain a cause they are wrong about something that matters.
- That the Counting Woman, Knee Deep and the guest are one woman. Said by the town. Right.

About the water and the weirs
- That the gap is a hand lower because the weirs were the cause, and the deep water not back is because one gap is one gap. Said by the ferry keepers. Wrong or unproven: a hole in a weir passes the brook and scours where it runs, which the weir hands say and which does not say whether the ford is the weirs' doing.
- That a gap scours as a gap does and says nothing of the ford. Said by the head of the weir hands. Right in what a gap does, wrong in what it spares: the ford is a different place from the gap, and nobody has shown that the two are not one water.
- That every other weir is a half hand fuller because the oldest weir's gap has taken the brook's silt and laid it on the others. Said by the weir hands' wives. Wrong or unproven; nobody can say where a silt went.
- That a flood lays silt thin and the low water shows what stays. Said by the head of the weir hands. Right, and the Reckoner wrote the season beside every figure for it.
- That the head of the weir hands will keep his pole on its peg and eat it next year if the deep water comes back, and that the ferry keepers will remind him. Said at the fire, kindly.
- That the fish came to the gap because the gap is the river's door, and a door is where a run stands. Said by the old women and some of the weir hands. Wrong in the cause: the fish stood at all the weirs and thickest at the gap, and nobody knows why they came.
- That the fish came to the gap because the weirs are wrong, and the fish wanted to go up and the weirs held them. Said by some of the ferry keepers, softly, and not said by the others, who say they are waiting to hear it said. Wrong or unproven: the fish came thickest at the one weir that was open, which proves the gap and not the rest.
- That the fish are the river's thanks for the gap. Said by the Fond and the children. Wrong in what they think a river does.
- That the guest brought the fish, since she sits in the town through the ice and the fish came in the ice; and that she did not go to the water and so did not bring them, since she does not need to. Both said, by the Wary and the Fond in turn, at the same fire. Wrong in both ways, or in neither; no one knows, and the weir hand's child who asked her got no answer the town can repeat.
- That a fish taken by hand is the hand's and the hand's house's, and that the oldest kin house's gap sent its fish back by cord as it sent its eel. Said by the Reckoner and then by everyone. Right, and fair.
- That the oldest kin house has done three fair things. Said by the Fond. The young Wary say two were talked into him.

About the star
- Three nights this year, fewer than last and as few as the first year, none in the summer, none in the winter. The old women say the eye closes less because the river sleeps less, and the midwives say the star's pains have slowed and that a labor that slows ends one way or the other. The salt carriers say the sky god blinks less because the south is quieter. The Reckoner says it does not count by ones, three, five, six, seven, three. The Ferry Elder says it dimmed before she came and will dim after. All wrong or unproven, and none know a cause.
- That it did not dim while she walked the carriers' road, and dimmed in the town three times of three. Said by the Wary. The ferry keepers answer that she was away about twenty four nights and the star came three times in the year, which is no sort of count. The Reckoner says he does not think it is nothing and does not think it is something. Which is true, and proves nothing.
- That it dims fewer since she answered the weirs' people and said she did not know, and that a thing asked is a thing quieted. Said by some of the children and the old women. Wrong in the cause.

About their rulers and neighbors
- That this year's Speaker, the head of the second oldest kin house, says less than any Speaker in memory and has the Warden say the cord back after each thing. Said by the council's own, kindly.
- That the carriers cross less because the sky god blinks, and because they have heard of the woman who stands in the river, and because the roads are closed. Said at the fire, all three, whichever the speaker likes. Wrong in all three; the carriers came less before she came.
- That the reed cutters of the lower channels know where the salt comes ashore and will not say. Said by the Wary. Wrong: they said far to the west, by land, and the town hears "will not say" because it did not hear a place.
- That the sea is salt and the salt does not come up the river, and that the ferry keepers could have told the council so. Said by the children, with pleasure. Right in the saying, and what the council makes of it is the council's.
- That the road is not the town's, as the larger river was not. Said by the Elder and the young Wary. Wrong or unproven.

About the road beyond the ford
- What the party saw, who they met and what was said is not set down here; it is told as the Keeper gives it. What the town says of it is theirs, and where they explain a cause they are wrong about something that matters.

What they say about gods
They still hold no god. A few of the Fond say she is not a guest any longer and does not know what else to be called. Some of the Wary say that a guest who says she does not know what dims the star, and that she wants others like herself, is a guest who is looking for something, and that the town should ask what. The children say she is the one the boy was right about and the one who says it a third time. No one has taken her for a god.
