# What the people now say, end of Year 2

Where they explain a cause, they are wrong about something that matters. Year 1 sayings still told are kept in short; what is new in Year 2 is marked.

## About the woman at the water gate
- That she is the guest the cup has waited for since Nessa Ferry first set it. Said by the children, the Seat Warden's neighbors and a few old women. The Warden says only that the cup has not been empty of her, and now that it is hers and the town's. Wrong, or unproven, in taking her for what the cup is for.
- That she is a namer who forgot to sing. Wrong: the namers pay the ferry with a song and go on. Told less now.
- That she is Nessa Ferry's kin come back from the water's side. Said by the old women. Wrong in the cause of her coming.
- That she is a salt carriers' counter, sent to count the town for a master upriver. Said by the Wary. Year 2: they add that she went to the ford to count the carriers' road, since she asked the carriers where they go. Half right that she counts and asks, wrong in the master.
- That her cold hands are a woman giving her warmth to the sick and shrinking for it, a larger measure of the midwives' quieting touch. Said by the midwives and the hamlets. Year 2: after the hamlet foot and the winter mother, the midwives say they would learn it, and the eldest midwife says it has its limits as hers does. Wrong in the cause: they think it is the same craft as the midwives', only more of it.
- Year 2: that she woke at the spring head and so is a spring's, as the ferry child said, and every river is one spring come up in many places. Said by the children and the old women. Wrong in what it makes of it: she said plainly she woke there and did not know why she came; the town has filled the gap.
- Year 2: that she came for the ford, since the river sent her to mend it. Said at the weirs and the ferry. Wrong: she said she asks and does not know.
- Year 2: that she is the woman from the west who stopped the Stopped Spring and forgot her own name on the way home, come back to the water she stopped. Said by the Wary, softly. Wrong in everything but the forgetting, which she told the children herself.
- Year 2: that the ford keepers call her Knee Deep, because she stood in the larger river to ask its leave to cross. Said by the ford keepers and the ferry keepers. Wrong in the reason; she said nothing of it at the time.
- That she is the Counting Woman, the ferry child's name for her, which the children and many in the town use. Still used.

## About the water and the ford
- That the deep water leaves the ford because the ford is crossed too little, so it forgets the place. Said at the weirs. Unproven; nobody has a better cause.
- Year 2: that the weirs on the near side shoulder the deep water across to the far bank, so the ferry keepers and the weir hands quarrel at the fire, kindly. Said by some ferry keepers. Wrong or unproven; the weir hands say it left the weirs' side whether or not they stood there.
- That the water listens a little at the edge of a field and not in a market, and a long time at the water gate for a guest who asks. Year 2: and a short while there too, and the guest sleeps after it for the length of two hours, as a body sleeps after labor.
- That a flood mark on the gate stone will tell a bad flood early. Said by the ferry keepers. Wrong: a mark records only what has come. Year 2: the ferry keepers now say the low-water marks will tell it, and the Reckoner says they tell what the water is, which is not the same.
- Year 2: that a cord of marks is the town's own and is not hers. Said by the Counters. Right, and a fair pride; wrong only in forgetting whose asking it was, which they did not forget.

## About the star
- Five times this year, more than last. The old women say the star is an eye that closes for an hour when the river sleeps. The salt carriers say a sky god blinks. The midwives say the star labors and its pains come faster, and no one asks what it will bear. The weirs say it dims for the ones awake at dawn work. The Ferry Elder says it dimmed before she came and will dim after. The Reckoner keeps a cord of it, five knots for Year 2, three for Year 1, and says it has never fallen in a flood month. All wrong or unproven, and none know a cause.
- Year 2: some of the Wary say it dims oftener in the years she stays. The ferry keepers answer that it did not dim at the ford while she stood in the river, since she was there and it did not. Which is true and proves nothing.

## About their rulers and neighbors
- That the council is loud, quarrelsome, long and fair, and that this year's Speaker, a woman, hears to the end and asks the plain question.
- That the salt carriers cross less because the upriver gods are quarrelling and the roads are closed. Wrong in the cause, by their own stories.
- Year 2: that the carriers go a southern way because the southern crossing keeps a better ferry. Said by the Ferry Elder's rivals among the ferry keepers. Wrong or unproven: the carriers said only that others go that way, and gave no reason.
- That the boat bands go at the lowest tides to hold their own council in the deep water. Wrong in what they do, no one having followed.
- Year 2: that the strayed herd went to drink at the Dry Braid, where a great beast once drank a river dry. Said by the old, at the fire, whichever way the search came out. Wrong: the Dry Braid is a place in a story.

## About the well
- That the well turned last year because the stranger fouled it. Told now as a story against hasty blame. Year 2: the upper well that ran cloudy in autumn was named at once as the rain wash, and no one said the guest had fouled it.

## What they say about gods
They still hold no god. Some now say, softly, that if the river ever sent one, it would come on foot and ask for nothing and keep a cord. A few of the Fond say she is not a guest any longer and does not know what else to be called. No one has taken her for a god.
