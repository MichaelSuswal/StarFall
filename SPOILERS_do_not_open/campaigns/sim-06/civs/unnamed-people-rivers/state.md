# Unnamed people of god-rivers: state at the end of Year 3

Written by the civ agent at the close of Year 3, pass one. Where a number turns on an uncertain outcome or on what a power did, a range is given.

population: about 2,045 (about 1,885 in the walled town, about 160 in four reed hamlets), in about 350 hearths. Why: about 75 births and about 60 deaths in an ordinary year, a net rise of about 15 on the Year 2 figure of about 2,030. Deaths were the old and infants as in other winters, and the usual one or two to the channels. On the journey down the larger river the number may differ by one (a ferry keeper or the cord walker lost to the river); range about 2,044 to 2,045. No warm hands were asked this year.

stores: barley, millet, beans, flax, onions and dried eel and fish, enough to the next harvest in an ordinary flood. The flood store at the water gate stands at about one part in twenty of the barley and millet, the older grain turned to the table and new put by, with smoked fish and eel. Salt: none in store; the thaw took the last; what the carriers brought was little and is rationed by house cord. Why: ordinary harvest, though three hands were away at the opening of the cutting if the party was not home in time; the council's habit; the carriers crossed fewer times again. Livestock: the herd is down about 25 goats and 2 cattle from the autumn of Year 2, part made good by the spring kids; the winter's milk and fat were short. The pasture now keeps three hands, and counts at dawn and dusk through the harvest; nothing strayed.

treasure: little: cords, boats, a few bronze tools. New: the seventh flood mark on the gate stone; four more low-water marks at the gate stone and at the ford stone, eight each in all; the second small fish weir in the side channel on the far side, which took a little eel; the Reckoner's cord of the dimmed nights, with a mark for where she stood at each; the cord walker's knots of the landings and hearths below the ford, if the party was home. The first small fish weir stands. Why: the council's own keeping of the god's asking; one small new work, twenty worker-days.

trade: reed work, eels, linen and pots out; salt in at the ford, less than last year. The boat bands traded at dusk as before. The carriers who came said again that others go a southern way and gave no place, no distance and no reason. Why: the carriers come less each year and the town cannot make them come. The town's own going down the larger river was to look for where they cross; what it found is the Keeper's.

strength: a standing watch of about 30 and a call-up of about 300, unchanged. Earth wall, palisade and ditch repaired after the flood. Why: no fighting, no new arms.

mood: curious, steady, a little worried. The ford worry is the same, now with seven marks and eight cords to it; the salt worry is the same; the star dimming six nights, the first in a flood month, worries the weirs and the midwives more than the ford. If the party came home as counted, the ferry keepers are proud and the Elder grumbles that the larger river is not the town's; if not, the town is quiet and sore and the Wary say they were right. They are easy about the guest, who is a guest, and many say she is the town's own.

factions: the Wary (the head of the oldest kin house and some of the weir hands) stood against the going and say the star dims oftener in the years she stays; the Fond (the midwives, the ferry keepers, the ford keepers, the children) are fonder, the ferry keepers more for the going; the Counters (the Reckoner and his cord walkers, with the ferry keepers who cut the marks) say the cords are the town's and the going was the town's, and keep the star cord with where she stood; the rest keep their own counsel.

## Keeper, Year 1, pass two
Nothing came against the sleeping woman at the water gate; no roll. Each of the three she laid hands on recovered, as her card gives: the numbers take the year file's branch for that.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The strayed herd [r-0295]: part home, part lost: about thirty-five goats and three cattle home, no searcher hurt.
- Nothing came against the short listening. Both whom the god warmed recovered. The council had a mark set to read by at the autumn low water.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- The journey: three days down the larger river, about ninety miles, into the reed country of many channels below the hills, and about eight days back. They were back at the ford by the counted day. Met below: reed cutters at their staithes, and one dusk a band of the boat people, who traded reed work for a knife. The reed cutters say the salt for the stone towns now comes up from the sea in the south and does not need the ford.
- She stood in the larger river each dusk and dawn and felt nothing pull.
- Nothing came against the listening at the ford. She slept about two hours.
- What she told the council in autumn: what the keepers measured; what the reed cutters said of the salt; that the deep water lies farther again from the ford stones on the side away from the weirs and the bed behind each weir is shoaled; that fewer laden beasts crossed; and that she found none like herself in that water. The council's course is the one you wrote for that telling.
