# Unnamed people of god-rivers: state at the end of Year 5

Written by the civ agent at the close of Year 5, pass one. Where a number turns on an uncertain outcome or on what a power did, a range is given.

population: about 2,075 (about 1,915 in the walled town, about 160 in four reed hamlets), in about 355 hearths. Why: about 75 births and about 60 deaths in an ordinary year, a net rise of about 15 on the Year 4 figure of about 2,060 (the Keeper's Year 4 line took the child's recovery and all four home). Deaths were the old and infants as in other winters and the usual one or two to the channels. On the salt road the number may be lower by one (the younger ferry keeper or the cord walker lost) or by two (both, or someone came against the party); range about 2,073 to 2,075.

stores: barley, millet, beans, flax, onions and dried eel and fish, enough to the next harvest in an ordinary flood; the harvest was ordinary. The flood store at the water gate stands at about one part in twenty of the barley and millet, the older grain turned to the table and new put by, with smoked fish and eel. Fish: the run of the eleventh month gave the largest fish store any weir hand remembers, about two parts in three of the take kept, in the cold of the yards, in smoke and with a little salt; a part of it will not last the thaw without salt. Salt: none in store; the thaw took the last; what the carriers brought was little and dearer and is rationed by house cord. Why: ordinary harvest; the council's habit; the carriers crossed fewer times again; the fish were not brought by anyone. The oldest weir's households carry a loss of about one part in four of that weir's year this year, the gap open through the autumn run and the whole year, about one part in twenty of the town's eel, made good by the other houses by cord, a small share of dried eel and barley, a little larger than last year's; the head of the oldest kin house sent shares of the fish back by cord to the houses that made it good. Livestock: the herd is back to its count before the Year 2 loss. The pasture keeps three hands, counts at dawn and dusk through the harvest; nothing strayed.

treasure: little: cords, boats, a few bronze tools. New: the ninth flood mark on the gate stone; four more low-water marks at the gate stone and at the ford stone, sixteen each in all; the Reckoner's cord of the weir measures, now with the flood's fall and the low water of this year written beside last year's (behind the oldest weir along the gap eight hands, to either side nine; the second seven and a half, the third five and a half, the newest of the four three and a half; the first small fish weir a hand and two fingers, the second two fingers); the Reckoner's cord of the dimmed nights, three knots this year with a mark for where she stood at each, sixteen across the three years of marks and twenty four knots across the five; the cord walker's knots of the stages, hearths and gifts on the carriers' road, if the party was home. The gap in the oldest weir, a third of its width, stands open a second year. A bundle of linen, six pots, two bronze knives and dried eel went out as the fee for the carrier family and for the road. Why: the council's own keeping of the god's asking; one gap held.

trade: reed work, eels, linen and pots out; salt in at the ford, less than last year and dearer. The boat bands traded at dusk as before. The carriers who came said little. The town's own going with the carriers was to see the salt road; what it found is the Keeper's. Why: the carriers come less each year and the town cannot make them come.

strength: a standing watch of about 30 and a call-up of about 300, unchanged. Earth wall, palisade and ditch repaired after the flood. Why: no fighting, no new arms.

mood: curious, steady, a little worried, the worry now more the salt than the star. The ford worry is the same, with nine flood marks, sixteen low-water marks at each stone, a cord of the weirs at two seasons and the deep water still not back; the salt worry is the same and sharper; the star dimmed three nights, none in the summer, none in the winter, which eased the weirs and the midwives. The fish run lifted the town and puzzled it. The weir hands are less sore: the gap went down a hand and the pole stays on its peg. The ferry keepers wait for the fish to be said to be the weirs' fault. If the party came home as counted, the ferry keepers are proud, the ferry child has been to the ford again, the Elder grumbles that a road is not a river; if not, the town is quiet and sore and the Wary say they were right. They are easy about the guest, who is a guest, and many say she is the town's own.

factions: the Wary (the head of the oldest kin house, some of the weir hands, the younger of his house) are split: the head of the oldest kin house kept his house's gap open a second year and sent fish shares back by cord, and the Fond say he has done three fair things; the young Wary say he was talked into it twice and that the star dims in the town. The Fond (the midwives, the ferry keepers, the ford keepers, the children, the head herd keeper in her way) are fonder; the old women took her answer of what she wants as said for them. The Counters (the Reckoner and his cord walkers, with the ferry keepers who cut the marks) keep the star cord with where she stood and agree with her, in the Reckoner's phrase, that it is not nothing and not something. The weir hands are a body of their own, fair to the cord, less sore. The ferry keepers are for the road if it brings salt. The rest keep their own counsel.

## Keeper, Year 1, pass two
Nothing came against the sleeping woman at the water gate; no roll. Each of the three she laid hands on recovered, as her card gives: the numbers take the year file's branch for that.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The strayed herd [r-0295]: part home, part lost: about thirty-five goats and three cattle home, no searcher hurt.
- Nothing came against the short listening. Both whom the god warmed recovered. The council had a mark set to read by at the autumn low water.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- The journey: three days down the larger river, about ninety miles, into the reed country of many channels below the hills, and about eight days back. They were back at the ford by the counted day. Met below: reed cutters at their staithes, and one dusk a band of the boat people, who traded reed work for a knife. The reed cutters say the salt for the stone towns now comes up from the sea in the south and does not need the ford.
- She stood in the larger river each dusk and dawn and felt nothing pull.
- Nothing came against the listening at the ford. She slept about two hours.
- What she told the council in autumn: what the keepers measured; what the reed cutters said of the salt; that the deep water lies farther again from the ford stones on the side away from the weirs and the bed behind each weir is shoaled; that fewer laden beasts crossed; and that she found none like herself in that water. The council's course is the one you wrote for that telling.

## Keeper, Year 4, pass two
- The journey: five days down, to where the river meets the sea in a shifting delta of reed and channels, and about twelve days home; all four came back. No salt comes up that river. Reed cutters of the lower channels say the salt for the stone towns is landed far to the west and goes up by land. Nothing came against the party.
- Warm hands: the child's fever came down. Your numbers take that branch.
- What the water does at the gap in the oldest weir shows at next spring's flood.
