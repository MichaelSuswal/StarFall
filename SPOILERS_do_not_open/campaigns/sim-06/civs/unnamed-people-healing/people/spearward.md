# The Spearward

Nature and history, fixed. A broad man of about forty five at the end of Year 2, who holds the levy of about four hundred, little trained, with no walls to hold. Practical, short of speech, not fond of what he cannot count. He had not met the stranger before month 5 of Year 2, though the Warden's tale of the heap had reached him. At the seat's hearth in month 5 he sat by the door and asked it one thing straight, whether it could do to a granary what it did to the heap, and was answered in front of the hearth. He has not said what he made of the answer. He has changed nothing in the levy. His name was not said aloud this year.

Year 3: month 8, at the seat's hearth, he asked the stranger from his place by the door whether it would rot a granary, ours or another's, if the seat bade it, in war. It answered in front of the hearth that it would not turn it on any store of the people, in war or out of it. He said nothing in the hall. In the yard he put two men on the court steading's grain house by night, on his own word, and said to them that a promise is not a lock. The levy is otherwise unchanged. His name was not said aloud this year.
