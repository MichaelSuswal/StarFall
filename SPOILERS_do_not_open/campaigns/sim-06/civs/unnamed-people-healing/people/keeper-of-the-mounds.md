# The Keeper of the Mounds

Nature and history, fixed. A man of about sixty, quiet, who keeps the grave-ground and its records. In the spring he watched the stranger walk the mounds from the field edge and marked the mound it stayed at longest. He told no one, and has said nothing of it except to the Reckoner of Bodies, with whom he sits at the autumn count. His name was not said aloud this year. He knows what the people's old tellings of the mounds leave out and says nothing.

Year 2, month 2: he came out to meet the stranger and the sitters at the mounds and walked beside it among the moss, saying little. Month 3: at the bank he agreed, as a trial, to lay the court steading's next dead on the rise behind the court's yard for those whose kin would have it, and moved no laid body. After the Judge-Mother's ruling in month 8 he writes each such name at both places. By the winter's end about eight lie on the rise. He does not take the stranger for the one awaited. His name was not said aloud this year.
