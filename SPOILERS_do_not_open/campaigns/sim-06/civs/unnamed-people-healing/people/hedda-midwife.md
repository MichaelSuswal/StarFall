# Hedda, midwife of the court steading

Nature and history, fixed. A broad grey woman of about fifty at the end of Year 1, midwife of the court steading and the one who took the stranger in. She has no sitter's gift. She gave her name aloud to the stranger at her door in month 1, because it gave its need first. She asked the stranger for each hard case in the sitters' way, nursed it through each shake, and kept its bed in late summer. She connects the star's dimming in month 5 with the stranger's going out under the sky that night and has told no one. She is the person in the court steading who knows the stranger best and says least.

Year 2: she asked the stranger for about nine hard cases, against about thirty the year before, and kept its bed after each. She went with it to the mounds in month 2. In the first days of the thaw she saw the shake leave its hands. She counted the five dim nights against the stranger's doings and has told no one how they fell.

Year 3: she asked the stranger for about seven hard cases, against nine the year before, and kept its bed after each. Month 3: the old sitter of the mounds told her the moss was common, and she told the stranger at her hearth that evening. She went with it to the mounds at midsummer and after the first frost. She saw the bone-handled knife at its belt and saw it cut bread and cord with it and nothing else. She counted the six dim nights against the sendings and found no tie, one of them while the stranger was away on its walk, and has told no one. She kept its bed ready the fourteen days it was gone.

Year 4: she asked the stranger for about four hard cases, against seven the year before, and kept its bed after each, and she kept it ready the twelve days it was gone downstream. In month 2 she stood at the mounds and put the sitters' question about brewing to it. In month 3 she set the Keeper's words in order at the bank with the Reckoner's runner. In month 5, with the stranger away, she turned a runner from a steading below the court away without an answer and sent two sitters. She counted the seven dim nights against the sendings: four fell in months when it was sent for, three did not, none while it was away. She told the Judge-Mother, who asked, and no one else.
