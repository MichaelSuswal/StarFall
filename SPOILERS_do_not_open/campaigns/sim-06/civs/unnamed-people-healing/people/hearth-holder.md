# The Hearth-Holder

Nature and history, fixed. A man of the line, about sixty at the end of Year 1, who holds the throne in the Tender's name. He is not a man who asks, and in month 11 he sent to Hedda when his youngest grandchild, a girl of four, took the wet chest. His name was not said aloud this year. He has not met the stranger. In the last month he sent a runner to say he would be glad to have the stranger sit at the seat's hearth when it was well. He has said nothing in the open about who the stranger might be.

Year 2, month 1: the Reckoner carried him the stranger's word and he said the day was hers to name and that he would send no second runner. Month 5: he sat the stranger at the seat's hearth, asked it straight whether it was the one the seat is kept for, and was answered truly; he asked its name and was told it. He did not take its word that it was the one and did not refuse it. Month 12: he said in the open that it was welcome at his hearth, and said no more. His name was not said aloud this year.

Year 3: in month 7 three old women of the nearest steadings asked him that the seat be filled, since the moss had failed; he sent them home with beer and no answer. In month 8 he sent a runner to the Reckoner to say the stranger might sit at the hearth again; at the end of the month it did, with the Judge-Mother across the fire and the Spearward by the door, and he asked it nothing. He said it was welcome as long as it would sit there and that the seat was not kept for a moss. At the thaw he asked to see the Reckoner's cords, the first thing he is recalled asking for, and looked a long while. He has not taken its word that it is the one and has not refused it. His name was not said aloud this year.

Year 4: month 8 he sent a runner to the Reckoner and sat the stranger at the seat's hearth a third time; he asked it nothing and said it was welcome as long as it would sit there and that the seat was kept and not claimed. In month 12 he sent three old women home with beer again when they asked that the seat be filled. At the thaw he asked again to see the Reckoner's cords. He has not taken its word that it is the one and has not refused it. His name was not said aloud this year.

Year 5: month 7 he sent two old women of the nearest steadings home with beer when they asked that the seat be filled, and none came in winter. Month 8 he sent a runner to the Reckoner and sat the stranger at the seat's hearth a fourth time, with the girl of eight beside him; he asked it nothing and said it was welcome as long as it would sit there and that the seat was kept and not claimed. At the thaw he asked again to see the Reckoner's cords and she told him in her own words what she counts; he looked a long while and did not ask for the stranger. He has not taken its word that it is the one and has not refused it. His name was not said aloud this year.
