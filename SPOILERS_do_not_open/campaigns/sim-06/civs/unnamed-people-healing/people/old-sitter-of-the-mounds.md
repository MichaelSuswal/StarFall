# The old sitter of the mounds

Nature and history, fixed. An old woman of a steading nearest the mounds, past sixty five, grey and short-lived as sitters are, blunt and dry. She was among the two sitters who sat in Hedda's evenings the summer of Year 1. In Year 2, month 2, at the mounds, she asked the stranger in front of the others whether the moss was dying or only tired, and was answered in front of her. In Year 3, month 2, she and the old women of the three nearest steadings found the moss did not bite the tongue at all and was no different from common moss; she told no one outside their number for some weeks, and in month 3 told Hedda at her door. At midsummer, at the mounds, she asked the stranger straight whether it was it that had done this or whether the moss was going already; it was quiet a long while and no answer was given that was written. She does not blame it aloud and has not said what she thinks. Her name was not said aloud this year.

Year 4: month 2 at the mounds she asked nothing; the stranger told her to her face that it did not know whether it did this or it was going already, and she said that was an answer, the first that did not lean. She walked back with the others. In month 5, with the stranger away, she was one of two sitters Hedda sent to a labor below the court, and sat the night. Her name was not said aloud this year.
