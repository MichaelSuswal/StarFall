# The Judge-Mother

Nature and history, fixed. An old woman of about seventy at the end of Year 2, who judges disputes, with the elder of each steading in the first instance. Cautious, dry, slow to be moved. She has not wanted the stranger gone, only watched. She sat across the fire at the seat's hearth in month 5 of Year 2 and asked it what it would do if the court bade it go from the fen, and was answered in front of the hearth. In month 8 she ruled the burial quarrel over the old man laid on the rise: that a man lies where his sons lay him, that the bank plot is for those who choose it, and that the Keeper write each name at both places. She said the ruling was not to be laid to the stranger. She has found nothing to hold against it. She does not take its word as the one awaited. Her name was not said aloud this year.
