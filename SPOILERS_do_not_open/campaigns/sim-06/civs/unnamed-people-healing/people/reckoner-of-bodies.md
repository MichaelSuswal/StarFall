# The Reckoner of Bodies

Nature and history, fixed. A woman of fifty five at the end of Year 1, thin, bad hipped, dry, exact. She has held the office for twenty years and sits at the autumn count with the Keeper of the Mounds each year. She does not like to be asked for and answers a stranger who asks the right thing. Her cords hang by the year. Last winter's count was the worst she has hung. She asked the stranger straight in summer whether it was the one the seat is kept for, was answered truly, said she would count what she saw through the wet season, and at the end of month 9 told the stranger she takes its word. She has told only the Keeper of the Mounds that it claimed the seat, and she has asked nobody to keep it. She keeps her office to the people and not to the god. She is not significant for her name, which was not said aloud this year.

Year 2, month 1: the stranger came to her door before any other office and answered her request of the autumn before; she carried its word to the Hearth-Holder that week and told him to ask it straight himself. Month 5: she named the day and went with it to the seat's hall. Month 12: at the thaw she showed her winter cords, the closed-well steadings beside the kept-well ones, to the Warden and then to the Judge-Mother. She keeps her office to the people.

Year 3: month 1, first days: the stranger came to her door before any other office and told her its downstream readings; she said she had hung the cords herself and did not need to be told what they meant, and carried its asking for test wells to the Warden. Month 5: it asked her leave to walk the ground above the low steadings alone; she asked what it looked for, gave leave, and told it to be back before the reaping. She alone knew what it looked for and told no one. At the autumn count she said only that the chest rattle was fewer in the low steadings that closed their wells. At the thaw her winter count was lower again; she showed the cords to the Warden, the Judge-Mother and, at his asking, the Hearth-Holder. She keeps her office to the people. Her name was not said aloud this year.

Year 4: month 1, first days: the stranger came to her door before any other office and told her what it read on its walk; she carried its three askings, two to the Warden and one to the Keeper, and said she carried what she was asked to carry and not her own opinion of it. Month 2 she hung the winter cords in the hall again for the elders. Month 5 it told her, not asked her, that it would walk downstream about twelve days; she asked what it looked for, was answered, and said it should be back before the reaping. Month 6 it came to her door first on its return; what it told her she has told only as the Keeper rules. Month 8 she named the day for the third sitting at the hearth. At the thaw her winter count was lower again; she showed the cords to the Warden, the Judge-Mother and the Hearth-Holder, and a third of the steadings that kept wells closed them. She keeps her office to the people. Her name was not said aloud this year.

Year 5: month 1, first days: the stranger came to her door before any other office and told her four things and asked three; she told it the woman and the child lived, told it that it had said about twelve days to her last summer and that Hedda had been given no days, said she had never needed its leave to show the cords, and carried its words about the sitters to the elder of the steading below the court and to two other steadings that asked, and its word on salt to the Warden, bringing back his answer. She told the Keeper at the thaw that the rows laid since his peg had not been read. Month 5, first day: it told her the days of a five day walk west into the trees, and she sent runners to the steadings with them. Month 5, ninth day: it set out; on the fifth day it came to her door first on its return, and what it told her she has told only as the Keeper rules. Month 8 she named the day for the fourth sitting at the hearth. At the thaw her winter count was lower again; she showed the cords to the Warden, the Judge-Mother and the Hearth-Holder, and told him in her own words what she counted. She is teaching her runner, a girl of the court steading, the cords. She keeps her office to the people. Her name was not said aloud this year.
