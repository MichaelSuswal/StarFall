# The Reckoner of Bodies

Nature and history, fixed. A woman of fifty five at the end of Year 1, thin, bad hipped, dry, exact. She has held the office for twenty years and sits at the autumn count with the Keeper of the Mounds each year. She does not like to be asked for and answers a stranger who asks the right thing. Her cords hang by the year. Last winter's count was the worst she has hung. She asked the stranger straight in summer whether it was the one the seat is kept for, was answered truly, said she would count what she saw through the wet season, and at the end of month 9 told the stranger she takes its word. She has told only the Keeper of the Mounds that it claimed the seat, and she has asked nobody to keep it. She keeps her office to the people and not to the god. She is not significant for her name, which was not said aloud this year.

Year 2, month 1: the stranger came to her door before any other office and answered her request of the autumn before; she carried its word to the Hearth-Holder that week and told him to ask it straight himself. Month 5: she named the day and went with it to the seat's hall. Month 12: at the thaw she showed her winter cords, the closed-well steadings beside the kept-well ones, to the Warden and then to the Judge-Mother. She keeps her office to the people.
