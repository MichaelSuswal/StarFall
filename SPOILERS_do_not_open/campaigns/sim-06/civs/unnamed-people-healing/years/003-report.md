# Report to the Keeper: Year 3, unnamed people of god-healing

## How the god was met
Spring. Month 1, first days: the stranger went to the Reckoner of Bodies at her door before any other office and told her its downstream readings and its asking for test wells on the rise. She said she had hung the cords herself and did not need telling what they meant, carried the asking to the Warden of Waters that week, and did not take back her word of Year 1. The Judge-Mother judged the going to her first for manners again. Month 1: the Warden heard her carry it with his arms folded, said he dug where he judged, and agreed to dig, with the elders' leave; he sent the Reckoner's cords to the steadings that kept their wells. Month 2: the elders of the court steading gave leave, one against; the Keeper of the Mounds set the pegs so that no well stands within the dead's ground. Month 3: the Warden had three wells dug on the rise; the stranger stood at each before anyone drank, with the Warden, the Keeper and the elder who gave leave. Neither the Warden nor the Keeper took it for the one awaited.
Summer. Month 4: the old elder of the low steading whose people were asked to carry creek water heard it read his well before his steading, took its reading as it was told, and did not close the well. Month 5: the Keeper of the Mounds walked beside it at the mounds at midsummer; the sitters of the three nearest steadings and the old sitter heard it. Month 5: the Reckoner heard its asking for leave to walk, asked what it looked for, was answered, and gave leave. Month 6: the Reckoner heard it first on its return.
Autumn. Month 7: the Judge-Mother heard the weir steading's old women ask that it be kept from the mounds, and ruled against them; the Keeper wrote the ruling. End of month 7: three old women asked the Hearth-Holder to fill the seat; he gave no answer. Month 8: the Hearth-Holder sent a runner to the Reckoner and sat it at the seat's hearth a second time, with the Judge-Mother across the fire and the Spearward by the door. The Hearth-Holder asked it nothing and said it was welcome as long as it would sit there. He did not take its word that it was the one and did not refuse it. The Judge-Mother asked nothing. The Spearward asked once and was answered in front of the hearth. Neither took its word.
Winter. The sitters sent for it fewer times. At the thaw the Hearth-Holder asked to see the Reckoner's cords and said in the open that the seat is kept and not claimed. The Reckoner is the only office that has taken its word. The Warden acts on its Readings only.

## What was done in the god's name
The Reckoner of Bodies carried the god's asking for test wells to the Warden of Waters in month 1, and gave it leave to walk the ground above the low steadings in month 5, as her own leave. The Warden's three test wells, his runner with the cords, the Keeper's pegs, the Judge-Mother's ruling on the moss and the Spearward's two men at the grain house are theirs, in their own offices. Nothing else.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper
1. Mendings, about seven cases across the year, one at a time with rests: a woman of a low steading with a fever past the ninth day (month 2); a man of a steading below the court with a chest that would not clear (month 4); a small child with the chest rattle and an old woman with a fever that would not break (month 8); an old man (month 9); a child of a low steading with the wet chest (month 11); an old woman (month 12). Who tries: the stranger, at Hedda's or an elder's or a sitter's asking, after the sitters have done what they can, by sitting a night with a hand on the sufferer. Success: the fever breaks by morning, the stranger takes the chill and the shake. Failure: the sufferer dies or the stranger is hurt worse than seen. Bearing on it: the sitters sit in the same way and cannot mend past an ordinary fever; the mendings of Years 1 and 2 held, each with days of shaking after; no child of the line fell ill; it walked unseen for about fourteen days in months 5 and 6 and mended none then. Written both ways: if mendings fail in whole or part, deaths rise by up to about 7, the Reckoner's winter count is higher by as many, and the sitters send for it less.
2. The three test wells on the rise, first reading in month 3 before any drank and again at the end of month 6 (written both ways in the year file: sound, and the Warden lets the nearest households draw and asks for more; foul, and he covers that well and the talk of the rise as sound ground goes quiet). What it reads is yours.
3. The old elder's well, read in front of his steading in month 4 (written both ways: foul, and some of his households carry creek water while he keeps the well; sound, and the Warden comes and says it may stand). What it reads is yours.
4. The summer walk, month 5 to month 6, alone. What it read and where it went is yours; see What touches anyone else. Written both ways in the year file: the foulness lies under the bank graves and the low ground alone, so the Warden's rule stands and the Keeper begins to talk of where else the dead might lie; or it comes down from upstream or from ground beyond my people's, so the closing of wells cannot clear it by itself and talk rises of sending upstream, with no one sent in my year.
5. The Reading of the moss at midsummer and after the first frost. The release says the old women found it common from month 2; what the Reading found beyond that is yours. Written both ways: found something the old women could not, and told them plainly, and they did not drop their saying; or found only common moss, which they knew.
Nothing else in my year is a fight, a search or a persuasion of someone outside my people. The elders' leave, the old elder's refusal, the weir women's asking, the Judge-Mother's ruling and the Hearth-Holder's answers are my people's and are written as they went.

## What turns on a power
The mendings and the shake after each (item 1). That the shake left its hands at the thaw. The Readings of the test wells, the old elder's well, the moss and the ground it walked (items 2 to 5). That it called the rot out of nothing this year: no carcass or spoiled store lay by a wellhead, and none of Hasten the Rot was seen by my people. What it did on its walk, unseen, is not known to my people. The six dim nights of the star (month 1, two in month 5, two in month 7, month 8): not known to be anyone's doing. I wrote that the old women tie them to the half-filled seat and to the stranger, that one of the month 5 pair fell while it was away from the court steading on its walk, and that Hedda found no tie to the sendings. The moss's failure is given in your release and I wrote nothing of its cause.

## What touches anyone else
Salt boats came up the river about five times; flax, linen and smoked eel went down. I took "no stranger came" to leave the ordinary boatmen, and no walker out of the forest came. No party left my people's land but the stranger alone: it set out in the middle of month 5 at dawn from the court steading, on foot, by the river bank upstream, then by the creeks that feed the river, then toward the trees on the west, and my people did not see it for about fourteen days, returning at dusk early in month 6 by the river path, before the reaping. I give no distance or time for the way; if you price it longer or shorter, the end of month 5 and the start of month 6 move with it and the reaping runs as written. If its walk met ground or people not in my record, that is yours. Nothing was carried to another people. Talk of going back to the two emptied steadings rose and no one went.

## Asked of the god
The Reckoner, month 1, at her door: that it say what it wished carried to the Warden. Answered in front of her.
The Warden of Waters, month 1, through the Reckoner: how often it would read each well. Answered in front of him: before any drank, and at the end of a season.
Hedda, for each hard case, seven times: whether it would sit with the sufferer. Answered by its going.
The old elder, month 4, at his wellhead: what the well read now. Answered in front of his steading.
The old women at the mounds, month 5, asked of them by it and not asked of it: nothing.
The old sitter at the mounds, month 5, in her words: "Was it you that did this, or was it going already?" Not answered in front of her; it was quiet a long while and no answer was given.
The sitters of the three nearest steadings, through Hedda, in autumn: what they should brew for the wasting now the moss does nothing. Not answered by the year's end.
The Reckoner, month 5: "What do you look for?" Answered in front of her: where the foulness begins and where it stops, whether under the bank graves, under the whole low ground, or from upstream.
The Hearth-Holder, month 8, by runner through the Reckoner: that it sit at the seat's hearth on the night she named. Answered by its coming.
The Spearward, month 8, at the hearth: "Would you rot a granary, ours or another's, if the seat bade you, in war?" Answered in front of the hearth: that it would not turn it on any store of the people, in war or out of it.
The sitters, winter: that it sit with the ones they failed. Answered by its going.
Three old women of the nearest steadings asked the Hearth-Holder, not the god, that the seat be filled. Not asked of it.

## Names said aloud
The old elder of a low steading, month 4, at his wellhead before his steading and the stranger: "Tolm", his father, who dug the well. No other name was said aloud. The god's name was not said aloud this year.
