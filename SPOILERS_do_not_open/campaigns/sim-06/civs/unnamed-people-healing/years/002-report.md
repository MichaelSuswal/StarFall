# Report to the Keeper: Year 2, unnamed people of god-healing

## How the god was met
Spring. Month 1, first days: the stranger went to the Reckoner of Bodies at her door before any other office. She heard its answer to her request of the autumn and its word to carry, carried it to the Hearth-Holder that week, and did not take back her word of the year before. She told the Hearth-Holder only to ask it straight himself. The Hearth-Holder said the day was hers to name. The Judge-Mother judged the going to the Reckoner first for manners and not slight.
Month 2: the Keeper of the Mounds met it and the sitters at the mounds and walked beside it, saying little. The sitters of the three nearest steadings said they were answered about the moss. He did not take it for more than a reader of ground.
Month 3: the Warden of Waters heard it at the Reckoner's house and again at the bank, and set a rule in his own office that no well is dug below the bank graves. The Keeper of the Mounds agreed, as a trial, to lay the court steading's next dead on the rise behind the court's yard for those whose kin would have it, and asked it to read the rise. Neither took it for the one awaited.
Summer. Month 5: the Hearth-Holder sat it at the seat's hearth, with the Reckoner, the Judge-Mother and the Spearward there. He asked it straight whether it was the one the seat is kept for and was answered truly. He did not take its word that it was the one, and did not refuse it. The Judge-Mother watched and asked what it would do if the court bade it go. The Spearward, met for the first time, asked whether it could rot a granary. All were answered in front of the hearth. Neither the Judge-Mother nor the Spearward took its word.
Autumn. Months 7 and 8: the elders of the low steadings heard it at their wellheads. About half of those that had kept their wells the year before closed or drew them off. One old elder refused, in the open, and kept his well. A sitter of a nearer steading went with it and spoke for it. Winter: the sitters sent for it fewer times; the Hearth-Holder said in the open that it was welcome at his hearth. No office but the Reckoner has taken its word as one that takes the god's word. The Warden and the Keeper act on its Readings only.

## What was done in the god's name
The Reckoner of Bodies carried the god's word to the Hearth-Holder in month 1, at its asking. The Reckoner sent for the Warden of Waters and told him to bring the Keeper of the Mounds to the bank in month 3 at the god's request put in her hearing; he did, as her asking and not as the god's. The Warden's rule on wells and the Keeper's rise are theirs, in their own offices. The Judge-Mother's ruling of month 8 is hers. Nothing else was done in its name.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper
1. Mendings, nine cases across the year, one at a time with rests: a labor gone wrong in a steading below the court (month 3); a child with the early chest rattle (month 4); a small child with the chest rattle and an old woman with a fever that would not break (month 8); an old man (month 9); one in month 10, one in month 11, two in month 12. Who tries: the stranger, at Hedda's or an elder's or a sitter's asking, after the sitters have done what they can, by sitting a night with a hand on the sufferer. Success: the fever breaks by morning, the stranger takes the chill and the shake. Failure: the sufferer dies or the stranger is hurt worse than seen. Bearing on it: the sitters sit in the same way and cannot mend past an ordinary fever; last year's mendings held, each with days of shaking after. No child of the line fell ill. Written both ways: if mendings fail in whole or part, deaths rise by up to about 9, the Reckoner's winter count is higher by as many, and the sitters send for it less.
2. The downstream walk is the god's, with a sitter, by the river bank; priced by the Keeper (see What touches anyone else). Nothing contested turns on it.
Nothing else in my year is a fight, a search or a persuasion of someone outside my people. The elders' answers to its asking, the burial quarrel and the Hearth-Holder's answer at the hearth are my people's and are written as they went.

## What turns on a power
The Reading of the moss of the mounds in month 2 (written both ways in the year file: found something, or found nothing it could read). The Reading of the rise in month 3 (sound, and the first burials follow; or foul, and no rise this year and no quarrel). The Readings of the wells and soil of the two empty steadings and the low steadings below the bank burials in months 6 to 8 (foul where it reads foul and the elders are asked again; sound where it reads sound). The winter cords I wrote that the closed-well steadings sickened fewer and the kept-well steadings as many as before, from your release. The rotting of a drowned ox lodged in a creek weir above a steading's wellhead in month 8, once; the people saw it go to black slurry in an afternoon; whether it did, and what it cost it, is yours, and it mended no one that day or the next night in my year. The mendings and the shake after each (item 1). That the shake left its hands in the first days of the thaw. The five dim nights (months 2, 6, 9, 11 and 12): not known to be anyone's doing; I wrote that the people tie some to it and that two fell where no one had been mended within the moon.

## What touches anyone else
Salt boats came up the river about five times; flax, linen and smoked eel went down. I took "no stranger came" to leave the ordinary boatmen, and no walker out of the forest came. No party left my people's land. The stranger and a sitter of a nearer steading set out at the end of month 6 downstream by the river bank, for the two emptied steadings and the steadings below the bank burials, all within my people's own country, and in my year came back before the wet season's illnesses began in month 8. I gave no distance or time; if you price the way longer, the autumn tending below moves later. Nothing was carried to another people. Talk of going back to the two emptied steadings rose and no one went.

## Asked of the god
The Reckoner, autumn of Year 1, answered now: that it take no step toward the court without coming to her first. Answered at her door, month 1, in front of her.
The Hearth-Holder, by runner, last month of Year 1: that it sit at the seat's hearth when it was well. Answered by the Reckoner's carrying, month 1, and in person, month 5.
The sitters of the three nearest steadings, through Hedda: "look at the moss". Answered by its going, month 2.
An old sitter at the mound, in her words: "Is it dying, or is it only tired?" Answered in front of her.
The Keeper of the Mounds, at the bank, month 3: "Stand on the rise and say what the ground is." Answered by its going.
Hedda, for each hard case: whether it would sit with the sufferer. Answered by its going, nine times.
The Hearth-Holder, month 5, at the hearth: "Are you the one the seat is kept for?" and "What are you called?" Both answered in front of the hearth.
The Judge-Mother, month 5: "What will you do if the court bids you go from the fen?" Answered in front of the hearth.
The Spearward, month 5: "Can you do to a granary what you did to the heap?" Answered in front of the hearth.
An old elder of a low steading, month 7 or 8: "Why should my people carry water like children, when my father dug this well?" Answered in front of his steading.
The sitters, winter: that it sit with the ones they failed. Answered by its going.

## Names said aloud
The god's name, told by it to the Hearth-Holder at the seat's hearth in month 5, when he asked it; the Reckoner, the Judge-Mother and the Spearward heard it said. That name is not written in the files here. No other name was said aloud.
