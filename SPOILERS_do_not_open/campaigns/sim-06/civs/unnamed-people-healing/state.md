# Unnamed people of god-healing: state at the end of Year 1

Set by the civ agent. The numbers assume the god's mendings did what the people saw; if the Keeper rules otherwise, deaths rise by about 25 to 30 and mood falls.

population: about 5,025 (births about 205, deaths about 180, in scattered steadings along the lower River Morrow [f02], one larger seat [f55]). Why it moved: an ordinary sickness winter and no wasting fever, so no steading was emptied, and about thirty fewer deaths than the sitters could have borne, by the Reckoner's count, in the wet autumn and the late winter.
stores: barley, beans, dried fish and smoked eel, adequate to the thaw. One steading short of seed barley. Why: ordinary harvest, ordinary eel and fish runs, a part of one grain store set apart on the stranger's word (see the year file for the branch).
treasure: little: cloth, pots, a few bronze things laid by for burials. Unchanged but for the usual grave goods given up to burials.
trade: flax, linen and smoked eel out; salt in, by river. About five salt boats this year, as in most years. No new trade. Why: no stranger came before the god, and nothing changed the river traffic.
strength: a levy of about 400, little trained; no walls. Unchanged.
mood: wary, a little hopeful; the court steading and the three nearest steadings are warmer toward the stranger than the far ones, which have only heard of it. The Judge-Mother is cautious. The Hearth-Holder's mood is not given. The Reckoner of Bodies takes the god's word and tells nobody. Why: a bad winter was followed by a survivable one, and the stranger sat with the dying and was not turned from the door.
