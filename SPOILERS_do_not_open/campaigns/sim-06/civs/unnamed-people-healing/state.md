# Unnamed people of god-healing: state at the end of Year 3

Set by the civ agent. The numbers take the branch in which the mendings held (about seven cases) and in which the Readings found what the Reckoner's winter cords show. If the Keeper rules that the mendings failed in whole or part, deaths rise by up to about 7 and mood falls among the sitters and the near steadings.

population: about 5,115 (births about 210, deaths about 155, in scattered steadings along the lower River Morrow [f02], one larger seat [f55]). Why it moved: no fever winter fell; in the low steadings whose wells were drawn off or closed fewer sickened again, in those that kept their wells as many as before, and about half of the steadings that still kept wells at the thaw closed or drew them off that spring; the god was sent for about seven times against about nine last year and about thirty before.
stores: barley, beans, dried fish and smoked eel, adequate to the thaw. No steading short of seed. Why: an ordinary thaw, ordinary harvest, ordinary eel and fish runs. The fields of the two emptied steadings still lie unplanted, and talk of going back rose again and came to nothing.
treasure: little: cloth, pots, a few bronze things laid by for burials. Unchanged but for the usual grave goods, some laid on the rise.
trade: flax, linen and smoked eel out; salt in, by river. About five salt boats, as in most years. No new trade. Why: nothing changed the river traffic; no stranger came.
strength: a levy of about 400, little trained; no walls. Unchanged, but the Spearward has set two men on the court steading's grain house by night, on his own word. Why: the stranger told him at the hearth it would not turn the rot on any store of the people, and he holds that a promise is not a lock.
mood: the Reckoner stands as she stood. The Warden of Waters has dug three test wells on the rise on its asking and reads its Readings as a reader of water, no more. The Hearth-Holder keeps the seat, has sat the stranger at the hearth a second time and asked it nothing, and has neither taken its word that it is the one nor refused it; he says the seat is not kept for a moss. The Judge-Mother watches and has found nothing to hold against it; she ruled that no one is barred from the mounds for looking. The Keeper of the Mounds pegged the rows clear of the wells and says nothing. The Spearward asked once and guards the granary himself. The sitters and the old women of the three nearest steadings are shaken and divided over the failed moss: some say the stranger is the Tender come too late, some that it cannot be, some that it looked the gift away; the weir steading's old women are cold to it and some keep from the mounds. The elders who kept their wells are sore, and the old elder keeps his. The bank plot's kin are sore at wells being dug for the court on the rise while theirs are called foul. The far steadings have heard of it and little more. Why: the moss failed; fewer sick in the closed steadings for a second winter; the walk upstream was unseen and the court talked; the wells on the rise.

## Keeper, Year 1, pass two (kept)
Each mending did what the card gives, the Hearth-Holder's granddaughter among them: the numbers take the year file's branch in which the mendings held. The court steading's well and store read sound; the branch for that stands.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- All nine mendings held; the numbers take that branch.
- The moss read as sound living moss. The rise read sound; the first burials stand there. The kept wells below the bank read foul as before; the closed wells fainter, still foul; the emptied steadings' wells foul.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- All seven mendings held, each with its days of shaking after. Your numbers take that branch.
- The three test wells on the rise read sound before any drank and sound at the season's end.
- The old elder's well read foul, as before.
- The summer walk: the river and every creek and spring above the bank burials read clean; the ground under the bank burials and the wells below them read foul; nothing foul comes down from upstream. Your year takes the branch where the foulness lies under the bank graves and the low ground alone.
- The moss read living and sound, with nothing in it that common moss lacks. It found only what the old women knew.
- The old sitter's question had no answer.
