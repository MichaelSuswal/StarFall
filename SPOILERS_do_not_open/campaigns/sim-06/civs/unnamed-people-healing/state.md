# Unnamed people of god-healing: state at the end of Year 2

Set by the civ agent. The numbers take the branch in which the mendings held (about nine cases) and in which the Readings found what the Reckoner's winter cords show. If the Keeper rules that the mendings failed in whole or part, deaths rise by up to about 9 and mood falls among the sitters and the near steadings.

population: about 5,060 (births about 205, deaths about 170, in scattered steadings along the lower River Morrow [f02], one larger seat [f55]). Why it moved: no fever winter fell; in the low steadings whose wells were drawn off or closed fewer sickened this winter than last, in those that kept their wells as many as before; the god was sent for about nine times against about thirty last year.
stores: barley, beans, dried fish and smoked eel, adequate to the thaw. No steading short of seed. Why: an ordinary thaw, ordinary harvest, ordinary eel and fish runs. The fields of the two emptied steadings still lie unplanted.
treasure: little: cloth, pots, a few bronze things laid by for burials. Unchanged but for the usual grave goods, some now laid on the rise.
trade: flax, linen and smoked eel out; salt in, by river. About five salt boats, as in most years. No new trade. Why: nothing changed the river traffic; no stranger came.
strength: a levy of about 400, little trained; no walls. Unchanged. The Spearward has met the god and asked it one question; he has changed nothing in the levy.
mood: the court steading and the nearest three steadings are warmer toward the stranger and some are wary of it; the Reckoner stands as she stood. The Hearth-Holder keeps the seat, has sat the stranger at the hearth, and has neither taken its word that it is the one nor refused it. The Judge-Mother watches and has found nothing to hold against it. The Warden of Waters and the Keeper of the Mounds act on its Readings and do not call it more than a reader of ground and water. The elders who kept their wells are sore at being asked twice, and one is loud. A part of the bank plot's kin are sore at the rise. The far steadings have heard of it and little more. Why: fewer sick in the closed steadings, a sound hearing at the hall, a quarrel over burials settled in court, and a second thing done to a carcass that frightened one steading again.

## Keeper, Year 1, pass two (kept)
Each mending did what the card gives, the Hearth-Holder's granddaughter among them: the numbers take the year file's branch in which the mendings held. The court steading's well and store read sound; the branch for that stands.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- All nine mendings held; the numbers take that branch.
- The moss read as sound living moss. The rise read sound; the first burials stand there. The kept wells below the bank read foul as before; the closed wells fainter, still foul; the emptied steadings' wells foul.
