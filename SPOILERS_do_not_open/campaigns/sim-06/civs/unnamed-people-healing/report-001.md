# Report to the Keeper: Year 1, unnamed people of god-healing

## How the god was met
Spring, month 1. The Keeper of the Mounds saw the stranger on the mounds from the field edge and said nothing to anyone. Hedda, midwife of the court steading, took the stranger in at the door when it asked for work, and gave her name aloud. She took its word that it asked only food and a place by a hearth. The Warden of Waters heard it at the wellhead, took its word about the well and the store, and acted on it (see the branch in the year file if the Reading showed nothing). He did not take it for anything more than a stranger who knows wells.
Spring to early summer. Hedda asked the stranger for eight hard cases. The sitters of the nearer steadings began to come to Hedda's house in the evenings.
Summer. The Reckoner of Bodies received it, told it her counts, asked it straight whether it was the one the seat is kept for, was answered truly, asked its name and was told it, asked what it wanted of the court and was answered, said she would count through the wet season. She did not take its word that summer.
Autumn. The Reckoner took its word at the end of month 9 after the count. The elders and sitters of the steadings it walked saw it as a stranger who tends the sick and knows wells. The Warden of Waters carried the heap story to the court.
Winter. The Hearth-Holder sent to Hedda when his granddaughter took the wet chest. He has not met the stranger. In the last month he sent a runner with an invitation to sit at the seat's hearth. The Judge-Mother wants it watched. No office but the Reckoner has taken the god's word. The Hearth-Holder, the Judge-Mother, the Warden of Waters, the Keeper of the Mounds and the Spearward have not taken it for the Tender. The Spearward was not met.

## What was done in the god's name
Nothing. The god asked nothing of any office. The Reckoner kept a cord of her own for the cases the stranger sat with, as part of her count, not in its name.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper
1. Mending, about thirty cases across the year, one at a time with rests: eight in spring and early summer (a labor gone wrong, a fever past the ninth day, a wound gone red and hot, an early chest rattle, and four more), four in summer, about twelve in autumn, about six in winter, and the Hearth-Holder's granddaughter in month 11. Who tries: the stranger, at Hedda's or an elder's or a sitter's asking, by sitting a night with a hand on the sufferer. Success: the fever breaks by morning, the stranger takes the chill and the shake. Failure: the sufferer dies or the stranger is hurt worse than seen. Circumstances bearing on it: the people's sitters sit in the same way and cannot mend past an ordinary fever. Written both ways: if mendings fail in whole or part, deaths rise by about 25 to 30 and the Reckoner's count is flat.
2. The Hearth-Holder's granddaughter, age four, wet chest, three nights of the court's sitters failed. The stranger is asked for. Success: she lives and the stranger is carried to bed with the shake; the Hearth-Holder sends an invitation. Failure: she dies in the morning, the court goes cold, the Judge-Mother asks that the stranger be asked to leave, and the Reckoner stands alone for it.
3. The Reading of the well, the grain store and the water at the mounds. What the stranger said it found at the wellhead and the store is not mine to write; I wrote that a part of the store was set apart and the well drained, and noted the branch if it found nothing foul.
4. The late summer walk. The stranger set out for some days along the river bank upstream and downstream and toward the western treeline. I gave no distance and no time and wrote only that it went and came back. The Keeper prices it and says what it found.
5. The Spearward was never met; nothing turns on a fight.

## What turns on a power
The mendings and the shake after each (item 1 and 2). The Reading at the mounds, the wellhead, the store and the water (item 3). The rotting of the heap in month 8 (the people saw it go to black slurry in an afternoon; whether it did, and what it cost the stranger). That those mended know the stranger's nearness with eyes shut (mended people said so to Hedda). The three dim nights of the star are not the stranger's doing as far as I know; I wrote that the people tie them to it.

## What touches anyone else
Salt boats came up the river about five times; flax, linen and smoked eel went down. Boatmen at the court steading's landing in summer saw the stranger tending a child and went on, carrying no word that I know. No party left the people's land. No traveler from outside the fen came but the boatmen. Nothing was carried to another people.

## Asked of the god
Hedda at the door, month 1: what it wanted. Answered in front of her: food, a place by a hearth, its hands.
Hedda, and later the elders of the steadings, for each hard case: whether it would sit with the sufferer. Answered by its going.
The sitters, in the evenings: what would it have them do. It asked them what they knew and asked them to teach Hedda's apprentice.
The Reckoner, summer: whether it was the one the seat is kept for. Answered truly in front of her. What it wanted of the court. It asked what the court would have it do.
The Reckoner, summer: its name. Answered.
The Reckoner, month 9: that it take no step toward the court without coming to her first. Not answered in words that I wrote.
The sitters of the nearest steadings, through Hedda: that it look at the mound moss. Not answered by the year's end.
The Hearth-Holder, last month: that it sit at the seat's hearth when well. Not answered by the year's end; carried on to it by a boy.

## Names said aloud
Hedda, to the stranger, at her door in month 1, because it gave its need first. The stranger's name, to the Reckoner of Bodies, in summer, when she asked it. That name is not written in the files here, since the god's decisions file is the only place it stands and I wrote no name for it. No other name was said aloud.
