# Report 002: unnamed people of god-dreams, Year 2, pass one, for the Keeper

## How the god was met
Autumn, month 1, day 6. At dusk the seat spoke the guest's answer from the wall, with the Warden of the Door giving the words: she does not know why the star dims, does not know that she makes it dim, will take neither the credit nor the blame, and asks the Keeper of the Count to tally the dim nights with the dates and whether any dying fell on them. Seat: spoke it and added the Count's open door on the first of each month. Keeper of the Count: took the task that night, began the cord. Warden of the Door: gave the words. Headmen and the Night Sitter: honest. Some kept households: let down. Lowest streets: "she did not say no". Gate Bench: no word.

Day 7. At the scar the guest told the cutters to their faces that she does not know who made the lowest cuts and does not know that she is a giant's kin, and asked to be shown the lowest cuts where she could reach them on four legs. The old cutter said "Then we are no wiser than we were." Three cuts were shown; she looked a long time, touched none, claimed none. The cutters differ on what they saw.

Day 10. In the yard of the watch the Warden of the Wall heard her ask what he distrusts and what he would have her do. He distrusts the dipping flames, a watch split between two lamps, and asking that is done without an order. He would have her bring no flame within the watch's yards, light none for a man on duty, ask the wall nothing but through him, and tell him the days she walks beyond the gate. He said he would answer after a night what honest thing she could do; next morning: let the wall hold her door, and let the men who sit it be told by her what they sit against. The seat gave leave for the first. He has not taken her word and has not refused it.

Month 2. The seat heard the salt asked and the offices one at a time. Steward of the Store: for it, chose the leader and the goods, took her word as before, grudgingly. Warden of the Wall: asked two of his men as rowers; refused by the seat; said six and not four. Seat: sent six. Warden of the Door: named the runners.

Month 3. The old Warden of the Door named his successor, a woman of the lower town who keeps both lamps. From the first frost she carries the guest's asks. She has said she carries faithfully and will hold her own counsel on what the guest is until she has heard her herself. At the year's end she has not taken her for the awaited.

Winter. Keeper of the Count: held the cap of two and the three named nights. Steward of the Store: counted aloud again in month 4. Night Sitter: sat with the guest in month 5, declined the offer, then accepted it through her pupil in month 6. The wall held her door from the first night of winter.

Spring and summer. The Warden of the Wall gave a half score as escort to the hamlets. Headmen heard her, some kept the flame. The cutters ringed the three cuts with stones unasked.

Where each office stands at the year's end: old Warden of the Door, taken her word; the new Warden of the Door, carries it, has not said she takes her for the awaited; the seat, taken it; Keeper of the Count, taken it; Steward of the Store, taken it grudgingly; Warden of the Wall, has not; Gate Bench, split, agreed on the custom of the table.

## What was done in the god's name
By the Warden of the Door (the old man to month 3, the new one after): gave the guest's words from the wall, taught the salt party's leader the words and looked through the baggage at the quay for lamps and embers, named the runners, took her to the Night Sitter, carried the Night Sitter's pupil's asking, named the three hardest nights to the Keeper of the Count the day before.
By the seat: spoke the answer from the wall, sent the salt party, gave leave for the wall to hold her door, named the new Warden of the Door.
By the Keeper of the Count: kept the cord of the dim nights, held the cap of two, drew the lots (three on three named nights), kept the roll (about 415), wrote down the household that lit its own niche at night.
By the Steward of the Store: chose the leader and the goods, counted the winter aloud in month 4, gave stone for the river-side footing.
By the Warden of the Wall (as the wall's own work, not as her helper): kept the beacon watch, sat her door from the first night of winter, gave the escort.
By the court's runners: carried to the hamlets that no household is to set a night's lamp in the guest's name beyond the lot.
Nothing was done in the god's name by the Gate Bench.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper
1. The salt party. Six of the court: the leader (a man of about forty, the Steward's chief cord-keeper of the trade goods), four runners named by the Warden of the Door (three of last year's river party who have met the coasting ship's crew, one a young woman new to it), and one boatman of the fishing households who knows the low-water bars. Goods: twenty bolts of linen, a hundred pots in straw and reed baskets, fifteen jars of flax oil, as gifts and trade. No bronze. No lamp, wick or ember from any niche; the first fire struck from flint. They set out in the third week of month 2 in one of the court's planked river boats. The leader means to go by the river to the mouth (112.4 miles, 4 days down, 10 up). At the mouth he means to ask the coasting ship's crew, if a ship is there, for passage to the harbor town west along the coast where the salt is boiled, for linen, and go on by the ship; if no ship is there he means to leave the boat, the pots and two hands at the mouth and go round the shore on foot with the linen, the oil and four carrying (97.9 miles, 7 days on foot, longer laden). He says he will not put the river boat to sea. Coming home the same in reverse, the boat up the river with the salt.
   The words, exactly as given: "I am called Vael. I came down to a town in a river bend, four days up from the river's mouth, in the autumn of last year, and I stayed as a guest, and I light lamps in the houses that ask. Your salt carriers took word of me to you and you asked what I am. I am a guest who stayed, half a woman and half a hind, and I keep a lamp. I ask nothing of you but a fair trade in salt at the price you take at the boil. The people who bring this are guests at your table, and they keep a guest's custom: they ask for bread and three nights and do not ask your business until the third. If you will tell them what you want of us, they will carry it home." The leader speaks it from memory on the third night to whoever sets the price of salt. Orders: if no one will hear it, buy salt as any trader would and come home; if hostile, do not fight, do not argue, do not give up the goods already promised, come home with what can be had or winter where it is safe; if asked questions, answer only what was seen and told: the guest woke on the stony ground above the town in the dark before an autumn morning, does not know why, the seat takes her for the awaited, she keeps a lamp she lights in a house that asks; do not say what the lamp does or that it can be taken; if pressed, say he does not know.
   Success: the party is received, bread and three nights, the words given and heard, salt bought at the harbor's price, home with salt, with whatever the harbor answers. Failure: refused, robbed, lost on the coast, sick, drowned, or the harbor answers badly. Partial: salt bought and the harbor's answer carried home late.
   Circumstances from my record that bear on it: last year's river party reached the mouth, was fed by the crew of a coasting ship beached for the night, traded linen and pots for salt and came home in month 12; the sailors said the salt is boiled at a harbor town west along the coast and fetches far less there than the carriers ask at the gate; the harbor towns have been told of the guest by the carriers and asked through them what she is; the carriers have an interest in the price and the party undercuts them (my inference, not a thing anyone said); the party has no spear and no flame; the river is low in autumn and the cold begins in month 4; upstream with a load takes 10 days by the table and may be worse in rain or low water; the leader is not at ease with a boat or the sea. The party's way home depends on the ship or the foot road and the winter closes coastal sailing.
   The year both ways: A, home before the hard cold, in the last days of month 3, with salt; the late pigs fully cured, the Steward's winter count easier, the carriers' spring visit met by a town that has seen the harbor's price. B, not back until spring, no word all winter, the late pigs cured lightly and the meat gone early as last year or a little worse, the party up the river in the high water in month 7 or 8 as the Keeper prices it, with the harbor's answer. If the party is lost or never returns, the court has no word of why and the town grieves six and does not send another before the next autumn.
2. The Night Sitter's household kept, month 6, night 13. See "What turns on a power" below for the power part. As an attempt of mortals: the Night Sitter had not slept since the ninth, her pupil asked, she did not refuse. Success: she slept and her hands steadied. Failure: she did not sleep. A and B in the year file are for this, not the salt.
3. No fight, search, persuasion of anyone outside the people, or craft beyond what the people know was attempted by anyone else this year.

## What turns on a power
1. The three-household nights. Month 5, night 14; month 6, night 3; month 6, night 25. The Warden of the Door named them to the Keeper of the Count the day before, the seat's crier told the town, the court asked no plan of her the next day, and the wall set four men at her door. What the wall's men saw at those dawns, and whether anything the card says (first impulse, no plan across an hour, no shut or crowded room) was seen, is the Keeper's. I write nothing of it.
2. The Night Sitter's household kept on month 6, night 13. Whether Keeping reaches a woman who is not under a fear, and what arrives in the guest at dawn. At dawn she was shaken and the flames near her dipped, as on other mornings. Whether the Night Sitter slept is the Keeper's.
3. The dim nights: month 2 night 9 (she awake in the yard, no death in the town), month 6 night 20 (asleep, lot night, an old man died in a kept house), month 9 night 3 (awake in a western hamlet a morning's walk from the wall, with the hamlet and the town both seeing it, no death in the town), month 11 night 15 (asleep, lot night, a young woman in childbed died in a house not kept, the child lived), month 12 night 27 (awake in the yard, no death in the town). Whether any is hers is not known to anyone and not asked of me.
4. A flame lit at night by a household from its own kept flame, month 4, on a night of two drawn. Whether that counts as a third household kept, and whether the cap of two was kept, is the Keeper's.
5. The flames near her dipping at dawn on every Keeping morning: the wall's men saw it every morning of the winter and say it is at the same hour.
6. The dying in kept houses. About a dozen winter deaths were in kept houses; the households say again that the dying were quiet. Nothing was cured.
7. What the lowest cuts at the scar are, and whether the guest is a giant's kin: a thing of her nature, not mine. The cutters ringed three cuts with stones.

## What touches anyone else
- The salt party (above) is the only party sent out. It carries the guest's message to the harbor town west along the coast where the salt is boiled, and goods. Nothing else was sent out this year.
- The western party came home in month 1, found no one and no smoke in the hills. The court has their word.
- The salt carriers came twice (late autumn and spring) at last year's price, ate the three nights, watched, and said little. Iron came down from the mining town as in other years.
- No stranger came to the gate but the carriers and the ordinary traders.
- Nothing was given or sold that carries a flame, a lamp or a token of the guest. Nothing of the guest was carried out of the town by anyone except the words the leader has by heart.

## Asked of the god
In the mortals' words as the people repeat them, and whether answered in front of them. Where her decisions fix an answer I say so; elsewhere I write none.
- Old cutter, at the scar, after hearing her: "Then we are no wiser than we were." Not a question. Answered as nothing; she asked to see the cuts.
- Headman of a western hamlet, at dawn after the dim in month 9: "Was it you?" Answered as her decisions fix: she does not know, does not know that she makes it dim, will take neither the credit nor the blame.
- The Warden of the Wall, in the yard of the watch: his three distrusts and what he would have her do or not do were said to her face, not asked. No answer from her that I can write. She listened to the end.
- The Warden of the Wall, by his captain next morning: "Let the wall hold your door, and let the men who sit it be told by you what they sit against." Not answered in front of them that I know. The seat gave leave for the first. For the second, no answer I can write.
- The Night Sitter, in her work yard, to the guest: "Nothing you could give back." (what the work takes) and "I will not ask for what I cannot see the bottom of." Answered as the decisions fix: she will not take her work from her; she offered to keep her household's sleep on the nights after if asked.
- The Night Sitter's pupil to the new Warden of the Door, month 6: "Will she keep our mistress's house?" Answered by the household being kept on the night after, one of the night's two.
- The Keeper of the Count to the new Warden of the Door, month 4: whether a household that lit its own niche at night was a third household kept. No answer I can write.
- Households: "May we have the flame?" Answered by her lighting it where asked.
- Elder of the Gate Bench who is against the guest, on the successor: a woman who keeps two lamps may keep two minds. Not put to her.
- No one asked her about the barrow songs, the giants' cuts beyond the cutters' own, or the harbor.

## Names said aloud
None said aloud in the town this year. If the salt party's leader gives the words at the harbor, he says "Vael" aloud on the third night to whoever sets the price of salt; that is the only name I know to be spoken, and it is the Keeper's whether he reaches that moment. No name was said aloud for the people, the home, or any office holder. The people still call her "the guest" and "the guest with the lamp"; some in the lowest streets call her "the keeper". These are not names.
