# Report 004: unnamed people of god-dreams, Year 4, pass one, for the Keeper

## How the god was met
Autumn, month 1, day 4. In the yard of the watch the guest answered the two questions of last year, as her decisions fix, to the captain and the four who sit her door, with the Warden of the Wall by. Captain: heard it; then said the wall has seen her lie still on lot nights for two winters and written it asleep, and asked what the wall has been sitting if a lying hind is not her in a dream. One of the four: heard that they are to move her by the cord at her waist if fire comes, leaving the lamp on the cord and on her. Warden of the Wall: heard her to the end; asked which house is nearest to a broken body; carried the part about the break to the seat himself; had the way to the south-east gate staked and kept clear; has not taken her word as the awaited and has not refused her. Gate Bench elder against her: a god that can be broken and mended is a guest that needs the town. The wall's men and the streets: every house keeps a lamp burning through the night, so the nearest house is any house, so she cannot be killed; the lowest streets near the yard said she will come back to their street, some said as a child of the house.

Month 1, day 6. The second house of the four. The new Warden of the Door found that the Steward's quay roll of parties out still showed the four as out, because the four came home by the west road and the quay keeper strikes a party when its leader comes to the quay; she thinks a runner read her the roll when the guest asked which parties were out, and cannot swear it. The guest gave the father her answer and the Warden of the Door's as the Warden of the Door's. Father: a plain thing plainly said; his son came home two autumns past and not last year; brought bread out to the lane and the guest took it; said the roll should be struck. Steward of the Store: had the roll struck and read over by the quay keeper and a runner together. Elder against her: a god who asks whose word a thing was is a god who can be told anything once.

Month 1, day 7. Keeper of the Count: received her list of afraid days of Year 3, wrote it onto a cord, and at the Count's door on month 2, day 1 laid it with last year's cord and the cord of the dim nights, said a count shows how many and not why. I do not have her list.

Month 2. The guest asked that the court judge the harbor's order. Steward of the Store and the old cutter: judged sixty blocks, the fifth of last summer's cutting dressed to the harbor's cord (four replaced from the stack beyond the footing), no more; the old cutter on the picks' wear. Warden of the Wall: asked a third time for two of his men as rowers; refused; will not ask a fourth. Keeper of the Count: a word sent west cannot be called back, the Count will knot what goes. Gate Bench: the elder against her said the stone is the town's footing and that she asks the stranger to our gate; the two for her said it is the custom. Seat: accepted on the ninth day, held the bargain as the town's, allowed the leader to go on by the coast with two runners and the guest's word. Warden of the Door: taught the words three evenings, asked the guest whether the words meant four days down or four days up from the mouth. Not answered that I can write.

Winter. Night Sitter: took the guest's hands for the kettle and lamp on month 4, day 16, and said the work sits easier with three. Hamlet girl: found she could; stood before the seat on month 4, day 22 with her household and asked for a bed in the lower town and no more than two sittings in ten; seat: gave both and the court's bread. Wall: held her door from the first night of winter, told every man what he sits against. Keeper of the Count: kept the cap of two and the three named nights, now writes lying and not asleep. Seat's crier: told the three nights. Steward of the Store: counted the winter aloud in month 4 by his own habit. Barrow households: asked again, after the singer's death, whether the guest would hear what they have of the third song.

Spring. Month 7, day 12, the court's four heard together on what the court would do if she were away (see below); the guest listened to the end and said she would write it as a rule and keep it, and that she does not leave. New Warden of the Door: said to the seat that evening, in front of the old man, that she takes the guest for the awaited. Warden of the Wall: said to the captain that a ruler who asks it is a ruler; has not said it to the guest. Headmen: the guest walked the near hamlets with a half score of the watch the Warden of the Wall gave; the days told to him beforehand.

Summer. Keeper of the Count: read the cord at the turn of the year. New Warden of the Door: kept the guest's asks all year.

Where each office stands at the year's end: old Warden of the Door, taken her word; the new Warden of the Door, taken it and said so to the seat in month 7; the seat, taken it; Keeper of the Count, taken it; Steward of the Store, taken it grudgingly; Warden of the Wall, has not and has not refused; Gate Bench, split, agreed on the custom of the table.

## What was done in the god's name
By the Warden of the Door: carried the guest to the yard of the watch, the second house, the Night Sitter's yard, the hamlets and the court's four; carried her ask on the harbor's order and the delta word to the seat; taught the party's leader the words and looked through the baggage at the quay for lamps and embers; named the runners; carried the hamlet girl's case to the seat; named the three nights to the Keeper of the Count the day before; carried the barrow households' ask to the guest.
By the seat: accepted the harbor's order and sent the party with the guest's word and leave to go on by the coast; heard the four together; heard the hamlet girl's household and gave the bed and bread; spoke from the wall at dusk in month 7 that the guest was not leaving; refused the Warden of the Wall's two men a third time.
By the Keeper of the Count: kept the cord of the dim nights and the cord of her afraid days, kept the cap of two, drew the lots (three households on three named nights), kept the roll (about 590), entered about 110 shrines, knotted the four offices' answers on the guest's absence.
By the Steward of the Store: judged the stone with the old cutter, chose the leader and the goods, lent the third boat from the fishing households, counted the winter in month 4, had the quay roll struck and read over.
By the old cutter: judged the stone and replaced four short blocks.
By the Warden of the Wall (as the wall's own work, not as her helper): sat her door from the first night of winter, had every man told what he sits against and what the standing body is, staked the way to the gate, gave the half score as escort.
By the court's runners: carried to the hamlets that no household is to set a night's lamp in the guest's name beyond the lot.
Nothing was done in the god's name by the Gate Bench.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper
1. The harbor party with the stone and the guest's word. Nine of the court in three planked boats (two of the court's, one lent by the fishing households): the leader (about forty-two, the Steward's chief keeper of the trade cords, who led the last two parties and heard the stranger's tale on the quays), two cutters (the journeyman, about thirty-five, and a younger man to haul), three runners (the young woman who has been twice, and two of last year's eight), three boatmen of the fishing households (one the man who knows the low-water bars). Goods: twenty-five bolts of linen, sixty pots, twelve jars of flax oil (of which three bolts, twelve pots and two jars are a guest's gift for the one in the delta), and sixty blocks of the scar's grey stone dressed to the harbor house's cord. No bronze. No lamp, wick or ember; the first fire struck from flint. Each carries a belt knife; the cutters their tools. They set out from the quay on the twenty-fourth day of month 2, on the water the first autumn rain raised.
   The way. By the court's boats down the river to the mouth (112.4 miles, 4 days down, 10 up). At the mouth the leader means to wait ten days for the house's ship, to lift the stone. If it comes, the stone goes to it and the party goes on in it or on foot to the harbor to buy salt with the linen and oil at the standing price, and to give the guest's words to the factor of the house that took the stone. If no ship comes in ten days he means to stack the stone above the high water under hides and reed with the three boatmen and the cutters, and go round the shore on foot with the linen, the oil and two runners (97.9 miles, 7 days on foot, longer laden) to ask the factor why the ship has not come. He will not put the river boats to sea. The seat's leave: from the harbor he may go on to the delta market town with two runners and the guest's word, by the house's ship or carriers or on foot, if the factor offers a road he judges safe and he can be home or settled for the winter before the gales; he may turn back at any time and say so; the cutters and the boatmen do not go on; he binds the court to nothing. I give no distance or time to the delta; the record has none.
   The words, exactly as the guest gave them: "I am Vael. I am half a woman and half a hind, and I keep a lamp in a town in a river bend, four days down the river from its mouth. I have heard that you say before the market that you are a god. I do not know whether you are. I do not ask you to prove it. If you are one like me, I would like to meet you face to face, at a gate, as a guest meets a house, and I will ask nothing of you but to know you. I tell you where I am so that you may come or not as you choose. I do not send anyone to bring you and I do not ask what you can do. If you are not one like me, I wish you a good market, and I ask you to forgive a stranger's question. The people who bring this are guests at your table, and they keep a guest's custom: bread, three nights, and no questions of your business until the third. If you will send me a word, they will carry it home." The words put the town four days down the river from the mouth; the town lies four days up. The leader will say the words as given, and if asked where the bend lies he will say four days up from the mouth by the town's own count.
   The orders: bread and three nights and no business asked until the third; the leader speaks the words to the factor on the third night with the court's own words and asks the house's ship or carriers to take them on to the delta; if the factor will not, or the house has no road there, he goes on by the coast only if he judges it safe, otherwise he comes home and says so. If the stranger is hostile or cannot be found: no fight, no argument; keep the goods promised to the court, come home with what can be had or winter where it is safe; at the delta, ask at the market for whoever keeps the weights, give him the words, and if none will hear them buy what is needed as any trader would. If asked questions: answer only what was seen and told (she woke on the stony ground above the town in the dark before an autumn morning, does not know why, the seat takes her for the awaited, she keeps a lamp she lights in a house that asks); do not say what the lamp does or that it can be taken; do not give or sell a flame, lamp or token; do not say how many days she walks or which nights she keeps; if pressed, say he does not know. If the stranger says it is a god, say what he heard and no more; do not say it is false and do not say it is true. Listen, bring home any answer and any word of another like her on that coast.
   Success: the stone reaches the house's ship, the party is received, the words given and heard, the terms for the year learned, salt bought, the words on to the delta and an answer or word of the stranger carried home, the party home with salt. Failure: a boat grounds or founders on a low-water bar under its stone, the stone is robbed or lost at the mouth, no ship comes, the harbor answers badly or withdraws its order, the party is lost on the coast, taken ill or drowned, the word to the delta is refused or never reaches it, or the stranger is hostile. Partial: the stone is lifted and the word does not go on; the word goes on and no answer returns; salt is bought and the party home late.
   Circumstances from my record that bear on it: last year's eight went by two boats with thirty blocks, one boat grounded on a low-water bar under its stone, no ship lay at the mouth, they went round the shore on foot with the samples, were given bread and three nights, the leader spoke the words on the third night, the house took the samples and sold salt at a standing price below the carriers', named its order, the gales shut the shore, they wintered and came up at high water in month 8, all eight; this year's loads are sixty blocks in three boats, twenty to a boat with crew and goods, in the low water of autumn, with the rain's raise; the house has not told the town when its ship comes, so the leader waits ten days; the leader is not at ease with a boat or the sea, did not ask to be replaced this year, and heard last year on the quays the tale of the stranger; the carriers have an interest in the price and the party undercuts them (my inference); the party has no spear and no flame; I have no distance or road to the delta and the town has never been there; the guest's words as given put the town below the mouth; the cold begins in month 4, winter closes coastal sailing and the coast road, and upstream with a load takes 10 days by the table and may be worse in rain or low water. The harbor towns know of the guest from the carriers and from last year's party.
   The year both ways, briefly. Salt A: home before the hard cold, in the last days of month 3 or the first of month 4, with salt, the harbor's answer and what was heard of the stranger; the late pigs fully cured; the Steward's count easier; the cutters cut next year's fifth to the cord in the dry months. Salt B: not back until spring, no word all winter, the late pigs cured lightly and the meat gone early, the party up at the high water in month 7 or 8, the cutters cut the fifth to the cord on the strength of last year's order. If the party is lost or never returns, the court has no word of why, the town grieves nine, the guest's word may or may not have gone on, and no party is sent before the next autumn.
2. No fight, search, persuasion of anyone outside the people, or craft beyond what the people know was attempted by anyone else this year. The third boat is of the fishing households' own craft.

## What turns on a power
1. The three-household nights: month 5, night 16; month 6, night 2; month 6, night 24 (the last on the hardest night of the frost). The Warden of the Door named them to the Keeper of the Count and to the wall men at her door the day before, the seat's crier told the town, the court asked no plan of her the next day, and the wall set four men at her door, told in the yard what they sit against. What the four saw at those dawns is the Keeper's. I write nothing of it, except that the captain told the Warden of the Wall they saw what she told them they would see.
2. The guest's body in the yard. On lot nights the wall's men have seen her lie still, and the cord has said asleep; her answer in the yard says a lying hind is not her in a dream and that she does not sleep. The captain asked what the wall has been sitting and has no answer. What the lying hind is on a lot night, what the standing body is on a dream night, and which of the two the wall saw on each night of this year, is the Keeper's. The Keeper of the Count now writes lying beside the old tag.
3. Walking the last sleep of the dying in kept houses that asked, about eighteen kept-house deaths in winter. The households say the dying were quiet and some spoke of a hind or a lamp. Nothing cured. Whether the oldest of the barrow singers could have been walked is not a thing anyone asked: her house did not keep the flame and no one asked. Whether a walked sleep could have given up the last third of the song is the town's guess and is a thing of her power.
4. The break and the nearest house, as she told the four. The town made of it that she cannot be killed. Whether it is so, and what comes back at the nearest house with a lamp, is the Keeper's.
5. The dim nights: month 2 night 14 (no lot, she awake in the yard, no dying); month 3 night 23 (no lot, awake in the yard, no dying); month 4 night 7 (lot, lying, an old man died in a kept house, no one asked her to walk his sleep); month 8 night 17 (no lot, awake in a western hamlet a morning's walk from the wall, hamlet and town both saw it, no dying in the town); month 10 night 5 (no lot, awake in the yard, no dying); month 11 night 19 (lot, lying, an old woman died in a house not kept); month 12 night 11 (no lot, awake in the yard, no dying). The wall's watch and the Keeper agree the hour: about an hour. Twenty-one in all; a dying in the town on seven. Whether any is hers is not known to anyone.
6. The list of afraid days of Year 3 and its cord set beside the six dim nights of Year 3. I do not have her list and do not know whether the two agree. The Keeper of the Count said only that a count shows how many and not why.
7. The flames near her dipping at dawn: the wall's men say it is at the same hour every dawn after the three-household nights.
8. The Night Sitter's work yard in month 4: the lamp near the guest did not dip, by the Night Sitter's word. The guest's hands on the kettle and the lamp and nothing more were seen. The hamlet girl found she could (Keeper's Year 3 line, taken); the Night Sitter sleeps more.
9. The warm wind after the hard frost: the town says it was hers, because she had kept three households the night of the frost. She was asked and said she did not know. Whether any wind was hers is not known to anyone.
10. The shrine word: about 110 shrines lit from a kept flame, entered by the Keeper of the Count; whether any costs her anything or counts toward the seven is the Keeper's.
11. The lowest cuts at the scar: the cutters left the ring of stones round the three as it was.

## What touches anyone else
- The harbor party (above) is the only party sent out. It carries the guest's words, the court's word, sixty blocks of stone, linen, pots and oil, and a guest's gift for the one in the delta, to the river's mouth and the harbor town west along the coast where the salt is boiled, and by leave of the seat to the delta market town if the factor offers a road. Nothing else was sent out. No spears went out.
- The salt carriers came twice (late autumn and spring) at last year's price, ate the three nights, watched and said little. In Salt B the Steward asked them hard for word of the party and they gave none.
- Iron came down from the mining town as in other years, greyer and softer than ever. The carriers said they carry what they are given. The smiths say the mining town sends it down before it is worked enough. I give no cause.
- No stranger came to the gate but the carriers and the ordinary traders.
- Nothing was given or sold that carries a flame, a lamp or a token of the guest. What of the guest goes out of the town is the words the leader has by heart. The shrine word means a household may light a neighbor's niche in the town at the neighbor's asking; no flame went out of the town by it.
- The town's knowledge of the stranger in the delta is only what the leader heard on the quays last year.
- Where the court's four said what the town would do if the one from the delta or any other asked for the guest by name: bread and three nights in the guest house yard, the wall watching, the business asked on the third, the court telling him she is away and where only if she left word. That is a plan, not an event.

## Asked of the god
In the mortals' words as the people repeat them, and whether answered in front of them. Where her decisions fix an answer I say so; elsewhere I write none.
- Captain of the wall, last year, in the yard: how a man is to know the standing body from the sleeping one. Answered in front of him in month 1, as her decisions fix.
- One of the four, last year, in the yard: whether, if fire came into the yard, they were to move her. Answered in front of him in month 1, as her decisions fix.
- Father at the second of the four houses, last year: "Who told you they had not come home?" Answered in front of him in month 1, as her decisions fix, with the Warden of the Door's answer given as the Warden's.
- Captain of the wall, month 1, in the yard: "If a lying hind is not her in a dream, what has the wall been sitting?" Not answered that I can write.
- Warden of the Wall, month 1, in the yard: which house is nearest to a broken body. Not answered that I can write.
- Father at the second house, month 1: not a question, a correction: his son came home two autumns past and not last year. No answer that I can write.
- Warden of the Door, month 2, the morning after the first evening of teaching the words: whether the words mean the town lies four days down the river from its mouth, since the town lies four days up. Not answered that I can write.
- A man of one of the two young households of barrow singers, to the Warden of the Door, first week of winter: whether the guest will hear what they have of the third song, two parts in three, now the oldest singer is dead. Carried to her. Not answered that I can write.
- Woman of the lowest street, after the warm wind: whether the thaw was hers. She said she did not know.
- Headman of a western hamlet, at dawn after the dim in month 8: whether it was she. No word of hers this year that I can write; the answer she gave the headmen in other years is what the people repeat.
- Hamlet girl, before the seat, month 4: for a bed in the lower town and no more than two sittings in ten. Put to the seat, not to the guest; the seat gave it.
- Households: "May we have the flame?" Answered by her lighting it where asked.
- No one asked her about the iron, the salt or the harbor beyond the leader's orders. No one asked her about the singer's death.

## Names said aloud
"Vael," by the leader and by the Warden of the Door to each other, each evening of the three evenings in the Warden of the Door's house in the middle of month 2, as part of the words the leader has by heart. If the leader reaches the third night at the harbor, he says "Vael" aloud to the factor of the house that took the stone; that is the Keeper's if he gets there. If he reaches the delta and the stranger, he says "Vael" aloud there. No name was said aloud for the people, the home or any office holder. The people still call her "the guest" and "the guest with the lamp"; some in the lowest streets call her "the keeper". These are not names.

## What the people tell their god
The seat, from the wall, in her own words: "The guest asked what we would do if she were away. We told her. She asked in time, and a keeper who asks that is a keeper who means to be back. The stone is gone down to the sea for the second time and the nine with it and your word. We hold the bargain as ours. We have not spent the iron's worth of it yet."
The Warden of the Door: "I carry what I am given and I have said I take you for the one awaited. You said at a father's door that you were wrong, and asked whose word it was. I found it was the roll at the quay that nobody struck. I think a runner read it to you. I cannot swear it. The Steward has had the roll struck. I asked you whether you meant four days down the river from the mouth, for the town lies four days up. You gave me nothing I can say. The leader will say it as you gave it and will say up if he is asked."
The Warden of the Wall: "The wall holds your door and has held it a third winter. You told the four what they sit against, and told them to move you by the cord if fire comes. The captain has staked the way to the gate. The wall has seen you lie still on the lot nights and has called it asleep, and you say a lying hind is not you in a dream. I have not been told what the wall has been sitting. The iron is soft, and the old heads are fewer than last year. The wall has stopped being surprised at the dawns, which is not the same as not minding. I have not taken you for the awaited, and I have not refused you."
The Steward of the Store: "Grain will reach the harvest with a thin margin, as it has three years. Fuel and oil are enough. Salt is as the party made it. The stone costs the town more in picks than it knew. The old iron is a third down. I lent a third boat and counted the winter aloud without being asked. I took your word grudgingly and I have not changed."
The Keeper of the Count: "Seven nights the star dimmed, twenty-one in all, and a dying in the town on seven. The roll of kept households is about five hundred and ninety. Five of this year's seven you were awake, and two you were lying. I have written lying beside the old tag. The cord does not say why. I have your list beside the cord. The seventh place is empty."
The old cutter: "The picks wear twice as fast. We cut the fifth to the cord and the wall had four parts of the five. The three cuts are as they were, with the stones round them. No one told us."
The Night Sitter: "I sleep more. The hamlet girl found she could. A sitter is not taught. You are good with a kettle."
The barrow singers' households: "The oldest of us died in month 3 and did not die alone. She gave us what she could and her voice would not hold the last. We have two parts in three of the third song and sing it to the gap. We asked if you would hear it, and have had no answer. Some say you could have taken it from her sleep. Her house did not ask."
The town: "A star that dims seven times in a year is a star that dims. The iron is soft. The salt came or did not. There is a stranger in the delta who reads scales and says he is a god, and we do not know whether he is. We lit our lamps. We counted the children at dusk."
The father of the second house: "My son came home two autumns past and not last year. A plain thing was plainly said. Thank you for the bread."
The hamlet girl: "I can sit. I will sit two nights in ten. My hands shake."
