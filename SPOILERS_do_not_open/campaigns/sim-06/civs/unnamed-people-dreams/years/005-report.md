# Report 005: unnamed people of god-dreams, Year 5, pass one, for the Keeper

## How the god was met
Autumn, month 1, day 4. Yard of the watch. Captain: asked last year what the wall has been sitting if a lying hind is not her in a dream; heard "I do not know, I do not sleep, I have no memory of lying down, I will not say it is a dream and will not say it is not"; said he had his answer and it was no answer, and had the four write a tablet for each lot night. Four who sit her door: heard to keep sitting it and write what they see, and to lead her out by the cord if fire comes. Warden of the Wall: asked last year which house is nearest a broken body; heard "the nearest to where it breaks that keeps a lamp through the night, any hearth will do", that she returns after a season and may lack a small thing, and that she asks for bread and not to be carried; said to the captain that a god who says she does not know her own body has said more than one who says she does, and he did not know if that was a comfort; has not taken her word and has not refused it. Elder of the Gate Bench against her: a god who does not know her own body needs a town that does. The lowest streets: she may come back lacking, and as a child of the house. The storytelling households: a keeper who may come back lacking a song knows what a song is worth.

Month 1, day 4, the same dusk. Keeper of the Count: received her list of afraid days of Year 4, wrote it onto a cord, said he would set it beside his cord with awake or lying against each night and say what the two show. I do not have her list. Warden of the Door: told that she had meant up, not down; told the Steward, who said the roll is struck, read over by the quay keeper and a runner, and since spring the west road gate keeper strikes a party that comes in by that road; said she wished it had been asked sooner.

Month 1, day 9. The barrow, by day. Man of the young households of barrow singers and his household: heard her say she could not have walked the old singer's sleep (her house kept no flame) and would not have taken a song from a sleeper's sleep; heard her listen to the two parts to the end; the man said they asked in earnest a year ago and now had an answer that was neither the one they feared nor the one they hoped. Daughter of the old singer's house: stood at the edge of the barrows that afternoon and went home. Storytelling households and lowest streets: said what they said.

Month 1, day 12 to month 3. Seat: sent six runners through the headmen on the Warden of the Door's asking. No singer in any hamlet holds the third part; old people remember it and some can give the first words of its second turn, none its last; at the farthest hamlet an old woman wept and said her mother sang it at the harvest. Warden of the Door: gave the answers to the two households at the barrows.

Month 2. Seat, Steward, old cutter, Warden of the Wall, Gate Bench: judged the harbor's order (below). Steward: for forty blocks and twenty held back. Old cutter: forty, not sixty, against the picks. Elder against her: the stone is the town's footing and she asks it for strangers a third time. Warden of the Wall: did not ask a fourth time for two men; said stone is a thing a man will rob a boat for. Keeper of the Count: would knot what goes. Seat: accepted on the eighth day.

Winter. Wall: held her door from the first night of winter; four tablets per lot night written as in the year file. Keeper of the Count: laid the tablets beside the cord, wrote lying against each lot night. Night Sitter and hamlet girl, month 4, day 18: took her hands for the kettle and lamp and no more; the hamlet girl said she had thought her gift did not reach it. Seat: said the bed and bread stand and the Steward sends the girl's father a measure of grain in the sitting winters. Steward of the Store: counted aloud in month 4.

Spring. Month 7, day 9, the four offices and the seat in the hall: heard her say she means to walk to the harbor in the dry months and be back before the year ends, and her asks. Seat: gave leave from the wall at dusk. Warden of the Wall: asked the day, the road and the day back; said the road is not one he knows; did not choose the four, the captain put it to the watch and four came of themselves; said to the seat he would not have gone and would not have stopped them. Steward: gift of four bolts of linen, six pots, two jars of oil; asked the leader, who said he would come as guide, and that he did not know he was a guide. Keeper of the Count: two households a night by his deputy, awake or lying written as seen. Warden of the Door: would hold her place and the cord of questions. Elder against her: a woman who walks to the sea is a day nearer the other god. The lowest streets: she is going west to the one in the delta. The old woman of the lower town who keeps the old tale: she will walk out one night as she came.

Summer. The guest set out on month 10, day 5 (below). Warden of the Door: held the place and the cord all summer.

Where each office stands at the year's end: old Warden of the Door, taken her word; the new Warden of the Door, taken it; the seat, taken it; Keeper of the Count, taken it; Steward of the Store, taken it grudgingly; Warden of the Wall, has not and has not refused; Gate Bench, split, agreed on the custom of the table.

## What was done in the god's name
By the Warden of the Door: carried the guest to the yard of the watch, the barrow, the Night Sitter's yard and the hall; carried her ask on the harbor's order, the stone and the two messages to the seat; sent the runners for the third song; taught the leader the words in three evenings in month 2 (with "up" in them) and looked through the baggage at the quay; held her place and the cord of questions while she was away; walked beside her to the river road.
By the seat: sent the runners for the third song; accepted the order for forty blocks and sent the party with the guest's two messages; gave leave for the walk and said it from the wall; said from the wall that anyone who says "the guest says" of a thing she has not said answers to the Bench; heard the hamlet girl's case again and gave grain.
By the Keeper of the Count: kept the cord (three dim nights, twenty-four in all), the cap of two, three named nights, the roll (about 605), the lots; wrote lying beside each lot night from the wall's tablets; named two households a night while she was away by his deputy.
By the Steward of the Store: judged the stone, chose the leader and goods, counted the winter, had the roll struck and the west road gate keeper told to strike, sent the girl's father grain, set apart the guest's gift for the factor and the carrier.
By the old cutter: judged the stone; held twenty blocks back against the picks.
By the Warden of the Wall (as the wall's own work, not as her helper): had the four write a tablet for each lot night, held her door, wrote her days in the wall's tablets, did not choose the four of the watch who went with her.
By the court's runners: carried the third song's question and its answers.
Nothing was done in the god's name by the Gate Bench.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper
1. The party to the river's mouth and the harbor with forty blocks and the guest's two messages. Seven of the court in two planked boats of the court: the leader (about forty-three, chief keeper of the trade cords, who has led the last three parties and spoken the words on the third night; he did not ask to be replaced); the journeyman cutter (about thirty-six) to read the stone against the cord; three runners named by the Warden of the Door (the young woman who has been three times, and two of last year's nine); two boatmen of the fishing households (one the man who knows the low-water bars). Goods: twenty bolts of linen, fifty pots, ten jars of flax oil, forty blocks of the scar's grey stone dressed to the harbor house's cord, and set apart a guest's gift for the factor (four bolts, six pots, two jars) and a gift for any carrier not of the house who takes the guest's word to the delta (three bolts and two jars). No bronze. No lamp, wick or ember; first fire from flint. Each carries a belt knife, the cutter his tools. They set out from the quay on the twentieth day of month 2, on the first autumn rain's water. No spears. The seat did not allow the leader to go on to the delta himself.
   The way. By the court's boats down the river to the mouth (112.4 miles, 4 days down, 10 days back up). At the mouth the leader means to find the stone that lay there and learn whether the house's ship lifted it, and to tell the court. If a ship lies there within ten days the new stone goes to it and he goes on in it or on foot. If none comes he means to stack the new stone above the high water under hides and reed with the boatmen and the cutter, and go on round the shore on foot with the linen, the oil and two runners (97.9 miles, 7 days on foot, longer laden) to ask the factor. He will not put the river boats to sea.
   The words. For the factor: "I am Vael, the guest at the bend who keeps a lamp. I thank you and your table for the bread and the three nights you gave my leader last year, and for the salt at the price you set. I understand that your house does not carry a stranger's word to a market it sells to, and I do not ask you to. I ask only for what you will tell: how many days the delta market town lies from your harbor, by the coast and by ship, who goes there, and whether anyone outside your house would carry a word there for pay. If you will not say, I ask no more. My court asks whether your house sells iron or picks, and whether the iron on your coast is soft as ours is, and that is the court's question and not mine. The people who bring this keep a guest's custom: bread, three nights, and no questions of your business until the third." The guest's own words at the harbor, if she goes, are hers. For the one in the delta: "I am Vael. I am half a woman and half a hind, and I keep a lamp in a town in a river bend, four days up the river from its mouth. If another word of mine has reached you, it said down where it should have said up, and I ask you to forgive that. I have heard that you say before the market that you are a god. I do not know whether you are. I do not ask you to prove it. If you are one like me, I would like to meet you face to face, at a gate, as a guest meets a house, and I will ask nothing of you but to know you. I tell you where I am so that you may come or not as you choose. I do not send anyone to bring you and I do not ask what you can do. If you are not one like me, I wish you a good market, and I ask you to forgive a stranger's question. The people who bring this are guests at your table, and they keep a guest's custom: bread, three nights, and no questions of your business until the third. If you will send me a word, they will carry it home." The leader gives the first to any carrier not of the factor's house, a coasting ship's crew or a free trader going to the delta, for linen or oil from the Steward's goods, if the factor does not object; if none, he brings it home and says so. He does not ask the factor to carry it. If asked for the stranger's name he says he does not know it and asks for the one who reads the scales. If asked questions he answers only what was seen and told: she woke on the stony ground above the town in the dark before an autumn morning, does not know why, the seat takes her for the awaited, she keeps a lamp she lights in a house that asks; he does not say what the lamp does or that it can be taken, does not give or sell a flame, lamp or token, does not say how many days she walks or which nights she keeps; if pressed, he does not know. If the stranger says it is a god, he says what he heard and no more. If anyone asks whether another like her stands on that coast, he has seen none and heard of none, and he listens.
   Success: the stone is lifted or left safe, the party is received, bread and three nights, the second message given and heard, the factor's answer on iron and the road to the delta learned, the order and terms for the year learned, salt bought, the first message taken on by a carrier outside the house, the party home with salt. Failure: a boat grounds or founders on a low-water bar under its stone, the new stone is robbed or lost at the mouth, no ship lies there, the harbor withdraws its order, the party is lost on the coast, taken ill or drowned, no carrier will take the word, or the factor objects to one that would. Partial: the stone is lifted and the word does not go on; the word goes on and no answer returns; salt is bought and the party home late.
   Circumstances from my record that bear on it: last year's nine went by three boats with sixty blocks; a boat grounded on a bar and three days were lost; no ship lay at the mouth in ten days; the stone was stacked above the high water and the leader and a pair of runners went round the shore to the harbor; the factor gave bread and three nights, sold salt at the standing price for linen and oil, said his house's ship lifts the stone at the first fair weather after the gales, and would not have the guest's word carried on to the delta; all nine home in the second week of month 4 with a little salt; this year's load is forty blocks in two boats with seven; no third boat; the factor did not offer iron last year; the iron in the town is soft and the old iron is a quarter down; the carriers have an interest in the price (my inference); the party has no spear and no flame; I have no distance or road to the delta and the town has never been there; the harbor towns know of the guest from the carriers and from the last three parties; the cold begins in month 4 and winter closes coastal sailing and the coast road; upstream with a load takes 10 days by the table and may be worse.
   The year both ways, briefly. Party A: home before the hard cold, in the last days of month 3 or the first of month 4, with salt, the harbor's answer and terms and what was heard of iron and the road; the late pigs cured as they ought; the Steward's count easier; the guest heard him in the first week of month 4. Party B: not home until the high water in month 7 or 8, no word all winter, the late pigs cured lightly and the meat early gone; the guest heard him in spring. If the party is lost, the town grieves seven and sends none before the autumn.
2. The guest's walk to the harbor. The throne gave leave and the Warden of the Wall did not say the road was unsafe; he said it is not a road he knows. She goes with four of the watch who came of themselves, not spears (a man of the four who sit her door, two of the south-east gate, a man of a hamlet family that had her flame), and with the leader as guide, a hind at her own pace, the lamp on her waist, down the river road to the mouth and along the shore to the harbor. Set out month 10, day 5. Named days: the latest she turns for home is month 11, day 28; the day she means to be at the wall is month 12, day 20. The Warden of the Wall wrote them in the wall's tablets and will hold her door empty a month past the day, then say so to the seat. I give no days for the road and the harbor; I have none that are not in the record, and the Keeper prices the walk at her pace. Gift for the factor's table: four bolts of linen, six pots, two jars of oil. Goods are carried by the four and the guide. While she is away the Keeper of the Count's deputy draws two households a night and knots away nights; the Warden of the Door holds her place and the cord of questions. What she meets on the road and at the harbor, and who is at the factor's door, are the Keeper's.
   Success: she reaches the harbor, is received as a guest, is home by the day named. Failure: a hurt or loss of the party, a refused door, a night road taken ill, the guest not home by the day named. Walk A: at the wall by the twentieth of month 12, met at the gate by the town. Walk B: not at the wall by the day named; the wall holds her door empty and says so in month 1 of Year 6; the town says she has gone to the one in the delta.
   Circumstances from my record that bear on it: she has not left the town in four years; she cannot pass a low or barred door, cannot climb a ladder, and the gate takes her width; she does not sleep and stands at night with the watch to guard her body; she has walked the near hamlets with a half score of the watch in other years; her fear reaches her at dawn on lot nights wherever she is; the town keeps two households a night while she is away and she carries the fear as she carries two; the harbor knows of her from the carriers and three parties; the party of last year was fed at the harbor and left at no harm; the factor would not carry her word to the delta. No one outside the court knows the day she set out.
3. No fight, search, persuasion of anyone outside the people, or craft beyond what the people know was attempted by anyone else this year. The runners for the third song were an ordinary search of the hamlets by the court's runners, and the Keeper settled it in the release.

## What turns on a power
1. The lying hind on lot nights, and what the wall wrote. The four wrote, night by night, that she lay still with the lamp lit and did not stand, that her breathing was slow and even, that the ears did not turn, that the lamp did not dip in the night, that she did not stir to a hand laid on the flank (three nights, the captain's order), and that at dawn she was shaken and the flames near her dipped at the same hour. I wrote these as the wall's tablets say and no more. They are what the wall saw as I understand it from your release ("what they have seen before"); if any of these is not what a watcher at her door would see, the Keeper should say. What the lying hind is, and what the standing body is, are the Keeper's.
2. The three-household nights: month 5, night 14; month 6, night 3; month 6, night 25. Named the day before; the crier told the town; the wall set four men. What the four saw at those dawns is the Keeper's. I write nothing of it.
3. Walking the last sleep of the dying in kept houses that asked, about nineteen kept-house deaths in winter. The households say the dying were quiet and some spoke of a hind or a lamp. Nothing was cured.
4. The dim nights: month 2, night 9 (no lot, she awake in the yard, no dying); month 2, night 22 (lot night, lying, an old woman died in a kept house); month 7, night 15 (no lot, awake in the yard, no dying). The wall's watch and the Keeper agree the hour: about an hour. Twenty-four in all; a dying in the town on eight. Whether any is hers is not known to anyone.
5. Her list of afraid days of Year 4 and its cord beside the cord of seven. I do not have her list.
6. The flames near her dipping at dawn, the same hour each time.
7. The break and the nearest house. She told the wall which house is nearest. What comes back, and as what, is the Keeper's. The lowest streets say a child of the house.
8. The warm wind: none this year after the hard frost; the town says it was not asked for, and the elder against her says wind follows frost in any country.
9. What she may lose between a break and a return, a name or a song: the storytelling households say she knows the worth of a song. Whether it is so is the Keeper's.
10. The shrine word: about 125 shrines lit from a kept flame entered this year; whether any counts toward the seven is the Keeper's.
11. The lowest cuts at the scar: the ring of stones round the three stands as it was.

## What touches anyone else
- The party to the river's mouth and the harbor (above) is the only party sent out in the autumn. It carries the guest's two messages, the court's word, forty blocks, linen, pots and oil, a gift for the factor's table and a gift for any carrier not of the house.
- The guest's walk (above): she and four of the watch and the guide leave the town on month 10, day 5 for the harbor. The guide is the leader of the autumn party, home in Party A before the hard cold, and in Party B home at the spring's high water.
- The salt carriers came twice (late autumn and spring) at last year's price, ate the three nights, watched and said little. In Party B the Steward asked them hard for word of the party and they gave none.
- Iron came down from the mining town grey and soft, as for two winters. The carriers said they carry what they are given. The Steward asks the factor, by the leader, whether his house sells iron or picks and whether his iron is soft.
- The stone at the river's mouth: the town does not know what became of it. What the harbor says of it is the Keeper's. What the leader finds there he tells the court, and I write none of it.
- The six runners for the third song walked the hamlets and the three farther ones and came home in month 3. Nothing was carried out of the town by them.
- No stranger came to the gate but the carriers and the ordinary traders.
- Nothing was given or sold that carries a flame, a lamp or a token of the guest. What of the guest goes out of the town is the words the leader has by heart, and she, in person, if she goes. The shrine word means a household may light a neighbor's niche in the town at the neighbor's asking; no flame went out of the town by it.
- The town's knowledge of the stranger in the delta is only what the leader heard on the quays in Year 3.

## Asked of the god
In the mortals' words as the people repeat them, and whether answered in front of them. Where her decisions fix an answer I say so; elsewhere I write none.
- Captain of the wall, last year, in the yard: "If a lying hind is not her in a dream, what has the wall been sitting?" Answered in front of him in month 1 as her decisions fix: she does not know; she does not sleep; she has no memory of lying down; she will not say it is a dream and will not say it is not.
- Warden of the Wall, last year, in the yard: which house is nearest to a broken body. Answered in front of him in month 1 as her decisions fix.
- Warden of the Door, last year, the morning after the first evening of teaching the words: whether the town lies four days down or up from the mouth. Answered in month 1 through the Warden of the Door as her decisions fix: up.
- Father at the second house, last year: not a question. Nothing further this year.
- A man of the young households of barrow singers, last year: whether the guest will hear what they have of the third song. Answered in front of him in month 1 at the barrows: she listened to the end and said what her decisions fix.
- Woman of the lowest street, last year, after the warm wind: not asked again this year. In the summer, while she was away, she sent to ask whether the guest had gone to see if the wind would come: knotted on the cord of questions, no answer, it waits.
- Headman of a western hamlet, in the summer while she was away: whether she would come to the flood line, since the weir below his field came down in the winter thaw. Knotted on the cord, no answer, it waits.
- A man of the barrow households, in the summer while she was away: whether she would hear the third song again in winter, with what they now have and a verse from the far hamlet's old woman. Knotted on the cord, no answer, it waits.
- Hamlet girl, month 4, day 18: not a question. The guest told her plainly that her gift does not reach what the sitting takes from her. She said she had thought not.
- A man of the lower town, who said "the guest says" of the weir while she was away: not a question. The Bench fined him a day's labor.
- Households: "May we have the flame?" Answered by her lighting it where asked.
- No one asked her about the iron, the salt or the harbor beyond the leader's orders.

## Names said aloud
"Vael," by the leader and by the Warden of the Door to each other, each evening of the three evenings in the Warden of the Door's house in the middle of month 2, as part of the words the leader has by heart. If the leader reaches the third night at the harbor he says "Vael" aloud to the factor; if a carrier outside the house takes the first message he says "Vael" aloud there; that is the Keeper's. No name was said aloud for the people, the home or any office holder. The people still call her "the guest" and "the guest with the lamp"; some in the lowest streets call her "the keeper". These are not names.

## What the people tell their god
The seat, from the wall, in her own words: "You asked what we would do if you were away, and you went, and you named the day. We kept our nights and the table kept your place. We hold the harbor bargain as ours. We sent forty blocks and held twenty back for the picks. We did not find the rest of the song. We have not forgotten."
The Warden of the Door: "I carry what I am given and I take you for the awaited. You said up, not down, and I told the Steward, and the roll is struck. I held the cord of questions at your door. Three were knotted, and I answered each that it waits, and no one answered in your name. I asked myself whether I was afraid for you, and I was."
The Warden of the Wall: "The wall holds your door and has held it a fourth winter. The four wrote what they saw, night by night, and the Keeper of the Count has the tablets. The wall does not know what it has written. The iron is soft and the old heads are fewer. I have not taken you for the awaited, and I have not refused you. I did not choose the four who went with you, and I did not stop them."
The Steward of the Store: "Grain reached the harvest with a thin margin, as it has four years. Fuel and oil were enough. The old iron is down by a quarter and the picks wear twice as fast. I sent forty and held twenty back. I counted the winter aloud. I took your word grudgingly and I have not changed."
The Keeper of the Count: "Three nights the star dimmed this year, twenty-four in all, and a dying in the town on eight. The roll of kept households is about six hundred and five. Two of this year's three you were awake, one you were lying, and I wrote lying. I have your list beside the cord and the wall's tablets. The cord does not say why. The seventh place is empty."
The old cutter: "The picks wear twice as fast. We cut the fifth to the cord and held twenty back. The three cuts are as they were, with the stones round them. No one told us."
The Night Sitter: "I sleep more. The hamlet girl sits two nights in ten, and her hands shake. You are good with a kettle."
The barrow singers' households: "We have two parts in three of the third song and the gap. The runners came home and no one in any hamlet has the last. An old woman at the farthest hamlet wept. We asked if you would hear what we have, and you did. We have a verse of the second turn from her and we would sing it to you."
The town: "A star that dimmed three times is a star that dimmed fewer. The iron is soft. The salt came or did not. We lit our lamps. We counted the children at dusk. The keeper went to the sea and we kept her place."
The seat's crier and the headman of the western hamlet: "The weir came down in the thaw. We wanted you at the flood line."
