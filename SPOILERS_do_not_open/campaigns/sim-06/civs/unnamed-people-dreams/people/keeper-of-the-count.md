# The Keeper of the Count

Unnamed. No mortal has said his name aloud in play.

A man of about fifty winters at the end of Year 2. Keeps the tally cords, the roll of households, the count of the children and, from month 1 of Year 2, the cord of the dim nights. Exact, dry and fair; he counts what he is shown and says what the count says and no more. He took the guest's word from the first lots.

Year 2: kept the cord as asked, a knot for each dim night and a second thread for each dying, tagged kept or not. For the three nights of Year 1 he took the dates from the wall watch and the runners and marked them remembered. Opened the Count's door on the first of each month to any who wished to read the cord. At the turn of the year laid it on the table and said: five nights, eight in all, a dying in the town on three of eight, which is what the town's roll of the dead would give on its own; the cord does not say why. Kept the cap of two households a night, and three on three named nights, and wrote down a household that lit its own niche at night.

Year 3 (end): about fifty-one winters. In month 1, day 5, received the guest's answer that a flame a household lights from its own kept flame is a shrine and not a household kept that night, and her list of the days of her second year on which she was afraid, which he wrote onto a cord of his own, a knot to a day. Said he had waited a year for one line. Kept the cap of two and the three named nights, drew the lots, entered about seventy shrines, kept the roll at about 520. At the turn of the year laid the cord of the dim nights on the table: fourteen in all, a dying in the town on five; said the roll of kept households and the dim nights have risen together and the cord does not say why.

Year 4 (end): about fifty-two winters. In month 1, day 7, received the guest's list of the days of her third year on which she was afraid, wrote it onto a cord of his own, and laid it beside the cord of the six dim nights at the Count's door on month 2, day 1; said the count shows how many and not why. Kept the cap of two and the three named nights (month 5 night 16, month 6 night 2, month 6 night 24), drew the lots, kept the roll at about 590, entered about 110 shrines. After the guest's answer in the yard of the watch he writes lying beside the cord's old tag asleep, and says the old word was the wall's and not hers. In month 7 knotted a cord of his own of the four offices' answers on what the court would do if she were away. At the turn of the year laid the cord of the dim nights on the table: twenty-one in all, a dying in the town on seven; said again that the roll of kept households and the dim nights have risen together and the cord does not say why.
