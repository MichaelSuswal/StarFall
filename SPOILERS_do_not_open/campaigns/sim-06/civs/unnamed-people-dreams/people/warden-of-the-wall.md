# The Warden of the Wall

Unnamed. No mortal has said his name aloud in play.

A hard man of about fifty-two winters at the end of Year 1. Keeps the spears (about six hundred by beacon, about two hundred on the wall watch by turns) and the wall. Does not hurry, does not break the custom, and does not say what he thinks of a thing until he has watched it for a season.

Year 1: stood last and nearest the door when the court's hearth was lit. Has not taken the guest for the awaited one and has not refused her. Keeps the beacon, tells her of strangers as he said he would, chooses her escort. Told the seat that the flames near her dip when she is afraid and that a guest who is afraid in the dark is a thing the wall must think about. Sent no spears against her and none out for her.

Year 2 (end): about fifty-three winters. In month 1, in the yard of the watch, told the guest what he distrusts (the dipping flames, a watch split between two lamps, and asking that is done without an order) and what he would have her do or not do (no flame within the watch's yards, ask the wall nothing but through him, tell him the days she walks beyond the gate). Named as the one honest thing: let the wall hold her door. From the first night of winter two men of the watch a night, four on the three named nights, sit her door, not from kept households. Asked for two of his men in the salt party and was refused. Gave a half score as escort in spring. Has not taken the guest for the awaited and has not refused her.

Year 3 (end): about fifty-four winters. In month 1, day 5, in the yard of the watch, heard the guest keep his four rules and tell the four men who sit her door what they sit against, and said it was the answer he asked for at the start of last year, given at the start of this; had the captain tell every man who sat her door, before his first night, in her words. Asked again in month 2 for two of his men as rowers for the harbor party, and was refused again. Ordered the watch to note the hour of each dimming of the star from month 5. Tested the wall's spearheads in spring and found four in ten of the soft iron bent against one in ten of the older, and issued the old heads. Has not taken the guest for the awaited and has not refused her.

Year 4 (end): about fifty-five winters. In month 1, day 4, in the yard of the watch, heard the guest answer the captain and the one of the four, asked which house was nearest to a broken body and had no answer of hers he can say, and carried the part about the break to the seat himself. Had the way to the gate staked. Asked a third time in month 2 for two of his men as rowers and was refused, and said he would not ask a fourth. In month 7 gave his answer on the days of her absence: tell him the day and the road and the day she means to be back, and the wall holds her door empty a month past the day and then says so to the seat, sends no spear after and none with. Gave a half score as escort to the hamlets. Tested the wall's spearheads in spring and found the old heads fewer. Has not taken the guest for the awaited and has not refused her.
