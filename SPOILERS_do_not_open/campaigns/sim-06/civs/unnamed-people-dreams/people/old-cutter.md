# The old cutter

Unnamed. No mortal has said his name aloud in play.

A man of about sixty-one winters at the end of Year 3. The senior cutter at the scar, who asked the guest in Year 1 whether she knew who made the lowest cuts, and in Year 2 said "Then we are no wiser than we were." He is not sure what he saw when she looked at the three cuts and says he is not.

Year 3: with the Steward of the Store judged what stone could be spared to the harbor, over four days at the scar and the wall's footings. Held that the wall's winter footing takes four parts in five of the dry months' cutting and the fifth is what the scar can spare in a year, and that no more should be promised, for the wall is the town's and the sea is not. Named about a boat's load already stacked beyond the footing's need as the first. Chose the journeyman who went with the salt party. Says the picks wear twice as fast on the soft iron.

Year 4 (end): about sixty-two winters. Walked the stacks at the scar and the footings with the Steward over three days in month 2, held the fifth of last summer's cutting to the cord, replaced four short blocks from the stack beyond the footing, and said no more than a fifth should be promised. Says the picks wear twice as fast on the soft iron and the harbor's stone will cost the town more in iron than it knows. The cutters left the ring of stones round the three lowest cuts as it was.

Year 5 (end): about sixty-three winters. Judged the stone over three days in month 2 with the Steward; held that forty blocks go and twenty be held at the scar against the picks. Has one pick in three of the old iron.
