# The Night Sitter and her pupil

Both unnamed. No mortal has said either name aloud in play.

The Night Sitter: a woman of the lower town, about fifty-four winters at the end of Year 1. By sitting through the night with a low song she steadies the breathing of the very sick and of a child in fever. It does not cure. She cannot sleep for three nights after and her hands shake; done often, she wears out, and a winter of it is wearing her. Practical, dry, not much moved by gods.

Her pupil: a young woman of about nineteen, who came by sitting beside her and found she could. Quieter than the Night Sitter and more afraid of the work.

Year 1: the guest sat beside the Night Sitter through a night in winter with a child in fever and did not work, at her own asking. The Night Sitter says the guest kept the leave she asked for and that she liked the company. Neither has taken a flame for the other's house; both keep one.

Year 2 (end): about fifty-five winters; her pupil about twenty. In month 5 sat with the guest through a night and told her the work takes three nights' sleep and her hands. Declined her offer to keep her household's sleep. In month 6, after sitting three nights of a fever in the lower town, her hands shook so that she spilled the broth and her pupil went to the Warden of the Door and asked for her; the household was kept on the thirteenth night, one of that night's two. She said the girl was a fool and she would not say no. What came of it is in the report.

Year 3 (end): about fifty-six winters; her pupil about twenty-one. In month 4, day 20, sat with the guest in her work yard and took her hands for the kettle and the lamp, and said she was good with a kettle. Said a sitter is not taught; a girl sits and finds she can or does not. The seat's crier called for young women to sit beside her; nine came, five came back, three sat the winter. Whether any found she could is the Keeper's. Still cannot sleep for three nights after a sitting; her hands shook.
