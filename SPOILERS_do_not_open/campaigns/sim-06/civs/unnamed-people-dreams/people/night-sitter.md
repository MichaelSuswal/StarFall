# The Night Sitter and her pupil

Both unnamed. No mortal has said either name aloud in play.

The Night Sitter: a woman of the lower town, about fifty-four winters at the end of Year 1. By sitting through the night with a low song she steadies the breathing of the very sick and of a child in fever. It does not cure. She cannot sleep for three nights after and her hands shake; done often, she wears out, and a winter of it is wearing her. Practical, dry, not much moved by gods.

Her pupil: a young woman of about nineteen, who came by sitting beside her and found she could. Quieter than the Night Sitter and more afraid of the work.

Year 1: the guest sat beside the Night Sitter through a night in winter with a child in fever and did not work, at her own asking. The Night Sitter says the guest kept the leave she asked for and that she liked the company. Neither has taken a flame for the other's house; both keep one.
