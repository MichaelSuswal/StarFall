# What the people now say, end of Year 3

These are the people's own tellings. They explain causes, and are wrong about something that matters in at least one of them; I do not read the Keeper's truth, so where the error lies is for the Keeper to mark. Items kept from earlier years are marked as such.

## About the guest
- That the guest with the lamp has come, as the first of the seated line promised in a dream, and that she came on the first morning because the dark had a keeper who had waited long enough. The old tale never named a hind, and some on the Gate Bench say so. (Year 1, still told.)
- That the empty place and its lamp were kept for her, and that the Warden of the Door carried the table to her. (Year 1.)
- That her lamp holds off fever and the night. A house with the flame is a house no sick child dies in the dark of. People died in kept houses again this winter, about fifteen; the households say "kept and not lost". (Year 1, still told.)
- That she walks in the sleep of the dying and leaves her body standing pale in the yard, slow and without voice, and that it can be moved. The households who say she is a traveller's ghost say this is the proof of it, that a ghost's body is where it fell. The wall's men do not say what they have seen. (Year 3.)
- That she is a giant's kin. The cutters say she looked at the lowest cuts as one reads a name, or as one looks at a stranger's hand, and they disagree, and the ring of stones round three cuts is said to be for her. (Year 2, still told.)
- That she is afraid, and that her fear makes the flames dip. The wall watch say it is at the same hour each dawn and they have stopped being surprised. (Year 1, still told.) This year the wall's men say she told them herself what they sit against, and that it is what they had seen.
- That she asks and does not order, and that this makes her a true guest. The Warden of the Wall says a thing done without an order is a thing no one can be held to, and the watch repeat it. (Year 2, still told.)
- That she does not see what happens in the town unless she is told. She went to the houses of the four who came home from the west as though they had not, and the town says a keeper of the dark who cannot tell the living from the dead is a keeper of the dark and no more. Others say she asked after them to be sure, and that she does not take a thing on a rumor. (Year 3.)
- That the flame may be passed from niche to niche by anyone, and that she bade it so. It is told as if the guest had said it of every flame, and the Bench's ruling that a flame is lit only at a household's own asking is told as the Bench's, and not hers. (Year 3.)

## About the strange things of the year
- The star dimmed on six nights, fourteen in all since she came. The cord says five of the fourteen had a dying in the town. The town says: it dims for the dying of any house; it dims when she is afraid or asleep; it dims when she is awake and in the yard; it dims when she is in a hamlet and the hamlet is under it too. The young say that the star pays for the flames: that every kept niche takes a little from the star, and the roll rose from three hundred and thirty to four hundred and fifteen to five hundred and twenty as the nights went three, five, six. The old say it dimmed in their grandfathers' time on nights when nothing happened, and that more nights are not different nights. The Keeper of the Count says two things that rise together are two things that rise together and the cord does not say why. Everyone has read the cord and everyone has a reason.
- That the iron went soft because the star dims, and the blue gets into it. The smiths say the mining town is sending its iron down before it is worked enough. The lowest streets say a god's lamp is near iron and iron goes soft near a god. The Warden of the Wall says it is the iron and not the guest, and says it so that no one will say otherwise of him. The carriers say they carry what they are given.
- That the west is empty: the party that went to the hills saw no one, and the town says no one lives there. This is told as certain. Four people walked and did not see. (Year 2, still told.)
- That the harbor sells salt cheap because the sea gives it free, and that the carriers keep the price up. (Year 2.) This year: that the harbor house wants the scar's stone because it is the giants' stone and holds against the sea, and that the old giants' scar will feed the town. The cutters are proud of it and the old cutter says the harbor wants stone and has not said what for.
- That the oldest of the barrow singers has given the third song as far as he can, and two young households have two parts in three, and that no one has asked the guest about them. (Year 2 and 3.)

## About their rulers
- That the seat speaks only what she has weighed, and that she held the bargain with the harbor as the town's and not the guest's, and that a ruler who says so is a ruler.
- That the old Warden of the Door chose the lower town's woman because she was the one who kept the table when he was away, and that she keeps two lamps and two minds. Both are told. This year it is added that she says she is nearer, and no one has asked to what.
- That the Warden of the Wall asked the guest the same thing a year ago and has his answer, and that he is not easy with it. He says the wall holds what sleeps within it.
- That the Steward of the Store sent eight because the stone is heavy and sent no spears because the seat said so, and that he does not trust the sea.
- That the Gate Bench fined two fathers a day's labor for their lads over a yard wall and was right to, and that it held a flame is lit at a household's asking and in no other way.
- That the Keeper of the Count waited a year for one line and read the two cords at the turn of the year and would not say what they meant, and that this was wise.
- That the old cutter said no more than a fifth of a year's stone should go to the harbor because the wall is the town's and the sea is not.

## About neighbors
- That the court has sent a second party to the harbor, with stone, and that it is the guest's doing and the court's own. The town says the party will bring salt or the terms for it. Nothing is known in the town beyond that at the year's end except as the Keeper's pass two says.
- That the carriers know more than they say. This is said of carriers in any year. This year it is said they are more careful about the iron than the salt.

## About themselves
- That the Night Sitter and the guest sit well together, and that the guest is good with a kettle. The Night Sitter says she liked the company and that she is not under a fear. She says a sitter is not taught, a girl sits and finds she can or does not, and most go home the first night. Sitter A: that a girl of a hamlet found she could and the Night Sitter sleeps more. Sitter B: that none found it, and the Night Sitter says so and says no shame to any.
- That the Dusk Table sets the guest's place now, and that a town that counts its own dim nights is a town that has begun to count its gods. (Year 2, still told.)
