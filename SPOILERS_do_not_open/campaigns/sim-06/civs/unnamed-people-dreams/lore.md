# What the people now say, end of Year 2

These are the people's own tellings. They explain causes, and are wrong about something that matters in at least one of them; I do not read the Keeper's truth, so where the error lies is for the Keeper to mark. Items kept from Year 1 are marked as such.

## About the guest
- That the guest with the lamp has come, as the first of the seated line promised in a dream, and that she came on the first morning because the dark had a keeper who had waited long enough. The old tale never named a hind, and some on the Gate Bench say so. (Year 1, still told.)
- That the empty place and its lamp were kept for her, and that the Warden of the Door carried the table to her. (Year 1.)
- That her lamp holds off fever and the night. A house with the flame is a house no sick child dies in the dark of. People died in kept houses again this winter; the households say "kept and not lost". (Year 1, still told.)
- That she said from the wall she does not know why the star dims. The headmen and the Night Sitter say this is a guest who will not cheat the table. Some of the kept households say a keeper of the dark should know her own dark. In the lowest streets: that she did not say no, and a keeper that does not say no has said yes.
- That she is a giant's kin. The cutters say she looked at the lowest cuts as one reads a name, or as one looks at a stranger's hand, and they disagree, and the ring of stones round three cuts is said to be for her.
- That she is afraid, and that her fear makes the flames dip. The wall watch say it is at the same hour each dawn and they have stopped being surprised.
- That she asks and does not order, and that this makes her a true guest. The Warden of the Wall says a thing done without an order is a thing no one can be held to, and the watch repeat it.
- That the lamp cannot be carried from the town: the court sent a party for salt with no flame and no ember, and the town says it was because the flame is the guest's and does not go where she does not. This is told as the reason and no one has asked the Warden of the Door, who sent it so for another reason.

## About the strange things of the year
- The star dimmed on five nights, eight in all since she came. The cord says three of the eight had a dying in the town. The town says: it dims for the kept dying (the old man of month 6); it dims for the dying of any house (the childbed woman of month 11); it dims when she is afraid or asleep; it dims when she is awake and in the yard; it dims when she is in a hamlet and the hamlet is under it too, so she has the whole country in her. The old say it dimmed in their grandfathers' time on nights when nothing happened and that more nights are not different nights. The Keeper of the Count says the cord does not say why. Everyone has read the cord and everyone has a reason.
- That the west is empty: the party that went to the hills saw no one, and the town says no one lives there. This is told as certain. Four people walked and did not see.
- That the harbor sells salt cheap because the sea gives it free, and that the carriers keep the price up. The sailors' word is that the harbor's price is far less than the carriers ask. The town does not know why.

## About their rulers
- That the seat speaks only what she has weighed and that she added the Count's open door to the guest's word herself.
- That the old Warden of the Door chose the lower town's woman because she was the one who kept the table when he was away, and that she keeps two lamps and two minds. Both are told.
- That the Warden of the Wall put his men at the guest's door because he is afraid of her, and that he does it to keep her in. He says the wall holds what sleeps within it.
- That the Steward of the Store chose the salt party's leader for his counting and sent six because he does not trust the coast.
- That the Gate Bench fined two men a day's labor and was right to.
- That the Keeper of the Count read the cord at the turn of the year and would not say what it meant, and this was wise.

## About neighbors
- That the court has sent its own party to the harbor for the salt. It is said the guest bade it and the court was not slow to. The town says the party will bring salt or the knowledge of it. Nothing is known in the town beyond that at the year's end except as the Keeper's pass two says.
- That the carriers know more than they say. This is said of carriers in any year.

## About themselves
- That the Night Sitter and the guest sit well together. The Night Sitter says she liked the company and that she is not under a fear. A: that the guest kept her house one night and she slept. B: that the guest kept her house one night and she did not, and she says so.
- That the barrow songs stand at three of five, and two young households have half of the third from the oldest singer. No one has asked the guest about them.
- That the Dusk Table sets the guest's place now, and that a town that counts its own dim nights is a town that has begun to count its gods.
