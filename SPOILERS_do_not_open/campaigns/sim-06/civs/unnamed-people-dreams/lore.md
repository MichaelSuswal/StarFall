# What the people now say, end of Year 1

These are the people's own tellings. They explain causes, and are wrong about something that matters in at least one of them; I do not read the Keeper's truth, so where the error lies is for the Keeper to mark.

## About the guest
- That the guest with the lamp has come, as the first of the seated line promised in a dream, and that she came on the first morning because the dark had a keeper who had waited long enough. The old tale never named a hind, and some on the Gate Bench say so.
- That the empty place and its lamp were kept for her, and that she sat in it. They do not forget that the Warden of the Door carried the table to her.
- That her lamp holds off fever and the night. Households that keep the flame say a house with the flame is a house that no sick child dies in the dark of. People died in kept houses this winter; the households explain these as deaths "kept and not lost".
- In the lowest streets: that she is the keeper of the dark come to the table at last, and that a keeper who comes to a table has come to be fed and will want something. They keep the ancestors' lamps lit from the old flame and say so in low voices.
- That she is a giant's kin, since the stone cutters have heard of the giants' lowest cuts and she is large. This is told by the cutters, half in jest.
- That she is afraid, and that her fear makes the flames dip. This is true as seen. They explain it as the dark pressing on her in the night, and some say she is afraid for them. They take the fear to be the dark's work and a sign that it is stronger than she is.

## About her way
- That she asks and does not order. The headmen and the Night Sitter say so most. They say this makes her a true guest, and that a god would not ask. Others say a god asks first.
- That a dream is a dream and not a command. The court says so from the wall. Households sit up for orders anyway.

## About the strange things of the year
- The star dimmed on three nights, two in the first month and one in the middle of winter. Most of the town says the star dims for the guest: bowing to her, or for the dying, since an old woman died in a kept house on the third night. The old who remember such nights in their grandfathers' time say it dimmed on nights when nothing happened, and are listened to and not followed. She did not tell anyone the dimmings were hers.

## About their rulers
- That the seat held the table and the line's word, and did not claim the guest's voice. They say the seat is wise and slow.
- That the Warden of the Door moved the table, and did not move the guest. This is told as the best thing he has done.
- That the Warden of the Wall is a hard man who did not break the custom. Some say he is afraid. He says he is the wall.
- That the Gate Bench had the sense to say no house is to be shamed.
- That the Steward of the Store spoke the salt aloud in the council, and that it was good that someone did.

## About neighbours
- That the harbor towns have been told of the guest and have asked what she is. The carriers said so. The town takes this to mean that the harbor towns are anxious and that the price of salt will rise because of it. Whether the harbor towns' price rose for that reason is not known to the town.
- That the runners sent west and down the river will bring salt or the knowledge of it. Everyone says so and no one knows.

## About themselves
- That the Dusk Table has grown a place. The streets set the extra place for the guest now, not for the awaited one.
- That the barrow songs have not changed, three of five. No one has asked the guest about them.
