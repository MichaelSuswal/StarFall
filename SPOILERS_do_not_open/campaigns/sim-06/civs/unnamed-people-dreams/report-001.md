# Report 001: unnamed people of god-dreams, Year 1, for the Keeper

## How the god was met
Autumn, month 1, the first morning. Two of the wall watch at the south-east gate saw her come down from the stony ground. They did not open the gate wide and did not drive her off. One ran for the Warden of the Door. Asked who she was, she answered as her decisions fix: called Vael, woke on the stony ground, did not know why, came to be a guest. She waited most of a morning in plain sight. The Warden of the Door walked out, gave her the custom's words, and carried his own Dusk Table out to the court's guest house yard because she cannot pass a low door. He took her. The gate takes her width.

Autumn, the three nights. She was watched and spoke of nothing. The Warden of the Wall came once, looked, left. On the third night she was asked her business and answered as fixed, and said the lamp makes a flame that may be set in a niche, lit only where asked, not a due. The Warden of the Door took her for the awaited one that night and told the seat alone.

Autumn, the court. The seat heard the offices one at a time. Warden of the Door: for her. Warden of the Wall: a guest is a guest, he keeps the wall as it was. Steward of the Store: the bread is the court's and the count his. Keeper of the Count: asked what a household must do to ask for a flame. Gate Bench: at first one for, one against, one undecided; by winter the undecided one came round, two for and one against. The seat let her stay on the court's bread after three nights and spoke the custom from the wall (asks only where asked, none shamed). Late autumn: the court's hearth was lit from her lamp, and the seat then said she took the guest for the awaited one. Households began to ask from that day.

Winter. Steward of the Store counted grain, fuel and salt aloud before the council at her asking and took her word thereafter, grudgingly. Keeper of the Count kept the lots and the roll and took her word from the first lot. The Night Sitter sat with her through a night. The Gate Bench ruled no house is to be shamed for not keeping the flame.

Spring. She went out with an escort of about twenty of the wall watch to the nearest hamlets. Headmen heard her and some kept the flame. One woman would not come to the field's edge; the guest did not ask.

Summer. The stone cutters sat with her; the Warden of the Wall walked the wall with her and named its lacks. Runners were sent out.

Where each office stands at the year's end: Warden of the Door, taken her word. The seat, taken it since late autumn. Keeper of the Count, taken it. Steward of the Store, taken it grudgingly. Warden of the Wall, has not. Gate Bench, split, agreed on the custom of the table.

## What was done in the god's name
By the Warden of the Door (after the third night): spoke for her to the seat, carried the table, took her to the Night Sitter, asked that the court's hearth be lit, put two court runners at the door of her lean-to on the nights of Keeping.
By the seat (after late autumn): spoke her custom from the wall, lit the court's hearth, let her stay on the court's bread, sent the runners out in summer on her asking.
By the Keeper of the Count: drew the lots for Keeping Sleep (two households a night from those who had asked) and kept the roll of households that asked, about 330 at the year's end.
By the Steward of the Store: counted the winter aloud before the council, gave stone and timber for the wall repairs.
By the Warden of the Wall: kept the beacon watch as it was, sent no spears out, chose her escort, tells her of strangers (none came). He did these as the wall's own work and not as her helper.
By the court's runners: carried the word to the hamlets that she lights only where asked.

## Uncertain outcomes for the Keeper
1. The western party. Four of the court's runners, no spears, set out in month 11 (second month of summer) for the Rudd Hills [r18] by the road, carrying linen and pots as gifts and a word that the town keeps a table for travellers. Their orders: learn what neighbours lie there and what salt they have, bring back no quarrel. Success: they reach neighbours, are received, learn of salt, and return. Failure: they are refused, lost, taken ill, or a quarrel starts. Circumstances from my record: the Rudd Hills are reported only, brown hills to the west; no people there are known by name to the town; the town's record gives no road time to them.
2. The river party. Four runners set out in month 11 by boat down the River Ellow [f07] toward its mouth. The record gives the mouth as 112.4 miles, 4 days downstream by boat; the way back is upstream and is the Keeper's to price. The same orders and gifts. Success and failure as above; a boat in a swell on a river in the dry months is the risk.
Neither party was back when I closed the year. If they come back before the next autumn opens, the court has their word, which I do not write; if they do not, the court waits, the Steward asks the carriers, and the anxiety over salt deepens. I wrote the year's end without either result and the Keeper should apply it in pass two.

## What turns on a power
Everything below is written only as seen and said by mortals. I did not settle what any power did.
1. The lamp's flame. A flame lit from her lamp, or from one lit from it, sits in a household niche and the household calls it kept. The court's hearth carries one. Whether the court's hearth counts as a household kept and within the seven is the god's question to the Keeper (decisions, ruling 1).
2. Keeping Sleep. Two households a night from the lot, drawn by the Keeper of the Count from those who asked and were not kept. On those nights she lay still in the yard with the lamp lit and two runners held the door of the lean-to. At dawn she was shaken and the flames near her dipped. She said when asked that she was afraid. Whether the fear counts, and whether grief at a deathbed is the fear of the night's sleep, is the god's question (decisions, ruling 2). The seventh place was left empty.
3. Walking dreams in kept houses, winter only. About a dozen deathbeds in kept households. The households say the dying one was quiet and some spoke of a hind or a lamp. Nothing cured anyone.
4. The flames near her dipping with her fear. The wall watch and the Warden of the Wall saw it.
5. The three dimmings of the star. Two in month 1, one in month 5. The people say it is hers. I do not know. If it was hers, the Keeper should tell the next pass.
6. Whether any of what the households report of the kept dying is a thing the power did or only a thing they say.

## What touches anyone else
- The salt carriers from the harbor towns came twice (late autumn, spring), ate the three nights, saw the guest, and carried word of her to the harbor towns, who asked through them what she is. This is the first the harbor towns hear of her, by this road.
- Two parties of court runners are out (see above). They are the only people of the town who left it this year.
- No stranger came to the gate other than the god. No trade, raid, migration or message beyond the above.

## Asked of the god
In the mortals' words as the people repeat them, and whether answered in front of them. Where her decisions fix an answer I say so; elsewhere I do not write one.
- Watch at the gate: "Who are you?" Answered: she gave her name, that she woke on the stony ground, did not know why, and came to be a guest.
- Warden of the Door at the gate: "What is your name?" Answered, the same.
- Warden of the Door on the third night: "What is your business?" Answered as fixed in her decisions.
- Warden of the Door: "What is that lamp?" Answered: it is a lamp, and it makes a flame that may be set in a niche, lit only where asked, not a due.
- Warden of the Door: "Are you the guest the town keeps the place for?" The Warden of the Door says she did not say yes and did not say no. I have no word of hers.
- The wall watch and the court: "Why do the flames dip?" Answered as her decisions fix: she is afraid.
- Households: "May we have the flame?" Answered by her lighting it, as asked.
- Stone cutters: "Do you know who made the lowest cuts?" and "Are you a giant's kin?" The cutters say she looked at the cuts a long time. No answer I can write.
- Households (after the third dimming): "Does the star dim for the dying?" Not answered in front of them that I know.
- The seat's court (from the wall): "Is a dream a command?" Answered by the guest and the seat alike: no.

## Names said aloud
Vael, spoken by the guest herself, to the gate watch and to the Warden of the Door on the first morning, and again on the third night. No mortal said a name aloud for the people, the home, or any office holder. The people call her "the guest" and "the guest with the lamp"; some in the lowest streets call her "the keeper". These are not names.
