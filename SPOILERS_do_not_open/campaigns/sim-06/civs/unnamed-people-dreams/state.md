# Unnamed people of god-dreams: state at the end of Year 4

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent after Year 4 (the year closes as autumn opens again). One thing turns on the Keeper. Where a number turns on the harbor party with the stone and the guest's word, Salt A is the party home before the hard cold with salt and Salt B is the party not home until spring. Unmarked numbers are the same either way. The Keeper's pass two chooses.

population: about 5,080 (Salt A about 5,082, Salt B about 5,075): about 3,040 in the walled town [f50], about 2,040 in hamlets. Births about 195, deaths about 175 (Salt A about 173, Salt B about 180). No one arrived. Nine left in the harbor party; they are counted in the people either way. Deaths: about fifty winter fevers and cold among the old and small, about eleven of them in the week of the hard frost in month 6, about eighteen of the winter deaths in kept houses, the rest age (among them the oldest of the barrow singers), the river, farm and quarry accidents and childbed. Why it moved: an ordinary year and winter, one hard frost late, no flood loss; the hamlet girl as a third sitter eased the frost week a little; Salt B loses a few more to meat gone early.

stores: grain in after an ordinary harvest, threshing done by midwinter, enough to reach the next harvest with a thin margin, as the three years before. Fuel enough. Oil enough. Salt: Salt A, a load in hand from the harbor from late autumn, the late pigs fully cured and some over, and the carriers' spring load bought small; Salt B, none by the second month of winter, the late pigs cured lightly and the meat gone early, and only what the party brought in spring. Iron: the stock of the older iron put by for spearheads and the cutters' picks is about a third lower than a year ago; the new iron stays soft and the smiths draw it out oftener. Why: the carriers' two small loads at last year's price in both; the party's load in Salt A from late autumn, in Salt B only from spring; the picks wear twice as fast on the soft iron.

treasure: middling, a little down again: cut stone, linen, pots and oil. The court spent twenty-five bolts of linen, sixty pots, twelve jars of flax oil, the sixty blocks of the scar's fifth part of last year's cutting, and the cutters' days on the harbor party, and bread and the bed for the hamlet girl. Some bronze in the court's keeping, untouched. No gift came in. What the harbor gave for the stone and the terms of it are the Keeper's. Why: the stone and the party cost as last year, the iron wears faster, and the guest's other asks cost bread and time and no coin.

trade: linen, pots, flax oil, cut stone and grain out; salt and iron in, dear, and the iron worse than before. The salt carriers came twice, late autumn and spring, at last year's price. The court's second sending of stone to the harbor's order, a third boat borrowed from the fishing households, the guest's word to the delta carried with it; what the harbor answered and where the word went are the Keeper's. The bargain the court holds is for no more than a fifth of a year's cutting. The quay roll of parties out was corrected in month 1 and is now read over by the quay keeper and a runner together.

strength: about 600 by beacon, about 200 of them on the wall watch by turns, unchanged in number. Two men of the watch a night sit the guest's door in winter and spring, four on the three named nights, by turns and lot, not from kept houses; every man told by the captain before his first night, in her words, what he sits against and what the standing body is. None asked off. The way from the guest's lean-to to the south-east gate is staked and kept clear. The old spearheads are fewer: the Warden of the Wall issued them to the watch, set the soft heads by for the beacon stores, and says the stock of the older iron will not last another year at this wear. A half score of the watch walked with the guest to the hamlets in spring. No spears went out.

mood: settled and hospitable, uneasy under the star and under the word of a second god in the delta. Seven dim nights this year, twenty-one in all. Salt still the town's worst want (Salt A, eased; Salt B, not). The guest's saying to the wall's men how she may be broken has been told in the streets as a promise that she cannot be killed, and the lowest streets near the yard have begun to keep their lamps bright; the guest's saying sorry at the second house's door and asking whose word a thing was has been liked; her asking the court what it would do if she were away has frightened some and steadied the court. The death of the oldest barrow singer has grieved the storytelling households and the lower town and divided them on whether the guest should have been asked. Two lads and a yard wall were fined again. The Gate Bench has kept the custom of no house shamed. Nothing has broken.

households keeping the flame (the Keeper of the Count's roll): about 590 of about 1,020: about 430 in the town and about 160 in the hamlets. The court's hearth is one. About twenty households let the flame go this year, none for a clear cause. About 110 of the roll are shrines lit from a kept flame since the shrine word of Year 3. The seventh place of the guest's keeping is empty. Keeping Sleep ran at two households a night, three on three named nights in deep winter (month 5 night 16, month 6 night 2, month 6 night 24).

dim nights, the Keeper of the Count's cord: twenty-one in all, three of Year 1 (remembered), five of Year 2, six of Year 3 and seven of Year 4 (counted). This year: month 2 night 14 (no lot, she awake in the yard, no dying); month 3 night 23 (no lot, awake in the yard, no dying); month 4 night 7 (lot night, she lying still in the yard, an old man died in a kept house); month 8 night 17 (no lot, awake in a western hamlet a morning's walk from the wall, hamlet and town both saw it, no dying in the town); month 10 night 5 (no lot, awake in the yard, no dying); month 11 night 19 (lot night, lying still, an old woman died in a house not kept); month 12 night 11 (no lot, awake in the yard, no dying). A dying fell in the town on seven of the twenty-one. The Keeper of the Count now writes lying and not asleep for her on a lot night, on her own answer in the yard of the watch. The wall's watch notes the hour: about an hour, in the middle of the night, each time.

helpers (who has taken the god's word): the old Warden of the Door, from the third night of Year 1; the seat; the Keeper of the Count; the Steward of the Store, grudgingly; the new Warden of the Door, who said to the seat in month 7 that she takes the guest for the awaited and carries her asks faithfully. The Warden of the Wall has not, and keeps her door as the wall's own work. The Gate Bench is split, two for and one against, and agrees on the custom of the table. A rule for the guest's absence, as the four offices gave it in month 7, is held by the guest and by the Keeper of the Count's cord.

offices: no office changed hands.

## Keeper, Year 1, pass two
The river party reached people at the river's mouth, was received and came home in month 12 with word of salt [r-0251]. The western party found no one and had not come home at the year's end [r-0250]; it comes in early in Year 2.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The salt party: branch B [r-0294]. It reached the harbor, was received, bought salt at the harbor's price, wintered there and came home in month 8 with the salt and a spoken answer. The second lean winter for salt stands.
- The Night Sitter did not sleep on the kept night (branch B).
- The flame a household lit from its own kept flame is a shrine, not a third household; the cap of two was kept.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- The harbor party did not come home before the cold. A boat grounded on a bar under its stone; no ship lay at the mouth; they went round the shore with the samples, were given bread and three nights, and the leader spoke the words on the third night. The house took the samples, sold salt at a standing price below the carriers', and named its order: sixty blocks a year dressed to a cord it gave, to be lifted by its own ship at the river's mouth. The gales shut the shore. They wintered at the harbor and came up at high water, home in month 8, all eight. Your numbers take the branch with no word all winter.
- At the harbor the leader heard this on the quays and brought it home: at the market town in the western delta a stranger who reads scales for a pinch of barley has been seated as keeper of the weights, and says before the market that it is a god.
- Of the three who sat the winter beside the Night Sitter, the hamlet girl found she could. Your numbers take that branch.
- At the dawns after three households the wall's men saw what the guest had told them they would see, and no more.
