# Unnamed people of god-dreams: state at the end of Year 5

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent after Year 5 (the year closes as autumn opens again). Two things turn on the Keeper. Party A is the harbor party home before the hard cold with salt; Party B is the party not home until spring. Walk A is the guest at the wall by the day named; Walk B is the guest not yet home at the year's end. Unmarked numbers are the same either way. The Keeper's pass two chooses.

population: about 5,095 (Party A about 5,097, Party B about 5,092): about 3,050 in the walled town [f50], about 2,045 in hamlets. Births about 197, deaths about 182 (Party A about 180, Party B about 185). No one arrived. Seven left in the harbor party; they are counted in the people either way. Deaths: about forty-five winter fevers and cold among the old and small, about eleven of them in the week of the hard frost in month 6, about nineteen of the winter deaths in kept houses, the rest age, the river, farm and quarry accidents and childbed. Why it moved: an ordinary year and winter, one hard frost late, no flood loss; the hamlet girl as a third sitter eased the frost week again; Party B loses a few more to meat gone early.

stores: grain in after an ordinary harvest, threshing done by midwinter, enough to reach the next harvest with a thin margin, as the four years before. Fuel enough. Oil enough. Salt: Party A, a small load in hand from the harbor from late autumn, the late pigs cured as they ought, and the carriers' spring load bought small; Party B, none by the second month of winter, the late pigs cured lightly and the meat gone early, and only what the party brought in spring. Iron: the stock of the older iron put by for spearheads and picks is about a quarter lower again than a year ago; the new iron stays soft and the smiths draw it out oftener; the cutters have about one pick in three of the old iron. Twenty blocks of the scar's stone are held at the scar against the picks. Why: the carriers' two small loads at last year's price in both; the party's load in Party A from late autumn, in Party B only from spring; the picks wear twice as fast.

treasure: middling, a little down again: cut stone, linen, pots and oil. The court spent twenty-seven bolts of linen, fifty-six pots, twelve jars of flax oil (gifts for the factor's table and for a carrier of the guest's word among them), the forty blocks of the fifth of last summer's cutting, and the cutters' days on the harbor party. Bread and the measure of grain for the hamlet girl's household. Some bronze in the court's keeping, untouched. No gift came in. What the harbor gave for the stone and the terms of it are the Keeper's. Why: the stone and the party cost as last year, the iron wears faster, and the guest's other asks cost bread and time and no coin.

trade: linen, pots, flax oil, cut stone and grain out; salt and iron in, dear, and the iron no better. The salt carriers came twice, late autumn and spring, at last year's price. The court's third sending of stone to the harbor's order, forty blocks in two boats; what the harbor answered, what became of the stone that lay at the mouth, and where the guest's word went are the Keeper's. The bargain the court holds is for no more than a fifth of a year's cutting. The quay roll of parties out is struck by the quay keeper and by the west road gate keeper, and read over.

strength: about 600 by beacon, about 200 of them on the wall watch by turns, unchanged in number. Two men of the watch a night sit the guest's door in winter, four on the three named nights, by lot, not from kept houses; every man told by the captain before his first night, in her words, what he sits against, and each lot night a tablet is written and given to the Keeper of the Count at dawn. None asked off. Four of the watch went of themselves with the guest on her walk, not spears. The old spearheads are fewer again and the soft heads are set by for the beacon stores. No spears went out.

mood: settled and hospitable, uneasy under the star, under the word of a second god in the delta, and now under the guest's absence in the summer. Three dim nights this year, twenty-four in all, the fewest since Year 1; the town says the star rests, or that she was less afraid, and the Keeper of the Count says the cord does not say why. Salt still the town's worst want (Party A, eased; Party B, not). The guest's saying "I do not know" of her lying body has been told in the streets both as honesty and as a god not knowing her own body; the lowest streets keep their lamps bright and say she may come back lacking. The guest's going to the barrow and listening to the two parts to the end has been liked among the singers; the third part is gone from every hamlet. Two lads were not fined this year; one man was fined a day's labor for saying "the guest says" of a thing she had not said. The Gate Bench has kept the custom of no house shamed. Nothing has broken.

households keeping the flame (the Keeper of the Count's roll): about 605 of about 1,030: about 440 in the town and about 165 in the hamlets. The court's hearth is one. About fifteen households let the flame go this year and about nine of them asked it back. About 125 of the roll are shrines lit from a kept flame since the shrine word of Year 3. The seventh place of the guest's keeping is empty. Keeping Sleep ran at two households a night, three on three named nights in deep winter (month 5 night 14, month 6 night 3, month 6 night 25), and two a night by the Keeper of the Count's deputy while the guest was away.

dim nights, the Keeper of the Count's cord: twenty-four in all, three of Year 1 (remembered), five of Year 2, six of Year 3, seven of Year 4 and three of Year 5 (counted). This year: month 2 night 9 (no lot, she awake in the yard, no dying); month 2 night 22 (lot night, lying still in the yard, an old woman died in a kept house); month 7 night 15 (no lot, awake in the yard, no dying). A dying fell in the town on eight of the twenty-four. The wall's watch notes the hour: about an hour, in the middle of the night, each time. The wall's tablets of the lot nights lie beside the cord.

helpers (who has taken the god's word): the old Warden of the Door; the seat; the Keeper of the Count; the Steward of the Store, grudgingly; the new Warden of the Door. The Warden of the Wall has not and has not refused, and keeps her door as the wall's own work. The Gate Bench is split, two for and one against, and agrees on the custom of the table. The rule for the guest's absence, as the four offices gave it in Year 4, was kept in the summer by the Warden of the Door and the Keeper of the Count's deputy.

offices: no office changed hands. The Keeper of the Count has a deputy who drew the lots in the summer, a woman of the Count's.

## Keeper, Year 1, pass two
The river party reached people at the river's mouth, was received and came home in month 12 with word of salt [r-0251]. The western party found no one and had not come home at the year's end [r-0250]; it comes in early in Year 2.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The salt party: branch B [r-0294]. It reached the harbor, was received, bought salt at the harbor's price, wintered there and came home in month 8 with the salt and a spoken answer. The second lean winter for salt stands.
- The Night Sitter did not sleep on the kept night (branch B).
- The flame a household lit from its own kept flame is a shrine, not a third household; the cap of two was kept.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- The harbor party did not come home before the cold. A boat grounded on a bar under its stone; no ship lay at the mouth; they went round the shore with the samples, were given bread and three nights, and the leader spoke the words on the third night. The house took the samples, sold salt at a standing price below the carriers', and named its order: sixty blocks a year dressed to a cord it gave, to be lifted by its own ship at the river's mouth. The gales shut the shore. They wintered at the harbor and came up at high water, home in month 8, all eight. Your numbers take the branch with no word all winter.
- At the harbor the leader heard this on the quays and brought it home: at the market town in the western delta a stranger who reads scales for a pinch of barley has been seated as keeper of the weights, and says before the market that it is a god.
- Of the three who sat the winter beside the Night Sitter, the hamlet girl found she could. Your numbers take that branch.
- At the dawns after three households the wall's men saw what the guest had told them they would see, and no more.

## Keeper, Year 4, pass two
- The stone party: a boat grounded on a bar and three days were lost; no ship lay at the river's mouth in ten days; the stone was stacked above the high water. The leader and a pair of runners went round the shore to the harbor. The factor gave bread and three nights, sold salt for the linen and oil at the standing price, said his house's ship lifts the stone at the first fair weather after the gales, and would not have the Guest's word carried on to the delta. The leader turned for home. All nine were home in the second week of month 4 with a little salt. Your numbers take the branch nearest that: home before the hard cold, with little salt, the late pigs cured lightly.
- The Guest's word did not go on to the delta.
- The oldest barrow singer died in the third month; no one holds the third song.
