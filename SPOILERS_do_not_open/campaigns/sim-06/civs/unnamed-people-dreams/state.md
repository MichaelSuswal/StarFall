# Unnamed people of god-dreams: state at the end of Year 2

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent after Year 2 (the year closes as autumn opens again). Where a number turns on the salt party, A is the party home before the hard cold with salt and B is the party not home until spring; the Keeper's pass two chooses.

population: about 5,040: about 3,020 in the walled town [f50], about 2,020 in hamlets. Births about 190, deaths about 170 (A about 165, B about 175). No one arrived. Six left in the salt party; they are counted in the people either way. Deaths: about 45 winter fevers and cold among the old and small, the rest age, the river, farm and quarry accidents and childbed. About a dozen of the winter deaths were in kept households, as last year. Why it moved: an ordinary year, an ordinary winter, no flood loss.

stores: grain in after an ordinary harvest, threshing done by midwinter, enough to reach the next harvest with a thin margin, as last year. Fuel enough. Oil enough. Salt: A, a load in hand from the harbor, the late pigs fully cured and some over; B, very short again, the late pigs cured lightly and the meat gone early, and only what the party brought in spring. Why: the carriers' two small loads at the old price in both; the party's load in A from late autumn, in B only from spring.

treasure: middling, a little down: cut stone, linen, pots and oil. The court spent twenty bolts of linen, a hundred pots and fifteen jars of flax oil on the salt party, stone and timber on the river-side footing, and bread on the party's outfit. Some bronze in the court's keeping, untouched. No gift came in.

trade: linen, pots, flax oil, cut stone and grain out; salt and iron in, dear. The salt carriers came twice, late autumn and spring, at last year's price. Iron came down from the mining town as in other years. The court sent its own party to the harbor town (see the report); what it bought and at what price is the Keeper's.

strength: about 600 by beacon, about 200 of them on the wall watch by turns, unchanged. The river-side footing slumped after the spring water and was relaid in the dry months. Two men of the watch a night sit the guest's door in winter and spring, four on the three hardest nights, by turns and lot, not from kept households. One asked off and was let off. No spears went out.

mood: settled and hospitable, salt still the town's worst want (A, eased; B, not). The guest's answer from the wall and the tally have divided the town less than they might have and settled nothing: the headmen and the Night Sitter and most of the town call it honest; a number of kept households were let down; the lowest streets hold that she did not say no. Five dim nights this year and the cord laid out on the Count's table; each street reads it as it likes. The Gate Bench has kept the custom of no house shamed. A blow was struck in the lower town in month 6 and fined. Nothing has broken.

households keeping the guest's flame (the Keeper of the Count's roll): about 415 of about 1,000: about 300 in the town and about 115 in the hamlets. The court's hearth is one. About nine households let the flame go this year, five in the lowest streets after the answer from the wall, four for their own reasons. The seventh place of the guest's keeping is empty. Keeping Sleep ran at two households a night, three on three named nights in deep winter.

dim nights, the Keeper of the Count's cord: eight in all, three of Year 1 (remembered) and five of Year 2 (counted): month 2 night 9, month 6 night 20, month 9 night 3, month 11 night 15, month 12 night 27. A dying fell in the town on three of the eight: two in kept houses and one not.

helpers (who has taken the god's word): the old Warden of the Door, from the third night of Year 1; the seat; the Keeper of the Count; the Steward of the Store, grudgingly; the new Warden of the Door carries the guest's asks faithfully and has not said she takes her for the awaited. The Warden of the Wall has not, and keeps her door as the wall's own work. The Gate Bench is split, two for and one against, and agrees on the custom of the table.

offices: the old Warden of the Door named his successor in month 3; the seat named the office hers at the winter's first dusk. No other office changed hands.

## Keeper, Year 1, pass two
The river party reached people at the river's mouth, was received and came home in month 12 with word of salt [r-0251]. The western party found no one and had not come home at the year's end [r-0250]; it comes in early in Year 2.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The salt party: branch B [r-0294]. It reached the harbor, was received, bought salt at the harbor's price, wintered there and came home in month 8 with the salt and a spoken answer. The second lean winter for salt stands.
- The Night Sitter did not sleep on the kept night (branch B).
- The flame a household lit from its own kept flame is a shrine, not a third household; the cap of two was kept.
