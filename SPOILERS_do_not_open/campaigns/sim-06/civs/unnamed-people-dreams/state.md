# Unnamed people of god-dreams: state at the end of Year 3

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent after Year 3 (the year closes as autumn opens again). Two things turn on the Keeper. Where a number turns on the salt party, Salt A is the party home before the hard cold with salt and Salt B is the party not home until spring. Where it turns on the young women who sat beside the Night Sitter, Sitter A is one of them finding she could and Sitter B is none. Unmarked numbers are the same either way. The Keeper's pass two chooses.

population: about 5,060 (Salt A about 5,062, Salt B about 5,055): about 3,030 in the walled town [f50], about 2,030 in hamlets. Births about 190, deaths about 170 (Salt A about 168, Salt B about 176). No one arrived. Eight left in the salt party; they are counted in the people either way. Deaths: about 45 winter fevers and cold among the old and small, about fifteen of the winter deaths in kept households, the rest age, the river, farm and quarry accidents and childbed. Why it moved: an ordinary year, an ordinary winter, no flood loss; Salt B loses a few more to meat gone early.

stores: grain in after an ordinary harvest, threshing done by midwinter, enough to reach the next harvest with a thin margin, as the two years before. Fuel enough. Oil enough. Salt: Salt A, a load in hand from the harbor from late autumn, the late pigs fully cured and some over; Salt B, none by the second month of winter, the late pigs cured lightly and the meat gone early, and only what the party brought in spring. Iron: stock of the older iron put by for spearheads and the cutters' picks; the new iron soft. Why: the carriers' two small loads at last year's price in both; the party's load in Salt A from late autumn, in Salt B only from spring.

treasure: middling, a little down: cut stone, linen, pots and oil. The court spent twenty bolts of linen, sixty pots, twelve jars of flax oil, about a boat's load of cut stone with the cutters' days, and bread for the eight's outfit on the harbor party. The scar's fifth part of the year's cutting was cut for the harbor and not for the wall. Some bronze in the court's keeping, untouched. No gift came in. Why: the party cost less in pots and more in stone than last year; the iron wears faster and the smiths draw it out oftener.

trade: linen, pots, flax oil, cut stone and grain out; salt and iron in, dear, and the iron worse from late winter. The salt carriers came twice, late autumn and spring, at last year's price. Iron came down from the mining town as in other years, greyer and softer from late winter on. The court sent its second party to the harbor town, with stone (see the report); what it bought, what the harbor answered and the terms of a standing price for stone are the Keeper's. The bargain the court holds is for no more than a fifth of a year's cutting.

strength: about 600 by beacon, about 200 of them on the wall watch by turns, unchanged in number. Two men of the watch a night sit the guest's door in winter and spring, four on the three named nights, by turns and lot, not from kept households; every man told by the captain before his first night. None asked off. The soft iron bent four spearheads in ten against one in ten of the older; the Warden of the Wall issued the old heads to the watch and set the soft ones by for the beacon stores. A half score of the watch walked with the guest to four hamlets in spring and the dry months. No spears went out.

mood: settled and hospitable, uneasy under the star. Six dim nights this year, fourteen in all. Salt still the town's worst want (Salt A, eased; Salt B, not). The guest's telling the wall what the four sit against has divided the town less than it might: the wall's men and the Warden of the Wall are quiet about it, the kept households are easier, the lowest streets say a keeper who tells only the wall has chosen her hearers, and the young say the star pays for the flames. The shrine word has made the flame easier to pass and the Bench has held that it is passed only at a household's own asking. Two lads and a yard wall were fined. The Gate Bench has kept the custom of no house shamed. Nothing has broken.

households keeping the flame (the Keeper of the Count's roll): about 520 of about 1,010: about 385 in the town and about 135 in the hamlets. The court's hearth is one. About fifteen households let the flame go this year, none for a clear cause. About seventy of the roll are shrines lit from a kept flame since the shrine word of month 1. The seventh place of the guest's keeping is empty. Keeping Sleep ran at two households a night, three on three named nights in deep winter (month 5 night 18, month 6 night 4, month 6 night 26).

dim nights, the Keeper of the Count's cord: fourteen in all, three of Year 1 (remembered), five of Year 2 (counted) and six of Year 3 (counted): month 1 night 12 (no lot, she awake in the yard, no dying in the town); month 5 night 10 (lot night, she asleep, an old woman died in a kept house); month 5 night 26 (no lot, she awake, no dying); month 7 night 6 (lot night, she asleep, a man died in a house not kept); month 7 night 20 (no lot, she awake, no dying); month 8 night 13 (no lot, she awake in a western hamlet, no dying in the town). A dying fell in the town on five of the fourteen. The wall watch noted the hour of each from month 5: about an hour, in the middle of the night, each time.

helpers (who has taken the god's word): the old Warden of the Door, from the third night of Year 1; the seat; the Keeper of the Count; the Steward of the Store, grudgingly; the new Warden of the Door carries the guest's asks faithfully, told the seat in month 10 she was nearer, and has not said she takes her for the awaited. The Warden of the Wall has not, and keeps her door as the wall's own work. The Gate Bench is split, two for and one against, and agrees on the custom of the table.

offices: no office changed hands.

## Keeper, Year 1, pass two
The river party reached people at the river's mouth, was received and came home in month 12 with word of salt [r-0251]. The western party found no one and had not come home at the year's end [r-0250]; it comes in early in Year 2.

## Keeper, Year 2, pass two
- The salt party: branch B [r-0294]. It reached the harbor, was received, bought salt at the harbor's price, wintered there and came home in month 8 with the salt and a spoken answer. The second lean winter for salt stands.
- The Night Sitter did not sleep on the kept night (branch B).
- The flame a household lit from its own kept flame is a shrine, not a third household; the cap of two was kept.

## Keeper, Year 3, pass two
- The harbor party did not come home before the cold. A boat grounded on a bar under its stone; no ship lay at the mouth; they went round the shore with the samples, were given bread and three nights, and the leader spoke the words on the third night. The house took the samples, sold salt at a standing price below the carriers', and named its order: sixty blocks a year dressed to a cord it gave, to be lifted by its own ship at the river's mouth. The gales shut the shore. They wintered at the harbor and came up at high water, home in month 8, all eight. Your numbers take the branch with no word all winter.
- At the harbor the leader heard this on the quays and brought it home: at the market town in the western delta a stranger who reads scales for a pinch of barley has been seated as keeper of the weights, and says before the market that it is a god.
- Of the three who sat the winter beside the Night Sitter, the hamlet girl found she could. Your numbers take that branch.
- At the dawns after three households the wall's men saw what the guest had told them they would see, and no more.
