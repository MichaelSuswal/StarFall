# Unnamed people of god-dreams: state at the end of Year 1

Campaign: sim-06. Written by the civ agent after Year 1 (the year closes as autumn opens again).

population: about 5,020: about 3,010 in the walled town [f50], about 2,010 in hamlets. Births about 190, deaths about 170, no one arrived and no one left. About 45 of the deaths were winter fevers and the cold among the old and the small; the rest were age, the river, farm and quarry accidents and childbed. About a dozen of the winter deaths were in households keeping the guest's flame. The flame did not cure anyone.

stores: grain in after an ordinary harvest, threshing still to do, enough to reach the next harvest with a thin margin, as this year's did. Fuel enough. Oil enough: a kept flame is the household's own clay lamp lit from another flame, so it burns no more oil than before; the court's hearth uses a little more. Salt: very short, worse than the start. Two small loads came from the harbor towns, one in late autumn and one in spring, and the late pigs were cured lightly; some meat went early. The lowest hamlet's seed debt is made good in part.

treasure: middling and unchanged: cut stone, linen, pots; some bronze in the court's keeping. The court spent its bread and a little oil on a guest, and some cut stone and timber on the wall repairs. No gift came in.

trade: linen, pots, cut stone and grain out; salt and iron in, dear. The salt carriers came twice, with less than was wanted, at last year's price or a little over. Two parties of the court's runners are out, one west toward the Rudd Hills by the road and one by boat down the Ellow, to learn what salt neighbours have. Nothing has come of them yet in these records.

strength: about 600 by beacon, about 200 of them on the wall watch by turns, unchanged. A settled stretch of footing on the river side of the wall was relaid and two rotted posts of the beacon platform were replaced in the dry months. No spears went out for or against the guest.

mood: settled and hospitable, still anxious over salt. New this year: most of the town is glad of the guest and some households are fiercely so; a smaller number, mostly in the lowest streets, hold her to be the keeper of the dark and keep their ancestors' lamps lit from the old flame only. The wall watch are uneasy that the flames near her dip when she is afraid. The Gate Bench has ruled that no house is to be shamed for not keeping the flame. Nothing has broken.

households keeping the guest's flame (the Keeper of the Count's roll): about 330 of about 1,000: about 250 in the town and about 80 in the hamlets. The court's hearth is one of them. The seventh place of the guest's keeping is empty.

helpers (who has taken the god's word): the Warden of the Door, from the third night; the seat, from late autumn, when the court's hearth was lit; the Keeper of the Count, from the first lots; the Steward of the Store, from the winter count, grudgingly. The Warden of the Wall has not. The Gate Bench is split, two for, one against, and agrees on the custom of the table.
