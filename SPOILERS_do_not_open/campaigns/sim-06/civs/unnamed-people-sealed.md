# The player's people: the world's side

Written by the Keeper on creation Night 3a, 2026-11-24. The world's answers to the DM's reconcile list in the public meeting file, and what else the world holds about this people that they do not say or do not know. The civ agent does not read this file. The deep history behind each answer is written on Night 3b.

## Where they are
The unnamed town [f20] stands on an old clay levee in the Reedmouths [r14], where the Bank Way [f21] meets the main channel of the River Alder [f03], about a mile and a half south-east of the god's landing. It has no wall. The town is unnamed and the people unnamed, by the player's choice, until a mortal names them in play.

## The reconcile list, answered
1. How long they have been there. As they say it: since the time of the great-grandparents' grandparents, about a hundred and sixty years. The wish is kept. Before that the four groups lived apart on the channels, the flats, the clay banks and in the hills.
2. What came before. The oldest weights in the hall are older than the town and no one can explain them: true. They are standard weights of the vanished trading power, stone and old bronze, found on the levee when the first houses went up. The house itself stands on older footings. Whose they were and why that power ended is 3b's.
3. The floods, and the market older than any lord. The floods are true: a run of great floods drowned the channel hamlets and the old salt works and pushed the groups together onto the high levee. The market ground is older than the town: true. There has been a lord: the kings of the Aldermark [r10] count the delta market among their old dues and once kept a reeve on the levee. No one has come to collect in three generations. The people say the market is older than any lord, and by the ground they are right; they do not say that a lord still thinks it his.
4. Past their borders. The ground toward the hills is not empty: it is the Pellow Downs [r15], low clay downs with diggers' pits and a few steadings, and beyond them the Rudd Hills [r18]. There is an old road, the Old Rudd Way [f23], sunken and grassed over in stretches, which the ore carriers still follow; Ruddfold [f25] is on it, 123.9 miles from the town on foot along the road, 7 days. The people say the downs are "the old yards" and that it is ill-omened to build near the Pellow Vaults [f26]. Upriver is the Aldermark [r10]; across the forest, the Sutherwold [r12], a fen people they know only as buyers of salt; on the water, the boat bands of the water folk.
5. The drowned one. A belief. What stands behind "a larger one that drowned, and that is why the tide keeps coming" is set in the deep history on 3b, with the Maun Stair [f27] as its ground. The small gods of channel and bank, fed with the first of a catch, are a custom that claims no origin. That no god has ever stayed is true within their memory.
6. The old keeper of the standards. Placed as fact: the town kept an office for the old weights, its holder died last spring, and the council has not agreed on another. The hall is poor, the clay standards cracked and mismatched, the hall's scale distrusted. How he died, and what the office and its vacancy hold, is written on 3b under ruling L-0029.
7. Places wished. The house on the highest firm bank where the main track meets the main channel: placed, in the unnamed town [f20]. The town's own house, with a gate and no wall. A flood drum on the highest bank: the town has one already. Salt pans: on the Maun Flats [f22], whose near edge is 1.6 miles from the town on foot over the marsh. The hill country of ore and charcoal: the Rudd Hills [r18], as in 4.
8. Ships and open water. The town has flat-bottomed boats and a few gulf coasters. One of the council's ship-owners works the channels; the other owns two coasters that work the Gulf of Maun [r31] and in summer go out past the Dunmar Horn [r17]. From the town to the mouth of the River Alder [f03] on the gulf: 45.5 miles by boat downstream, 2 days. Beyond the gulf there is somewhere to go: the harbor towns of Sutter Strand [r28], whose ships come into the gulf a few times a summer. The tool cannot price a coasting voyage round a headland, so no time is written for it.
9. The town's own names for the offices. The player's Warden of the Hall is the town's Assay-master. The player's Reeve of the Gate is the Stallreeve. The office the player's Clerk of the Seals is to grow from is the Tally-keeper, who keeps the kept halves of split sticks for debts and bargains witnessed before the council. The vacant keeper of the standards was called the Weightward. These are the town's names, told in the public knowledge file; the god learns each when a mortal says it.
10. The tide-reckoner. Fitted and confirmed as priced (L-0024). In this world's one system: she stands barefoot on the flats at slack water, holds still and listens, and takes the coming flood's weight into her feet; the cost is hers, the feeling in her hands for a season. Over a life the cost runs toward her name: she already answers to nothing but what she does. Not told. Other workers of small things live in the town, as in any town of this world; how many is found in play.
11. The licence's cost. Fitted (L-0025): holding costs nothing; each day a factor sets a seal costs the night's sleep that follows.
12. Debt-bonded folk. The town holds them as the player supposed: about two hundred. By the town's custom now, a bond is made by split stick before the council's witness; it has no fixed term unless the stick says so; it passes to the heir. The god's three rules would change the first and last of these.

## Heard, not promised

## Their neighbors and their trouble
The Aldermark [r10] upriver, with its unpressed claim. Hill kin at Ruddfold [f25]. The water folk in the channels, who trade reed work at dusk and are blamed for missing nets. Their trouble: the market has quarreled since the Weightward died; the clay diggers and the kiln-masters are near a break; and a clerk of the Aldermark [r10] was seen in the town last summer, asking how many stalls there are.

## Night 3b: what stands behind their tellings
Added on creation Night 3b, 2026-11-24. The truth of each is in `deep-history.md`; nothing here is told to anyone.
- The oldest weights are the Assize of Maun's, and the keeper's office was its ward of the standard. There were seven; six are on the rack (mystery S01).
- The drowned one is Maunath, which did not drown (mystery L03). Its ground is the Maun Stair [f27].
- The floods of 165 to 160 BA came when the last of the Assize's held banks failed. Hale the Raker led the salt people onto the levee.
- The old yards on the Pellow Downs [r15] are where the Assize's school worked; the clay from there has no temper (mystery L05). Some diggers have been cutting the delta's flood-silt clay with it, which is what the kiln-masters half suspect.
- The list-singers the old remember are the carriers (mystery L04); their last round was in 29 BA and the next reaches the town in Year 9.
- The last reeve of the Aldermark [r10] on the levee was Odo Penn; his last reckoning is in the sealed jar in the hall.
- What the town says of the old keeper's death, when asked, is in the public knowledge file, and is true as far as it goes.
