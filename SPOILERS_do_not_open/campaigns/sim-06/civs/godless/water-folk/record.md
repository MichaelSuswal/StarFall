# Godless people: the water folk

## Who they are
Not human. A people of the water margin: slight, long-fingered, grey-brown of skin, quiet, at ease in water for hours. They own no land, build nothing that stays, and give their dead to the water. They deal by barter and mostly at dusk and by night, and they are hard to read in grief.

## How many
About 3,000 in all, in boat bands of a few score.

## Where
The lower rivers and sheltered coasts of the south and south-west: the Braidwater [r26], the mouth of the River Ellow [f07], the Gulf of Maun [r31] and the channels of the Reedmouths [r14], the lower River Morrow [f02]. No band is in sight of any seat at landing.

## How they live
In reed boats and on rafts; fish, fowl, eels, shellfish; reed work finer than any town's, traded for metal and salt. A band has a boat-mother; bands meet at certain tides.

## Their code
What the water takes is not asked for back. A bargain struck at dusk is kept at dawn. They do not sleep ashore.

## Their history in the land
They say they were here before the banks were raised, and that the land people's towns stand on their old fishing grounds. The true account is written on Night 3b.

## What they believe about gods
They do not use the word. They say there are things in deep water that keep, and that it is rude to name them.

## What they want
To be let alone on the water, and metal fishhooks.

## Whether and how they touch the great events
Set in full on Night 3b.

## Trouble
Fewer children each generation. Land people's weirs close channels they have always used.

## Night 3b: what the old tell, and their part in the great events
Added on creation Night 3b, 2026-11-24. The telling may be wrong; the truth is in `deep-history.md`.
- They say they were here before the banks were raised. They were: they are the last of the first people of this country.
- They keep the drowned lower hall of the Maun Stair [f27]: at certain tides they clear its weed and set reed lights on the steps. They say it is rude to say for whom.
- An old boat-mother of the Gulf of Maun [r31] has it from her mother's mothers that "the heavy one went light at the steps" (mystery L03).
- The bands gather at the islets off the Whin Flats [r29] at the lowest tides of spring and autumn, three nights each (row PH-2). In a band no one is struck and no one is put out; a child is every boat's.
