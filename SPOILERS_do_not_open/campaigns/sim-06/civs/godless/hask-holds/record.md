# Godless people: the mining holds of the Hask Ridge

## Who they are
Mortal folk of the common kind: miners, smelters, charcoal burners and smiths in a league of small stone towns. Close, hard-handed, and aware that iron is short everywhere else.

## How many
About 15,000: about 4,000 in Haskhold [f72], about 2,000 in Southhold of Hask [f73], the rest in smaller holds.

## Where
The Hask Ridge [r20], its middle and south. From Southhold of Hask [f73] the unnamed bend town [f50] is 127.1 miles on foot over open ground, 11 days.

## How they live
Ore, charcoal and bloomery iron; goats and thin barley; salt bought dear. Each hold has a master chosen by its smelters; the masters meet at Haskhold [f72] once a year. They cut timber on both faces of the ridge for charcoal.

## Their code
A measure of iron is a measure of iron: short weight is the one unforgivable theft. A hold feeds its own injured. No stranger goes below ground.

## Their history in the land
They say the first holds were dug by people fleeing a king. The oldest workings are older than any hold and no one made them that the holds remember. The true account is written on Night 3b.

## What they believe about gods
That the ridge is nobody's and should stay so. They leave the first bloom of a new furnace in the adit and do not say for whom.

## What they want
Salt, grain and timber, and to set the price of iron themselves.

## Whether and how they touch the great events
Set in full on Night 3b.

## Trouble
The near timber is gone; they are cutting the Ossenwold [r21] and the herders have noticed. Two holds have not paid the league's levy.

## Night 3b: what the old tell, and their part in the great events
Added on creation Night 3b, 2026-11-24. The telling may be wrong; the truth is in `deep-history.md`.
- They tell that the first holds were dug by people fleeing a king. The first holds were held by bonded miners who walked out on the Assize's stewards under Jory Hask; the oldest workings are far older, the Kennoch kings'.
- The oldest adit under Haskhold [f72] ends on a floor of blue-grey glass that rings under a pick. The smiths call it the anvil of the ridge and let no stranger see it.
- At Haskhold [f72] a bargain struck with a hand on the anvil stone at the masters' meeting is remembered by every hold, and a breaker gets no iron from any of them. They call it the anvil word.
- At Southhold of Hask [f73] hangs a bell that cracked and was mended by a worker some two hundred years ago, and has not cracked since. It is rung for the injured. Her name is not remembered.
- Southhold of Hask [f73] sold a string of sick draught oxen east last summer (mystery S04).
