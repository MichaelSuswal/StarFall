# Godless people: the Aldermark kingdom

## Who they are
Mortal folk of the common kind: an old river kingdom, proud and rooted, of farmers, river traders, clerks and a small landed nobility. A quarter of a few hundred of the slow long-memoried people, not human, lives in the chief city under its own law of guest and debt and keeps its own books; they hold no land.

## How many
About 40,000: about 9,000 in Aldermoot [f70], about 3,000 in Wenlow [f71], the rest in villages along the River Alder [f03] and Hollin Water [f04].

## Where
The Aldermark [r10], with a claim it has not pressed in three generations on the lower river and the delta market of the Reedmouths [r14]. The Alder Road [f24] is theirs to the head of the delta. From Aldermoot [f70] to the unnamed town [f20]: 353.1 miles by boat down the River Alder [f03], 12 days; the same upstream, 30 days; 309.5 miles on foot by the Alder Road [f24], 17 days.

## How they live
Grain, cattle and flax on the river plain; river barges; a king in Aldermoot [f70] with a council of landed houses and a chancery of clerks. Bronze common, bloomery iron bought from the Hask Ridge [r20]. They pay tolls to the Sarrow for the Pass Road [f65] and resent them.

## Their code
Rank is kept and written down. A debt outlives the debtor and passes to the house. A written due is a due for ever. Hospitality to a peer; charity, by rule, to the poor on set days.

## Their history in the land
They say they are the heirs of the old trading power whose weights and roads lie all over the west, and their kings count their line from its last stewards. Their records are long, torn and argued over. The true account is written on Night 3b.

## What they believe about gods
That gods are old patrons of old houses, to be honored in form and kept out of the chancery. They have had none in living memory and are not sure they want one.

## What they want
Their old dues: the tolls of the delta market and the salt of the gulf. Cheaper passage over the mountains. To be owed, and to have it in writing.

## Whether and how they touch the great events
Set in full on Night 3b. Their country is among the oldest held ground of the west.

## Trouble
An old king with two heirs who do not speak; a chancery that has begun to find old claims in its rolls.

## Night 3b: what the old tell, and their part in the great events
Added on creation Night 3b, 2026-11-24. The telling may be wrong; the truth is in `deep-history.md`.
- They tell that their first king, Corvin Alder, was the Assize's last steward and received its rights whole. He was a steward's son, and one line of the founding roll is scraped (mystery S09).
- They tell that the patron of the old houses went down to the sea in the hundred and eighty-fourth year and will return when the dues are paid in full.
- An old survey roll in the chancery lists "the stones on the grass": the Gannel Stones [f80], added to the map on Night 3b, 182.7 miles from Aldermoot [f70] on foot over open ground, 13.0 days. Grade of their knowledge: rumored. No one from the kingdom has gone in living memory.
- The reciters, as they call the singing walkers, last came when the old king was a young man, and the Mollen counted every name.
- Named mortals, by the Keeper: the old king, Hale Aldren; his daughter Marit; his nephew Edric; the chancery clerk Peverel Stane, who went down to the delta last summer. The traveler Calla Wend, who came in 36 BA and is buried at Wenlow [f71].
- The Mollen quarter: about three hundred of the slow people, under the keeper of the Ninth Book, Emmerin. What they know and how they may be asked is in `cast.md`.
