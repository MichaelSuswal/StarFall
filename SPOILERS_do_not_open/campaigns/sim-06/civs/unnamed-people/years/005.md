# Sealed: the unnamed people, the stretch from the fourteenth day of the seventh month of Year 3 to the fourteenth day of the tenth month of Year 4

Written by the civ agent for the Keeper. This is what the people did, said and knew out of the Weightward's sight and hearing, including the eleven's shut sittings and what is drawn from the sealed side release. The public file is `campaigns/sim-06/civs/unnamed-people/years/005.md`. Names are the Keeper's list; none of them has been said in the Weightward's hearing in this stretch, so none is in a public file.

## What is held and what moved

The held things stood as the earlier notes gave them. Nobody in the town knows where the seventh weight is or what it weighed. The Tally-keeper (Annis Furrow), the Stallreeve (Tam Wherry), the Assay-master (Osric Marl), the clay head and the eleven have what each had. Nobody asked any of them plainly in the stretch, so none spoke of it. The Weightward was not asked about it and nobody went looking.

**The Assay-master's lamp.** He lit the lamp in the hall's small room before light and sat before the empty seat with the chest locked, on the morning of the second of the first month of Year 4 (the ring's day), and on the mornings of the ring's sittings of the fifteenth of the sixth month and the fifteenth of the ninth month of that year. He did the same on the night of the twentieth of the twelfth month of Year 3, after the carrier's account. He did not say why to anyone. He took the rack from the chest on the ring's day with the pair standing by, and the seventh seat in it was as it had been: a cut seat, unwiped. He did not touch it. When the ring had risen the four who know of the coat and boots (the Assay-master, the Stallreeve, the Tally-keeper, the clay head) stood a moment at the foot of the loft stair again, and nobody went up.

## The shut sittings

All with the door shut, the Stallreeve at it, the Tally-keeper at the end of the bench with her stick, her apprentice (Wat) at the door with nothing to call. The Weightward was not sent for and nobody moved to send for it. The master of the smiths did not ask it again. Nobody was asked in session about the seventh weight.

### The sixteenth of the ninth month, Year 3

1. **The hall's cost of the thirty-three spares and the one firing.** The Assay-master told it as his own act, on his word, out of the pinch at the bench, and said he would not have the hall's keeper pay for a proving the hall had not agreed to. For (seven): the Assay-master, the clay head, the head of reed and fish, the head of the hill goods, the ship owner of the channels, the master of the boatwrights, the ship owner of the open water. Against (three): the head of salt (a weight is paid for before it is shaped, not after), the master of the weavers (he would not have it said that the hall buys its own proof), the master of the smiths (he had no quarrel with the sum, only with the order of it). Not voting (one): the master of the potters, the spares being hers to shape. The ring gave no reason in the open; the Stallreeve did not ask for one.
2. **The second stick cut for the carrier by the ring.** The head of the hill goods moved it, to cut the six things the ring had taken, for the carrier to carry with the first, since the carrier going south with half a word was the worse thing. For (five): the head of the hill goods, the ship owner of the channels, the master of the boatwrights, the head of reed and fish, the master of the potters. Against (five): the head of salt, the master of the weavers, the master of the smiths, the Assay-master, the clay head. The Assay-master said the six things were the ring's to say to the Weightward and the hall, not to carry south as a price. The clay head said he had said before that the ring would not cut the town's word for it and that he had not changed. The ship owner of the open water did not vote: a carrier does not vote on his load. Tied. Not taken.
3. **Whether to give the Weightward the reason.** The head of salt said the ring owed the Weightward nothing on a tie. The clay head said a stick cut on a tied vote is a word the ring is not agreed on, and that that one reason could go to it. The ring let the Stallreeve carry it without a count. He gave it at the post on the seventeenth in those words.
4. **The term.** The head of reed and fish said the ring had said it would speak on the term at the first sitting after the grain of the third year of the seat, and that this was the second. Nobody moved it. Three called a sitting on the seat: none. Not one of the eleven spoke of putting the Weightward out, and the head of salt said aloud it would be a sin against the hall's own order to do so before the term was out.

### The second of the first month of Year 4, at first light, and after the day

At first light the ring sat shortly on the day's order. The Assay-master moved that the eleven stand on the hall's bench under him, and that the hall's ten fired pieces stand at the end of the same bench, not to be filed, mended, replaced or carried out, and not to go to the gate, serving only to reckon a bargain made before the day. Ten for, none against, one not voting (the ship owner of the open water, on the old pieces his carriers still hold). The head of salt moved that the clause be kept as written a year before, that every bargain made before stands as made. For: all but the master of the weavers, who did not vote. The ruling that a bargain made after the day by the old pieces is to say so and have it cut was the Stallreeve's, and the ring let it stand without a count.

After the day, at dusk, the ring sat again, shortly, in the hall, with the carrier. See below.

### The fifteenth of the sixth month, Year 4

Winter sitting. Three things.
1. **The hall's ten.** Left at the end of the bench. Nobody moved to change it.
2. **The stalls' pieces.** The stalls' fired pieces read against the eleven at the gate: most about a twentieth heavy, copies of the hall's old pieces. The head of salt asked whether the ring would set a day by which the stalls must have theirs filed to the eleven. The master of the weavers, the master of the smiths and the head of the hill goods said they would not set a day for another man's weights. The ring set none. Not taken.
3. **The braziers.** The five braziers under the hall's roof, the charcoal for them from the hall's pinch. For: the Stallreeve's ask, all eleven. The head of salt said so shortly and went.

No word was set for the Weightward. The Stallreeve was told to bring any that was.

### The fifteenth of the ninth month, Year 4

Spent on the term and the first sitting after the grain. The head of salt said the seat's term is three years and a day, as set, and that the ring would ask nothing of the Weightward until the day. The master of the smiths said he would not ask it at the day either unless the ring did, and that it had told him what it would answer. The head of reed and fish said the ring could not tell the town what the Weightward would do when the term came, since the Weightward had not said, and that it had said "past that I don't know." The head of the hill goods said nothing and counted the months on his fingers: it is a year to the term. The clay head said he would not have the Weightward asked on the term by a count, but that anyone of the eleven who wanted it asked might ask it at the post as himself. Nobody said he would. The ring set no day. Nothing was asked of the Weightward and the ring did not sit again before the held moment.

## What the carrier did, saw and told

**At Corrith, Year 3, months 10 to 12.** The ship owner of the open water (Davin Sculler) went out past the Horn on the tide of the fourth of the tenth month with his two coasters and lay at Corrith for some weeks. He said the town's word on the quay as cut, with the half stick in his hand, to the masters of the houses his coasters trade with. He put no price on it. He looked for a hull or a man of the southern coaster's house, to put the word to it by name as the Weightward had asked, and found none; its port is another town farther along that coast. So it was not put to her house by name. The council's people at Corrith did not ask for it and he did not go to them. Nobody gave him an answer, a stick or a token.

**What he heard.** It was old news on the quay. A coaster's master had been selling it since the winter as news: the delta market has a reader of scales who takes barley and no silver, seated as keeper of the hall's weights, who says before the yard that it is a god; the hall's clay pieces are a twentieth heavy; the old weights are six of seven. New to them were the figures and the ring's day. Two masters said they will reckon delta salt and pots by the old six once the market itself does and not a day before. One asked what the Weightward is, and he said the Weightward of the hall and gave no more, as he had told it he would not promise more. One asked whether a southern scale may be read against the hall's, and he said a scale is read at the gate like any. The saying he heard most there of a god in a market: that a god ashore is a landlord. Nobody asked him about the fifth old weight and nobody there knows of it.

**What he told, and to whom.**
- At the post on the twentieth of the twelfth month, before the yard, his own account, in the words in the public file. That is public.
- That night at his landing house, to the Stallreeve and the Assay-master, who came down after the market, the rest, kept out of the post: what the quay had had in the winter, the saying of the landlord, and the two masters who will reckon by the old six when the market does. He told it flatly. The Assay-master asked whether anyone on the quay had named the seventh. He said no. The Assay-master said nothing for a time and lit the lamp in the hall's small room that night.
- To the ring, at dusk on the second of the first month of Year 4, after the day, in the hall, the same as the two had it and no more. The head of salt asked whether the south had ever asked the price of the hall's keeping. He said the quay had not. The master of the smiths asked nothing. The ring thanked him, which it does not often do.
- He did not tell the Weightward more than he had said at the post, because it did not ask him and he did not offer it.

**The southern coaster, the evening of the twelfth of the tenth month, Year 4.** He was at his landing, with his coasters loaded, when the southern hull came up the channel. He knew it at once by the stem and the mended strake: it is the hull of the house whose name he holds, the hull he looked for at Corrith and did not find. He went down the plank and stood at the rail of his own boat and looked, and did not go up to her. He said nothing to anyone that night. He holds the name and has not said it to the Weightward, who has not asked him. He did not go to the gate on the thirteenth. Whether he stands in the yard on the fourteenth is not told here.

## What the master of the potters worked out, and what Orrin asked

**The master of the potters (Wynne Daub).** She had the two sticks read to her in the eighth month of Year 3 by the Tally-keeper, pair after pair, and she sat with them four nights at her own hearth. By the end of the eighth month she had it plain for herself: her clay loses in the fire between one part in twelve and one part in fourteen of a piece's dry weight, piece by piece, about one part in thirteen over the load, and pieces weighed alike before the fire do not come out alike. She did not say it at the post or in the ring. She had no reason to, she said afterward to her foreman, until someone asked.

The cracking of the downs clay worries her more each year. In the third month of Year 4 the kilns that fire the downs clay cracked a load in three, and she told her boys to say nothing of it.

**Orrin of the east kilns.** After the third month's cracked loads he went up to the potters' hall, to her door, on a day with no market, with a tile in his hand and the stick of his own notched by his apprentice, and asked her plainly whether the pieces she weighs alike green come out alike. She said no. He asked whether it was the same for the downs clay. She said it was worse. He asked how much worse. She said she had not the weight for it, only the cracking, and that the cracking of the downs clay worries her more each year. He said he had a thirteenth and she had a thirteenth, and that they were the same thirteenth. She said she supposed they were. He thanked her and went. She did not tell anyone, and he did not either, except his apprentice.

## What the head of salt, the head of the hill goods and the other eleven said among themselves in the stretch

- **The head of salt.** At the pans after the ring's day he said that a weight is what it is and that the six were the six. Asked by the head of the raking household whether the town would be held to a twentieth, he said it would not, and that the clause was his and he was glad of it.
- **The head of the hill goods.** On hearing the carrier's account of the quay he said that the south would sell the town as news for as long as the town was news, and that it had cost him nothing. He counted. He is against the seat for the same reason as before and did not say so in the stretch.
- **The master of the weavers.** At the ring's day he said to the Stallreeve that a thing which will call a twentieth heavy before the whole yard and not lift a hand to change it is a thing he can sell cloth by. He did not say it to anyone else.
- **The master of the smiths.** He stood in the open at the post on the seventeenth of the second month when the boy asked about the star, and heard the line tell him to hush. He said to the head of the hill goods, "A thing that does not answer is not a thing that lies." He did not ask it anything.
- **The ship owner of the channels.** He asked his captains to say the weight in the eleven's ones from the first market of the autumn and to have the Tally-keeper cut it, and was pleased to find it cost him nothing.
- **The clay head.** He stood for the pair at the opening of the chest with the head of salt, and was the one who said the beam agreed before the Assay-master did. He has the weight of the thing in him still, and said so to nobody.

## Names

None of the Keeper's names was said in the Weightward's hearing in the stretch. The Assay-master keeps his word that he will not say his until the Weightward has one of its own to put in the other pan. Orrin said his name at the post, as he has before. The fish seller (Hob Sedge) did not say his. Bren's was already known.
