# Sealed report to the Keeper: the stretch from the fifth day of the first month to the fourteenth day of the seventh month (Year 3 told again), civ unnamed-people

Public report: `campaigns/sim-06/civs/unnamed-people/years/004-report.md`. Sealed year file: `SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/sim-06/civs/unnamed-people/years/004.md`. Everything the god could not witness is here and in the sealed year file. Nothing from the sealed side release is in a public file except as the ring's behavior shows, and the public files do not say that anything was asked, told or known in private.

## What I drew from the sealed side release, and how

- **The clerk, the punt, the coat and boots.** Each is known to the mortals the release names and, since the winter sitting of Year 2, to the eleven. In the stretch it appears only in the ring's vote on the seventh seat (sealed year file, item 2 of the sitting). All eleven knew of it. The eight who voted for it took it anyway, and said why in their own terms. The head of salt and the master of the weavers did not vote and the ship owner of the open water never votes on his load. Nobody put the question to the Tally-keeper in session. Nobody asked the Stallreeve about the boatman. Nobody went looking again.
- **The house of the southern coaster's master.** The ship owner of the open water has it by name and has not said it. The Weightward did not make him answer, and nobody else did. He does not mean to say it before the quay. That is a thing for the Keeper to rule on when the going comes, and nothing in the stretch changes it.
- **Where the weight is.** Nobody knows. The door-stone is a door-stone and I made it one: the old net-maker's husband's family had it from the old quay, they believe. I have not made it more than that.

## Names

The Keeper's list is used in sealed files only. Nobody said any of them in the Weightward's hearing in the stretch. The Assay-master keeps his word. The fish seller (Hob Sedge) did not say his on the fifth, or on the eighth.

## Things I decided that the Keeper may overrule

- The ring's counts and reasons on the second stick. The public report has the counts as the Stallreeve gave them.
- That the ring sat with the door shut, did not send for the Weightward, and did not ask it anything in session. That keeps the settled point that the master of the smiths does not ask it again.
- That the ring gave the Stallreeve leave for one reason and no more, and that the reason is a thing of place.
- That the Stallreeve gave the day at the pans on his own terms and that the household was told the night before.
- That the household was in debt to the head of salt for the clean salt of the year before. It touches nothing the Weightward can see.
- That the old net-maker's stone is a door-stone from the old quay, as she believes.
- The calendar: thirty-day months, so that the release's two market days fall on the market count.

## Nothing held but the moment given

I held nothing in the stretch. The moment at the door of the potters' hall is the Keeper's board to write. I did not write what the Weightward asks the master of the potters, what she answers, the weighing, the three spare pieces of each size, the Stallreeve's leave for the kiln day or the firing.

## Past the first held moment: not yet what happened

Nothing is written. It is worked on the night after the moment is played.
