# The ship owner of the open water, the carrier (Davin Sculler): what the god could not witness

Name from the Keeper's list. Not said in the Weightward's hearing.

**Corrith, Year 3.** He said the town's word on the quay as cut, to the masters of the houses his coasters trade with, with the half stick in his hand. No price. He found no hull or man of the southern master's house in the weeks he lay there, because her port is a town farther along the coast where his coasters do not go. He did not put the word to her house by name. The council's people did not ask him for it and he did not go to them. Nobody gave him an answer, a stick or a token.

**What he heard.** Most of it was old news there: the reader of scales who takes barley and no silver and says before the yard that it is a god, the hall's clay pieces a twentieth heavy, the old weights six of seven. New to them were the figures and the ring's day. Two masters will reckon delta salt and pots by the old six when the market does. One asked what the Weightward is, and he said the Weightward of the hall. One asked about a southern scale, and he said a scale is read at the gate. The saying most heard: that a god ashore is a landlord. Nobody asked him about the fifth old weight.

**Whom he told.** The post, on the twentieth of the twelfth month, his own account. That night at his landing house the Stallreeve and the Assay-master had the rest. The ring had it at dusk on the second of the first month of Year 4. He did not tell the Weightward more than he said at the post.

**Year 3, month 9.** He did not vote on cutting the second stick. He voted for the spares' cost.

**Year 4, month 10, the twelfth.** He knew the southern hull by her stem and mended strake as the hull of the house whose name he holds. He went down his plank, looked from his own rail and did not go up. He told nobody. He has not said the house's name to the Weightward, who has not asked him.
