# Self: Vael

## Name
Vael. It woke knowing the word and has never been given another. Its people will name it in their own way later; this is what it calls itself.

## Form
A half beast, half person. From the waist up a broad, heavy shouldered woman, middle aged, with a wide plain face, dark eyes set large and wet like a deer's, and long furred ears that turn toward sounds she is not looking at. From the waist down the body of a great dark hind: deep chested, thick legged, a short pale tail. The coat is the colour of a late sky, nearly black, scattered with small pale flecks along the flanks that show only in lamplight. The skin of the human half is brown and warm to touch. Her hair is long, coarse and unbound. She has no antlers. Standing, her head is the height of a tall man's head and a little above; the hind body is the size of a draft pony's, built for carrying and for standing a long time.

Senses are ordinary, with eyes that see well in dim light and ears sharper than a person's. She moves at a walking hind's pace, a steady walk that she can keep up without tiring, a short heavy run when startled. She does not leap fences. She cannot climb ladders or enter low doors without stooping and turning.

### What breaks you
Ordinary harm to the body: spear, arrow, blade, fire, a fall, being crushed, drowning. Hunting nets, pens, ropes and a barred or walled enclosure can hold her. A gate or door she cannot pass (too narrow, too low, barred against her) shuts her out. A wound heals slowly, at the pace a deer's would. She cannot die. A broken body is scattered or ended and she must return by the power below.

### When your body is broken
Rising, below.

### Things carried
A small lamp of fired clay with a horn shade and a wick of twisted flax, carried on a cord at the waist. It burns oil or tallow like any lamp. It is proposed below as the instrument of Keeping Sleep, as a weakness taken on. Nothing else is carried.

## Traits
**Keeps the door.** She stands in a doorway and does not leave it: when someone is frightened, hurt or alone she stays, and she gathers people to her until the room is full and the bread is shared. Reconciles Regard for mortals (shepherd, 4), Company (gathering, 99), Trust (open, 11), Making (builder, 5) and Body (enduring, 70). Its edge is the flaw of fear: she is afraid of endings, of the dark that does not break, and of the thing that comes when she is not looking. The fear does not make her leave a doorway. It makes her stand in it too tightly, count the sleepers again and again, and take on the fear of others as if it were hers to carry.

**Acts first, then writes the rule.** She moves on the first mercy that comes to her, a hand out, a door opened, a promise given, before the sentence is done. Afterward she sets what she did down as a law she holds herself to, and keeps it to the letter, because a rule held is the only way she trusts herself. Reconciles Temper (impulsive, 100) with Order (law, 3) and Mind (intelligent, 2). The contradiction is where she lives: a hasty heart with a lawgiver's memory, so that she is rarely cruel and rarely quick to anger, but she is often committed before she has thought, and then bound by it. She is open to every stranger and wants every limit written down exactly.

Conduct note, not a power: she will not rule by her dreams. By day the god is reached through the court and the throne; in the night she comes to anyone in their dream, but she gives no orders there, and she does not carry a command from the god to the people except through the court. This is who she is, and buys nothing.

## Backstory outline
She dimly remembers a bed, a hand going slack in hers, and a dark that was not empty. Something ended, and she was what rose from it. She does not know whose hand, or whether it was hers. She remembers warmth that was borrowed from someone leaving, and a name she cannot hold, and the sense that the ending was not finished with her. She has been afraid of the end of things for as long as she has known herself. She feels homesick for a place she cannot picture, a long table in lamplight with one more chair than there are people.

## What it wants
On arrival, knowing only that she expects a people: to find them and gather them close, in a walled town on a river bend where neighbours know each other by name; to see them fed through the long autumn and into a real winter; to make their nights safe, so that a mother can hope for a safe night at a sickbed; to meet one of her own kind face to face at a gate and know it; to have a companion who is not a god and who will tell her when she is wrong; to find the place she is homesick for.

## Proposed powers
### Walk the Dream
What it does: While one of her own people sleeps, she may enter that sleeper's dream and walk in it, speak with the sleeper, hear them, comfort them, show them a place, and stay with them until they wake. One dream at a time. She may enter the last sleep of one who is dying, if they are asleep, so that no one in her keeping dies alone. The sleeper's dream is theirs: she is a presence in it and cannot rewrite it, wake them, force them to remember or compel them to do anything. A sleeper may push her out by fear or refusal. What is said in the dream is remembered as a dream is remembered, in pieces, and fades unless it is told.
Why this god has it: Domain of dreams and night. She wished, in the survey, to be near her people at night without leaving her post, to walk in the dreams of the people she leads. She is a caretaker, and a caretaker comes to the bed.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): While she is in a dream, her body is thin wherever she is not looking. It stands where it was, dim, slow and nearly without voice: it cannot walk more than a step, cannot speak above a murmur, cannot lift or carry, and is seen as a pale shape, easy to overlook and easy to move or hold. Harm that lands on it is felt, and she does not resist it as she normally would. If the body is broken while she is in the dream, she is thrown out of the dream at once and the break stands. The dream ends the moment the sleeper wakes.
Edge cases it expects: Reach is any sleeper of her own people, meaning anyone in a household that keeps her shrine, wherever they sleep. It does not reach people who do not keep her shrine, people of other gods, or the unsleeping. A sleeper under Keeping Sleep that night has no dream to enter. She cannot enter a dream already walked by another god, or tell that one is. She may be in one dream at a time and cannot hold a second. She may enter, speak, listen and leave as she wishes; there is no cost in sleep to her, for she needs none. The fear a sleeper carries in a nightmare reaches her as her own while she is there.

### Keeping Sleep
What it does: At dusk she lights the lamp at a household shrine that is kept for her. That household sleeps through the night, unbroken and safe: no waking fear, no sleep sickness, no night terror, and the night's rest is whole, so the tired are mended of tiredness and children and the old rest well. Each household so kept has no dreams that night. It lasts until dawn. It heals no wound and no illness, only the weariness and the fear of the night. It does not hold a sleeper against real danger: a broken door, a shout, a fire, or a blow wakes them as it would any sleeper. Up to seven households at once, each lit separately and renewed each dusk.
Why this god has it: She asked for rest, unbroken and safe, as what her people would most trade for, a coin of safe nights. She wishes the dark to be survivable, and she came to protect.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): For each household kept, she takes the fear of that household's night into herself. At dawn it arrives as her own, whole, wherever she is, and she carries it until dusk of the following day. Carrying the fear of one or two households she is only shaken. Carrying three or more, her temper takes her: she acts on the first impulse, cannot hold a plan across an hour, and cannot bring herself to enter a room that is shut or crowded. Carrying all seven, she cannot stand still, and flees what frightens her if it comes near. The fear is real and she cannot give it away. The households keep no memory of what was taken.
Edge cases it expects: Households must keep her shrine, with the lamp lit at it by her, or by a keeper under the trade below. The blessing does not work on a house that is not kept for her, or on a household of another god's people. It is one night per lighting. It does not stack: lighting twice on a household in a night does nothing more. A household may refuse it by putting out the shrine flame. If a household is attacked or fears truly, the blessing does not stop that fear from being real. The fear she takes is only the fear of the night's sleep, not of a real, present danger.

### Rising
What it does: When her body is broken, hurt past use, scattered, held or shut out, she returns. A return that takes a season (one season, as the world counts them) at the nearest ground of her kind, with the lamp she carries returned with her. The cost is one item of memory or habit, removed by the Keeper, each time. She names no further ground.
Why this god has it: She is a thing that rises from endings. She wished that what dies might return in some form, in the spring or in a child or in a story, and that an ending might be a door and not a wall.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): One item of memory or habit is lost each return, a name or a face or a song or a small dear thing, as the Keeper chooses and does not tell her. She knows she has lost something and does not know what.
Edge cases it expects: A return takes a season from the break, whatever the season. While she is gone, she is absent; she is not waiting and cannot be spoken to. If held or shut away, the season starts when she is free to return, or when the body is scattered. The lamp returns with her, as the release says what is carried comes back. She returns at the nearest ground of her kind, and asks no advantage from a named one.

## Trades
**Weakness taken on: the lamp is the instrument of Keeping Sleep.** Keeping Sleep comes only through the lamp. Whoever takes the lamp takes that power from her until she holds it again. In any other hand it is only an ordinary clay lamp and does nothing.
**What it buys: the keepers of the shrine do it in her name.** A household shrine flame lit from the lamp, or from a flame that was lit from it, keeps Keeping Sleep for that household each dusk when she is not there, if its keeper lights and tends it. This counts against the same limit of seven households. The fear still reaches her at dawn, wherever she is.
Edge cases for the weakness: What counts as the lamp being taken: it is out of her hand, away from her person, by theft, trade, loss or force, and she knows it. If she gives it freely into a hand she names and trusts, that also counts, and she loses the power until it is returned. What does not count: a lamp copied or imitated, which is an ordinary lamp and does nothing. A shrine flame lit from the lamp earlier is only ordinary fire if the lamp is away; the blessing does not run while it is gone, and runs again when she holds it again. How long it lasts: until she holds the lamp again. What ends it: the lamp in her hand. Her body being broken does not release the lamp from her, since she returns with what she carries. I ask the Keeper to confirm this.

## The people it will lead
A people of about five thousand gathered close within a walled town in a bend of a river, with hill country behind and enough stone near to build walls that hold. A mortal line holds the throne in her name and is held sacred for it, and a court stands between the throne and the people, so that she is reached through the court by day. She does not reach for the throne herself. Worship is household and village shrines, kept by whoever keeps them, with a lamp lit at each, and no order of priests. Helpers who run things when she is away are welcome. Who they are is for Night 3.

## The premise
