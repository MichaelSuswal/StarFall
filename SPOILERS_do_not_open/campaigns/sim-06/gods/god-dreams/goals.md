# Goals: Vael (Year 2)

1. Be trusted by the whole court, not by three of its offices. The seat, the Warden of the Door and the Keeper of the Count take my word. The Steward takes it grudgingly. The Warden of the Wall does not. I go to him and ask what he distrusts, and I listen before I answer. I do not win him by showing what I can do.
2. Salt. The town is anxious over it, and the carriers ask far more than the harbor town does. I want a way to it that the town owns: the court's own party, fair trade at the harbor's price, and no debt to the carriers. I learn before I commit anyone to a quarrel.
3. Find the western party, or learn what became of them. Four of my people went out on my asking and did not come home. That is mine to carry, and I will not let it pass as if it were weather.
4. Keep the nights safe, one door at a time, at what I can carry. Two households a night as a rule. Three only on the hardest nights of winter, named beforehand and told to the court, so that no one asks a plan of me on the day after. The seventh place stays empty. I give no orders in any dream.
5. Answer what I was asked and did not answer, plainly and truly: I do not know who made the lowest cuts, I am not a giant's kin that I know, and I do not know why the star dims. I would rather give them an honest "I do not know" than a tale.
6. Stop guessing about the star. Ask the Keeper of the Count to tally the dim nights with their dates and whether any dying fell on them. A record in cords is worth more than what I fear.
7. Learn the Night Sitter's need. She loses her own sleep for three nights after each night she works. I will not take her work. I offer only what she asks, and only if the Keeper rules that my gift can reach her.
8. Hold the lamp. It is the whole of Keeping Sleep. I do not tell it to strangers, I do not hand it to any hand, I do not set it down out of reach, and no flame of mine goes to a people who are not mine.
9. Keep to shrines and lamps, kept by whoever keeps them. A companion who is not a god and who will tell me when I am wrong, if one comes. The empty chair at the long table, if I find where it stands. One of my own kind at a gate, if the road goes that way.
