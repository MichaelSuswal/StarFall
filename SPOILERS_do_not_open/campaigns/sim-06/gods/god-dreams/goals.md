# Goals: Vael (Year 5)

1. Pay what I owe in answers, in the first month. Three questions stand unanswered from Year 4. The captain: if a lying hind is not me in a dream, what has the wall been sitting. The Warden of the Wall: which house is nearest to a broken body. The young barrow singers: whether I will hear what they have of the third song. I give words to each, plainly, in the places named in my decisions, and I keep what I say to the letter. I also answer the Warden of the Door: I meant up, not down.

2. Say true things in the right direction. I sent a word that put my town four days down the river from its mouth, and it lies four days up. A father was told a wrong thing at his door on a roll that nobody had struck. I ask the court who gave me a thing before I carry it, and I check what I say against the people who know the ground.

3. Hear the third song. The oldest of the barrow singers died and two parts in three of the song live in two young households. I go to them by day and listen to the end. I ask the hamlet headmen, through the court's runners, whether an old singer in any hamlet holds the third part. I cannot take a song from a sleeper who keeps no flame, and I will not.

4. Send the delta a corrected word, and carry it part of the way myself. The factor will not carry it. I send it by the court's own party, hold it for any carrier outside his house, and send the factor a separate plain question about the road. In summer, with the court's leave and the Warden of the Wall's days told to him, I walk to the harbor and no farther, to ask the factor to his face, as a guest asks, and to learn how long the road to the delta is. I do not go to a place I cannot see. I am afraid of the road, and I go to the harbor anyway, so that fear is not what keeps the door.

5. Leave the nights in good hands. I go under the rule the four offices gave me. While I am away no more than two households a night are kept, the Keeper of the Count names them, the keepers of the chain light them in my name, and the fear still reaches me at dawn wherever I walk. I hold the lamp on my waist the whole way.

6. Keep the road to salt as the town's own, and help it find iron. The iron is soft, the picks wear twice as fast, and the old iron is a third down. I ask the Steward and the old cutter to judge whether the stone should go to the harbor again this year, and whether the house will sell iron or picks for it. The court holds the bargain and the cord. I send no flame and no spears.

7. Keep the nights, one door at a time. Two households a night by lot as a rule. Three only on named nights of deep winter, told the day before. The seventh place stays empty. I give no orders in any dream.

8. Keep the star honest. Seven dim nights this year, and the count rises. I give the Keeper of the Count my list of afraid days again and ask what the wall sees on the lying nights. I do not claim the star and I do not clear myself.

9. Be a guest to the sitters. The Night Sitter sleeps more and the hamlet girl can sit two nights in ten, and her hands shake. I sit beside them with my hands for the lamp and the kettle, and take no work. I ask the seat whether the girl's household is heard.

10. Hold the lamp. I do not tell it to strangers, I do not hand it to any hand, I do not set it down out of reach, and I send no flame to a people who are not mine. Any god who learns the lamp can take my people's kept sleep by taking it. If one comes to my gate, I meet it with the lamp on my waist.

11. Be reached through the court. The Warden of the Door takes me for the one awaited and has said so. The Warden of the Wall has neither taken nor refused me. The Steward took my word grudgingly and has not changed. The seat holds the bargain as ours. I do not press any of them and I do not rule by my dreams. A companion who is not a god and who will tell me when I am wrong, if one comes. The empty chair at the long table, if I find where it stands.
