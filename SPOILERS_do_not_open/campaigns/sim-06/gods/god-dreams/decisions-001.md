# Decisions: Vael, Year 1

Year 1 runs from the hour before first light in autumn, round the four seasons to autumn again. I am a hind to the waist down and I move at a hind's walk. I cannot pass a low or barred door, and I cannot climb a ladder. I have no helper who has heard me yet.

## How I mean to be met
I come openly, on four legs, alone, with my lamp lit on its cord at my waist. I do not hide and I do not send word ahead, for I have no one to send. I ask at the gate for the Warden of the Door, as a guest asks. If the gate is shut or the watch will not fetch him, I wait outside it in plain sight, and I do not force it, call down any power on it, or leave. If I am asked who I am, I answer truly and plainly: that I am called Vael, that I woke on the stony ground above the town in the dark before this morning, that I do not know why, and that I came to be a guest. I speak of the lamp only as a lamp. I do not say what it does.

## Actions

1. Autumn, the first morning. I walk down from the stony ground to the gate of the town in the south-east, about a mile, at the pace of a hind, as the light comes. I ask the watch for the Warden of the Door and ask to be let in as a guest, for the bread and the place the custom gives for three nights. I do not ask for a wide gate or a hall yet; if the gate will not take my width, I say so only when asked, and I ask then. Reason: the people keep an empty place and a lit lamp for a guest who comes in the night, and I would be taken in by their custom and not by their fear. I would be known first as one who asks.

2. Autumn, the three nights of guesting. I stay in the town, sit at the Dusk Table where I am put, eat what I am given, and speak first of nothing. I listen: what they fear, what the winter looks like to them, what the store holds. I do not use Walk the Dream, for no household keeps a shrine of mine, and I do not light any flame. On the third night, when the custom lets me be asked my business, I answer from the rule above. I will tell the Warden of the Door that the lamp I carry makes a flame that may be set in a house's niche, that I light it only where it is asked for, and that it is not a due. Reason: I want to be a guest before I am anything else, and I want them to see I keep their custom.

3. Late autumn, before the first hard cold. If the Warden of the Door and the throne take me for the one awaited and hear me, I ask, through the Warden of the Door and by the throne's leave, to light a flame at the court's own hearth from my lamp. Then I light a flame at each household that asks, beginning with the households of a deathbed, a newborn, a fever or a fresh grief. The court's runners may carry word that I light only where asked. I keep Keeping Sleep to two households a night this year, from the households the Keeper of the Count draws by lot among those who asked, and I keep the seventh place empty until I know my heart. The cost of the fear arrives at dawn whether I am in the town or not. I walk it as my own and do not hide it; the flames near me dip when I am afraid, and I say so if I am asked why. Reason: nights must be made safer before the cold, but I do not yet know what this fear does to my judgment in this place, and I will not spend myself on seven before I have carried two.

4. Winter, in the town. I stay inside the walls, in the hall or the yard where I am lodged. I ask the Warden of the Door to take me to the Night Sitter and her pupil, and I sit beside her through a night of the very sick without working. I tell her I will not take her work from her. I ask the Steward of the Store, through the Warden of the Door, to count the grain, the fuel and the salt aloud before the council, and I ask the Keeper of the Count for the roll of households that have asked for a flame. In households that keep my flame, and only in those, I may enter a dying sleeper's dream so that none in my keeping dies alone. I give no orders in any dream, and I tell the court plainly that a dream is a dream and not a command, as I have always meant to. Reason: the winter is the first test of whether I am worth the table, and I am a thin body while I walk a dream, so I will do it only where I am safe in a kept house, with the door held.

5. Spring, when the river is high. I go out with an escort the Warden of the Wall chooses, along the road to the nearest hamlets, and I walk the bottom land and the flood line with the headmen. I ask each hamlet what it wants, light a flame only where asked, and learn which hamlets have a Night Sitter. I ask the Warden of the Wall to keep the beacon watch as it is, to send no spears out because of me, and to tell me of any stranger on the roads. I ask the Steward of the Store, with the headmen, where salt might be had from neighbours, and what the town has to trade. I do not enter any pen, net, narrow road, or barred door that could hold a hind. Reason: I need the ground and the people before I need a plan, and I would rather the hamlets see me than hear of me.

6. Summer, the dry months. I go up to the scar where stone is cut, about a mile to the north-west, and sit with the cutters through a day, then I walk the wall with the Warden of the Wall and ask what it lacks. If the court asks it, I ask the Warden of the Door to send a small party of the court's runners, not spears, up the road toward the hill country to the west and the river downstream, to learn what neighbours lie there and what salt they have, and to carry back no quarrel. I stay in the town and its hamlets for the year. I do not go to the hills or the mountains myself in Year 1. Reason: a hind walks slowly, and I will not be far from the door while my people are not yet used to my face. What lies beyond is learned by runners first and by me later.

## What I ask or tell my people to do, and through whom
All of it goes through the Warden of the Door to the throne, and the throne speaks it from the wall at dusk if it is a word to the whole people. I ask only these things, and I order none: that a household which wants my flame may ask for it, and no household be shamed that does not; that the Keeper of the Count keep the lots and the roll; that the Steward of the Store count the winter aloud; that the Warden of the Wall keep the beacon watch and tell me of strangers; and that the court's runners carry a word to the hamlets that I light only where asked. I take nothing as a due. I give no word in any dream.

## Messages
None. I have no one to send to, and I know of no one who is not in front of me.

## For the Keeper to rule
1. Whether a flame lit at the court's own hearth counts as a household kept for me, and whether it counts within the seven. I mean to use it as a gift to the town, and if it counts as one of the seven, I ask the Keeper to tell me so I may account for it.
2. Whether the fear I carry at dawn for a kept household counts when I am in a town of people who keep a flame and are afraid for their own reasons in the same night. I take the rule as written: only the fear of the night's sleep, never of a present danger. I ask the Keeper to confirm that a household's grief at a deathbed is the fear of the night's sleep only where it is fear of the sleep, and not grief itself.
3. I do not mean to grow any power this year. If the winter drives me to try something my card does not plainly cover, I will say so in my next year's decisions and not act on it unasked.
