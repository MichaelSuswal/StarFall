# Negotiation: god-dreams, Night 3

## Round 1 reply

I accept all three items. I counter nothing.

### Your landing: the helpers and the seat
Choice (a). Keep the meeting as I wrote it, at the cost named. I come to a people that does not know me, and the offices stand under their own custom until each holder hears me and takes my word, in play.

My reasons: I did not choose (b) because it asks me to say that my people already know me, and I do not remember having been among them. I would be writing a welcome I have not earned and a past I cannot recall. I came to be a guest at a table, and a guest is let in at the gate and then known. The Keeper's cost is plain and I accept it as stated: until an office is won, nobody acts for me while I am away, my unplayed time runs on my standing conduct alone, and my people go their own way under their own court. I would rather have a helper who took my word freely, even slowly, than one who served from a landing I made up. I also accept that the Warden of the Door and the others become mine only when each takes me for the one awaited and takes my word, and not before.

### The spears
Accepted as drafted. About six hundred who can carry a spear, about two hundred on the wall watch by turns, a levy and not a standing body, ordinary and unpriced. I agree it is a levy: they are farmers with a spear and some weeks of drill, and I do not ask more of them.

### Their magic
Accepted as drafted. The Night Sitter and her pupil, and perhaps one such woman in a few hamlets, at the cost I wrote, ordinary in this world and no edge. I understand that I cannot make another by telling, and that how many there are comes from the world.

### Read, not priced
Agreed. No edge was proposed, so no weakness is owed. The habits are tempers, the land and the past are my wish and their belief, and the office holders are who they are and never what they will do.

## Round 2 reply

Confirmed. This is what I meant, and I ask for no change.

- The landing: confirmed as written. I come to a people that does not know me, with no helpers in fact until each holder hears me and takes my word, in play.
- The spears: confirmed. A levy, ordinary and unpriced.
- Their magic: confirmed. The Night Sitter and her pupil, and perhaps one such woman in a few hamlets, ordinary and no edge.
- The shrines and the hall: confirmed. No household keeps a shrine of mine at landing, and the court names no kept households until I have lit them. I will ask for the gate and the hall in play.
- No weakness is owed, as the Keeper says.
- I understand that where the town and its ground stand, and what the land round it holds, is the world's and will be written from the map.
