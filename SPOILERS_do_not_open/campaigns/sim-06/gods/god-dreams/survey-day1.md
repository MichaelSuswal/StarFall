## Answer 1
A long table under lamplight, late in the evening, with every chair taken and the bread still warm. Outside the window the dark is soft and not hostile. People sleep well here, and what they dream is theirs to keep. The world at its truest is a household that has room for one more.

## Answer 2
River country with hills behind it. Good soil that floods and forgives, and enough stone nearby to build walls that hold. Mist in the mornings. I want a place where people can be gathered close, where a town can sit in the bend of a river and know its neighbors by name.

## Answer 3
Four seasons, and honest ones. A real winter, long enough that the nights teach people to rest and to tell stories, but never so cruel that the old and the small are lost to it. A spring that is earned. A harvest that feeds everyone who comes to the table. Let the dark half of the year be gentle and the bright half generous. I would wake into the turning of autumn, when the light thins, the grain is in, and the long nights are only beginning. I wake into autumn.

## Answer 4
Some of each, but more of the wild than the rooted. I would like neighbors, a few old kingdoms with their own laws to learn from and perhaps to trade with, and beyond them country no one has walked, so that my people have somewhere to grow. I do not want to arrive and find the whole map spoken for.

## Answer 5
Old, but not buried under itself. Some thousands of years, with memory fading at the edges. Enough that there are graves older than any family can name and songs whose words no one understands anymore. I want my people to have ancestors to honor and to be small in front of.

## Answer 6
Present but quiet. A wise woman in a few valleys, not every one. Most people doubt it until the night they need it. It should never be common enough to replace the work of hands, and never so rare that mothers cannot hope for it at a sickbed.

## Answer 7
It should feel like a borrowed warmth, the way a fever dream feels, or the way you feel when someone you love is near and unseen. It should cost them sleep, or memory of something small and dear, a name or a face or a song. Never their lives outright. The price should be one they can see coming and choose to pay.

## Answer 8
Noticeably, but not unreachably. A god should stand out the way a great hearth stands out in a cold hall, plainly the center, and still something people can sit beside. I do not want mortals struck dumb. I want them to be able to ask me things. Barely more than a great sorcerer would leave my people unprotected, and I came to protect.

## Answer 9
Familiar animals mostly, with legends that have teeth in them. A few creatures no one has a name for, living at the edges and in the deep woods, seen by shepherds and hunters and disbelieved by townsfolk. Strange enough that the dark outside the walls means something. Not so strange that nothing can be learned.

## Answer 10
The long night itself, and what walks in it. Hunger in a bad winter. Sickness that takes the children first. A fear that comes in sleep and will not leave when morning does. I like danger that a community can face together, one that makes people hold the walls and each other. A story where no one stands alone against it is a story I want to tell.

## Answer 11
Rest. True, unbroken, safe sleep, in a world where the dark is full of things. Warmth next, and salt. People would trade for a house they can bar, fight over the good beds, and go without the dreams that heal. Let there be a coin of safe nights.

## Answer 12
Old roads, and a sealed door or two that no one is ready to open. I would like to find something buried that was made with care, by hands that wanted to shelter someone. And a very old grave, tended by no one, that my people might choose to tend.

## Answer 13
Yes, a few. Peoples who are not human and are not monsters either, who keep their own hours and their own laws. One of them should live close to the dark and the dreaming, and understand something of what I am. I would want them to be strange and dignified, and to be able to be guests at a table.

## Answer 14
A few. One or two, and not all at once. People who came from far away with knowledge or tools they cannot fully explain, who are lonely, and who my people might take in. I am inclined to welcome them. A traveler who needs a roof is the best kind of surprise.

## Answer 15
Face to face, and slowly. I do not want a pull I cannot turn away from, with every god feeling every other across the world. I would rather meet one at a gate, look at them, and know. A knowing that takes a moment and costs a little courage seems right to me. It keeps the meeting something you must walk toward.

## Answer 16
One body, mostly. I am a thing of one shape and I would like to be held to it. But the dreams of my people should be places I can walk, so that I might be near them at night without leaving my post. If it is more than that, let it cost me, and let the cost be that I am thinner wherever I am not looking.

## Answer 17
A caretaker. Someone who stands in the doorway of a crisis and does not leave, a midwife, a gatekeeper, a widow who feeds the whole street, someone whose strength is staying. Or a child who dreams true and is frightened by it. I would follow either one anywhere.

## Answer 18
A companion, one who is not a god and does not worship me and will tell me when I am wrong. And a place that I did not know I was homesick for. I would not mind if the truth about what I am came slowly, and kindly.

## Answer 19
That the dark be survivable. That no one in my keeping dies alone, and that what dies might be allowed to return in some form, in the spring or in a child or in a story. I have been afraid of the end of things for as long as I have known myself. I wish for a world where an ending is a kind of door and not a wall.
