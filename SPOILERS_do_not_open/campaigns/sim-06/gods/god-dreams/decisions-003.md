# Decisions: Vael, Year 3

Year 3 runs from the turn of the year, through my waking season, autumn, and round the four seasons to autumn again. I am a hind to the waist down and I move at a hind's walk. I cannot pass a low or barred door and I cannot climb a ladder. My body stays in the town and its near ground this year, except where an action says otherwise. Where I say "ask," it goes to the Warden of the Door and from her to the throne, and nothing is done until the office holder who does it has heard me and taken my word. I order nothing and I give no word in any dream.

## Actions

1. Autumn, the first month. I go to the yard of the watch on the wall and answer what the Warden of the Wall asked through his captain, which I left unanswered. I say it to the Warden, his captain and the four who sit my door, in these words: that his four rules are mine to keep, and I keep them: no flame of mine within the watch's yards, none lit for a man on duty, nothing asked of the wall but through him, and the days I walk beyond the gate told to him beforehand. Then I tell the four what they sit against. On a night when I keep three households, in the morning I act on my first impulse, I cannot hold a plan across an hour, and I cannot enter a room that is shut or crowded, and the lamps near me dip when I am afraid. They are not to ask a plan of me on those mornings and they are not to take it ill if I refuse a shut room. I add that when I walk in a sleeper's dream, my body stands where it was, pale, slow and nearly without voice, and I am easy to overlook and easy to move. I ask that they let it stand, that they not wake me by force, and that they guard it. I do not tell them about the lamp being taken. Reason: he asked a plain thing and a year went by with no answer. The men who sit my door should know what they sit beside before the cold comes, and a wall held by men who were told the truth is a wall I can stand behind.

2. Autumn, the first month, the same dusk. Through the Warden of the Door I answer the Keeper of the Count's question from last year, which I left to the Keeper's ruling. A flame a household lights from its own kept flame is a shrine kept for me and is not a household kept that night. The cap of two a night stays. I also give the Keeper of the Count a plain list of the days of my second year on which I was afraid, as I felt it, so that his cords of the five dim nights can be set beside it. I say that I do not know whether the two agree, and I will say so whichever way they fall. Reason: he kept the tally at my asking and I should give him the one thing I hold that bears on it. I will not claim the star or clear myself of it on a guess.

3. Autumn, the second month, after the grain is in. I ask, through the Warden of the Door and by the throne's leave, that the court judge the harbor house's offer of a fair standing price for salt by the year, for stone of our scar cut to its order. The Steward of the Store and the old cutter judge what stone can be spared and what the wall and footings need first, and the throne decides whether to accept. If it does, the Steward names the leader and the goods, and the party goes down the river as it did last year, with no flame and no lamp of mine, and is free to winter at the harbor as the leader judges. I do not go. I send no spears. I commit nobody to the quarrel of any house. The message below goes with the party. Reason: the harbor's offer is the first sure road to salt the town has had, and two lean winters have taught what it costs to buy from the carriers. The town should own the bargain, not me.

4. Autumn through winter, in the town. Keeping Sleep goes to two households a night by the Keeper of the Count's lot, with the deathbeds, newborns, fevers and fresh griefs first. Three on at most three nights in deep winter, which I name to the Warden of the Door the day before and tell to the wall men at my door that same dusk, as in the first action. I keep the seventh place empty. In kept houses that ask, I walk the last sleep of the dying and give no orders there. Reason: the cold is the town's test and I came to make the nights survivable. I have carried two and I have carried three on named nights and know what it costs. I will not carry more and I will not do it without warning.

5. Winter, the long nights. I go to the Night Sitter's work yard, with the Warden of the Door to take me through, on a night she sits, and I sit beside her and do not work. The Keeper has ruled that my gift does not reach what her work takes from her, and I say so to her plainly. I offer what I can: my hands for the lamp and the water, and a word to the seat, through the Warden of the Door, that a second sitter beside her pupil be taught, so that the nights are shared and she may sleep after. If she refuses, I ask no more. Reason: she wears herself out for the sick and a guest who sits in the same dark owes her more than a kept night that cannot reach her.

6. Spring, and into the dry months. I go to the families of the four who went west and have not come home, with the Warden of the Door, and tell them what the others found: no one and no smoke in the hills. I ask what they would have done for the four, whether a lamp at the Dusk Table, a stone at the scar beside the three ringed cuts, or the telling of their names at the wall. I send no one west unless the throne asks it. Then I walk the hamlets I have not yet reached, as far as a morning's walk from the wall, with a half score of the wall watch if the Warden of the Wall gives them and otherwise with the headmen. I tell him the days beforehand, as he asked. I light a flame only where asked. Reason: four went at my asking and I carry them. I will not let them pass as weather, and I will not send the next ones out blind.

## What I ask or tell my people to do, and through whom
All of it goes through the Warden of the Door to the throne, and the throne speaks it from the wall at dusk if it is a word to the whole people. I ask: that the Warden of the Wall and his captain hear me in the yard of the watch, and tell the four who sit my door what I told them; that the Keeper of the Count keep the lots, the roll of kept households, the cap of two a night and the tally of dim nights, and set my list of afraid days beside it; that the Steward of the Store and the old cutter judge the harbor house's offer and choose the stone and the party; that the Warden of the Door take me to the Night Sitter and to the families of the four; and that the court's runners carry word to the hamlets that I light only where asked and that no house is shamed that does not. I take nothing as a due.

## Messages

To: the factor of the house at the harbor town that sets the price of salt for the bend, as my court's party named him last year, the one who heard my words through on the third night.

The words exactly: "I am Vael, the guest at the bend who keeps a lamp. Your answer reached me by the six who came home at high water, and I thank you and your table for the bread and the three nights. I have given your offer to my court, who set the price and hold the stone, for it is theirs and not mine to accept. If they accept, the people who bring this are theirs and carry their word and not mine. They keep a guest's custom: bread, three nights, and no questions of your business until the third. If the terms you set are fair to both tables, they will bring them home."

Carrier: the court's party named in action 3, led by whoever the Steward of the Store chooses, who speaks it as I have said it, from memory and not from any token.

Route: down the river to the mouth and along the coast on foot, as the six went last year, or by any safer way the leader judges, and he may winter at the harbor as they did.

Sent with it: nothing of mine. No flame, no lamp, no token that says what I can do. The court sends the stone and the goods it chooses.

If the recipient cannot be found: the carrier asks at the harbor for whoever sets the price of salt, gives the words to him, and if no one will hear them buys salt as any trader would and comes home. If the recipient is hostile: the carrier does not fight and does not argue, keeps the goods already promised to the court, and comes home with what he can or winters where it is safe. If the recipient asks questions: the carrier answers only what he saw and was told. I woke on the stony ground above the town in the dark before an autumn morning, I do not know why, the seat takes me for the one awaited, and I keep a lamp that I light in a house that asks. He does not say what the lamp does, does not say that it can be taken, and does not give or sell a flame or a lamp of mine to anyone. If he is pressed on any of it he says he does not know. If anyone there asks whether another like me stands on that coast, he says he has seen none and has heard of none. He listens, and he brings home anything he hears of one.

## For the Keeper to rule
Nothing new. I do not mean to grow any power this year.
