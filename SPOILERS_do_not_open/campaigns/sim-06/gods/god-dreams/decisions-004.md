# Decisions: Vael, Year 4

Year 4 runs from the turn of the year, through my waking season, autumn, and round the four seasons to autumn again. I am a hind to the waist down and I move at a hind's walk. I cannot pass a low or barred door and I cannot climb a ladder. My body stays in the town and its near ground this year, within a morning's walk of the wall, except where an action says otherwise. Where I say "ask," it goes to the Warden of the Door and from her to the throne, and nothing is done until the office holder who does it has heard me and taken my word. I order nothing and I give no word in any dream.

## Actions

1. Autumn, the first month. I go to the yard of the watch on the wall and answer the two questions put to me there, to the captain and the four who sit my door, with the Warden of the Wall by if he will. To the captain, who asked how a man is to know the standing body from the sleeping one: when I walk a dream, my body stands where it was and does not lie down. It is dim and slow, it will not walk more than a step, and it will not speak above a murmur. I do not sleep, so a hind lying down is not me in a dream. A standing body that answers only in a murmur is me in a sleeper's dream, and it is not to be moved, startled or held without need. If a man is in doubt he asks me and waits, and if I do not answer above a murmur he lets me stand. To the one of the four who asked whether they are to move me if fire comes into the yard: yes. Move me. Take me by the cord at my waist and lead me out, and leave the lamp on the cord and on me. Harm that lands on my body while I am in a dream I feel and do not resist, and I would rather be moved than burned. If my body is broken I am thrown out of the dream, and the break stands, and I return after a season at the nearest house with a lamp burning through the night. I tell them this too, so they are not afraid for me past what is true. I do not tell them the lamp can be taken. Reason: I left both questions a year and they were asked in earnest by men who sit my door. They should know what to do before the cold, and a rule I speak I hold to the letter.

2. Autumn, the first month, the same week. Through the Warden of the Door I go to the second house of the four who went west and answer the father who asked me "Who told you they had not come home?" I say: I was told it through the court, by way of the Warden of the Door, and I carried it to your door as true. I did not ask whose word it was first. It was wrong, and your son came home in the first month of last year, thin and alive. I should have asked, and I am sorry that you were told so at your door. I do not know whose word it first was, and I will not name one. I ask the Warden of the Door whether she knows, and I say whichever answer she gives. From now on I ask who gave a thing to me before I carry it to a house. Reason: he asked a plain thing and was owed a plain answer, and I will not let a wrong thing I carried be left standing because it is hard to say. I also give the Keeper of the Count my plain list of the days of my third year on which I was afraid, as I felt it, so that his cords of the six dim nights can be set beside it. I say I do not know whether the two agree, and I will say so whichever way they fall.

3. Autumn, the second month, after the grain is in. I ask, through the Warden of the Door and by the throne's leave, that the court judge the harbor house's standing order for this year: sixty blocks dressed to the cord it gave, to be lifted by its own ship at the river's mouth. The Steward of the Store and the old cutter judge what stone can be spared and what the wall and footings need first, and the throne decides whether to send it and who carries it. The people who carry it are the court's and carry its word, and I send no flame, no lamp and no token of mine with them. I do not go. I send no spears. Reason: the order is the town's first sure road to salt, the party came home last year safe and all eight, and the town should hold the road and the bargain itself. I ask the Steward also that the leader of this party, if the court sends one, be one who listens as well as trades.

4. Autumn, the second month, with the stone party. I send a word to the one in the delta market town who says it is a god. The message below goes with the court's party to the harbor and to the factor of the house, to be carried on by the house's ship or carriers. I do not send anyone to fetch the stranger or to spy on it. I ask the court's leader only to listen again on the quays and bring home what he hears, as before. Reason: I have felt none of my kind in three years. One report is one report, and I do not know what it is. But it is a thing I can answer with a word, and I will not let it go by because I am afraid of what I do not know. I will not go myself. I cannot judge from here whether it is a god, a man or a tale, and I will not leave the nights and the lamp on a guess.

5. Winter, the long nights. Keeping Sleep goes to two households a night by the Keeper of the Count's lot, with deathbeds, newborns, fevers and fresh griefs first. Three only on named nights of deep winter, told the day before to the Warden of the Door and to the wall men at my door that same dusk, as in the first action. The seventh place stays empty. In kept houses that ask, I walk the last sleep of the dying and give no orders there. I sit beside the Night Sitter and her new pupil, the hamlet girl, on a night they sit, if the Night Sitter will have me, with my hands for the lamp and the kettle and no work taken. I ask the seat, through the Warden of the Door, whether the girl's household is heard by the court on what she needs. Reason: the cold is the town's test and I came to make the nights survivable. I have carried three on named nights and I know what it costs. The girl found she could, and a girl who finds she can is owed a seat at the table.

6. Spring, when the river is high. I ask the Warden of the Door, the Warden of the Wall, the Steward of the Store and the Keeper of the Count, together if the throne will have it, what the court would want done if I were away from the town for a season or more: who names the seven households on a night I am away and tends the chain of flames, who answers a question put to me while I am gone, how the Warden of the Wall wants the days of my absence told to him, and what the town would do if the one from the delta or any other asks for me by name. I listen to the end, I write what they say down as a rule and I keep it. I do not leave. Then I walk the hamlets within a morning's walk of the wall, with the days told to the Warden of the Wall beforehand and a few of the watch if he gives them, and light a flame only where asked. Reason: if the road goes west, I will not be a god who vanishes and leaves her people's nights to drift. I want the answers in hand a year before I need them.

## What I ask or tell my people to do, and through whom

All of it goes through the Warden of the Door to the throne, and the throne speaks it from the wall at dusk if it is a word to the whole people. I ask: that the Warden of the Wall and his captain hear me in the yard of the watch and tell the four who sit my door what I told them; that the Warden of the Door take me to the second house of the four, and tell me whether she knows whose word it was; that the Keeper of the Count keep the lots, the roll of kept households, the cap of two a night, and the tally of dim nights, and set my list of afraid days beside it; that the Steward of the Store and the old cutter judge the harbor's order and the throne choose the party; that the court's leader carry the message below and listen on the quays; that the Warden of the Door take me to the Night Sitter's yard and carry the girl's case to the seat; and that the four offices answer me what the town would do if I am away. I take nothing as a due. I give no word in any dream.

## Messages

To: the one in the delta market town in the west, who is seated as keeper of the town's weights and says before the market that it is a god. I do not know its name. If the carrier cannot name it, he asks for the one who reads the scales.

The words exactly: "I am Vael. I am half a woman and half a hind, and I keep a lamp in a town in a river bend, four days down the river from its mouth. I have heard that you say before the market that you are a god. I do not know whether you are. I do not ask you to prove it. If you are one like me, I would like to meet you face to face, at a gate, as a guest meets a house, and I will ask nothing of you but to know you. I tell you where I am so that you may come or not as you choose. I do not send anyone to bring you and I do not ask what you can do. If you are not one like me, I wish you a good market, and I ask you to forgive a stranger's question. The people who bring this are guests at your table, and they keep a guest's custom: bread, three nights, and no questions of your business until the third. If you will send me a word, they will carry it home."

Carrier: the court's party named in action 3, led by whoever the throne and the Steward choose, who speaks it as I have said it, from memory and not from any token. At the harbor he gives it to the factor of the house that took the stone, with the court's own words, and asks that the house's ship or carriers take it on to the delta. If the factor will not, or the house has no road there, the leader may go on by the coast if the court allowed it before he left, and otherwise he comes home and says so.

Route: down the river to the mouth, along the shore to the harbor, and from the harbor to the delta market town by whatever road the house's carriers or the coasting ships use, as the leader or the factor judges safest.

Sent with it: nothing of mine. No flame, no lamp, no token that says what I can do. A guest's gift of linen, pots or oil at the Steward's judgment, from the court.

If the recipient cannot be found: the carrier leaves the words with the factor, asks at the market for whoever keeps the weights, gives them to that man, and if none will hear them he buys what he needs as any trader would and comes home. If the recipient is hostile: the carrier does not fight and does not argue, keeps the goods already promised to the court, and comes home with what he can, or winters where it is safe. If the recipient asks questions: the carrier answers only what he saw and was told. I woke on the stony ground above the town in the dark before an autumn morning, I do not know why, the seat takes me for the one awaited, and I keep a lamp that I light in a house that asks. He does not say what the lamp does, does not say that it can be taken, does not give or sell a flame or a lamp of mine to anyone, and does not say how many days I walk or which nights I keep. If he is pressed on any of it he says he does not know. If the stranger says it is a god, the carrier says what he heard and says no more. He does not say it is false and does not say it is true. He listens, he brings home any answer, and he brings home any word of another like me on that coast.

## For the Keeper to rule

Nothing new. I do not mean to grow any power this year.
