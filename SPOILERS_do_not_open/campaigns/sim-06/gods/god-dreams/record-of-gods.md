# Record of other gods: Vael

## Others like me
I have now lived three years on the ground and have felt none of them. No weight in the air at the gate, in the hall, on the wall, at the scar, on the hamlet roads or at the Night Sitter's yard. No one like me has come to my door and no word has been sent me. I hold nothing against any god and nothing for any, and no promise is made to me or owed by me.

What is new this year is one report, and I weigh it for what it is. My court's leader listened on the harbor's quays, as I told him to, and brought home this: at a market town in the delta of the great river in the west, a stranger who reads scales for a pinch of barley has been seated as keeper of the town's weights, and says before the whole market that it is a god. It is one man's hearing, carried home by one leader from quay talk, and no one at the harbor was asked whether another like me stands on that coast. I have not seen the stranger and the leader did not. I do not know that it is a god. It may be a god who speaks plainly, a god who plays, a mortal who has learned that the word draws trade, or a tale grown on the way. A man who reads scales and sits among the weights is a man the market has a use for, and I do not know what use. I hold all of these open.

If it is one like me, it has not hidden itself, and it has taken an office from a market. That says something about what it wants, and I do not know what. It does not say whether it is kind, whether it has people, or whether it has heard of me. The harbor knows of me, and the harbor trades with the delta, so I must think it may have heard. It may know where I am and that I keep a lamp. It may know less. I told the harbor nothing of what the lamp does, and I will tell this stranger nothing of it either.

My guess, which may be wrong, is unchanged: others stand on ground of their own with their own people, and the land between is long. The western party of four found no one in the hills, and I do not take that to mean no one stands there.

The star dimmed on six nights of my third year, against five in my second and three in my first: one in the first month, a pair in the fifth, a pair in the seventh and one in the eighth. Each time the blue deepened and the star stood dim about an hour in the middle of the night. Fourteen since I came. I felt nothing in myself on those nights that I can name. It may be the world, or another of my kind, or a thing no god does. The count is rising, and I now ask whether it rises with something far off that I cannot feel. I do not claim the star and I do not blame any god for it.

I still mean to know one of my own kind face to face, at a gate, and I do not mean to be pulled to it. I have now a place to send a word. I may send one. I may not go.

## Entries by name
The one in the delta market town (rumored, unnamed, never seen). Heard of in Year 3 through my court's leader, from quay talk at the harbor. Says before the market that it is a god. Keeper of the town's weights by the market's seating. Reads scales for a pinch of barley. I have no name for it, no sight of it and no word from it. Trust: none given and none withheld. Grievance: none. Promise: none either way. Guess about its nature and powers: none that I will hold. I will send it a plain word asking to meet at a gate, and I will weigh what comes back, if anything does, by what it asks of me.
