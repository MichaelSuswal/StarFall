# Record of other gods: Vael

## Others like me
I have now lived four years on the ground and have felt none of them. No weight in the air at the gate, in the hall, on the wall, at the scar, on the hamlet roads or at the Night Sitter's yard. No one like me has come to my door and no word has been sent me. I hold nothing against any god and nothing for any, and no promise is made to me or owed by me.

What I have of the one in the delta is still one man's hearing, now heard twice. In Year 3 my court's leader brought home from the quays that a stranger who reads scales for a pinch of barley has been seated as keeper of the weights in a market town in the delta of the great river in the west, and says before the market that it is a god. In Year 4 he listened again and heard nothing new: the stranger is still seated, by last year's talk. This year my own people say it to me unasked: "a stranger in the delta who reads scales and says he is a god, and we do not know whether he is." They say "he". I do not know whether that is a man's shape or only their word. I have not seen the stranger, and no one I trust has.

My word did not reach it. The harbor's factor gave my leader bread and three nights and would not carry my word on, because his house does not carry a stranger's word to a market it sells to. So the stranger has not been asked to meet me, and has not refused. There is one more thing I must hold against my own side. The Warden of the Door tells me that the words I sent said the town lies four days down the river from its mouth, and it lies four days up. A runner at the quay may have misread me, or I may have said it wrong. If any word of mine did reach the delta by another road, it pointed the wrong way, and a god who came looking by it would have come to the wrong place. I will say up.

I do not know that it is a god. It may be one like me who speaks plainly, a god who plays, a mortal who has learned that the word draws trade, or a tale grown on the way. It has taken an office from a market and has not hidden itself, and that says something about what it wants, and I do not know what. It does not say whether it is kind, whether it has people, or whether it has heard of me. The harbor knows of me and trades with the delta, so it may have heard. It may know where I am and that I keep a lamp. It may know less. I will tell it nothing of what the lamp does.

My guess, which may be wrong, is unchanged: others stand on ground of their own with their own people, and the land between is long. The western party of four found no one in the hills, and I do not take that to mean no one stands there.

The star dimmed on seven nights of my fourth year, against six in my third, five in my second and three in my first: in the second, third, fourth, eighth, tenth, eleventh and twelfth months. Twenty-one since I came. The count has risen every year, by one or two. The Keeper of the Count has set his cords beside my list. He says five of the seven I was awake and two I was lying, and that a count shows how many and not why. I felt nothing in myself on those nights that I can name. It may be the world, or another of my kind, or a thing no god does. I do not claim the star and I do not blame any god for it, and I do not clear myself.

I still mean to know one of my own kind face to face, at a gate, and I do not mean to be pulled to it. I have a place to send a word and the word has not gone. I may carry it myself part of the way. I do not mean to go as far as a place I cannot see.

## Entries by name
The one in the delta market town (rumored, unnamed, never seen). Heard of in Year 3 through my court's leader, from quay talk at the harbor, and again in Year 4 and from my own town this year. Says before the market that it is a god. Keeper of the town's weights by the market's seating. Reads scales for a pinch of barley. The town says "he". I have no name for it, no sight of it and no word from it. My word to it did not go on past the harbor, and what I sent said down where it should have said up. Trust: none given and none withheld. Grievance: none. Promise: none either way. Guess about its nature and powers: none that I will hold. I will send it a corrected word asking to meet at a gate, and weigh what comes back, if anything does, by what it asks of me.

The factor of the harbor house (a mortal, not a god). Gave my leader bread and three nights, sold salt at the standing price, and would not carry my word to a market his house sells to. I take that as his house's custom and not as hostility. He is owed a plain thanks and a plain question, not a favor.
