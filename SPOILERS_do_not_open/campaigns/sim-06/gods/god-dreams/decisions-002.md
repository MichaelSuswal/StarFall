# Decisions: Vael, Year 2

Year 2 runs from the turn of the year, through my waking season, autumn, and round the four seasons to autumn again. I am a hind to the waist down and I move at a hind's walk. I cannot pass a low or barred door, and I cannot climb a ladder. My body stays in the town and its near ground this year, except as the actions say. Where I say "ask," it goes to the Warden of the Door and from him to the throne, and nothing is done until the office holder who does it has heard me and taken my word.

## Actions

1. Autumn, the first month. I answer what I was asked and left unanswered. The throne speaks it from the wall at dusk, through the Warden of the Door, in these words: that I do not know why the star dims, that I do not know that I make it dim, that I will not take the credit or the blame, and that I ask the Keeper of the Count to keep a tally of the dim nights, with the dates and whether any dying fell on them, so that the town may judge by its cords and not by my fear or theirs. Then I walk up to the scar, about a mile north-west, and tell the stone cutters to their faces that I do not know who made the lowest cuts and that I do not know that I am a giant's kin, and I ask them to show me the lowest cuts where I can reach them on four legs. I take no cut as mine and say nothing of them to anyone who has not asked. Reason: they asked me straight and I left it, and a guest who leaves a plain question unanswered for a year is not a guest the table can trust. I would rather say "I do not know" than give a tale.

2. Autumn, the first month, after the first. I go to the wall and ask the Warden of the Wall, in the yard of the watch, what he distrusts in me and what he would have me do or not do. I listen to the end. I do not argue or show him anything. I ask him only that the beacon watch be kept as he keeps it, that he tell me of strangers on the roads, and, if he will, that he name one thing I could do for the wall that he would count as honest. Reason: he keeps the town's safety and does not take my word, and a wall held by a man who does not trust me is a wall I cannot ask for anything. I would rather learn his reasons than go around him.

3. Autumn, the second month, after the grain is in. I ask, through the Warden of the Door and by the throne's leave, that the court send a trade party of its own by the river to the harbor town west along the coast where the salt is boiled, with linen, pots and flax oil the Steward of the Store judges the town can spare, to buy salt at the harbor's price and not at the carriers'. The Steward chooses the goods and the leader and the Warden of the Door names the runners who go. The river is four days down by boat and the return ten, and the coast is farther by the sailors' ship, so I ask that the party be back before the hard cold, or else winter at the harbor and come up in spring, as the leader judges. I do not go. I send no spears. I send no flame and no lamp of mine with them, for a flame lit from mine in a stranger's house would make that house kept for me, and its fear would arrive in me at dawn, wherever I am. Reason: salt is the town's worst want and the carriers have learned it. I would rather the town hold its own road to it. The message below goes with them.

4. Autumn through winter, in the town. Keeping Sleep goes to two households a night, as last year, by the Keeper of the Count's lot, with the deathbeds, newborns, fevers and fresh griefs first. I ask the Keeper of the Count and the keepers of the households to see that the number lit on any night, by me or by any keeper lighting in my name, never exceeds two, and I ask the court to name no more than two while I am away. On the hardest nights of deep winter, and only then, I may keep three, and I name those nights to the Warden of the Door the day before, so that the court asks no plan of me on the day after and no one is surprised that I will not enter a shut or crowded room and cannot hold a plan across an hour. I keep the seventh place empty. In kept houses that ask, I walk the last sleep of the dying, as last year, and give no orders in any dream. Reason: the cold is the town's real test and I came to make the nights safe. But I have carried two and not three, and I will not find out what three does to me on a night with no warning and no one told.

5. Winter, the long nights. I ask the Warden of the Door to take me again to the Night Sitter and her pupil, and I sit with them through a night of the very sick, as before, and do not work. I ask the Night Sitter what her work takes from her and what she would have of me, and I tell her I will not take her work from her. If she asks it, I offer to keep her own household's sleep on the nights after she has sat, so that she may sleep. I do not offer it unasked, for I do not know whether my gift can reach the sleeplessness her work gives her, and I will not promise it. Reason: she is the town's keeper of the sick at night, she wears herself out for it, and I am sitting beside the same work in the same dark.

6. Spring, when the river is high, and into the dry months. I ask the Warden of the Door to have the headmen of the hamlets on the western road send word of any sight of the western party since they went, and the Steward of the Store to have the hamlets keep the traders' gossip. If nothing has come home by the first month of spring, I ask the seat to send a small party of four runners, not spears, along the road to the hill country to find the first party or learn what became of them, with the Warden of the Wall's say on who goes. I walk the hamlets I did not reach last year, with an escort of the wall watch if he will give it, and otherwise with the headmen, as far as a morning's walk from the wall, and light a flame only where asked. I stay in the town and its near ground for the year. Reason: four people went at my asking and did not come home. I will not let it be forgotten, and I will not send the next ones out blind.

## What I ask or tell my people to do, and through whom
All of it goes through the Warden of the Door to the throne, and the throne speaks it from the wall at dusk if it is a word to the whole people. I order nothing. I ask: that the Keeper of the Count keep the lots, the roll of kept households, the cap of two lit a night, and the tally of the dim nights; that the Steward of the Store choose the goods and the leader of the salt party and count the winter aloud again; that the Warden of the Wall keep the beacon watch and tell me of strangers; that the court's runners carry word to the hamlets that I light only where asked and that no house is shamed that does not; and that the Warden of the Door take me to the Night Sitter. I take nothing as a due. I give no word in any dream.

## Messages

To: whoever speaks for the harbor town west along the coast where the salt is boiled, as the salt carriers name it; a headman, a harbor master, the one who sets the price.

The words exactly: "I am called Vael. I came down to a town in a river bend, four days up from the river's mouth, in the autumn of last year, and I stayed as a guest, and I light lamps in the houses that ask. Your salt carriers took word of me to you and you asked what I am. I am a guest who stayed, half a woman and half a hind, and I keep a lamp. I ask nothing of you but a fair trade in salt at the price you take at the boil. The people who bring this are guests at your table, and they keep a guest's custom: they ask for bread and three nights and do not ask your business until the third. If you will tell them what you want of us, they will carry it home."

Carrier: the court's trade party named in action 3, led by whoever the Steward of the Store chooses, who speaks it as I have said it, from memory, and not from any token.

Route: down the river to the mouth, and along the coast by the coasting ship's crew, whom the first party met and who know the harbor, or by whatever way the leader judges best. The leader and I have not settled the road, and he may choose a safer one than I would.

Sent with it: linen, pots and flax oil as gifts, at the Steward's judgment. No flame. No lamp of mine. No token that says what I can do.

If the recipient cannot be found: the carrier asks at the harbor for whoever sets the price of salt and gives the words to him, and if no one will hear them he buys salt as any trader would and comes home. If the recipient is hostile: the carrier does not fight, does not argue, does not give up the goods already promised, and comes home with what he can, or winters where it is safe. If the recipient asks questions: the carrier answers only what he saw and was told. I woke on the stony ground above the town in the dark before an autumn morning, I do not know why, the seat takes me for the one awaited, and I keep a lamp that I light in a house that asks. He does not say what the lamp does, does not say that it can be taken, does not give or sell a flame or a lamp of mine to anyone, and if he is pressed on any of it he says he does not know.

## For the Keeper to rule
1. Whether a household kept for me, under Keeping Sleep, can ease the sleeplessness that the Night Sitter's own work lays on her for three nights after she sits. I do not use it unless she asks, and I will not promise it until the Keeper has ruled. My own case: it is the night's fear and weariness it takes, and she is not under a fear, but a mended night is what I mean to give her. I ask whether it reaches her at all, and if it does, what arrives in me at dawn.
2. Whether three households on a named night in deep winter, in a town with the court told beforehand, carries what the card says and no more: that I act on first impulse, cannot hold a plan across an hour, and cannot enter a room that is shut or crowded. I take the card as written and ask only that the Keeper tell me if the town's winter changes it.
3. I do not mean to grow any power this year.
