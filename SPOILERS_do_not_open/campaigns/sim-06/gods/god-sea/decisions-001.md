# Decisions, Year 1: Skerr (god-sea)

## The year

1. Autumn, the first night. I go into the water at the point and lie there with my right hand in the salt, and give the night to listening (Water's Hearing). Reason: I know nothing of this coast. I want to hear what the sea carried in the last day: oars, hulls, voices at the town's shore, anything moving along the bottom. I accept the deaf day after and spend it in the water under the rocks, out of sight. I do not light the lamp and do not go to the town yet.

2. Autumn, the next dusks. I move by water along the shore to the harbor mouth and lie at the foot of the quay's lowest step, hooded, out of the lamp's light, and watch the town for several evenings. I learn who lights the harbor lamp, who walks the quay, who counts the boats in, where the tide-door and the lowest step are, and whether a bell hangs below low water as I hoped. I do not speak and do not show my shape. Reason: I trust no one yet and want to know the town before it knows me. Where I lie a night the shore will go quiet of gulls and seals, and I accept that the fishers may notice.

3. Autumn, before the first storm. I meet one holder only, the keeper of the harbor lamp, at the tide-door at dusk or the lowest tide, in my cloak, in a dim place, and I show my shape only if that holder keeps still and keeps the secret. I say little: that I am the one who keeps the keeping, that I want the boats hauled and the stores counted before the storm, that a word left on the lowest step in a sealed jar, with the bell struck once, will reach me, and that I will answer with a stick and a mark before dawn. I tell the keeper that my lamp is not to be lent or snuffed, and nothing more of it. I ask the keeper to tell the court in the keeper's own way and to name me to no one outside it. If the keeper will not hear me I do not press. I go back to the water and try again at the next season, with another holder. Reason: nobody acts for me until an office holder has heard me and taken my word, and I mean to win one, not take all.

4. Late autumn. Once an office holder has taken my word, I do one piece of Sound Timber at the quay in the dark: a pile or a lowest step first, with my right hand, joined and shaped by me alone. I give the Master of Timber the word that one piece is mine and that he should not say which. Then I keep my hand idle a day. Reason: the quay is what the first storm will test, and I wish to be a smith again, not a watcher only. I do one piece and no more, as the card says. I ask the court, through the keeper, to haul the boats, caulk and tar them, haul the late boats in early, keep the harbor chain ready, and keep the harbor lamp lit every dusk through the dark half of the year.

5. Winter. I keep out of the town. I lie in the water off the headland and the point, and give one more night to listening in the dead of the year, to learn what the winter sea carries to this shore. I keep the lamp in reserve for one night only: if a boat must come in through the harbor mouth in a swell that is not yet a storm, I light it by my own hand at the bow or the water's edge, and the court's lamp-tenders refill it. I understand it will not still a storm, and that water I still I cannot enter for a season. I do not use it for comfort. I tell the people to count the storms and the boats and to leave the tally on the step.

6. Spring, the lean time. I walk the shore west and east of the point, slowly, at an old man's pace and by night, to find the old places, the sunken roads and anything the tide uncovers. I go myself and send no mortal into a bad place. I mark what I find and what the sea took, and I say nothing of it to the town until I know what it is. I tell the court, through the keeper, to keep the stores lean and shared, and to send nothing out in the worst weather for the sake of a catch.

7. Summer. I return to the town's edge and look for a keeper of something small who stays: a ferryman, a midwife, a lamp-tender. I only watch. I do one more piece of Sound Timber, a keel of the boat that goes out latest and comes in last, chosen by the Master of Timber, if the court still takes my word. I say what is mine and what is not, and I ask for nothing in return. Reason: those are the boats the sea will take first, and I will not lose a crew this time.

## How I mean to be met
Rarely, at dusk or the lowest tide, hooded, in a dim place, through the lamp-warden at the tide-door, one holder at a time. I do not show my shape in the open, and I do not give my name beyond Skerr unless asked. My word is short. I do not ask for rites or worship.

## Messages
None. I send no message to anyone not in front of me this year.

## For the Keeper to rule
- The sheet asked for a bell below low water and a jar on the lowest step. I do not know if the town has them. If not, I will use whatever the town has, and I ask the Keeper to tell me in the release.
- Calm Under the Lamp: I plan to use it at most once, and only for a swell short of a storm. If the Keeper holds that the lamp's light covers less than the harbor mouth, I accept the narrower water.
- I try to grow no power this year.
