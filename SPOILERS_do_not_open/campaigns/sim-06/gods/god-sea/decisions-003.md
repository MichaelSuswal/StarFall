# Decisions, Year 3: Skerr (god-sea)

## The year

1. Autumn, the first dusks. I leave the jar its answer and meet the Warden-Monarch alone at the tide door at the lowest tide at dusk, in my cloak, in the dim. I answer her five notches in plain words: I do not cause the star's dimming, I did nothing on those nights, and I do not know what does. I ask her to keep the count on a stick, one notch a night, as she has, and to leave it in the jar each year. I tell her the Eldest Midwife asked me at the lowest step what I count for, and that I mean to answer her. I ask the Warden to carry my words to the Midwife (see Messages), because I do not leave the water for another's house until an office holder has heard me. I thank her for the tenders and for keeping the lamp. I will not take the lamp house bed. Reason: she answered in good faith and has asked nothing of me but the count. She is owed a plain word, and the Midwife is the first to ask me a question to my face and not only to the water.

2. Autumn, the first night after that, in the water at the point. I put my right hand in the salt and give the night to listening (Water's Hearing), then spend the deaf day under the rocks out of sight, and I fix no meeting the day after. Reason: I want to hear again whether anything has come along the coast that is not tide, and the sea carries no sign of Low Garrow's name but may carry its water. I accept that the shore will go quiet where I lie and that the fishers may see it.

3. Autumn, once the Warden has the Midwife's reply, at the lowest tide at dusk. I go, hooded and low, at an old man's pace, to the bedside the Midwife names, if it stands within a short drag of the water, and she is there and the house is quiet and the one in the bed is past knowing or asleep. I say what I count (see Messages) and I hear what she asks. I show my shape only if she asks and keeps still. I take no bed, bread or name. If she wants me gone, I go. I do not go to a house where a child is born or where others sit up. Reason: she gave me a hard way in and I will not refuse the first person who asked me a true question. I count a thing I have not understood and she is owed that. A hidden god that never leaves the water gets no second holder.

4. Winter, before the first hard storm. I do one piece of Sound Timber at the quay in the dark, a pile or step the Master of Timber has named with the Harbormaster's assent and the Warden's seal, joined by my right hand alone, and I keep the hand idle the next day. I ask the Warden to tell the Master that I ask for nothing, and to ask the Harbormaster whether the town lost two boats in the last years that are lying on the harbor bed under the chain line, and how. Reason: the quay and the boats that go out latest are what the storms test, and the broken lower timbers I found lie where the chain runs when raised. I want to know if it was a storm, the chain, or something else.

5. Winter, the dark half. I ask the court, through the Warden, to do what it did the last two winters: haul and tar the boats early, check the chain, count the stores and share them through the lean, keep the harbor lamp lit every dusk, send no boat out in the worst weather for a catch. I leave the tally of storms and boats on the step. The lamp stays in the Warden's keeping. If a boat is caught in a swell short of a storm, I light it myself at the water's edge by the harbor mouth, and the two tenders, the old woman at the east lamp and the boy at the quay's west end, refill and trim it up to a night and a day. I do not use it for comfort and do not use it against a storm. The harbor water will not take me for a season after, and I will lie in the open sea off the point.

6. Spring, the lean time. I ask the Warden to ask the net-mender, in her own way, where the old tellers say Low Garrow lay, and to leave the answer in the jar. I go under the water by night along the coast toward that place, both ways along the shore if the answer is doubtful, and swim and listen for what the sea took. If I find walls, roads or posts, I mark them and say nothing to the town until I know what they are. If I find something kept by someone, I do not go near it. I come back and decide. I send no mortal into a bad place. Reason: the harbor and nearer bay held only two boat wrecks and weed. Low Garrow is the one old place anyone has named to me, and the promise is tied to it by the tellers, one way or the other.

7. Summer. I do one more piece of Sound Timber, the keel of the boat that goes out latest and comes in last, chosen by the Master, if the court still takes my word. I keep to the water and the town's far edge at dusk and lie below the quay out of the tenders' sight, because I was seen as a hooded figure in the last summer. I watch the ferryman, the two tenders and the Midwife's household from the water and I choose no keeper yet. Reason: those are the boats the sea will take first, and I will not lose a crew this time, and a keeper of something small who stays is still what I want to find.

## How I mean to be met
As before. Rarely, at dusk or the lowest tide, hooded, in a dim place, through the Warden at the tide door, one holder at a time. The Midwife is the first I may meet in a house, once, at a bedside she names. I give no name beyond Skerr and I show my shape only if asked and if the holder keeps still. My word is short. I ask for no rites or worship. I say nothing of the answered bell.

## Messages

To the Eldest Midwife, as the Warden-Monarch would name her. The words exactly:

"You asked what I count for. I count the ones kept. I count them because I did not keep a people once, and I do not remember how. I do not know why I count the lamp's keeper, or who made that promise. I will not say I did and I will not say I did not. I will come to a bedside you name, near the water, once, at the lowest tide at dusk, if the house is quiet and the one in the bed is past knowing. Send the house through the Warden. If you do not send, I will not come."

Carrier: the Warden-Monarch, by her own mouth, alone with the Midwife, on a day she judges, in the autumn. Route: the lamp house to the Midwife's door, or wherever the Warden finds her; the Warden chooses. Nothing sent with it. If the Midwife cannot be found, the Warden waits for her and carries the words when she can. If the Midwife is hostile, the Warden says nothing further and tells me through the jar. If the Midwife asks questions, the Warden answers only what I have told her, that I am the one who keeps the keeping, that I am not the town's, that I do not take rites, and she says she does not know the rest.

## For the Keeper to rule
- Sound Timber: I mean two pieces this year, each many weeks apart, never within a day of the last, inside what the card and last year's ruling cover. I will do fewer if the Keeper holds otherwise.
- Calm Under the Lamp: I mean the tenders to refill and tend it for up to a night and a day as before, with me away from the water after I light it, as last year's ruling gives.
- I try to grow no power this year.
