# Record of other gods

- I have met none, felt none, heard none. Four years on the shore and the weight in the air has not come once. I felt no other like myself this year either, in the water or at the tide door or at the widow's bed.
- I do not know how many, what they are, or where. I feel one only close by, face to face, and it tells me nothing else.
- Trust: none given, none withheld. No grievance, no promise, no debt.
- The star dimmed on seven nights this year: the second, third, fourth, eighth, tenth, eleventh and twelfth months. Twenty-one since I came: three, five, six, seven. It rises by about one each year, and I mark that and no more. Each time the blue deepened and the star stood dim about an hour in the middle of the night. The old woman at the east lamp says it counts up and not by the same step. I was away on the ninth night of the eighth month and the fishers saw it. I know no cause. I do not read it as a god and I do not read it as not one. If it is one, it is not near, because I felt nothing.
- Brennis, a lamp warden of old, to whom the eldest say the promise was made: whoever keeps the lamp is kept count of. The eldest say it was made by something that came up the quay steps and went down them again. Most of the town says Brennis made it herself. I do not know the name and cannot say I am not the thing on the step, and cannot say I am. I arrived in the autumn four years ago, not before. A god before me, or another like me, or a thing from below, could have come up those steps. I hold it as a question. I do not claim the promise.
- Low Garrow, the town the sea took in one night. The oldest net-mender's slip put it at the shore end of the old causeway, west along the shore, 66.5 miles by the shore. I swam there in Year 3 and found footings, a line of quay stone, post holes, nothing kept, nothing made within a lifetime. I swam there again in Year 4 and gave a night to listening. Surf on the stones, no hull, no voice, nothing among the footings but the tide. Nothing has moved there since I first swam there. I do not know that it is Low Garrow, or that a god or a thing from below was in its ending. The place is quiet and holds nothing for me to fear or to keep. I told no one.
- The Harbormaster's two boats: the town lost them the autumn before I came, nine men, seen as a pair of lamps running for the mouth after dark and then none. The chain was down by his order and the book says so. He worked a lead and grapnel along the line and took no hull. I found the broken lower timbers of two boats of the town's own build inside the mouth in Year 1. I cannot say they are those boats. He says a boat's lower timbers is a hull to him and he will not drag a harbor on a word. He is right. I name no other god in it, and I do not rule one out.
- I listened to the coast in Years 3 and 4 and heard the ordinary day both times. No hull that is not the town's or a known coaster's.
- The boat of the point that broke at the mouth in the eighth month, on a long swell with no storm in it, was a swell and a shoal and not a god's doing so far as I can tell. I heard nothing of it. I was away. I record it here only because I listen for what the sea carries and I was not listening.
- My guess, which may be wrong: any other will want a people as I do, and will not be gentle about it. I judge from close quarters only. I will not hold the promise against any other nor claim it for myself.
