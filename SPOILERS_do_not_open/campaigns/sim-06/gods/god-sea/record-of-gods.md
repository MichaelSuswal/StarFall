# Record of other gods

- I have met none, felt none, heard none. Three years on the shore and the weight in the air has not come once. I felt no other like myself this year either.
- I do not know how many, what they are, or where. I feel one only close by, face to face, and it tells me nothing else.
- Trust: none given, none withheld. No grievance, no promise, no debt.
- The star dimmed on six nights this year: one in the first month, a pair in the fifth, a pair in the seventh, one in the eighth. Fourteen since I came: three, five, six. The count rises each year. Each time the blue deepened and the star stood dim about an hour in the middle of the night. I did nothing on those nights that I know of and told the Warden so. I do not know the cause. I do not read it as a god and I do not read it as not one. If it is one, it is not near, because I felt nothing.
- Brennis, a lamp warden of old, to whom the eldest say the promise was made: whoever keeps the lamp is kept count of. The eldest say it was made by something that came up the quay steps and went down them again. Most of the town says Brennis made it herself. I do not know the name and cannot say I am not the thing on the step, and cannot say I am. I arrived in the autumn of three years ago, not before. A god before me, or another like me, or a thing from below, could have come up those steps. I hold it as a question. I do not claim the promise.
- Low Garrow, the town the sea took in one night. The oldest net-mender's slip put it at the shore end of the old causeway, west along the shore. I swam there in spring, 66.5 miles by the shore. The causeway's cut stones run out under the water, worn round. At the shore end, under sand and weed, footings of walls, a line of quay stone, post holes. Nothing kept by anyone. Nothing made within a lifetime. I do not know that it is Low Garrow, or that a god or a thing from below was in its ending. I told no one.
- The Harbormaster's two boats: the town lost them the autumn before I came, nine men, seen as a pair of lamps running for the mouth after dark and then none. The chain was down by his order and the book says so. He worked a lead and grapnel along the line and took no hull. I found the broken lower timbers of two boats of the town's own build inside the mouth in Year 1. I cannot say they are those boats. A pair of lamps that ran for the mouth and vanished could be a storm, a chain, or something from below. I name no other god in it, and I do not rule one out.
- I listened to the coast in Year 3 and heard the ordinary day. No hull that is not the town's or a known coaster's.
- My guess, which may be wrong: any other will want a people as I do, and will not be gentle about it. I judge from close quarters only. I will not hold the promise against any other nor claim it for myself.
