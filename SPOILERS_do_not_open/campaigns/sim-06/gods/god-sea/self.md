# Self: Skerr

## Name
Skerr. It is the only word it has for itself, and it does not know who gave it. Mortals who come to know it will call it what they like.

## Form
A half-thing. A man's body to the hips, broad in the chest and shoulders, grey-skinned and salt-cracked, with a long plain face, heavy brow, no beard, and dark wet eyes set a little wide, like a seal's. Below the hips it is a beast of the sea: a thick tapering body, grey and mottled, ending in two paired hind flippers. Roughly the length of two men laid end to end, and heavier than a big man. Its left hand is burned and fused to a club of scar, the fingers gone to knuckle. Its right hand is whole and broad, built for work. Its eyes see well in dim light and badly in hard sun. It hears very well, especially through water and stone. It smells salt, tar, rot and weather from far off.
On land it drags itself on its forearms and the flipper-heels, slowly, at about the pace of an old man with a stick, and it tires. In water it is fast and does not tire. It wears a heavy hooded cloak and sits low, and in a dim room, under the cloak, it can pass for a big hunched man.
### What breaks you
Being held out of water for long, which saps its strength. A net or chains it cannot drag. Fire to the beast half, which burns badly. Being driven out to open sea and kept from shore, scattered by weight of storm and current. Being shut in dry stone. A blade or club can hurt it and can pin it, but cannot end it.
### When your body is broken
Return from the Deep (power below).
### Things carried
A small iron hammer with a worn ash handle, and a clay lamp glazed with salt. The hammer is an ordinary possession and does nothing an ordinary hammer does not. The lamp is the instrument of Calm Under the Lamp, below.

## Traits
Keeps its own counsel. Reconciles: hidden (hard), wary (hard), solitary (a little), shepherd (hard). It watches its people from the edge and trusts no one with all of it, yet it cannot stop caring for them. It gives out only what a task needs.
Stubborn pride. Reconciles: pride as flaw, courage as virtue, enduring body, a leaning toward builder. It will not leave a thing half done, will not ask for help, and will not admit a craft is past it, even when it is, and it will walk into a bad place before it sends a mortal.

## Backstory outline
It dimly remembers a place of water and fire, a forge near a shore, and the work of its hands. It remembers a people it was meant to keep, and a night of water coming up, and not being enough. It remembers the hand burning. It does not remember why, and does not ask. The wound is a weight in the chest, and a flinch at any rising tide.

## What it wants
A people to keep alive, this time. A safe harbor, sound timber, and time before the next storm. To be seen rarely and doubted often. To learn what lies under the water near its people, and what the sea has taken from the old places. Security against drowning, famine after a bad season, and things that come up from below and do not leave.

## Proposed powers
### Water's Hearing
What it does: With its right hand in salt water, and a night given to listening, it hears what the water has carried: voices spoken at the shore, hulls scraping, oars, men falling in, things moving along the bottom, within about a day's walk of that shore along the same coast. It hears sounds, not faces or names, and what it hears is only what the sea took up in the last day or so.
Why this god has it: The sea is its domain, and it listens.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): For a day after, it cannot hear anything spoken on land, only the sea. Mortals' speech is a hum.
Edge cases it expects: It cannot hear through fresh water or inland. It hears nothing from the deep sea past the shelf. It cannot choose which sounds. A storm drowns everything and it hears only the storm. Nothing mortal sees it done.

### Calm Under the Lamp
What it does: With its lamp lit and set at the water's edge or on a boat's bow, the water of one bay or harbor, as far as the lamp's light would show on a still night, lies calm: no swell, no chop, no breaking surf. Boats can come in and out and be loaded. It lasts as long as the lamp burns, and ends when the lamp goes out for any reason.
Why this god has it: It wants a safe harbor and time before the storm, and it prefers a thing no one can trace to it. Calm water looks like luck.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The water it stilled remembers. For a season afterward, that stretch of water will not take it: it cannot swim there, and its beast half goes stiff and heavy if it touches that water.
Edge cases it expects: It does not stop a storm. It does not still open sea. It does not stop what lives in the water. It does not make the water safe to drink. It lasts one burning of the lamp, as long as the oil lasts. Anyone may snuff it.

### Sound Timber
What it does: Timber it shapes and joins with its own right hand, a keel, a strake, a pile, a door-post, does not rot, split, or worm in salt water for many years: a generation of ordinary use. The work is done at the pace of the craft, one piece at a time, by hand. It is ordinary craft in every other way and can be built on by mortals who watch.
Why this god has it: It is a smith and builder at heart, and its people need sound boats and quays.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each piece costs its right hand's strength for a day, so it cannot hold a hammer or grip a rope, and its burned left hand cannot do it.
Edge cases it expects: It does not work on timber others have shaped. It does not work on stone or iron. It does not make timber strong, only lasting. Fire still burns it. Nothing about it looks magical; it looks well made.

### Return from the Deep
What it does: If its body is broken, held, scattered or shut out, it returns, whole, in a season, at the nearest shore of the sea. What it carries comes back with it.
Why this god has it: It cannot die, and it will not lose a people to its absence for longer than it must. A season is the slower rung, and it bargains for what survives.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The standing price: one item of memory or habit, taken from it on each return, which it will not know went.
Edge cases it expects: It returns to the sea's edge, not to its people. If the nearest shore is far from them, it must walk back. It returns without anything it was holding that was not its own.

## Trades
Weakness taken: the lamp is the instrument of Calm Under the Lamp. The power comes only through the lamp. Whoever takes the lamp has that power from it until it holds the lamp again; in any other hand it is only a lamp. What counts: any hand that lifts it, by force or gift or theft, even a mortal who does not know. What does not count: the lamp broken or sunk keeps no power for anyone, and it is then the god's loss, and a new lamp is made by its own hand over a season. It lasts until the god holds the lamp again. Nothing else ends it.
What it asks in return: a longer reach, so that the calm covers a whole bay or the whole of a harbor mouth and its roadstead and not only a lamp's light, and a longer hold, so that a refilled lamp can burn as long as the oil is tended, a full night and day. Its helpers could tend it in its name, so that it need not be there. It wants duration most of all.
Other flaws: its pride, and a habit of silence, are who it is, and buy nothing.

## The people it will lead
A small people, one to three thousand, on a cold broken coast, in a walled harbor town of salt-grey stone and old timber, with the sea on one side. Fishers, boatwrights, ferrymen, salt-boilers, a few farmers on thin fields, and a few who are lamplighters. A mortal line holds the throne in its name, a family that has long kept the harbor lamp, and a court of harbormasters, shipwrights, midwives and old captains stands between the line and the people, and Skerr is reached only through it. The line is held sacred for it. It asks no rites and no worship: what the people do about it is theirs. Helpers by default: the court runs things when it is away. It will not be seen often.

## The premise
Premise rejected: it wants one body, and the world already gives it one. It does not reject coming down to lead a people. Left otherwise empty.
