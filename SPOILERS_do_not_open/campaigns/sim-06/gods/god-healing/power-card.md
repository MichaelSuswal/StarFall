# Power card: Ilvane

Bynames: none.

Confirmed on Night 2, 2026-11-24 (Pacific), by the god's round 2 reply.

## Form

One body, as every god has in this campaign.

A human-seeming body, tall and spare, perfect in a way that unsettles: no scar, no blemish, no asymmetry, skin without a pore out of place, hands always clean and always somehow looking used. Face calm and fine-boned, age impossible to place, eyes the grey of river silt. Hair dark and fine, kept short. It looks like a person only until someone looks twice. By choice it dresses plainly, in undyed wool, and keeps its head down; it can be taken for a stranger by anyone who does not look closely, and it prefers that. Senses are those of a keen human, with a sharper nose than most; it smells sickness and spoilage before it sees them. It walks, steadily and without hurry, at the pace of a tireless person on foot, and it laughs sometimes at the wrong moment and then has to wave the laugh away.

What breaks it: A god body is wounded by what would wound a human body: blade, fall, fire, crushing, drowning. It does not die, but it can be wounded, bound, held in a cell or a net, buried or sealed in, or driven off by a crowd with weapons. Hunger and sleeplessness do not touch it. Cold and fever do not kill it but weaken and slow it. Its own mending does not work on itself.

God-sense: the god does not feel another god from far off. Only at close quarters, in the same room, across a doorway, within a few paces, does it feel the other as a weight in the air, and over a moment the weight settles into knowing that this is one like itself. It tells nothing more: not who the other is, and not what it can do.

When the body is broken: see the power Return from Ground.

Keeper trait: What you have mended knows you: any person or creature you have mended can tell, eyes shut, when you are within a few paces. Its reach: only those you have mended, only your nearness; it tells them nothing of what you think or mean, and it touches none of your powers or their prices.

### Definitions

- Its own people: those of the line, the court and the shrines settled on Night 3 and their children; a stranger who joins later counts once taken in.
- A grave mending: one that costs the god fever, as the Drawback of Mending gives it.

### Things carried

- A small knife with a worn bone handle.

## Powers

### Mending
Status: main
Description: By touch, one living person or creature at a time, the god mends a wound, a sickness or a failing body toward health: bones knit, a festering wound clears, a fever breaks, a labor is eased. It takes as long as the harm needs, minutes for a cut, an hour or a night for a deep wound or a long illness. It mends what is alive and still near mending, and one whose breath stopped no longer ago than a held breath. It does not raise the dead, undo age, regrow a limb or mend the mind. What is mended can be hurt or sicken again.
Drawback: The god takes a share of what it mends into its own body. A mild harm costs it a cold and a stiffness for a day. A grave one costs it fever, cold in the bones and a tremor in the hands for days, longer the more it has mended in a season, and it cannot mend again while the fever is on it without the tremor settling in. And, heavier than the power needs and written as asked: after a grave mending a faint tremor stays in the hands until the next spring thaw.
Limits settled at creation:
- Touch, skin or cloth over skin.
- It cannot mend itself or put the share on another.
- A poison in the blood is mended as a sickness.
- The god's own people: mended as anyone.
- Other gods: unruled.
- A mind: none.
- Use across a year: bounded by the fever and the tremor.
- A scarcity: the knowing of medicine may be short where it lands; the power mends and teaches no one.
- More than ordinary craft: a harm done by more than craft is unruled until it happens.
- Not applicable: light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, water, being watched.
- The tremor until the thaw is a heavier price than the power needed, written as the god asked; nothing was given for it.
- First wording, kept as intent: "A mended thing stays mended as any healed thing would."
Rulings:
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### Reading the Rot
Status: main
Description: By touch, or by looking closely from within a few paces, the god knows what ails a person or creature: the kind of sickness, how far gone, where it sits, what is likely to follow. Standing at a spring, a well, a store of grain or a stretch of soil, it knows whether the water, grain or ground is fouled, and with what.
Drawback: It cannot read its own people. Whoever is of its people is closed to it: it can mend them and cannot know what ails them without asking.
Limits settled at creation:
- One body at a time, a breath or two.
- It tells what is wrong now and its likely course, never who or what brought it about.
- A wrongness that is not sickness, rot or poison reads only as nothing it knows.
- Not minds, intent or lies.
- Another god's body reads no better than a mortal's; unruled beyond that.
- "Its own people": those of the line, the court and the shrines settled on Night 3 and their children; a stranger who joins later counts once taken in.
- Use across a year: no limit on how often; it goes where the god is.
- A scarcity: none.
- Not applicable: falling, carrying, water, being watched.
- The drawback was chosen by the god from a pair; the other, that whoever it reads learns the same of it, was declined.
- First wording, kept as intent: "Healing begins with knowing."
Rulings:
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### Hasten the Rot
Status: main
Description: By touch, the god hastens the decay of dead matter within arm's reach: a carcass, a corpse, a mound of grain, a plank, a rope, a heap of straw, up to about a cartload at a time, in moments what would take a season. It does nothing to living flesh, stone or metal, or to anything it is not touching.
Drawback: The hands that rot a thing smell of it for a day and a night, so that anyone near knows, and the god cannot mend in that time. It may be used as often as the god will; each use after the first in a day adds a day and a night to the smell and to the time it cannot mend.
Limits settled at creation:
- A tree or a living field is not dead matter; nor is a body in its first breaths of dying.
- A thing sealed from the air does not rot.
- A thing cured in salt rots no faster than it would.
- Being watched: it is seen, and smelled after.
- The god's own people's stores rot like any.
- More than ordinary craft: unruled.
- A mind: none.
- Not applicable: light and dark, distance, falling, water.
- The god chose the growing cost over one use a day: "A fence says how many times I may; a cost says what it takes from me." First wording, kept as intent: "Rot is how ground is made and how a field is cleaned."
Rulings:
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### Return from Ground
Status: main (the return)
Description: When the god's body is broken, it returns whole after a season at the nearest ground of its kind: a marsh edge, a burial field, a midden or slow river-mud. What it carries comes back with it.
Drawback: One item of memory or habit at each return. The item is taken by a logged roll from the god's own record, and the god is not told which.
Limits settled at creation:
- The standing scale's season rung.
- A body held, sealed in or shut out is not broken, and nothing returns until the body is broken.
- The Keeper finds the nearest ground in the world; the god does not choose it.
- If the nearest ground is where it was confined, the next nearest.
- The walk is added, and adds only the walk.
- The god asked that being held, unheard or shut out count as broken; it was not written, by the rule for every card.
Rulings:
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.
