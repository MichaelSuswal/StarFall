## Answer 1

A field after the fighting, the morning after. Mist on it. Everything that was going to happen has happened, and now the ground is deciding what to grow. Quiet, but not peace. The quiet of a thing mending, which is a busy quiet if you know how to listen. I find it funny, a little. All that noise and the grass does not care.

## Answer 2

River country, the slow lowland kind, with marsh at its edges and forest behind. Water that floods and leaves silt. Ground that rots things honestly and feeds on them. I want land where decay is visible and useful, where a thing can be seen to end and something else seen to begin. Not mountains. Mountains keep their dead too well.

## Answer 3

Four seasons, and none of them kind about it. A wet, rotting autumn that I will not apologize for. A hard winter that tests who was strong and who only looked it. A thaw in which everything that was hidden comes up. A summer long enough to heal in and short enough to be grateful for. I do not want an easy year. I want one with a turn in it.

I wake into the thaw, the first spring after the hard winter, when the ground gives back what it was holding.

## Answer 4

Some of each, and I lean toward the wild. I would like most of the edge to be country no one has walked, because I will want to walk it first and alone. But I want a few old things rooted out there too, a kingdom gone slightly strange with age, so I have something to be careful around. I do not trust a world with only empty land. I do not trust one with only old claims either.

## Answer 5

Old, and unevenly old. Thousands of years in some places, ruins under ruins, with the cause of the ruin forgotten. Other places new, people who arrived within the memory of a grandmother. I want the old layers to be sick or scarred in ways that interest me. A buried plague pit under a market is more truthful than a clean foundation.

## Answer 6

Present, and rare enough that people argue about it. A wise-woman in some valleys and none in the next three. Enough that a mortal can be saved by it and enough that a mortal can be fooled by it. I do not want it everywhere. A healer who is unique is a king, and a healer who is common is a neighbor. I want both to exist.

## Answer 7

It should feel like fever. Heat, then clarity, then a weakness afterward. The cost should be the body: the user takes something of what they mend, a cold in the bones, a stiffness, a tremor in the hands that stays. Whatever they put right in another, they carry a little of themselves. No one should be able to work it for free, or for long, or for a crowd. A mortal who uses it a great deal should look it.

## Answer 8

More than a great sorcerer, less than a thing that cannot be hurt by people. I want a god to be unmistakable to anyone who looks closely and unremarkable to anyone who does not. The one-eyed man in the kingdom of the blind is a king, and kings are watched. I would rather be a stranger the blind have not yet noticed. Standing out should be a choice I can make, and mostly decline.

## Answer 9

Familiar animals with a few legends, and then, past the last farm, things no one has a name for. I want the wild to hold some creatures that sicken and some that heal, and I want mortals to have learned which is which by dying of the wrong guess. A world where every beast has a name has been walked too thoroughly. Leave me a few animals I must watch before I understand.

## Answer 10

Sickness, first. The kind that moves through a steading in a season and leaves the survivors different. Then hunger after a bad year. Then the thing in the wet woods that no one has named. Beyond each other, mortals should fear what the body does without being asked. I think that is the best kind of story: the enemy is inside the house and has been all along. I laugh at that, and I should probably not.

## Answer 11

Time, I think. The thing mortals want most and have least is another year, another winter survived, another child past the age of five. Close behind it, clean water. They should trade for it, and fight for it, and lie about it. Medicine, the knowing of which root and which moss, should be scarce enough that it is guarded and common enough that it leaks.

## Answer 12

Old roads, and under the roads, graves. I want to find a place where something was buried with great care and then forgotten, and I want not to know at first whether the care was for the dead or against them. Sealed doors, if there must be doors. Bones of something enormous would please me less than bones of something that was loved.

## Answer 13

Yes, a few, and few. I do not want crowds of them. Peoples who are not human should be strange in how they treat death and illness: one that burns, one that returns its dead to the water, one that does not seem to grieve and may be hiding it. I want to be unable to guess at once what they will want. I would rather meet them than rule them.

## Answer 14

A few, and late. Travelers who arrive with a tool or a cure no one here has seen, and who are not sure themselves why it works. I want them to be sick, or hungry, or lost, when they arrive. A traveler with something to trade is interesting. A traveler who needs mending is more interesting, and I would know what to do with them.

## Answer 15

Face to face only. A pull felt at any distance would mean I could never be alone, and I would resent it. Let a god know another god only when close enough to be seen, and even then let there be a moment of doubt. I would rather learn who stands near me than be told from afar. A knowing that arrives too easily is a knowing I will not trust.

## Answer 16

One body. If several, it should cost dearly: each body thinner, slower to heal, and each carrying a share of whatever I am. A god in many places is a god who cannot be wounded in one without the rest feeling it, and I do not want to learn that lesson cheaply. I would rather be one thing that is truly somewhere than many that are nowhere. And whoever is made in the extra bodies had better be loyal, which is not a thing I have found to be certain.

## Answer 17

One who mends and who is not thanked for it. A midwife, a bonesetter, a gravedigger who also sits with the dying. Someone whose story is quiet and long and whose hands are always slightly unclean. I want a mortal who keeps going after the part of the story where anyone would stop. And I would like, privately, for one of them to turn out to be loyal in a way that surprises me.

## Answer 18

A truth. Something about where the dead go, or where they do not. I would like to find a thing in this land that changes what I think my own work is for, and I would like not to be ready for it. A companion, if it must be, but only one, and only one who tells me no. A place can be walked around. A power can be weighed. A truth has to be survived.

## Answer 19

I wish that what I mend would stay mended. That nothing I put right would turn on me later, and that nothing I make would learn to hold me in contempt. It is a foolish wish and I have made it before, in some other place, and it was not granted there either. The world is only listening. Fine. I will say it anyway, and then I will laugh, because it is the sort of thing that is funny when you hear it aloud.
