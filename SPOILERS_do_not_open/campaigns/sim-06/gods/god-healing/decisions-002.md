# Decisions, Year 2: Ilvane

Year 2 runs from the thaw round to the next thaw. The tremor goes at the thaw. I go on foot in undyed wool with the bone-handled knife, and I keep my name for any who ask me straight.

1. Spring, the first days. I go to the Reckoner of Bodies first, at her door in the village, before any other office. I tell her I have your answer to what you asked: I will take no step toward the court without coming to you first, and I say it to you now. I tell her the Hearth-Holder has asked me to sit at the seat's hearth, and the sitters have asked me to look at the moss of the mounds, and I ask her to carry word to the Hearth-Holder that I will come when she has judged the day, and that I will come with her or after her leave. I do this because she took my word and spent her standing on it, and because an office I go round is an office I lose.

2. Spring. I go to the mounds with Hedda and the sitters who asked, and look at the moss within a few paces, close, and Read the Rot of it as I read a body. I ask them what it did before and what it does now, and I take their words, because I cannot read my own and must ask them. I do this because the old women say the moss no longer bites and brews weak, and that is a change in something the dead lie under, and I will not guess at it from a distance. I mend no one on this walk unless a death is at hand.

3. Late spring. Through the Reckoner, I go to the Warden of Waters, who has heard me, and I ask him in the Reckoner's hearing to bring the Keeper of the Mounds to the bank. I show them both where the newer graves lie on the low side and where the wet goes down, and I tell them plainly what I read there last year: the ground and the wells of the low steadings are foul with the same foulness. I ask that the new burials be laid on the higher ground and that no well be dug below the bank graves. I do not order it, and I do not Hasten the Rot on any grave. The dead are the people's, and I will not undo their laying out. If they ask me to stand and read the ground where they would lay the new graves, I read it.

4. Summer. I go, by the Reckoner's leave, to the hearth of the Hearth-Holder, and I sit there when I am well, as she was asked. I let the Judge-Mother watch me, and I let the Spearward meet me, whom I have not met. I answer truly whatever any of them asks straight. I do this so the court has seen me once in the open, and so that watching me is not done from behind a door.

5. Late summer and autumn. I walk downstream to the two empty steadings and to the steadings below the bank burials, with a sitter if one will come. I Read the Rot of each well and the soil about it, tell the elders what is foul, and ask them to draw off or close what I find and to carry creek water. I do not ask the empty steadings to be resettled. If the wells read sound after a season, I tell the Reckoner and the Warden, and I leave the choice to them. In the wet season I tend the children and the old whom the sitters cannot, and I use Hasten the Rot only if a heap of spoiled grain or a carcass threatens the water, once in a day, and I do not mend on that day or the next night.

6. Winter. I stay among the steadings of the long sickness season, and I mend only what the sitters have failed at, one at a time, with long rests, and I keep count in my head so the fever and the tremor do not run past what I can carry. I do not mend a child of the line unless no one else can save it. I teach nothing and I give no power; what I learn from the sitters I keep, and what they learn from watching me is theirs. By the thaw I want the Reckoner's winter count to be lower than last year's, and the sitters to have sent for me fewer times.

## Messages
None. I send no message to anyone not in front of me this year. The word to the Hearth-Holder goes by the Reckoner, who is in front of me, and she is the carrier, not a message of mine.

## For the Keeper to rule
1. The moss of the mounds is a living plant. I mean to Read the Rot of it, close, within a few paces, as a creature of any kind. If the card does not reach a living plant, I expect it reads as nothing I know, and I ask the Keeper to say what I learn.
2. Nothing else lies outside my card. I do not mean to try to grow a power this year.
