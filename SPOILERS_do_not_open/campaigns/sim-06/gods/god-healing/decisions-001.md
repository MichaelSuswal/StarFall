# Decisions, Year 1: Ilvane

Year 1 runs from the thaw, round to the next thaw. I go on foot, in undyed wool, with the bone-handled knife, and I give my name to no one unless one asks me straight.

1. Spring (the first days). I walk the low mounds to the north-west alone, then the river bank between the mounds and the village, slowly. I look at what is buried and how it is kept, and I stand on the ground and Read the Rot of the soil and of the river water where it runs by the village. I do this before I meet anyone, to learn what ails the land and where the dead are laid, because I will not act on a place I have not seen.

2. Spring (after the mounds). I walk to the village 2 miles to the south-east and come in at the edge as a plain stranger who tends the sick. I ask at the first house for the midwife, the laying-out woman, or whoever sits with the sick, and I offer my hands and my work in exchange for food and a place by a hearth, no more. I Read the Rot of their well and their grain store, tell them plainly what is fouled if anything is, and help clean it. I tell them the water must be boiled and stored apart if I find it fouled, in terms of their own custom of boiling for the sick.

3. Spring through early summer. In the village I mend only what the sitters and the bonesetter cannot: the fever that will not break, the wound past binding, a labor going wrong. I read first, always, and mend one at a time, with long rests between so that the fever and the tremor of a grave mending do not stack. I decline to be thanked and do not tell them what I am. I accept that each person I mend will know my nearness with eyes shut. I will not mend any child of the line unless no one else can save it.

4. Summer. I ask in person to meet the Reckoner of Bodies. I go as the stranger who has been tending in the village, not as the Tender. I ask what the Reckoner counts each spring and autumn and ask to hear it in the plain words of the sitters, because I cannot read those of my own people and will need to ask. I let the Reckoner hear me and judge for himself or herself what I am. If the Reckoner asks me straight whether I am the one the seat is kept for, I answer truly and ask what the court would have me do, and ask nothing of the court that I have not first earned by work. No office takes my word as the Tender's until its holder has heard me and chosen to; I do not claim it.

5. Late summer. I walk alone for some days along the river bank, upstream and downstream, and toward the western treeline as far as is sensible, then return. I look for old things, burial fields, middens and marsh edges, and for sicknesses in other steadings. I do this to see the edge before settling anywhere, and to know the ground where I would return from if broken. I mend no one on this walk unless a death is plainly at hand.

6. Autumn. I return to the village for the wet season, when children and the old fall ill. I Read the wells and stores again each steading I reach, tend the sick the sitters cannot, and, if a heap of spoiled grain, a carcass or a midden threatens the water, I use Hasten the Rot once in a day only, and on that day and the next night I do not mend. I ask the sitters and midwives what they know and learn it, so that they need me less.

7. Winter. I stay among the people through the long sickness season, move from steading to steading as the need is greatest, and keep a count in my head of whom I have mended and when, so the tremor and the fever do not run past what I can carry. By the end of the year I want to know who keeps the dead, who holds the throne, and what the court wants of the Tender, without having bound myself to any of them.

## Messages
None. I send no message to anyone not in front of me this year.

## For the Keeper to rule
1. Reading the Rot at the mounds: I mean to stand on the ground of the mounds and read the soil, and the river water, as a "stretch of soil" and water under the card. If the burials there are old bone and not sickness, I expect it reads as nothing I know. Please say what I learn.
2. Teaching the sitters: I mean to learn from them, not to give them any power. I do not ask for any ruling beyond what the card says.
3. Nothing else is outside my card. I do not mean to try to grow a power this year.
