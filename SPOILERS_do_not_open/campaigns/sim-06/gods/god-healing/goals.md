# Goals: Ilvane (Year 4)

1. Learn what the foulness is tied to, in time as well as place. Year 3 showed me where it lies: under the newest bank graves and under the wells of the low ground below them, and nowhere upstream. The rise, the river, the creeks, the springs and the old mounds on higher ground read sound. My card tells me what is wrong now and never what brought it, so I cannot say the graves caused it. I can find out which graves read foul and which do not, by age and by place, and ask the Keeper of the Mounds, who holds the records, when each was laid. Read the ground grave by grave with him, and let the pattern say what I cannot.
2. Keep faith with the Reckoner, and widen the circle only through her. She carried my asking to the Warden of Waters and three test wells were dug and read sound twice. I go to her before any door, still.
3. Let the Warden and the Keeper make the water safe themselves. The test wells on the rise are sound and the nearest households draw from them. I advise and read and stand on the ground when asked. I do not order, and I do not Hasten the Rot on any grave. The dead are the people's. Whatever is asked about where new graves go, the Keeper answers, not I.
4. Answer the two questions put to me and not answered, truly and without claiming what I do not know. The old sitter asked whether I did this or it was going already. I do not know, and I will say so. The sitters of the three steadings asked what they should brew now the moss does nothing. I know no brewing, and I will say so, and offer what I can: to read the sick before and after, and tell them what changed.
5. Mend less and later. Seven in Year 3, all held, and the shake left at the thaw. Keep to what the sitters cannot reach, one at a time, with long rests, and keep the count. Let the winter count fall again and the sitters send for me fewer times.
6. Understand the moss and the sitters. The moss reads sound, living, and does nothing that common moss does not do. Whether it was tired and has recovered, or was something and is now ordinary, I do not know. Learn what the sitters know. Teach nothing and give no power.
7. See the edge of the land to the south and downstream, as I have seen it to the west and north-west, and learn whether the same foulness lies under other bank burials there. Do it alone and short, and return before the wet season. I want to know whether this is the people's land only or a thing of low ground and graves everywhere.
8. Hold the pride and the clench. Do not think the families beneath me for their weeping. Do not let the Keeper's trait turn their knowing of me into a crowd at a door. Keep to the stranger's undyed wool.
9. Do not bind myself to any office or to the line. I sat where a guest sits and told the Spearward I will not turn the rot on any store of the people, in war or out of it, and I hold to that. Give no hold that can be turned on me.
10. Learn who the others like me are, only close up, and trust none until I have seen them act. Watch the sky and count the nights: fourteen so far, six this year. Do not name the cause until I know it.
