# Goals: Ilvane (Year 5)

1. Test what the foulness follows, now that the pattern has a shape. Year 4 on the bank ground with the Keeper of the Mounds: under the rows laid in the wasting winter and since, many in one place on the low bank, the soil is foul, and the wells below them; under the older rows it is sound; the rise where he means to lay the next is sound. That points at many dead laid close and fast on low wet ground, which is a hypothesis from known rot and not a fact, because my card tells me what is wrong now and never what brought it. Read the same graves again, in the same order, to learn whether the foulness fades with years, holds, or spreads, and read any rows laid since his peg. The pegged limit on rows in one place is his and I keep out of it.
2. Keep faith with the Reckoner and go to her before any door. Her cords are lower again, and a third of those that kept their wells have closed them since the cords went round. The old elder keeps his and his people carry from the creek, and I leave that his. I do not say what the count means to anyone who has not asked me, as she does not.
3. Leave the wells and the graves to the Warden and the Keeper. The three test wells on the rise carry thirty households, no fourth is dug, and the rule that no well is dug below the bank graves is the Warden's. I read when I am asked and I do not order. I use Hasten the Rot on no grave and no store.
4. Answer what was put to me and not answered, in words. The runner's question, "Will it come?", for a woman in hard labor while I was on my walk. I was gone and could not be found, and I will say so plainly and say what a steading should do when I am. I will not let a question go unanswered again because I walked off without saying how long.
5. Learn the salt. Salt came up the river about five times this year. I do not know where the sea is, and I walked 58 miles of fen without reaching it. Learn whether the salt touches the wells and creek mouths below, and what it does to what it touches.
6. Mend less and later. Four in Year 4, each held, each with days of shaking, and the tremor stays until the thaw. Keep to what the sitters cannot reach, one at a time, with long rests, and keep the count. The Reading is closed on every steading's sick, so I ask the sitter what ails them. The younger sitter found my saying of what changed in the body more than the court gave her, and the two others want to be told what to give. I know no brewing and I will say so again. I can read before and after, and what they learn from it is theirs.
7. Keep the count of the star by the month beside my own doings, and do not name a cause. The count is three, five, six, seven. I will not tell my people it means nothing and I will not tell them it means me.
8. Hold the pride and the clench. Do not think the families beneath me for their weeping, and do not take it as a slight when Hedda tells me a count I would rather not hear. Keep to the stranger's undyed wool and do not let the Keeper's trait make a crowd at a door.
9. Do not bind myself to any office or to the line, and claim nothing of the seat. It is kept and not claimed, and the Hearth-Holder asked again to see the cords. The cords are the court's, and I will not be the reason the seat is kept from them. I told the Spearward I will turn the rot on no store of the people, in war or out of it, and I hold to that. Give no hold that can be turned on me.
10. Learn who the others like me are, only close up, and trust none until I have seen them act. I have felt none in four years. Do not go looking for them by leaving my people for long, and do not pretend I am not looking.
