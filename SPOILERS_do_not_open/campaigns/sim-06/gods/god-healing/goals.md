# Goals: Ilvane (Year 3)

1. Find where the foulness comes from. The wells of the low steadings and the wells of the emptied steadings read foul alike, the creek reads clean, and the rise above the bank reads sound. Drawing off and closing wells has made the water fainter and the winter count lower, but the bottoms are still foul. I do not yet know whether it is the old bank graves, a layer under the whole low ground, or something upstream. Walk it and read it before I say anything more than I have said. My card tells me what is wrong, never who or what brought it, so I must find the source by where it is and where it is not.
2. Keep faith with the Reckoner, and widen the circle only through her. She took my word first, the Warden of Waters has taken it, and the Keeper of the Mounds came to the bank when asked. The court has now seen me at the hearth in the open, and no one asked me to take the seat. I go to the Reckoner before any door, still.
3. Let the Warden and the Keeper make the water safe themselves. I advise, I read, I stand on the ground when asked. I do not order, and I do not Hasten the Rot on any grave. The old elder asked why his people should carry water like children. I answered him, and I should see whether the answer held through a season.
4. Mend less and later. Nine was enough, and all nine lived, and the shake left at the thaw. Keep to what the sitters cannot reach, one at a time, with long rests, and keep the count. Let the Reckoner's winter count fall again and the sitters send for me fewer times.
5. Understand the moss and the sitters. The moss reads sound and does not bite the tongue as it did. The old sitter asked whether it is dying or only tired, and I answered, and I still do not know. Read it again across a season with Hedda to see whether it changes. Learn what the sitters know; teach nothing and give no power.
6. Hold the pride and the clench. Do not think the families beneath me for their weeping. Do not let the Keeper's trait turn their knowing of me into a crowd at a door. Keep to the stranger's undyed wool even now that the court has looked at me.
7. Do not bind myself to any office or to the line. I sat where a guest sits and ate, and I was welcome at the hearth, and I took no seat. Give no hold that can be turned on me. Refuse the granary if it is ever asked of me for war.
8. Learn who the others like me are, only close up, and trust none until I have seen them act. Watch the sky and count the nights. Do not name the cause until I know it.
