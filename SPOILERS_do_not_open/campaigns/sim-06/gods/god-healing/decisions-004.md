# Decisions, Year 4: Ilvane

Year 4 runs from the thaw round to the next thaw. The tremor left my hands at the thaw. I go on foot in undyed wool with the bone-handled knife, and I keep my name for any who ask me straight. My body stays in the people's land until the summer walk, and goes out of it only then, for about twelve days.

1. Spring, the first days. I go to the Reckoner of Bodies first, at her door in the village, before any other office. I tell her plainly what I read last summer on the upstream walk: the river, every creek and every spring above the bank burials read clean, the old mounds on higher ground read sound, and the foulness begins at the newest bank graves and stops where the low ground's wells stop. I tell her I do not know what brought it, only where it lies. I ask her to carry two askings, in my words, to the Warden of Waters: that the households nearest the rise who now draw from the test wells be joined by the low steadings' households as the elders agree, and that no well be dug on ground below the bank graves; and one asking to the Keeper of the Mounds: that he let me stand with him on the bank ground and the mounds and read it, grave by grave, while he tells me from his records when each was laid. I do this because the Reckoner took my word first, because the Warden answers for wells and the Keeper for graves and I do not, and because the winter count was lower where the wells are closed.

2. Spring, after the Reckoner. I go to the mounds with Hedda and the sitters. To the old sitter who asked whether I did this or it was going already, I say in my own words, to his face, that I do not know. I say that I read what is wrong now and never what brought it, that I laid no hand on the moss and used Hasten the Rot on no grave and no mound, that I found nothing in the moss that common moss lacks, and that I cannot say whether it was already going. To the sitters of the three nearest steadings, through Hedda, who asked what they should brew now the moss does nothing, I say that I know no brewing and cannot teach what I do not know. I say that if they send for me when they have brewed for a sick one, I will read that sick one before the brew and after, and tell them in plain words what changed, and they may judge the brew from that. I do this because each of them asked me straight and silence is a kind of lie, and because I will not claim a knowledge I lack.

3. Late spring and early summer. With the Keeper of the Mounds, if the Reckoner's word reaches him and he comes, I stand on the bank ground and read the soil at the graves he points to, and I ask him to say aloud from his records which were laid in which year, while Hedda or the Reckoner's runner sets the words in order so I can follow, since I cannot read them. I read the oldest bank graves and the newest and any between, and the wells below each. I am looking for whether the foulness follows the age of a grave, the number laid in one place, or the low ground alone. I do not order any grave moved and I do not rot any. If he asks me to read ground where he means to lay the next, I read it. I do this because the pattern in the records is the one thing that can say what my nose cannot.

4. Summer. Having told the Reckoner first that I go and for how long, I walk alone about twelve days downstream along the river past the two empty steadings, to the lower reaches and the edge of the people's land, and as far beyond as is sensible, reading the water at each spring and well I reach and the soil at any burial ground I find. I look for whether the same foulness lies under other bank burials, and for any other people or steading and what sickness is among them. I mend no one on this walk unless a death is plainly at hand. While I am away no one acts for me and a question put to me waits. I return to the Reckoner's door before I go to any other, and tell her what I found, including if I found nothing. I do this to see the edge before I settle on any guess, and because Year 3 showed me that a walk that was meant to learn something did.

5. Autumn, the wet season. I return to the village for the season when the children and the old fall ill. I sit at the seat's hearth if the Hearth-Holder asks me, with the Reckoner or after her leave, and I let the Judge-Mother and the Spearward see me again. If the Spearward or anyone asks, I tell them as before that I will turn the rot on no store of the people, in war or out of it. I tend the sick the sitters cannot reach. I read the test wells on the rise again at the end of the wet season, before any elder asks me to, and tell the Reckoner and the Warden what they read. I use Hasten the Rot only if a carcass or spoiled grain threatens a wellhead, once in a day, and on that day and the next night I do not mend.

6. Winter. I stay among the steadings through the long sickness season and go where the need is greatest. I mend only what the sitters have failed at, one at a time, with long rests, and I keep the count in my head so the fever and the tremor do not run past what I can carry. I do not mend a child of the line unless no one else can save it. When a sitter has brewed for a sick one and sent for me, I read before and after as I said. By the thaw I want the Reckoner's winter count lower again, told to me by wells closed and wells open, and the sitters to have sent for me fewer times than seven. I count the nights the star dims, and note whether I had mended anyone within the moon.

## Messages
None. I send no message to anyone not in front of me this year. The words to the Warden of Waters and the Keeper of the Mounds go by the Reckoner, who is in front of me, and she is the carrier, not a message of mine. The words to the old sitter and to the three steadings' sitters are said by me at the mounds, to the old sitter's face and through Hedda, who is in front of me.

## For the Keeper to rule
1. Reading the sick before and after a sitter's brew: I mean to Read the Rot of a person as the card gives it, once before and once after, and tell the sitter what changed in the body. I do not mean to read the brew itself for any virtue it has. If the sitters of those steadings are counted as my people under the card, I expect the Reading closes to me and I will have to ask them what ails the sick, and I ask the Keeper to say which it is.
2. Nothing else lies outside my card that I know of. Reading the Rot tells me what is wrong now and never what brought it, so I do not ask to be told the cause of the foulness. I do not mean to try to grow a power this year.
