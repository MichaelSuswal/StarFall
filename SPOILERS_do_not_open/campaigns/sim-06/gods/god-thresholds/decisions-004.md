# Decisions: god-thresholds, Year 4

The year runs from the turn of the year, through autumn, round the four seasons to autumn again. Seven actions, in order. I begin on the road, coming down with the columns to the winter ground, and I am not yet at the ford when the year turns.

## 1. Autumn, first days: stand at the ford, ask the old reckoning, then go up to the gate and the hall
My body goes to Marle Ford first, on foot, with the staff and the key. I ask the older toll watcher for the count since I was last there and set it beside what I know by the blessing. Then I ask him, and the younger watcher if he will answer, one thing I have not asked: in his father's time, and in the old cords, was the toll reckoned by the beast, the load or the head, and what was it for a train of the size that crosses now. I write down what he says on a cord of my own and I hold it as his answer and no more. I tell him I do not accuse the toll house of anything. I ask whether any train this season has been met at the ford by someone other than a watcher, and what for.
Then I walk the two miles to the gate and the hall. Reason: the cords were thin by half and the Marshal and the captain could not break a house's account. The only part of the reckoning I have not heard is the old one, from the people who kept it.

## 2. Autumn, in the hall, within the first days: Kessan alone, then the three answers aloud, then the letter
Before I speak to anyone else I ask Kessan to sit with me alone at the hall table, with bread between us, and I tell him what I will say in the open the next day, so that he does not hear it for the first time before the offices. I tell him that I do not call the raise a theft of my leave, that I know a man afraid acts alone, and that I mean to keep his road and his spears. I do not read him. I tell him I will not unless he asks.
The next day at the hall, with Kessan, Idris Veld, the Steward of Salt and Stores, the Marshal of the Road and the Mother of the Hearth present, I answer aloud:
- To the Steward, who asked what he is to do if what is brought is less than the cord: the household's keep is not cut. If the offerings fall short, the shortfall is drawn from the hall stores and entered on the separate cord as the Guest's debt, and it is cleared from the next offerings before any is put to other use. I take no part of the offerings for my own table, and I tell him the number I want at first snow. Idris Veld enters it and stands witness.
- To the Mother of the Hearth, who asked whether the toll is the host's to raise or the sill's: the toll is the sill's to keep and the host's to take. A raise is the host's to propose and the hall's to hear, with Kessan, Idris and me at one table, and it is not made by one mouth. Kessan's raise stands through this year, since the stores need it, and I ask no one to unsay it. After the first trains of the thaw are counted, the three of us sit with the cords. If the trains have fallen by more than the raise has bought, the toll comes down to where it was or to what the cords bear. The Mother carries the answer to the households and tells them it was made in front of her.
- To the keeper of rite who asked whether the river king's letter should be answered: yes, and not by a clerk. Kessan's answer, that the Sarrow owe no share to a king they have not met, stands as far as it goes, and I add to it that the sill hears a claim made in person. I tell Kessan and Idris that I will send that word under my own hand to the chancery of the river kingdom, and I tell them what it says before it goes, in the words below. The keepers of rite do not answer for the Sarrow in this; the answer is Kessan's and mine together, and Idris is witness.
Reason: three people asked me in the open and have waited a year for my words. A host who keeps the sill answers what it is asked.

## 3. Autumn to first snow: two counts, the seed set apart, grain asked first
To the Steward and the Mother of the Hearth: the winter stock of grain, salt, fuel and dried meat is counted twice before the first snow, his and the hearth mothers', and the Mother sets the two side by side and tells me where they differ. The ration from the first snow is set at the lower of the two counts. The stores are shared by hearth from the first snow, the poorest first, the war band last, and I tell the captains why myself, before they hear it from anyone else, and I thank them for last winter's patience and ask for this one's.
To the Steward, with Idris Veld standing witness: the seed grain and seed barley is counted, set apart under the seal of the Steward and the Mother of the Hearth together, and not eaten before the thaw, whatever else fails. If the stores fail, the herd goes first, and then the dried meat, and the seed last of all. I tell him this plainly now so that no captain or hearth mother has to choose it in the dark of the last fortnight.
To the Marshal of the Road: for this year the toll is taken in grain and seed barley first, wherever a train carries it, before cloth, iron or coin, and the toll houses send word of every train that carries grain. To the Marshal also: send word by rider to the far houses that the hall asks of them, as a loan, in the open, a share of grain and seed from their full smokehouses and stores, to be returned from next year's toll, and tell me which houses send and which do not. I do not accuse any house. I ask what a host asks of a host.
Reason: the stores failed short last winter and the sowing was short, and a second short sowing is the thing that would undo this people.

## Messages

### To the king of the river kingdom, at his chancery
To: the king of the river kingdom, or the chancery that wrote to whoever holds the ford.
The words, exactly: "To the one who wrote to whoever holds the ford. Your letter was heard at a rite fire of the Sarrow. The Sarrow keep the sill. A claim made by letter is a claim not yet made, and the sill hears a claim only face to face. If the king, or one he trusts to speak for him, will come to Marle Ford, he will be met at the ford as a guest, fed and seated and left in peace until he has eaten and slept, and heard at the hall table. No share is owed by the Sarrow, and none is refused, until the claim has been heard. Bring the old measure you name, and the word of how it was kept. I have no name to sign. They call me Guest, and Mother of the Door."
The carrier: the older toll watcher of Marle Ford, who gives it, folded and tied with a pinch of salt, to the master of the first train leaving the ford for the river kingdom this autumn, with my toll for the train waived for carrying it. If no train leaves before the snow, the watcher keeps it dry and gives it to the first train after the thaw.
The route: the train master's own road to the chancery of the river kingdom, by the road north-east and the river, as he would choose it.
Sent with it: nothing else.
If the recipient cannot be found: the train master gives it to any clerk of the chancery or any officer of the king and asks that it be passed to the one who wrote. If the master will not carry it, the watcher does not press him, and brings it back to me. If the recipient is hostile or asks questions: the carrier says only that the Sarrow keep the sill and that a guest who comes in peace is fed. He does not say where I am, what I can do, or the size of the host, and he does not argue the claim.

## 4. Winter: hold the hall, hear any envoy at the sill, mark the winter dead
My body stays in the winter city through the cold and I do not step.
If an envoy of the river king comes before the thaw, he is bidden in at the ford and the hall door as any guest, fed first by the host, and I use the Reading on him once, in the open, and tell him I have. If he reads yes I say nothing of it, I eat no more with him, and I tell Idris and Kessan alone. If I feel a weight in the air at the table I stop, let it settle and look at the face before I say a word.
I ask the Mother of the Hearth to bring me the names of the winter's dead of last year, by hearth, and I ask Idris to say them at the rite fire at the first snow, with the empty seat set. I stand at the fire. Reason: about three hundred died over the ordinary, the old and the youngest, and I did not stop it, and they were my people.
I sit at the households' tables in the low quarter one hearth a week, chosen by the Mother of the Hearth, as before. I seat Kessan at my right in the hall as before.

## 5. Spring, at the thaw: walk the road to the farthest toll house, mark its door, watch the first trains meet the toll
I go to the farthest toll house on Pass Road with the Marshal of the Road, on foot, with a few riders. At the hall, before we go, I tell Idris Veld why I mean to lay a sixth full blessing on that door: to know by my own marking how many go in and out of it, and not to accuse any man. The toll house is a place my people hold and I may stand in its threshold unbidden; I tell the keeper of the house what I am doing before I do it. I lay a handful of salt, spare from the Steward's count, and speak the guest oath over the door with the key in my hand. That makes six of seven. I do not shut it, I do not step.
While there I watch the first trains meet the raised toll, and I ask the watchmen what the trains said of it and whether any turned back or took another road. I look at what the house holds of grain. I bring back to the hall what I find and not what I think the hall wishes to hear. At the hall, with Kessan and Idris Veld, we sit with the cords and the counts of the first trains and settle the toll, as I said I would in action 2.
Reason: the cords are one tally and my own count is another. A door counted by me is a third, and it costs one handful of salt.

## 6. Summer: go up with the columns I have not sat with, and set the mark on the grass
From the thaw I travel with the columns I have not travelled with, at their pace, and sit at their rite fires and tables. When they go up to the high grass I go with them. At the old cutting and one other, chosen by the oldest herders, I ask the herders to set a cairn at the foot of the line of the grass and to cut a notch in a stone beside it at the thaw and again at the first snow, and to tell the keeper of rite what the line has done, so that it is counted year on year. I do this through the Mother of the Hearth and the keepers of rite at those fires, and I tell the herders what I found last year and that I do not know what it means. At each new fire I ask once what as Marda held it means, and I hold the answer as that fire's. I send no one looking for anything. My body is with the columns on the high grass, away from the hall, and a question put to me there waits until I am down.

## 7. Late summer to autumn: be back at the ford before the snow
I come down with the columns and stand at Marle Ford before the snow to know who has crossed and to take the sill again. I order every column down at the winter city by first snow and the Marshal to confirm each by rider. I keep the empty seat and the unlatched door at the first night of snow as the people do. I do not step this year. I lay no seventh blessing. If I meet a weight in the air on the way, I stop and let it settle and look at the face before I say a word.

## For the Keeper to rule
Nothing outside my card. I use the Latch once, to lay a sixth full blessing on the door of the farthest toll house on Pass Road, a place my people hold with the Marshal's post, standing in its threshold with the keeper told. I use the Reading of the Host on any envoy of the river king, once, in the open. I do not use the Breaker's Hand unless a door held against a refused traveler forces it. I do not use the Step. I do not try to grow a power this year.
