# Decisions: god-thresholds, Year 1

The year runs from the hour before first light, in autumn, round the four seasons to autumn again. Seven actions, in order.

## 1. Autumn, first light: go down to the ford and bless it
I walk south-east to the ford, about a mile, at the pace of a tall woman, stooping under nothing because there is no roof, with the staff in my hand and the key at my neck. I do not go to the road first and I do not go to the city first. I stand in the water at the middle of the crossing, lay a handful of salt on the stones, and speak the guest oath over it with the key in my hand. That is my first blessed threshold. Reason: it has no keeper, so no one can refuse me and I need no one's leave. It is where my people will expect me, since they set the seat for a guest who comes to a door at the ford. And once it is blessed I will know who crosses it and how many, which is the first true thing I will know about this country. I mark about a spear's length either side of where I stood and no more. I do not try to shut it, because a ford has no frame.

## 2. Autumn, the same morning: go up to the city gate and wait to be bidden
From the ford I walk to the city two miles south-east and stand at its gate, stooping, with the key showing at my neck and my hands empty and open. I do not go in. I do not break anything. I stand where the gate guard can see me, say "I am a guest and I ask the sill," and wait until someone who keeps the gate bids me in by word or plain gesture. Reason: the law I took, and because I want to see how they meet a tall woman with a key before I say anything of myself. If the gate is barred against me I stay outside and wait the day, and I do not use the Breaker's Hand on it, however my fear rises. If they ask who I am I say what is true: I have no name, they may call me Guest, and I have come to lead those who keep the sill. I do not say more.
How I mean to be met: by the warlord Kessan and the First Doorwarden Idris Veld at the winter hall, with the other offices (the Steward of Salt and Stores, the Marshal of the Road, the Mother of the Hearth) sent for. I ask that the door of the hall stand open to me and the seat be set, as their custom is, and that the host break bread first and eat first. I eat at that table, knowing that once I have eaten I cannot speak a lie until I rise, and I speak little.

## 3. Autumn, in the hall, that day or the next: read the two, then speak the first word to the offices
At the table, before the bread is broken, I use the Reading of the Host once on Kessan and once on Idris Veld, each across the table, to ask the one question: does this one mean harm to a guest at this table. They will each learn that I have read them, and that I mean no harm to a guest. I accept that, and I will not hide that I did it. If either reads yes, I say nothing of it, I eat nothing more, and I watch their hands, and I tell no one until I know what I know. If both read no, I speak to the offices, in this order:
- To Kessan: that the host is his to hold on the road and in the gates, for me, and that I ask him for the count of the war band, the count of the columns and when each comes down, and the state of the passes. I ask that nothing be done that I have not heard.
- To Idris Veld: that the rite and the table law are hers, that I ask the salt rite as it is kept, and that I will teach the oath to her first and to those she names in my own person, because only a keeper of rite who has taken it from me may lay a mark at my order.
- To the Steward of Salt and Stores: the count of the winter stock, of grain, salt, fuel and dried meat, and what the host will need to see through to the thaw.
- To the Marshal of the Road: the state of the toll houses, the two passes (north-west and north-east), the ford, and the road to the north-east, and who has crossed them this season.
- To the Mother of the Hearth: that I will hear a grievance from the households before I hear one from anyone else, and that she may bring one to me or to Kessan or to Idris.
I name no guests in bulk this night. I name Idris Veld a guest, since she is the one I most need to be able to bring through a shut door, and no one else yet. Reason: every guest I name is one I may not harm under a roof or within a day's walk, my hand is quick, and I do not yet know any of these people. I would sooner bind myself to few and know them than to many and learn too late.

## 4. Late autumn, before first snow: bless the hall door and the gate, and have the rite fire taught the oath
In the winter city, a place my people hold, I may stand in the threshold without being bidden to the sill. I bless the door of the winter hall in full first, then the gate of the winter city, with the key, a handful of salt each, and the oath. That makes three full blessings (ford, hall door, gate), well under seven, and I leave the rest free. I teach the oath in person to Idris Veld and to the Doorwardens she chooses for their sense and not their rank, and I order three lesser marks laid by them: on the toll house at the ford, on the door of a hearth mother's standing house where a Doorwarden has taken the rite fire, and on one more doorway of a place my people have built, which Idris chooses and tells me. I do not ask Kessan's captains to lay anything, since that office is the Doorwardens'. I ask the Steward to tell me how much salt the host holds and how much I may draw for blessings without hurting the winter stock, because my own bag is small. At the first snow I leave the door of the warlord's house unlatched, set the empty seat and fill a bowl on the sill, as the people do, and I sit at that table myself that night. Reason: this is how they keep their year, and I want to be seen to keep it.

## 5. Winter: hold the city, hear the sill courts, and ask the one question
I stay in the winter city through the cold and do not step anywhere. I sit at the hall door when the First Doorwarden hears a hard matter, one that touches the warlord or is too hard for a column, and I judge by the sill law, which is my justice. I walk the gates by night once a week, unannounced, and see who keeps them and whether they keep them. I hear the Mother of the Hearth. I ask the Steward weekly for the count of food and fuel, and I order the stores shared by the hearth mothers if the count runs short, with the poorest households fed first, because a bad winter is the thing I cannot afford. Once, privately, I ask Idris Veld whether any rite her people keep says a name over a sill, and whose, and I do not say why I ask or ask again. I do not use the Breaker's Hand all winter unless a door held against a refused traveler forces it, and if it does I do it and I own it.

## 6. Spring: walk the passes and the road with the Marshal
When the thaw opens the passes, I walk the road to the north-east with the Marshal of the Road and a small escort of his riders, and I walk both passes, the north-west and the north-east, on foot. I use the Latch to bless one pass, whichever the Marshal names as the one most crossed, standing in it where no one keeps it, with salt and the oath, so that I know who goes through. I do not step. I leave the host's columns to Kessan and the captains as they go up with the herds. I order the toll houses to keep an honest count and to feed any traveler a night before he is asked his business. I ask the Marshal to bring me, not turn away, any stranger with odd tools, odd speech, or any who is wrong in one way, tall or shadowless, so that I can see the face. That is how I look for the others like me, without searching, by standing at a crossing and noticing.

## 7. Summer to the next autumn: go with the columns, test the strangers at the table, and come back before the snow
From summer I travel with one column at a time, at their pace, the walk of a host on the move, and I sit at their rite fire. I do not ask them to treat me as more than a guest and a mother. I eat at tables the Doorwardens set for strangers, and I use the Reading on any I do not trust, one at a time, and I do not hide it. I judge disputes at the nearest sill if the Doorwarden asks it. Orders for the year go out through the three channels as they are: by horn and smoke from the city and the high watch posts for the plain words (move, hold, gather), by the Marshal's riders along the road, and by the keepers of rite at every column's fire. I order all columns to be down and at the winter city by first snow, and the Marshal to confirm each column's coming by rider. I am back at the ford by the first snow, to bless it again if the season has dulled it, and to take the sill once more. If I meet a weight in the air along the way, I stop and let it settle, and I look at the face before I say a word.

## Messages
None. I send no message to anyone not in front of me this year. Everything above is spoken in person to my people, or carried by the Marshal's riders and the keepers of rite as their own custom carries it.

## For the Keeper to rule
Nothing outside my card. I use the Latch at fords, passes and the doors of my people's places, the Reading of the Host once on each person I read, and the Breaker's Hand only if a door held against a traveler forces it. I do not try to grow a power this year. I do not use the Step in Year 1, because I have no wish to be bound within sight of a door for a day, and I have no need.
Two readings I rely on, so the Keeper may correct me: first, that the winter city, its gate, the hall door and the toll house at the ford are places my people hold; second, that the ford I bless in action 1 is the ford the landing release names a mile south-east. If the city two miles south-east is not my people's winter place, I will learn it at the gate, and I will say so next year.
