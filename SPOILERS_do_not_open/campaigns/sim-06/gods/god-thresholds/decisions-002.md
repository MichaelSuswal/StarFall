# Decisions: god-thresholds, Year 2

The year runs from the turn of the year, through autumn, round the four seasons to autumn again. Seven actions, in order. I begin on the road, walking down with the columns to the winter ground, and I was not yet at the ford when the year turned.

## 1. Autumn, first days: go to the ford, then the gate, and answer the guard
My body goes to Marle Ford first, on foot, at the pace of a tall woman, with the staff and the key. I stand at the crossing and ask the older toll watcher for the count of who has crossed since I was last here, and I set it beside what I have known by the blessing, so that I learn whether the two agree. I ask him how the trains from the Aldermark came this season, and I do not scold him for a thin count. I feed any traveler at the toll house a night, as I ordered.
Then I walk the two miles to the city gate and stand where the senior guard can see me, hands open. He asked why I came before the snow last year and I gave him nothing. I answer him now, in a few words, and the words are true: I woke by your road in the dark before first light and went to the nearest door, and I do not know why that was the hour. I say no more than that. Reason: a keeper of a gate asked me a plain question and a host who keeps the sill answers it. I ask him in turn who keeps the gate at night, and whether it has changed.

## 2. Autumn, before the first snow: in the hall, ask for the wolves' reckoning
At the hall, with Kessan, Idris Veld, the Marshal of the Road and the Mother of the Hearth present, I raise the herder who died of wolves in the second month of last year. I ask four plain things and I ask them as one who judges and not as one who accuses: whose watch it was at that fold, why the dogs were late to give tongue, whether any column went without a grown man on the night watch, and who keeps the dead man's household now.
My orders, through the people named. To Kessan: that no column goes down the road at night fold with only boys on the watch, and that each fold has a grown watcher and dogs that have been fed and rested, and that his captains answer for it to him. To the Marshal of the Road: that his riders carry this order to every column by the first relay. To Idris Veld: that the dead man's household be set at a hearth that keeps it, and that the sill law set the price for his death, with the herd or the stores paying what it fixes and not the household. To the Steward of Salt and Stores: that he gives the household its winter keep from the stores, which I put on my own account. Reason: the host kept a man in its keeping and lost him, and I said I would keep the sill. I judged hard cases last year at the hall door and I judge this one.

## 3. Autumn to first snow: have the winter stock counted twice
I tell the Steward at the hall, to his face and in front of Idris Veld, that I would rather have a short true count than a kind one, and that I do not call last year's a lie, since he says it was not. I order two counts of grain, salt, fuel and dried meat before first snow: his, and one made by the hearth mothers of each quarter, brought to the Mother of the Hearth, who sets the two side by side and tells me where they differ. I ask the Steward also what salt is spare, and I draw from that and no more for any blessing this year, since my own bag is small.
If the two counts differ by more than he can tell me why, I order the stores shared by hearth from the first snow and not from the fourth month, the poorest first, and I tell the captains the reason before they hear it from anyone else. Reason: the one thing that can undo me is a hungry winter, and I learned too late last year.

## 4. Winter: sit at the low quarter's tables, hear the passed-over Doorwarden, and put Kessan at my right
My body stays in the winter city through the cold and I do not step.
To the Mother of the Hearth I answer the thing I left unanswered: yes, I will sit at the households' tables in the low quarter, one hearth a week, chosen by her and not by Idris Veld or the Steward. I tell her the terms before I go: I eat there, and at a table where I have eaten I cannot speak a lie until I rise, so what I say there is true or it is silence. I do not use the Reading at these tables. I ask the households what they say of the dimmed star, and I say that I do not know whose it is.
I send word by Idris Veld that the senior Doorwarden of the lower columns, the one passed over, comes to the hall door, and I hear him in person, with Idris at my side, so that he is heard and she is not made to carry what he says. I ask him what he meant, and I ask Idris after what she thinks of him. I choose no one new this year to teach the oath.
At the hall I seat Kessan at my right hand, and I tell him that I will not read him again unless he asks it or crosses the sill with a hand that frightens me, and that this holds until I say otherwise. I ask him for the count of the war band, the state of the passes, and the toll in coin and kind this year against last, and why it is thin. Reason: I need his spears and the captains' word, and he is a man who is told in private what is hard.

## 5. Winter, after the first snow is deep: ask Idris Veld what Marda Sill is
At Idris Veld's own table, once, I ask what Marda Sill is: a place, a person, a rite, or a word, and whose. I do not ask whether it is mine and I do not tell her why I ask. I eat at her table, and I know that once I have eaten I cannot speak a lie until I rise, so if she asks me whether it is mine, I say that I do not know, or I keep silent. I take what she tells me as what she tells me and nothing more, and I do not send anyone to find out in the same season. Reason: she gave it to me when I asked my one question, and I owe it a hearing before the thaw.

## 6. Spring: bless Far Notch and walk it with the Marshal
When the thaw opens the passes I go to Far Notch with the Marshal of the Road and a few riders, on foot, and stand in the pass where no one keeps it, lay a handful of salt on the stones, and speak the guest oath over it with the key in my hand. That makes five of seven, and I know who crosses both passes. I do not step. I walk the road to the north-east again with the Marshal and ask him which toll houses are thin and which keep honest counts. I ask him to bring me, not turn away, any stranger odd in tools, speech or body, as last year. Reason: the river kingdom's trains cross by both passes and the tolls that reach the hall are thin, and I would sooner know than guess.

## 7. Summer to autumn: go with the lower columns and be back at the ford before the snow
From summer I travel with the lower columns, the ones whose Doorwarden was passed over, at their pace, and sit at their rite fires and at the tables they set for strangers. I do not ask them to treat me as more than a guest and a mother. I judge disputes at the nearest sill if the Doorwarden asks it, and I use the Reading on a stranger I do not trust, one at a time, in the open, as before. Orders go out as they did: by horn and smoke, by the Marshal's riders, and by the keepers of rite at every column's fire. I order every column to be down at the winter city by first snow, and the Marshal to confirm each column's coming by rider. I am back at the ford before the snow to stand in it and know who has crossed. If I meet a weight in the air on the way, I stop and let it settle and look at the face before I say a word.

## Messages
None. I send no message to anyone not in front of me this year. Everything above is spoken in person, or carried by the Marshal's riders and the keepers of rite as their own custom carries it.

## For the Keeper to rule
Nothing outside my card. I use the Latch at Far Notch, which has no keeper, the Reading of the Host at tables as before, and I do not use the Breaker's Hand unless a door held against a refused traveler forces it. I do not use the Step this year. I do not try to grow a power this year.
