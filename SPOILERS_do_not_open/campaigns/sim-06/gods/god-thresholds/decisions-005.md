# Decisions: god-thresholds, Year 5

The year runs from the turn of the year, through autumn, round the four seasons to autumn again. Seven actions, in order. I am with the columns coming down to the winter ground when the year turns, and I come down to the ford first, as before. Salt is short at the turn, and three people have waited since summer for my words, so the first action is the answers.

## 1. Autumn, first days: the ford, the gate, then the hall, and the three answers aloud
My body goes to Marle Ford first, on foot, with the staff and the key. I ask the older toll watcher for the count since I was last there. At the toll house table I eat with any master of another people's train who stands there, and I ask him over bread, plainly, whether he has heard why the river kingdom's trains did not come this summer, and what his people say of the star. I tell him I ask for no more than his telling, and I note it as his and no more. I do not use the Reading unless a hand frightens me.
Then I walk the two miles to the gate and the hall. At the hall, with Kessan, Idris Veld, the Steward of Salt and Stores, the Marshal of the Road and the Mother of the Hearth present, I say first that three of them asked me in the summer and I was on the grass and they were not answered, and that was my doing and not theirs. Then I answer, aloud:
- To Kessan, who asked whether he may send riders beyond the houses to learn why the river's trains do not come: yes. The terms are in action 2. They are his riders and his choosing, and I hold him to the terms.
- To the Steward, who asked what to do for salt if none comes before the snow: the order is in action 3, and it stands for the whole winter without asking me again. The Steward carries it out, with Idris Veld witness, and enters the salt on the cord.
- To the Mother of the Hearth, who asked whether the households are to be told the salt will not last at the ration: yes, tell them. Tell them the number the Steward gives and what the hall is doing about it, and tell them what I could not answer in the summer and why. She tells it at the hall fire the first night of the cold, in front of the hearth mothers, and I stand beside her and say the part about the grass that I was not there. A host that is short says so to its households before they find it out at the pot.
Before I go up the mountain or out on the road at any time this year I leave standing answers, aloud and before these five. While I am away Idris Veld may answer in my name any question that touches the rite, the households or the stores, and Kessan any question that touches the road, the passes and the riders. A question that touches both, or that the two of them would answer differently, waits for me. I say it now so that no one has to guess it in the dark.
Reason: three asked in the open and have waited. Salt is a thing that does not wait for the thaw.

## 2. Autumn, the same days, before the passes close: Kessan's riders go beyond the houses, as guests
To Kessan and the Marshal of the Road together: Kessan names a captain and the Marshal names four riders and a guide who knows the road north-east and the river. Idris Veld names one keeper of rite to go with them to keep the guest law at every table they sit at. They go beyond the farthest houses as guests, by the road north-east, to the river kingdom if the road runs there, with toll cloth, iron and felt from the Steward's count to trade for salt, and with my word below. They are to learn three things and to bring them to me as their telling and not as a thing I may wish to hear: why no train of the river kingdom crossed in the tenth, eleventh and twelfth months, where salt may be had and at what price, and what is said of the star there and whether the people have seen it dim.
Terms, as I give them to Kessan in front of Idris: no blood, no seizing of any train or house, no taking of any thing unasked, no oath sworn in my name beyond the guest oath of the table. They say who sent them if asked, that it is the Sarrow of Marle Ford, that they keep the sill, and that they came as guests. They do not say my size, my place, my need or what I can do. If they are turned away they do not press, and they turn back. If they are held they ask for a guest's meal and a hearing at the sill, and they send word by anyone who will carry it. They are back before the deep snow, or they winter where they are given guest right and send word by the first road open in the thaw. The captain brings them home. If they find a house that sells salt on the road, they buy only as much as they can carry and bring it to the Steward, and they do not sell my toll to get it.
Reason: the river's trains stopped and I do not know why, the Marshal and Kessan each have a reading, and a reading is not a witness. Salt is short and a man who carries a bag home feeds a winter.

## 3. Autumn to first snow: salt, two counts, the seed under two marks
To the Steward of Salt and Stores and the Mother of the Hearth: the winter stock of grain, salt, fuel and dried meat is counted twice before the first snow, his and the hearth mothers', and the ration is set from the first day at the lower of the two. The seed grain and seed barley is sealed under two marks, the Steward's and the Mother's, as before, and nothing but the last of all breaks it. If the stores fail, the herd goes first, then the dried meat, then the seed.
For the salt, to the Steward with Idris Veld witness: the salt goes first to the meat that must be put by for the winter, as much as the stock can bear, then to the hearths' bread and the pot, last of all to the toll houses and the rite. All salt offerings brought to the winter hall and the rite fires, which I have counted as the Guest's debt, are given to the common stock this winter and entered on the separate cord. My own small bag I give him but for one handful, which I keep in case a mark must be laid again. I lay no blessing this year that I can help.
To the Marshal of the Road, at the ford and at every toll house, by rider: any train that carries salt is passed without toll this winter, and the hall will buy salt in iron, felt or the toll cloth at a price the Steward names. The grain first order stands. The toll houses send word of every train that carries salt or grain. The Marshal tells the masters of other peoples' trains at the ford this in the open, so that it is known.
Then, as before, the stores are shared by hearth from the first snow, the poorest first, the war band last, and I tell the captains why myself before they hear it from anyone else. They went last a third winter and said the thanks were the first they had had, and I thank them for a fourth and ask for it.
Reason: the two counts and the seal held the winter before last and the ration at the lower count held again last winter. Salt is the new thing, and a train that pays nothing to carry it is a train that will come.

## 4. Winter: hold the hall, mark the dead, hear the herders, and sit with the cords
My body stays in the winter city through the cold and I do not step.
At the first snow I ask Idris Veld to say the names of last winter's dead at the rite fire, hearth by hearth, with the empty seat set, and I stand through it again. I set the empty seat and leave the door unlatched on the first night of snow as the people do. I sit at the households' tables in the low quarter one hearth a week, chosen by the Mother of the Hearth, as before, and I tell them the true things and keep silent about the rest, since at a table where I have eaten I cannot speak a lie.
I ask the Mother of the Hearth and the keepers of rite to bring two of the herders who cut the notches to the hall, to say before Kessan, Idris, the Steward and the Marshal what they said at the cairns: that the line stood where the oldest say it stood last year, no higher, that the flower did not come, and that a stone cut to count a line does not hold it. I do not tell them what it means, and I tell the hall that I do not know. Reason: the herders say it and the households do not hear them, and what is said at the cairns is no use to the hall if the hall does not hear it.
I seat Kessan at my right as before. If an envoy of the river king comes before the thaw he is bidden in at the ford and the hall door as any guest, fed first by the host, and I use the Reading on him once, in the open, and tell him I have. If a weight in the air comes at the table I stop, let it settle, and look at the face before I say a word.

## 5. Spring, at the thaw: hear the riders in the open, break the seed seal before witnesses, sit with the cords
When Kessan's riders are home, or word has come from them, I hear them at the hall in front of the five, aloud, and I ask Kessan and the Marshal to say it to the captains after. I ask what they saw and what they were told, and I ask them to say which is which. I do not decide on the river kingdom the day I hear it. I sit with Kessan and Idris and the Marshal for a night and a day before I answer, since my hand is quick and the first answer is rarely the right one.
At the sowing the Steward and the Mother break the seed seal together at the hall door in the open, with Idris Veld witness, and count what the sowing has. I tell the households the count.
After the first trains of the thaw are counted I sit with Kessan and Idris and the cords of the first trains, as last year, and we set the toll by the cords and not by one mouth. If the river kingdom's trains have come back, I ask the Marshal to tell me by what road and in what number.

## 6. Spring, on a train day at the farthest toll house: step to its door, see what is carried in, and say so
When the thaw is open and the trains are on the road, I stay at Marle Ford. I know by the sixth blessing when a train stands at the farthest toll house, and when more cross its door than the house's own number. On such a day, with the Marshal and Kessan and Idris told beforehand at the hall, in the open, that I may do it on any day without word and why, I step through the ford to the farthest toll house door. I do not tell the keeper beforehand. I tell him the moment I stand in his door, to his face, what I am doing and why: that I came to see with my own eyes what is carried in at his door on a train day, that I accuse no man, and that I ask nothing but to stand in his threshold and count. I stay the day. I eat at his table if he bids me, and I say that I eat knowing I cannot lie until I rise. I look at what goes in and who carries it, by the beast and the load and the head, and what the keeper has on his cord for the same day. I ask whose goods are carried in and for how long they stay. I do not use the Breaker's Hand on the door, the smokehouse, the store or anything else, whatever I find. If my hand moves I stand still and I say aloud that it is moving.
After the dawn after a full day I step back through that door to the ford, and I tell what I found at the hall table to Kessan, Idris and the Marshal together before I say it to anyone else. If there is a second trade I do not judge it that night. It is judged at the sill, with the keeper heard, by Idris Veld and Kessan together, and I give my view last. I use the Step once this year and no more. I know that the ford, which I leave, stands open for a night and tells me nothing of who crosses, and that I am the guest of the farthest door until the dawn after a full day and cannot lift or shut it.
Reason: the cords say one thing and the door says another, and the keeper told me last year the cord had nothing in it that the knots did not say. A cord can be true and still be short of the trade. I want to see what the cord does not say.

## 7. Summer to autumn: stay within two days of the hall, go up once, and be back at the ford before the snow
From midsummer I stay in the country near the hall, at the ford, the gate, Stair Pass and Far Notch with the Marshal, where trains cross and a rider reaches me in two days, so that no question put to me waits a season. I go up to the cairns once only, to see the second notch cut and the line with my own eyes, and I come down in a week, with the standing answers of action 1 held by Idris Veld and Kessan while I am gone. I ask the herders what the line has done, and I tell them what I find and that I do not know what it means.
I order every column down at the winter city by first snow and the Marshal to confirm each by rider. I am at Marle Ford before the snow to take the sill again. I lay no seventh blessing. I do not step again this year.

## Messages

### To the king of the river kingdom, at his chancery
To: the king of the river kingdom, or the chancery that wrote to whoever holds the ford.
The words, exactly: "To the one who wrote to whoever holds the ford. A word of the Sarrow went to you in the autumn of last year, by a train master, and no answer came. Since the tenth month no train of your kingdom has crossed the road. The toll was lowered at the ninth month, and the road is open to you as to any guest. I do not charge you with the absence. If it is the toll, say what would bring your trains. If it is another thing, say so. The sill is the same as it was. A claim is heard in person, at Marle Ford, and nothing is owed or refused until it is heard. I have no name to sign. They call me Guest, and Mother of the Door."
The carrier: the captain of Kessan's riders, who gives it into the hand of the first officer of the king or clerk of the chancery that will take it, and waits for an answer for as long as a guest may be asked to wait.
The route: the road north-east and the river, as the guide would choose it.
Sent with it: nothing.
If the recipient cannot be found: the captain gives it to any clerk or officer of the king and asks that it be passed to the one who wrote, and tells me by a rider who took it. If the recipient is hostile or asks questions: the captain says only that the Sarrow keep the sill and that a guest who comes in peace is fed. He does not say where I am, what I can do, the size of the host, or that salt is short, and he does not argue the claim. If he is asked who he is he says whose rider he is.

## For the Keeper to rule
Nothing outside my card. I use the Step once, in action 6, from the blessed threshold at Marle Ford to the blessed door of the farthest toll house, alone, and I stay within sight of that door until the dawn after a full day, as the card says. The one question I put: when I stand in the arrival door for that full day and then step back through it to the ford, is the second step a step from inside a blessed threshold that I hold in full, so that the ford stands open for a second night and the guest limit falls on me at the ford and not on the farthest door, as I read the card? If the Keeper reads that I must wait out a second full day before returning on foot or by step, I will do as the Keeper rules and say so to the hall. I use the Latch only to know who crosses what I hold, the Reading of the Host on an envoy of the river king once in the open and on any stranger I do not trust, and I do not use the Breaker's Hand. I do not try to grow a power this year.
