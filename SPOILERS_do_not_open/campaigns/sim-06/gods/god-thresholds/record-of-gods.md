# Record of other gods: god-thresholds

## Others like me
Others like me came down when I did. I do not know how many, who they are, where they stand, or what they can do. I will know one only when I stand near it, in the same room or across a doorway, and feel the weight of it settle into this is one like me, and that will tell me nothing more.

## What I have met
No one, in three years. I have stood at the ford, the city gate, the hall door, both passes, the toll houses, the old cutting at the high gates and the rite fires of the lower columns, and sat at about a dozen low quarter tables and at the tables of the warlord, the First Doorwarden, a river trader, a packer, a captain's cousin and, in the fourth month of Year 3, the senior Doorwarden of the lower columns. I felt no weight in the air at any of them. Eleven strangers were brought to me in the first year, seven in the second and five in the third, and each was ordinary. I name no god, I hold no grievance, I am owed no promise and I owe none.

## What three years taught me about the watching
- A mortal at a table who reads clean tells me nothing about whether I stand near one of my own kind. The weight in the air is the only sign I trust, and I felt none.
- My Reading does not work on a god. If I ever read across a table and get no answer where I should get one, that is a thing to note, not to trust. I have not met it yet.
- The strangers the Marshal brings are fewer each year and always ordinary. A god who wished to be found would be easier to find than I am, and one who wished not to be found would not come to a toll house. Neither is knowledge.
- My people and the far houses are most of what I have seen of this country. A god could stand a long way off, in a place with no door I have blessed, and I would not know it. Three years of standing at crossings is not a search of the world.
- The chancery of the river kingdom wrote to whoever holds the ford. That is a mortal king's clerk, not a god. I do not know what stands behind the old power it names, and I will not guess it into a god because I want one. A king who comes in person will cross my sill, and I will stand near him and read him.

## The star
The star dimmed on three nights in my first year, five in my second and six in my third. In the third: one in the first month, a pair in the fifth, a pair in the seventh and one in the eighth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour in the middle of the night, the star stood dim for about an hour, and then it was as before. My people count fourteen since I came, and the number grows each year, three, five, six.
It does not follow my marks. I laid three full blessings in the first year and three dimmings came; I laid one in the second and five came; I laid none in the third and six came. I have no power that touches the sky, and I do not know whose it is. It may be another like me. It may be the world's own weather. It may answer to something I cannot see. I hold all of these as guesses and I will not tell anyone it is mine, or that it is not, in a voice that sounds sure. If a household asks me, I say I do not know.

## What I will watch for
- The weight in the air at a gate, a hall door, a toll house, a ford, anywhere strangers cross and I stand near. If it comes I will stand still, let it settle, and look at the face before I decide anything.
- A traveler odd in a way that is not mortal: too tall, no shadow, wrong joints.
- Whether a stranger turns up at a crossing of mine within days of a dimming.
- A reading that gives me nothing.
- An envoy of the river king, if one comes. He crosses my sill as a guest. I read him, and I note who stands behind him.
- The far toll houses, where I saw wealth I cannot yet explain.

Nothing else is in this record, because nothing else has happened.
