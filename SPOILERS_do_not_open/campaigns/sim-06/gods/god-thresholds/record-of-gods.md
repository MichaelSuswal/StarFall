# Record of other gods: god-thresholds

## Others like me
Others like me came down when I did. I do not know how many, who they are, where they stand, or what they can do. I will know one only when I stand near it, in the same room or across a doorway, and feel the weight of it settle into this is one like me, and that will tell me nothing more.

## What I have met
No one, in two years. I have stood at the ford, the city gate, the hall door, both passes, the toll houses and the rite fires of two columns, and sat at about a dozen low quarter tables and at the tables of the warlord, the First Doorwarden, a river trader, a packer and a captain's cousin. I felt no weight in the air at any of them. Eleven strangers were brought to me in the first year and seven in the second, and each was ordinary. I name no god, I hold no grievance, I am owed no promise and I owe none.

## What two years taught me about the watching
- A mortal at a table who reads clean tells me nothing about whether I stand near one of my own kind. The weight in the air is the only sign I trust, and I felt none.
- My Reading does not work on a god. If I ever read across a table and get no answer where I should get one, that is a thing to note, not to trust. I have not met it yet.
- I do not know whether a god would come to a toll house at all, or would keep to the high places, or would be like me and stand where doors are. I now hold that a god who wished to be found would be easier to find than I am, and that one who wished not to be found would not come to a toll house. Neither is knowledge.
- My people and the far houses are most of what I have seen of this country. A god could stand a long way off, in a place with no door I have blessed, and I would not know it. Two years of standing at crossings is not a search of the world.

## The star
The star dimmed on three nights in my first year and five in my second: one in the second month, one in the sixth, one in the ninth, one in the eleventh and one in the twelfth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour in the middle of the night and then it was as before. That is eight in two years, and more this year than last. The Doorwardens now count five dimmings against five blessed thresholds. The count does not fit my own: I laid three full blessings in the first year and three dimmings came, and I laid one in the second and five came. I did not do it. I have no power that touches the sky, and I do not know whose it is. It may be another like me. It may be the world's own weather and nothing more. It may be something answering to the marks, though the numbers by year do not say so. I hold all of these as guesses and I will not tell anyone it is mine, or that it is not, in a voice that sounds sure. If a household asks me, I say I do not know.

## What I will watch for
- The weight in the air at a gate, a hall door, a toll house, a ford, anywhere strangers cross and I stand near. If it comes I will stand still, let it settle, and look at the face before I decide anything.
- A traveler odd in a way that is not mortal: too tall, no shadow, wrong joints.
- The nights the star dims: whether a stranger turns up at a crossing of mine within days of one, and whether a dimming falls within days of a mark of mine being laid or crossed in some unusual way.
- A reading that gives me nothing.
- The far toll houses, where I saw wealth I cannot explain from the cords. A stranger who has paid for roofs and horses is a stranger I have not met.

Nothing else is in this record, because nothing else has happened.
