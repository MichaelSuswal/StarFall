# Self: god-thresholds

## Name
It has none. It remembers having one and cannot say where the place is. Mortals who meet it will call it what they like; it answers to "Guest" and to "Mother of the Door" without correcting anyone, and it listens, every time, for the one name that is not there.

## Form
A human woman seeming past her middle years, broad in the shoulder, lined at the mouth and eyes, grey eyes, hair gone iron grey and braided up and pinned. Her wrongness is one thing only: she is too tall, near eight feet, and she stoops by habit under every lintel and bough, so that she is always seen bending toward a doorway. Hands large and warm. Human senses, no better. She walks a long unhurried stride at the pace of a tall woman.
### What breaks you
Ordinary harm: blade, fire, crushing, drowning, a fall. A body that is wounded enough is broken. She can be bound with rope, chain or iron, caged, or walled in a room that is barred from outside, and she cannot step out of a held place by any means but force and time. She can be shut out: a ground whose keepers refuse her and bar the way, if she will not break it, stays closed to her. She can be scattered by a body broken beyond gathering.
### When your body is broken
Return in a month: see "The Return Home" below.
### Things carried
- A key of dark iron on a leather cord at her neck, cut wrongly, with teeth that fit no lock she has found. Proposed below as the instrument of The Latch.
- A small leather bag of coarse salt, an ordinary possession at no price. It does nothing but season, preserve and mark a sill; it can be stolen or spilled.
- A travelling cloak, a staff of ash as tall as herself. Ordinary possessions.

## Traits
**The Host Who Counts the Cost.** Warm, mothering, patient in speech, with iron under it. She is clever and wary, and she hides what she is until she has weighed the one in front of her. She feeds a stranger, seats him, asks nothing, and watches his hands. It reconciles Mind (clever), Trust (wary), Presence (a little hidden), Regard for mortals (sovereign: the kindness is real and it is also hers to give or withhold), and the virtue of justice: she keeps accounts of who was welcomed and who was turned away.

**The Slammed Door.** Impulsive, hungry, a breaker. When a wrong is done at a threshold, or when fear rises, she acts before she has weighed, and what she does is to shut, break, take or burn. It reconciles Temper (impulsive), Making (breaker), Appetite (hungry), Body (strong, in the manner of a woman who has never been refused a heavy door), and the flaw of fear: she is afraid of being shut in, of being shut out, of a door that does not open, and she breaks things to be sure of them. The two traits live in the same body. The host decides slowly and kindly; the other one slams a door and regrets it in the morning, or does not.

## Backstory outline
She dimly remembers a house, or many houses, and herself standing in a doorway holding a lamp for someone coming up out of the dark, and the feel of a latch under her thumb. She remembers being a keeper of something, and that it mattered to be right about who was let in. She remembers that someone said a name over a threshold and that it was hers, and that afterward it was not there. She does not know whether it was taken, given, or spent. She feels it as a smooth place where a word should be, and she touches it the way a tongue touches a lost tooth. She does not know what it cost, or who stood on the other side.

## What it wants
On arrival she wants her people: to find them or gather them, to lead them, to give them a road and a rule of guest and host that they can live by, and to see that they are not robbed or sold on it. She wants land with crossings in it, fords, passes, gates, because she is at home where doors are still being decided. She wants to see what the strangers of this world do at a threshold: who feeds the traveler and who turns him out. She wants safe passage for her own, and she wants those who refuse hospitality to be found out. She wants, without being able to say why, to hear someone say something over a doorway that is hers.

## Proposed powers

### The Latch
What it does: She blesses a threshold by standing in it, laying salt on the sill, and speaking the guest oath over it with the key in her hand. A door, gate, ford, pass, or tent flap may be so blessed, any single crossing a body goes through. She may hold up to seven blessed thresholds at once; a new one beyond seven releases the oldest. While a threshold is blessed she knows, wherever she is, when anyone crosses it and how many, and, if she has seen their face, who; she knows nothing of what is said or done there. She may, by a word spoken to the key, shut a blessed threshold: it will not open to anyone, by hand, ram or tool, until she lifts it or until its frame is broken, and a shut blessed door admits anyone she has named as guest by speaking the oath over them. Only the key does this; she must hold it to bless, to shut, to lift, and to name a guest.
Why this god has it: She is the guest who tests the house, and a door she has blessed is a door that keeps the oath for her. Thresholds are what she is. Safe passage is what she wants to be able to sell, give and withhold.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The oath binds her. A guest she has named at a blessed threshold, and anyone who has eaten at a table under it with her leave, cannot be harmed by her while under that roof or within a day's walk after, and if she breaks it by her own hand the mark turns: it shuts against her and every other blessed threshold she holds goes dark for a season, so she knows nothing of them and cannot shut or lift them. Her impulsive hand makes this a real danger.
Edge cases it expects: A threshold with no frame, as a ford, counts as blessed where she stood in the water to speak it, with a span of about a spear's length either side; the river crossing is the one she marked, not the whole river. A door removed and set elsewhere is not the blessed threshold; a door whose frame is rebuilt in place is. Someone who crosses carrying a name she does not know she knows only as a body. A host who dies leaves the mark standing. Her own people and her priesthood may be named guests in bulk.

### The Step
What it does: From inside a blessed threshold she may step through and arrive at the same moment in another threshold she holds blessed, in the time of one pace, carrying what she holds and wears. She may bring one person who holds her hand and has been named guest under The Latch. She cannot step to a threshold she has not blessed.
Why this god has it: She is the one at the door, not a hundred shadows at a hundred doors; she is still one body in one place, and it is the door that moves her. It gives her reach among peoples on the move and a way to appear in the doorway where she is least expected, and to be at a crossing when it is shut.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each step leaves the door she left standing open behind her, in the sense that nothing of the oath holds there for one night: anyone or anything may pass through it, the threshold will not shut and will not tell her who crossed. And each step is owed: the one who steps owes a day to the door she arrived in, and for one day she cannot use The Latch to shut it or lift it.
Edge cases it expects: A broken threshold, or one whose mark has gone dark, is not a place she can step to. If the arrival threshold is physically blocked she arrives in the nearest open place within a pace of it. She cannot step while bound or held; bound hands cannot hold the key.

### The Breaker's Hand
What it does: She may break what is held shut: bar, lock, gate, a wall of a house, the seal of a sealed door, a ford's weir or a chain. She lays a hand on it and it gives: wood splits, iron parts, a seal breaks, mortar fails. The strength is hers in a single stroke and no more than a very strong person with a ram could manage with a day to spare, done at once; it does nothing against a living body, a bound limb or an animal, and does nothing to a thing that is not shut.
Why this god has it: She is a breaker by nature, and fear drives it: when a door will not open for her she needs it to. It is also a means of enforcing the guest oath on those who refuse it: the door that turned a traveler out is a door she may break.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): She cannot unmake what she breaks. A thing she has broken she cannot mend by any means of her own, and a threshold she has blessed and then breaks loses the blessing for good. It may not be mended by her key, or her people at her order, for a season.
Edge cases it expects: A thing broken in the hand of another, by a person using something she has handed to them, is not her breaking. A wall she breaks is only broken where she touched it. A sealed thing set by another god, if there is such a thing, is priced and ruled as the Keeper sees fit, and she asks no more than that.

### The Reading of the Host
What it does: She may know, of a person seated across a table or standing across a threshold from her, whether that person means harm to a guest. It tells her, yes or no, of that one person, for the length of one meal or one crossing. It says nothing of why, nor whom.
Why this god has it: The seed of her is the one who tests the host and the traveler. Justice is her virtue, and she is wary; this is what she wants most of all, to know before the bread is broken.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Whoever she reads learns the same of her: that she has read them, and whether she means harm to a guest. (I choose this of the pair offered; the other would have me never read my own people, and I would rather read them and be seen doing it.)
Edge cases it expects: It does not work on a person who has never been a host nor a guest, such as a sleeping or insensible one. It does not read an animal, or a god. It answers only the one question, and only for the one person; a crowd is read one by one.

### The Return Home
What it does: When her body is broken, she returns whole in a month, at the nearest ground of her kind. The rung is the month rung, two items of memory or habit as the standing scale prices it, because she is afraid and she will not be away longer. What she carries comes back with her.
Why this god has it: She cannot die, but she can be held, scattered or shut out, and her people drift without her. A month away from a people on the move is long enough.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Two items of memory or habit, chosen by the Keeper and removed from her record; she does not know what went.
Edge cases it expects: Ground of her kind is the nearest ground a god of her kind would stand in or return to; she names no ground and asks for no advantage over the nearest. If she is bound, the month runs from the breaking of the body, not from release.

## Trades
**The key as instrument (weakness taken on).** The Latch comes only through the key of dark iron on her neck. Whoever takes the key takes The Latch from her, and with it The Step, which stands on the blessed thresholds, until she holds the key again; in any other hand it is only an ordinary iron key that fits no lock. What counts as taking: out of her hand and her person, held or kept by another, whether stolen, handed over, or picked up from where she dropped it. What does not count: a copy of it, which is only an ordinary key; the key lying in her own keeping out of arm's reach, as in her bag or on a table beside her, since she holds it in her keeping. How long: as long as another has it, and ends the moment she holds it again, by hand. Marks already laid stay on the thresholds while she is parted from it, but they are dark to her, and she cannot shut, lift, or hear them.
What it buys: her helpers, in her name, may bless a threshold at her order, in the lesser way, as keepers of rite under her: a doorway in a house of her people, by salt and the oath and the sayings of the rite, bound to her count of seven and known to her like any other she holds. Her priesthood does this, not her war captains, and only for a place her people hold. It is a faint blessing, not a full one: a lesser mark may be known to her and not shut by her, and may not be a place she steps to.
Edge cases of the trade: A priest who does it must have been given the oath by her, in person. If the key is taken, the lesser marks stand but are dark to her as the rest. The helpers' blessing does not count a threshold of a people she does not rule.

Her fear, her impulsive hand, and her habit of breaking what will not open are not trades. They are who she is and buy nothing.

## The people it will lead
A very large people, fifteen to thirty thousand, a close band on the move with no fixed seat, that may raise a city at a crossing where it winters. A mortal warlord rules them under her, by force, through a war band and its captains, and answers to her; he rules for her, and she does not rule over his head from day to day. A priesthood of keepers of rite stands beside him, with standing among the people, keeping the guest law and the salt and the doorway rite; offerings are brought and no tithe is laid by law. I will build the particulars on Night 3. I take the leans as given and give no reason to depart.
