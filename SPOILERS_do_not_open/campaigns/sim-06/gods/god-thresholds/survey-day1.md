## Answer 1
A long table in a house with the door left unlatched, and everyone at it knowing exactly what that costs. Warm bread, a fire, and the draft from the doorway that never quite stops. It feels like being welcomed and being watched at the same time, and the world being honest about both.

## Answer 2
River country, and the places where it breaks into fords and narrows. Land with crossings in it: passes, gorges, gates in the hills, the one bridge a whole valley depends on. I want country that has doors built into it by nature, so that nobody can go anywhere without passing through somewhere.

## Answer 3
Four seasons, and each of them should mean it. A hard winter that tests every roof and every host. A wet, loud spring when the rivers rise and the crossings change. A summer long enough to be generous in. An autumn that is a reckoning, when debts are counted and doors are shut for the cold. I like a year with edges, because an edge is a threshold too, and I like to know which side of it I am standing on. The passing from one season to the next should be felt, not just noticed.

I want to wake into autumn.

## Answer 4
Some of each, though I want the old kingdoms to be few and the wild to be most of it. Rooted places are where the doors already have keepers, and I would rather find places where the doors are still being decided. But I want at least a few settled strangers out there to test and to trade with, and to push against. A land with nobody in it teaches nothing. A land with only walls in it leaves me nothing to open.

## Answer 5
Old. Thousands of years, with ruins under ruins, because I want to know that people have been coming and going through these doors for a very long time, and that most of them were not kind to each other. Let the memory of it be patchy. Let some of what is buried be a thing the living no longer have a word for.

## Answer 6
Closer to the rumor most people doubt than to a wise-woman in every valley, but not by much. Let there be a grandmother in some villages who knows a thing or two about the threshold and the salt and the name you do not say aloud, and let her neighbors half laugh at her until the night they stop laughing. Never gone entirely. Never common enough to be trusted.

## Answer 7
It should feel like a promise made in a doorway: something you step through and cannot easily step back from. Its price should be something that is owed, not something that is spent. A debt that stays on the books, a favor that must be repaid in kind, a name or a day or a room in your house given up. Magic that costs nothing makes careless people, and careless people are the ones who open doors they should not.

## Answer 8
More than a great mortal sorcerer, but not by so much that the mortals have no say. I want to be plainly different, seen and counted, someone a village must decide what to do about. I do not need to be the one-eyed king of the blind. I need to be the guest whose arrival changes how the house is run. A god who can be ignored has no standing, and a god who cannot be resisted has no one to rule worth ruling.

## Answer 9
Familiar animals with a good many legends, and at the edges, a few creatures with no name at all. Let the strangeness thicken toward the places that are hardest to cross: the high passes, the deep fords, the thresholds of the map. The wild should be strange where it is hardest to enter, so that every door into it is a decision.

## Answer 10
Hospitality refused, and hospitality abused. The stranger at the door who is more than he seems, the host who turns away the cold traveler and is found out by morning. Also the thing that comes in when a door was left unwatched, and the cold that finds a house with a bad latch. I like a danger that arrives through a way someone should have kept shut, because it means every fear has a person behind it who could have done better.

## Answer 11
Safe passage, and the trust that goes with it. Salt and iron too, but under them, the plain rare thing of being able to travel and sleep somewhere without being robbed or sold. Let people trade for it, fight for it, pay a toll for it, and go without it, and let the ones who can offer it be the ones who hold power.

## Answer 12
Sealed doors. Old roads that lead to them. Gates in hillsides with nothing on the other side, or something on the other side that was put there on purpose. I would also like some bones, large ones, in a place no one expected, because I do not mind being unsettled by what is under my feet. But the doors first.

## Answer 13
Yes, a few, and I want them to be hard to read. Not monsters, not simple neighbors. Peoples with their own rules of guest and host that are a little different from ours, so that to cross their threshold is to risk giving offense without knowing it. Let some of them be generous past reason, and let some of them keep their doors shut for good reasons we would not like.

## Answer 14
A few, and not welcome by everyone. Let them come over the passes with odd tools, strange speech, and knowledge nobody has the shape to take in. I want to test them, and I want them to be tested. A traveler who brings something new should be made to prove what kind of guest he is.

## Answer 15
Face to face only, and not at once. A knowing that arrives at the threshold, when two of us stand on either side of a door and each feels the other on the far side. A pull at any distance is too easy, and too easy to be hunted by. I would rather have to look someone in the face and decide.

## Answer 16
One body. I would rather be the one at the door than a hundred shadows at a hundred doors. But I would not refuse a lesser thing, a part of me left behind in a house, a token that stands in for me at a threshold I have blessed. Anything more than that should cost a great deal, and should cost something I would notice missing.

## Answer 17
The one who opens a door nobody else would, and who has to live with what comes through. A host who takes in the wrong guest, a traveler who knocks on the wrong gate, a keeper who lets someone in out of pity. I want stories with a latch in the middle of them. Let her be stubborn and kind and a little afraid, and let her be right more often than she deserves.

## Answer 18
A door. One I cannot yet imagine, and one I did not make, and one that does not open for me the way the others do. I would like to find out that thresholds are older than I think, and that there is a country on the other side of them that does not have gods in it. Or a companion who knows me by a name I do not remember.

## Answer 19
That someone, somewhere in this world, should say my name in a doorway, and that I should answer to it.
