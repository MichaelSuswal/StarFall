# Decisions: god-thresholds, Year 3

The year runs from the turn of the year, through autumn, round the four seasons to autumn again. Seven actions, in order. I begin on the road with the columns coming down to the winter ground, and I am not yet at the ford when the year turns.

## 1. Autumn, first days: stand at the ford, then the gate, then the hall, and answer what I left unanswered
My body goes to Marle Ford first, on foot, with the staff and the key. I ask the older toll watcher for the count since I was last there and set it beside what I know by the blessing, and I ask how the trains came. Then I walk the two miles to the gate and the hall.
At the hall, with Kessan, Idris Veld, the Steward of Salt and Stores, the Marshal of the Road and the Mother of the Hearth present, I answer three things aloud. To the Steward, on the account for the dead herder's household: it is mine, and it is paid from the offerings brought to the winter hall and the rite fires, the bread, salt, grain and milk that are given to the Guest, and I tell him the amount to be drawn is what he enters on the separate cord, no more. Idris Veld enters it and stands witness. To the Mother of the Hearth, for the herder who asked at a rite fire in the tenth month whether the cold is coming down: I do not know, I will go up and look this summer, and she carries that to him. To the Steward again: that I do not call his count a lie, and that I would have a short true count over a kind one. Reason: a host that says it keeps the sill pays what it owes and answers what it is asked.

## 2. Autumn, before first snow: judge the matter of grass between the two households, at the sill
The younger Doorwarden of the lower columns asked that I judge a matter of grass between two households of his column and my decisions last year gave no verdict, so the two have waited a year. When the columns are down I go to the sill of that column's standing house in the winter city, with the younger Doorwarden and both households present, and I hear both. I eat at the sill table, knowing that at a table where I have eaten I cannot speak a lie until I rise, so what I say is true or it is silence. I set the line by the sill law and not by my own wish, and the Doorwarden has it confirmed by the captain of the column. I do not use the Reading unless a hand frightens me. Reason: two households have waited a year on my silence and that is mine to mend.

## 3. Autumn, from first snow: two counts again, and the cords of every toll house brought to the hall
To the Steward and the Mother of the Hearth: the winter stock of grain, salt, fuel and dried meat is counted twice before the first snow, his and the hearth mothers', and the Mother sets the two side by side and tells me where they differ. If they differ by more than he can tell me why, the stores are shared by hearth from the first snow, the poorest first, the war band last, and I tell the captains why myself, before they hear it from anyone else, and I thank them for the last winter's patience. I ask him what salt is spare and draw only that.
To the Marshal of the Road, through his own riders: every toll house sends its cord for the year to the winter hall by first snow, the far houses too. I set them beside my own count of who crossed Stair Pass and Far Notch, which I have by the blessing, and which the Marshal does not know I keep. To Kessan I say only that I want to see the cords, and I ask him to sit with me when I do. Reason: the cords are thin by half and a count I made by other hands is the only way I will learn why.

## 4. Winter: hear the senior Doorwarden of the lower columns again, and decide whether to teach him the oath
My body stays in the winter city through the cold and I do not step.
I send word by Idris Veld that the senior Doorwarden of the lower columns who asked for the oath comes to the hall door with her sitting beside me. I tell him before he sits that I will use the Reading on him once, in the open, because what I teach lets him lay a mark in my name, and that he may refuse the table. If he sits and reads clean, I teach him the oath in person and he may lay a lesser mark at my order, on a standing doorway of a place my people hold, only when I tell him to and only when Idris has been told. I lay none this year unless a place I hold loses its mark. If he reads yes, I say nothing of it, I eat no more, and I tell Idris alone. Reason: he asked in the open, he took my answer standing, and a rite held by four is held by four. I do not want one old woman's sense to be the only sense I have, and I will not teach him in a corner.
I seat Kessan at my right as before, and I tell him that the Doorwardens' slight at it is theirs and not his, and that it stays so.

## 5. Spring, at the thaw: send the Marshal and a captain of Kessan's choosing to walk the two farthest toll houses unannounced
I do not walk the road myself this year. To the Marshal of the Road and to Kessan together, at the hall, once the cords have been set beside my count of the passes: the Marshal walks the two farthest houses and the two near ones himself, and a captain Kessan names walks with him, and neither sends word ahead. They count the stores, the beasts and the roofs, and they ask each watchman where the new horses were bought and with what. They bring me what they find and not what they think I wish to hear. I tell the Marshal that I do not accuse him, that he went quiet last year and I would rather he learned it himself than through me. I ask whether any stranger has used the road, and ask that any odd in tools, speech or body be brought to me and not turned away. Reason: I am one body and the war band is not mine to command by day; if there is a theft or a second trade on that road, it is Kessan's and the Marshal's to find, and I want to see whether they will.

## 6. Spring to summer: the lower columns, the cutting, and Marda Sill at their fires
From the thaw I travel with the lower columns, at their pace, and sit at their rite fires and their tables. Orders go out as before: by horn and smoke, by the Marshal's riders, by the keepers of rite at every column's fire. When the columns go up to the high grass I go up with them to the old cutting where the blue flower was not seen, and I look at it myself and ask the oldest herders how the line of the grass has moved in their lives and whether this is a bad year or a long one. I tell the herder what I found, plainly, and say I do not know what it means. I ask, at each fire, once, what the lower columns mean by "as Marda held it" and by Sill, and I hold each answer as that fire's answer and nothing more. I do not send anyone looking.

## 7. Late summer to autumn: be back at the ford before the snow
I come down with the lower columns and stand at Marle Ford before the snow to know who has crossed and to take the sill again. I order every column down at the winter city by first snow and the Marshal to confirm each by rider. I keep the empty seat and the unlatched door at the first night of snow as the people do. If I meet a weight in the air on the way, I stop and let it settle and look at the face before I say a word. I do not step this year, and I lay no sixth full blessing. Two of seven stay free.

## Messages
None. I send no message to anyone not in front of me this year. Everything above is spoken in person, or carried by the Marshal's riders and the keepers of rite as their own custom carries it.

## For the Keeper to rule
Nothing outside my card. I use the Latch only to know who crosses what I hold. I use the Reading of the Host once, in the open, on the senior Doorwarden of the lower columns before I teach him the oath, and on a stranger I do not trust. I do not use the Breaker's Hand unless a door held against a refused traveler forces it. I do not use the Step. I do not try to grow a power this year.
