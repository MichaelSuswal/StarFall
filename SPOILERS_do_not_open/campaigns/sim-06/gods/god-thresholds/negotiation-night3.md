# Negotiation: god-thresholds, Night 3

## Round 1 reply

### Your landing: the helpers and the seat
Choice: (b). I do not take (a). Under (a) I come down to a people whose offices answer to their own custom and carry no word of mine, and I am left to win each one in play with no helper behind me, which is the cost of a god who lands unlooked for. That is not the god I wrote. I wrote a people who set the seat and leave the door unlatched for a tall woman who stoops, with a key, and who have told their households to bid in the first one who does. If I come to them as that woman, they have not been waiting for a stranger. They have been waiting for me.

The meeting, rewritten in my words: At the first snow I come down by the pass to the ford, stooping, with the key at my neck, and my people know me on sight. They know me for the Guest the old women describe, and for whose they are: the warlord, the First Doorwarden and the captains know the tall woman and the key, and they know the host is mine. The offices I described serve me from the first night: the Warlord holds the host and the road for me, the First Doorwarden keeps the rite and the table law for me, the Steward of Salt and Stores, the Marshal of the Road and the Mother of the Hearth answer to them under me, and my word is carried to the far end of the host while I am away by horn and smoke, by the Marshal's riders and by the keepers of rite. I do not say they know my name, because I have none, or what I can do, or that they will always do as I say. The warlord is wary of the First Doorwarden and afraid he will not be ready; the First Doorwarden may stay silent when she should speak. Each can fail me in play. They know whose they are, and that is all I ask.

I note for the Keeper that this is not a bend of any rule. The Keeper says neither way is priced and neither buys anything, and I take it so.

### How many: the cost of eighteen thousand
Accepted as written, all four: food and fuel, moving at about half a walker's pace, slow growth, and grass. I wrote that I accepted the cost and did not ask for the number to be cheap, and I do not ask it now. I keep eighteen thousand.

### The war band
Choice: (a), three hundred and sixty sworn spears under the eight captains, the rest of the nine hundred among those who come when the host calls. I do not take (b). I could offer a weakness for nine hundred, but the only ones I can name are costs the world already charges a host this size, and I will not sell a cost twice. A band of three hundred and sixty is a war band, and the three thousand who carry a spear when the host calls are unchanged; that is where my people's strength is, and I do not lose it.

One counter, for a ruling and not a new edge: the Marshal's riders and the watchers at the toll houses and crossings are keepers of a trade, paid in tolls and in kind and posted in ones and twos, not a body kept and drilled as a war band. I ask that they be ruled ordinary and not counted against the three hundred and sixty. My reason: the Keeper's own measure is a force kept as a body. A man who stands a ford and takes a toll is not kept as a body. If the Keeper reads them otherwise, I take the three hundred and sixty as the whole and drop the ask, and the toll watch stays as it is, a trade.

### The two tempers
Accepted. Neither priced, neither an edge, both able to fail.

### Their magic
Accepted as written: the salt wives, fitted as the world fits them, her own cost the whole price, no part of my powers, and no more made by my telling. I do not argue that twelve is fewer than the world would give. I did not write it to be a number, I wrote it to be a few, and I take what the world gives.

### Names
Accepted. The name of the people, and the names of the two office holders, are written as given, in my words, and whether the mortals I named are as I wrote is theirs to decide.
