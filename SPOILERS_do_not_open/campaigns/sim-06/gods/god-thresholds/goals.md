# Goals: god-thresholds

Year 2. In the order I would defend it. I have one year behind me among them, and I know now what I did not.

1. Keep the winter honest. We lasted to the thaw, but the Steward's count was kind by about a fortnight, and I found it out in the fourth month, when it was late to mend. He says it was not a lie. I do not need to settle what it was. I need two counts, his and one made by other hands, before the snow, and I need to say to him, to his face, that a short true count is better to me than a kind one. A hungry winter is still the thing that would undo me, and the captains took the sharing badly and said nothing, which is not the same as taking it well.
2. Owe the dead their reckoning. A herder died of wolves in the second month while I was in the city, with the dogs late and the fold broken. I did not ask whose watch it was, or who keeps his household now. A host that says it keeps the sill and does not ask is a host in name. I mean to ask, and to do what the sill law does for a household that lost a man in the keeping of the host.
3. Mend with Kessan without giving up what I know. He did not like being read, and he sat at the foot of the table at the first snow. I will not read him again unless he asks, or crosses the sill with a hand that frightens me. I need his spears and his road and his word to the captains. I also need to know, without his telling me, how thin the tolls have gone and why.
4. Do not let Idris Veld's sense be the only sense. A passed-over senior Doorwarden said to the fire that the sense will run out. I read that as grief and as a warning, and I do not know which is larger. I mean to hear him in person, and to hear Idris's mind on him, before I choose again whom I teach. I hold three lesser marks, which is all I hold; a fourth would release the oldest, so I will not lay one this year.
5. Sit at the low quarter's tables. The Mother of the Hearth asked it of me in the fourth month and I gave her no answer. The answer is yes, on terms I will tell her plainly: I eat there, and at a table where I have eaten I cannot speak a lie until I rise, so what I say there is true or it is silence.
6. Marda Sill. Idris said it to me in private in the fifth month, when I asked my one question, and I have carried it since. I do not know if it is a place, a person, a rite, or a word she says to quiet a woman who asks too much. I do not know if it is mine. I fear wanting it to be, and a wish is a poor witness. I mean to ask Idris once more, only what it is and whose, not whether it is mine, and to hold her answer as her answer and nothing more.
7. Hold the doors I hold and add few. I hold four in full: Marle Ford, the hall door, the city gate, Stair Pass. I hold three lesser marks. I want Far Notch known to me as Stair Pass is, since the road from the river kingdom comes by both, and I want two of the seven left free. The salt bag is small and I do not know where more is; I will draw only what the Steward says is spare.
8. Find the others like me, still by standing in doorways and noticing. Nothing came in a year, and I do not take that as a sign of anything. I do not know how many there are or whether I am early or late.
9. Keep the oath and my hand. I named one guest this year and broke nothing, and no door was held against a traveler. I am impulsive and afraid, and a year without a slammed door is not a promise about the next one.
