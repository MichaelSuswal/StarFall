# Goals: god-thresholds

Year 3. In the order I would defend it. I have two years behind me among them, and I know more of what I do not know.

1. Keep the winter honest, and owe the Steward a straight word. The stores lasted to the thaw because I ordered sharing by hearth at the first snow, and the captains took it badly and said nothing. The two counts differed by about eighteen days and he explained most of them. I need two counts again before the snow, his and the hearth mothers', and I need to tell him to his face that I do not call him a liar and that a short true count is what I ask. A hungry winter is still the thing that would undo me.
2. Pay what I put on my own account. I entered a dead man's household winter keep on my cord and the Steward does not know how it is paid. I am a guest with no coin. The offerings brought to the winter hall and the rite fires are given to me, in bread, salt, grain and milk, and I mean the keep to be paid from them and said so plainly, so that no one pays what I owe by guessing.
3. Know why the tolls are thin. The cords are near half of the old cords for a generation back. The ford and the two nearest houses are called poor, the two farthest rich, and what I saw at the farthest were new roofs, good horses and full smokehouses over cords that agreed with every other tally. Something is wrong or something is true that I have not been told. I hold both. I know who crosses the two passes by the blessing; I will set that against the cords before I say more.
4. Mend with Kessan without giving up what I know. He sits at my right. The Doorwardens took it as a slight and I mean to answer them without moving him. I will not read him again unless he asks, or crosses the sill with a hand that frightens me. I need his spears and his road and his word to the captains. He has never counted the far toll houses, and the Marshal went quiet when I walked them. I want the Marshal and Kessan to find the thing together and not through me.
5. Do not let Idris Veld's sense be the only sense. A senior Doorwarden of the lower columns came to the hall door and asked that the oath be taught to him or one of his, saying a rite held by four is held by four. I took his answer from him standing and have not yet given him one. A lesser mark can be laid only by one I have taught. I mean to decide this year and not let it sit. I hold three lesser marks, all I hold, and a fourth releases the oldest.
6. The cold and the blue flower. A herder at a rite fire asked whether the cold is coming down, since the flower was not on the old cutting. I did not answer. I do not know. I mean to go up and look, and to ask the oldest herders of the lower columns how the line of the grass has moved in their lives, and to say what I find and no more.
7. Marda Sill, held lightly. Idris says a person in the recital, the first to take the toll at the ford for the Sarrow, and a word in the oath's old form, as Marda held it; the high rite says a woman's name, the lower columns say it is older than the Sarrow's speech and that Sill may be what one is called who stands. I do not know whether it is mine, and a wish is a poor witness. I will ask at the lower columns' fires what the words mean to them, once each, and hold every answer as an answer.
8. Hold the doors I hold and add few. I hold five of seven in full: Marle Ford, the hall door, the city gate, Stair Pass and Far Notch, and three lesser marks. Two are free. I will lay no sixth this year without a reason I can say to the Doorwardens at the table. The salt bag is small and I draw only what the Steward says is spare.
9. Find the others like me, still by standing in doorways and noticing. Nothing came in two years. The star dimmed five times this year and I do not know what that means. I will not go looking beyond my road, since leaving my people to look is a worse risk than not finding.
10. Keep the oath and my hand. I named one guest the first year and broke nothing in either, and no door was held against a traveler. I am impulsive and afraid, and two years without a slammed door is not a promise about the third.
