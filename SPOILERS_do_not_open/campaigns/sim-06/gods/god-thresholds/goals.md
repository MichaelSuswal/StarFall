# Goals: god-thresholds

Year 4. In the order I would defend it. Three years among them. The winter took three hundred over the ordinary, and I was not able to stop it, and I hold that as mine.

1. Do not lose another winter. Stores failed some weeks short of the thaw for all the sharing, the last fortnight was eaten on dried meat, herd and seed, and the sowing was short. The hearth mothers' count was nearer the truth than the Steward's, twenty days against twelve. This year the seed is set apart and sealed before the first snow, the ration is set at the lower of the two counts from the first day, and grain is what the host asks of the trains before anything else. A short sowing twice is a people that does not stand.
2. Answer what I was asked and did not answer. The Steward asked what he is to do if the offerings fall short of the cord. The Mother of the Hearth asked whether the toll is the host's to raise or the sill's. The keeper of rite asked whether the river king's letter should be answered. Each asked in the open and each has waited a year. I answer all three aloud at the hall in the first days, and I do it with Kessan beside me and having heard it from me first, alone.
3. Hold Kessan and the sill together. He raised the toll by half without asking my leave. I have not read him since the first year and I will not, unless he asks or comes across the sill with a hand that frightens me. He is afraid he will not be ready, and a man afraid acts alone. I want the toll set in the hall by him, Idris and me, and not by one mouth, and I want him to keep his road and his spears. My hand is quick and I am afraid too; I must weigh before I answer him, not after.
4. Know why the tolls are thin. The cords of every house agree with one another and with the trains that crossed, near enough, and the toll laid up is half what the old cords give for as many. So the trains are paying less per train than they did, or something in the old reckoning is no longer counted. I do not know which. I ask the oldest watcher at the ford what the old toll was reckoned on, by the beast, the load or the head. The far houses were rich in roofs and horses and each had an account of a good year's barter that the captain could not break. A count of who goes into those houses by their own doors, not along the pass, would tell me something the cords cannot.
5. Meet the river king's claim face to face, if he will come. A claim to a yearly share of the ford and the passes, as heir of the old power whose wardens kept the road, in a measure no one at the hall uses. Kessan answered that the Sarrow owe no share to a king they have not met. Idris said the sill hears a claim made in person. I stand with both: I send word that he or one he trusts may come to the sill and be heard, as a guest, and that nothing is owed or refused until then. I do not know what the old power was. A claim is a witness, not a proof.
6. The cold and the grass. The oldest herders say the line of the grass has come down a bowshot in their lives, and I saw the cutting and no flower. I told the herder so. I do not know what it means, and I will not say it is the cold coming down. I mean to have the line marked each year at the thaw and again at the first snow, so that it is a count and not a memory, and to set what the herds need against what the grass gives before I am asked.
7. Marda Sill, held lightly. I asked at each fire what as Marda held it means and hold each answer as that fire's. I have no answer I trust beyond the fire it came from. I will ask once more at the fires I have not yet sat at, and then leave it. A wish is a poor witness, and the smooth place where my name should be is not evidence of anything.
8. Hold the doors I hold and add one with a reason said aloud. I hold five of seven in full: Marle Ford, the hall door, the city gate, Stair Pass and Far Notch, and three lesser marks. Two are free. I will lay a sixth only on the door of the farthest toll house, only with the Marshal beside me, and only after I have told Idris why. The salt bag is small and I draw only what the Steward says is spare.
9. Find the others like me by standing in doorways and noticing. Nothing came in three years. The star dimmed six times this year, more each year, and I do not know whose it is. I will not leave my people to search for them, since a people left in a hungry winter is a worse loss than a god not found.
10. Keep the oath and my hand. I have named one guest and broken nothing, and no door was held against a traveler. I am impulsive and afraid, and three years without a slammed door is not a promise about the fourth.
