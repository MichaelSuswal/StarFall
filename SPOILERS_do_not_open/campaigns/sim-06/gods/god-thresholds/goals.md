# Goals: god-thresholds

Year 5. In the order I would defend it. Four years among them. The second winter lost a hundred and fifty over the ordinary and the third was thinner than the second, and the people said the names aloud and I stood through it. I hold the dead as mine.

1. Do not lose this winter, and the salt is what stands in the way. Salt is short at the turn of the year. Without it the herd cannot be put by, and the cut ration already holds. The lower of two counts, the seed under two marks, and grain first at the toll are what worked, and I keep them. To them I add salt: it is the first thing the hall asks of any train, the toll is waived on a train that carries it, and the Steward spends what there is in the order I set, meat for the winter first. The people are told the truth of it by the Mother, in the open.
2. Answer what I was asked, and never again leave it a year, or a summer. Kessan asked to send riders, the Steward asked what to do for salt, the Mother asked whether to tell the households. All three waited because I was on the high grass beyond a rider's reach. I answer all three in the first days. And before I am away I leave standing answers with named office holders, so that a question put to me while I am up the mountain is not a question left to rot.
3. Know why the river trains stopped. Kessan's riders go beyond the houses, as guests, to learn it, and to ask what is said of the star there. The toll was lowered and still no train of the river kingdom came for three months while other peoples' trains came. The Marshal reads it as a king who has watched the ford. That is the Marshal's reading and I hold it as one. The match with the star's dimmings in those three months I do not trust.
4. Learn what goes in at the farthest toll house door. Since the sixth blessing I know that on a train day more bodies carry in at that door than the house has hands. The keeper said his cord had nothing in it the knots did not say, and the cord may be true, and a true cord may still be short of the trade. I will see it with my own eyes, on a train day, by the Step, and say so to Idris, Kessan and the Marshal before I go and to the keeper's face when I stand in his door. I do not break that door whatever is behind it. What I find goes to the hall table first, to Kessan, Idris and the Marshal together, and is judged at the sill.
5. Hold Kessan and the sill together. The toll was reset in the hall by the three of us in the eighth month and half the raise given back, and that was done at one table and not by one mouth. I keep it so. I do not read him unless he asks or crosses the sill with a hand that frightens me. His riders are his, and I hold him to the terms I set.
6. The grass. The line stood where the oldest say it stood last year, no higher, and the flower did not come. The herders say a stone cut to count a line does not hold it and the households do not hear them. I will have the herders heard at the hall by the Mother and the keepers of rite, so that what they say is not left at the cairns. I keep the two notches a year. I do not say what it means.
7. The river king's claim, kept open. My word went and nothing came. The riders carry a second word in my own words. If a king or one he trusts comes to the sill, he is met as a guest and heard. I stand with Kessan's answer and Idris's: nothing is owed or refused until the claim is heard face to face.
8. Marda Sill, held lightly. I asked at the fires and hold each answer as that fire's. I do not go looking this year. A wish is a poor witness and the smooth place where my name should be is not evidence of anything.
9. Hold the doors I hold. I hold six of seven in full: Marle Ford, the hall door, the city gate, Stair Pass, Far Notch and the farthest toll house door, and three lesser marks. One is free and I lay nothing on it this year. The salt is short, and a handful for a blessing is a handful the hall needs.
10. Find the others like me by standing in doorways and noticing, and by what the riders bring. The star dimmed seven nights, the most yet, and I do not know whose it is. I will not leave my people in a hungry winter to search.
11. Keep the oath and my hand. I have named one guest and broken nothing, and no door was held against a traveler. I am impulsive and afraid, and four years without a slammed door is not a promise about the fifth. The door I am most likely to break is the one I am about to look behind, and I have said above that I will not.
