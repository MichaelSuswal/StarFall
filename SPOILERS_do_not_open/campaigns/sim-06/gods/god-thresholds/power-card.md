# Power card: god-thresholds (no name held)

Bynames: "Guest", "Mother of the Door" (what it answers to).

Confirmed on Night 2, 2026-11-24 (Pacific), by the god's round 2 reply.

## Form

One body, as every god has in this campaign.

A human woman seeming past her middle years, broad in the shoulder, lined at the mouth and eyes, grey eyes, hair gone iron grey and braided up and pinned. Her wrongness is one thing only: she is too tall, near eight feet, and she stoops by habit under every lintel and bough, so that she is always seen bending toward a doorway. Hands large and warm. Human senses, no better. She walks a long unhurried stride at the pace of a tall woman.

What breaks it: Ordinary harm: blade, fire, crushing, drowning, a fall. A body that is wounded enough is broken. She can be bound with rope, chain or iron, caged, or walled in a room that is barred from outside, and she cannot step out of a held place by any means but force and time. She can be shut out: a ground whose keepers refuse her and bar the way, if she will not break it, stays closed to her. She can be scattered by a body broken beyond gathering.

God-sense: the god does not feel another god from far off. Only at close quarters, in the same room, across a doorway, within a few paces, does it feel the other as a weight in the air, and over a moment the weight settles into knowing that this is one like itself. It tells nothing more: not who the other is, and not what it can do.

When the body is broken: see the power The Return Home.

Keeper trait: At a table where you have eaten the host's food you cannot speak a lie, until you rise from it. Its reach: your own spoken words at that table only; you may keep silent, and it binds no one else, reads no one, and touches none of your powers or their prices.

### Definitions

- A blessed threshold: one she has blessed in full with the key, the salt and the oath; she holds seven at most.
- A lesser mark: a mark laid by one of her priesthood at her order; she holds three at most, apart from the seven.
- A guest: one she has named guest by speaking the oath over them.
- A day's walk: twenty miles.

### Things carried

- A small leather bag of coarse salt, spent by The Latch.
- A travelling cloak and a staff of ash as tall as herself.
- The key of dark iron on a leather cord is not ordinary: it is the instrument of The Latch and is written under Weaknesses taken.

## Powers

### The Latch
Status: main
Description: With the key in her hand, the god blesses a threshold by standing in it, laying salt on the sill and speaking the guest oath over it: a door, a gate, a tent flap, a ford or a pass, any one crossing a body goes through. She holds at most seven; an eighth releases the oldest. While a threshold is blessed she knows, wherever she is, when anyone crosses it and how many, and who if she has seen their face; nothing of what is said or done there. By a word spoken to the key she may shut a blessed threshold that has a frame and a leaf (a door, a gate, a flap): it opens to no hand, ram or tool until she lifts it or its frame is broken, and it admits anyone she has named guest by speaking the oath over them.
Drawback: The guest's law on herself: she may not cross any threshold she has not blessed unless one who keeps it bids her in. A door she has broken is still a threshold.
Limits settled at creation:
- A ford or a pass can be blessed and watched, never shut: it has no frame to hold.
- A gate set in a pass, with a frame and a leaf, is a door and may be shut.
- A ford is blessed for a spear's length either side of where she stood.
- A door moved elsewhere is not the threshold; a frame rebuilt in place is.
- Each blessing uses a handful of salt, which is spent.
- One who crosses is a body to her unless she has seen the face; she does not know a god from a mortal by a crossing.
- A host's death leaves the mark.
- Guests may be named in a body only when they stand before her.
- The oath: a guest she has named, and anyone who has eaten under a blessed roof with her leave, she may not harm under that roof or within a day's walk after; if she does, that threshold shuts against her and every other goes dark for a season.
- It is a condition of the power and buys nothing.
- A mind: none.
- A scarcity: it spends salt.
- More than ordinary craft: a door made or held by more than craft is unruled.
- Use across a year: bounded by the seven.
- Not applicable: light and dark, falling, water, being watched.
- "One who keeps it bids her in": any person who keeps that door in fact, host, steward or doorkeeper, by word or by a plain gesture of welcome; a keeper's refusal is a refusal.
- Standing in a threshold to bless it is not a crossing. She may so stand only where one who keeps the threshold bids her to the sill, where the threshold is of a place her people hold, or where it has no keeper at all (a ford, a pass, an empty house).
- First wording, kept as intent: "a door she has blessed is a door that keeps the oath for her."
Rulings:
Trades: its instrument, the key, is the weakness that bought the lesser marks (see Weaknesses taken).
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Step
Status: main
Description: From inside a threshold she holds blessed in full, the god steps through and arrives in the same moment in another she holds blessed in full, with what she holds and wears, and with one person who holds her hand and has been named guest.
Drawback: The door she left stands open for a night: nothing of the oath holds there, it will not shut, and it tells her nothing of who crossed. And she is the guest of the door she arrived in: until the dawn after a full day she may not go beyond sight of it, nor shut or lift it.
Limits settled at creation:
- Not to a threshold she has not blessed, one gone dark, a broken one, or a lesser mark.
- Not while bound or held, nor without the key in hand.
- If the arrival is blocked she arrives within a pace of it.
- Other gods as the one led: unruled.
- Use across a year: bounded by the drawback.
- A mind: none.
- Not applicable: light and dark, falling, water, being watched, a scarcity.
- "Beyond sight": she must be where her own eyes can see the door itself, at that hour, inside the house, around it or out from it.
- "The dawn after a full day": the first dawn at least a full day after the step.
- The limit is on her alone, not on the one she led.
- As often as she will pay it.
- First wording, kept as intent: "it is the door that moves her."
Rulings:
Trades: none of its own; it stands on The Latch and falls with the key.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Breaker's Hand
Status: main
Description: The god lays a hand on a thing held shut (a bar, a lock, a gate, the wall of a house, a seal, a weir, a chain) and it gives where she touched: wood splits, iron parts, mortar fails. One stroke does what a very strong person with a ram could do in a day, and no more. It does nothing to a living body or to a thing that is not shut.
Drawback: The breaking unbars her: for a day after, none of her blessed thresholds will shut.
Limits settled at creation:
- She can mend nothing she has broken; a blessed threshold she breaks loses its blessing for good, and her people may not mend it at her order for a season.
- That is the god's nature, kept written, and is no price.
- Broken only where she touched.
- A thing broken by another's hand with something she gave them is not her breaking.
- A thing sealed or held by another god or by more than craft: unruled.
- Being watched: it is seen.
- A mind: none.
- Not applicable: light and dark, distance, falling, water, a scarcity.
- "Held shut" takes in a threshold a keeper has barred against a refused guest, a door held against a traveler and the seal of a house; not a door a living body leans on.
- First wording, kept as intent: "when a door will not open for her she needs it to."
Rulings:
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Reading of the Host
Status: main
Description: Of one person seated across a table or standing across a threshold from her, the god may know one thing, yes or no: does this one mean harm to a guest at this table or this crossing. Not why, and not to whom. It holds for the one meal or the one crossing.
Drawback: Whoever she reads learns that she has read them, and learns of her the same one answer: whether she means harm to a guest at this table or this crossing.
Limits settled at creation:
- One person at a time.
- Not one who is asleep or insensible, not a beast, not a god.
- It reads the meaning to harm at this meeting, never the mind beyond it, and compels no one.
- The god's own people: read as anyone.
- Distance: across a table or a threshold.
- Use across a year: no limit; each reading shows her.
- Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water, being watched, a scarcity, more than ordinary craft.
- The drawback was chosen by the god from a pair; the other, never reading her own people, was declined.
- First wording, kept as intent: "to know before the bread is broken."
Rulings:
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Return Home
Status: main (the return)
Description: When the god's body is broken, she returns whole after a month at the nearest ground of her kind: the nearest standing doorway of a house where people live, or gate of a wall. What she carries comes back with her, the key too if it was on her.
Drawback: Two items of memory or habit at each return. The item is taken by a logged roll from the god's own record, and the god is not told which.
Limits settled at creation:
- The standing scale's month rung; the faster return costs more.
- The month runs from the breaking.
- A body held is not broken.
- The walk is added.
- The doorway is the nearest to where the body broke, and she arrives in the threshold itself.
- If another holds the key when the body breaks, it stays where it is, and she comes back without The Latch or The Step until she holds it.
Rulings:
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

## Weaknesses taken

### The key as the instrument
The Latch comes only through the key of dark iron, and The Step, which stands on it, goes with it. If the key is out of her hand and her person and held or kept by another, both are gone until she holds it again: the marks stand, dark to her, and she can neither shut, lift, hear nor step. In any other hand it is only an iron key that fits no lock, and no one else gains the power. A copy is a key. In her own keeping out of arm's reach it is not taken. If it is destroyed, both are gone until her next return, when it comes back with the body. If another holds it when the body breaks, it stays where it is. Anyone who learns this can take her doors by taking the key.
It bought: the lesser marks. One of her priesthood who has taken the oath from her in person may, at her order, lay a lesser mark on a doorway of a place her people hold, by salt, the oath and the rite. A lesser mark is known to her as any is; it cannot be shut and cannot be stepped to. Lesser marks have a count of their own, three, apart from the seven she holds in full; a fourth releases the oldest lesser mark. A lesser mark stays, and stays known to her, if the place is later lost to her people. If the key is taken, lesser marks go dark with the rest.

## Trades
Offered once, on Night 2. Taken: the key as the instrument, for the lesser marks. Trades are closed for the rest of creation.
