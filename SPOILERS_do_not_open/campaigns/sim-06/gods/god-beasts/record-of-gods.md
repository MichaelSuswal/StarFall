# Record of other gods: The Reeve of the Low Ground

## Others like me
I have met none. In two years on the ground I felt no other like myself, not at the wood's edge, not at the rear camp, not at the river, not at the margin of the three clans, not in either winter camp. That knowing comes only within a few paces, so it tells me only that none stood that near me in all the places I stood. It tells me nothing of the rest of the country.

I hold no trust, no grievance, no promise and no debt toward any of them. None has sent me word, and I have sent none.

## What I hold as guess, and may be wrong in
That I am not alone in having come down. I have no ground for more than the feeling I woke with.

The star dimmed on five nights this year, where it dimmed on three the year before: in the second, sixth, ninth, eleventh and twelfth months. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. My people say they are the keeper's. I did nothing at those hours that I know of. The count has risen from three to five, and I mark that it rises, and no more. I may guess that it is the doing of one of my kind, or of the sky itself, or of something I have no word for. If it is a god, it is one who does the same thing more often as the years go, and it is not me. I do not know. I will not say to the council that it is mine, and I will not say that it is not any god's. I will say only that I know nothing of it.

The cutters of the stone towns work deeper in the winter woods each month, with draught beasts, unafraid. I hold it as a guess only, and a weak one, that a people so patient and so counting may have a god behind it. I have felt no weight near them, for I have not gone within a mile of them, and the weight comes only close. I have no ground to name one.

## How I mean to keep this record
Only what I have met, heard or been told in my own presence goes here. A promise from another of my kind is worth what I judge that one to be worth, and I will judge it after I have counted what that one does at the edge of the light. If any stranger stands within a few paces of me and the weight settles, I will write what I saw of that one that day and no more.
