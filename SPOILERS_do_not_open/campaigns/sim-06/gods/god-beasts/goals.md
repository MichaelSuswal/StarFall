# Goals: The Reeve of the Low Ground

Year 5, at the turn of the year, at the margin of the band's camp, in spring. Four years at the edge. The Houndwarden has twice sent hunters on my reading of wolves at the calving by the council's leave, and lost about six calves where last year I saw thirty fall. The Speaker is slower, and the clans talk of who will follow him and have not said a name. The three clans that camp apart say the band should part at the next going down, and they say my not saying no is a yes. I do not ask a seat, a share or a title.

1. Keep what the council gave and ask nothing past it. The Houndwarden may send hunters on my reading of wolves at dusk, for the calving only. Be right when I read, say plainly when a reading is inference, and tell her at the first dusk and not the second. Do not ask to widen it. The eldest head says a thing given twice is a custom. I will not argue it to him or against him. If the council widens it of itself, take it.
2. Keep what the herder of the two clans asked of me. Read his herd at dusk each few nights through the winter and the spring, name the gatherings that read hurt in part, and say each time that I do not know with what. About forty died this winter, far fewer than the hundred before, and I take no credit that is not mine. His clan heard blame in my words; I did not mean it, and I will say so if asked.
3. Say sooner and say small when a herd is hurt at a pool. This year it worked: one dusk, said with how little one dusk is, and the camp was not moved for nothing. Do it again.
4. Answer the apart clans plainly and once. I did not say yes to their young herder. I said I do not know. If the question comes again, I say that, and I say what I want: the band many and various and unhurried, not parted for my sake or the star's. Whether it parts is the council's and the clans', and I will not press either way. If it parts, a body can keep one margin and not two, and I say that before it is asked.
5. Give the Marchwarden something he can use of the star. He has notched seven nights and finds no rule. I can stand still on a night when the star dims and read what the herds do under it, afraid or resting or hurt, and tell him. I cannot read the sky, and I say so. I take the reading as one more mark for his notches and not as an answer.
6. Watch the stone towns' cutters from beyond a mile. Count the gatherings and how deep in. Take no trail on a people who have done no wrong. Tell the Marchwarden at his fire, and say what the Listening does not give.
7. Hold my anger. A storm falls on innocent and guilty alike. Take no trail, sound no horn, unless the wrong is plain and the doer is not of the people I keep. Speak with no beast unless a herd is in danger and no other way serves. The cost of boldness is mine.
8. Learn what ruling costs by watching the council pay it. The Speaker is slower and no successor is named. I watch who the clans speak of, and I do not name anyone, and I do not favour anyone.
9. Say plainly that I know nothing of the dimming star, if I am asked. It dimmed seven times this year, twenty-one since I came, and the count rises. Do not take credit and do not deny what I cannot know.
10. Find what answers the verdict not given at the beginning of me. I have seen nothing yet that looks like it. I will know it when I see it.
11. Keep a way out of every room, and keep the horn on me and out of every other hand.
