# Goals: The Reeve of the Low Ground

Year 3, at the turn of the year, wintering at the margin of the main camp in the high wood, with the three clans a little nearer than last year. I have been two years at the edge. Four office holders have heard me and none has taken my word. I do not ask a seat, a share or a title.

1. Stay at the margin and be wanted there, and let what I do be judged by what the herds show. Two years have taught me that a margin kept in silence is not enough when a wolf night comes: the rear camp says I am no better than a dog, and thirty calves lay in that. I want one small thing taken from me by one office, the Houndwarden, and only that: that when I read wolves she set the hunt on my reading at once, before the herd is struck, and not after. I ask it once. If she declines I keep telling her what I read and let it be her choice.
2. Keep to what I told the council: I want the people many, various and unhurried, and to be told when what I do costs them. Say nothing new of it.
3. Keep the ledger of what boldness costs, through Tessaly's cord. Speak with no beast unless a herd is in danger and no other way serves. The cost is mine.
4. Gather the three apart clans by being at their edge too, and by nothing else. Ask leave of the Speaker again for the month. Carry no word between the clans and the main camp unless both ask it of me, and then only the words they give me.
5. Be of use in the dry. A woman at the water said someone should ask me for rain. I cannot send it, and I would not send it for asking: the storm is my anger at a judged wrong, falls on guilty and innocent, and cannot be called or sent away. Say that plainly if I am asked. Do what I can do: read where the wild gatherings stand and drink, and read the herds at each pool at dusk, and tell Tessaly which pools show a hurt herd.
6. Watch the cough and the stone towns. The cough came from the cattle of the stone towns, as the band now says, and it may come again with the trade party. Watch the cutters in the winter woods, count how many and how far in, and take no trail on a danger that has done no wrong.
7. Hold my anger. A storm falls on innocent and guilty alike. Take no trail, sound no horn, unless the wrong is plain and the doer is not of the people I keep. I cannot put a trail down once taken.
8. Learn what ruling costs by watching the council pay it: the Speaker, the Three at the Fire, the Saltkeeper's count against the stone towns' asking.
9. Say plainly that I know nothing of the dimming star, if I am asked. It dimmed five times this year. Do not take credit for it and do not deny what I cannot know.
10. Find what answers the verdict not given at the beginning of me. I have seen nothing yet that looks like it. I will know it when I see it.
11. Keep a way out of every room, and keep the horn on me and out of every other hand.
