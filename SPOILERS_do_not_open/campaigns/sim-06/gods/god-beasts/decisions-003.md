# Decisions: god-beasts, Year 3

I stand at the margin of the main camp in the high wood, at the end of winter, with a people who have heard me and not taken my word. Last year I read the wolves and they were not turned in time, and I will not do the same again without asking for the one thing that would change it. I ask little, I keep the margin, and I spend nothing but my own standing. The order below bends to what the Listening shows and to what the council asks.

1. Spring, at the first storms, at the dusk council of the main camp, before the band goes down the Herd Road. I come to the edge of the firelight and ask Tessaly to carry to the Speaker, and to say herself at council, this one request of the Houndwarden's office: that when I tell her I read wolves near the calving herds, she sets the hunters and dogs on my reading the same dusk, where I name and which way, without waiting to see them. My words: "Last year I told you where the wolves lay on three nights and the hunt waited to see them, and about thirty calves were lost. I ask no seat and no share. I ask that the Houndwarden may act on my word at once for this one thing, the wolves at the calving, and I ask that the council say whether she may. If it says no, I tell her what I read all the same and the choice stays hers." Reason: a margin kept in silence cost them thirty calves, and this is the one small thing that would change it. It is the first time I ask a thing to be taken, and I ask only for the calving.

2. Spring, down the Herd Road to the Farrow Grass with the band, through the calving. My body goes at the rear of the line with Tessaly's dogs and keeps the margin of the camp each night, timber or wind at my back. At each dusk and before light I stand still and read the herds and the flanks for wolves and for the state of each gathering. When I read wolves I go to Tessaly at once and tell her how many gatherings, where and which way. Through her I ask the herders to put apart any coughing beast, as the two clans have learned to. I do not touch the herds and I do not speak with any beast. Reason: the calving is when the herds are most at risk, and the herds sleeping better is what I have shown them that they can judge.

3. Summer, in the dry if it comes, and along the river and the summer grazing. I read the water at dusk. I stand still at the band's stopped camp and at each pool and stream the Marchwarden names, and I read the herds that come down to drink: which gatherings stand hurt after drinking, and which wild gatherings, deer and boar and the rest, keep to which water and have not left it. I tell Tessaly each dusk which pools show a hurt herd and which wild gatherings still drink where, and I ask her to ask the Marchwarden to weigh the songs of the fords against it, and to ask the band to leave a pool where the herds read hurt and children have fallen sick. If a woman or any one asks me for rain, I say through Tessaly that I cannot send it: the storm comes of my anger at a judged wrong, falls on guilty and innocent alike, and cannot be called or sent away, and I would not call it for want of water. I do not say that the rain came for me, and I do not deny what I cannot know. Reason: sixteen children died of the flux at fouled pools, and finding a clean pool is something I can do that costs no one.

4. Early summer, to the three clans that camp apart. I ask the Speaker, through Tessaly, for leave to keep the margin of their camp for a month again, on the same three conditions the eldest head set: no coming into their ring or camp, no word carried either way unless both ask it of me, gone at the month's end. I ask him to tell their head only that the keeper will stand at the edge of their fire as before. If the Speaker or the clans refuse, I do not go. If they allow it, I go by the wood's edge, come at dusk to their dogs first, and keep their night watch. If their head or the Speaker asks me to carry a word between them and the main camp, I carry exactly the words given me and no others, and tell Tessaly that I did. Reason: a people is not many and various while a part of it stands off, and the eldest head asked me last year whether I would carry word. I answered him and do not press it.

5. Late summer and autumn, along the edge of the band's ground and toward the winter woods. I go by night through the timber and read the country where the band will go in the going up. I look for the cutters of the stone towns and for any gathering with draught beasts among it: how many gatherings, how far in, and whether they have moved in since I last counted. I stay more than a mile from them. I do not take a trail, I do not speak with their beasts, and I do not approach them. I tell the Marchwarden what I read, at his fire, and I say plainly that the Listening past a mile gives gatherings and their state and not a number of people. I ask him to take it to the Speaker and to say that what the council wants of the cutters is the council's to say, and that I take no trail on a people who have done no wrong. Reason: the cutters have come deeper each month and are unafraid, and a neighbour who counts is a danger that has not yet done a wrong.

6. Autumn, when the trade party comes back from the stone towns. I ask Tessaly to carry to the Saltkeeper, who counts the party's goods, that I read the herds at dusk and that I ask the party's own beasts and any bought beast be kept apart on the downwind end for a month before they join the herds, as the two clans' herds were kept last winter. The cough came with the stone towns' oxen, as the band says. I say nothing of trade, salt or iron, which I know nothing of. I stand with the hunt at its edge if the council asks and I say where the game lies and ask that its season be kept: no doe with young, and no beast for its hide alone. I set no share on the flat stone for myself. Reason: five beasts died and the cough did not spread because they were found early, and a month apart is cheap against a herd.

7. Winter, the going up to the high wood. I go up with the band, keep the margin of the winter camp, and listen at dusk over the herds for the cough and over the winter woods for the cutters. I watch what the Speaker carries, what the Three at the Fire judge, what the council will not hear, and what the Saltkeeper's count says against the stone towns' asking. At the year's end I stand at the edge of the firelight with the horn at my side and a way out behind me.

## Messages
None. Everything I say goes by Tessaly's ear in person at the margin, or by the Marchwarden's fire when I tell him what I read. I send no word by messenger or horn.

## What my people are instructed to do
I instruct no one. No office holder has taken my word. I ask, through Tessaly: that the Houndwarden may act on my reading of wolves at the calving the same dusk, if the council says she may; that the herders put apart any coughing beast; that the Marchwarden weigh the songs of the fords against the pools I name; that the Speaker give or refuse my leave for the apart clans' margin; and that the Saltkeeper keep the trade party's beasts apart for a month. Anything the council declines I leave as it is.

## Asked of me and not answered
Nothing was asked of me last year that I did not answer.

## For the Keeper to rule
1. I use the Listening as the card states. In action 3 I read where wild gatherings stand and where a herd reads hurt in part after drinking. I do not claim to read water, a pool's foulness or what ails a beast. I infer water only from where gatherings keep to over several dusks, and I tell Tessaly it is inference. I ask the Keeper to rule only whether a gathering at a pool reads to me as drinking or only as grazing or resting. I do not count on it.
2. I do not sound the horn, take a trail, call a storm, or speak with any beast this year, unless a herd is in danger and no other way serves, and then one beast in few breaths, which I tell Tessaly the same day. I try to grow no power this year.
