# Decisions: god-beasts, Year 2

I stand at the margin of the main camp in the high wood, at the end of winter. I have a people who know my name for what I am and have not taken my word. I answer what I was asked, I keep the margin, and I do little else that costs them. The order below bends to what the Listening shows and to what the council asks.

1. Spring, at the first storms, at the dusk council of the main camp. Before I go down with the band, I come to the edge of the firelight and ask Tessaly to carry to the Speaker, Orrin Haddock, my answer to the eldest head of the three clans, who said before me and not to me that a keeper that asks nothing will want something later. My words are: "It is true, and I will say what I want once. I want this band many and unhurried, and I want to be told when what I do costs it. I ask no share, no seat and no rite. The council may keep me at the edge or send me from it." I say it from the margin, in my low even voice, and I wait. I do not press for an answer. Reason: a thing left unanswered becomes a suspicion, and I would rather be known to want something small than be thought to want something large.

2. Spring, down the Herd Road to the Farrow Grass with the band, and through the calving. My body goes at the rear of the line with Tessaly's dogs, at the margin of the camp each night, with the timber or the wind behind me. Each dusk and again before light I stand still and listen over the calving herds and the flanks for wolves, and for the state of each gathering: hurt, afraid, resting. I break the listening with a breath of effort before I walk back to the fire. When I read wolves, I go to Tessaly and tell her where and which way, and she and the hunters turn them as last year. Through her I ask the herders to keep apart at once any beast that coughs, as the two clans have learned to. I tell Tessaly what I read of the herds' state at each dusk, and she tells the herders. I do not touch the herds myself. Reason: the calving is when the herds are most at risk, and the herds sleeping better is the one thing I have shown them that they can judge.

3. Spring and early summer, to the three clans that camp apart. I ask the Speaker, through Tessaly, for leave to keep the margin of the apart clans' camp for a month, as I keep the main camp's, and I ask him to tell their head only that a keeper will stand at the edge of their fire and ask nothing. If the Speaker or the clans refuse, I do not go, and I keep the main camp only. If they allow it, I go by the wood's edge and not by the Herd Road, I come at dusk to their dogs first, I keep their night watch on their flock as I keep the main band's, and I say to their head what I said to the council: that I ask nothing. I do not carry any word between the clans on my own and I do not speak of the quarrel over fuel wood. Reason: a people is not many and various while a part of it stands off, and I will not draw them in by anything but being at their edge too.

4. Summer, at the stopped camp on the river. I give the council my ledger as Tessaly's cord has it: each bold beast, where, and what it took. I do not speak with any beast this year, with one exception named here. If a herd is in danger and no dog or hunter can serve, I may speak with a single beast, in few breaths, and I tell Tessaly the same day. I ask Tessaly to read me the cord, and I answer to the council for it. If the council asks whether the dimming of the star is mine, I say that I know nothing of it, that I did nothing at those hours that I know of, and that I do not say it is not mine because I cannot say what I do not know. Reason: the cost of boldness is mine, and the star is a matter on which I am only as sure as I am.

5. Summer and autumn, along the edge of the band's ground and toward the winter woods. I go in the long fattening season by the timber, at night, along the band's range toward the winter woods, and I read the country by the Listening where the band will go in the going up. I look for the cutters of the stone towns, how many, how far in, and what they take. I do not approach them, I do not take a trail, and I do not speak with their dogs or beasts. I tell the Marchwarden what I count, at his fire, and I ask him whether the songs say the cutters came so far in before. I tell the Saltkeeper nothing of trade, since I know nothing of it. Reason: a neighbour who is patient and counts is a danger that has not yet done a wrong, and I take no trail on a danger.

6. Autumn, the hunt in the timber. If the council asks again, I stand with the hunters at the hunt's edge and say where the game lies, and I ask the hunt to keep its season: no doe with young and no beast for its hide alone. If the council does not ask, I do not offer and I keep the margin of the camp. I set no share on the flat stone for myself. Reason: they asked last year and I served. I will not take the asking for granted.

7. Winter, the going up to the high wood. I go up with the band, I keep the margin of the winter camp, and I listen at dusk over the herds for the cough and over the winter woods for the cutters. I watch who counts and who rules: what the Speaker carries, what the Three at the Fire judge, what the council will not hear. At the end of the year I am at the edge of the firelight, with the horn at my side and a way out behind me.

## Messages
None. Anything I say goes by Tessaly's ear in person at the margin, and I send no word by messenger or horn.

## What my people are instructed to do
I instruct no one. I ask, through Tessaly, that the Houndwarden keep the cord count of bold beasts, that the herders put apart any coughing beast at once, and that the Speaker consider my answer to the eldest head and my leave to keep the margin of the apart clans. No office holder has taken my word. No one acts for me.

## For the Keeper to rule
1. I use the Listening as the card states, and I ask only whether reading a gathering's state as hurt, afraid or resting lets me tell the herds that cough from the herds that do not, or whether I only read the beast as hurt. I do not count on it. If the Keeper rules it reads no more than the card says, I tell Tessaly only what it shows.
2. I do not sound the horn, take a trail, call a storm, or speak with beasts beyond the one exception in action 4. I try to grow no power this year.
