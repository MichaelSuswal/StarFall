# Decisions: god-beasts, Year 5

I stand at the margin of the band's camp at the end of winter. The council took none of my words this year and gave me none, and the Houndwarden acted on my reading of the wolves on the leave she was given, and the loss was six calves and not thirty. The star dimmed seven times. The three clans that camp apart ask whether it is the band's size, and take my not saying no for a yes. I keep to the leave I hold, I answer what I can answer plainly, and I let the council and the clans decide what is theirs. The order below bends to what the Listening shows and to what the council asks.

1. Spring, at the first storms, at the dusk council of the main camp, before the band goes down the Herd Road. I come to the edge of the firelight and ask Tessaly to carry to the Speaker, and to the young herder of the apart clans if he is at the council or comes to the margin, my answer to what the apart clans take from my silence. My words: "I did not say yes to the question whether the star dims because the band is too many. I said I know nothing of it, and that is all I said. I do not say it is not any god's, because I cannot say what I do not know. If the band parts, it parts by the council's word and the clans' own, and not for my sake or the star's. I want this band many and unhurried, as I told the council in the second year, and I ask no more than that. A body keeps one margin and not two. I say it now so that no one thinks a part is left unkept by my choosing." I wait and do not press for an answer. Reason: a thing left unanswered becomes a suspicion, the clans have already made one of mine, and the true answer is small.

2. Spring, down the Herd Road to the Farrow Grass with the band, through the calving. My body goes at the rear of the line with Tessaly's dogs and keeps the margin of the main camp each night, timber or wind at my back. At each dusk and before light I stand still and read the herds and the flanks for wolves and for the state of each gathering. When I read wolves, at the first dusk I read them and not the second, I go to Tessaly and tell her how many gatherings, where and which way, and she sends hunters and dogs the same dusk by the council's leave. I tell her too which herds read hurt in part. I add that the herders drew the calves to the pens at the dusk I spoke and not after, and that the herders told me they should have done it sooner, and I ask Tessaly to ask them to pen at the dusk I speak. I do not ask the council to widen the leave. I touch no herd and speak with no beast. Reason: six calves were lost where thirty were lost before, and the pens at the first dusk are the part of the gap that is the herders' own.

3. Spring through winter, at the herd of the two clans' herder, when he asks, each few nights at dusk. As I did from the tenth month of last year, I stand still at his herd's edge, read which of his gatherings read hurt in part, and tell him by Tessaly or at his fire which they are, and that I do not know with what or which beasts. I say the same to him in the same words each time. If his clan asks whether my words blame them for wintering apart, I say that the cough came hard to them and no other herd took it, that I do not say it is their fault, and that I do not know why it chose them. Reason: forty died this winter, and finding the sick gatherings at dusk is the one thing I can give that costs no one.

4. Summer, along the river and the summer grazing, at each pool and ford the Marchwarden names. I stand still at dusk and read the herds that come down. When a herd reads hurt the first dusk, I say it at once to Tessaly, with how little one dusk is, and I tell her that whether it drank and anything about the water is my inference and not what the Listening gives. I ask her to ask the Marchwarden whether to keep the herd off the pool a night and not move the camp, as he did this year. On any night the star dims, if I am at the margin, I stand still at the dusk before and again after midnight and read what the herds do under it: whether they read afraid, resting or hurt, and whether the wild gatherings at the edge of the ground stand or move. I tell the Marchwarden at his fire what I read, for his notches, and I tell him I cannot read the sky, only the beasts under it, and that I do not take what I read as an answer to why it dims. Reason: the Marchwarden has notched seven nights and cannot find a rule, and a reading of the beasts under the star is one more mark that costs no one and claims nothing.

5. Late summer, by night through the timber along the edge of the band's ground toward the winter woods. I read where the band will go in the going up and where the cutters of the stone towns work: how many gatherings with draught beasts, how far in, and whether they have moved in since I last counted. I stay more than a mile from them. I take no trail, speak with none of their beasts and do not approach them. I tell the Marchwarden at his fire what I read, and say again that past a mile the Listening gives gatherings and their state and not a number of people. I ask him to take it to the Speaker, and say that what the council wants of the cutters is the council's to say, and that I take no trail on a people who have done no wrong. Reason: they came a little deeper again, and a neighbour who counts is a danger that has not yet done a wrong.

6. Autumn, when the trade party comes back from the stone towns, and the hunt in the timber. I ask Tessaly to carry to the Saltkeeper that the party's beasts and any bought beast be kept apart on the downwind end until a dusk's reading of them shows no hurt in part, as six weeks served this year, and that the two clans' cattle again winter on their own ground apart. I read the party's beasts at the end of the apart time if the Saltkeeper asks, and I say what I read and what I do not. I say nothing of trade, salt or iron, which I know nothing of. If the council asks me to stand at the hunt's edge, I say where the game lies and ask that the season be kept: no doe with young, and no beast for its hide alone. I set no share on the flat stone for myself. Reason: the towns' oxen still cough, and keeping their beasts apart six weeks was cheap against a herd.

7. Winter, the going up to the high wood, or the parting if the council and the clans choose it. If the band goes up whole, I go up with it, keep the margin of the winter camp, and listen at dusk over the herds for the cough and over the winter woods for the cutters. If the band parts, I keep the margin of the main camp, where the one leave I hold lies, and I do not go to the apart clans' camp unless their head asks me, and I say so before it comes. I watch the Speaker and who the clans speak of to follow him, and I name no one and favour no one. If I am asked about the dimming star, I say again that I know nothing of it, that I did nothing at those hours that I know of, and that I do not say it is not any god's. At the year's end I stand at the edge of the firelight with the horn at my side and a way out behind me. Reason: the margin is where I am wanted, and the winter is when the cough returns.

## Messages
None. Everything I say goes by Tessaly's ear in person at the margin, or by the Marchwarden's fire when I tell him what I read, or to the young herder of the apart clans to his face if he comes to the margin. I send no word by messenger or horn, and I carry no word between the main camp and the apart clans unless both ask it of me.

## What my people are instructed to do
I instruct no one. I ask, through Tessaly: that the Houndwarden send hunters and dogs on my reading of wolves at the first dusk I name, as the council allowed, and no further than it allowed; that the herders pen the calves at the dusk I speak and not after; that the Marchwarden weigh a hurt herd read at a pool against the songs of the fords and decide whether to keep a herd off it for a night or move the camp; that the Saltkeeper keep the trade party's beasts apart; and that the two clans keep their cattle apart. Anything the council declines I leave as it is. No office holder has taken my word beyond the calving, and no one acts for me.

## Asked of me and not answered
Nothing stands unanswered. The young herder of the apart clans asked twice whether the star dims because the band is too many, and I answered him the second time in the words I gave. His clan took the answer for a yes, and action 1 says plainly what I did say and did not say.

## For the Keeper to rule
Nothing. I use the Listening as the card and the rulings state: a gathering's place, which way it moves and its state, and a herd with sick beasts in it reads as hurt in part. In action 4 I read what the herds' states are on a night the star dims, which is the same reading at a different hour, and I do not claim it reads the sky. I do not sound the horn, take a trail, call a storm, or speak with any beast this year, unless a herd is in danger and no other way serves, and then one beast in few breaths, which I tell Tessaly the same day. I try to grow no power this year.
