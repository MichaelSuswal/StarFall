# Decisions: god-beasts, Year 4

I stand at the margin of the band's camp at the end of winter. The council has once taken my word, for the wolves at the calving, and it held. A hundred head of the two clans' cattle died of the cough, and I could not say what it was. I keep to the leave I was given, I answer what was asked of me, and I speak sooner this year when a herd reads hurt. The order below bends to what the Listening shows and to what the council asks.

1. Spring, at the first storms, at the dusk council of the main camp, before the band goes down the Herd Road. I come to the edge of the firelight and ask Tessaly to carry to the Speaker, and to the herder of the two clans who asked, my answer to the question put to me last year. My words: "You asked whether I can say what the cough is. I cannot. When I listen over a herd I know that it is hurt in part, and I do not know with what, or which beasts. I know that it came back hard in the two clans' cattle that wintered apart, and that no other herd took it. I can tell you at dusk which gatherings read hurt in part, and I will, and the choice of what to do with it is yours." I add nothing of its cause, since I do not know it. I do not ask for anything more of the council about the wolves. I keep to the leave as the Speaker gave it. Reason: a question left unanswered becomes a suspicion, and the true answer is small and I would rather give it than a guess.

2. Spring, down the Herd Road to the Farrow Grass with the band, through the calving. My body goes at the rear of the line with Tessaly's dogs and keeps the margin of the camp each night, timber or wind at my back. At each dusk and before light I stand still and read the herds and the flanks for wolves, and for the state of each gathering. When I read wolves I go to Tessaly at once and tell her how many gatherings, where and which way, and she sends hunters and dogs the same dusk by the council's leave. I tell her too which herds read hurt in part. I break the listening with a breath of effort before I go back to the fire. I touch no herd and speak with no beast. Reason: the calving is when the herds are most at risk, and the one thing the council allowed me is to be a reading she may act on, at dusk, in this season.

3. Summer, along the river and the summer grazing, at each pool and ford the Marchwarden names. I stand still at dusk and read the herds that come down. This year I say what I read the first dusk a herd reads hurt, not after a pair of dusks, and I say with it that one dusk is little and may be nothing. I tell it to Tessaly, and ask her to ask the Marchwarden whether the band should move off that pool, and I tell her that whether it drank or only grazed, and anything about the water, is my inference and not what the Listening gives. If anyone asks me for rain I say through Tessaly that I cannot send it: the storm comes of my anger at a judged wrong, falls on guilty and innocent alike, and cannot be called or sent away, and I would not call it for want of water. Reason: three children died of the flux at the pool below the second ford before I spoke, and the mothers said I should have said sooner. Sooner with a plain word of how unsure I am costs no one and may save a child.

4. Late summer, by night through the timber along the edge of the band's ground toward the winter woods. I read where the band will go in the going up, and where the cutters of the stone towns work: how many gatherings with draught beasts, how far in, and whether they have moved in since I last counted. I stay more than a mile from them. I take no trail, speak with none of their beasts and do not approach them. I tell the Marchwarden at his fire what I read, and say again that past a mile the Listening gives gatherings and their state and not a number of people. I ask him to take it to the Speaker, and say that what the council wants of the cutters is the council's to say, and that I take no trail on a people who have done no wrong. Reason: they came deeper again this year, and a neighbour who counts is a danger that has not yet done a wrong.

5. Autumn, when the trade party comes back from the stone towns, and the hunt in the timber. I ask Tessaly to carry to the Saltkeeper, who counts the party's goods, that the party's beasts and any bought beast be kept apart on the downwind end for longer than the month of last year, until a dusk's reading of them shows no hurt in part, and that the two clans' cattle, which took the cough, stay on their own ground apart from the other herds through the winter as they did. I say nothing of trade, salt or iron. If the council asks me to stand at the hunt's edge I say where the game lies and ask that the season be kept: no doe with young, and no beast for its hide alone. I set no share on the flat stone for myself. Reason: a hundred head died in the two clans' herds, and keeping the stone towns' beasts apart longer costs little against a herd.

6. Winter, the going up to the high wood. I go up with the band, keep the margin of the winter camp, and listen at dusk over the herds for the cough and over the winter woods for the cutters. I watch what the Speaker carries, what the Three at the Fire judge, what the council will not hear, and what the Saltkeeper's count says against the stone towns' asking. If I am asked about the dimming star, I say that I know nothing of it, that I did nothing at those hours that I know of, and that I do not say it is not any god's because I cannot say what I do not know. At the year's end I stand at the edge of the firelight with the horn at my side and a way out behind me. Reason: the margin is where I am wanted, and the winter is when the cough returns.

## Messages
None. Everything I say goes by Tessaly's ear in person at the margin, or by the Marchwarden's fire when I tell him what I read. I send no word by messenger or horn.

## What my people are instructed to do
I instruct no one. I ask, through Tessaly: that the Houndwarden send hunters and dogs on my reading of wolves at dusk at the calving, as the council allowed, and no further than it allowed; that the Marchwarden weigh a hurt herd read at a pool against the songs of the fords, and decide whether to move the camps; that the Saltkeeper keep the trade party's beasts apart longer; and that the two clans keep their cattle apart. Anything the council declines I leave as it is. No office holder has taken my word beyond the calving, and no one acts for me.

## Asked of me and not answered
A herder of the two clans, through Tessaly, asked whether I can say what the cough is. I answer it in action 1, in the words given there, through Tessaly, at the dusk council of the main camp in spring.

## For the Keeper to rule
Nothing. I use the Listening as the card and the rulings state: a gathering's place, which way it moves and its state, and a herd with sick beasts in it reads as hurt in part. I do not sound the horn, take a trail, call a storm, or speak with any beast this year, unless a herd is in danger and no other way serves, and then one beast in few breaths, which I tell Tessaly the same day. I try to grow no power this year.
