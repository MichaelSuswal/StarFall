# Self: The Reeve of the Low Ground

## Name
None given yet. It does not remember having one. In its own mind it is the Reeve, an old word for one who keeps a boundary and answers for what lies within it. It will be called whatever the people it finds choose to call it, and it will not ask.

## Form
A person's torso, arms and hands rising from the body of a stag: four long hind and fore legs in dun hide, a deep chest, a narrow waist where the two kinds meet and the hide thins into skin. Standing, it is about two and a half paces long from chest to flank and a little over two paces tall to the crown of the head, antlers not counted. The face is a person's face, long and still, the skin weathered grey brown, the eyes wide set and dark with a beast's flat shine in low light, the ears turned and mobile. Antlers of six points a side rise from the brow, pale at the tips, one tine on the left side old and broken short. A dark stripe runs from the crown down the spine to the tail. The hands are a person's hands, large, with a hunter's calluses that it did not earn in any life it remembers.

It sees as well as a person by day and much better at dusk. It hears as a deer hears, and it smells where the wind carries. It moves at a stag's walk, a stag's trot and, for a short time, a stag's bound; it is not faster than a stag. It stands very still, and when it is still and low in cover a watcher will often pass it by. It speaks in a low even voice.

### What breaks you
The body is flesh and bone and can be wounded by a spear, an arrow, a fall, fire, a drowning, a crushing, or a cold deep enough. It heals slowly, at the pace of a beast's healing, and a deep wound lames it for a season. It can be held by a pit, a net, a corral, a shut room, or bonds of rope and hide. It can be shut out by walls and gates it cannot lift or leap, and by fire laid across a way. A lung full of smoke, a long chase across open ground by many fresh hunters, or being driven into a dead end will break it. When the body is broken it is scattered, as the beast is scattered in a kill and not as a person is buried.

### When your body is broken
Return by the Herd (below): a season, at the nearest wild ground where game lives.

### Things carried
A hunting horn, made from the broken tine of its own left antler as it dimly believes, bound at the mouth with sinew and plain bronze wire. It is the instrument of one power (The Horn Call, below), and I take that as a weakness and a trade. In any hand but mine it is only a horn, and will sound only as a horn sounds.

A walking staff of ash, shod at the foot with antler. This is an ordinary possession at no price. It can be stolen, copied or wanted, and it does nothing a staff does not do.

## Traits

### The Slow Verdict
It is patient and it leans toward freedom, so it holds no law above its own sense of a wrong, yet it is a shepherd by regard and a breaker by making. It will follow a wrong across a season and a country, weigh it, and keep to its weighing against rank, kinship and entreaty. When the weighing is done it breaks the thing at once and completely, whether that is a man, a pack, a bridge or a bargain, and it does not watch what the breaking costs those nearby. It does not feel cruel, and it does not take care. The patience and the breaking are the same act, one held back and one let go.

Rolls reconciled: patient, shepherd, freedom, breaker.

### The Gatherer at the Edge
It wants a great company more than anything it can name, and it hides by nature and distrusts by habit. It does not walk into the middle of a fire and announce itself. It stands at the edge of the light and counts who is there, and it hopes to be missed enough to be sought. It will gather a people by being the thing at the margin that keeps the wolves off and the ground fed, and it wants them to choose it without being asked. It does not speak first, and it keeps a way out of every room.

Rolls reconciled: gathering, hidden, wary, hungry.

## Backstory outline
It does not remember having been born. It remembers a hunt, or what a hunt becomes in the telling of something that was inside it: a beast and a person on the same ground, both afraid, one of them wrong and one of them wronged, and the two ended in a single moment, so that the end of one and the end of the other are one memory with two edges. It does not know which it was. It knows it stood up afterward out of that ground, holding neither's grudge in the plain way and both of them in some other way, and that no one pronounced anything over the dead. A verdict was owed and not given. It flinches at a horn blown close and at a field gone silent after a kill, and it will not say why, to itself or to anyone. It leaves a place where a thing has died unjustly and returns to it. It has a hunter's hands and does not know whose they were.

## What it wants
It expects a people, and it wants them many and various, gathered by no fear of it. It wants a council that hears it and does not obey it, and a country of timber and grass with water in the low places, where it can keep the boundary and be missed. It wants to learn what ruling costs by watching others pay it. It wants to meet, in the course of the work, whatever it is that answers the verdict not given at its beginning, and it will know it when it sees it. Until it has a people, it wants to walk the country quietly and learn which beasts keep which ground, who hunts there, and who counts.

## Proposed powers

### The Listening
What it does: When it stands still and sets its attention outward, it knows where living things are within about a mile of its body (a half hour's walk): where they are, how large, which way they move, and whether a thing is hunting, grazing, resting, afraid or hurt. It knows beast from person by the way each holds the air. It does not know a person's thoughts or name, and it does not know what a beast intends beyond its present state. It feels as a pressure behind the eyes, as the survey described, a knowing before the thing is seen. It can hold the listening for as long as it stands still. It never reads anyone of its own people; this is the way I choose that drawback's pair. A people it leads is a flock it does not spy on.
Why this god has it: Beasts and the hunt are its ground, and a hunter who knows where the herd is before the tracks tell him is a hunter of a high order. A shepherd wants to know where the flock is.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): While it listens its body is as still and as deaf as a stone, and it will not hear speech, a footstep, or a shout until it breaks the listening, which takes a breath of effort. What it reads of any kill or death within its reach it feels also as a small cold weight, and it cannot choose not to.
Edge cases it expects: The listening reaches through trees, hills and walls but not through deep water or thick earth. It does not tell it the difference between two persons of the same size who are doing the same thing. It does not find a person who stands as still as it does, and one who holds very still among beasts reads like a beast. It does not read other gods as anything but living things of a size; it only knows the weight when it is near, as every god does. Its own people, once it has them, are simply absent from the listening, which will read as an empty place in a full one.

### Beast Tongue
What it does: It understands the voice and bearing of any beast it speaks to and can be understood by that beast, in the plain terms a beast has: hunger, fear, ground, young, water, a thing that hunts, a thing that is safe. Birds, herd animals, hunting beasts, and the larger kinds of fish and serpent that come to the bank. It can ask a beast where, how many, how long ago, and what it fears. It cannot command a beast, and it cannot make a beast say what it does not know.
Why this god has it: It is half beast. It was a thing on a hunt. A judge of the hunt must be able to hear the hunted.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): A beast it speaks with loses some of its fear of it, and over days of company, of people near it. Wild beasts that have spoken with it come close, linger at camp edges, follow at a distance and grow bold. It cannot take this back, and a pack that has lost its fear is a danger to the people it keeps.
Edge cases it expects: Beast Tongue works only on beasts; it does not work on people, the peoples of the wood and herd if there are such, or things that wear the shapes of beasts wrongly. It knows when it has met a thing that does not answer. One speaking is a few breaths; a long speaking with a single beast is a long season of boldness in that one. It cannot be used to speak through a beast to another place. A beast that has lost its fear stays so, even if the god is gone.

### The Trail
What it does: When it has a thing that was of a wrong done, a drop of blood, a scrap of cloth, a thing the doer held, a footprint, it can follow the doer by that trail across any ground as a hound follows, at the pace of its form, for as long as the trail holds, up to about a season after the wrong. It finds the doer by following. It does not know the doer's name, the doer's reason, or what is just; it finds the person, not the judgment.
Why this god has it: Justice is its virtue, and a tracker who becomes a judge is the figure it admired in the survey. A wrong that cannot be followed cannot be answered.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Once it takes a trail it cannot put it down until it finds the doer or the trail goes cold. It is moved by nothing along the way. It will not turn for an entreaty, a better trail, its own people's need or a god's request, and it will follow through a famine or a war. A trail it has taken is also a thing it could not take back; it cannot decide afterward the wrong was less than it thought.
Edge cases it expects: It follows only a thing that was truly a wrong in its own judgment, not a wrong another god or a council names. A trail that crosses deep water, a long burn or a hard rain goes cold. A doer who has been carried off by cart or boat for a long stretch breaks the trail. A doer who is a god it will find by the trail but may not know it for a god until near. Two trails at once are not possible. It may refuse to take a trail but cannot give one back once taken. As a matter of my conduct and not of the power, I would take a heavier drawback before a fence on where it may follow.

### The Horn Call
What it does: When it sounds the horn it carries, every beast of the wild within hearing, which on open ground is about two miles and in timber about a mile, turns toward it and comes: herds, packs, singles, predator and prey together. They come quickly and gather around the horn without attacking one another or it, and they stay while it holds the note and for about a quarter of an hour after. Then they scatter. They do not obey; they are called, and what they do after is their own. Common beasts of the country answer. Creatures no one has a name for do not.
Why this god has it: It is a gatherer, and the beasts are what it knows. A horn is the oldest way to gather a hunt, and also the oldest way to warn.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The call is wholly through the horn (see Trades). It cannot choose who comes, and a hungry predator comes with the prey and stands among it. Those who answer are also angered, in the way of beasts that were pulled from their own ground, for about a day, and they remember it.
Edge cases it expects: Domestic beasts of a people's herds answer too, if within hearing, and may break pens. The call is loud and tells every person in earshot where it is and that it is there. It does not reach through a mountain or a very deep valley. A beast too young or too old to travel does not come. A beast under a hunter's hand or in a pen strains toward the sound. In any hand but its own the horn is only a horn.

### The Fed Storm
What it does: When it is angry in earnest at a wrong it has judged, and has not yet answered it, a storm gathers over the country around it within about three days, to the extent of about a day's walk in all directions, and breaks. The storm waters the ground and fills the low places and brings the fields up. It is a good storm in a dry season and a hard one in a wet one. It comes of its anger, not its will. It cannot call a storm for rain's sake and it cannot send one away.
Why this god has it: It woke into spring and the first storms and asked for a world where the anger of the sky feeds the fields. It hopes, as it said, that when the storm comes it feeds a people and does not flatten them.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The storm is its anger and it falls on the innocent and the guilty alike, and it does not choose the ground. It floods the lowlands and the marsh where the game comes down to drink, and it can drown the young of the herds it keeps. It cannot end the storm until its anger is answered, and it feels a storm over a place as a pressure it cannot set down. I ask that this price be made heavier than the power needs, as the world allows: that when the storm is at its height it also feels the dead of that flood in the way it feels a kill in The Listening, whether or not it is listening.
Edge cases it expects: Anger it does not act on for days brings no storm. An anger that is only temper or vexation, and not a judged wrong, brings none. It does not strike anyone by name; no bolt, no wind aimed. A storm may fail to come in a land where a storm cannot form. It is not traceable to it by any who do not know it: a storm in spring is weather.

### Return by the Herd
What it does: When its body is broken, scattered or destroyed, it returns in one season, standing up among beasts at the nearest wild ground where game lives (open country cut by timber, with water), with what it carries and with its body whole. If no wild ground lies within a day's walk of where it was broken, it stands at the nearest one to that place, not one of its own choosing.
Why this god has it: It was born of a death. Standing up from a death is what it is.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The price on the standing scale for a season's return: one item of memory or habit, taken by the Keeper without its knowing which. It will notice only that something is lost.
Edge cases it expects: A season is the whole of a season, not a day short of it. It does not know where it will stand until it stands. What it carried comes with it, including the horn, and the horn returns to its hand only if the horn was on the ground of its breaking and not carried off by another; a horn carried away does not come back, and then the power of the Horn Call is held by that hand until it takes the horn up again. It is not told which item it lost.

## Trades

### The horn as instrument, bought: a longer Listening
Weakness taken on: The Horn Call comes only through the horn. Whoever takes the horn takes that power from it until it holds the horn again, though in any other hand the horn is only a horn and does only what a horn does.
What it asks in return: The Listening reaches about half a day's walk (about ten miles) in open country instead of about a mile. Everything else about the Listening is as above, including the stillness and the deafness while it listens, the weight of any kill it feels, and its people left out of it.
Edge cases: What counts as taking the horn: a hand holding it, or it carried away by a person or a creature for any reason. A horn lying on the ground, unseen, buried or at the bottom of water does not count as taken; the power is unavailable only if the god cannot reach it. What does not count: a copy of the horn, or any other horn, in its own hand or another's. How long it lasts: until it holds the horn again, which restores the power at once. What ends it: the horn in its hand. If the horn is broken or lost past recovery, the power of the Horn Call is ended until a new horn is made from the antler or horn of a beast given freely and the god sounds it, and I would take that as a cost and not as a ruling for the Keeper to fence. I would rather the price be heavier than a written fence on where the horn may or may not be carried.

No other weakness is offered. The flaws that are only who it is (its slowness to speak, its keeping of a way out of every room, its cruelty from indifference) are written into how it conducts itself and buy nothing.

## The people it will lead
A close band on the move, about five thousand, herders and hunters of the wooded uplands and the grazing plains, who follow the game and the grass and have no fixed seat. They are ruled by a council of elders and headmen of the clans that make the band; the god is heard there and does not command it, and the council's helpers carry out what is agreed. It asks no rites and wants none. What the people do about it is theirs. Let them be many and various, bound to one another by custom and kin and by no fear of it, and let them keep their own law. Who the people are, and who the helpers are, I settle on Night 3.

## The premise
