# Negotiation: god-rivers, Night 3

## Round 1 reply

We accept what was read and not priced: no edge, no weakness owed, tempers and not guarantees, wishes and beliefs written as such. We have nothing to add to it.

### Your landing: the helpers and the seat
We choose (a). Keep the meeting as we wrote it, at the cost the Keeper names: we land among a people that does not know us, with no helpers in fact and no seat that is ours in fact. Each office becomes our helper when its holder hears us and takes our word, in play.

Our reasons. First, we asked in the survey to walk up a river we have never seen and find someone who did not know we were coming, and to be a stranger who is fed at the first hearth. (b) would take that from us. Second, our wish is a council that can say no to us, and a people that has never been told whose it is will say no honestly. Third, the cost is the one any god pays who lands so, and we would rather win an office than be handed it. We know that until then no one acts for us while we are away, and that the people go their own way under their own council. We are content that they do. We were never going to command them.

### The low seat
We accept it as ordinary culture and a courtesy with no remembered beginning. We accept that where the town stands, and whether a spring rises by its wall, is the world's. We do not argue it.

### Their magic
We accept it. We asked for none and left it to the world, and two or three who work a small thing among the ferrymen, midwives and well keepers is as it should be. We ask only that each bears her own cost, as the Keeper says, and we take it as written in their record and not ours.

### The spears
We say: about three hundred carry a spear for the town when it comes to that, as a call-up of working people (the heads of houses and their grown sons and daughters, each with a spear, a sling or a reed shield of their own making). That is a call-up and ordinary. We also say that a standing watch of about thirty is kept at the water gate and along the wall, fed from the town's stores in turn by the houses. That is fewer than one in fifty of two thousand, and so ordinary on the Keeper's scale, and no edge. We ask no more than that. They are not drilled as a body beyond the watch, and the people do not mean to be a people that fights if it can be fed, or ferried, or talked to first.

We counter no item and refuse none.
