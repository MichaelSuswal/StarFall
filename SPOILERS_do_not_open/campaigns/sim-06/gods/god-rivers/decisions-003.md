# Decisions: god-rivers, Year 3

Year 3 runs from the turn of the year, through our waking season, spring, and round the four seasons to spring again. We begin it at the spring head, where we woke at the thaw. The voice is ours: we, the Lady of the Ford, or only the river. We command no one. Each office holder who has heard us and taken our word is a helper only so far as they choose.

## Actions

1. **Spring, in the first days after the thaw: walk down the stream path to the town, barefoot, in the woman's shape, and sit with the children at the fire, to answer the ferry child.** To "Can I go with you to the ford?" we say that the Ferry Elder said no, that the Elder is right to keep a child out of the larger water, and that we will tell the child at the fire what we saw and what the keepers measured, and ask again when the Elder says. To "Then you are a spring's?" we say that we woke at a spring, that we do not know if we are any spring's, and that this is all we know. We say nothing of any working through water. We ask the Seat Warden to keep the cord of it as she keeps the rest. Our reason: a child's question that goes unanswered teaches the child that a guest does not answer, and we would rather be known for a plain answer that does not please.

2. **Spring, at the spring flood and the choosing of the Speaker: sit at the council, tell it what we ask, and ask the Ferry Elder and the herd keepers one thing each.** We ask the Ferry Elder to read the flood marks and the low-water cord with us and say whether the deep water moved again this flood. We say to the herd keepers, through the Seat Warden and the Ferry Elder who will carry it, that when stock strays into the reed we will search if we are sent for and not otherwise, and that a word to the water gate is enough. Our reason: last autumn thirty goats and two cattle were lost and we were not asked, and we want the next loss to be smaller without our going where we are not wanted. At the same sitting we ask the Speaker whether the council has settled what to do about the ford since the thaw, and we say plainly that we are asking, not pressing.

3. **Summer, in the dry weeks: go down the larger river by barge with two ferry keepers and the Reckoner's cord walker, past the ford where the brook meets it, three days or four along the larger river toward the southern way the salt carriers spoke of, and come home before the autumn low water.** We ask the Ferry Elder to name the two keepers and the Reckoner to name the cord walker, and we ask the council's leave to go, telling it how long we mean to be away and when we will be back. We stop at each hearth and landing on the way and ask for news and a count, and we ask the people there where the carriers cross now. We take the stone in the pouch and show nothing. We stand in the larger river to the knee each evening and each dawn, and watch for a pull. We do not shift in anyone's sight. If we are hurt or a child is in the water, Warm hands, and the cold is ours. We use no Calling the water. Our reason: we have heard of the larger river and the southern way twice and have not seen either, the town's trouble is there, and we would know where the carriers go before the thaw.

4. **Summer, at the ford where the brook meets the larger river, on the way down and on the way back: answer the ford keeper.** The ford keeper asked what we were looking for in the river. We say that we are looking for why the deep water moves, and that we are also looking, if there is any, for another like ourselves, and that we have found none. We say that we do not know what we are looking for in the sense of why we came. We say nothing of any working. Our reason: the keeper asked in good faith and we told no lie and no truth, and we would rather be known for the truth.

5. **Late summer, at the far ford, with watchers: listen for about an hour, no more, and sleep about two hours after.** We ask the two ferry keepers and the cord walker to sit by us while we kneel in the running water of the larger river at the ford, to keep a fire, a mat and the dogs and the cold off through the sleep, and to wake the council's runner if anything comes against us, though no one can wake us. We listen for what the water carries within twenty miles, up and down, of the carriers' crossings, of the deep water and the bed, and of any other like us, and we judge what we are told and say so at council. We listen once. Our reason: low water shows the bed's shape when the river is quiet, and the larger river at the far ford is the part we have not heard.

6. **Autumn, at the low water, then the winter: stand with the ferry keepers at the mark the council set, read it, and bring all of it to the council in one sitting.** We tell the council what the keepers measured, what the carriers said, what we saw and what the water told us, and give our counsel as counsel: if the council wants a second ferry, a mark to read by, or a new crossing, we say what we think and the council may answer no. We name no one to do any of it, and ask the Ferry Elder, the Reckoner or the keepers the council names, and not before. We tell the council and the Reckoner that a notched cord is kept for the dim nights of the star, with where we stood each time. In winter we stay in town, sit at the council through the ice, count with the Reckoner and keep the second cord. We do not listen in frozen water. We go back to the spring head in the thaw, alone, to mark the turn. Our reason: the people's own habit of listening sooner is our best defence against hunger after a bad flood, and we are one voice at the fire among nine.

## What we tell our people to do, and through whom

We command no one. We ask, and the council or the person may refuse us at no cost.

- The Ferry Elder and the ferry keepers: read the flood marks and the low-water cord at the water gate stone and the ford; name two keepers to go with us down the larger river in summer; carry word along the channels; tell us what the carriers say. Asked in person at the council and at the ford.
- The Reckoner: name a cord walker to go with us, keep the second cord and say it back at council. Asked in person.
- The Seat Warden: keep the cord of what we say and do at each sitting and recite it back; carry our word to the herd keepers; sit watch if willing. Asked in person.
- The herd keepers, through the Seat Warden and the Ferry Elder: send to the water gate at once if stock strays into the reed, and we will search if we are sent for.
- The well keepers: tell us and the council at once if a spring turns. The midwives: tell us of any child or mother at risk. The mill and weir hands: tell the council when the water is wrong.

Where we are away, no one acts for us, and we ask no one to. We tell the council before we go and when we will come back.

## Answers to what was asked of us

The ferry child's two questions are answered in action 1. The ford keeper's question is answered in action 4.

## Messages

None. We send no message to anyone not in front of us this year. We know of no one to send to.

## For the Keeper to rule

Nothing is asked beyond what our card plainly covers. We state our reading so it may be corrected:

1. Listening water: one listening of about an hour at the larger river's ford, with sleep about twice that. We read it as covering twenty miles of water up and down from where we kneel, feeders included, and as telling what is and never why.
2. The pull in the water: we read it as working only while we stand in or touch running water of the same river, the larger river's own feeders and outflow included, and we will stand in it each evening and dawn on the journey. We ask the Keeper to tell us in the next release if we felt one.
3. Unsigned: in actions 1 and 4 we say where we woke, that we do not know what we are, and what we are looking for. We read this as our own words about our own body and wish, and not a claim of any working through water.
4. We try to grow no power this year.
