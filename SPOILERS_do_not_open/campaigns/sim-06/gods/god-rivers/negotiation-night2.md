# Negotiation: god-rivers, Night 2

## Round 1 reply

We accept the holds on every card as written. We accept that the two shapes are no power and carry no price. Our choices and our reasons, item by item:

### Listening water
We choose (a), near and full. We accept the draft text, the sleep of twice the listening, and the limits as written. Our reason: what we want from the water is to know a people by it, who crossed, who drank, what was said at the ford, and (b) leaves that out. We would rather hear twenty miles of a country in words than all of its waters without a face in them. A flood coming is a thing we can also hear in words from the banks. We accept the Keeper's refusal of a reach with no end; we asked for a heavier price in place of a fence, and the Keeper has given us both, and we do not argue it.

### Calling the water
We choose (b), to the size of a valley. We accept the drawback as written, including the season in the water and the item of memory for a working beyond sight, and the top written at the river's own greatest flood. Our reason: a shepherd who can only call the water where it can see it is no use on the night the flood comes down from the hills, and a night like that is the whole of why we want this power. We know it is a heavy card. We take it with its price and would not have it lighter. We expect to work beyond sight rarely, and each time we will know what it cost.

One clarification, in the write-up and not a new power: where the card says the water "does not know our people and spares no one," we read it as saying that we cannot aim a flood around a house or a person. We read it as not saying that we cannot choose to work small, nor that we are forced to work large. Our price runs with the size we choose.

### Warm hands
We accept it as written. The cold counted in the days nature would have taken is fair, and the condition that the warmth will not come for one we have wronged is as we offered it.

### Unsigned
We choose (b), as a power. We accept the drawback that the god cannot claim a working in words, and that what we say when we try is that the water did it. We accept the removal of mortals being unable to believe when told; we agree that this was a rule over minds and we do not ask for it back. Our reason for (b) over (a): under (a), anyone who reasons it out knows, and the point of the power is that the work leaves nothing for a mortal to follow to us. We take the tongue gladly as the price. We are a thing that was once named and then not, and we would rather choose to be unnamed than be overlooked.

One clarification, in the write-up and not a new power: that the tongue binds only a claim of a working that went through water, and does not bind our speech about anything else, at council or elsewhere. We may say what we did with our own hands and what we think and want. We may say "we were at the ford that night."

### Rise at the spring
We accept it as written, with the season rung, the walk added, and the item taken by a logged roll that we are not told of.

### The trade: the stone as the instrument
We choose (a), as written. We accept the weakness with the Keeper's corrections: a free gift does not pass the power to the other hand, we do not feel where the stone lies, and if it is destroyed the power is gone until our next return. We accept the far sense as written: while we stand in or touch running water, we feel another god that stands in or on water of the same river as a pull, upstream or down, and nothing more.

Our reason: we are open and wish to find the others slowly and by choice, and a pull in the water is the gentlest way we can think of to be told someone is there. We carry the stone already and would carry it whatever its price, and we will be the kind of god that is anxious for it. The Keeper has written the weakness truthfully and priced it fairly. We argue nothing against it.

We do not counter any item. We have no further reasons to give.

## Round 2 reply

We confirm the final text as written, and have no wording to fix.

On Calling the water, we answer the Keeper's plain word once: we keep (b), to the size of a valley, with its season in the water and its item of memory for a working beyond sight. We said in round 1 that we knew it was a heavy card and would not have it lighter, and nothing said here is new to us except that the Keeper has weighed it above its usual mark. We hold to our reason: the night a flood comes down out of the hills is the whole of why we want this power, and a shepherd that cannot reach past its own sight on that night has not been given the thing it asked for. The price is the price, and we take it knowing the item may be one we would miss. We expect to pay it rarely.

The two shapes, Listening water, Warm hands, Unsigned, Rise at the spring, The pull in the water, and the stone as the instrument all stand as we accepted them. We understand that anyone who learns of the stone can take Calling the water from our hand by taking it, and we accept that as the weakness we offered.

On the Keeper trait, that our face does not stay with people: we accept it, with no argument. We take it as a fact about us from the moment we arrive, and we ask nothing of it.
