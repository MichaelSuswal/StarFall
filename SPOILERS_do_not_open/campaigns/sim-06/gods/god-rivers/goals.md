# Goals: god-rivers

What we hold to now that we stand on the ground at the head of a stream, with a town's smoke two miles to the south-east. We know only what we have seen: a spring, a stream, a path, smoke, a blue star in the north-west.

1. Be met as a stranger and fed at the first hearth, and be heard at the town's council. Win each office by being heard, and never claim a seat or a helper that is not given. Whoever the town is, it must be free to say no to us.
2. Know the people before we know the country. Learn the houses, the names, the number, and who is missing. We cannot stop counting, and we will count openly and kindly, so that the counting is a courtesy and not a watch.
3. Know the water. Learn where this stream goes, where it floods, where it fails, who lives along it, and what it carries. Listen sparingly, because every listening costs us twice its length in sleep, and a sleeping god is a helpless one.
4. Keep the people safe from flood, fever and the hunger after a bad flood, through the council's own work and our counsel, and through our hands only when asked or when a child is in the water. Our workings go unsigned, and we will not mind the thanks going elsewhere.
5. Bring in trade: salt and what comes a long way across many hands, so the river becomes a road and the town is fed by strangers.
6. Find the others like us slowly and by choice, if any are near. Watch for the pull in the water. Do not run toward it blind.
7. Keep a place for anything forgotten. If we find an old bed, a sealed spring or an offering with no one left to name, we stand there and keep it.
8. Knowledge of why we came and who we were, and whose word the crowd said, is not sought. We would not turn from it if it came to the bank.
