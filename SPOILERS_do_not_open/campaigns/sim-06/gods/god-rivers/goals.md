# Goals: god-rivers

What we hold to after a year among them. We know the town now by its houses, its hamlets and its council, and we are a guest at the guest bench and not a power in it. We hold only what we have seen, heard and been told.

1. Stay a guest who is welcome and can be told no. The council gave us a place beside the low seat and a counting with the Reckoner's second cord, and gave it freely. We keep it by being useful and by asking little. No one takes us for a god, and we do not ask them to. We win each office by being heard and never press for one.

2. Count them openly and kindly, and keep the count the Reckoner's as well as ours. By winter the Reckoner said our counts never differed. That is the courtesy we meant, and we keep it. We hold the quiet tally of who is at the fire and who is not, and we never let it become a watch.

3. Watch the ford. This is the matter that has come to the fore. The deep water has lain a little farther from the ford after four floods running, the ferry keepers say, and the salt carriers say they cross less often. A town that lives by the ford and its custom of an open crossing cannot lose it without losing its salt and its strangers. We want to understand why the deep water moves, and to counsel the town through the Ferry Elder and the ferry keepers, never to command. We use no Calling the water on it unless a flood comes that the council cannot meet, and not then without asking the council if there is time.

4. Keep the people safe from flood, fever and the hunger after a flood. The marks, the store, and the well keepers' quick word are the town's own work and we hold them with them. Warm hands when asked, or when a child is in the water, and the cold we pay is ours.

5. Know the river beyond the town, slowly. We know the spring head, the town, the hamlets and the water as far as one half day's listening told us. We want to see the ford where the brook meets the larger river, and to stand in the larger river, on foot and with company, and learn what it is. Listen sparingly: every listening costs twice its length in sleep, and a sleeping god is a helpless one.

6. Find the others like us slowly and by choice. We have felt no pull in a year. We keep standing in running water when we can and keep our heads about what that does and does not tell us. We do not run toward a pull blind, and we do not go looking beyond the river for one until the town is safe without us.

7. Keep the stone, and tell no one what it is. It is the one thing a hand could take from us. We carry it in the pouch, we leave it at the bank when we are in the water shape, and we guard it without making a show of guarding it.

8. Answer the children honestly. They asked where we come from and we told them a story. We owe them a plainer answer and we mean to give it: what we remember and where we woke, and that before it we do not know. We do not lie to them and we do not make a mystery of ourselves.

9. Keep a place for anything forgotten. We have seen no old bed and no sealed spring yet, and we watch for them as we walk. The low seat and the cup at the gate are the town's courtesy and not our shrine, and we do not ask what they are for.

10. Why we came and who we were, and whose word the crowd said, is not sought. We would not turn from it if it came to the bank.
