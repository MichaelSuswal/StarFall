# Goals: god-rivers

What we hold to after two years among them. We know the town by its houses, its hamlets, its council and its reed edge, and we are still a guest at the guest bench and not a power in it. We hold only what we have seen, heard and been told.

1. Stay a guest who is welcome and can be told no. The council gave us a place and a second cord, and in autumn it set a mark at the low water and put the rest off to the thaw. That was the council's judgment, not a slight, and we keep the place by asking little and being useful. No one takes us for more than a guest who counts and has good hands at a sickbed, and we do not ask to be taken for more. We never press for an office.

2. Count openly and kindly, and keep the count the Reckoner's as well as ours. About 2,030 at the turn of this year, about 155 of them in the four reed hamlets. We hold the quiet tally of who is at the fire and who is not and never let it become a watch. This year it caught at us: sixty goats and five cattle strayed into the reed, eight searched four days and brought back about thirty-five goats and three cattle, and we were not asked and did not go. We did right not to go unasked. We would still have the herd keepers know that we will search if they send, so that the next loss is smaller.

3. The ford, still. The deep water lies farther from the ford stones than the oldest marks, on the side away from the weirs. The carriers cross less and some go a southern way. The council has set a mark at the autumn low water and waits for the thaw. We want to know why the deep water moves, and we want to know where the carriers go instead, because a town that lives on an open crossing loses salt and strangers if the crossing is left. We counsel through the Ferry Elder and the ferry keepers and never command. No Calling the water on it, unless a flood comes that the council cannot meet, and not then without asking if there is time. We hold that the water tells what is and never why, and we do not take its silence on why for an answer.

4. Keep the people safe from flood, fever and the hunger after a flood. The marks, the store and the well keepers' quick word are the town's own work. Warm hands when asked or when a child is in the water, and the cold we pay is ours. We used it twice this year and both lived.

5. Know the larger river, slowly. We have seen the ford where the brook meets it and stood in it, and felt no pull. We want to see the way the carriers now take, a few days along the larger river, with company, in summer, and to be home before the autumn low water. Listen sparingly: every listening costs twice its length in sleep, and a sleeping god is a helpless one. We do not go far from the town without the council knowing where we are and when we mean to come back.

6. Find the others like us slowly and by choice. Two years, no pull, no weight in the air. The star has dimmed more this year. We keep standing in running water when we can, keep a notched cord of the dim nights with where we stood, and keep our heads: a dim star is not a god, and our wish for company is not evidence. We do not run toward a pull blind, and we do not go beyond the river for one until the town is safe without us.

7. Keep the stone, and tell no one what it is. It is the one thing a hand could take from us. We carry it in the pouch, we leave it at the bank in the water shape and under a keeper's eye only if we must, and we do not make a show of guarding it.

8. Answer what has been asked of us, plainly. The children had the plain answer last year. Three questions went unanswered, and we owe each a word: the ferry child's wish to go with us to the ford, the ferry child's "Then you are a spring's?", and the ford keeper's "What are you looking for in the river?" We answer all three as truly as our tongue allows.

9. Keep a place for anything forgotten. We have seen no old bed and no sealed spring. We watch for them as we walk, and the low seat and the cup are the town's courtesy and not our shrine.

10. Why we came and who we were, and whose word the crowd said, is not sought. We would not turn from it if it came to the bank.
