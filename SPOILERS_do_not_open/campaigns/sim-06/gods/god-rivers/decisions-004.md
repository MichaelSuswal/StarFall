# Decisions: god-rivers, Year 4

Year 4 runs from the turn of the year, through our waking season, spring, and round the four seasons to spring again. We begin it at the spring head, where we woke at the thaw. The voice is ours: we, the Lady of the Ford, or only the river. We command no one. Each office holder who has heard us and taken our word is a helper only so far as they choose.

## Actions

1. **Spring, in the first days after the thaw: walk down the stream path to the town, barefoot, in the woman's shape, and sit with the children at the fire, to answer the small boy.** To "A spring cannot walk" we say that he is right. A spring cannot walk, and we are not a spring. We woke at one and we walk, and we do not know why the one came with the other. We say nothing of any working through water. We ask the Seat Warden to keep the cord of it as she keeps the rest. Our reason: a child's word that goes unanswered teaches the child that a guest does not answer, and the boy said a true thing.

2. **Spring, at the spring flood and the choosing of the Speaker: sit at the council, answer the head herd keeper, and bring the marks.** To the head herd keeper, who asked why we would search for their goats and what we would do that a boat and a dog cannot, we say before the council: we do not tire and we do not sleep, so we can walk the reed edge through a night and a day with the dogs and count heads when the boats must rest. Beyond that, nothing that a boat and a dog cannot do, and we would be one more pair of feet. We say we will search if we are sent for, a word to the water gate is enough, and the keepers may say no. We ask the Ferry Elder to read the flood marks and the low water cord with us and say whether the deep water moved again this flood. We then tell the council what last year gave: the deep water lies farther again from the ford stones on the side away from the weirs, the bed behind each weir is shoaled, fewer laden beasts crossed, and the reed cutters say the salt for the stone towns now comes up from the sea in the south and does not need the ford. We say plainly that this may be the whole of it, that it is for the council to say what the ford is worth to the town if the salt goes round it, and that we are asking and not pressing. Our reason: the head herd keeper asked in good faith, and a town that sees its own ford and its own salt move will decide better than one that is told.

3. **Spring, after the council has heard us: ask the mill and weir hands, through the Ferry Elder, to measure the shoal behind each weir at the low water, and ask the council whether it will try opening one weir for a season.** We say that this is a guess and not a finding: the weirs may slow the water, the bed behind them may silt, and the silt may push the deep water away from the stones. The water tells what is and never why, and we cannot say it is so. We say the measure costs the town only a few hands and a cord, and that opening one weir is the council's to grant or to refuse, and we would not blame it for refusing a thing that costs the eel catch. We use no Calling the water. We ask the Reckoner to keep the second cord of the measures with the marks. Our reason: if the weirs are the cause, it is the one cause that the town's own hands can mend, and if they are not, the measure will say so and we will have lost nothing.

4. **Summer, in the dry weeks: go down the larger river by barge with two ferry keepers and the Reckoner's cord walker, past the ford where the brook meets it and past the low country of many channels where we turned last year, five days down or until we meet the salt, whichever comes first, and come home before the autumn low water.** We ask the council's leave first, and tell it how long we mean to be away and the day we turn. We ask the Ferry Elder to name the two keepers and the Reckoner to name the cord walker. Where the carriers and the reed cutters come to the river, we ask where the salt joins it and whether the ford is needed. At the ford, on the way down, we answer the ford keeper, who asked "What is another like yourself?": we say that we do not know, that we have met none and felt none, that we would know one at close quarters by a weight in the air and not before, and that if one came we would sit it by a hearth and ask its news after it had eaten. We make no claim of any working. We stand in the larger river to the knee each dusk and each dawn and watch for a pull. We take the stone in the pouch and show nothing, and we do not shift in anyone's sight. If a child is in the water or a person is hurt or fevered and asks us, Warm hands, and the cold is ours. We use no Calling the water. We listen once, at the farthest reach, for about an hour and no more, and sleep about two hours after, with the two ferry keepers and the cord walker to keep a fire, a mat and the dogs and the cold off, and to send for the council's runner if anything comes against us, though no one can wake us. Our reason: we have been ninety miles down and have not seen where the salt goes, the town's trouble is there, and a council cannot weigh a ford it does not know the worth of.

5. **Autumn, at the low water: stand with the ferry keepers at the mark the council set, read it, and bring all of it to the council in one sitting.** We tell the council what the keepers measured, what the weir hands measured, what the carriers and the reed cutters said, what we saw on the farther river and what the water told us, and we give our counsel as counsel: whether the ford is worth a second ferry, a mark to read by, an opened weir, or a letting go of it, and the council may answer no. We name no one to do any of it, and we ask the Ferry Elder, the Reckoner or the keepers the council names, and not before. We tell the council and the Reckoner the count of the dim nights on the notched cord, with where we stood each time. Our reason: the people's own habit of listening sooner is our best defence against hunger after a bad flood, and we are one voice at the fire among nine.

6. **Winter, and the turn of the year: stay in the town, sit at the council through the ice, count with the Reckoner and keep the second cord, and go back to the spring head alone in the thaw to mark the turn.** We tell stories at the fire, learn the new children's names, and keep the cup at the low seat only as a guest's courtesy. We do not listen in frozen water. Our reason: we stay where we are wanted, and the town is held together by itself and by its own offices.

## What we tell our people to do, and through whom

We command no one. We ask, and the council or the person may refuse us at no cost.

- The Ferry Elder and the ferry keepers: read the flood marks and the low water cord at the water gate stone and the ford; name two keepers to go with us down the larger river in summer; carry word along the channels; tell us what the carriers say. Asked in person at the council and at the ford.
- The Reckoner: name a cord walker to go with us, keep the second cord with the marks and the weir measures, and say it back at council. Asked in person.
- The mill and weir hands, through the Ferry Elder: measure the shoal behind each weir at the low water and tell the council what they find. The council may grant or refuse it.
- The Seat Warden: keep the cord of what we say and do at each sitting and recite it back; carry our word to the herd keepers; sit watch if willing. Asked in person.
- The herd keepers, through the Seat Warden and the Ferry Elder: send to the water gate at once if stock strays into the reed, and we will search if we are sent for.
- The well keepers: tell us and the council at once if a spring turns. The midwives: tell us of any child or mother at risk.

Where we are away, no one acts for us, and we ask no one to. A question put to us then waits. We tell the council before we go and when we will come back.

## Answers to what was asked of us

The small boy's word is answered in action 1. The head herd keeper's question is answered in action 2. The ford keeper's question is answered in action 4, at the ford, on the way down.

## Messages

None. We send no message to anyone not in front of us this year. We know of no one to send to.

## For the Keeper to rule

Nothing is asked beyond what our card plainly covers. We state our reading so it may be corrected:

1. Listening water: one listening of about an hour at the farthest reach of the journey, with sleep about twice that. We read it as covering twenty miles of water up and down from where we kneel, feeders included, and as telling what is and never why.
2. The pull in the water: we read it as working only while we stand in or touch running water of the same river, the larger river's own feeders and outflow included, and we will stand in it each dusk and dawn on the journey. We ask the Keeper to tell us in the next release if we felt one.
3. Unsigned: in actions 1, 2 and 4 we say that a spring cannot walk and that we do, what we can do with our own body at the reed's edge, and that we do not know what another like us is. We read this as our own words about our own body and wish, and not a claim of any working through water. The weir guess in action 3 is our own reasoning said at council and not a claim of any working.
4. We try to grow no power this year.
