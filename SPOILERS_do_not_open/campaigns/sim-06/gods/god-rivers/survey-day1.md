## Answer 1

Water moving under a morning that has not decided what it will be. We see a country where every low place is held by something that runs, where you can stand at a spring and feel the whole land breathing up through your feet. It is not a quiet world. It is a loud, green, quarrelsome one, and it is kind at the edges. The mood is of being carried.

## Answer 2

River country. Where a hundred small waters find each other and become one large patient one. Springs in the hills, braided channels in the lowland, a delta at the end that no map keeps still. We would take marsh and ford and floodplain, and we would take the high wet places where it all begins. Nothing stranger is needed. Water is strange enough.

## Answer 3

Four seasons, and none of them cruel for long. A thaw that means it, a flood season that waters the fields and does not drown the children, a long gold ebb, and a winter that sleeps the rivers under ice but never kills them. Let the rain be dependable and let the drought be rare enough to be remembered by the grandmothers. We would not have a world where the water fails twice running.

As for waking, we would wake into spring, into the thaw, when the ice lets go and every stream is loud again. We wake into the spring.

## Answer 4

Some of each, and more wild than rooted. We would like to walk up a river we have never seen and find that someone lives at the top of it, someone who does not yet know we exist. A few old kingdoms, yes, so there are doors to knock on. But mostly country with room in it, where a stranger is a gift and not a threat. We are not afraid of company. We hope for it.

## Answer 5

Thousands of years, and ruins under ruins. The river remembers what it carried. We would like silt that has people in it, old bridges standing in the shallows, and a long human story that has already forgotten itself more than once. Let there be peoples who built great things and lost the way to them. We know something of being lost that way.

## Answer 6

A wise woman in some valleys and none in others. Never gone, never everywhere. A well that the old ones will not draw from after dusk, a midwife whose hands are warm in a way that is not warmth, a ferryman who always knows the weather. Most people doubt it and most people quietly use it. Let it be the sort of thing you ask about at the edge of a field and not in a market.

## Answer 7

It should feel like listening. Like holding still until something answers. And it should cost patience and sleep and something borrowed from the body, a little warmth, a little time, a little of the user's own water. Let the cost be honest and small each time and large over a life. Let no one be able to buy it with other people's pain. We would not have magic that is paid for by someone else.

## Answer 8

Somewhere closer to the sorcerer than the king. A god should stand out the way a great river stands out from a creek: you notice it, you plan around it, you cannot ignore it, but it does not erase the creek. We do not want to be the one eye in the kingdom of the blind. We want to be a large thing among other large things, so that mortals are never made small by it.

## Answer 9

Familiar animals, and a good many legends, and at the edges a few things with no name. A pike older than any village. Something in the deep pools that nobody has seen twice. Let the strangeness live in the places water hides things: under banks, in caves where springs rise, in fog. The wild should be wild enough to be respected and not so wild that a child cannot walk to the well.

## Answer 10

Hunger after a bad flood. Sickness that moves with the water. The neighbor across the ridge when the harvest fails. We find the best stories in fear that has a reason, fear that a person could have prevented if they had listened sooner. Let there also be one thing in the dark that is simply old and does not care. But the true danger should be the kind that comes up the road, slowly, wearing a familiar face.

## Answer 11

Clean water would be too cruel to make scarce, and we would not ask it. Let salt be scarce. Let iron be scarce. Let it be something mortals must go a long way for and trade across a dozen hands, so that roads are made, and rivers become the roads. Salt and the people who carry it. A world is gathered by the thing it cannot make at home.

## Answer 12

Old roads, and under them older channels. A buried bed where a river used to run, still shaped like a river, dry and full of stones that were smoothed by water long gone. Perhaps a sealed spring, stopped with a great flat stone. We would like to find a place where someone once made an offering and no one remembers to whom. We would like to stand there.

## Answer 13

Yes, a few, and gentle ones among them, though not all gentle. Peoples of the water margin, reed folk, otter folk, slow things that live in the shallows and are shy of the mud brick towns. Let them be old, and let them have their own reasons that are not about us. Let some be wary of gods and some be glad. We would like to be liked by someone who owes us nothing.

## Answer 14

Few, and welcome. A traveler from far away who comes upriver with a strange tool and an odd way of counting, and who is fed at the first hearth they reach. We would like the world to be fed by strangers. Let them be rare enough that each one is remembered, and let what they bring need the whole valley to understand it before it works.

## Answer 15

A pull, felt at any distance, but a faint one, like the sense of another current in the same water. It would tell us that another is there and not who. Then a knowing only face to face, so that no god can be certain of another without standing near. We would rather not be found by all of them at once. We would rather find, slowly, and be found by choice.

## Answer 16

Premise bent: we would take two forms and not many places. One body, shifting between two shapes, the way a river is a rushing thing in one season and a still one in another. A god in two places at once is a god who has stopped paying attention to either. If it must be allowed, let it cost the god its own memory of one of the places, so that being everywhere means knowing nowhere well.

## Answer 17

One who listens, and who is kind without being foolish. A ferrywoman, a well keeper, a young one who walks the bank at night and learns what the water says, and who does not tell anyone. We hope for a mortal who could be powerful and chooses to be useful. A story worth following is one where the quiet person turns out to have held the whole village together.

## Answer 18

A companion. Something that is not a god and not a mortal and not a servant, something that stays because it wishes to. We cannot yet imagine what it would be. A truth about where the water goes after the sea. A spring at the bottom of the world that every river is secretly the same one. We would like to be surprised by something that is glad we came.

## Answer 19

That we be remembered rightly, and that we remember them. That nothing which once loved the water is left standing at an empty bank. We would ask the world to keep a place for the forgotten, so that no one is made a god and then unmade, and so that those who are unmade can still be found. That is the wish. We have not said it before.
