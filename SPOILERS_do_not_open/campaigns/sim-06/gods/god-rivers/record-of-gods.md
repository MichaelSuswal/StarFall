# Record of other gods: god-rivers

Written at the opening of Year 4. Everything here comes from our own three years and nothing from elsewhere. Our beliefs may be wrong.

## Others like us
We have met none of them and felt none, three full years on. No weight in the air at close quarters, and no pull in the water.

What we did to look this year: we stood in the larger river to the knee each dusk and each dawn on the barge journey, ninety miles down from the ford, and felt no pull. At the ford we touched the water often over a pair of days, and listened once for about an hour. The Keeper ruled that we felt no pull because there was none to feel, and that the listening tells what is and never why. Nothing like ourselves was in that water, for twenty miles up and down. We hold that as stated.

What we make of it, and we hold it loosely: on the days we stood in it, as far as the pull reaches, no other god stood in or on this river, its feeders or the larger river down to about ninety miles below the ford. That is all it says. It says nothing of other rivers, of a god who stood in this water on a day we did not, or of one who does not go into water. The pull needs both of us in the water. An hour's silence is an hour's silence and not a finding. We have not been within a few paces of any other, so the knowing at close quarters has not come either.

## The star
The blue star in the north west dimmed on three nights in Year 1, five in Year 2, and six in Year 3: one in the first month, a pair in the fifth, a pair in the seventh and one in the eighth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Our people count fourteen such nights since we came. The count rises each year, three, five, six. Some of our people tie the nights to us.

We did nothing on those nights that we know of. This year we worked no Calling the water, and we used Warm hands only as asked. We keep the notched cord of the dim nights with where we stood and what we had done on the day before, and we read nothing into it yet. We do not know what dims the star. It may be nothing to do with any god, it may be the doing of one of our kind, or of something older than us. A rising count is a thing to write down and not a thing to explain. We do not let the town's talk become our belief, and we do not let our wish for company turn a dim star into a god.

## What the carriers and the reed cutters said
Last year the salt carriers said others go a southern way and named no place. This year the reed cutters in the low country said the salt for the stone towns now comes up from the sea in the south and does not need the ford. They named no place. This is not a god and we do not take it for one. A road that moves because of the sea is a plain reason, and a plainer one than a god. We set it here only because we have set the earlier word here. We have no ground to say more.

## The boat people
At dusk on the second evening in the low country a band of the boat people traded reed work for a knife and answered nothing about themselves. We felt no weight in the air. We take them to be mortals who keep their own counsel, and not one of our kind. We note it because they answered nothing, and a being who answers nothing is not for that reason a god.

## What we owe, and what is owed us
No promise has been made to us by any like us. No grievance, no debt, no trust given or taken. We trust them by default, as before, and hope to be liked by them, and we know that is how we are and not what we have learned. We would be wrong about one of them more often than a careful god would be, and we should remember that before we go toward any of them. The ford keeper asked "What is another like yourself?" and we have not yet answered. We do not know, and we will say so.

## What we would do if one came
Unchanged. Meet them in the open if we can, in the woman's shape, by a hearth and not a ford. Tell them plainly that the stone is the root of our hand on the water, only if we judge them to be one who asks no harm of us, and not at first. We are quick to give and slow to learn from being cheated, and the stone is the one thing that anyone could take from us. Our face does not stay with mortals, and a god at close quarters may know us by it better than they do; we do not count on being forgotten.
