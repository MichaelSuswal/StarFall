# Record of other gods: god-rivers

Written at the opening of Year 3. Everything here comes from our own two years and nothing from elsewhere. Our beliefs may be wrong.

## Others like us
We have met none of them and felt none, two full years on. No weight in the air at close quarters, and no pull in the water.

What we did to look this year: on the fifth morning at the ford where the brook meets the larger river we stood in the larger river to the knee until the sun cleared the reeds, and felt no pull. We stood in the water at the reed hamlets and the ford on the barge journey, and at the ford for about six days, touching it often. In late summer we knelt in the running water below the gate for about an hour with three watchers, and the water told us of no pull in this river. Last year we did the same at the spring head and below the gate. The Keeper told us in the Year 2 release that we felt nothing, and we hold that as stated.

What we make of it, and we hold it loosely: as far as we have stood in this river, its feeders and the larger river at the ford, on the days we stood, no other god stood in or on the water. That is all it says. It says nothing of other rivers, of a god who stood in this water on a day we did not, or of one who does not go into water. The pull needs both of us in the water. An hour's silence is an hour's silence and not a finding. We have not been within a few paces of any other, so the knowing at close quarters has not come either.

## The star
The blue star in the north-west dimmed on three nights in Year 1 and on five in Year 2: one in the second month, one in the sixth, one in the ninth, one in the eleventh and one in the twelfth. Each time the blue deepened first, the star stood dim about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. It is more than last year. Some of our people tie the nights to us.

We did nothing on those nights that we know of. We worked no Calling the water this year, and we used Warm hands twice (a hamlet man with a fevered foot, a young mother after a hard birth) and listened once, for about an hour, in late summer. We cannot say for certain whether the sixth or the ninth month fell on the days we were at the ford or kneeling below the gate, because we did not mark the nights against our days. We mean to keep a notched cord this year, one notch for each dim night, beside a plain word for where we stood and what we had done. We do not know what dims the star. It may be nothing to do with any god, it may be the doing of one of our kind, or of something older than us. Five nights in a year against three is a thing to write down and not a thing to explain. We do not let the town's talk become our belief, and we do not let our own wish for company turn a dim star into a god.

## What the salt carriers said
At the ford the carriers who crossed told us that others now go a southern way. They named no place. This is not a god and we do not take it for one, but a road that moves because of a river, or because of a person, is a thing a god might do or might feel, and so we set it here beside the star. We have no ground to say any more.

## What we owe, and what is owed us
No promise has been made to us by any like us. No grievance, no debt, no trust given or taken. We trust them by default, as before, and hope to be liked by them, and we know that is how we are and not what we have learned. We would be wrong about one of them more often than a careful god would be, and we should remember that before we go toward any of them.

## What we would do if one came
Unchanged. Meet them in the open if we can, in the woman's shape, by a hearth and not a ford. Tell them plainly that the stone is the root of our hand on the water, only if we judge them to be one who asks no harm of us, and not at first. We are quick to give and slow to learn from being cheated, and the stone is the one thing that anyone could take from us. Our face does not stay with mortals, and a god at close quarters may know us by it better than they do; we do not count on being forgotten.
