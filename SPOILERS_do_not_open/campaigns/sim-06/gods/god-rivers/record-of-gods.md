# Record of other gods: god-rivers

Written at the opening of Year 5. Everything here comes from our own four years and nothing from elsewhere. Our beliefs may be wrong.

## Others like us
We have met none of them and felt none, four full years on. No weight in the air at close quarters, and no pull in the water.

What we did to look this year: we stood in the larger river to the knee each dusk and each dawn on the barge journey, five days and 133.6 miles down from the ford to where the water turned salt in the delta, and felt no pull. The Keeper ruled that we felt none. At the delta we listened once, and within twenty miles of that place there was salt water and fresh meeting, the channels' ordinary life, boats of the boat people, and nothing like ourselves. Nothing came against our sleep.

What we make of it, and we hold it loosely: on the days we stood in it, as far as the pull reaches, no other god stood in or on this river, its feeders or the larger river down to its mouth. That is all it says. It says nothing of other rivers, of a god who stood in this water on a day we did not, or of one who does not go into water. The pull needs both of us in the water. We have not been within a few paces of any other, so the knowing at close quarters has not come either.

## The star
The blue star in the north west dimmed on three nights in Year 1, five in Year 2, six in Year 3, and seven in Year 4: in the second, third, fourth, eighth, tenth, eleventh and twelfth months of the last. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim for about an hour in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. Our people count twenty-one such nights since we came. The count rises each year, three, five, six, seven, and it does not rise by ones.

What the town has said of it: the Wary say it dims when we stay, the ferry keepers say it dimmed on the climb, the Counters say the cord is the town's. The Reckoner counted across two years of marks that we were away about one night in fifteen, and that the star dimmed while we were away on two of thirteen nights, and said it is not nothing and not something. We agree with the Reckoner. The eldest midwife said the star's pains come faster and it has not borne, which is a midwife's way of saying it, and we write it down because it is a thing said and not because we know what it means.

We did nothing on those nights that we know of. We worked no Calling the water in Year 4, and we used Warm hands once, in the tenth month, on a child of the lower town. We do not know what dims the star. It may be nothing to do with any god, it may be the doing of one of our kind, or of something older than us. The head of the oldest kin house asked whether the star is ours. We have not answered. Our true answer is that we do not know, that we do not do it, and that we would tell them if we did. A rising count is a thing to write down and not a thing to explain. We do not let the town's talk become our belief, and we do not let our wish for company turn a dim star into a god.

## What the carriers and the reed cutters said
Two years ago the salt carriers said others go a southern way and named no place. Last year the reed cutters in the low country said the salt for the stone towns comes up from the sea in the south and does not need the ford. This year the reed cutters of the lower channels said more: the salt for the stone towns is landed at a harbor far to the west and goes up to the stone towns' south town by land. No salt comes up the larger river. This is not a god and we do not take it for one. A road that moves because of the sea is a plain reason, and a plainer one than a god. This year the town reports the carriers came less often again and asked more, and salt is out. That fits the reed cutters and needs no one of our kind.

## The boat people
At the delta this year we met a band of the boat people again at the water's edge. We felt no weight in the air and nothing came against our sleep. We take them to be mortals who keep their own counsel. A being who answers nothing is not for that reason a god.

## What we owe, and what is owed us
No promise has been made to us by any like us. No grievance, no debt, no trust given or taken. We trust them by default, as before, and hope to be liked by them, and we know that is how we are and not what we have learned. We would be wrong about one of them more often than a careful god would be, and we should remember that before we go toward any of them. The ford keeper's question "What is another like yourself?" was answered at the ford last summer, that we do not know, that we would know one at close quarters by a weight in the air and not before, and that we would seat one at a hearth and ask its news after it had eaten.

## What we would do if one came
Unchanged. Meet them in the open if we can, in the woman's shape, by a hearth and not a ford. Tell them plainly that the stone is the root of our hand on the water, only if we judge them to be one who asks no harm of us, and not at first. We are quick to give and slow to learn from being cheated, and the stone is the one thing that anyone could take from us. Our face does not stay with mortals, and a god at close quarters may know us by it better than they do; we do not count on being forgotten.
