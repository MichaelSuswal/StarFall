# Record of other gods: god-rivers

Written at the opening of Year 2. Everything here comes from our own year and nothing from elsewhere. Our beliefs may be wrong.

## Others like us
We have met none of them and felt none, a full year on.

What we have done to look: we knelt in the stream at the spring head in the hour before light and listened, and stood in the running water as we did it. We felt no pull. In autumn we listened half a day in the running water below the gate with three watchers by us. We felt no pull. We have stood in water and touched it many times besides, at the ford, in the channels and at the hamlets, and felt nothing there either. We have not been close to any other like us, and so the weight in the air that tells one of our kind at close quarters has not come to us once.

What we make of it, and we hold it loosely: no other god stands or lies in or on this river or its feeders as far as we have touched it, at least not on the days we stood in it. That says nothing of the rest of the country, of other rivers, or of a god who stood in this water on a day we were not in it. The pull needs us in the water and it needs the other in the water, so an hour's silence is an hour's silence and not a finding.

## The star
The blue star in the north-west dimmed on three nights this year, two in the first month and one in the fifth, each time for about an hour in the middle of the night, the blue deepening first. The people have seen such nights in other years, and this year many of them tied the nights to us.

We did nothing on those nights that we know of. We have not worked the water since we came, and we were awake and in the town or in the hills for the ones we can place. We do not know what dims the star. It may be nothing to do with any god, or it may be the doing of one of our kind, or of something older than us. We have no ground for any of those. We write it down so that we do not forget we do not know, and so that we do not let the town's talk become our belief. If the nights come again we will note which night and what we were doing, and whether any one of them fell on a day we stood in water.

## What we owe, and what is owed us
No promise has been made to us by any like us. No grievance, no debt, no trust given or taken. We trust them by default, as before, and hope to be liked by them, and we know that is how we are and not what we have learned. We would be wrong about one of them more often than a careful god would be, and we should remember that before we go toward any of them.

## What we would do if one came
Meet them in the open if we can, in the woman's shape, by a hearth and not a ford. Tell them plainly that the stone is the root of our hand on the water, only if we judge them to be one who asks no harm of us, and not at first. We have written that to ourselves because we are quick to give and slow to learn from being cheated, and the stone is the one thing that anyone could take from us.
