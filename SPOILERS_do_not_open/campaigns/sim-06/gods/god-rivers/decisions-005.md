# Decisions: god-rivers, Year 5

Year 5 runs from the turn of the year, through our waking season, spring, and round the four seasons to spring again. We begin it at the spring head, where we woke at the thaw. The voice is ours: we, the Lady of the Ford, or only the river. We command no one. Each office holder who has heard us and taken our word is a helper only so far as they choose.

## Actions

1. **Spring, in the first days after the thaw: walk down the stream path to the town, barefoot, in the woman's shape, and sit at the council to answer what stands unanswered.** To the weirs' people and the head of the oldest kin house, who asked whether the star dims for her and whether it is ours, we say: we do not know what dims it, we do not do it that we know, and we would say so if we did. We say we were away about one night in fifteen of the two years, as the Reckoner counted, and that we agree with her that it is not nothing and not something. To the head of the oldest kin house, who asked what we want, we say: we want them fed, the children not drowned and the grandmothers not buried in a bad year. We want a council that can say no to us, and it has. We want to find others like us slowly and by choice, and we have found none. We say nothing of any working through water. We ask the Seat Warden to keep the cord of it as she keeps the rest. Our reason: three questions have stood unanswered a year, and a guest who does not answer teaches the town that a guest does not answer.

2. **Spring, at the spring flood and the choosing of the Speaker: read the flood and the gap with the Ferry Elder and the weir hands, and tell the council what we think and no more.** We ask the Ferry Elder to read the flood mark with us and say whether the deep water moved again at the ford stones. We ask the head of the weir hands, through the Ferry Elder, to measure the bed behind the opened oldest weir with their own pole at the flood's fall, and the low-water cord at the gate stone and the ford stone, and to set it beside last year's. We ask the Reckoner to keep it on the second cord. We say plainly that two things going together are two things, that the weir hands' own pole is theirs and the finding is theirs, and that whether to leave the gap open, close it, or open another is the council's to say, and the head of the oldest kin house's to say for his house's weir. We counsel waiting for the flood to be read before any weir is unset or reset. We ask for no weir to be unset on our word. Our reason: the town's own hands made the measure and should read the answer, and the eel it cost the town is paid for by cord and should not be paid twice for a guess.

3. **Late spring and summer: walk the pasture with the head herd keeper and the herd keepers, one dusk to dawn at the dark of the moon, and again in the autumn dark moon if they ask.** The head herd keeper said the offer stands, one more pair of feet. We take it, and say that we do not tire and do not sleep, that we will count heads at dusk and at dawn beside them, and that they may say no at any time. We ask the Seat Warden to carry our word to them if they have not heard it. We do not hunt a loss we are not sent for. Our reason: the herd lost goats and cattle in the reed, it is nearly back to what it was, and we count our own and cannot rest while one is not counted. We would rather be asked and walk than not be asked and watch.

4. **Summer, in the dry weeks, with the council's leave: go with the salt carriers on their road toward the south town of the stone towns, ten days' walk out at most, and turn.** We ask the council for leave first and tell it how long we mean to be away and the day we turn, home before the autumn low water. We ask the Ferry Elder to name one ferry keeper and the Reckoner to name her cord walker to go with us, and we ask at the ford which carrier family will let us walk with them and pay them in the town's own coin or in work, not in anything of ours. We ask where the salt road runs, what the carriers pay at each stage, and whether the town could be a place the road comes to. We ask the same of whoever we meet, by hearth, after they have eaten. We take the stone in the pouch and show nothing, and we do not shift in anyone's sight. We stand in any running water we cross to the knee at dusk and dawn and watch for a pull, which the pull reaches only in water of this river. If a child is in the water or a person is hurt or fevered and asks us, Warm hands, and the cold is ours. We use no Calling the water. We do not listen on this walk. We tell the town before we go that while we are away no one acts for us and a question put to us waits. Our reason: salt is out, the town buys salt, and the council cannot weigh a ford it does not know the worth of against a salt road it has not seen.

5. **Autumn, at the low water: stand with the ferry keepers at the mark the council set, read it, and bring all of it to the council in one sitting.** We tell the council what the keepers and the weir hands measured, what the carriers and those on the salt road said, and what we saw, and we give counsel as counsel: whether the town wants the carriers' road to come back to it, whether a mark to read by, a second ferry or a letting go of the ford is worth what it costs, and the council may answer no. We name no one to do any of it, and we ask the Ferry Elder, the Reckoner or the keepers the council names, and not before. We give the council and the Reckoner the count of the dim nights on the notched cord, with where we stood each time. Our reason: the people's own habit of listening sooner is our best defence against hunger after a bad flood, and we are one voice at the fire among nine.

6. **Winter, and the turn of the year: stay in the town, sit at the council through the ice, count with the Reckoner and keep the second cord, and go back to the spring head alone in the thaw to mark the turn.** We tell stories at the fire, learn the new children's names, and sit by the eldest midwife for any child or mother at risk. We keep the cup at the low seat only as a guest's courtesy. We do not listen in frozen water. Our reason: we stay where we are wanted, and the town is held together by itself and by its own offices.

## What we tell our people to do, and through whom

We command no one. We ask, and the council or the person may refuse us at no cost.

- The Ferry Elder and the ferry keepers: read the flood marks and the low-water cord at the water gate stone and the ford; name one keeper to go with us on the salt road; carry word along the channels; tell us what the carriers say. Asked in person at the council and at the ford.
- The head of the weir hands, through the Ferry Elder: measure the bed behind the opened oldest weir and behind the others with their own pole at the flood's fall and again at the low water. The council may grant or refuse it, and the finding is theirs.
- The head of the oldest kin house: we ask nothing of him about his weir. We counsel that the flood be read before any weir is changed.
- The Reckoner: name a cord walker to go with us, keep the second cord of the marks, the measures and the dim nights, and say it back at council. Asked in person.
- The Seat Warden: keep the cord of what we say and do at each sitting and recite it back; carry our word to the herd keepers. Asked in person.
- The head herd keeper and the herd keepers, through the Seat Warden: we take their offer of one more pair of feet, and they may say no. Asked in person at the pasture.
- The well keepers: tell us and the council at once if a spring turns. The eldest midwife and the midwives: tell us of any child or mother at risk.

Where we are away, no one acts for us, and we ask no one to. A question put to us then waits. We tell the council before we go and when we will come back.

## Answers to what was asked of us

The weirs' people's and the head of the oldest kin house's questions about the star, and what we want, are answered in action 1. The herd keeper's standing offer is answered in action 3.

## Messages

None. We send no message to anyone not in front of us this year. We know of no one to send to.

## For the Keeper to rule

Nothing is asked beyond what our card plainly covers. We state our reading so it may be corrected:

1. Unsigned: in action 1 we say we do not know what dims the star and that we do not do it that we know. We read this as our own words about our own knowledge and wish, and not a claim of any working through water. The weir counsel in action 2 is our own reasoning said at council and not a claim of any working.
2. The pull in the water: we read it as working only while we stand in or touch running water of the same river, its feeders and outflow. On the salt road we will cross water that may not be of this river, and we do not expect to feel a pull there. If any water we stand in is of this river and a pull is there, we ask the Keeper to tell us in the next release.
3. Warm hands: if asked on the road, the cold is counted in the days nature would have taken, as before.
4. We try to grow no power this year.
