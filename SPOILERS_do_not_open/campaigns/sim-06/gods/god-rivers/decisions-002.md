# Decisions: god-rivers, Year 2

Year 2 runs from the turn of the year, through our waking season, spring, and round the four seasons to spring again. We begin it at the spring head, where we went back to at the thaw. The voice is ours: we, the Lady of the Ford, or only the river.

## Actions

1. **Spring, in the first days after the thaw: walk down the path to the town, barefoot, in the woman's shape, and ask the Ferry Elder and the Speaker for a hearing at the council about the ford.** We go by the path, stooped, in the grey wrap and hemp cord, with the stone in the pouch, and sit at the guest bench beside the low seat, not on it. We tell the council what the ferry keepers and the salt carriers have told us and what the water told us in autumn: the deep water has lain farther from the ford after four floods, and the carriers cross less often. We say plainly that we do not know why and that we are asking, not ordering. We ask the Ferry Elder to have the ferry keepers cut the flood marks again this year and to add a second cord of marks: the depth at the ford in low water in each season, taken at the same stone, so that the town has its own count of the ford and does not rest on ours. Our reason: a town that sees its own ford move will decide better than one that is told, and our counsel is worth what the council takes of it.

2. **Spring, before the first market: answer the children, and speak to the Seat Warden.** We sit with the children at the fire and tell them plainly: we woke at the spring head up the stream, in the thaw, and before that we do not remember. We tell it as what we know and not as a story, and we tell them we do not know why we came. We say nothing of the water's work, and nothing is asked that our tongue forbids. To the Seat Warden we say that we will not ask again whether the cup waited for us, that the cup is hers and the town's to keep as it likes, and that we thank her for the first hearth. Our reason: they asked twice, and the first time we told a story, and we would rather be known for a plain answer than a pretty one.

3. **Summer: go with the ferry keepers by barge and on foot down the channels to the ford where the brook meets the larger river, and see it with our own eyes.** We ask the Ferry Elder to name two ferry keepers to go with us, and the Reckoner to name one to walk with us and carry the second cord, since we are counting along the way. We take the stone, and we leave it on the bank in a keeper's keeping only if we go into the water in the water shape, which we do not mean to do in anyone's sight. We ask the salt carriers at the ford what they have seen of the larger river's bed, its floods and its crossings, and where they go instead when they cross less. We stand in the larger river, in the woman's shape, up to the knee, and watch for a pull. We ask at each reed hamlet on the way, as before, for its news and its count, and we sit at each hearth. Warm hands if we are asked, and the cold is ours. We use no Calling the water. Our reason: we have heard of the ford and the larger river twice from the water and many times from the keepers and we have not seen it, and the town's trouble is there.

4. **Late summer, in the low water, at the ford below the gate: listen, for a short time, with three watchers.** We ask the Seat Warden, a ferry keeper and a well keeper, as in autumn, to sit by us while we kneel in the running water below the gate and listen for about an hour and no more, and to keep a fire, a mat and the dogs off through the sleep that follows, about two hours. We ask what the water says of the deep water and the bed upstream of the ford, of the salt carriers' crossings, of what comes down from the hills, and whether any pull stands in this river. We know that the water tells what is and never why, so we judge what we are told and say so at council. We do not listen longer than this, because a long listening has cost us a day and a night of helpless sleep and we would not make the town stand watch more than it must. Our reason: low water shows the bed's shape when the river is quiet, and we would know it before the autumn flood.

5. **Autumn, after the harvest: bring what we have learned to the council in one sitting, and ask nothing but that they hear it.** We tell what the keepers measured, what the carriers said, what we saw at the ford and what the water told us, and we give our counsel: whatever the council judges the ford needs, whether a new crossing, a second ferry, a mark to read by, we say it as counsel and the council may answer no. We name no one to do any of it. If the council takes a thing up, we ask the Ferry Elder, the Reckoner or the ferry keepers whom the council names, and not before. We ask the well keepers to tell us and the council at once if any spring turns, as last year, and the mill and weir hands to tell the council when the water is wrong. Our reason: the people's own habit of listening sooner is our best defence against the hunger after a bad flood, and we would be one voice at the fire among nine.

6. **Winter, and the turn of the year: stay in the town, sit at the council through the ice, count again, and be at the spring flood and the choosing of the Speaker.** We tell stories at the fire, learn the new children's names, count with the Reckoner and keep the second cord. We do not listen in frozen water. We go back to the spring head in the thaw, alone, in the woman's shape, to mark the turn. Our reason: we stay where we are wanted and no one needs to be asked to hold the town together for us.

## What we tell our people to do, and through whom

We command no one. Each office holder who has heard us and taken our word is a helper only so far as they choose. We ask, and the council or the person may refuse us at no cost.

- The Ferry Elder and the ferry keepers: cut the flood marks and the low-water marks at the water gate stone and at the ford, carry word along the channels, and tell us what the carriers say. Asked in person, at the council and at the ford.
- The Reckoner: walk with us and the second cord in the count, and say it back at council. Asked in person.
- The Seat Warden: keep the cord of what we say and do at each sitting and recite it back, and sit watch at the autumn listening if she is willing.
- The well keepers: tell us and the council at once if a spring turns, and sit watch if willing.
- The midwives: tell us of any child or mother at risk.
- The mill and weir hands: tell the council when the water is wrong.

Where we are away, no one acts for us, and we ask no one to.

## Answers to what was asked of us

The children's question, where we come from, is answered in action 2. The Seat Warden's silence on whether the cup waited for us is answered in action 2, as a letting go.

## Messages

None. We send no message to anyone not in front of us this year. We know of no one to send to.

## For the Keeper to rule

Nothing is asked beyond what our card plainly covers. We state our reading so it may be corrected:

1. Listening water: a listening of about an hour, with the sleep twice that, at the ford below the gate. We read it as within a day's walk of the water up and down, feeders included, and as telling what is, never why.
2. The pull in the water: we read it as working only while we stand in or touch running water of the same river, and we will stand in the larger river at the ford for that. We ask that the Keeper tell us in the next release if we felt one.
3. Unsigned: in action 2 we tell the children where we woke, with our own body, which we read as not a claim of a working through water. We state that we make no claim of any working in what we tell them.
4. We try to grow no power this year.
