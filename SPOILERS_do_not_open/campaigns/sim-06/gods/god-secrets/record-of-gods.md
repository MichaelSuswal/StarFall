# Record of other gods

## Others like me
Others like me came down when I did. In two full years among the walls, on the south-west track, and on the river to its mouth and back, I have not once felt the weight in the air that tells me one stands near. None has come through the gate, sat in the market, crossed my path on the track, or stood on a landing at the mouth. I hold no trust, no grievance, no debt and no promise with any of them. I do not know how many there are, where they stand, or what they want.

At the river's mouth I asked, as a trader asks, who keeps the water below the town and whether anything strange moves on it. I heard of no one and of nothing strange. The coasting crews name a harbor town along the coast to the west, as my own people already tell it. That tells me there are people there. It tells me nothing of whether one like me is among them. I do not guess.

## What the sky showed
The star dimmed on three nights in my first year and on five in my second: in the second month, the sixth, the ninth, the eleventh and the twelfth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. The town counts eight such nights since I came and many tie them to me. I was inside the walls for all five of this year's, and for all three of last year's. I did nothing on any of those nights that I know of.

My guess, held loosely: it is some doing of one like me, or of the world, and the town points at the stranger because the stranger is the newest thing in it. That it came more often in my second year than my first is a fact I note and do not read. If it is one like me, that one does something large and does it more often, and I would sooner know that one than guess at it. If I am asked, I say what I said to the old woman at the tanners' end, that I do not know, because it is true and costs me nothing. I take neither credit nor blame.

## What I will do with it
I enter nothing about any god I have not felt. If the weight settles in some room, I will write here what I see of that one and no more, and I will price any promise by the one who makes it, not by the promise. I do not go looking for one. I would be a coward in that meeting and I know it, and I would sooner be found at my own table than find one on the road.
