# Record of other gods

## Others like me
In three full years among the walls, on the south-west track, on the river to its mouth and back, and up the river to its head, I have not once felt the weight in the air that tells me one stands near. None has come through the gate, sat in the market, crossed my path, or stood on a landing. I hold no trust, no grievance, no debt and no promise with any of them. I do not know how many there are, where they stand, or what they want.

## What I was told, and by whom
At the boat families' landing at the river's head, among the drovers who come down from the grass country, I heard a tale. The pass herders beyond the plain have had a guest at their winter hall for three winters: a woman with a staff and a key, whom the whole people feed and call Guest, and whom no one has seen eat. The drovers themselves think it a herders' tale and tell it for the telling. I heard it from drovers, who had it from herders or from others who had it from herders. It is hearsay at two or three removes, from people with no reason to lie and no reason to be exact.

My guess, held loosely: it may be one like me, or a herders' tale grown from a traveller who was fed and not seen at table. A woman who is not seen to eat is what a god is, if she is one, and also what a tale makes of any quiet guest. A staff and a key are things a tale would add. I note that I carry a key, and that the tale has one, and I do not read more into that than a coincidence a drover could make up. I did not go to the hall. I turned the key on nothing. I hold her as unfelt and unmet, and I enter no trust or grievance.

## What the sky showed
The star dimmed on three nights in my first year, five in my second, and six in my third: one in the first month, a pair in the fifth, a pair in the seventh and one in the eighth. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. The town counts fourteen such nights since I came and many tie them to me. A girl of the boat families asked me outright whether I make the star go dim, and I said I do not know. I do not.

The count rises each year, three, five, six. That is a fact I note. If it is one like me, that one does something large and does it more often, and I would sooner know that one than guess at it. I take neither credit nor blame.

## What I will do with it
I enter nothing as fact about any god I have not felt. If the weight settles in some room, I will write here what I see of that one and no more, and I will price any promise by the one who makes it, not by the promise. I do not go looking for one at a hall door. I would be a coward in that meeting and I know it. If I go up the drovers' way, it is to see the ground and hear more, and I turn back before I stand in a room I have not looked at.
