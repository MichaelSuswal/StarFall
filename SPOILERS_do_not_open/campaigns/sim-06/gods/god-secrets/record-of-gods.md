# Record of other gods

## Others like me
In four full years among the walls, on the south-west track, on the river to its mouth and back, up the river to its head, and out on the plain with drovers, I have not once felt the weight in the air that tells me one stands near. None has come through the gate, sat in the market, crossed my path, or stood on a landing. I hold no trust, no grievance, no debt and no promise with any of them. I do not know how many there are, where they stand, or what they want.

## What I was told, and by whom
The pass herders beyond the plain have had a guest at their winter hall for some winters: a woman with a staff and a key, whom the whole people feed and call Guest, and whom no one has seen eat. I first heard it at the boat families' landing at the river's head, from drovers. This year I went out onto the plain with drovers and heard it again, told the same way. No one I met had stood nearer the pass than the drovers of last year. It is hearsay at two or three removes, told by people with no reason to lie and no reason to be exact, and the sameness of the telling across two years is not a second source. It may only mean the tale has one root and travels well.

My guess, held loosely: it may be one like me, or a herders' tale grown from a traveller who was fed and not seen at table. A staff and a key are things a tale would add. I carry a key and I do not read more into that than a coincidence. I did not come within many days of the pass. I hold her as unfelt and unmet, and I enter no trust or grievance.

## What the sky showed
The star dimmed on three nights in my first year, five in my second, six in my third, and seven in my fourth: in the second, third, fourth, eighth, tenth, eleventh and twelfth months. Each time the blue deepened for about an hour, the star stood dim in the middle of the night, and then it was as before. The town counts twenty-one such nights since I came and many tie them to me. Two of this year's nights fell while I was out on the plain, and I felt no weight in the air on either. I did nothing on any of them that I know of.

The count rises each year: three, five, six, seven. That is a fact I note. If it is one like me, that one does something large and does it more often. That I was far from the town on two of the nights and the star dimmed anyway is a small thing in my favor with the town, if the town counts it, and I will not press it. I take neither credit nor blame.

## What I will do with it
I enter nothing as fact about any god I have not felt. If the weight settles in some room, I will write here what I see of that one and no more, and I will price any promise by the one who makes it, not by the promise. I do not go looking for one at a hall door. I would be a coward in that meeting and I know it.
