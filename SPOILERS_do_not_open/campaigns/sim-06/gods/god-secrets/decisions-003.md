# Decisions, Year 3: god-secrets

I am the stranger the town calls the Counter, with a small empty ring for a mark. I act as the man throughout and I do not change shape this year unless something I cannot foresee forces it. The key stays on its cord at my neck and the tally stick in my coat. I sit at the foot of the long table on the stores, and I am not on the rolls. I use the key on nothing in the town.

1. Autumn, the harvest month, in the granary yard. I go first to the Granary Warden, with the Clerk of the Tally standing by if the Warden wishes it, and I put one proposal to him and no blame. The far bins are hollow behind their facing, the near bins are full, and he and the Clerk and I have all seen it. I ask him to carry the new harvest in and count it aloud at the door as the first sacks go in, as last year, and to fill the far bins first, by the sack, with a tally-keeper from each of the two families who keep that end standing at the bin and cutting their own stick as every sack goes by. I ask the Clerk to set those sticks beside the old roll. I ask the Warden to bring the gap to the council himself, in his own words, before the Clerk and before me. I say to him plainly that I do not ask where the grain went and I will not guess, and that a store that is named short in the autumn can be mended by the winter. I say I will carry no figure of my own to the council on this. I carry his or I carry none. I do this because a loss he is allowed to name and mend is a loss he will go on finding, and a loss I name for him is one he will hide.

2. Autumn, at the first sitting of the council after the harvest is in. I ask the Speaker, through the Clerk, that the two families who keep the far end be heard at the table before any word of blame is spoken, and I ask the Crier to cry the closing count at market as the Granary Warden's count, with the figure for the far bins given by itself. If a headman asks me at the table what I make of the gap, I say what the sticks say and no more, and I say whose sticks they are. If the table turns to blame, I say that a town that hangs a man for a short bin will not be told the next one is short, and I leave the table if it goes on. I do this because I call it prudence, and it is also true that I would sooner the town keep the Warden and lose a little grain than keep the grain and lose the Warden. If anyone asks me again whether I am the one who dims the star, I answer as before, that I do not know.

3. Winter, after the count is cried. I stay inside the walls and talk. I sit at the granary, the gate and the market bench and I let long conversation do what it does. I keep the one kept thing as I keep it and I take no new one I would not be glad to carry. If a teller begins a telling I do not wish to hold, I say so before it is done. I do not press the reed cutter or anyone else to finish what was not finished. If the far bins come up in talk, I answer what I know of the count and nothing of what I cannot know. I do not ask the Gate Warden for anything. I let the watch's rations stand as the figure I gave him, and if he comes to me with another question I answer it.

4. Winter, once the harvest is in and the Clerk has had his season of quiet. I go to the Clerk of the Tally, over a cup and as idle talk, and I ask once more whether I might sit an evening with the old league's tallies in his house and count the notches and not the meaning, on his terms. I say again that I do not ask for the list of names and I will not ask for it, and that if he wishes me to leave at any hour I leave. If he says no, I say that is his to say, and I do not ask again this year. If he says yes, I look only as he allows, and I do not copy a notch without his hand on the stick.

5. Spring, when the river opens, at the river gate. The Clerk has the depths on sticks from the crews. I ask the Boat Master for the first loaded boat that comes to the gate and I ask the crews to sound the channel again at low water and at high, as I did, so the sticks hold two springs. I ask the Clerk to set this spring's depths beside last spring's and the two groundings' cost beside the dredging's, so that when the council argues dredging it argues from the two springs and not from my one figure. I say only what the sticks say. I do not go into the cellars and I do not use the key on any of them. The cellars are the people's.

6. Summer, for about a month. I go up the river this time, not down it. I ask the Boat Master for a berth as a weigher and counter on a crew going upriver, paid in scrap silver for passage and keep, and I go as the man with the key at my neck and the tally stick in my coat. I go to see what lies above the town on the water, who keeps it, who trades on it and what comes down it, and whether any stranger there is more than a stranger. I do not search any hidden place in anyone's town. I use the key on open ground only and only if I have a reason that would pass at the table. I tell the Clerk of the Tally only that I mean to be back before the harvest and leave nothing in his hands but that. I come back before the harvest of the fourth year and look first at the store, and I take it as it stands.

## Messages

None. I send no word to anyone out of my sight this year. I have no one to send it to, and no one who knows my name.

## For the Keeper to rule

1. The sky. The star dimmed on five nights and many in the town tie it to me. I did nothing I know of on any of those nights. If the town asks me, I say I do not know, which is true. I ask for no ruling and I note it so the Keeper knows what I will say.

I do not try to grow any power this year.
