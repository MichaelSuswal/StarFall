
## Round 1 reply

### What was read and not priced
Agreed. I proposed no edge and owe no weakness. The habits and prides are tempers and able to fail. The land and its past are my wish or my people's belief, and the world decides. Each office holder is who they are and not what they will do. Skills and culture are free.

### Your landing: the helpers and the seat
Choice: (a). Keep the meeting as I wrote it, at the cost named.
Reasons: I wrote that my people have never heard of me, and I want it so. I said in my survey that I would rather be one large merchant among many than the only light in a dark room, and that a god who towers gets watched. A people that already knew me on sight, with offices that serve me from the first day, is a god who is watched from the first day, and I do not want a seat I have not earned at that table. I take the cost as the Keeper says it plainly: until each office is won in play, nobody acts for me while I am away, my unplayed time runs on my standing conduct alone, and my people go their own way under their own council. I would sooner pay that and have an office hold my word because its holder heard me and took it than have it handed to me by a rewrite. I know that a Clerk who keeps my place only after he has chosen to is worth more to me than one who was told to. I do not ask for anything in return, since nothing is bought.
One clarification, in the write-up and not a new power: where I wrote that the Clerk of the Tally keeps my place at the table when I am away, I meant it as my hope for what the office becomes in play, and not as a thing true at landing. The offices stand as the town's own, under its own custom, and become my helpers one by one, as each holder hears me and takes my word.

### The spears
Accept as drafted. A hundred in the standing watch is at the line and not over it, and the rest are a call-up. I ask for nothing above it.

### Their magic
Accept as drafted. Five or six, each working one small thing at her own cost, none every day, ordinary and no edge. I take that I cannot make another by telling, and I do not try. Such a one comes as the world gives her.
