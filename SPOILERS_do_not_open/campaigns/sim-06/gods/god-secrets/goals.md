# Goals

Held now, at the opening of the third year, in order of weight.

1. Let the far bins be named by the town and mended by the town. I walked the granary with the Warden and saw the near bins full to the mark and the far bins hollow, faced with sacks and empty behind. The Warden saw it with me and sent for the Clerk. The two families who keep that end say the roll is wrong. I do not know who emptied them, or when, or whether anyone did, and I do not ask. I want the Warden to keep his office and his face and bring the gap to the council in his own words. I want the two families heard before any word of blame, and the new harvest counted into the far bins by sack, with their own tally-keepers at the door, so that the next count cannot be argued. A town that learns the true size of its store in the autumn can feed itself through the winter. A town that learns it in the spring cannot.
2. Be heard by the offices one at a time. The Clerk took my counsel and still trusts me with the old tallies, but not yet. The Boat Master took one weighing and the crews sounded the channel. The Gate Warden took the cup, put his questions, took the watch's rations in one figure, and said he owed no man a favor. I took that as an honest thing to say and I ask him for nothing. I want his trust more than his liking, and I get it by doing him no harm over years. No office is mine until its holder has heard me and taken my word.
3. Stay one large merchant among many. Be useful in the books, say true things about them, and be asked last. Not be the only light in the room, and not be watched for my size. The town ties the dimmed star to me. I cannot mend that by speaking, and I will not feed it. I say I do not know, and I keep working at the books.
4. Hold what I hold. I carry one kept thing, told to me in the dead of a winter. It binds me entirely, I may not use it, and I will not bend toward it even where it would be easy. A reed cutter began a telling and at my word did not finish it. I do not press anyone to tell me more, and I leave the door as it was. Of twelve places I hold one, and I would keep it so.
5. Learn the ground further. I know the river down to its mouth and the coasting boats that work the shore both ways, and I know a harbor town lies along the coast to the west by their talk. I do not know what lies up the water above the town, who keeps it, or what comes down it. I want to see it with my own eyes and not by report.
6. Do not ask yet about a lost name. The Clerk has told me of an old league's tallies in a chest no one can read, and of a private list of names sung by walkers, twenty and some unlike any on the rolls. I asked once for an evening with the tallies. He asked why, heard me, and said he will not answer until the harvest is in. I wait for his answer. I claim nothing, I do not ask for the list, and if he offers it I look only as he allows. I do not yet know whether any name there is mine.
7. Be missed a little by someone when I am away. I was gone a month this year on the boats. At the mouth no one looked for me, and I did not expect them to. Whether the town looked for me I will know on the store first and the faces after. I do not count it as settled either way.
8. Do not use the key where the people live. Their hidings are closed to me, the granary among them, and I keep it that way. If a finding is wanted in the town, I do not do it.
