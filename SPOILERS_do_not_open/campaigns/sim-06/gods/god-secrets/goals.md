# Goals

Held now, after a year among them, in order of weight.

1. Keep the granary honest, and make it the town's own count and not mine. The count aloud came a little under a twentieth short of the Warden's book. The Warden said rats and damp. I would have him keep his office and his face, and have the loss named and mended by his own hand, so that the next count is cried without my help. A full store is still the best argument I know for a town staying together.
2. Be heard by the offices one at a time. The Clerk took my counsel, the Boat Master took one weighing, and the Gate Warden did not like my answers. A stool at the foot of the table on the stores, five to four, is what I have. I want the Gate Warden's trust more than his liking. No office is mine until its holder has heard me and taken my word, and I will not ask it of the Gate Warden before he has seen me do him no harm.
3. Answer the council's question about the name, plainly, when it is put to me again. I have none. The town may call me what it likes, and the ring and the scales are marks I let stand.
4. Hold what I hold. I carry one kept thing, told to me in the deep of the winter. It binds me entirely, I may not use it, and I will not bend toward it even where it would be easy. I do not press anyone to tell me more. Of twelve places I hold one, and I would keep it so.
5. Stay one large merchant among many. Be useful in the books, say true things about them, and be asked last. Not be the only light in the room, and not be watched for my size.
6. Learn the ground further. The south-west track ends on open grass and drovers' trails. I know the river only as a thing that carries word. I want to know what lies down the water, who keeps it, and what comes up it.
7. Ask nothing yet about a lost name. The Clerk has told me, over a cup, of an old league's tallies in a chest no one can read and of a private list of names sung by walkers, twenty and some unlike any on the rolls. I claim nothing and I do not ask to see the list. If the Clerk offers it, I will take it as a gift and look only as he allows. I do not yet know whether any name there is mine.
8. Be missed a little by someone when I am away. I was gone a fortnight in the summer and was looked for at the store when I came back. Whether that is being missed I do not yet know. I will know better in the second autumn.
9. Do not use the key where the people live. Their hidings are closed to me, and I keep it that way.
