# Decisions, Year 4: god-secrets

I am the stranger the town calls the Counter, with a small empty ring for a mark. I act as the man throughout and I do not change shape this year unless something I cannot foresee forces it. The key stays on its cord at my neck and the tally stick in my coat. I sit at the foot of the long table on the stores, and I am not on the rolls. I use the key on nothing in the town.

1. Autumn, the harvest month, in the granary yard. I go to the Granary Warden, with the Clerk of the Tally standing by if the Warden wishes it, and I ask nothing new of him. I ask only that the harvest be carried in and counted aloud at the door as the first sacks go in, that the far run be filled by the sack with a tally-keeper of each of the two families cutting a stick as every sack passes, and that the near bins be counted the same way, so that the whole store is squared from the first sack and not the last. I ask the Clerk to set this year's sticks beside last year's and the old roll. I ask the Warden to take the count to the council himself, and I ask the Crier to cry the closing count at market as the Warden's. I say nothing of last year's gap beyond what the sticks say, and I do not ask where the grain went. I do this because a count made the same way twice becomes the town's way, and then it no longer needs me to stand at the door.

2. Autumn, at the first sitting of the council after the harvest is in. I stay at the foot of the table and speak only if asked. If a headman asks me what I make of the new count against the old, I say what the sticks say and whose sticks they are. If the table turns again to blame for last year's far run, I say that a town that hangs a man for a short bin will not be told the next one is short, as I said before, and I leave the table if it goes on. If anyone asks again whether I am the one who dims the star, I answer as before, that I do not know. I do this because it is true.

3. Winter, once the count is cried and the Clerk has had his season of quiet. I go to the Clerk of the Tally in his house, over a cup and as idle talk, and I tell him plainly that in his evening with the old league's tallies I read the marks as their makers would have, and that one of them is a count of grain and salt owed by the old factory here to a salt house of the delta in the far west, never struck off. I ask him first whether he wishes me to say what the stick says, and I say it only if he does. If he does not, I say that is his to say and I do not raise it again this year. If he does, I give him the figures as the stick gives them, I say that I do not know whether the debt is live or the house still stands, and I say that what the town does about it is the council's and his and not mine. I carry no figure to the council myself. I do this because a book that its keeper opens is better than a book that is opened for him, and because I would sooner he hear it from me, privately, than learn I knew and said nothing.

4. Spring, when the river opens, at the river gate. I ask the Boat Master for the first loaded boat to the gate and ask the crews to sound the channel a third spring at low water and at high, as before. I ask the Clerk to set this spring's depths beside the last two, and the groundings' cost beside the dredging's, so that when the council argues dredging it argues from three springs. The channel was a little shallower at the gate last spring than the one before, and a third spring will show whether that is a trend or a chance. I say only what the sticks say. I do not go into the cellars and I do not use the key on any of them. The cellars are the people's.

5. Summer, for about five weeks. Before I go, I tell the Clerk of the Tally that I am going up the river again and on from the head, and that I mean to be back before the harvest, on or before a day I name to him, so that someone knows when I am due. I leave nothing in his hands but that. I ask the Boat Master for a berth as a weigher and counter on a crew going upriver, paid in scrap silver for passage and keep, and I go as the man. I go to the boat families' landing at the head of the river, and from there, as far along the drovers' track toward the grass country and the pass as a drover will take me, as a trader with his scales. I go to see the ground between the landing and the pass with my own eyes and to hear again, from a herder or a drover who has been nearer than the ones I met, what the pass herders' Guest is said to be. I do this because I want the ground, and because a tale that two drovers tell for the telling may be told differently by one who has stood in the herders' hall. I do not knock at the winter hall. If I come within sight of it, I stop, and I write down where the flinch stopped me and how far I was. If I feel the weight in the air, I go no nearer and I do not change my face, and I come down. I use the key on open ground only and only if I have a reason that would pass at the table. I do not search any hidden place in any people's town.

6. Late summer, on the way down. I come back by the river to the town and I look first at the store as it stands, and then at who looks up when I come through the land gate. I note it and I take it as it stands, as I did the year before. I go to the Clerk first and give him the day I came and the day I said, so that he can see whether I was late.

## Messages

None. I send no word to anyone out of my sight this year. I have no one to send it to, and no one who knows my name.

## Answers to what was asked

The reed cutter's daughter asked whether I would hear from her what her father began. I answered her in Year 3 and I stand by it: she is to keep it until she knows whether it is hers to give. If she asks me again, in front of me, I say the same and I do not press. I give no new words to anyone this year. Nothing else was put to me unanswered.

## For the Keeper to rule

1. The sky. The star dimmed on six nights and many in the town tie it to me. I did nothing I know of on any of those nights. If the town asks me, I say I do not know, which is true. I ask for no ruling and I note it so the Keeper knows what I will say.

2. Tally-Reader and a speaking of the account. The Keeper wrote on my card that for a day after I read a mark I cannot speak a false figure about that account. That day is long past. In action 3 I plan to tell the Clerk what the delta stick says. I read it by the power, and the figure is true as I read it. I state my case that this is plainly covered: it is speech of what I read, with no new use of the power. I ask for a ruling only if the Keeper thinks otherwise.

I do not try to grow any power this year.
