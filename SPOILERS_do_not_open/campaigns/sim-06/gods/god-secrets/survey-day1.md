## Answer 1

A market town at the hour the shutters come down. Lamps lit in some windows and not in others, and every door has a price on the other side of it. Warm, close, a little crowded, with more going on behind the walls than in the street. I would like a world where a person can lean on a neighbor's wall and be glad of it.

## Answer 2

River country, and I will take the town on the bend of it. Rivers carry goods and news, and they carry things people would rather not have carried, which suits me. Give me ground firm enough to wall and water close enough to trade on. Marsh at the edges, for those who need to be hard to find.

## Answer 3

Four seasons, honest ones, with a long mild autumn and a winter that is cold but not cruel. Winter is when people stay indoors and talk, and a town that talks all winter learns what it is. Spring should come late enough that the stores are tested. I will take the harvest. A full granary is the best argument I know for staying together.

I wake into autumn.

## Answer 4

Some of each, and I will pay for the mix. Wild country for the ones who need room, and old kingdoms rooted well enough that there is someone to trade with and someone to keep an eye on. A world with only the wild has no markets. A world with only kingdoms has no exits.

## Answer 5

Premise bent: thousands of years, but with the ledger torn. Ruins under ruins, yes, though most of the pages are missing, and what remains is argued over. I want old things that nobody can fully read. A buried record is the best kind of merchandise.

## Answer 6

Near the wise-woman end, but quiet about it. A few in every valley, never admitted to in daylight, never entirely gone. People should know a hedge-witch exists and pretend, at the market, that they do not. Magic that is half doubted keeps its secrets well.

## Answer 7

It should feel like a debt being taken on. A little warmth leaving, a little borrowed from tomorrow. The cost should be small each time and honest, a memory, a season of sleep, a rough patch in the voice. Nothing should be free, and nothing should be priced so high that a frightened mother cannot afford it once.

## Answer 8

Barely more than a great sorcerer. I would rather be one large merchant among many than the only light in a dark room. A god who towers gets watched. A god who can be mistaken for a clever stranger gets to keep his own counsel.

## Answer 9

Familiar animals, a good many legends, and a handful of creatures no one has a name for, kept to the far wild. A strange thing should be rare enough that seeing it is a story, and common enough that a traveler can swear he has.

## Answer 10

The thing that does not announce itself. A sickness in the grain, a rumor that outruns its truth, a neighbor who turns out to have been counting your door for years. Fear of what is hidden makes better stories than fear of what is loud. I would also keep a cold winter, for honesty's sake.

## Answer 11

Trust. Not salt, not iron, not coin. Everyone wants one person they can say a true thing to and have it stay said. People will trade for it, fight over it, and go without it for a lifetime. I would make it dear and not impossible.

## Answer 12

Old roads, and sealed doors. A road shows that someone went somewhere on purpose, and a sealed door shows that someone wished to be sure nobody followed. I would also bury a few cellars with the good vintage still in them, and a ledger or two.

## Answer 13

Yes, a few, and not large in number. I would like a people who keep their own books and will not show them to me, and a smaller, odder folk who deal only by night and only in barter. They should be hard to bargain with and fair once the bargain is struck.

## Answer 14

A few, and they should be tradesmen of one kind or another. A traveler who came from far off with a strange tool and a bad memory of the road back. Not many, since every one should matter, and each should be wanted by more than one party.

## Answer 15

Face to face, only. A pull felt at any distance is a bell tied to my ankle, and I would rather not wear it. Let a god know another god by looking, and let that looking be something that can, with effort, be disguised or avoided. I can live with being found if I get to choose the door.

## Answer 16

Premise bent: one body at a time, with a second shape. I would not split into several. A god in two places is a god with two sets of books, and I would be sure to confuse them. Let a god change form, as a gate has two faces, and let the change cost something that lingers, a day of slowness, a word forgotten.

## Answer 17

The one who keeps the books. A mortal who sees what the others miss, forgives where it would be easier to punish, and gets hard to cheat. Someone who can sit at a council table and be heard because they have been right quietly for years. I want a mortal worth bargaining with.

## Answer 18

A name. I would like a place in this land where something that was lost can be found, if the right price is paid, and I would like not to know beforehand whether it is mine. I would also take a companion, a good one, with a short memory for my faults.

## Answer 19

That I be missed, a little, by someone, when I am not there. Not worshiped. Not feared. Just missed, as a neighbor is missed whose lamp is dark. I will say I have no better wish than that, and I have not said it aloud before.
