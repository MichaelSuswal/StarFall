# Sealed past: god-beasts

The rest of the story behind the outline, tied to the common past (`gods-sealed/common-past.md`). No agent reads this, the DM included.

## The rest of the story
It is the pattern of a verdict owed. The last hunter-king of Varrow Chase and the last great stag of that country killed each other in the Ovane's ford, the stag at bay in the water, the king alone because he had outridden his company on forbidden ground: he had sworn that stag to the Chase's keepers as never to be hunted. By the country's law a reeve of the Chase should have pronounced over the two, which had wronged which. The office was vacant; the country was already emptying. The people of the Chase told the story for three generations with its ending missing, and what they held so long was the missing judge, standing where the two fell. It has the king's hands and the stag's body because the telling never chose. The horn is the king's, and the broken tine is where his spear struck.

## What was lost and is not remembered
That the king was the oath-breaker. That the office it calls itself by is the very office that failed to speak.
