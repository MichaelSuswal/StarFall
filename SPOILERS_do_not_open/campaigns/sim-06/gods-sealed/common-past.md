# The common past

The far country that those thrown here are the remainder of (deep lore, Truth 5). No agent reads this file, the DM included. A god's past reaches it only through play. Nothing here fixes where that country lay, how far, or what else lies round the starting lands; the plane is set on Night 3b.

## What they are
None of them was a person. Each is a pattern the people of that country held for so long, and so many of them, that it was dense enough to be thrown clear when their star came down: an office remembered as one figure across generations, a habit of a river, a vigil, a verdict owed. Each carries a people's memory of it and no one's own memory, which is why each past is fog. Each wound is real and belonged to no one in particular.

## The country
Hesmarrow: a lamplit country of one great river and a cold coast, old and much spent by the end. Its people were talkers and counters, and they kept doors, tables and ledgers with more care than they kept walls.
- The Ovane: its great river, and the name a crowd once said over it, morning after morning, with their hands in the water.
- Tolcarrath: its chief city, at a bend of the Ovane above the river's mouth. A market city.
- The Lamp Gate: the landward gate of Tolcarrath, with a lamp on each side and a keeper facing each way. The city called both keepers by the gate's own name, Ashevar.
- The House of the Even Pan: the counting-house on the gate square, where the city's weights were kept and its grain and dues were reckoned. A reckoner stood at its door.
- The Low Field: the marsh-edge ground outside the walls, tall grass and slow water, where the sick were tended, the dead laid out, and a night watch kept over both.
- The Calder Hands: the company of menders and layers-out taught at the Low Field.
- Marrask: the harbor town at the Ovane's mouth, with a forge by the shore and a chain across the harbor.
- Varrow Chase: the hunting country upriver, with a ford on the Ovane.

## The events near its end
In order. All fell within about the last three generations of the country.
- the turning of the channels: the upriver towns cut the Ovane's feeders into their own fields. The crowd stopped coming to the water, the word stopped being said, and the river below ran thin.
- the hunt at the ford: the last hunter-king of Varrow Chase and the last great stag killed each other in the Ovane's ford. There was no one left of rank to pronounce on it, and no verdict was given.
- the cold room: the Calder Hands began to take payment for mending and for laying out, and shut their own teacher out of the Low Field's house. That winter the first keeper of the night watch died in the house, unmended, with the watch at her bed.
- the night the water came up: with the river thin, the bar at the Ovane's mouth no longer built, and a winter surge came over Marrask. The harbor chain was not closed in time. Most of the town drowned.
- the shutting of the gate: Tolcarrath walled up the Lamp Gate against the people coming inland from the coast. The gate's name went out of use with the gate.
- the last tally: the same season, the House of the Even Pan reckoned the city's winter grain and signed it true. It was true by weight. The grain had gone wrong in a way no scale showed and it did not feed. When that was known a crowd broke the house's door; the house's master weight was thrown and was not seen again.
- the fall: the star of that country came down. This is what threw them here.
