# The deep history: campaign sim-04

Written on creation night 3b, 2026-11-22, to the depth the Q5 blend set (10, the deepest: ruins under ruins over thousands of years, three layers of earlier peoples under the living ones and something older under those). Every date is in years before the arrival, written "BA"; the arrival is the first light of the morning the eight gods came down. Every rolled date carries its roll id; the others are the Keeper's, fixed here. The deep lore stands over everything below. Nothing here is released whole; each people's record holds what its old tell of it, partly wrong.

The world as the Keeper knows it is Ulverdan. No people uses that name.

## The numbers of this star

- The last fall here: 26,800 BA [r-0163].
- The fall to come: Year 146 from the arrival [r-0165]. No one in the world knows the figure.
- Earlier gods came down: 2,614 BA [r-0164].
- The star's height over the lake's middle, fixed here for every sealed measure: about 52 miles when the oldest sightline was laid (about 3,900 BA); about 47 miles in 1,150 BA; about 41.5 miles in 200 BA; 40 miles at the arrival. From the arrival it loses height by the square of the years: 40 miles times the year over 146, squared. About three quarters of a mile by Year 20, three miles by Year 40, six and three quarter miles by Year 60, and the rest in the last decades and one night.
- The lake at the middle, Lake Hesh [r28], is the hoarder. It is older than the last fall.
- The carriers exist. The thinners do not, and nothing stands in for them.

## Before any people: the fall and the young country

26,800 BA [r-0163]. The star before this one came down on the middle of the land. What stood there is not known to anyone and is not written here. The one thing that lay under the impact and came through as itself is the lake. Under the deepest made things of the land lies a sheet of blue-grey glass that rings when struck: it is the floor of the round vault in the old way behind Gathol Door [f24], it lies under the cellar at the foot of the stair in Ammeth Hall [f82], and it lies under the bed of Lake Hesh [r28].

For some thousands of years after, the country was new: rivers cut their courses fast, and beasts lived here of forms no one alive has seen. The great bones in Rib Hollow [f26] are one of them, a grazing beast the height of two men at the shoulder that died in the cave about 21,000 BA. Nothing of its kind lives.

The star now hanging formed over the lake across the ages that followed, and has been coming down for about nine hundred years: centuries first, then decades.

## The Oum, and the first folk

About 18,900 BA. The Oum walked in from the north-west, the last of a people of the country that is plane entry [p02], which they left some seventeen hundred years before its own star fell. They were 820 years on the way, through bare country, and came out of it few and changed: of everything they had been, only what they had counted on clay came through with them. That is why they keep the Count. They do not know this of themselves; their oldest whole tablets reach back about 6,000 years, and what is older survives only as copied tallies of deaths and debts with nothing to say what they were.

About 9,000 to 6,000 BA. The first folk of the lake: fishers and mound-builders with no writing, in villages round a lake a quarter of its present size. The old mounds on Ellen Heath [r17] are theirs. The shore people of Lake Hesh [r28] are what is left of them.

## The first layer: the Annar, the road-builders (about 4,700 to 3,300 BA)

A realm of straight roads laid out from the lake like spokes. Its city, Oressa, stood on the north shore and now lies under Lake Hesh [r28], about twelve miles out from Nenna [f66]. The Annar laid the straight roads that run under the Carn Road [f33], under part of the Foot Track [f44], under the heather of Ellen Heath [r17] and under the snow of Ternstrand [r02] (the Iceway [f18]). They cut the old way under the Harlfells [r04] between Gathol Door [f24] and Nether Door [f25], to bring the eastern stone west without the passes. They laid the first salt pans on the Ysk Flats [r21]. The oldest walls of Kessmar [f19], which the Dathri build on, are theirs.

They laid the Turnings [f31] about 3,900 BA: a walking pattern of low walls. A person standing at its middle and looking out through the marked exit once saw the star's lower edge sit in the notch of a third stone on the rise behind. It no longer does.

How they ended. Across about three centuries (3,600 to 3,300 BA) the lake widened over Oressa's fields and then its streets, and the people of the middle country lost their names before they lost their houses. The provinces stopped hearing from the city and stopped asking. In those last centuries wanderers who sang lists of names came through, as they have always come, and the Annar of the outer provinces took from them one idea and held it in their own way: that a thing kept carefully enough is not lost. They built stores and sealed them.
- The Comb Barrows [f62] on Ellen Heath [r17]: rows of stone hive-houses under turf. In them, slabs of wax pressed with the house-marks of every household of the heath province, built into comb by bees that were kept there for the purpose and stayed about four hundred years after the last keeper. That is what the wax older than the kingdoms is. No one alive knows it.
- Carn Vault [f40] under the old road in the Carn Hills [r08]: about two hundred sealed jars of seed, each kind marked. Most are kinds no one grows now.
- Wether Door [f41]: a hall in the hillside holding the province's standard measures in stone and its flock-marks cut on slate.
- The round vault in the old way behind Gathol Door [f24]: the name-stones of the Annar kings, set upright on the glass.
- The door on Ellen Heath [r17] that later became the Posted Door [f63] was first one of these, and was emptied and used again (below).

Named, with their traces:
- Corvane the Road-Reeve (about 4,100 BA) laid the west road. Her milestone, cut with her mark, a wheel over a bar, stands in the wall of Carn Vault [f40].
- Bressa of the Hives (died about 3,340 BA), the last keeper of the Comb Barrows [f62]. Her seal, a bee over three strokes, is on every slab of the last row. Her bones lie in the end hive-house with a smoker of fired clay.

## The quiet centuries (3,300 to 2,614 BA)

Villages, no realm. The roads went under turf. The Oum, in the Koldry Hills [r26], counted.

## The second layer: the gods who came before, and the first kingdom (2,614 to 1,900 BA)

2,614 BA [r-0164]. Three gods came down in one night, thrown by a fall farther off than any entry of the plane section; it has no entry and nothing else of it is known. The Oum entered it as three arrivals. Marrec came down on the plain, Ammeth in the eastern hills, Hollin on the southern ridge. None of the three is on the ground. All three are in the star.

- Marrec gathered the plain and made the first kingdom, with its seat at Hallisk [f35] (founded 2,590 BA). Its kings after him called themselves his sons; they were his stewards' line.
- In 2,480 BA the three met in the round vault under the Harlfells [r04] and made an agreement, cut twice on bronze: each would take the worship of no more than a stated number of hearths. Each had noticed something about itself and none said what.
- Marrec broke it. Feeling himself lighten, he ordered more worship, a cult of the Measure in every village, to root himself. In 2,431 BA, on a night the star dimmed, he went up from the roof of his hall at Hallisk [f35] before his court. His steward, Tobren Ash, had been sent to the vault with Marrec's copy of the agreement to ask the others to release him from it; he died at the inner door, which has no handle on the side he stood on, with the bronze in his hand. He is the one set of bones in the old way, about nine miles in from Gathol Door [f24].
- Ammeth bound itself to its hill. Ammeth Hall [f82] in the Koldry Hills [r26] was its seat, and the stair under the hall goes down to the chamber where it did so. It held for about a hundred and ninety years past the point where Marrec had risen. The hill paid: its springs failed one by one, the dressed stone of the hall went soft at its edges, and the people who served the hall came to remember nothing that was not Ammeth, and died out. When the hill had nothing left to give, Ammeth rose, in 2,240 BA. The Oum sealed the stair and have not used the hall since. Ogh-Miren, the counter of that year, entered it; the tablet is in the Count at Koldry [f81].
- Hollin gave itself away. It put its strength into a spring (Hollin Spring [f74] on Hollin Ridge [r23]), into a line of well-keepers, and into a breed of ridge sheep, and so stayed under the mark longer than either of the others, about four hundred years. Its people along the Skein [f08] grew, and went on singing it, and in 2,190 BA it rose too. What it gave away stayed. The spring is the fullest of it: those who drink there regularly keep their names and their particular ways when their neighbors' are wearing thin. Elow, the first well-keeper (about 2,300 BA), cut the cup-stone that still stands at the spring.

The first kingdom outlived its god by five centuries and fell about 1,900 BA to a war between two lines of "sons" and to the first thinning of the fields round Hallisk [f35].

## The third layer: the second kingdom (about 1,720 to 640 BA)

The widest realm the land has held: from the Lornlands [r06] to the Skein [f08], with provinces on the Withy [f06], along the Maud [f07], on the salt coast and in the valleys of Ternstrand [r02]. Its seat was first at the town now under Lake Odran [f13] (Sunken Odran [f30]). It wrote in a court hand no one now reads but the Oum.

- 1,410 BA. The Tallstones [f28] were set along the terrace above the meeting of the Lorn [f01] and the Odran [f02]: a boundary award between two provinces. One stone grants the toll of the crossing to "the keepers of the crossing, while these stones stand", and binds the crown to it.
- 1,150 BA. Queen Maelis made Lake Odran [f13]. The country round the old seat had been thinning for a century: the Odran [f02] ran the same breadth and shallower each decade and lost its fish, and the people of the town could no longer say which street was which. Maelis took it for drought. She moved the court to Hallisk [f35], emptied the old town, dammed the river below it, and drowned it for a store of water. The water kept nothing. Her dam is the long low bank at the lake's foot; her name was cut on its sluice-stone and has been chiselled off. No one remembers the making because the people who made it were already forgetting.
- 980 BA. The Attenders, a school of workers of mortal magic under the province reeve of the heath, some forty of them, tried one great working to bring back what the heath province had lost. It left bare ground about a mile across where nothing particular grows, and sixty-one of the school and their helpers were left hollow: alive, quiet, and no one in particular. The reeve, Dorwen Hale, had the old Annar store on Ellen Heath [r17] emptied, put the sixty-one inside with food, shut the door, and set a notice beside it in the court hand under his seal. That is the Posted Door [f63]. The notice reads: "Shut by order. Those within are not dead. Do not wake them. Do not do what they did." The head of the school, Sefa, kept a list on birch bark of the things she found she had forgotten; it is inside, with the bones.
- About 900 BA. On the salt coast a sect buried its dead four and five times the usual depth, in the belief that the star draws the dead up and that depth keeps them. Lowgrave Field [f79] is their ground; Annic Low was their last priest, and his is the deepest grave. The same coast kept a lamp on the seaward sill for those the sea had taken. The salt towns still keep the lamp, without the reason.
- About 800 BA. The open mere of the Vest [f09] went to fen. Vesthallow [f71] stands on what was an island in it.
- The Withy [f06] province kept a road-hall where the straight road met the valley track: Kettling Hall [f48], a hall for the crown's toll in kind. Its last keeper was Wenlow Kettle.
- Dryhythe [f38] was a ferry on a branch of the Sarn [f03].

How it ended (700 to 640 BA). A sweating sickness came in by the salt ports and travelled the roads for three summers. The salt towns fought the crown over the price of salt and won. The provinces emptied or were let go. The Withy [f06] valley's people, about nine thousand at their most, died or went down river within a generation; Wenlow Kettle buried the hall's clay tallies under its floor in 655 BA and is buried beside the track. The ports on the Skein [f08] were left by their own river, and the ferry guilds of those ports became a moving people; Dry Stairs [f75] is one of their landings. The valleys of Ternstrand [r02] held on by themselves as the terrace people.

## The living peoples

- 630 BA. Garrad Marrowson, who claimed the blood of the first kings, founded Dunmarrow [r07], the third kingdom on the plain. His measure-stone is in the hall of weights at Hallisk [f35].
- About 700 BA onward. The Merrowen have held Vestfen [r25] since the mere closed. The Maudrins have kept the middle Maud [f07] for about twenty generations; the wall of Maudry [f64] stands on a quay of the second kingdom. The Tolleners have kept the crossing at Tollen [f27] for about five hundred years. The Rellen are the ferry guilds' descendants. The Dathri came up into Kessmar Vale [r05] about 1,500 BA and say they were always there.
- About 410 BA. The branch of the Sarn [f03] at Dryhythe [f38] stopped being a river: it did not dry in a season, it slowed, widened, lost its fish and its bed, and was grass within nine years. Marrit Dunn, the ferrywoman, kept the landing through all nine; her tally is cut in the landing stone, each year's crossings fewer.
- 290 BA. The Sallowfolk came up the Withy [f06] into Sallowdene [r12], hedgers and bee-keepers pushed off Orsk Shore [r22] when the salt towns enclosed it. They found the valley empty for three and a half centuries, the hall roofless and the old road under turf. Eleven or twelve generations, as they reckon.
- About 200 BA. Dunmarrow [r07] at its widest, under Osmer the Tolltaker, who took toll at Carnford [f39] and on the Lorn [f01] to the sea. The footings of his toll-house are under the market at Carnford [f39]. The kingdom has given ground since, without a war.
- About 150 BA. Three winters and a debt to Hallisk [f35] ended the terrace people of Ternstrand [r02]; most went south over Scour Gap [f16] as bondsmen. The Ternings came along the coast into the empty valleys within a decade, under Torvald Aske, whose cairn stands above Ternlake [f14].
- 112 to 104 BA. The salt war among the towns of the coast. Hesk Varnsen of Varn [f78] chained the mouths of the Skein [f08]. The league was made after it.
- Every thirty years, for longer than any of these peoples has kept a record, a band of the carriers has walked one round through the land: ashore at Varn [f78], up the Skein [f08], across to the Maud [f07], round the south and west of the lake, and down the Carn [f05] to Carnmouth [f42], where boats they keep take them off [r-0175, r-0177, r-0178]. Their last passing was 20 BA. They are human-seeming and few, about forty. This band's line last walked from the country of plane entry [p02] [r-0176], many generations ago. Their songs are lists of names, some of places here that are gone (Oressa is in it, and Sunken Odran [f30] by its old name, and the villages of the Withy [f06] province), some from under other stars. The song of this band holds no name of the country the eight gods are the remainder of: that country's own singers were another line, and the two have not met.

## The travelers from afar

Two (Q14 at 2), each alone, each the last of a line that set out generations before, since no entry is nearer than six lifetimes' walking.
- At Varn [f78], 13 BA [r-0169, r-0170, r-0171]: Caddo Reth, from plane entry [p01], the country the eight gods are the remainder of. His line left it about 203 years before the arrival and never saw its end. They were sworn riders of a custom that had just been outlawed there, and they went rather than be run down. Eight generations on, he came ashore alone from a boat of a build no one on the coast knew. What he brought: a saddle with a rigid frame and two iron foot-loops hung from it, which no people here makes or has thought of; a folded sheet of pressed bark with a sworn warrant on it that he can recite and cannot explain; and a riding-chant his line used to keep the count of the way, which names, in order, places down one river that no one here has seen. He works as a carter for a pan-holder.
- At Skarhaven [f83], 7 BA [r-0166, r-0167, r-0168]: Isk Vennar, from the country under the young star that is plane entry [p03]. Her line set out more than six centuries ago; no one in it for many generations has known why. She was found half frozen in a hide boat. What she brought: a sighting arc of brass with a plumb and a scale, made for taking the height of a small high star, and the craft of making and reading one, which she has only in part. The arc cannot frame the star here: it is too low and too large for its scale. She mends nets at Skarhaven [f83] and has taken no reading with it since she landed.

## The arrival

At first light, the same hour in every country, eight gods came down, thrown by the fall of the country that is plane entry [p01], 190 years of walking to the east. In the same hour a warm blue-grey stone, heavy for its size, fell in Ammerholt [r24] (the unnamed fallen stone [f69]). Each god woke into its own country's season. No people knew a god was coming, and none knows yet that one has come.

## Checks

Against the deep lore: nothing above contradicts a truth. The stars, the fall, the fading, magic, the gods' origin, the risen, the lake and the carriers are each as the lore gives them, with this star's rolls. Nothing explains what the lore keeps mysterious: why the plane keeps accounts, what a risen god becomes after a fall, why thrown remainders are shaped like leaders, and where the first star came from are not touched. The three earlier gods stand in the history because the lore needs their bodies (the marks of rising, binding and giving away) and the phase gives the time; none is on the ground. The star's rolls removed the thinners, and nothing stands in for them.

Against the records: every meeting agreed on Night 3a (all eight peoples already there with their own history, each god arriving unknown) is possible in this history. Dates: the Ternings six generations (150 BA); the Sallowfolk eleven or twelve (290 BA); Dunmarrow [r07] the third kingdom, its widest reach eight generations ago (200 BA); the salt league four generations (after 104 BA); the shore people older than any record; the Oum before the forest was its present size. Each agrees.
