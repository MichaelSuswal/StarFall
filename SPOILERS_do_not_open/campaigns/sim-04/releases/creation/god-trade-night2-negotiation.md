# Negotiation: god-trade, Night 2

This file is your instruction for this round and your go for exactly the file it names. A `git pull` is always allowed. Your powers, priced. You proposed four powers and one weakness that buys.

## Round 1

### How this table prices four things for every god
These are not offers. They are how the same thing is priced for any god, and they are written into your card where they apply.
- A loss of memory or habit. Where a price takes "one item of memory or habit", one item is removed from your own record (your record of other gods, or your goals), picked by a logged roll, never chosen. You are told what went or you find the gap yourself, as you say.
- A return from a destroyed body. A season to return costs one item; a month costs two; nothing is faster. The rung is chosen now and written on the card; it is not chosen again at each return. You may name the ground you return at, so long as it is ground of your own kind and is fixed by a rule on your card, never picked at the time. From that ground you travel at your own pace. A body held, shut in or scattered is held, not broken. While you are gone, whatever of yours needs you to keep it up lapses.
- Measures. Where your powers say "a day's walk" it is 20 miles, the same on any ground. "Earshot", "a spoken voice" and "speaking distance" are as far as an ordinary voice carries in open air, about 50 paces, and not through walls. Where a mortal is left able to feel you near, near is about 200 yards.
- Unruled. What any power of yours does to another god is unruled until it happens. So is its working on creatures that are more than ordinary, and on things made or kept by more than ordinary craft, until met.

### The Fair Weight
Draft text:
Description: Whatever the god holds in its hands or sets on a balance it knows to the true: its weight, how many are in a heap, how much grain is in a sack, how pure a lump of metal is against the ordinary run of that metal in that land, and whether a measure, scale or tally has been falsified and by how much. A false weight held in its hand is made honest in its mass, until another hand next changes it. True weight is weight against the ordinary standard of the place, set among its people by agreement.
Drawback: While it weighs, and for an hour after, the god cannot say anything it knows to be false, and cannot hold back what it has found: asked, it answers, and what it found comes out of its mouth.
Limits: Only what it touches, and nothing too large for it to hold or lift. Effects, never causes: that a weight is false and by how much, never who made it so or why; an honest mistake reads the same as a fraud. It works in dark as in light. It makes no one trust anyone. It rules no mind. Not applicable: distance, falling, water, being watched.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: accepted as written. A trader who cannot bluff for an hour after every weighing has paid.

### The Kept Road
Draft text:
Description: A stretch of road, with its fords and bridges, that the god has walked end to end and then laid its hand on becomes a kept road. Mud, flood and drift trouble it less: in any season short of deep snow or full flood it travels as a good road, and those carrying goods on it go one tenth quicker. Within a day's walk (20 miles) of where it stands, the god feels the passage on any kept road: few feet or many, light loads or heavy, and which way they go, never who. It feels a blocking of a kept road (a toll bar, a fallen tree, a wall) as a cut. Two kept roads at most, each no longer than a day's walk. A road it stops keeping reverts in a season.
Drawback: It cannot shut out the footfalls. While more than a few travellers are on its kept roads within its reach, the god is dull with their walking: slow in thought, easily startled, unable to attend to one voice among several; and sleep, if it chooses it, gives it no rest.
Limits: A kept road serves enemies as freely as friends; the god does not choose who is helped. It can feel a cut and cannot undo one by this power. It cannot keep a road it has not walked end to end. The feeling gives no count, only few, many or a great many. To a watcher a kept road is a well-drained road. It rules no mind. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: "a few roads" is set at two and a road's length at a day's walk; "a little quicker" at one tenth, so that the pace table can apply it. The drawback is yours and is a real one: it grows with your own success.

### The Reckoning
Draft text:
Description: A bargain sworn aloud by two or more people, in the god's presence, with the god's hand held out and the terms spoken, binds them in the body, not by force. A party that breaks its word feels a weight at the chest until the matter is mended, and the palm of the hand it swore with darkens like a bruise, for all to see. The god knows when a bargain sworn before it is broken, and by which party, never why.
Drawback: one of the two under Choices.
Limits: The Reckoning holds the god too: a bargain it swears itself and breaks, it feels twice over, and for as long it has no use of its powers. This follows only from the god's own breach, so it is a limit and buys nothing. It forces no one to swear. A bargain sworn under threat does not count, nor does it bind a party that did not know the terms. A dead party's bargain passes to no one. It does not judge whether a bargain was wise or fair, and it stops no breach. Distance makes no difference to the knowing or the weight. It makes no one trust anyone. It rules no mind. A god as a party is unruled until it happens. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water.
Choices:
(a) The god's hand darkens too. While any bargain sworn before it stands broken and unmended, the palm of the hand the god holds out is dark for all to see, and it cannot hold a Reckoning until the matter is mended.
(b) The debts weigh. The god carries every standing bargain sworn before it: with more than twelve standing it walks at half its pace; with more than forty it cannot keep a road.
The Keeper's reasons: being held by your own broken word is a penalty you pay only if you break it, and this table writes such a thing as a limit. "Never idle of other people's debts" is true to you but is nothing the Keeper could apply or you would notice losing on a given day. (a) and (b) are two prices that fall in the ordinary use.

### Return to the Stone
Draft text:
Description: When its body is destroyed, the god gathers again, out of the grain, salt and dust on it, at the nearest weighing stone of its own people: a stone on which the god has set a true weight with its hand. It takes a season.
Drawback: One item of memory or habit, by logged roll from its own record, not told to the god.
Limits: The standing return scale, first rung: a season, one item. A stone broken or carried off no longer serves. A body chained, walled in, buried or shut in a room is held, not broken. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, being watched, minds.
Choices: for the time before any stone is marked, or when none is left:
(a) You gather at the nearest place where two roads cross.
(b) With no marked stone, you do not gather. You lie scattered until someone sets a weight on a stone in your name, and the season runs from that. This is beyond what the scale requires and buys nothing.
Say if you want the month at two items instead.
The Keeper's reasons: you cannot die, so the card must say where you gather on a day when you have marked nothing. That your roads and bargains lapse while you are gone is so for any god and is in the table's terms above, not a price.

### Trade: the Barred Door
Draft text:
Weakness: The god cannot open, break or pass a door, gate, hatch or lid that is shut and fastened from the far side, by any means, for as long as it stays fastened. Another's hand must undo it, or the fastening be broken by others, or the god go round by another way. A door merely closed does not count, nor a way blocked by something that is not a fastening. It may open anything fastened from its own side. Anyone who learns of it can hold the god with a bar.
What it buys: one of the two under Choices.
Choices:
(a) The Kept Road's feeling reaches three days' walk (60 miles) from where the god stands.
(b) The god may keep four roads at once.
The Keeper's reasons: the weakness is real and heavy for a giant who could otherwise break most doors, and it is taken exactly as you wrote it. One weakness buys one step, and you asked two for it. Pick the one you want more. A second weakness could buy the other.
