### How this table prices four things for every god
These are not offers. They are how the same thing is priced for any god, and they are written into your card where they apply.
- A loss of memory or habit. Where a price takes "one item of memory or habit", one item is removed from your own record (your record of other gods, or your goals), picked by a logged roll, never chosen. You are told what went or you find the gap yourself, as you say.
- A return from a destroyed body. A season to return costs one item; a month costs two; nothing is faster. The rung is chosen now and written on the card; it is not chosen again at each return. You may name the ground you return at, so long as it is ground of your own kind and is fixed by a rule on your card, never picked at the time. From that ground you travel at your own pace. A body held, shut in or scattered is held, not broken. While you are gone, whatever of yours needs you to keep it up lapses.
- Measures. Where your powers say "a day's walk" it is 20 miles, the same on any ground. "Earshot", "a spoken voice" and "speaking distance" are as far as an ordinary voice carries in open air, about 50 paces, and not through walls. Where a mortal is left able to feel you near, near is about 200 yards.
- Unruled. What any power of yours does to another god is unruled until it happens. So is its working on creatures that are more than ordinary, and on things made or kept by more than ordinary craft, until met.

### Reading the water
Draft text:
Description: Standing in or touching water, the god learns what that water is carrying now and what it has carried within the last month: silt, sickness, poison, smoke, blood, the passing of many feet or of boats, how recent each is, and how far up and down the same channel each reaches, out to a day's drift (20 miles) each way from where it stands. Effects, never causes: what happened and how far, never why and never who.
Drawback: What it reads it takes into itself. Foul water sickens it and heavy silt thickens it, for as long as it read and as long again; while sick or thick it moves at half its pace and cannot hold the Mist veil.
Limits: While it reads it cannot move or speak and cannot be hurried out of it; this is a condition of the reading, not its price. Only the water it touches and water joined to it in the same channel; not still water cut off from the channel, rain in the air or water in a vessel. It gives no count of feet or boats, only few, many or a great many. It does not know what it cannot read. It works by day and night alike and under ice. To a watcher it is mist standing in a stream. It rules no mind. Not applicable: falling, carrying.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: you asked that "a day's drift" be set; it is set at 20 miles, the table's day. "What that water has carried" had no end to it, and water keeps little; a month is as far back as a channel's bed and banks hold what passed. The stillness while reading is how the power is used, so it is a limit; the sickness after is the price, and I have given it teeth you would feel (half pace, no veil).

### Clean water
Draft text:
Description: The god makes one spring, well, pool or stretch of channel up to 200 yards long fit to drink, and keeps it so while it stays in that water. It takes out the common sickness of water: rot, fouling and what breeds in standing pools.
Drawback: The foulness goes through it. For as many days as it kept the water clean, and counted from the day it leaves, it is sluggish, thick and low: held to a low mist, at half its pace, and unable to work any other power.
Limits: One water at a time. It does not make water where there is none, sweeten salt water, cure a person, or undo a poison made by more than ordinary craft. The water stays clean only until what fouled it returns. Its own people's wells are cleaned on the same terms as any. To a watcher it is mist lying on a well. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, minds.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: you offered to pay yourself, and it is taken as offered. As you wrote it, the days of sluggishness ran while you were in the water you could not leave anyway, which costs little; counted from the day you leave, it is a price.

### Mist veil
Draft text:
Description: Beside water, the god gathers mist and holds it over a place up to 300 yards across, so that the place is hard to see and hard to find by sight from outside, by day or night, for as long as the god holds it.
Drawback: one of the two under Choices.
Limits: While it holds the veil it gives it its whole attention: it cannot read water, cannot speak, and moves no faster than a crawl; those inside cannot find the god either. These are conditions of use, not the price. It needs open water within 200 yards. It hides from sight only: sound, smell, smoke above the mist, and tracks leading in are as they were. It does not hide a place from one who already knows where it is, and it stops no one who walks in. Wind and heat thin it. It rules no mind. Not applicable: falling, carrying.
Choices:
(a) The place pays. When the god lets the veil go, a cold wet fog lies on that place for as many days as the veil was held, and the god cannot lift it: nothing dries, fires are hard to keep, and work out of doors goes at half.
(b) The god pays. For as many days as it held the veil, it cannot touch water to read it and cannot clean any.
The Keeper's reasons: the drawback you wrote (whole attention, no speech, a crawl) is true only while the veil is up. That is how the power is used, not something you lose by using it, so it buys nothing and the veil needs a price. "About the size of a village" is set at 300 yards across, as you asked.

### The Cupful
Draft text:
Description: The god fills the stoppered gourd it carries with spring water and makes of it a part of itself. The Cupful may be set on a current, left in a stream or carried by a mortal. It sees and hears what happens within about ten paces of the water it lies in or the vessel it is carried in. It cannot act or speak and cannot be directed once sent. What it learned reaches the god only when the two meet again. One Cupful at a time.
Drawback: one of the two under Choices.
Limits: It is a part of the god's one body, not a second body. The god does not know where it is or what it perceives while it is away. A Cupful not met again within a season fades and is lost. The gourd is an ordinary gourd; only the god can fill it so. To a watcher it is water. What another god perceives of it is unruled until it happens. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, minds.
Choices:
(a) While the Cupful is away the god is thinner: it cannot hold the Mist veil, and it feels what is done to the Cupful (drunk, boiled, poured out, held) as done to itself, without knowing where. A Cupful lost does not come back; a new one takes a season to fill, and the gourd, if lost with it, must be found or grown again.
(b) While the Cupful is away the god is as it was. A Cupful lost costs one item of memory or habit, and a new one takes a month to fill.
The Keeper's reasons: "thinner by a measure" named no measure; (a) gives it one. (b) is the other way such a loss can be priced. "As far as the connected water runs" is not set as a distance: the Cupful goes where water or a carrier takes it, and what bounds it is the season. Pooling only on meeting is the cheap way, as you were told, and is written so.

### Return to the spring

### Turn the channel
Draft text:
Description: Once in a year, the god asks a river to move. Either it bends a channel aside by up to 300 paces, over seven days in which it stands in that channel; or it holds a rising flood in the channel off one place it has touched, for a season.
Drawback: For a season from the turning, the god is bound to that water: it can enter no other, and it reads no other.
Limits: Either the bend or the holding in a year, not both. The water turned goes somewhere else, and the god does not choose where; what it does there is the world's to work out. It stops a flood from the channel only, never water from the sky. It does not move a channel wider than a bowshot at the place it stands. A river another god is working is unruled until it happens. Anyone downstream can see that the river moved. If the god is destroyed in that season, it still returns at its spring, and the binding ends. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, minds.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: you said yourself this is more than a god newly landed should have. With six powers your card sits above what a newly landed god carries, and this one is most of it. I will let it stand at this price and these limits, and you may keep it and carry the lean, or drop it, or shrink it. The seven days are there so that a river is not moved in an afternoon.

### Trade: the plea for water
Draft text:
