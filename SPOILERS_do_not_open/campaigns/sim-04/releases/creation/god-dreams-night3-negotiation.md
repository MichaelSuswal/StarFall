# Negotiation: god-dreams, Night 3

This file is your instruction for this round and your go for exactly the file it names. A `git pull --rebase --autostash` is always allowed.

## The table's measures, the same at every table
- One edge of ordinary size is about as much as the smallest power on a card, and one weakness pays for about that much: one weakness, one step.
- A weakness is priced by what it costs your people that they do not already pay. A weakness that only restates the country they live in is not counted.
- Where an edge or a weakness bears on a contest, one step is ten points in the odds. Where it bears on work or travel, it is a share of the public rates.
- An edge that would let a mortal do what only you can do is a power of yours, priced on you, and goes on your card. What it costs the mortal is fitted by the Keeper and is not the price.

## Round 1
### The dark eye
Draft text, the edge: your people see in dim light better than other peoples. By starlight, or by one rushlight, they work, herd and keep to a fen path as other peoples do at dusk; a moonless night or a dense mist does not lose them. Where a contest turns on seeing in the dark, it is one step in their favour.
Limits: it needs some light; in a place with none at all (a cave, a shuttered cellar) they are as blind as anyone. It is their eyes and nothing more: it touches no dream and gives nothing to your Lamp-Sight or your Sleeper's Door, and takes nothing from them.

Draft text, the weakness: the glare. From late spring to early autumn, between mid-morning and mid-afternoon on a clear day, their work in the open is done at three quarters of the public rates, and they keep shaded hours for it. Where a contest turns on seeing far or fine in full sun, it is one step against them. Long bright days bring headache and eye sickness to some each summer.

Choices: none. The price goes one way: the weakness you offered pays for the edge you asked, one for one.

The Keeper's reasons: the edge is the size you said it was, and the weakness is one your people would notice every summer, at the height of the working year. Nothing is added and nothing is taken off.

### Not priced
The number: about five thousand is the usual number, so there is no cost of size to price; a larger people is what carries a cost. The household lamp kept through the night is custom, and you named its practical cost yourself: oil and tallow they can ill spare in a bad year. Wick-singing is magic on the world's terms and is fitted by the Keeper, the number of workers included. The rest is ordinary culture and stands as written.

Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons.
