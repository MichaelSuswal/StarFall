### Reading the Stone
Draft text:
Description: With its palm flat on rock or worked stone, the god perceives what has marked the faces of that stone, in the order it happened: what has passed over it, what was cut, burned, broken, cracked or carried, and how far each reaches across the connected stone about its hand, out to about 300 yards. Effects, never causes: what happened and how far, never why and never who. A hand-span of time takes in what lies nearest; the whole reach takes an hour.
Drawback: For as long as it read, and counted from when it lifts its hand, the god is stiff and slow: half its pace, and it cannot shape stone.
Limits: While it reads it is as still as the stone, cannot walk, speak or defend itself, and neither hears nor sees the open air; a condition of use, not the price. Rock, mortar and fired clay only: not living things, metal, wood, water or bone. Not through a gap of air. It gives the order of marks, never their age, and no count, speech, face or name. It reads faces of stone that something has touched: floors, walls, roads, the sides of a cut. What lies hollow beneath, it knows only as its soles and palms already tell it. Water wears marks away. It works in dark as in light. To a watcher it is a standing stone with a hand on a wall. Not applicable: falling, carrying, minds.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: this is a power that shows the past, so it is held to effects and to order: no ages, no counts, no who. "About the size of a village" is set at 300 yards. The stillness is how the power is used and is a limit; the stiffness after is the price, and I have said what it stops.

### Warding the Threshold
Draft text:
Description: The god lays its hand on a pass, gate, door or narrow way cut in stone or standing in it, no wider than 50 paces, and declares it closed. No creature without the god's leave can pass it: the way turns them, and they come out where they went in. Anyone the god names may pass. A ward holds a season and may be renewed by the god's hand on the threshold. The god may lift a ward at will from anywhere. Three wards at most.
Drawback: one of the two under Choices.
Limits: The god cannot pass its own warded threshold while the ward stands. The ward works on the way, not on minds: those turned know they were turned. It stops no arrow, voice, message or thing thrown or carried by water; it stops nothing that goes over, around or under by another way; it fails if the stone of the threshold is broken so far that the threshold is gone. It holds by night and day. It closes to the god's own people as to any, unless named. A named one may lead no one through. Against another god it is unruled until it happens. Not applicable: falling, light and dark.
Choices:
(a) The tether. While any ward stands, the god is bound to its oldest standing ward: more than a day's walk (20 miles) from it, the god can lay no ward, read no stone and shape none.
(b) The weight. Each standing ward stiffens the god: with one, it walks at half its pace; with two, it cannot shape stone; with three, it cannot leave the valley or the mountain it stands in.
The Keeper's reasons: you wrote that you are "uneasy" more than a day's walk from your oldest ward. Unease is not something the Keeper can apply or you would notice losing. A pass closed to an army for a season, three times over, is a large thing, and its price has to be one. Not passing your own ward is kept as a limit. Fifty paces is set so that a threshold is a threshold and not a valley mouth.

### Shaping the Stone
Draft text:
Description: With both hands on rock, the god softens it and moves it as clay, fits it and lets it harden: standing stone drawn into a wall, a lintel, an arch, a stair or a cistern. In a day it does the work of twenty masons with their tools.
Drawback: For each day it works, it is stiff and heavy the day after: half its pace, and it cannot shape. If it works more than seven days running it seizes, and cannot move for as many days as it worked.
Limits: Natural rock and stone already laid only: no metal, glass or fired clay. It conjures nothing: the design is the god's, and what stone cannot hold does not stand. Seams show to those who know stone. It is building work at a builder's pace: it does not throw down a wall or open a rock face faster than twenty masons could. It works in dark as in light. Stone shaped by more than ordinary craft is unruled until met. Its work for its own people is on the same terms. Not applicable: falling, water, minds, distance.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: "ten times the pace of a mortal crew" named no crew; twenty masons' work in a day is set as the rate. The stiff day after each day worked is taken as you offered it, and "past my body's strength" is set at seven days running.

### Trade: Oath at the Threshold
Choices:

Weakness: A bargain, oath or guest-right the god speaks aloud, with stated intent to bind, in a door, gate or pass cut in stone, binds it in body: it cannot act against it while it stands. It ends when the other party releases the god, when the term runs out, or when the bargain is fulfilled.
(b) Widened, so that it buys.
Weakness: A bargain, oath or guest-right the god speaks aloud, with stated intent to bind, anywhere under a roof of stone or between walls of stone, built or cut, or in any pass, binds it in body: it cannot act against it while it stands, whatever it would lose.
