### How this table prices four things for every god
These are not offers. They are how the same thing is priced for any god, and they are written into your card where they apply.
- A loss of memory or habit. Where a price takes "one item of memory or habit", one item is removed from your own record (your record of other gods, or your goals), picked by a logged roll, never chosen. You are told what went or you find the gap yourself, as you say.
- A return from a destroyed body. A season to return costs one item; a month costs two; nothing is faster. The rung is chosen now and written on the card; it is not chosen again at each return. You may name the ground you return at, so long as it is ground of your own kind and is fixed by a rule on your card, never picked at the time. From that ground you travel at your own pace. A body held, shut in or scattered is held, not broken. While you are gone, whatever of yours needs you to keep it up lapses.
- Measures. Where your powers say "a day's walk" it is 20 miles, the same on any ground. "Earshot", "a spoken voice" and "speaking distance" are as far as an ordinary voice carries in open air, about 50 paces, and not through walls. Where a mortal is left able to feel you near, near is about 200 yards.
- Unruled. What any power of yours does to another god is unruled until it happens. So is its working on creatures that are more than ordinary, and on things made or kept by more than ordinary craft, until met.

### Sealed Telling
Draft text:
Description: When a mortal within a spoken voice of the god knowingly tells it something and asks that it be kept, the god may take it as a confidence and seal it, saying so. While the god holds it, the telling cannot be drawn out of that mortal by any power, drug, craft or magic that works on the mind. The teller may still speak it of their own will. The god may hold any number of confidences. A confidence ends when the teller releases the god, when the god releases it, or when the teller dies.
Drawback: The god cannot speak, write, signal or act upon a held confidence, even to save a life or to bring the teller out of danger, and it cannot forget it or lay it down except by releasing it, which ends the seal.
Limits: It guards the teller's mind, not the teller's body: force, fear and bribery work as they always did. It does not guard the same secret where another holds it. An overheard secret is no confidence. A confidence told by one who does not know what the god is still counts. A confidence that touches a threat to the god's own people is held like any other. "Act upon" means doing what the god would not have done had it not been told; the Keeper judges it when it arises. The seal makes no one trust anyone. Against another god's power on the teller it is unruled until it happens. Not applicable: light and dark, distance beyond a spoken voice, falling, carrying, water.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: accepted nearly as written. Being unable to use what it knows is a real price for a god of secrets. I have added that a confidence ends with the teller's death, since a seal on a mind needs the mind; say if you meant otherwise. You wrote that secrets of the dead you hold remain held: what you remember from before the fall is yours to remember and is no confidence under this power.

### Unlatching
Draft text:
Description: By touch, over time, the god opens what a person has shut by ordinary craft: a lock, a bar, a seal, a sealed vessel, a sealed door or tomb. A latch takes a breath, a barred door an hour, a tomb door a day. The thing is not damaged and can be closed again by ordinary means.
Drawback: one of the two under Choices.
Limits: It opens; it does not break or unmake. It does not work on bodies, minds or anything living. The god must touch the thing for the whole of the time. It works in dark as in light and under water. One who watches sees a man with his hand on a door for an hour. Things made or kept by more than ordinary craft are unruled until met. Not applicable: distance, falling, carrying.
Choices:
(a) For a day and a night after each opening, whatever its size, the rightness leaves the god's face: it goes loose and strange, and the god cannot pass for ordinary to anyone who looks at it.
(b) The same, scaled: after a latch, until the next dawn; after a barred door, a day and a night; after a tomb door or anything that took a day, seven days.
The Keeper's reasons: your drawback said two things, a day and a night after each opening, and then a cost that runs with the size of the door. They are different prices and I have written both so that you choose. As you wrote the second ("a latch a few breaths"), small openings were nearly free, so (b) starts at the next dawn.

### Reading the Crossing
Draft text:
Description: The god knows where, within a day's walk (20 miles) of it, a mortal person lies dead within the last season, how long since, and whether the body has been found or tended. What, never why and never who: no names, causes or last words.
Drawback: For each death so learned the god stands still for an hour: it can do nothing, and cannot be roused. The hours run one after another.
Limits: Mortal persons only: not beasts, not gods. It tells the dead from the living and nothing of the dying. It knows a body, not anything that may be left of a person besides. A body is learned once. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water, minds, being watched (a watcher sees a man standing still).
Choices:
(a) The knowing is always open: each death within reach comes to the god when it happens or when the god comes within reach of it, and the hour follows at once, wherever the god is.
(b) The knowing is opened by the god at will: it then learns every death within reach it has not yet learned, and the hours follow at once.
The Keeper's reasons: "I know at once" could be read either way, and the two are different lives for you, so both are written. A day's walk is the table's 20 miles. "Recently" is a season, as you said.
