# Negotiation: god-ice, Night 3

This file is your instruction for this round and your go for exactly the file it names. A `git pull --rebase --autostash` is always allowed.

## The table's measures, the same at every table
- One edge of ordinary size is about as much as the smallest power on a card, and one weakness pays for about that much: one weakness, one step.
- A weakness is priced by what it costs your people that they do not already pay. A weakness that only restates the country they live in is not counted.
- Where an edge or a weakness bears on a contest, one step is ten points in the odds. Where it bears on work or travel, it is a share of the public rates.
- An edge that would let a mortal do what only you can do is a power of yours, priced on you, and goes on your card. What it costs the mortal is fitted by the Keeper and is not the price.

## Round 1
### The Prince's Term
This is a power of yours, as the Night 2 ruling said it would be: a mortal who binds in your name does what only you can do. It is priced on you. If it is agreed it goes on your card as a power marked "added Night 3".

Draft text:
Status: main (added Night 3)
Description: One merchant-prince, sworn to the god in its hearing, may speak a term in the god's name, face to face, to one mortal who understands the words and agrees aloud. The term then holds that mortal for the stated years as the god's own Spoken Term would, with a breach felt as the same creeping cold. Only terms of debt, toll, tally, delivery, carriage, trade or service.
Drawback: one of the two choices below.
Limits settled at creation:
  - Never a term on a person's body, name, kin, faith or movement. Never to a child too young to understand, to a god, or to anyone under threat of force; none of these is bound.
  - One mortal at a time, who must say yes. Never a people as a mass.
  - One prince at a time. When he dies or is released the power sleeps, and the terms he spoke stand until they end; it wakes when the next is sworn to the god in its hearing.
  - A term he speaks outside these bounds does not hold. The god may end any term of his by its own word.
  - What the prince bears, fitted by the Keeper and no part of the price: each term he speaks stands as cold in his own belly; with more than twelve standing he is at half his pace; he cannot lie inside a term he speaks; if he breaks the letter of one the cold is in him until he mends it. The people see it.
  - Intent, the god's first wording: "The merchant-prince may speak a term in my name and under my seal, face to face, to one mortal who comes before him, and if that mortal agrees aloud and understands the words, the term holds them for the stated years, as mine would."

Choices: the price on you could go two ways.
(a) One count. Every term the prince speaks stands in your own count as well as in his: your twelve and your forty hold the two of you together. He stops at forty with you.
(b) Counts apart, as you asked. His own ceiling is twenty standing, not forty. And each time he speaks a term, you are slow for one day wherever you are: half your pace, as after a working of cold, with no cold about you. Days owed for several terms run one after another.

The Keeper's reasons: what the prince carries is his, and a mortal's cost does not pay for a god's power; your card must bear something you would notice. A second mouth for the Spoken Term reaches across a whole people in a year, which one mouth does not, and the Spoken Term is already the larger half of your card. With counts wholly apart and nothing falling on you, the power would be free to you. Under (a) you pay in room; under (b) you pay in days.

You may also refuse the power. Then the prince rules by ordinary bargain, witness and tally, as any merchant-prince does, and the people's custom of the spoken contract stands as custom.
