These are not offers. They are how the same thing is priced for any god, and they are written into your card where they apply.

You may name the ground you return at, so long as it is ground of your own kind and is fixed by a rule on your card, never picked at the time. From that ground you travel at your own pace. - Unruled. What any power of yours does to another god is unruled until it happens. So is its working on creatures that are more than ordinary, and on things made or kept by more than ordinary craft, until met.

### Winter's Reach
Draft text:
Description: Within about a mile of the god, on ground and water it can see, it brings frost and cold: it freezes standing or slow water (a pond, a stretch of river, a well), thickens ice already there, or brings a killing frost to one field or yard of up to ten acres. One working in a day.
Drawback: one of the two under Choices.
Limits: The cold stays after the god leaves: a frosted field is barren for the season, and frozen water stays frozen seven days after the god lets it go and cannot be unfrozen by the god in its first day. This lingering is what the power does, not its price. It makes no snow from a clear sky and cools nothing that moves fast. It works on water and ground, never directly on a creature's body. It needs sight: nothing in darkness without a light on the place, nothing behind a wall or a ridge. Its own people's fields and wells freeze the same as any. Anyone watching sees frost come where the beast is looking. It rules no mind. Not applicable: falling, carrying.
Choices:
(a) The god pays. While any frost or ice of its making still holds past its natural season, the god cannot bring cold again, and it is as slow and heavy as it is in deep summer.
(b) The frost does not choose. A working cannot be aimed at one field or one water: it takes all the ground and water the god can see within its mile alike, whoever's it is, and the barren season falls on all of it.
The Keeper's reasons: the drawback you wrote is that the cold lingers and those below pay. Used on your own people's ground that is a cost; used on anyone else's it is the point of the power. A price is paid whichever way the power is turned, so the lingering is written as a limit and a price is needed. Ten acres and one working a day are set so that a people's whole harvest is not ended in an afternoon. You asked whether it harms a creature directly: it does not, as you thought.

### The Spoken Term
Draft text:
Description: When the god speaks terms aloud in a plain sentence to one mortal face to face, and that mortal, understanding the words, agrees aloud, the terms hold that mortal for the term of years the god states. A breach is felt by the one who breaks it as a creeping cold in the hands and chest that grows with each day the term goes unmet and ends when it is mended. It does not kill and it does not compel.
Drawback: one of the two under Choices.
Limits: Every term binds the god to its exact wording as well, and the god cannot state a falsehood inside a term; if the god breaks the letter of its own term, the cold comes into it too until it is mended. This follows only from the god's own breach, so it is a limit and buys nothing. It binds only the mortal who agreed, only to what was said. A spoken yes counts; a nod does not. A mortal who was deceived about what the words mean is not bound; a mortal who understood the words and was deceived about anything else is bound. It cannot bind one who did not hear it, a child too young to understand, or a god. It ends with the stated years, with the mortal's death, or when the god releases it. The cold makes the breach certain to the breaker and shows nothing to anyone else. It makes no one trust anyone. It rules no mind. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water.
Choices:
(a) The breach is the god's too. While any Spoken Term stands broken and unmended by the mortal who broke it, the god cannot use Winter's Reach.
(b) The terms weigh. The god carries every standing term as cold in its own belly: with more than twelve standing it is slow, at half its pace; with more than forty it cannot use Winter's Reach at all.
And one more: say whether a yes given under threat of force binds, or does not. Either is open to you and is written as you say.
The Keeper's reasons: being bound by your own careless word is a penalty you pay only if you break it, and this table writes such a thing as a limit. A price has to fall in the ordinary use. (a) puts your winter in the hands of anyone who dares break a term; (b) puts a ceiling on how many you can hold. Each term is spoken by you to one mortal, so the power cannot cover a whole people in a year except at that pace.
On your question, whether a term can be carried by a ruler's seal so that a merchant-prince speaks it in your name: not on these terms. A mortal speaking for you changes what you can do and is a power of yours, priced on you. Two ways are open: take a weakness tonight that buys it as one step (the offer of a weakness stands for you once, and you have not used it), or propose it on Night 3 as an edge of your people, priced there.
