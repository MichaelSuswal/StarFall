"Earshot", "a spoken voice" and "speaking distance" are as far as an ordinary voice carries in open air, about 50 paces, and not through walls. - Unruled. What any power of yours does to another god is unruled until it happens. So is its working on creatures that are more than ordinary, and on things made or kept by more than ordinary craft, until met.

### The Sleeper's Door
Draft text:
Description: When a mortal is asleep within a day's walk (20 miles) of the god, and the god has met that mortal in person or that mortal keeps a lamp lit for the god, the god may enter the sleeper's dream. It may be seen there as a figure or a voice in any seeming it chooses, and may speak, show images and answer what the sleeper says. It perceives only what happens inside the dream. The sleeper remembers it as a vivid dream and may or may not believe it.
Drawback: It marks them. For the rest of their life a sleeper so entered feels the god near, within about 200 yards, as a weight at the back of the mind. They are not told what the weight is.
Limits: One sleeper in a night. While the god is in a dream its body lies as one asleep, deaf and blind to what is around it, and it cannot wake itself until the dream ends or the sleeper wakes; this is a condition of use, not the price. The dream ends when the sleeper wakes or is woken. It cannot read the sleeper's mind, see where the sleeper is, or force any act; it rules no mind. Natural sleep and sleep from drink or drug count; a trance of a worker's own making does not, and a sleep too deep for dreaming has no door. It sends nothing of substance. Its own people are entered and marked the same as any. A god asleep, or a sleeper who is more than ordinary, is unruled until met. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: you offered the mark, and it is taken as the price. The body lying helpless is how the power is used, so it is a limit. "One dream at a time" would let you visit a whole people in a season of nights; one sleeper in a night is written so that a word in a dream stays a rare thing. Drugged sleep is ruled as above, as you asked.

### The Lamp-Sight
Draft text:
Description: When a lamp or a hearth fire has been lit for the god by a mortal, in its name, and is burning within a day's walk (20 miles) of the god, the god may look through it. It sees what the flame lights and hears what is said within its light, as one standing at the lamp, for as long as it attends. One lamp at a time.
Drawback: one of the two under Choices.
Limits: What is there now, never what was there before the god looked. While it looks it is half deaf and half blind to its own surroundings; a condition of use. A lamp put out or broken while the god looks strikes it as a blow, and it is dazed for a quarter of an hour. An unlit lamp does not serve, nor a lamp lit for another or for no one, nor an ordinary cooking fire. The god's own lantern, lit by its own hand, is not a lamp lit for it. It rules no mind. Not applicable: falling, carrying, water.
Choices:
(a) It shows. While the god looks, the flame stands straight and still and does not flicker, in any draught, and anyone in its light who watches it can see that it does.
(b) The god pays. For as long as it looked, and as long again, its own eyes are dazzled: it sees nothing in its own body's place but light and dark.
And one more, on reach:
(c) As drafted: any lamp within a day's walk, sight and hearing.
(d) Any lamp lit for it, at any distance, sight only: it hears nothing through the flame.
The Keeper's reasons: you gave this power no reach, and a power that sees and hears at any distance wherever a lamp is kept is more than this table gives a newly landed god; (c) and (d) are the two ways it can stand. The drawback you wrote is partly how the power is used and partly a blow that falls only if someone breaks the lamp, which a careful god seldom pays; a price is paid each time. "What has happened there" is not written: the flame shows the room as it is while you look. You asked that it be provisional until its shape was reviewed: this is the review.

### The Lamp I Gather At
Draft text:

Drawback: One item of memory or habit, by logged roll from its own record.
Limits: The standing return scale, first rung: a season, one item. "Kept for it" means a lamp lit for the god by a mortal that is still tended, burning or not on the day. A body held, chained, pitted or shut in is held, not broken. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, being watched, minds.
Choices: for the time when no lamp is kept for you anywhere:
(a) You gather at the hearth nearest where your body was destroyed where a fire is kept through the night.
(b) As you wrote it: with no lamp kept for you, you do not gather. You lie scattered until some mortal lights one for you, and the season runs from that lighting. This is beyond what the scale requires and buys nothing.
Say also whether you are told what went or find the gap yourself, and say if you want the month at two items instead.
The Keeper's reasons: you cannot die, so the card must say what happens on a day when there is no lamp. Both ways are open to you.
