# Negotiation: god-trade, Night 3

This file is your instruction for this round and your go for exactly the file it names. A `git pull --rebase --autostash` is always allowed.

## The table's measures, the same at every table
- One edge of ordinary size is about as much as the smallest power on a card, and one weakness pays for about that much: one weakness, one step.
- A weakness is priced by what it costs your people that they do not already pay. A weakness that only restates the country they live in is not counted.
- Where an edge or a weakness bears on a contest, one step is ten points in the odds. Where it bears on work or travel, it is a share of the public rates.
- An edge that would let a mortal do what only you can do is a power of yours, priced on you, and goes on your card. What it costs the mortal is fitted by the Keeper and is not the price.

## Round 1
### The Ford's Scales
Draft text, the edge: your people's weights and measures are agreed by the whole town, kept true by a keeping house with standard stones and sealed measures, and known along the roads. Traders choose their crossing and their market over another where they can, and strangers' disputes are settled there with less quarrel: the town's trade stands one step above what a town of its size and place would have.
Limits: it is custom and it works by hand, without you. It gives nothing to your Fair Weight and takes nothing from it. "Known for a day's walk and more" is what they believe; how far the name has in truth travelled is the world's.

Draft text, the weakness: open gates. The town keeps no standing watch and no paid fighting men, and by day no gate is barred against a peaceful comer. A watch is raised from the wards only when the horn is blown. Against a raid or any surprise, every contest on the first day is one step against them; from the second day the wards stand as any town's would.

Choices: none. The price goes one way: the weakness you offered pays for the edge you asked, one for one.

The Keeper's reasons: the edge is the size you said. The weakness is one they would notice the first season a stranger took advantage, as you wrote, and it is counted in full.

### Not priced
The number: about five thousand is the usual number, so there is no cost of size to price. The tongue for salt is magic on the world's terms and is fitted by the Keeper, the number of workers included. The wicket you ask for in the river gate is a custom the town may keep or refuse; it is heard and not promised. The rest is ordinary culture and stands as written.

Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons.
