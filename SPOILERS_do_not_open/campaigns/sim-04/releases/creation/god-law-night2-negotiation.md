# Negotiation: god-law, Night 2

This file is your instruction for this round and your go for exactly the file it names. A `git pull` is always allowed. Your powers, priced. You proposed three powers and one weakness that buys.

## Round 1

### How this table prices four things for every god
These are not offers. They are how the same thing is priced for any god, and they are written into your card where they apply.
- A loss of memory or habit. Where a price takes "one item of memory or habit", one item is removed from your own record (your record of other gods, or your goals), picked by a logged roll, never chosen. You are told what went or you find the gap yourself, as you say.
- A return from a destroyed body. A season to return costs one item; a month costs two; nothing is faster. The rung is chosen now and written on the card; it is not chosen again at each return. You may name the ground you return at, so long as it is ground of your own kind and is fixed by a rule on your card, never picked at the time. From that ground you travel at your own pace. A body held, shut in or scattered is held, not broken. While you are gone, whatever of yours needs you to keep it up lapses.
- Measures. Where your powers say "a day's walk" it is 20 miles, the same on any ground. "Earshot", "a spoken voice" and "speaking distance" are as far as an ordinary voice carries in open air, about 50 paces, and not through walls. Where a mortal is left able to feel you near, near is about 200 yards.
- Unruled. What any power of yours does to another god is unruled until it happens. So is its working on creatures that are more than ordinary, and on things made or kept by more than ordinary craft, until met.

### Sworn Word
Draft text:
Description: A mortal who freely swears an oath aloud to the god, within earshot of it, naming it as the Surety or the Tall Witness, becomes sworn. Each oath is a plain exchange, both sides spoken and exact: what the mortal binds themselves to, and what the god binds itself to in return. For as long as the oath stands, the god knows when it is broken: that it was broken, by that person, and how far the breach reaches (a small lie, a broken bargain, a killing). It does not learn why, and it does not learn where the oath-breaker is.
Drawback: The god's own side of every oath binds it exactly as the mortal's binds the mortal, and it must give a side to every oath it accepts. And it feels each breach as a blow, wherever it is: it flinches, loses what it was doing, and is dazed for a quarter of an hour; ten breaches in a day lay it down until the next dawn.
Limits: If the god breaks its own side, the power goes dull for a season: it feels nothing of any oath until the season turns. This follows only from the god's own breach, so it is a limit and buys nothing. Does not count: an oath sworn under force, in a language the swearer does not understand, to another god, on another's behalf, or one ended or released by both sides. An oath ends with the swearer's death. One person may swear many. The god may refuse an oath. Distance makes no difference to the knowing. The power enforces nothing: what a breach costs the breaker is law and men's work. It makes no one trust anyone. It rules no mind. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, water, being watched.
Choices: none.
The Keeper's reasons: accepted nearly as written. I have set "a small blow" so that the Keeper can apply it: a quarter of an hour, and a day's worth of ten puts you down. Having to give your own side to every oath is the larger price and is written first.

### Unmarked Hand
As you wrote it, this hides the trail that leads back from a working of your powers. Your powers leave no such trail: Sworn Word is a knowing, and your return happens when you are gone. So as written it describes nothing only a god can do. It is offered both ways, and you choose.
Choices:
(a) As manner, at no price: you work unseen by ordinary care, as anyone might, and it is written under your Form as how you go about things. It is not a power.
(b) As a power.
Description: What the god does with its own hands, unseen at the doing (a wall mended, a stone set, a load carried, a debt paid), leaves no sign of whose work it was: no giant's footprint, no handprint, no mark of size or strength that a mortal could read back to it.
Drawback: No one will thank it. Work done so has no name on it, and no shrine, oath or worship ever follows from it; if the god later claims it, it is not believed on its word.
Limits: It hides the trail of the work, not the god: seen at the doing, it is seen. It makes no one forget. It does not cover what a mortal does for it. Another god may perceive the work as anyone could. It rules no mind. Not applicable: falling, water.
The Keeper's reasons: an ask that describes no act only a god can do is offered as an ordinary act at no price or as a real power, and the god picks.

### Return to the Ford
Draft text:
Description: When its body is destroyed, the god gathers again, as it was, at the oldest ford or crossing-stone on the river nearest where it was destroyed. It takes a season.
Drawback: One item of memory or habit, by logged roll from its own record, not told to the god.
Limits: The standing return scale, first rung: a season, one item. With no ford or stone on that river, the nearest crossing of any water; with none, the nearest standing boundary stone; with none, the nearest place where two roads meet. A body bound, walled in or sunk is held, not broken. Not applicable: light and dark, falling, carrying, being watched, minds.
Choices: none. Say if you want the month at two items instead.
The Keeper's reasons: the scale as it stands for every god, on the ground you named.

### Trade: Guest-law
Draft text:
Weakness: For a night and the morning after, once a mortal has eaten at the god's fire or table with its knowledge and by its leave, the god cannot raise a hand to harm that person and cannot speak a falsehood to them. It may keep silent. It ends at noon after the night, or when the guest first does violence or theft against the god or its household, or when the guest leaves. It does not hold for one who ate unbidden.
What it buys: one of the two under Choices.
Choices:
(a) As offered, the weakness leaves out the god's own people. It buys this: an oath sworn aloud at a household or village shrine of the god, in its name, within a day's walk (20 miles) of where the god then is, binds as if sworn in earshot.
(b) The weakness holds for the god's own people as well, whenever one of them eats at its fire or table by its leave. It buys this: an oath sworn aloud at any household or village shrine of the god, in its name, at any distance, binds as if sworn in earshot.
The Keeper's reasons: the weakness is real: it is something you do not already pay, and anyone who learns of it can sit at your fire and ask. But one weakness buys one step, and from fifty paces to every shrine wherever it stands is more than one: it would take the god out of its own power and put a whole people's oaths in reach in a year. (a) is one step for the weakness as offered. (b) is the reach you asked for, against a weakness that costs you among your own people too. In either, the god still gives its own side to each such oath; a shrine oath that names no side of the god's is no oath under this power.
