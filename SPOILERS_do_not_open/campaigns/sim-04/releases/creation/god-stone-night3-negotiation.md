# Negotiation: god-stone, Night 3

This file is your instruction for this round and your go for exactly the file it names. A `git pull --rebase --autostash` is always allowed.

## The table's measures, the same at every table
- One edge of ordinary size is about as much as the smallest power on a card, and one weakness pays for about that much: one weakness, one step.
- A weakness is priced by what it costs your people that they do not already pay. A weakness that only restates the country they live in is not counted.
- Where an edge or a weakness bears on a contest, one step is ten points in the odds. Where it bears on work or travel, it is a share of the public rates.
- An edge that would let a mortal do what only you can do is a power of yours, priced on you, and goes on your card. What it costs the mortal is fitted by the Keeper and is not the price.

## Round 1
### The fitting of stone
Draft text, the edge: the masons of your people read the grain of rock and lay dry stone with unusual skill. It is a skill of lore and practice, done at a mortal crew's pace with mortal tools. It takes nothing from Shaping the Stone and needs nothing from you.

As you asked it, the edge is two things, and each is about one step:
- Their walls stand. Dry stone of their laying stands through frost and ordinary earthquake as other peoples' does not.
- Their work is quick. You asked half the time and half the labour. On the public rates that is 3 worker-days a yard of built wall in place of 6, and 1 in place of 2 for dry stone to head height.

Draft text, the weakness: as you offered it, it is also more than one thing, and not all of it counts.
- Their harvests run close to the edge of their needs: at landing their stores cover the winter and little more; one bad year is hunger, two in a row are deaths among the old and the youngest. This counts. It is one weakness.
- Poor riders, poor sailors, slow to adapt outside the range: for a people of a high valley this restates the country they live in. They do not pay it in any ordinary year, so it is not counted.

Choices:
(a) One weakness, one step. The thin harvests pay for one of the two: walls that stand, at an ordinary crew's pace; or work at two thirds of the labour (4 worker-days a yard, not 3), with walls that stand as well as any good mason's. You say which.
(b) Both, as you asked them, for a second weakness that counts. You may name one. One the Keeper can price, as a measure of size and no more: every bronze tool they use comes in through the notches, they have no ore of their own and few smiths, so whenever the ways out are shut to their traders for more than a season, by snow, war or any other cause, their stonework falls to an ordinary crew's pace, and after a year it stops as the tools wear out.

The Keeper's reasons: half the labour on every wall, terrace, pier and gatehouse is a large thing for a people beside a god who already shapes stone at twenty masons' rate; one weakness buys one step at every table. The thin harvest is real and is counted in full.

### Not priced
The stone-feelers are magic on the world's terms and are fitted by the Keeper, the number of workers included. The moot house of timber and turf, the cairns, guest-right and the rest are ordinary culture and stand as written.
