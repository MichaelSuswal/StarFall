# The cast: campaign sim-04

Written on creation night 3b, 2026-11-22. A few world-level figures, each with its nature and history fixed and its place on the map. Everyone else rises from play. Drawn from the deep history and the Q17 pool (ordinary working people of standing in a small place, whose story turns on noticing, on standing between two sides, and on holding to a word or a question at a cost; no chosen heroes). No one here is scripted: each does what its nature and the year give it. Each `Limit:` line is copied from `mysteries.md`, never retyped.

## Lake Hesh [r28]
The lake at the middle of the land, under the star. One living thing, older than the last fall, that eats what makes things particular and stores it; fed from above on nights the star dims; patient past any god; swollen under this star. It wants the fall. It does not speak, answers nothing and cannot be bargained with. History: `deep-history.md`. Mystery: L01.

## Those in the star
The risen: gods that grew past the mark under this star and went up, the three who came down 2,614 years before the arrival among them (Marrec, Ammeth, Hollin), with others far older. Not one being, and they do not agree. Under this star most want the fall soon. They reach down as the thread to Lake Hesh [r28] and as voices in dreams. Mysteries: L01, L03.
Limit: The voices know what they were, what they want and the powers they offer. They do not know why the plane keeps accounts, what they will become after a fall, whether they are one or many, or the year of the fall, and asked these they say they do not know. They cannot lie as mortals lie and have no view but their own. They speak only in dreams, only to one sleeping on the shore of Lake Hesh [r28] on a night the star dims or to a god at or past four fifths of the ceiling, one exchange in a night, and they answer no question about the star's height or its rate.

## The Oum, at Koldry [f81]
A whole people, the one people of the land who are not human: about 800, tall, grey, slow, in the Koldry Hills [r26]. They keep the Count on baked clay. The counter who now holds it is Udh, old even for the Oum, at Koldry [f81]. They came from the country of plane entry [p02] before its star fell and do not know it. Mysteries: E01, L03, S08.
Limit: The Oum know only what is entered in the Count: arrivals, risings, deaths, debts, terms, and where the great light stood against the peaks at each hundredth year, in fists and fingers. They do not know causes, rates, the lake's nature, the star's nature, what a risen god becomes or anything the Count does not hold, and they say so. They do not lie and do not volunteer. They answer only a question put in person at Koldry [f81] by one who first gives an entry of equal weight for the Count, one question for one entry, no more than one in a season from any one asker, and a question that asks them to reckon, judge or guess is answered "It is not entered."

## The carriers
A band of about forty, human-seeming, who walk one round through the land every thirty years, from Varn [f78] to Carnmouth [f42], singing lists of names; due in Year 10 and Year 40. The one who walks first is called Marda; when she dies another takes the place and the name of first walker, not hers. Their old who can no longer walk live out their days on Farholm [f70], about a dozen at any time, keeping the boats that take the band off; there a list that has lost a part is made whole again from the memory of the old. The old on Farholm [f70] do not sing to strangers. Mystery: L04.
Limit: The carriers know the names in their lists, the order of their round, and that a name carried long enough comes back. They do not know why, what the star is, when anything will happen, or the name of the country the gods came from, and they say so. They answer only when spoken to directly by the one who asks, in the nights of a passing, once to any asker in a passing; they sing nothing under compulsion, and no one of them holds a whole song.

## The shore people, at Lowater [f67], Nenna [f66] and Ammot [f68]
A whole people, about 6,000, what is left of the first folk of the lake. Their names are going. Mystery: L01.
Limit: The shore people know what they have seen at the water and what their grandparents told them. They do not know what the lake is or why names go, and most do not answer at all. One of the oldest will speak, once, to a stranger who has stayed a night among them and asked nothing the first day.

## Caddo Reth, at Varn [f78]
A traveler from afar: a carter of about fifty for a pan-holder of Varn [f78], thirteen years on the coast, the last of a line of sworn riders that left the country of plane entry [p01] about 203 years before the arrival. Steady, courteous, close-mouthed, and tired of being the only one of his kind. He keeps a framed saddle with iron foot-loops, a warrant on pressed bark, and a riding-chant. Mystery: L02.
Limit: Caddo Reth knows his chant, his warrant's words, his craft with horse and saddle, and what his father told him: that the line rode out of a river country rather than be hunted. He does not know what the names in the chant are, what the warrant bound, how far the country lies or that it has ended, and he says so. He answers anyone who asks him civilly at Varn [f78], as often as they like, and recites the chant only to one he has come to trust, which takes a season of dealing.

## Isk Vennar, at Skarhaven [f83]
A traveler from afar: a net-mender of about thirty at Skarhaven [f83], seven years on the cold coast, the last of a line that set out from under the young star of plane entry [p03] more than six centuries ago. Quick, exact, impatient with guesses, and ashamed of how much of her craft she does not have. She keeps a sighting arc of brass. Mystery: E01.
Limit: Isk Vennar knows how her arc is held and read and about half of how one is made. She does not know why her line walked, what the star here is, its height or its rate, and she has taken no reading here, so she has no figure to give. She answers anyone who comes to her at Skarhaven [f83] and asks; teaching the making takes a year beside her, and the half she lacks must be worked out by the learner.

## Hesper Vane, at Hallisk [f35]
From the Q17 pool: a clerk of the hall of weights at Hallisk [f35], about forty, who has found that the numbers do not add up and has said so to people who did not want to hear it. Careful, stubborn, loyal to the rolls before the court. Mystery: S02.
Limit: Hesper Vane knows the granary counts and yields of the royal rolls for sixty years and that they fall in a pattern, worst on the oldest fields. She does not know the cause and believes it is theft. She answers one who comes to the hall of weights at Hallisk [f35] with the court's leave or her trust, and shows a roll only in the hall, one roll at a visit.

## The unnamed fallen stone [f69]
Not a being. A warm blue-grey stone in Ammerholt [r24] that fell in the hour the gods came down and dreams at whoever sleeps beside it. It answers nothing. Mystery: L02.
