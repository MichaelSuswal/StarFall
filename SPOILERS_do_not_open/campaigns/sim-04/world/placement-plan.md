# Placement plan: campaign sim-04

Written on creation night 3a, 2026-11-21, before the paint. A written geography and not a drawing. The sheets are painted from it, the data is traced onto the paint, and where the paint departs the plan yields. Names here are working names; the map's names and ids are set at tracing. No release or record takes a distance, direction or sight line from this file.

## The sheet and its scale

One continent on one square sheet, north at the top, with sea on all four sides so that no land runs off the sheet. The land is planned about 1,050 miles from west to east and about 900 from north to south, broad everywhere, with no long peninsula; nowhere under 600 across in its overall shape, about 1,000 on average. On a sheet 1,024 pixels square with the land filling about five sixths of it, that is about 1.2 miles to the pixel, set finally at tracing from the painted land. A day's walk on rough ground is about twelve pixels, far under one grid cell (the grid is five columns by four rows, each cell about 250 miles by 310).

The star's living radius is 70 days' walk from its center, the lake: about 980 miles on rough ground and 1,400 on a good road. No land on the sheet lies more than about 650 miles from the lake. The thinning country and the Pale are not on the sheet; they lie past the sea and the sheet's fogged edges.

## The center

A great round lake at the middle of the land, under the star's center (the star's roll, r-0004): about 70 miles across, in a wide shallow basin of meadow ringed at a distance by low worn hills. Its one outflow runs west. The hoarder is the lake. A godless people lives on its shore.

## The ring of seats

Eight seats in a ring round the lake, each about 330 to 400 miles from it and about 260 to 320 miles from the next seat round the ring: three weeks' walk and more on rough ground. None within sight of the lake or of another seat. Round the ring from the north, each in the country its own second answer asked and the placement draft gave:

1. North: the cold coast. A belt of snow-holding high ground along the north coast, cut by long narrow valleys that run north to a grey sea and close in winter. god-ice lands at the head of the longest valley, above the tree line; a walled seat lies far down it, steadings on its sides, a small lake below the landing.
2. North-east: the high range. The highest mountains of the land fill the north-east, with notched passes, and one broad valley cupped inside them. god-stone lands on a ledge on that valley's rim; a gathered town of fitted stone lies on the floor, terraces on the sides, two notches in the far wall.
3. East: forest and fen. A great dark forest, a wide fen along its southern edge with a slow river through it, hills beyond the water. god-dreams lands at the wood's edge above the fen; a walled seat stands on a rise at the fen's edge, steadings scattered round it.
4. South-east: the braided mist rivers. Many threads of river running south-east to the coast through low wooded country, with few fords. god-secrets lands on a gravel bar at a ford; its people are a moving people with a winter seat on a sheltered bend.
5. South: the long river. It rises in springs in the high heath south of the lake basin, runs south through braided reaches, then slow and brown through wet meadows to a marsh delta on the south coast. god-rivers lands on the bank at a bend in the middle reach, beside a walled town.
6. South-west: the flowering valley. A broad river valley running south-west to the sea between two lines of heather upland, with water meadows on the floor, hedged fields and orchards on the lower slopes and old woods in the side valleys. The mildest of the eight countries. The player's god lands on a low rise at a meadow's edge on the valley floor, as its Day 3 brief described: smoke from more than one place, laid hedges, pruned orchards, a rutted cart track along the foot of the slope, grazed turf.
7. West: the ford on the road. The lake's outflow, a wide river, runs west through low hills to the western sea; an old road crosses it at a ford, and a second road runs along the bank. god-trade lands on the road on a low hill above the ford; a walled town stands at it.
8. North-west: the slow rivers. Two wide slow rivers wander through flat lowland to the north-west coast, with hills at the edges and towns at the crossings. god-law lands on a river terrace above a crossing with a toll-post and market town, boundary stones along the terrace.

## The player's Day 3 wishes, placed or not

Each is placed only where the world can hold it; the map and the records say what stands.
- The valley's own name, as its people use it: stands, as the name of their home. The valley's river and other ground the player did not name and did not leave for play are the Keeper's to name.
- A dozen or so hamlets, no town, no seat: the valley can hold it. Thirteen hamlets, unnamed (placeholders "unnamed hamlet 1" to "unnamed hamlet 13"), strung along the valley; the highest stands where the upland starts, as the player wished for the mortal it made.
- The river crossing where the reeves meet, with a market ground that fills a few days a year: placed, a ford on the valley's river near its middle. It is how this people already orders itself, and a ford with a fair ground is ordinary.
- The old track along the valley foot, and the story of a larger people before: placed as the worn cart track the brief already showed. Under part of it runs one of the old straight roads the whole world has (pool, Q12, widespread). What the story gets right and wrong is 3b's.
- The roofless stone hall by the old track at the highest hamlet: placed, as a ruin of the kind the old roads have beside them in many countries. What wild swarms do there is settled in the sealed side of the people's record, from ordinary causes.
- "Other valleys", "the region", "neighbors", "a border": the country round the valley is open upland with scattered steadings, the western road towns to the north, and the high heath to the east. No kingdom borders the valley closely.

## Held and open land

About half the land is held and half open (Q4 at 5), by area and by count of regions. The eight god-led peoples are small and hold little. Most held land is the godless peoples'. Four godless peoples (Q4 result 5 lies in 4 to 7):

1. An old inland kingdom on the wide plain of the west and north-west, between the slow rivers and the western road, reaching toward the lake basin: the largest people of the land, rooted, with a court, roads, weights and measures of its own, old and wearing. Working name: the plain kingdom.
2. A league of old salt towns on the south coast, between the long river's delta and the mist rivers: they hold the salt pans, the one great source of the world's scarce thing, and trade it up every river. Working name: the salt towns.
3. The people of the lake shore, at the center: fishers and reed-cutters who do not fish the deep water, few, quiet, and thinning in their names. Working name: the shore people.
4. The one non-human people (Q13 at 2, the most shared kind): old, slow, patient, of long memory, keepers of their own count, few. They hold a small hill country behind the eastern forest, toward the east coast. Working name: the old people.

The rare second kind (a small folk of tunnels or deep wood) is not placed: three voices of eight named it, the scale came out at 2, "two at most", and nothing in the star or the lore needs it. The carriers are not a people of the land: they hold no ground and are a walking few from outside; their body and visits are 3b's (3b.4, 3b.6).

Open land: the high heath south of the lake; the rough hills between the high range and the eastern forest; the moor and grass between the plain kingdom and the cold coast; the uplands round the flowering valley; the country between the long river and the valley; the wild margins of every coast not named above. Open land is not empty: scattered steadings, herders and woodcutters who answer to no one.

## Waters, ranges and what follows from them

Rivers run downhill to a coast or to the lake. The high range sends short rivers south-west into the lake and east into the fen. The lake's one outflow is the wide western river. The long river and the valley's river rise on the high heath and the uplands and do not touch the lake. The slow rivers of the north-west rise in the low hills north of the plain. The cold coast's valleys are short and steep. Rain comes off the western sea: the west and south-west are the greenest country, the lake basin is mild, the country in the lee of the high range (the rough hills, the edge of the forest) is drier, and the north is cold with long snow. Borders lie where the land changes: the heath's edge, the hill lines, the fen, the rivers.

Every terrain on the Night 1 list is here: several separate river systems, a high range with passes and a cupped valley, snow country and a cold coast with closing valleys, forest and fen with hills, meadow and orchard country with heather uplands and old woods, marsh, low hills, wild country between, a wild margin, and the lake.

Salt: the salt pans of the south coast; a small brine spring country in the plain kingdom. Everyone else buys.

## The remainder

Fell in godless country the night the gods came (the star's roll, r-0006). Placed by roll among the eight adjacent pairs of the ring, the pairs listed in the roll's name before it was made: face 2, between god-stone and god-dreams [r-0140]. Where along that stretch: face 76, so 25 plus 38, 63 hundredths of the way from god-stone's seat toward god-dreams' [r-0141]. That is in the rough open hills between the high range and the eastern forest, nearer the forest: open land, with scattered hill steadings and no people's rule. Its point is set at tracing on the line between the two seats as painted.

## The underground

The world has an underground of limited extent (keeper log, 1.5, Q12): the ways behind the sealed doors. It is not a country below. By the run 02 principle it is carried as sites on the surface sheet, each door, stair or vault a point with what lies inside it in its notes. The longest is the old way under the roots of the high range, a few tens of miles of cut gallery between two sealed doors. An underground sheet is not approved in `campaigns/sim-04/state/images.md`; a request for one is written to the held folder and not sent, and the underground is traced as data only on the surface grid.
