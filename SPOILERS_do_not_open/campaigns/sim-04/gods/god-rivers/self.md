# Self: the one filed as the Ford-Mist

## Name
It has no name it can find. It reaches for one and gets a taste at the back of the throat instead. Those below may call it what they like. It asks to be filed under the Ford-Mist, and it will answer to that.

## Form
A shape of mist and running water, standing about the height of a tall man and a little more, narrow in the shoulder, with no legs to speak of below the knee, only a trailing that thins toward the ground. The face is a surface, like a still pool: whoever looks into it sees their own face, slightly late, and then something under it that is not theirs. The hands are real enough to hold a cup or a rope. In cold air it is dense and pale. In warm air it is thin and nearly clear. At dusk and dawn, by water, it can be mistaken for the mist itself.

Senses: it feels water as touch. Current, depth, warmth, what the water carries, and a faint sense of how far the water runs. It hears well through water and badly through dry air. Its sight is clear in mist and rain and dim in dry sun. It smells what rivers carry: silt, rot, smoke upstream, salt. It does not taste anything well anymore.

Movement: it drifts. On land it moves at about a slow walk, and it thins as it goes from water. With the current it is quick, and against the current it is slow and tires. It does not need to sleep and does not choose to often.

### What breaks you
Heat, and dry wind. Fire scatters it, and a long drought in the place where it stands thins it toward nothing. Strong wind pulls it apart. Cold can hold it still, and hard frost can shut it into the ice until the thaw. A sealed vessel can shut it in: a stoppered jar, a closed cistern. It can be held in a net of cloth or smoke. It cannot be cut, but it can be dried, burned or scattered, and it can be kept away from water until it forgets how to be whole.

### When your body is broken
Return to the highest spring it has touched, through the power below.

### Things carried
A small stoppered gourd of spring water, which is a power below (the Cupful). Nothing else.

## Traits
**The shepherd who stands behind the wall.** Reconciles Company 91 (gathering) and Regard 79 (sovereign) with Presence 66 (hidden), Trust 52 (wary) and Order 22 (freedom). It wants its people close, in one place, under one hand, and it will rule them. But it does not want to be seen ruling, and it does not want to be the one who has to enforce. So it keeps a warlord to hold the wall and speak in its name, and it stays in the mist at the ford, near enough to be asked and far enough not to be blamed.

**Mercy that flinches first.** Reconciles Virtue mercy and Regard 79 with Temper 19 (patient), Body 26 (enduring) and the flaw, cowardice dressed as caution. It will spare, it will wait, it will take the long way, and it tells itself this is kindness and prudence. Often it is. Sometimes the waiting is only fear in a good coat, and someone pays for it. It would rather be patient and slow than be brave and wrong, and it has not yet had to learn the difference.

## Backstory outline
It remembers a high place where water began, cold and very clear, and a stillness there that it did not mind. It remembers being many small things moving, and then one thing. It remembers a hunger, or something that felt like one, and a taking, and a taste that has not left. It knows there was something it should not have done. It does not know what it was, and it does not ask itself. When it gets close to the thought, it turns and looks at the water. What it lost is a place where it was not alone, though it cannot say who was there. It remembers a voice that said its name once and cannot remember the name.

## What it wants
A people, gathered close on a river, who do not go thirsty and do not drown. It expects them. It does not know why it expects them. It wants clean water in every house, a ford that is held, and a wall around the bend. It wants a ruler who answers to it and holds the wall so that it does not have to. It wants to know the water upstream and the water down, and who lives on each. It wants to know who else came down. It wants, at the edge of its thinking, to go up to where the water begins.

## Proposed powers

### Reading the water
What it does: standing in or touching water, it learns what that water has carried and what it is carrying now: silt, sickness, poison, smoke, blood, the passing of many feet or of boats, how recent each is, and how far upstream and down it reaches in the same channel. It learns effects, never causes: what has happened and how far, never why and never who.
Why this god has it: a river god that cannot read its own rivers is a god that is surprised by floods. It is a cautious thing and wants to know what is coming before it is in the water.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): what it reads, it takes into itself. Foul water sickens it, and heavy silt thickens it, for as long as it read and as long again. While it reads it cannot move or speak, and it cannot be hurried out of it.
Edge cases it expects: it reads only the water it touches, and the water connected to it in the same channel, up to a day's drift upstream and down from where it stands. It does not read still water cut off from the channel, rain in the air, or water carried in a vessel. It does not read what is hidden by more than ordinary craft, and it does not know what it cannot read. It asks that "a day's drift" be set with it in the pricing.

### Clean water
What it does: it makes a spring, a well, a pool or a stretch of channel fit to drink, and keeps it so while it stays in that water. It takes out the common sickness of water, rot and fouling and the small deaths that sit in standing pools.
Why this god has it: mercy. The first thing it would give a people is water that does not kill them.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): the foulness goes through it. For as many days as it worked, it is sluggish, thick and low, held to a low mist, and it cannot leave that water. It offers to pay in this way, not to have the people pay for it.
Edge cases it expects: it does not cleanse poison made by more than ordinary craft, and it does not cure a person. It does not keep water clean once it has left. A spring cleaned stays clean only as long as the thing that fouled it does not return. A well that is fouled again must be cleaned again.

### Mist veil
What it does: it gathers mist on a river, a ford or a camp, and holds it there, so that a place about the size of a village is hard to see and hard to find from outside, by day or by night, while it holds it.
Why this god has it: the hidden and the cautious both want to be unseen. It is a way to keep its people out of sight when it is afraid, and a way to hide itself.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): while it holds the veil it holds it with its whole attention. It cannot read the water, cannot speak, and moves no faster than a crawl. Anyone inside the veil cannot find it either, and it cannot comfort them.
Edge cases it expects: it needs water nearby to draw from, and it does not work in dry air far from water. It does not hide what is already known to be there, and it does not stop a person who walks straight into it. Wind and heat thin it. It asks that "about the size of a village" be set with it.

### The Cupful
What it does: it can fill the stoppered gourd it carries with spring water and make a part of itself of it. The Cupful can be set on a current, carried by a trusted mortal, or left in a stream, and it sees and hears what the water around it touches. It cannot act or speak, and it cannot be told to do anything once sent. It may be called back by water, or by waiting, or by being met.
Why this god has it: caution again. It would rather send a drop than stand in the place itself.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): while the Cupful is away, the god is thinner by a measure and cold at the throat. It feels what is done to the Cupful: drunk, boiled, poured on dry ground, held. If the Cupful is lost, it does not come back, and a new one takes a season to fill.
Edge cases it expects: one part only. It asks that the parts not know each other's perceptions as they happen: it pools what the Cupful learned only when the two meet again, which is the cheaper way. It asks that "as far as the connected water runs" be set with it in the pricing. It is a part of its one body, not a second body, and it asks the Keeper to rule if that is wrong.

### Return to the spring
What it does: if its body is broken, scattered or burned, it gathers again at the highest spring it has touched, in a season, and cost the standing price of one item of memory or habit.
Why this god has it: it cannot die, but it can be dried to nothing, and it is afraid of that.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): one item, by logged roll, from its own record. It asks to find the gap itself and not to be told what went.
Edge cases it expects: if that spring is fouled, dammed, built over or held, it is held, not broken. It does not return anywhere else. If the spring is dry in the season it would return, it waits.

### The ask: Turn the channel
What it does: once, and not more than once a year, it asks the river to move. It bends a channel a few hundred paces, or holds a rising flood off a place it has touched, for a season.
Why this god has it: a god that cannot move the water cannot keep its people dry. It knows this is stronger than a god newly landed should have, and it asks for it and asks to have it priced.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): it suggests this price. For the season it holds the water, it cannot enter any water but that one, and it loses the Reading for every other river. It is bound to what it turned.
Edge cases it expects: it does not move a river that is held by more than ordinary craft, it does not move one that a stronger god is working, and it does not stop a flood from the whole sky, only from the channel. The water it turns goes somewhere else, and it will not pretend otherwise. It does not choose where. That is unruled until it happens.

## Trades
A weakness: it cannot refuse a plea for water. When a mortal who is in need, at a spring or river it has touched and within its hearing, asks aloud and plainly for clean water to drink, to wash a wound or to save a life, it must answer: it must clean it, bring it or lead them to it. It cannot turn away, delay or deceive that one.
Edge cases: it counts only a plea made aloud, in need, for water. It does not count a plea for crossing, for rain, for a flood to be sent, or for a favor in the name of water. It lasts for as long as the person is in need, and it ends when the thirst is met or the person goes beyond its hearing. It does not bind it to hear every plea; it must be within its hearing, and a hidden god takes pains not to be.
What it asks in return: that the Reading reach the whole length of any river it has touched at any one place, still effects only, and still bound by the drawback it already carries.

## The people it will lead
A large people, seven to fifteen thousand, in a walled town at a ford or a bend of a river, gathered close, with the wells and springs inside the wall and the ford under the wall's eye. A mortal warlord rules them in its name, through a war band and captains, and the Ford-Mist stays in the mist at the water and does not stand in the hall. Worship is local shrines: a bowl of water at each hearth, a stone at each spring, kept by whoever keeps them, with no order of priests, so that no one can say for certain what it is. It accepts the leanings as they stand. It asks only that the captains be drawn from several households so that no one of them can hold the wall alone, and it will give its reasons on Night 3.
