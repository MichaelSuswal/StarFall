## Answer 1
Water finding its level. A wide valley at first light, mist lying on the river, and many small fires along the banks, each one close to the next. The world at its truest is a place where everything is connected by something that moves, and no one is left standing alone on dry ground. We would like it to feel crowded in the kind way.

## Answer 2
River country. Springs in the high places, braided channels in the middle, slow brown water and wet meadows below. We want a land that is cut by water in every direction, so that every road has to ask a river for permission. Marsh at the edges is fine. We do not care for deserts.

## Answer 3
Four seasons, with the turning of each one clear and not too harsh. A spring that is wet and loud with meltwater. A summer that is long enough to ripen things but does not dry the rivers out. An autumn of heavy rain. A winter that is cold but does not freeze the channels to the bed, so that the water keeps moving under the ice. We would not have the rivers fail. Failure of the water is the one thing we would ask the world to spare us.

When each new year finds us, we would wake into the spring melt, with the springs just opened. We wake into spring.

## Answer 4
Some of each, and we would like to meet the old kingdoms first. Wild country is good to have behind us, but we would rather find rooted neighbors downstream before we find the wilderness. Those who are already there know where the water goes. We would also like the wild country to be nearby, in case we need somewhere to go.

## Answer 5
Thousands of years, with ruins under ruins. A people who have been here long enough to have named every bend of the river and then forgotten half the names. We like that old things sit beneath the new things and that the water has been carrying their silt for a very long time.

## Answer 6
Closer to the rumor most people doubt, with a wise-woman in some valleys and not in all. It should be rare enough that those who have it are looked at sideways, and common enough that no one is surprised when a spring is said to be holy. We would not have it everywhere. Where it is everywhere, a god has less to offer.

## Answer 7
It should feel like thirst. A pull in the throat and the hands, a wanting that is satisfied only by the doing. Its cost should be something taken from the body, a little warmth or a little sleep, and never a thing that can be hidden easily. We would not have mortals pay with other mortals. That seems to us a cruel price and we would have the world refuse it.

## Answer 8
Closer to the one-eyed man, but not by much. A god should stand out enough that mortals can be led, and not so much that they cannot be hidden from. We would prefer that most mortals could not say for certain what a god is, only that something is working in the water and the weather and has not yet been caught at it.

## Answer 9
A few legends, and one or two creatures no one has a name for. Familiar animals along the rivers, with fish and otters and herons and wolves coming down to drink. Then, further off, something large in the deep pools that people do not speak of at night. We would like the strange to be rare, so that when it comes it is felt.

## Answer 10
Flood, and what the flood carries. Drowning, and sickness in the water, and a river that turns against the people who trusted it. Also the fear of being watched by something that does not move. We think the best danger is the kind that looks like kindness until it is too late. Then, of course, each other, but the question asks us to look beyond that, and we do.

## Answer 11
Safe water, and then salt. Clean water in the right place and at the right time. We would have the world make mortals bargain for it, with wells and weirs and channels that are fought over and shared. We would like salt to be hard to come by as well, because a people who need salt must travel, and travelers must be fed and welcomed.

## Answer 12
Old roads, and sealed doors under hills near the headwaters. Also a drowned town, somewhere, in a reservoir no one remembers building. We would like to find a thing that was put away with care. We would also like to find that someone else was here before us and was afraid of something.

## Answer 13
Yes, a few, and not many. Peoples of the river and the reed, who live at the edge of the water and who mortals trade with but do not wholly trust. They should be quiet, patient and slow to anger, and they should remember the water's old paths. We would like them to be strange enough that no one can say for certain what they want.

## Answer 14
A few, and they should come by water. Travelers who arrive on a boat that no one recognizes, carrying tools or a way of making things that the people here have not seen. We would want them to be few enough that they are guests and not conquerors. We would be glad of what they know, and wary of what they want.

## Answer 15
A pull felt at any distance, but a faint one, like a current against the leg when wading. We would like to know that others are near without knowing exactly where. Then a knowing only face to face, for the details. We would not have it so strong that a god cannot hide when it needs to. We would rather see than be seen.

## Answer 16
Premise bent: One body is what we would choose, but we would let a god send a part of itself downstream. A thing that is a little of the god, carried by the water, that sees and reports and cannot act. Its cost should be that the god feels it fade, and feels what the water does to it, and cannot take it back if the river is dammed. We would not have several full bodies. That is a thing for a braver god.

## Answer 17
One who is clever and kind, and who is not noticed until it is too late. A ferryman, a midwife, a keeper of a weir. Someone who knows where everyone is going and what they carry, and who is trusted for it. We would like the land to produce a mortal who is good at listening and who, when it matters, chooses mercy at some cost to themselves.

## Answer 18
A companion. Something that waits at a spring in the high places and has been waiting a long time. We would not say what it is. We would like to find it by following the water to the place where the water begins, and to find that it knows us, and that we cannot remember why.

## Answer 19
That nothing we love is ever left alone in the cold. That a people we have gathered stays gathered, and that none of them is ever lost to the water or to us. We do not dare to say this aloud usually, because we have not always kept such a thing. We would ask the world to forgive what we have done, though we do not say what it is.
