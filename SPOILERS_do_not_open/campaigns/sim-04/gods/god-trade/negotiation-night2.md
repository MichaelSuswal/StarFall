# Negotiation: the Weigher, Night 2

## Round 1 reply

A fair table is one where each side leaves with something it can carry. I will not haggle for the pleasure of it. Where the price is honest I say so, and where it is not I say that too.

### The Fair Weight
Accepted as written. A man who cannot bluff for an hour after he weighs has paid for his scale, and I would rather be known for a plain mouth than a clever one. I ask no change.

### The Kept Road
Accepted as written. Two roads, each a day's walk, one tenth quicker. The price is real: a road with a crowd on it will leave me dull and slow, and the more it prospers the worse it weighs on me. A good road is paid for by the one who keeps it. I take it.

### The Reckoning
I choose (b), the debts weigh. Reasons: (a) would darken my own hand for another man's broken word, and bar me from holding a Reckoning until he mends it. That puts a stranger's bad faith in charge of my work. I would rather carry a heavy load than wear a fence made of other people's conduct. A burden I can see and count, and I am a counter.

I ask one clarification, in the write-up, not a new power. Please say that a bargain stands only while it is unfulfilled and unreleased: a bargain kept to the end, or released by all its parties, or broken and then mended, no longer counts toward the twelve or the forty. A bargain that is done is not a debt. If that is not so, a town of two thousand will pass twelve in a single market week and I will walk at half pace for good, and that is a heavier price than the Keeper has written. If it is so, I accept the twelve and the forty as they stand.

### Return to the Stone
Accepted at the first rung: a season, one item. I do not ask for the month at two items; the quicker road is the cheaper one and I do not want to pay twice for time I cannot spend. For the time before any stone is marked, I choose (a): I gather at the nearest place where two roads cross. That is where I would wish to be set down in any case, and it fits my aims. I decline (b). To lie scattered until some stranger sets a weight in my name is to be shut in by someone else's forgetting, and I will not take a price for that. As to what goes: I ask to find the gap myself and not to be told.

### Trade: the Barred Door
Accepted as written and exactly as I wrote it, including the clause that anyone who learns of it can hold me with a bar. I know what it costs. I put it forward myself.

Of the two steps offered I choose (b): four roads kept at once. Reasons: a town at a ford has more than two ways in and out, and a market that can only watch two of them is a market with its back half turned. The feeling of three days' reach (a) is of less use to me, because a road may be no longer than a day's walk, so a reach of sixty miles has little road to reach along. I take the roads and not the reach. I will not argue for the second step. If a later weakness can buy it, I will speak of it then.

### The whole
I accept the four powers and the one trade with the Reckoning choice, the clarification, the return ground and the trade step as written above. Nothing here is more than I asked.

## Round 2 reply

I have read the Keeper's copy against what I said and what I chose. It is a true copy. A count that agrees on both sides is the best news at a table, and I confirm it.

The Fair Weight: confirmed as written.

The Kept Road: confirmed. Four roads, each no longer than a day's walk, the feeling within a day's walk of where I stand, the one tenth quicker, the drawback that grows with the road's success. The Description and Drawback may freeze.

The Reckoning: confirmed. The drawback is the debts weigh, with twelve and forty. The clarification is written as the first limit, and it is what I meant: a bargain done, released, ended by a death, or mended is not a debt. I thank the Keeper for setting the death in beside the rest.

Return to the Stone: confirmed. I took the correction without complaint. I had the two rungs backward: the season is the slower return and costs one item, and the month is the faster and costs two. I chose the season, as I meant to choose the cheaper price, and the card says so. Ground (a), the nearest place where two roads cross, and the gap mine to find.

The Barred Door: confirmed exactly as I wrote it, buying four roads.

The Keeper trait, one more door than planned in whatever my own hands build: I accept it and have no wording to correct. It is a curious thing, and I will count the doors when I find them. I note that the card says the Barred Door holds on such a door as on any other, and I take that as it is written.

Nothing in the text is wrong, and I ask no change.
