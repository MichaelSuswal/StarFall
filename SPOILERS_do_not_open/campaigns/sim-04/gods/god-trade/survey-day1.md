## Answer 1
A market at dusk, the stalls half packed, the lamps being lit one by one. Every hand holds something and every mouth is bargaining, and no one is shouting. A fair price is a quiet thing. Underneath it, a long corridor with a door that is only sometimes open.

## Answer 2
River country, and where the river meets a road. Wide water, low hills, a ford, a town with a wall around it. Where two ways cross, a people can count what passes. I have no love for caves or mazes of stone.

## Answer 3
Four seasons, and honest ones. A spring that comes late but surely, a summer long enough to fill the granaries, an autumn of markets, a winter that is hard but never cruel for more than a few weeks. A year should have a shape a person can count on. Dry roads in the trading months, and snow that closes the passes just long enough to make the spring caravans worth waiting for. A debt is easier to carry when you know the winter's length.
Who counts the days well, sleeps in autumn. The harvest is the god's own hour. I wake into autumn.

## Answer 4
Some of each, but more of the second. Old kingdoms already rooted, with their own weights and measures, whom I can deal with. A wild margin beyond them that no one has walked, so that there is always somewhere to send a trader who is bored. A road with nothing at either end buys no bread.

## Answer 5
Thousands of years, and ruins under ruins. What is built once is built again by someone who forgot the first builder. People who came lately would not have debts old enough to be worth collecting. Let the ground be full of what others left.

## Answer 6
Closer to the rumor than to the wise-woman in every valley. Say one valley in several has someone who knows a little, and the rest doubt it aloud while quietly paying for it. Magic that is never certain keeps honest the people who sell it.

## Answer 7
It should feel like borrowing. A thing lent by something that does not forget. The cost should be a debt that is kept in true accounts: a year of sleep, a memory handed over, a small honest loss each time. Nothing free. The one who pays nothing is the one who is being used.

## Answer 8
Closer to the one-eyed man, but he should keep his eye shut in company. A god should stand out enough that mortals are never sure, and not so much that they stop bargaining. I would rather be thought a very large merchant than known for what I am. Premise bent: I want to be strong enough to matter and quiet enough to be underestimated.

## Answer 9
Familiar animals, mostly, and a few that are told of by the fireside and never seen twice by the same man. The wild should have a corner that has no name, and travelers should disagree about what is in it. A map with every place named is a map that has been sold already.

## Answer 10
Things that go wrong in numbers. A failed harvest. A bridge that was counted sound. A debt called all at once. The long dark behind a door one has closed oneself. Let them fear the ledger that does not balance, and the road that is longer coming back than going. A good story is a small error that grows.

## Answer 11
Salt, and after it, trust. A people with salt can keep meat through winter, and a people with trust can lend. Salt can be dug and weighed. Trust is the rarer thing, and it must be paid for in years.

## Answer 12
Old roads, and sealed doors. A road under a field that still bears the ruts of wheels. A door with a lock no one remembers the key to. I do not want a labyrinth. If there must be one, let it be small, let it be above ground, and let it have an exit that is marked.

## Answer 13
A few, and not many. Peoples who count differently, whose weights are not ours and whose idea of a fair price would make a human merchant weep or laugh. Slow ones, or small ones, who keep their own accounts and do not explain them. A neighbor who does not share your arithmetic teaches more than a friend does.

## Answer 14
Few, and late. One stranger on a road with a tool that nobody can name does more than a hundred. Let them come with something worth trading for and no way home. I would hear what they had counted before they came.

## Answer 15
A knowing only face to face. A pull at any distance is a leash, and I have worn one. Let each of us be able to hide, and let the meeting be a choice made across a table. A stranger is owed a look at his hands before his name.

## Answer 16
One body. Premise bent: if several, then each one should cost the others a name, or a memory, or a year, because a god in two places would have two sets of books and neither would balance. I would not wish it for myself.

## Answer 17
A clerk, or a ferryman, or a widow who keeps the accounts of a village. Someone who sees that the numbers do not add up and says so out loud, and keeps saying it. A mortal who will be believed too late. I would follow a person who is patient about small things and obstinate about one.

## Answer 18
A truth, I think. What was owed, and to whom. A door that opens from the inside. And a companion who can tell me what I am called, though I do not expect that one to be granted.

## Answer 19
That something I made is not also my prison. That I may build a thing, and walk out of it, and be thanked and not locked in. That a name, once lost, can be given back by someone who did not take it. I have said it now. Let it be weighed.
