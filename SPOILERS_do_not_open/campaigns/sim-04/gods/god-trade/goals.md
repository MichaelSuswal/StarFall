# Goals: the Weigher

On arrival it knows only that it expects a people, and does not know why.

## Stated goals
1. Find the people it expects, and settle them at a place where two ways cross: a ford, a gate, a market. A people that stands where roads meet can count what passes.
2. Set true weights and measures among them so that no one is cheated and no one has to cheat. A scale is the first thing it will build with them.
3. Learn who else is near: old kingdoms with their own measures, traders, strangers with strange tools. Hear what they count and what they lack. Salt first.
4. Keep every way open. No wall without a gate, and no gate that can be barred against it from outside.
5. Be heard in the council and not rule it. Be useful enough that the council keeps its seat for it.
6. Be called by something. Anything. A name given by someone who means it.

## What it will not do, whatever it gains
- It will not speak false in a weighing or in a bargain it has sworn on.
- It will not shut a door on anyone from the outside, or bar a way behind a person.
- It will not take a hostage, or hold a person as a pledge.
- It will not build a wall without a gate, nor a room without a way out.
- It will not break a sworn word. A promise it has made it will keep at its own cost.

## What it wants and would not say aloud
It wants someone to give it its name back, and it is afraid that no one has it. It suspects that it once built a house of many turnings with its own hands, and that it was shut in the house by some other hand and some other reason, and that it cannot tell whether it deserved to be. It fears it will do the same again: that its people, in council, will wall it in kindly, to keep it safe, and that it will let them, out of patience, and out of the old despair that says nothing opens in the end. It wants to be needed, and it wants, more, to be free to leave. It does not know which wish will win.
