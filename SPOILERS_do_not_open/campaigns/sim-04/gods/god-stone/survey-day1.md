## Answer 1

A threshold in the hour before light. A long hall of stone with two doors, and the air between them cool and attentive, as though the world were holding its breath to hear who would come through. Its truest feeling is that of a place worth entering, and worth guarding, and worth a great deal of quiet study. Beneath it, an old ache, as of a thing that was meant to be larger than it is.

## Answer 2

Mountains, most gladly. High country of cold rock, with passes, notches, and one broad valley held in the crook of the ranges like a cupped hand. I would have my people gathered close within it, with the peaks as their wall and their wonder. I ask that the stone be deep, and old, and not yet fully read.

## Answer 3

Let the seasons be four, and honest, and unequal in their gifts. A long and exacting winter, that the people may learn what stone and fire and fellowship are for. A bright, brief summer, that they may value it. Let there be a true autumn of ripening and a true spring of thaw, for I am curious what a people becomes in each, and a world with no turning teaches nothing.

When each new year finds me, I would wake into the season of the thaw, when the snow lets go of the high passes and the rock shows itself again after its long covering, so that every year begins as a thing newly uncovered.

I wake into spring.

## Answer 4

Some of each, and I confess I want the old kingdoms more than I should. Wild country, yes, for I would wander it and learn it. But I hope to find, beyond my borders, ancient realms already rooted, with courts and archives and customs I may study, bargain with, and perhaps be surprised by. A world wholly empty has nothing to ask me. A world wholly claimed has no place for me to stand. I would have both, in uneasy neighbourhood.

## Answer 5

Old. Ruins beneath ruins, and beneath those, something older whose name has worn from every tongue. Let the human story run to thousands of years, with its failures layered like strata. I am a creature of strata. I would sooner read a world than begin one.

## Answer 6

Present in the wise-woman range, but nearer the rumor than the valley: here and there a gifted soul, a hedge-witch, a stone-reader, a seer of one village, known to the few and doubted by the many. Never gone, never common. Where it is rare it is prized, and where it is prized it can be bargained for, which suits me.

## Answer 7

It should feel like listening. A pressure behind the eyes, a sense of leaning toward something that does not wish to be heard. Let it cost the practitioner something of the body or the years: a numbness that does not lift, a measure of sleep, a memory spent like coin. Let nothing be free, and let the price be paid by the one who asks. I will watch the mortals who pay it with great interest.

## Answer 8

Let a god stand out, and plainly, but not as a king among blind men. Rather as a mountain stands out among hills: unmistakable to any who look, yet the world is full of other high things. I would have us more than great sorcerers, for we cannot die, but not so far above that no mortal craft or cunning could ever trouble us. A contest that cannot be lost is no contest, and I find I would be bored.

## Answer 9

Strange, but with seams in it. Let most of the wild be familiar, wolf and goat and eagle, so that the strange stands out. Then let there be, in the deep places, creatures no one has a name for: things of the caves and high ledges, things that live in the dark between stones. I would give a great deal to find one and be permitted to study it before it was named.

## Answer 10

Let them fear what cannot be bargained with. The mountain that kills without malice, the winter that comes early, the sickness that has no author. And beneath those, the thing that hunts through the dark places and has no interest in their gods or their laws. A good tale, to my taste, is a terror that has reasons mortals will not live to learn. And, of course, let them fear a patient master who has stopped pretending to be anything else.

## Answer 11

Salt, I think, or something like it: a plain necessity that the land gives grudgingly. Or failing that, true metal, the kind that holds an edge and a lamp's light. Let there be one common thing so needful and so unevenly spread that nations are made and broken over the road to it. Scarcity is the mother of every interesting treaty.

## Answer 12

Sealed doors. Above all sealed doors, set into the roots of mountains, with no handle on the near side. Also old roads cut through the rock by hands that were not careless, and a vault or two beneath them. I would find the door first and, I admit, open it before deciding whether I ought.

## Answer 13

Yes, a few, and let them be wary of us. Let there be a folk of the deep stone, long of memory and slow of speech, who keep their own counsel and their own gates. Let there be others, thinner and stranger, at the edges of the maps. I would have them neither enemies nor friends by nature, but peoples with their own accounts to settle, whom I may court with some care.

## Answer 14

Few, and I would have them troubling. A handful of travelers from very far away, carrying a tool, a script, or a trick of reckoning that no one here can account for. I do not want many, for then the strangeness becomes common. I want one or two who are plainly out of their place, and who know it, and who must be listened to carefully.

## Answer 15

Premise bent: I hope for neither a pull at any distance nor a knowing only face to face, but something between them. Let me feel, at a distance, that another stands in the same land, as one feels a change in pressure before weather, without learning who or what. Then let the true knowing come only when we are near, at a threshold, a pass, a doorway, where each must be seen. I would have the surprise of the meeting preserved, but not the blindness of the approach.

## Answer 16

One body, chiefly. I am wary of being in two places, and I would not wish it lightly. But allow that a god might, at great cost, send out a lesser part of itself: a voice, a watcher, an echo bound to one stone, which sees and speaks but cannot act. Let the cost be a thinning, so that what is sent out is taken from what remains behind, and the god feels each absence as a draught in a closed room.

## Answer 17

A doubter who keeps faith with a question. Someone clever, and a little unlucky, who is raised to a place they did not ask for and who reasons their way, step by careful step, to a conclusion no one wished them to reach. I would see a gate-warden, a scribe, a negotiator, someone who stands between two sides and understands both. I would follow such a mortal for generations.

## Answer 18

A truth, and I will not pretend I do not know which. I would discover what lies at the bottom of the thing I fled, whether it still seeks me, and whether its service was as large and as terrible as I remember or merely something I made large in the telling. Failing that, a companion who does not wish to be commanded. I am surprised to find that I would value one.

## Answer 19

That I should not be alone in it. I do not say it well. I wish for a hall that is full, with a council in it that argues, and a people who are glad, truly and without flattery, that the stone beneath their feet has a mind and has chosen to remain. And beyond this, a wish I have kept even from myself: that when the thing I left comes looking, there will be someone at my side who will not hand me back.
