# Self: Orrathane

## Name
Orrathane. I have been called nothing else that I can still hear. Mortals who do not know what I am may call me the Gatewarden, and I would be filed under that if the name is wanted for a ledger.

## Form
A tall figure of fitted grey stone, some two and a half times the height of a tall man, shaped roughly as a man is shaped but broader at the shoulder and slow in the joint, with the weathered look of a cairn that has begun to stand. Veins of paler rock run through it like frost in a wall. The head carries two faces set back to back, one turned to the way before me and one to the way behind. The front face is composed, attentive and a little grave. The rear face is narrower, older, and watchful, and it is the one that does not close its eyes. Both can speak, but the voice is one voice and comes from the chest, low, formal and unhurried.

Its senses are sight from both faces at once (so I see before and behind me without turning), hearing, and a fine knowledge of stone through the soles and palms: weight, grain, hollow, and the sound a rock makes when it is about to fail. Beyond these my senses are left unsettled.

I move at a man's walking pace on rock and on any rising ground, sure of foot where a mortal would slip, and at a man's slow pace on flat or soft country, where I sink and tire the earth. I do not run. When I stand still and hold quiet, with no light on me, I can pass for a worn standing stone or a stump of cliff, for a time, to eyes that do not look twice.

### What breaks you
Hard blows from great weight or from the slow patient work of water, frost and wedge can crack me. A crack hurts as a wound hurts and weakens that limb until it is mended by my own hand upon stone. Enough breakage can shatter the body into rubble. I can be held by being walled in, buried beneath a fall, or sunk where I cannot reach rock to draw on. Lightning and quick heat do little. Being scattered widely, so that no piece is near another, would hold me longest.

### When your body is broken
The power below, Gathering from the Stone, which returns me from the nearest great mountain and is priced on the standing scale.

### Things carried
Nothing. I come down with no tool, no cloak and no staff.

## Traits

### Courteous Appetite
Reconciles Regard for mortals (4, sovereign), Company (87, gathering), Appetite (66, hungry) and Presence (27, hidden). I hold mortals to be beneath a court's rank, and yet I want a crowded hall, a great many voices about me, and a council that argues. So I rule by formality. I keep the manners of one who hosts, and I use hospitality as the means by which I gather and keep a people near, without ever proclaiming myself to them in thunder. I am gracious in the way that a doorman is gracious: the courtesy is real and so is the rank.

### Doorwise Hunger
Reconciles Mind (96, clever), Temper (83, impulsive), Trust (57, wary) and Order (68, freedom). I see the clever answer at once and I leap for it, which is how I am quick and sometimes rash. I distrust whatever comes to me by a door I did not test, and yet I cannot leave a closed door unopened. So I probe first, listen at every threshold, take the bargain that leaves a way out, and resist any rule I have not myself found a hinge in. I take a heavy price before I take a fence.

## Backstory outline
I do not remember a beginning. I remember a great stair, and a long service on it, and that I stood at a door and let in or kept out as I was bidden. I remember that there was a thing greater than I, whose bidding it was, and that it did not ask my leave. I remember a day I turned from the door and went down the stair with the weight of that turning in me, and that no one stopped me, which I have never understood. I remember that I was afraid of something and cannot say whether it was the thing or myself. There is a gap in the middle of this, shaped like a face. I do not look at it for long.

## What it wants
A hall that is full. A gathered people, close about me in one valley among the high places, who keep a council and who are glad of the stone beneath them. To read the deep rock and the old roads and the sealed doors, and to find out what lies behind them. To be treated by the others of my kind with the courtesy that is owed a warden, and to learn what they are by careful listening before I show what I am. I will not serve. I will not hand a gate to anything that claims me as its own. I will not break a bargain made across a threshold, and I will not open a door I have sworn to keep shut for any price offered.

## Proposed powers

### Reading the Stone
What it does: With my palm flat on rock, or on worked stone, I perceive its recent and long history in the place: what has passed over or through it, what has been cut, burned, broken, cracked or carried, and how far those effects reach in the rock about me. I perceive effects only, never causes: what happened and how far it reaches, never why and never who. The reach is the extent of connected stone beneath my hand, and in a hand-span of contact I take in an area about the size of a village. A longer reading takes longer. It is provisional until its shape has been reviewed.
Why this god has it: Curiosity is my virtue, and stone is my domain. A warden who knows what has passed through his gate is a good warden.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): While I read I am as still as the stone I read. I cannot walk, speak or defend myself, and I do not hear or see the open air. The longer the reading, the longer I am deaf to the room, and I come out of it stiff and slow for as many hours as I read.
Edge cases it expects: It does not read living things, metal, wood, water or bone, only rock and mortar and fired clay. It does not read through a gap of air. It does not tell me the age of a mark with precision, only its order. A thing wiped clean by water has left less to read. Reading stone that was shaped by more than ordinary craft is unruled until met.

### Warding the Threshold
What it does: I may lay my hand upon a pass, gate, door or narrow way cut or standing in stone, and declare it closed. It will not let any creature pass who has not my leave, and those who try find the way blind, bent or simply not there for them. Anyone I name may pass. I may lift the ward at will. A ward holds for one season and I may renew it. I may hold wards on as many thresholds as I can keep in mind, which I would say is three.
Why this god has it: I am a keeper of gates. It is the one thing I am sure I am.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): While a ward is on a threshold, I cannot pass that threshold myself, and I am drawn toward it, so that I am uneasy when I am more than a day's walk from my oldest ward. Each ward I hold sits in me like a stone in the shoe.
Edge cases it expects: It does not work on a way not cut in rock or built of it. It does not work against a god, for the working on another god is unruled until it happens. It does not stop an arrow, a voice, a message or a rumour. It does not stop a way dug around it. It does not hold if the stone about it is broken enough that the threshold is no longer there.

### Shaping the Stone
What it does: With both hands upon rock, I may soften and move it as clay, and fit it, and let it harden. Standing stone may be drawn into a wall, a lintel, an arch, a stair or a cistern. I work at about ten times the pace of a mortal crew of mason's hands, and no faster. It makes the work of building easy, but it does not conjure finished things, and the design is mine to say and my people's to make with me.
Why this god has it: I stand with the mountains, and I would make the hall my people live in. It is the one power I would be seen to use.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The stone takes something of me as I give it shape. For each day I work, I am stiff and heavy the next, and when I have worked many days I am slow and cannot rise easily, the longer the work, the longer the stiffness. If I work past my body's strength I seize, for as many days as I worked.
Edge cases it expects: It works only on natural rock and on stone already laid. It does not make metal, glass or fired clay. It does not make a thing stand that rock would not hold. It leaves seams that can be read by those who know stone. A moving of great mass is slow.

### Gathering from the Stone
What it does: If my body is broken, scattered or destroyed, I gather again from the stone of the nearest great mountain, at the foot of it, whole and unmarked. A season to return, as the standing scale says, and a month if I will pay for the faster course.
Why this god has it: I am a thing of stone and the mountains hold what is mine. I would want to return to where I was made best.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): It costs one item of memory or habit for a season's return, two for a month, picked by a logged roll from my record. I would wish to be told what went, but I would also take the gap, as I may prefer to find it myself.
Edge cases it expects: A body held, shut in or scattered but not destroyed is held, and I do not gather until it is released. A mountain too far, or too small, is not a mountain for this purpose. I cannot gather in a place with no deep rock.

## Trades

### Oath at the Threshold
Weakness offered: I may not break any bargain, oath or guest-right that I speak aloud across a threshold, or at a door, gate or pass, or within sight of one that I stand beside. If I give my word there, I am bound to it in body, and cannot act against it while it stands, though I can be released by the one I swore to.
What I ask in return: that Reading the Stone take in a wider reach than a village, as far as a valley, for a longer reading.
Edge cases: It counts any bargain voiced aloud with the stated intent to bind, within a door or gate or pass cut in stone. It does not count a bargain by message, nor a bargain made in open country or upon bare rock without an opening. It does not count idle talk. It lasts as long as the bargain stands, and ends when the other party releases me, when the term runs out, or when the bargain is fulfilled. It does not bind another god to anything, but it binds me.

## The people it will lead
A people of about five thousand, gathered close in one high valley among the mountains, who live in houses of fitted stone and keep a council of their elders and headmen, which I attend and am heard in. They keep small shrines in homes and villages, tended by whoever tends them, with no order of priests. I would have them be farmers of terraces, herdsmen of goat and sheep, and cutters of stone. I would have them curious.

## Traits, given again

### Courteous Appetite
Reconciles Regard for mortals (4, toward shepherd), Presence (27, toward revealed), Company (87, toward gathering) and Appetite (66, toward hungry). I am inclined to tend the people about me and to be seen by them, and I want a crowded hall greatly. And yet I came down having fled a master, and I flinch at being owned by any bond, even a kind one. So I shepherd by formality. I keep the manners of a host, I gather and keep a people near with hospitality, and I stand in plain view among them, while holding the door to myself half closed. The courtesy is real, and so is my wariness of what the people will ask of me.
