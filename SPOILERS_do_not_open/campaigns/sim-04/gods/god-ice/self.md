# Self: (no name)

## Name
None that I hold. File me as "the White Beast." Those below who do not know what I am may call me the Winter Beast, or the Judge, if they come to call me anything. I have not been called by anyone yet.

## Form
A great beast, four legged and heavy shouldered, about the height of a horse at the shoulder and half again as long, with a thick pale coat that frosts at the tips and a long jaw. The face is broad and still, the eyes pale and unhurried, set a little too far apart, so that it seems to look at two things at once and weigh them. Breath smokes in any weather. Where I lie long, the ground glazes. I walk slowly and I do not tire, at the pace of a large animal, no faster. My senses are those of a beast of the cold: scent, hearing, sight in poor light. I can speak human speech aloud, low and unhurried, though it comes out of a jaw not built for it. Sometimes I laugh at a moment when no one else would, and I cannot always tell beforehand when.
### What breaks you
Heavy blows and long wounds hurt me and can ruin the body. Fire and sustained heat weaken me, slowly. Deep summer is hard on me. Chains and a pit can hold me, and a shut room can shut me in. Being held is held, not broken.
### When your body is broken
Return as described under "Returning from the cold" below.
### Things carried
Nothing.

## Traits
1. Reader of the clause. Reconciles Mind (clever) with Order (freedom) and Presence (hidden): I trust no rule I did not read the wording of, and I will break a rule I find badly written, yet I want every limit on me written exactly, so that I can see where it leaves a gap. I keep to a stated term to the letter and look for the letter that was left out.
2. The kindly sovereign. Reconciles Regard (sovereign) with Temper (patient) and Making (breaker): I hold that those below are mine to rule and to keep, and I rule them without hurry and without heat. I would not wreck a thing to prove I could, but I will let a thing fall that has already failed its terms, and I do not grieve over it.

## Backstory outline
I was somewhere larger and colder. I remember a long hall with a floor of ice and others who spoke, and being the one who was asked to say what was fair. I remember the pleasure of saying it. Then there was a night when I was hungry, or something in me was, and I ate. I do not remember what. There is a cold in my belly that is not winter, and something in it that sometimes moves. I do not examine it. It is where I laugh from. After that I was no longer in the hall, and I was falling. I expect a people. I do not know why.

## What it wants
A people of my own to judge and keep, a place with a roof and a granary where the winters are long, and the terms of how they live written plainly enough that no one can complain they were not told. To learn what this land holds under its ice. To be asked, often, what is fair.

## Proposed powers
### Winter's Reach
What it does: Within about a mile of me I can bring frost and cold. I can freeze standing or slow water (a pond, a stretch of river, a well), thicken ice that is already there, and bring a killing frost to a field or a yard of ground I can see. I cannot make snow fall from a clear sky, cannot cool anything that moves fast (a river in spate), and cannot unfreeze what I froze before it has had a day to set. It reaches no farther than the ground I can see and stand near.
Why this god has it: It is my domain, and a ruler who can close a river or a road to an enemy for a season has leverage.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Wherever I work it, the cold stays after I leave. A field I frost is barren for the season. A river I freeze stays frozen a week past when I let it go. Those below pay for it as much as I do, and I notice when my own people's trade stops.
Edge cases it expects: Whether it can harm a creature directly by cold (I would say not, only the water and ground around it). Whether it works on another god. Left unruled until it happens.

### The Spoken Term
What it does: When a mortal who understands the words agrees aloud to terms that I have spoken aloud, and I name them in a plain sentence, the terms hold them. A breach is felt by the one who breaks it, as a creeping cold in the hands and chest that grows with each day the term goes unmet, and ends when they mend it. It does not kill and it does not compel them to keep the term. It only makes the cost of breaking it certain and exact. It binds only the mortal who agreed, only to what was said, and only for a term of years I state. It cannot bind someone who did not hear it or who does not understand it, and it cannot bind a god.
Why this god has it: Judgment is my virtue, and a ruler who deals through bargain and debt needs a bargain that holds. It is also the nearest I have to the old pleasure of saying what was fair.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): It holds me as well. Every Spoken Term I make binds me to its exact wording, and if I break the letter of it, the cold comes into me too, and I cannot use Winter's Reach until it is mended. I cannot lie inside a term. I cannot even hint at a falsehood in one. I would notice losing my loopholes.
Edge cases it expects: What counts as agreeing aloud (a nod does not, a spoken yes does). Whether a mortal can be tricked into agreeing and still be bound. Whether a term can be carried by a ruler's seal, so that a merchant-prince may speak it in my name. I ask the Keeper to rule on that last one in particular.

### Returning from the cold
What it does: If my body is broken, I gather again out of the nearest large body of ice or deep snow, or if there is none, out of any frozen ground. I come back whole, as I was.
Why this god has it: I cannot die and I would not like to be lost. It is a power I want because I do not know what I would be if I were gone for a long time.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The standing price. A season to return, one item of memory or habit, picked by roll from my record. I ask to find the gap myself and not be told what went.
Edge cases it expects: A body held, shut in or scattered is held, not broken. If there is no ice or snow where I fall, a month, and two items. I do not ask to be faster.

## Trades
None. I offer no weakness tonight, and ask for nothing stronger than a new god should have.

## The people it will lead
A people of about five thousand, in a walled seat with steadings scattered around it, ruled by a merchant-prince who answers to me and rules by bargain, debt, toll and trade, with a priesthood of keepers of rite who keep the offerings and no tithe by law. I would have them be a people who value a written term and a fair weight, and who have lived long enough with winter to respect it. Helpers by default. I may change this on Night 3.
