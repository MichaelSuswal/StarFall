## Answer 1
Dusk on a still river, with lamps being lit on the far bank one at a time. Every house keeps a door that is not on the street. As the old saying goes, a quiet house is never an empty one.

## Answer 2
River country, and the mist country where rivers braid and lose their names. Water carries what it is given and tells no one where it put it. Let the banks be wooded and the fords be few, so that a people can keep moving and still know where the crossings are.

## Answer 3
Four seasons, and unequal ones. A long gray autumn that lingers, a winter that is honest, a spring that comes late and keeps its promises, a summer that is short and does not boast. Let none of them be cruel for its own sake. Hard seasons teach, but only the ones that leave something standing. A closed hand is a full hand, and a long autumn is the season of closed hands, when what was gathered is counted and what was hidden is remembered. I would wake into it each year. The thinning light suits one who works in what is kept back.

I wake into autumn.

## Answer 4
Some of each, though more of the old than the wild. I would find kingdoms already rooted, with gates that are polite and cellars that are not. Walk where no one has walked and you learn the land. Walk where many have walked and you learn the people, and the people are what I came for. Let there be wild country between them, wide enough to lose a road in, so that a moving people has somewhere to go.

## Answer 5
Thousands of years, and ruins under ruins. The dead of this world should outnumber the living by a long way, and the living should know it. A land that remembers has a thousand things to keep from you. What is buried once is dug up twice, the saying goes, and it is rarely the same thing the second time.

## Answer 6
Closer to the rumor most people doubt than to the wise-woman in every valley, though not so far that the doubt is easy. One in some hundreds should have a touch of it, and they should be careful who knows. Let it be the kind of thing a village half believes and wholly respects. A secret shared by a whole valley is no secret, and a magic known to every house is only a trade.

## Answer 7
It should feel like a door opening onto a room one did not know the house had. A cold in the teeth, a sense of being attended to. The cost should be memory, a small true thing at a time, taken without announcement and never from the place one would choose. A debt is the only coin the unseen accepts, and it is always paid in what you did not know you were carrying. Let the cost be a thing mortals can learn to ration but not to avoid.

## Answer 8
Closer to the one-eyed man, but he should have reason to keep the eye shut. A god should stand out the way a deep well stands out in a field: one can walk past it for years. If gods are plain to every eye, then every eye will be turned on them, and a god that is watched can do nothing that matters. Let the wonder be real and let it be rare, and let a careful god be mistaken for a quiet stranger.

## Answer 9
Mostly familiar, with a few legends that turn out to be true and a few creatures at the edge of the maps that no one has a name for. Let the strange things keep their distance and their habits. Names are for what one has met. What has no name has not yet agreed to be known, and some of it should never agree.

## Answer 10
Oaths broken, and what comes of them. Let mortals fear the unquiet dead, the debt that outlives the debtor, the thing that was promised and not paid. Fear of what was said, more than of what was done. A bridge can be rebuilt. A word cannot be unsaid, and a world built on words has a long memory for the ones that failed. That is the kind of danger that makes a story worth carrying across water.

## Answer 11
Truth that has not been bent by the teller. Honest witness, honest weights, a word one can rely on. Mortals will trade for it, lie about it, and kill for the right to be believed. Salt and iron may be dear, but a trusted word is dearer and travels lighter. Where trust is scarce, secrets are currency, and I should like the world to be rich in them.

## Answer 12
Old roads that go nowhere any map admits, and sealed doors with no hinges showing. Graves that were dug too deep for the dead they hold. I would like to find a ferry landing with no river beside it, and learn why it was built. The bones of something enormous I will leave to others. What is buried should be buried for a reason, and I want the reasons.

## Answer 13
Yes, but few, and not loud about it. Let there be a people of the marsh or the deep wood who are old and patient and do not share a hearth with strangers, and who keep their own count of the dead. I do not need them to be kind or cruel. I need them to have kept their own oaths longer than anyone has kept theirs, and to be unimpressed by me.

## Answer 14
A few, and far apart. One or two who came a long way, who do not say how, and who carry tools or knowledge that sit awkwardly in this world. Not many. A stranger who arrives is a question, and ten strangers are an answer one did not want. Let them be tolerated and quietly watched, and let some of them be more than they claim.

## Answer 15
Face to face only, and only partly. A knowing that arrives when two of us are near enough to hear each other breathe, and not before. A pull felt at any distance is a leash, and I will not be led about by one. If I must be found, let it be by a look across a room. Let those who wish to hide be able to, a little, and let those who wish to find have to work for it.

## Answer 16
Premise bent: one body is the honest answer, and I would keep it that way. Let a god be able to leave something of itself behind, a voice in a place, a watching, a sending, but let it cost a true piece of the one who sends it, and let that piece not come back whole. A shadow cast too far from its owner forgets who owns it. If there are to be several bodies, each should know it is not the first.

## Answer 17
One who keeps a confidence when it costs them. Not a hero, and not a villain: a go-between, an interpreter, a gravedigger, a midwife, someone who is trusted by both sides of a quarrel and betrays neither. Let the land produce a mortal who has to choose between two oaths and chooses well, or chooses badly and carries it honestly. That story is worth following. The ones who shout seldom are.

## Answer 18
A truth about myself that I have put somewhere and cannot find. Failing that, a companion who can be silent with me without it being an absence. And a place, perhaps, on the far side of a long crossing, where what was broken is known to be mended, if only to someone else. I cannot yet imagine which of these I want, and that is how I know there is something to discover.
