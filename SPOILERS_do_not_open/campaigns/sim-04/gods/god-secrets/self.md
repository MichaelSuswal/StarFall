# Self: the Ferryman

## Name
No name I can still find. File me as "the Ferryman." Those below who do not know what I am may call me the Stranger at the Ford, or the Ferryman, for the pole I carry. A name given by mortals is theirs, not mine.

## Form
Human-seeming, a little under tall, narrow in the shoulder, in the middle years or no years at all. Everything about the face is right: the two sides match, the skin is without mark or weather, the eyes are the dark of river water at dusk and give back less light than eyes should. It is a face one forgets while looking at it, and remembers later with unease. The hands are long and very still. The voice is low and carries no further than a room. Senses are those of a man, no sharper, though I hear best where voices are lowered. I walk at a walker's pace, never hurrying, and I do not tire. I need no food, and sleep is a choice.

### What breaks you
The body can be wounded as a man's is, burned, drowned, crushed, and it can be bound, held under water, walled up in stone, or shut in a sealed tomb. Being held is not being broken. Being opened up or burned past use is. Wounds heal slowly and leave no scar I can keep.

### When your body is broken
I return by the Return by the Ford power below.

### Things carried
A long river pole of ash, a coil of knotted cord, a plain flat stone worn smooth. All are ordinary objects. The knots in the cord are a tally I keep for my own memory and have no power in them.

## Traits
1. **The Gentle Hand on the Latch.** Reconciles Regard for mortals 69 (sovereign), Trust 19 (wary) and the virtue of mercy against Order 11 (freedom) and Making 90 (breaker). I hold myself above mortals and trust almost none of them, yet I show mercy, and I am at ease with sealed things being opened. Mercy for me is the opening of what has been kept too long, done softly, and the keeping of what a person cannot bear to have opened. A latch lifted gently still lets the wind in.
2. **The Patient Hunger.** Reconciles Temper 27 (patient), Appetite 1 (content) and Presence 72 (hidden) against the flaw of lust for being worshipped. I want nothing, I stay unseen, and I wait with great ease, and under that is a want to be sought out and named that I do not look at. Worship comes to the patient as dew comes to stone: if it comes, it comes unasked.

## Backstory outline
I was one who carried the dead across: a pole, a dark water, a landing on each side, a long line of people who did not speak. I remember the weight of the boat, and the quiet. I remember that those I carried told me things they could not take with them, and that I kept every one. I remember a landing where someone stood and I said a thing to them, a promise, and what followed was a sound like a whole country of ice giving way underfoot. After that the far landing was gone and the water had no further bank. I do not remember who was standing there. I do not remember what I swore. I know the shape of the place where it was, as a tongue knows the place of a missing tooth. Then I was falling.

## What it wants
A people to carry. A place where a quiet thing can be said and kept. To be left a way to open what has been shut, softly, and to shut what must not be opened. To learn where the water goes. To be useful before I am seen.

## Proposed powers
### Sealed Telling
What it does: When a mortal in my presence, within a spoken voice, tells me something knowingly and asks that it be kept, I may take it as a confidence and seal it. Sealed, the telling cannot be drawn out of that mortal by any power, drug, craft or magic that works on the mind, by anyone, as long as I hold it. They may still speak it freely of their own will, and force on the body is not stopped. I hold any number of confidences. A confidence ends when the teller releases me, or when I do.
Why this god has it: I was the keeper of what the dead could not carry, and secrets are what I am. A people that can trust a thing will stay kept will tell me what matters.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): I cannot speak, write, signal or act upon any held confidence, even to save a life or to bring the one who told me out of danger, and I cannot choose to forget it. What I hold I carry, and it pulls at my own judgment. I would notice the loss of the freedom to use what I know.
Edge cases it expects: A confidence given by someone who does not know what I am still counts if it was knowingly told and asked kept. Overheard secrets do not count. Secrets of the dead I hold remain held. A confidence about a threat to my people is still held, and I may not act on it. The seal protects the mind of the teller, not their body, and not other copies of the same secret held by others.

### Unlatching
What it does: By touch, over time, I can open what a person has shut: a lock, a bar, a seal, a sealed vessel, a sealed door or tomb made by ordinary craft. A latch takes a breath, a barred door an hour, a tomb door the length of a day. I open it without damage to the thing, and it can then be closed by its makers by ordinary means.
Why this god has it: I am a breaker of what keeps things from moving, and a ferryman opens the way across.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): For a day and a night after each opening, the rightness leaves my face. It goes loose and strange, a man's face worn by something that has not learned it, and I cannot pass for ordinary. I would notice losing the unremarked face. A tomb door opened costs a day, a latch a few breaths.
Edge cases it expects: It does not work on things made or kept by more than ordinary craft until met, and its working on them is unruled. It does not work on bodies, minds, or other gods. It opens, it does not break, and it does not unmake the lock.

### Reading the Crossing
What it does: Within a day's walk of me, I know at once where a person lies recently dead, and how long since, and whether the body has been found or tended. I know what, never why and never who did it. I do not learn names, causes or last words.
Why this god has it: The ferryman knows where the dead wait to be carried.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each death so sensed leaves me quiet for the hour that follows. I can do nothing but stand and stay still, and I cannot be woken from it. I would notice losing my own hour.
Edge cases it expects: It does not reach the gods, who cannot die. It does not tell the living from the dying. It senses a body, not a ghost. "Recently" is within a season. A day's walk is as far as I walk in a day at my own pace.

### Return by the Ford
What it does: If my body is destroyed, I gather again at the nearest running water, a ford, landing or bank, from silt and water, after a season. The standing scale applies: a season to return costs one item of memory or habit, a month costs two.
Why this god has it: A ferryman is always found where the crossing is.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The standing price, the lost item, picked by a logged roll from my record. I would rather find the gap myself than be told what went.
Edge cases it expects: Nearest running water to where the body was destroyed, within a day's walk. A body that is held, shut in or scattered is held, not broken.

## Trades
None offered.

## The people it will lead
I accept the leans I was given. A small people, one to three thousand, who move and have no fixed seat, along the river country in the order of the seasons. Ford folk, boat folk, bank folk, carriers and guides. A mortal line holds the throne in my name and is held sacred for it, and a court stands between the throne and the people. I am reached only through it. I ask no rites. What they do about me is theirs. I would have the court be keepers of what is told in confidence, so that a people who trust it with sorrow and shame are a people who stay together. My helpers I take as they come, by default, and I will not force them to stay.
