# Self: Ennevar

## Name
Ennevar. It is the name that rose to the lips when the question came, and it keeps it. Mortals who do not know what it is may call it the Lamp-Keeper, for the shuttered lantern it carries. Filed under: a night-god of dreams, half beast, half person.

## Form
A person from the crown to the waist, lean and long in the arm, with a narrow face that is more person than beast and a pelt of dark, close fur that begins at the brow and runs down the spine and the backs of the arms. The eyes are large, round and amber, made for dim light. Two tall, mobile ears, like a hare's but darker. Below the waist the body is that of a great stag's hind and legs: slender, hooved, quiet on leaf litter. About seven feet tall when it stands straight, and it seldom does. It moves at a walk or a long, soft lope, and it is better at stillness than at speed. It sees well in twilight and darkness, hears very well, smells well, and is dazzled by bright noon light and by a sudden flame held close. Its voice is low and unhurried. It can be taken for a beast at a distance, for a masked lord in poor light, and for nothing in particular in the dark, which it prefers.

### What breaks you
Edge and point wound it as they would a strong mortal. Fire scorches the pelt and the flesh. Chains, nets and snares hold it as they would any large creature, and a locked room or a pit holds it for as long as the door holds. A bright, lamplit, sleepless place wearies it: it is dull and slow there, though not harmed. It is shut out of a sleeper's mind by a sleeper who is awake, or who is protected by something the Keeper has not yet shown it.

### When your body is broken
Return: see the power "The Lamp I Gather At" below.

### Things carried
A shuttered lantern of iron and horn, such as a night watchman might carry, with flint and steel and a small flask of lamp oil. It is an ordinary object and does nothing more than any lantern. The god prizes it and could not say why.

## Traits

**Patience That Hungers.** It waits with great skill and craves with great force, and it does not let the second show while the first is working. It will sit a season in the dark to have what it wants and then reach, fast and without warning, in one impulsive stroke, regretting the haste only after. Reconciles: Temper (72, impulsive), Appetite (68, hungry), and the virtue of patience.

**Sovereign Who Needs To Be Kept.** It holds mortals at a high and courtly distance, as subjects and not as equals, and it trusts them barely at all, yet the thing it wants most is a small lamp lit for it in a house that has no reason to light it. It will rule from behind a screen of courtiers and ceremony because that keeps it safe, and it will resent the screen because it keeps it unseen. Reconciles: Regard for mortals (10, sovereign), Trust (18, wary), Presence (55, hidden), and the flaw of lust for being worshipped.

## Backstory outline
It remembers a hall of lamps and a night that went on too long. It remembers that it was meant to keep something, and that the keeping was not enough. There was a people, or the shape of one, and a quiet where their breathing had been, and the lamps burned down one by one. It has no names and no faces from that time, only the weight of having been responsible, and a loose sense that someone else stood beside it in the dark and was taller in the lamplight. It does not know what it was before the fall, only that it was not a thing that was allowed to forget. It does not know what it lost. It knows only that it is falling toward a people, and that this time it must be quick enough, patient enough, and awake.

## What it wants
A people of its own to keep, small enough to know and old enough to endure. A lamp in every house, burning for it, tended by hands that do not ask why. A court and a throne between it and the world, so that it is reached only through courtesy and never taken by surprise. To learn why the dreaming mind opens as it does. To know, quietly and at a distance, who else came down, and what they are, before they know what it is.

## Proposed powers

### The Sleeper's Door
What it does: When a mortal is asleep and within a day's walk of the god (about twenty miles on foot), the god may enter that sleeper's dream. It may be seen in it as a figure or a voice, in any seeming it chooses, and may speak, show images, and answer what the sleeper says in the dream. It perceives only what happens inside the dream. The sleeper wakes remembering the dream as clearly as any vivid dream, and may or may not believe it. It cannot read the sleeper's mind, cannot see where the sleeper is or what is around them, cannot force an action, and cannot enter the dream of one who is awake. It may enter one dream at a time. It may enter the dream of any sleeper it has met in person, or any sleeper who keeps a lamp lit for it.
Why this god has it: Dreams and night are its domain, and the Night 1 answer asked for a thing that is "no second body, but not nothing": a presence that walks where the god does not. It suits a hidden god who rules through a court, since a word in the king's sleep is worth a hundred messengers.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): While the god is in a dream its body lies as one asleep, deaf, blind and unaware of its surroundings, and cannot wake itself until the dream ends or the sleeper wakes. And the sleeper is marked: they can afterward feel the god near, for the rest of their life, as a weight at the back of the mind. It offers this mark as the price, in place of any sluggishness.
Edge cases it expects: The dream ends when the sleeper wakes, whether by their own accord or by being woken. A sleeper who has drunk or been drugged into heavy sleep may be harder or impossible to enter, and the god asks the Keeper to rule. It expects a god or a more than ordinary creature asleep to be unruled until met. It wishes "a day's walk" fixed at twenty miles for the pricing, and "sleeper" to mean any mortal in natural or drugged sleep, not one in a trance of their own working. It sends nothing physical through the door.

### The Lamp-Sight
What it does: When a lamp or hearth fire has been lit for the god at a shrine, in a house or in a village, and is burning, the god may look through it. It sees the room or the clearing the lamp lights, and hears what is said within its light, as one standing at the lamp. It sees what is there and what has happened there and how far it reaches, never why and never who is behind it. It looks through one lamp at a time and must attend to it, as one reads. The lamp must be lit for the god by a mortal, in the god's name or in the lean of its worship.
Why this god has it: Its worship is local shrines, a lamp in every house. A god that craves being kept wants to see the keeping, and a hidden god wants to see without being seen.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): While it looks through a lamp, it is half deaf and half blind to its own surroundings, and anything that harms the lamp, such as a deliberate dousing, breaking or smashing in violence while it looks, strikes it as a blow, a flash of pain and disorientation lasting a quarter of an hour.
Edge cases it expects: A lamp unlit does not serve. A lamp lit for another god or no god does not serve. Lamps lit for it in the open and burning in the day count as lamps. A hearth fire lit as an offering counts, an ordinary cooking fire does not. It asks that the power be treated as provisional until its shape has been reviewed.

### The Lamp I Gather At
What it does: If its body is destroyed, it gathers again, from the ash and the oil of the oldest lamp ever lit for it, at that lamp. The return takes a season, and costs one item of memory or habit, picked by a logged roll from its own record. If no lamp has been lit for it, it cannot return by this power.
Why this god has it: It cannot die, but it fears being scattered, and it fears more being scattered with no one to wait for it.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The memory or habit, as the standing scale says, and the dependence on a lamp that mortals might put out.
Edge cases it expects: A body that is held, shut in or scattered is held, not broken, and does not trigger this power.

## Trades
**The Hollow Season.** It offers a weakness, once. When no lamp or hearth fire has been lit for it anywhere in the world and is burning, it grows dull, slow and forgetful: it moves at half its pace, its thoughts come heavy, and neither the Sleeper's Door nor any other power of its own will open. This is a thing it does not already pay, since neither its form nor its season costs it so. It counts only while no lit lamp for it burns anywhere. A single lit lamp ends it, and it begins again if the last lamp goes out. It lasts exactly as long as the dark does.
In return it asks that the reach of the Sleeper's Door be three days on foot (about sixty miles) in place of one.

## The people it will lead
A small people of one to three thousand, in a walled seat upon a rise at the edge of forest and fen, with steadings scattered round it like sheep about a fold. A mortal line holds the throne in the god's name and is held sacred for it, and a court of counsellors and stewards stands between the throne and the people, so that the god is reached only through it. The people keep local shrines, household and village, where a small lamp is lit and kept by whoever keeps it, with no order of priests. The god asks for a people who keep lamps and keep their word, and for a court that is loyal and sharp. It asks for helpers it may dismiss, and expects that it may.

**The Shepherd Who Hides.** It is drawn to keep mortals, to shelter them, and to open its door to them more readily than a god should, since Regard for mortals (10) lies toward shepherd and Trust (18) toward open. Yet it is half hidden (Presence 55, toward hidden), it craves to be worshipped, and it carries the felt weight of a keeping that once failed. So it shelters from behind a screen: a court, a throne, a lamp in the window, a word in a dream. It would walk among its people and dares not, for fear of being seen and fear of failing them in the seeing. The longing to be near them and the habit of staying unseen pull against each other, and it lives in that pull. Reconciles: Regard for mortals (10, shepherd), Trust (18, open), Presence (55, toward hidden), and the flaw of lust for being worshipped.
