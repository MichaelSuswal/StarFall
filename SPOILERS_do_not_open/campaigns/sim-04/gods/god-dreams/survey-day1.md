## Answer 1

Dusk that does not finish ending. A great hall of lamps burning low, where every shadow leans a little toward the sleeper and every sleeper is watched over by something that has not slept. Quiet, but a quiet with a pulse in it, as of a thing breathing just beyond the door.

## Answer 2

Forest and fen where the two meet, with hills behind them and a river sliding between. Land that keeps its own counsel, where mist gathers at the hour before dawn and a walled seat might sit upon a rise, with steadings scattered round it like sheep about a fold. I would have woods thick enough to be dark at noon.

## Answer 3

Let the seasons be four and honest, each with its own temper, but let the autumn run long and the nights run longer than the days for a good part of the turning year. Let winter be hard but not murderous, so that a people may be tested without being ended. For myself, when each new year finds me, I would wake into the late autumn, when the leaves are down, the dark is gathering, and the world begins to dream.

I wake into autumn.

## Answer 4

Some of each, and I will be plain as to why. Wild country no one has walked, for room to move unwatched. Old kingdoms already rooted, for there is nothing so instructive as a neighbour who has survived long enough to be envied, and nothing so useful as one whose weakness may be studied from a courteous distance.

## Answer 5

Old, but not ancient past reckoning. Some centuries of ruin laid upon ruin, enough that mortals dig up what they cannot explain and tell themselves stories about it, and not so many that the memory of the first builders is wholly lost. I would have a few things remembered wrongly, and a few remembered true.

## Answer 6

Nearer the rumor than the wise-woman in every valley. Let most doubt it, and let a few know better and keep silent. A hedge-witch here, a dreamer there, a family in the hills whose grandmother's warnings have a way of proving sound. Rare enough to be prized, common enough that a god need not be alone in the dark.

## Answer 7

It should feel like listening. A pressure behind the eyes, a taste of metal, a sense of being observed by something kind or unkind, as the hour decides. The cost should be sleep: those who reach for it should lose their rest by degrees, and pay for each working in nights of wakefulness or dreams they cannot wake from. Nothing is given that is not taken from somewhere nearer to the heart.

## Answer 8

Let a god stand out, but not blaze. I would have it be that a god is recognized by those with eyes to see and mistaken by the rest for something lesser: a strange lord, a mighty sorcerer, a creature out of legend. A god should be able to walk among mortals without a trumpet announcing it, and should be able to be known when it chooses to be known, and not before.

## Answer 9

Strange, but with seams of the familiar running through it. Let the common beasts be as they are, and let the deep wood and the marsh and the high places hold things no one has named, so that a hunter returns with a tale and a trapper does not return at all. I am half a beast myself, and I would not have the wild too tame to claim me kin.

## Answer 10

The unseen. A thing that comes in the night and is not found by morning. A sickness of the mind. A sleeper who does not wake, a household that wakes with one fewer than it lay down with. I would have mortals fear what they cannot name, and then fear most of all that someone among them has learned the name.

## Answer 11

Trust, in the plainest sense: a hand that may be clasped without counting the fingers afterward. If a material thing is wanted, let it be salt, or good iron, or clean water in a bad year. But let the deeper scarcity be a person one may rely upon without reservation. Mortals will trade for the first and kill for the second.

## Answer 12

Sealed doors, and under them a stair that has not been walked in an age. I would have old roads grown over, and among them, here and there, a hall whose purpose no one living can say. Bones of something enormous, if it pleases the world, but let them be found in a cave and not in a field, and let the finder not tell anyone for a year.

## Answer 13

A few, and few of them kind to strangers. I would have a small folk of the deep wood, shy, watchful and wise in herbs, who keep to themselves and make bargains only in dusk. And I would have one other people, older than the human story, thinning, who remember what mortals have forgotten and will not say it for any price.

## Answer 14

A very few, and let them be strange. A wanderer with a satchel of instruments no one can name, who stays a season and is gone. A pair who arrive half dead, speaking no tongue known here, and are taken in by someone who should have known better. Let what they bring be fragmentary, so that it must be rebuilt by people who do not fully understand it.

## Answer 15

Face to face, and only so. A pull felt at any distance would leave me no room to be unseen, and I would keep the privilege of arriving unannounced. But let there be a flicker of recognition when two gods stand within speaking distance, a stillness in the air, so that neither may pretend not to have noticed the other. What follows after that is for the two of them to decide.

## Answer 16

Premise bent: I would have a god be one body in the ordinary way, but let the dreams of a god walk where the god does not. A god of my kind should be able to send a seeming, a presence in a sleeper's mind, a shadow that carries a word. That is no second body, but it is not nothing. Let the cost be that whatever is sent is not entirely returned, and the god sleeps a little less soundly for each errand.

## Answer 17

One who listens before she speaks. A mortal with a long memory and a short temper, who has been wronged and has not forgotten, but who has learned to wait. A counselor who becomes a king's conscience, or a king's undoing. I would have the land produce the sort of person who is remembered for what she refused to do as much as for what she did.

## Answer 18

A companion, though I would not say so aloud to the others. And failing that, a truth: why the dreaming mind opens as it does, and what lies on the far side of it. Let there be a place where the veil lies thin, and let a god who finds it learn something it would rather not have known and be glad of it nonetheless.

## Answer 19

That I be remembered. Not feared, not obeyed, but remembered, in the lamp left burning on a sill and the whispered word before sleep, kept up by hands that do not know why they keep it. That what is entrusted to me be kept, this time. I have said more than I meant to. The world listens; let it keep this one.
