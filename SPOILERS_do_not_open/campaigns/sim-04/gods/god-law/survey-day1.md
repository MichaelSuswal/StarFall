## Answer 1
A great market hall at dusk, every stall closed and every bargain still standing. Nothing is signed in the dark, but everything is kept. The world at its truest is a place where a word, once given, holds its weight, and where you can hear that weight when you walk through it. Quiet, but not empty. Ha. Quiet like a held breath.

## Answer 2
River country. Wide slow rivers with towns at the crossings and toll-posts at the fords. Land where people must meet to get anywhere, because the water decides the roads. I would take hills at the edges, for looking down from, but the rivers first.

## Answer 3
Four real seasons, each one honest about what it is. A hard winter that makes people count what they owe and what they are owed, a wet spring that floods the fords and forces new agreements about who crosses where, a long summer for trade, and an autumn of reckoning when debts fall due. Not too gentle. A world with no winter keeps no promises. I want to wake into autumn, when accounts are settled and the roads are still open.
I wake into autumn.

## Answer 4
Some of each, but more rooted than wild. I want old kingdoms with old customs, because customs are laws no one remembers writing, and I am curious what they say. And I want a stretch of wild country at the far edge, so there is somewhere to be unwatched. Ha. Mostly I want to read what others have already decided.

## Answer 5
Old. Thousands of years, with ruins under ruins. A people who arrived last week have no precedent, and I want to find the precedent. Every layer should have left a contract, a boundary stone, a broken treaty. Let the dead have written things down badly, so there is something to argue over.

## Answer 6
A little less than a wise-woman in every valley, a little more than a rumor. Say one in a few valleys, and the rest doubt it. Magic that is rare enough to be bargained for and common enough that no one can call it a lie. Never everywhere.

## Answer 7
It should feel like making a promise to something that listens. The cost is that the promise binds the one who makes it: the working holds only as long as the oath behind it does, and a broken oath breaks the working and takes something from the maker, a skill, a name, a year of luck. Magic that cannot be forsworn cheaply. That is the only magic I trust.

## Answer 8
Noticeably, but not absurdly. Taller than the tallest mortal, in my case, and I would not pretend otherwise, but a god should be answerable to the same rules a hard bargain is. A one-eyed man among the blind is a tyrant, and tyrants are easy to trace. I would rather be a great mortal sorcerer's worst surprise than a kingdom's obvious master. Let others be seen. Let the stakes be real for us too.

## Answer 9
Mostly familiar animals, with legends that turn out to be true at the worst moments. A few creatures with no name, living in the places the maps do not reach. Strange enough that a fieldhand's tale might be real, not so strange that nothing can be reasoned about.

## Answer 10
The broken oath. Mortals should fear the man who smiles, swears, and means none of it, and the thing in the dark that keeps every bargain to the letter and ruins you by it. Also hunger in a hard winter, and the knock at night from a creditor who has the right. A good story is one where someone is held to their word.

## Answer 11
Trust. Not coin, not grain. Mortals should want, above all, someone whose word they can rely on, and have very little of it. Let them trade for it, buy it, forge it, fight over it. Let the fraud of a false witness be the oldest crime they have.

## Answer 12
Sealed doors, and old roads that run under the new ones. And boundary stones, many, carved with terms no one can read yet. Not bones of something enormous. Ha. Well, one set of bones, if it must be, but let it be buried with a signed agreement in its hand.

## Answer 13
Some, yes, few. Peoples who keep their word differently than humans do: one that cannot lie but may withhold, one that remembers every bargain across generations and demands payment from grandchildren. I am curious what a different law looks like from inside it. Not many, and not friendly by default.

## Answer 14
A few, and strange. One or two who arrive with tools that do not fit the age, and who are bound by some contract of their own that they cannot explain. I would like to meet one and ask what they swore, and to whom. Too many travelers would make the world forget its own customs.

## Answer 15
Face to face only. A knowing at a distance feels like being watched, and I prefer not to be. When we meet, let there be a moment where each knows the other for what they are, and then let everything after be negotiated. I would like to arrive somewhere unnoticed and be recognized only by those I choose to be.

## Answer 16
One body. A god in many places at once has no single word to stand behind, and the whole point of an oath is that one person gave it. If several, let each one be a separate promise that can be broken separately, and let the cost be that what one does, the others owe. Ha. But one body, one word.

## Answer 17
A mortal who keeps their word at ruinous cost, and the mortal who learns to use that against them. I hope for a lawgiver, a witness, a hard honest merchant, a clever forger. Someone whose life turns on a single bargain, so that a story can be followed from the oath to the reckoning.

## Answer 18
A truth. What it was that was promised before any of us fell, and who holds the other end of it. I do not know what it would be and I would like to be surprised. A place would be good too, a hall where old oaths are kept. But a truth. I cannot yet imagine which.

## Answer 19
That I be given a people who will keep a shrine for me in their houses without being told to, and that I never have to ask. Ha. That is the wish I have not dared to say. Let them want to.
