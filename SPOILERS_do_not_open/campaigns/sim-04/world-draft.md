# World draft: campaign sim-04, Night 1

Written 2026-11-21, Night 1, from the blend of eight surveys (keeper log 1.5) and the star (`S/star.md`). Vote counts are in the keeper log and nowhere else. Everything marked unplaced is placed on Night 3. Nothing here is released whole. Places have no names or ids yet; they get both when traced.

## Tone (Q1 pool)

The hour at the edge of light, held long. Lamps and small fires lit one by one against a large dimness; still water, mist on rivers; a quiet that is attentive, like a held breath. Peoples who are busy, watchful and careful of their word: markets where a bargain stands after the stalls close, houses with a door that is not on the street. Peaceful, and not safe. The light is low and warm, and the blue above it is nearer than it ought to be.

Palette, within the house style: umber, ochre, gold and near-black, lamplight on wet stone and water, with cool blue as the accent.

Lone moods, each the color of one country (unplaced): a heavy gold late-summer afternoon where the whole land hums and nobody is in charge (flowering river meadows); a frozen lake, clear to the bottom, fair and not kind (the cold country); a stone hall with two doors and attentive air (the high passes).

## Climate and seasons (Q3 at 4)

Four true seasons everywhere, on the hard side of the middle. Winter long and serious: it closes passes and roads, tests granaries, and is the season people reckon by. Spring late, wet and loud with meltwater; fords flood. Summer real and not long, shorter with height and northing. Autumn the lingering season: harvest, markets, the settling of accounts. Milder in the low river country and near the sea; harsher in the high and northern country; as real climate varies with latitude, altitude and distance from the sea. No season is cancelled anywhere.

Waking seasons are per god and live on the sheets: spring (two), autumn (four), early winter (one). The player's is early spring, set to the clock in 3b.8.

## The star, as it bears on the land

- Falling phase: a large, low, bright star. Its descent can be seen by eye within the campaign. The fall is 100 to 300 years off (year set in 3b.4). Every living people has grown up under a low star and has its own account of it.
- Fading is far along: visible in many places, the oldest and most-worked ground first, and the far edges where the rain is thinnest. With thin mortal magic (below), most of it is age and overdraw of old ground, not the spend of many workers.
- Living radius: 70 days' walk from the center. Whether the sheet's edges reach the thinning country is settled in 3a.3.
- Center body: a lake, under the star's center, swollen. The surface painting must show it.
- Long game: the hoarder (the lake) and the carriers (a walking people who sing names). No thinners.
- Remainder: fell in godless country the night the gods arrived; placed in 3a.3.
- Star content: canals, in dreams only.
- Ascended: mostly those who want the fall soon; loud, to a god that nears the ceiling.
- For 3b.4: the human story is as deep as the scale goes (below), under a star in its last age. Both stand: a very old story lived out under a star that has been coming down through its latest chapters.

## Magic (Q6 at 3, final; Q7 pool)

Level, final tonight, inside what this star allows (2 to 4): low and real. Most people doubt it. A worker here and there, in a few villages of a region, looked at sideways and quietly paid. Never a worker in every valley; never gone. It may be spread unevenly (thicker toward the center, by the lore), and no place makes the rule read differently.

How it feels and costs, one system, consistent with Truth 4: a working is a close attending, a listening or noticing kept up past comfort, with the sense of being attended to in return. It is simple. It costs the worker, slowly and personally: sleep first (rest lost by degrees, dreams gone flat), then memory, a small true thing at a time and never the one she would choose; at the last, her name. The cost falls on the worker in nearly every working; it is not a rule that it never falls elsewhere.

Local understandings, each a tradition's own and each wrong about something that matters (unplaced): that a working is a loan from a lender who keeps accounts; that a working holds only while an oath behind it holds; that it is a thirst that the doing eases.

## The two rules

- God-sense: face to face only. Nothing is felt at any distance. Within speaking distance a god feels another god as a weight and a stillness in the air about it. The feeling is slow, can be mistaken, and a god that takes pains can pass for something lesser for a while. Weight only; never direction at range, never a count. Every god knows from landing that others came down when it did, and nothing more.
- Bodies: one body. No cost line. Things a god might send out (a part, a voice, a seeming, an echo) are no part of the rule; any god may propose one as a power on its own Day 2 or Night 2 and have it priced.

## How much gods stand out (Q8 at 7)

A god stands well above any mortal worker of magic. Mortals who see a god act know they are looking at something beyond their own kind, and their first reactions are awe, fear or calculation, not doubt. A god that keeps quiet can still pass, for a time, for a strange lord, a great sorcerer or a creature out of legend. The ceiling is in `S/star.md`.

## The wild (Q9 at 3)

Familiar animals nearly everywhere. Legends, some of which are true. A very few creatures with no name, kept to the deep, high, cold and dark places for ordinary reasons of ground and food. The same rules govern creatures everywhere.

Rare candidates from the answers (unplaced): a moth that tracks grief; a wasp that builds papery cities in dead trees; deer with something extra in their eyes; something large in the deep pools; a thing of the caves and high ledges; a thing the trappers do not return from.

## Past the borders (Q4 at 5)

About half the land held by rooted peoples and half open, by area and by count of regions. Old kingdoms with courts, customs, weights and measures of their own, with wild country between them wide enough to lose a road in, and a wild margin at the edges. Godless civilizations: three to five, set in 3a.3.

## Age of the human story (Q5 at 10)

The deepest the scale allows. Ruins under ruins over thousands of years: at least three distinct layers of earlier peoples under the living ones, and something older beneath. Names worn off, verdicts and boundaries that outlived their reasons, things remembered wrongly and a few remembered true.

## Non-human peoples (Q13 at 2, with its pool)

A human world. One non-human people, two at most, rare, keeping to themselves, neither friends nor enemies of humans.
- Most shared kind, the world's one: an old, slow, patient people of long memory who keep their own records, oaths or accounts and are unimpressed by gods and kings.
- A rare second, at most (unplaced, and may not be placed): a small folk of tunnels or deep wood who trade what they find on their own terms.
Which, where, and their real backstories: Night 3.

## Travelers (Q14 at 2, with its pool)

One or two, alone or as a pair, from under another star, each from a plane entry (3b.2). They carry tools that do not fit the world's craft, or a script, a reckoning or a way of making, in fragments. Candidates for how they came: unsure themselves; by water in a boat no one knows; half dead; with no way home; under some bond they cannot explain. None is brought to any god by schedule.

## Terrain the world must contain (Q2), and each god's placement wish

The continent is a land of rivers: several separate river systems, far apart, each its own country.
- The player: a river valley with many edges: broad flowering meadows, hedgerows, orchards, wet ground and dry, old woods, uplands of heather. Nothing harsh or barren.
- god-rivers: river country cut by water everywhere: springs in high places, braided middle channels, slow brown water and wet meadows below, marsh at the edges.
- god-secrets: river and mist country where rivers braid; wooded banks, few fords, room for a people that moves.
- god-dreams: forest and fen where they meet, hills behind, a river sliding between, a rise for a walled seat, woods dark at noon.
- god-ice: high ground holding snow into the warm months, a cold coast, long valleys that close in winter.
- god-law: wide slow rivers with towns at the crossings and fords, hills at the edges.
- god-stone: mountains: high cold rock, passes and notches, one broad valley cupped in the ranges; deep old stone.
- god-trade: where a river meets a road: wide water, low hills, a ford, a walled town. No caves or mazes of stone.
- From the star: a great lake at the center.
- Besides: marsh, low hills, wild country between the held lands, a wild margin.

## Pools

### Fears (Q10)
Widespread, as standing threats for the schedule: the slow trend with no author (failed harvest, blight, a hard or early winter, famine); sickness that travels the roads and the water; a patient, watching thing that does not come at you; the debt, oath or verdict that comes due.
Rare candidates (unplaced): sleepers who do not wake, a household one fewer by morning; the unquiet dead; a river that turns on those who trusted it; a bridge counted sound.

### Scarcity (Q11)
World scarcities: salt, as the thing in the land, unevenly spread, driving roads, trade and quarrels; and trust, as a temper of the peoples and never a thing: wary dealing, witnesses and tallies, the weight put on a kept word, written into each people's record as how they live.
Local shortages (unplaced): sweetness and honey; fuel and winter stores in the cold country; clean water in a bad year; good iron.

### Buried things (Q12)
Widespread: old straight roads, under the new roads and under fields; sealed doors in hills and mountain roots that nobody can explain.
Underground: the world has an underground layer of limited extent, behind the doors and under the old roads; extent set in 3a.3; data only unless an underground sheet is approved.
Rare candidates, single sites (unplaced): a huge and ancient abandoned apiary with wax older than the kingdoms (the player asked not to be told what it means; the DM is never given its meaning to tell); a drowned town under a reservoir no one remembers building; a ferry landing with no river beside it; graves dug too deep for their dead; a stair under a sealed door; a hall of forgotten purpose; great bones in a cave; straight roads under ice; a door shut on purpose with a notice beside it; boundary stones carved with unread terms; bones with an agreement in the hand; a vault under the old roads; a door with no handle on the near side; a road under a field still bearing wheel ruts; a small labyrinth above ground with a marked exit.

### Mortals worth following (Q17)
The kind the peoples tend to produce: ordinary working people of standing in a small place (midwife, ferryman, clerk, scribe, reeve, beekeeper, weir keeper, go-between, gate-warden, gravedigger), whose stories turn on noticing, on standing between two sides, and on holding to a word or a question at cost. No chosen heroes. Candidates for the small cast (3b.7); no one is scripted.

### Discoveries (Q18)
Widespread candidates: a truth about what the gods are and what lies behind their fogged pasts; a companion. The lore holds bodies for both.
Rare candidates (unplaced): something older than any god, neither friend nor enemy; a thing waiting at a spring at a river's head; a place past a long crossing where what was broken is known to be mended; a thin place of dreams; a hall of old oaths; a door that opens from the inside.

### Undared wishes (Q19), handled with care
None becomes a rule; none is placed where its own god is certain to find it. Candidates (unplaced): lamps kept on a sill by people who no longer know why; a people whose own law holds a maker to the letter of its wording (a custom, never a rule of the world); a place or being that can give back a name. One god skipped. The player's wish asks for nothing to be placed: the world is indifferent to every god until play changes it.

## Left out, and logged

A guarantee that rivers never fail; magic enforced by oath as a rule; a rule that magic's cost never falls on another; any sense of another god at a distance; more than one body. Each is in the keeper log with its reason.
