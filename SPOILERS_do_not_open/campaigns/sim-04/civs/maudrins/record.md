# People: the Maudrins

Agreed record, creation night 3a, 2026-11-22. The god gave no name; they call themselves the people of the Bend, and the river and the salt towns call them the Maudrins. Their town is Maudry [f64]. Wards, houses and wells are unnamed until play names them.

## Who they are
Human. A settled, close, careful river people of Maudvale [r18], behind a wall they keep in good repair. Wary of strangers, generous to a guest once he has eaten among them. Crowded, and they like it.

## How many
About 5,000, the number the god asked tonight, within the wall and in the fields and landings round it. The usual number, so no cost of size.

## How they live
A walled town at the bend, built close, lanes running down to a water gate; wells and a spring inside the wall; the ford under the wall's eye. Wet meadows, fish weirs, a ferry, a few outlying farms. Fish, grain, beans, eggs, fowl. Cloth, baskets, pots. Trade up and down the river.

## Their code
Do not foul the water above where you drink. Pour back the first draw of spring. Give water to any who ask it plainly. Keep your own house's well and take your turn at another's. Stand your place on the wall when the horn sounds. The captains do not fight each other inside the wall. A quarrel is settled at the water gate before dark.

## Skills and culture
Boats, ferries, weirs, nets and ropes; wells, channels and ditches; wattle, clay, timber and reed. The hearth bowl poured back at the turn of the year. Flood marks cut at the water gate and read each spring. Tallies on sticks and cords. Ordinary, unpriced.

## Edges and their weaknesses
Agreed in one round (ledger L-0025). One for one.
- Edge, clean water by custom: in an ordinary year they lose half as many to flux and water fever as their neighbours. In a year when such a sickness travels, any roll for it among them is one step in their favour. It does nothing against sickness carried by breath, touch or beast, and does not clean a well fouled past what settling and straining can mend; it neither gives to nor takes from the god's Clean water.
- Weakness, a people of the wet: more than three days' march from the river, or in dry country, a company of theirs moves at four fifths of the public rate for its kind and any contest is one step against them. Few horses. Poor stores for a long march. A low-river summer takes their mood down a step.

## Their magic
Bowl readers: from a bowl drawn from a well, what has already befallen that well (fouled, something dead in it, sickness passed through, how long ago). Never what is coming. For a day after she cannot drink and her hands are cold to the wrist for days; under that the one cost every worker pays runs slowly. Fitted by the world: about ten among them (ledger L-0027).

## What they believe about gods
That a mist lives at the ford and is kind to those who ask it plainly. They set out a bowl for it at the turn of the year. They think of it as the ford's own keeping, not a god. They do not know the god, nor that the saying binds anything.

## Rule and worship
A warlord rules the town through a war band and captains drawn from several houses, chosen from among the captains by the captains. A bowl at each hearth, a stone at each spring, a mark at the water gate, kept by whoever keeps them. No priests.

## Hierarchy
The warlord; the captains, each with a ward and a share of the wall; a law speaker who recites the code and holds a moot at the water gate with three well wardens; a ferry keeper; a water warden.

## Helpers
The water warden; the warlord; the captains; the law speaker; the well wardens; the ferry keeper. Orders pass at the water gate, by word, with the stone token of the ford. The offices exist and are filled on the morning the god lands. Its people do not yet know it as their god, so each office runs on the people's own custom and becomes the god's helper as its holder hears the god, in play. Until then no one acts for the god while it is away. What the god hopes the ferry keeper will carry is heard and not promised.

## How the god meets them
Already there, with their own history. The god comes down on the bank at the bend in the spring flood, unknown to them, and by its own conduct means to read the river for some days, clean the wells unseen, and let the water warden find it in the mist.

## The world's side
The land: the Maud [f07] rises in springs under the southern rim of Lake Hesh [r28], not in high places far off, and Maudvale [r18] lies between two heaths. History: the most-worked river in the land, weirs built on older weirs; the town's wall stands on an older quay. Neighbors: the salt towns down river and along the coast, who send salt up by boat; reed cutters of the Maudmire [r19]; herders on Ellen Heath [r17] and Sorrel Heath [r31]. The trouble the god did not ask for: the warlord is new, took the wall last autumn from a captain's house that has not forgiven it, and two captains have not yet eaten at his table.

## What they know of the land
- Maudvale [r18], the Maud [f07], Maudry [f64], the Weir Path [f65]: seen.
- The Maudmire [r19], seen by their boatmen. Orsk [f77] and Yskar [f76], reported, by salt boats.
- Ellen Heath [r17] and Sorrel Heath [r31], seen at their edges by herders.
- Lake Hesh [r28], reported: "the still water the river comes from under". Ammot [f68], rumored.
- The Comb Barrows [f62], rumored: a herders' tale of stone hives on the heath.
- Maudry [f64] to Orsk [f77], on foot, over open ground, no route: 250.7 miles, 24.4 days.
- Maudry [f64] to Yskar [f76], on foot, over open ground, no route: 332.7 miles, 23.8 days.
