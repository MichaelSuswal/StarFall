# People: the Rellen

Agreed record, creation night 3a, 2026-11-22. The god gave no name; the Rellen is what the salt towns call them and what they answer to. Their winter seat is Lowhythe [f73]. Camps, houses and boat companies are unnamed until play names them.

## Who they are
Human. A river people of the Skeinlands [r20]: ford keepers, boatmen, carriers, fishers and guides, who go where the water goes and do not own the banks they pass.

## How many
About 2,500, at the god's wish, in place of the usual 5,000, with its reasons (a people that moves must cross a ford in a day and feed from one reach). A default refused. Nothing is charged for it and no edge was asked for it to bear on. Its practical cost, stated plainly as it was to every god that refused the number: they make less, hold less and can lose less; a bad year goes deeper.

## How they live
A long round with the water. They winter at Lowhythe [f73] in houses of timber and turf; at the thaw they take to boats and banks; in summer they carry for others across channels no one else will risk; in autumn, at low clear water, they gather stores, count their dead and turn back toward the seat. Plank and pitch boats, flat barges, skin and reed craft. Wood, bone, flint, copper, a little traded bronze.

## Their code
What is told in confidence is kept. A word given binds. A stranger at the ford is carried across and asked nothing until carried. The dead are buried at the water's edge. No one is turned from a crossing for a debt. A quarrel is not carried across water.

## Skills and culture
Boatbuilding, pitch and cordage, reading water and the shape of a ford, fishing and smoking, herb lore, memory kept in knotted cord. Low songs on the water, for the dead and the crossing. That they cross rivers better than anyone is what they believe of themselves. Ordinary, unpriced.

## Edges and their weaknesses
None proposed, none priced.

## Their magic
The god asked none and hoped for a small thing. The world fits every people alike: about five workers among them (ledger L-0027), who can take a fever or a pain into themselves for some hours so that another may sleep. It costs her what she takes, for as long as she carries it; under that the one cost every worker pays runs slowly.

## What they believe about gods
They do not hold with gods. That the water carries what is lost to a far bank; that those who carry the dead are not to be looked at closely; a story that a stranger comes to the ford some autumn, asks nothing, and keeps what is told to him. They do not know the god.

## Rule and worship
A mortal line, the Held, holds the throne for life and is sacred for what it keeps: the people's cord, their crossings and their word. A court stands between it and the people. No worship, no priest, no shrine; the god asked none.

## Hierarchy
The Held; the court: the Hearers (drawn by lot and sworn), the Warden of the Ford, the Keeper of the Cord, the Marshal of the Bank, the Reeve of Stores, the Boatwright. Disputes go to the Hearers, each side heard in confidence; then to the Held; then to the lot.

## Helpers
The court's offices, as above. Orders go by knotted cord, carried by runner or boat to each company, which ties a return. The offices exist and are filled on the morning the god lands. Its people do not yet know it as their god, so each office runs on the people's own custom and becomes the god's helper as its holder hears the god, in play. Until then no one acts for the god while it is away.

## How the god meets them
Already there, with their own history, waiting for no one. The god comes down on a gravel bar at Grayle Ford [f72] in autumn, a stranger with a pole, and by its own conduct carries across whoever asks and says nothing of what it is.

## The world's side
History: they move over ground that settled kingdoms once held; there are landings where the water has since moved away (Dry Stairs [f75]). Neighbors: the salt towns (Varn [f78], Yskar [f76]), who hire them and think them thieves; shepherds of Hollin Ridge [r23]. The trouble the god did not ask for: the Skein's [f08] channels have been shifting faster than the cords record, two fords failed last year, and Varn [f78] has begun cutting its own ferry channel.

- The Ysk Flats [r21], Varn [f78], Yskar [f76]: seen by their carriers.
- Hollin Ridge [r23] and Hollin Spring [f74]: reported, by shepherds.
- Vestfen [r25] and Vesthallow [f71]: reported. The Koldry Hills [r26]: rumored.
- Grayle Ford [f72] to Lowhythe [f73], on foot, over open ground, no route: 44.8 miles, 3.2 days.
- Lowhythe [f73] to Varn [f78], by boat, by The Skein [f08]: 77.4 miles, 2.6 days.
- Lowhythe [f73] to Yskar [f76], on foot, over open ground, no route: 126.6 miles, 9.0 days.
