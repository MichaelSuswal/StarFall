# Godless people: the kingdom of Dunmarrow

## Who they are
Human. The old inland kingdom of the plain: farmers, herders, factors and a court at Hallisk [f35]. Rooted, proud of their weights and their roads, old and wearing.
## How many
About 70,000: Hallisk [f35] about 9,000 where it once held thrice that; Averby [f29] and Pellam [f36] about 3,000 each; the rest in villages across Dunmarrow [r07], the Sarn Meads [r09] and the eastern Lornlands [r06].
## Where
Dunmarrow [r07] and the Sarn Meads [r09], with the crossing of Averby [f29] in the Lornlands [r06]. The Marrow Road [f32] is theirs.
## How they live
Grain and cattle; a king, a court, a hall of weights; tolls on roads and crossings; brine boiled at Brinegarth [f37], never enough, so salt is bought from the south at a price that shapes every reign.
## Their code
The king's measure is the measure. A charter outlives the king who gave it. A witnessed word stands in court. Hospitality to envoys is sacred; to everyone else it is priced.
## Their history in the land
The third kingdom on this plain by their own count, and the plain holds older roads than theirs. Their greatest reach was eight generations ago, when they took toll at the Carn [f05] ford and on the Lorn [f01] to the sea. They have been giving ground since, without a war: fields that yield less, villages that empty, a court that grows as the land thins.
## What they believe about gods
That their first kings were gods' sons and that gods are a matter for charters. A court that hears a god has come down will send an envoy with a form of address ready.
## What they want
What they had: the tolls of the Carn [f05] ford and the Lorn [f01], cheaper salt, and a reason for the thinning that is not their fault.
## Whether and how they touch the great events
Yes. Theirs is the oldest and most-worked ground on the sheet outside the lake basin, so the thinning shows here first among the held lands; they will be among the first to look for someone to blame. How, and when, is 3b's.
## What they know of the land
Their own regions, seen. Tollen [f27], Carnford [f39], Kessmar [f19] by its traders, Ternaby [f15] by its carriers: reported. Lake Hesh [r28] and the shore villages: seen at Nenna [f66]. The salt towns: reported. Hallisk [f35] to Pellam [f36], on foot, by The Marrow Road [f32]: 136.5 miles, 6.8 days. Hallisk [f35] to Averby [f29], on horse, by The Marrow Road [f32]: 154.4 miles, 5.1 days. Pellam [f36] to Nenna [f66], on foot, over open ground, no route: 64.0 miles, 4.6 days.

## What the old tell (added creation night 3b, 2026-11-22)
Written from `deep-history.md`. What is told, and where the telling is wrong. Nothing agreed on Night 3a is changed.
The court tells that the first kings were the sons of a god who ruled at Hallisk [f35] and went up into the great light in glory, and that the kings of Dunmarrow [r07] are of that blood. Half right: a god did rule there, about 2,600 years before the arrival, and did go up; the kings after it were its stewards' line, and Dunmarrow [r07], the third kingdom, was founded by a man who claimed the blood, 630 years before the arrival. A torn court roll says "the king went up in his own light"; the court reads it as a blessing. They tell that Dryhythe [f38] was left when a jealous neighbor turned the river. Wrong: the branch slowed and went to grass in nine years and no one turned it. They hold that the fields yield less because the salt is dear and the people are idle.
