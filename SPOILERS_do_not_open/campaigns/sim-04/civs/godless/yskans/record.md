# Godless people: the salt towns of Ysk

## Who they are
Human. A league of three old towns on the south-east coast that hold the salt pans: Yskar [f76], Orsk [f77] and Varn [f78]. Panmen, boilers, factors, coasting sailors. People call them the Yskans.
## How many
About 30,000: Yskar [f76] about 8,000, Orsk [f77] and Varn [f78] about 5,000 each, the rest in pan villages on the Ysk Flats [r21] and farms on Orsk Shore [r22].
## Where
The Ysk Flats [r21] and Orsk Shore [r22].
## How they live
Salt, the one thing every people of the land must buy. Pans and boiling houses; coasting boats west to the Maud [f07] and the Withy [f06] and up every river; carriers north over the Salt Way [f80]. Each town has a council of pan-holders; the league meets at Yskar [f76].
## Their code
Salt is weighed in the open. A cargo sworn is a cargo delivered. No town undersells another in a bad year. A lamp is kept on a seaward sill in every house, by people who no longer know why.
## Their history in the land
The pans are older than the towns. The burial ground at Lowgrave Field [f79] has graves dug far too deep for their dead, and no one alive knows whose custom that was. The league is four generations old, made after a salt war among the towns.
## What they believe about gods
That the sea gives and the sun takes; small shrines at the pans. Gods who come down are customers or rivals.
## What they want
The price kept high, the Skein's [f08] mouths kept open, and the moving people of the Skeinlands [r20] kept useful and poor.
## Whether and how they touch the great events
Through salt: whoever squeezes or frees the salt moves every people at once. Whether the flats themselves change is 3b's.
## What they know of the land
Their coast and flats, seen. Maudry [f64], Eelstow [f46], Vesthallow [f71], Lowhythe [f73]: seen by their carriers. Sallowdene [r12]: reported, as a buyer of salt and a seller of tapers. Dunmarrow [r07]: reported. Yskar [f76] to Orsk [f77], on foot, by The Salt Way [f80]: 105.7 miles, 7.5 days. Yskar [f76] to Varn [f78], on foot, over open ground, no route: 107.4 miles, 7.7 days.

## What the old tell (added creation night 3b, 2026-11-22)
Written from `deep-history.md`. What is told, and where the telling is wrong. Nothing agreed on Night 3a is changed.
They tell that the pans were laid by the sea itself and that the first panmen found them ready. Wrong: the road-builders laid them, more than 3,300 years before the arrival. Lowgrave Field [f79] they hold to be a plague ground, dug deep against the sweating sickness. Wrong: it is two and a half centuries older than the sickness, the ground of a sect that buried deep for its own reasons. The lamp on the seaward sill they keep without a reason; some say it is for luck with salt. They are proud of the salt war and of the chain at Varn [f78]. They know the list-singers as people who come ashore at Varn [f78] every thirty years, pay for nothing and are let through, by custom. A foreign carter at Varn [f78], thirteen years on the coast, is known for a saddle no one has copied.
