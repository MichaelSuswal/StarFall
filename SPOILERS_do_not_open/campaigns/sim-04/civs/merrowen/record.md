# People: the Merrowen

Agreed record, creation night 3a, 2026-11-22. The god named the people and their walled seat, Vesthallow [f71]. Smaller places are unnamed until play names them.

## Who they are
Human. A fen and forest people of Vestfen [r25]: reed-cutters, eel-takers, charcoal burners, woodcutters, shepherds, and the smiths, weavers and potters of a small walled town. A keeping people, wary of strangers, generous to those who have been tried.

## How many
About 5,000, the number the god took as given: about two thousand within the walls, three thousand in steadings round it and along the fen's edge. The usual number, so no cost of size.

## How they live
Timber and daub houses in narrow lanes; steadings of a longhouse and outbuildings; fields on the drier ground, eel weirs and reed beds in the fen, charcoal stacks in the wood. They burn tallow, rushlight and oil in unusual quantity and know it is dear.

## Their code
Keep your word. Keep your lamp lit for the one who walks in last. Turn no one from your door who stands at the edge of its light and asks nothing. Swear by lamplight or not at all. Do not wake a sleeper to settle a quarrel. Abuse of hospitality is the gravest wrong.

## Skills and culture
Eel and reed work, basketry, charcoal, tallow and rushlights, fen boats, timber framing, coarse dark wool, plain pots, the lore of fen and wood. The household lamp is kept through the night in every house, for the dead and for the traveler: a custom, and it costs them oil and tallow they can ill spare in a bad year. Ordinary, unpriced.

## Edges and their weaknesses
Agreed in one round (ledger L-0024). One for one.
- Edge, the dark eye: by starlight or one rushlight they work, herd and keep a fen path as other peoples do at dusk; a moonless night or a dense mist does not lose them. Where a contest turns on seeing in the dark, one step in their favour. It needs some light; with none they are as blind as anyone. It touches no dream and neither gives to nor takes from the god's powers.
- Weakness, the glare: from late spring to early autumn, between mid-morning and mid-afternoon on a clear day, their work in the open is at three quarters of the public rates. Where a contest turns on seeing far or fine in full sun, one step against them. Headache and eye sickness for some each summer.

## Their magic
Wick-singing: a worker sits with a lit wick and sings low over a sufferer; the pain eases and a heavy dreamless sleep comes. Nothing more. It costs her a night's sleep for each night she sings, and it builds; under that the one cost every worker pays runs slowly. Fitted by the world: about ten among them (ledger L-0027).

## What they believe about gods
No named god. That there are watchers in the night who keep the sleepers, that they come out of the wood seldom and unannounced, and that one leaves a light and does not stare. They do not know the god.

## Rule and worship
A sacred line, the Wick, holds the throne; the one on it is the Wickholder, who keeps the great lamp of the town. A court stands between throne and people. Before the god is known the line is held sacred for the lamp it keeps, not for the god. Household and village lamps on a sill or in a niche, kept by whoever keeps them. No priests.

## Hierarchy
The Wickholder reigns and rules little alone. The court: the Voice, the Marshal of the Wall, the Reeve of the Steadings, the Steward of Oil and Tallow, the Hearthward, who settles disputes by lamplight at the gate of the hall. Heads of the oldest houses sit with the court on grave matters.

## Helpers
The court itself: the Voice, the Marshal of the Wall, the Reeve of the Steadings, the Steward of Oil and Tallow, the Hearthward. Orders go by runner on the fen paths, by cart on the dry road, and at night by a chain of shuttered lanterns that carries a few plain signals. The offices exist and are filled on the morning the god lands. Its people do not yet know it as their god, so each office runs on the people's own custom and becomes the god's helper as its holder hears the god, in play. Until then no one acts for the god while it is away.

## How the god meets them
Already there, with their own history, waiting for no one by name. The god comes down at the wood's edge above the fen in late autumn, unknown to them. No lamp is lit for it anywhere, so by its own card it lands dull, at half pace and without its powers until a mortal lights one in its name. What it hopes the first mortal it meets will do is heard and not promised.

## The world's side
History: the fen was open water once, and Vesthallow's [f71] rise was an island in it; the oldest house-platforms are boat landings. Neighbors: carriers of the salt towns come up the Salt Way [f80]; the shore people of Heshshore [r29] to the west; the Oum of the Koldry Hills [r26], who are not human, to the east, seen at the fen's edge once in some years. The trouble the god did not ask for: the Wickholder is old and has named no heir, and the court is already two courts.

## What they know of the land
- Vestfen [r25], the Vest [f09], Vesthallow [f71]: seen.
- Ammerholt [r24], seen at its southern edge; its heart rumored, "dark at noon, and no one's".
- Hollin Ridge [r23], seen from the fen; the Salt Way [f80], seen as far as the ridge, reported beyond.
- Yskar [f76] and the Ysk Flats [r21], reported, by salt carriers.
- Heshshore [r29], Lowater [f67] and Lake Hesh [r28], reported, by those who trade eels there.
- The Koldry Hills [r26] and Koldry [f81], rumored.
- Vesthallow [f71] to Yskar [f76], on foot, by The Salt Way [f80]: 399.8 miles, 28.6 days.
- Vesthallow [f71] to Lowater [f67], on foot, over open ground, no route: 78.4 miles, 8.1 days.
