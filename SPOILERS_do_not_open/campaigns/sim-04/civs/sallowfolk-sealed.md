## For the Keeper to reconcile: the world's answers
1. How long they have been in the valley. Eleven or twelve generations by their own reckoning; they say "always". The figure is fixed with the deep history in 3b.4.
2. What came before them. Their story is right that others were here: the Foot Track [f44] follows, for part of its length, one of the old straight roads the whole land has, and Kettling Hall [f48] is older than they are. Who those people were, how many, and what became of them is 3b's. The Sallowfolk hold it as story only, as the player wished, because that is all they have.
3. Past their borders. Up the valley, Withyhead [r13], open country with a few farms (Merestead [f45]) and the Withymere [f12]. Down the valley, Withymouth [r15] and the eel fishers of Eelstow [f46]. North, Heatherfell [r11]; south, Stonnerfell [r14]: summer grazing, no one's. No kingdom borders them and no border is drawn; a stranger comes by the track or up the river. Their tapers and honey go two ways: up the track and over the Fell Way [f43] with drovers to Carnford [f39], and down river to Eelstow [f46], where coasting boats of the salt towns bring salt and take wax. Iron and bronze come by the same two ways. "The region" to them is the Withy [f06] from the mere to the sea and the two fells.
4. The old saying. It is older than the god and has ordinary causes. Swarms settle where swarms have settled: on houses with old skep stands, sound thatch, a south wall and the smell of old comb, which are the houses of careful keepers; so the saying that swarms settle on the right houses is true as far as it goes. The valley is the mildest country of the land and sheltered by two fells, which is what the saying about the valley means to those who hold it. No other cause is placed tonight, and 3b adds none for this god's sake. What they believe it means: that the valley is looked after, by nothing in particular.
5. The market ground. Stands: Sallowford [f47], the ford on the Withy [f06] near the valley's middle, with a fair ground that fills a few days a year. The reeves meet there. They call it the crossing.
6. The roofless stone hall. Stands: Kettling Hall [f48], a roofless stone hall by the Foot Track [f44] at the edge of the highest hamlet (unnamed hamlet 13 [f61]). They call it the old hall. What the mortal the player made has noticed is so, as he has noticed it: each spring, wild swarms and casts from the upland edge cluster on its south wall for a day or two and leave. The cause is ordinary: warm dry stone out of the wind on the swarms' way down from the fell, and no cavity in it dry enough to take. No custom attaches to it. Nothing more is placed in the hall tonight; whether the deep history gives the hall a past is 3b's, and nothing is put there because this god may look.
7. The hamlets. Thirteen, unnamed, left for play: unnamed hamlet 1 [f49] to unnamed hamlet 13 [f61], strung along the Withy [f06] on both banks; the highest is unnamed hamlet 13 [f61], on the northern slope where Heatherfell [r11] begins. The Keeper's records use the placeholders until play names each.
8. The bee-wives. Fitted the same way as every people's workers (ledger L-0027): three, where the wish was about one hamlet in three. Which hamlets, by roll, the method named in each roll: unnamed hamlet 10 [f58] [r-0142], unnamed hamlet 7 [f55] [r-0145, replacing r-0143, a face outside 1 to 13], unnamed hamlet 9 [f57] [r-0146, replacing r-0144, a hamlet already taken]. None is in the highest hamlet. In the other ten hamlets it is rumor, as the player wished and as three in thirteen makes it. Under the bad night each use costs her, the one cost every worker pays runs slowly (sleep, then memory, then her name). The DM is told the count through `campaigns/sim-04/player/corrections.md`.
9. The name Sallowdene [r12]. It is the people's name for their home and is the map's name for the valley between the two fells. Drovers and salt boats use the same name. The river is the Withy [f06], the Keeper's name, taken from the willows the people are named for; the people use it.

## Starting numbers, beyond the public state file
