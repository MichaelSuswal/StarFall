# People: the Dathri

Agreed record, creation night 3a, 2026-11-22. The god named the people and their town, Kessmar [f19]. Terraces, hamlets and houses are unnamed until play names them.

## Who they are
Human. A mountain people of Kessmar Vale [r05]: terrace farmers, goatherds and stone-cutters, slow of speech, who weigh a word before spending it.

## How many
About 5,000, the usual number: a town of fitted and dry-laid stone on the south shore of Lake Arrun [f11], the rest in hamlets on the terraces. Gathered close, as the god wished.

## How they live
Barley, a hardy bean, roots and flax on terraces; goats and sheep on the high summer grass; lake fish; stone and charcoal. They store hard against winter. Spring is the lean season. What comes to them comes by Harl Notch [f20] and Venn Notch [f21].

## Their code
Guest-right is sacred. A word given is kept. A stone laid in a house's wall is not taken from it. The old are heard first and the young carry. A killing in the valley is answered by labour and goods, for a term the council sets.

## Skills and culture
Dry-stone building and terracing; herding on steep ground; charcoal; bronze and soft copper where they have it; weaving, felt, cheese. Long roundabout stories, set forms of address, a cairn stone for every child and every guest of note. Ordinary, unpriced. They are poor riders and poor sailors and slow to adapt outside the range: a fact of them, and it bought nothing.

## Edges and their weaknesses
Agreed in two rounds (ledger L-0023). Two steps for two weaknesses.
- Edge: their dry stone stands through frost and ordinary earthquake as other peoples' does not. Bought by the first weakness.
- Edge: their stonework takes half the labour of the public rates: 3 worker-days a yard of built wall in place of 6, 1 in place of 2 for dry stone to head height, and the like for a terrace, pier or gatehouse. Mortal crews, mortal tools; it takes nothing from the god's Shaping the Stone. Bought by the second weakness.
- First weakness: harvests close to the edge. Stores at landing cover the winter and little more. One bad year is hunger; two in a row are deaths among the old and the youngest.
- Second weakness: every bronze tool comes in through the notches; no ore of their own, few smiths. Whenever no trader of theirs can pass any way out for more than a season, by snow, war, a ward or any other cause, stonework falls to an ordinary crew's pace, and after a year of it the work stops as tools wear out. A way their named traders can pass is not shut.

## Their magic
Stone-feelers: through the palms, whether a wall will hold, a slope slide, or a hollow lie behind a face. It costs the worker numbness in fingers and hands for as many days as the work was long; under that the one cost every worker pays runs slowly. Fitted by the world: about ten among them (ledger L-0027).

## What they believe about gods
That gods are wardens of the ways between places and come at the thaw when the passes open. "What comes through the notch in spring is either a guest or a debt." They expect a guest, not a master. They do not know the god.

## Rule and worship
A Council of Nine, elders and headmen of the town and hamlets, with a Speaker chosen from among them. The god may be heard there once it is known; it does not command. Household cairns and a shrine stone at each hamlet's edge, kept by whoever keeps them; a pinch of salt, a drop of milk, a small stone. No priests. They do not worship the god by name.

## Hierarchy
The Nine and their Speaker; an Elder of Law who keeps the moot; under them a Gate-warden, a Master of Works, a Steward of Stores and the headmen. Disputes go to the council sitting as a moot, in a moot house of timber and turf.

## Helpers
The Gate-warden; the Master of Works; the Steward of Stores; the Elder of Law. Orders go by runner on the terrace paths and by signal fire on a high cairn, three signals. The offices exist and are filled on the morning the god lands. Its people do not yet know it as their god, so each office runs on the people's own custom and becomes the god's helper as its holder hears the god, in play. Until then no one acts for the god while it is away.

## How the god meets them
Already there, with their own history. The god comes down on the vale's floor about 2 miles south of the town, with the mountains of the vale's south wall on the skyline about 18 miles behind it, in spring, unknown to them, and by its own conduct means to stand a day in plain view and then come to the council as a guest at the thaw.

## The world's side
The land is not quite what the god hoped: the cupped valley's floor is mostly lake, Lake Arrun [f11], and the town and terraces sit on its shores; the two notches are in the south wall, near the town, not far across the valley. History: they say they have always been here; their oldest walls stand on older and better ones that are not theirs. A sealed door (Gathol Door [f24]) is a place they do not dig. Neighbors: Dunmarrow [r07] through Pellam [f36], where stone and wool meet salt and bronze; hill steadings of the Harrow Roughs [r27]. The trouble the god did not ask for: Pellam's [f36] factors have doubled the price of bronze in three years, and the council is split between paying and finding another way out.

## What they know of the land
- Kessmar Vale [r05], Lake Arrun [f11], Dath Water [f04], Kessmar [f19], Harl Notch [f20], Venn Notch [f21]: seen.
- The Harlfells [r04], seen round the valley; the Harl Way [f22] and the Venn Track [f23], seen by those who go.
- Pellam [f36] and the Sarn Meads [r09], reported, by their traders. Dunmarrow [r07] and Hallisk [f35], rumored.
- The Harrow Roughs [r27], reported, by herders. Ammerholt [r24], rumored: "the dark wood below".
- Gathol Door [f24], seen from outside. What is behind it, not known.
- Kessmar [f19] to Harl Notch [f20], on foot, by The Harl Way [f22]: 56.8 miles, 4.1 days.
- Kessmar [f19] to Pellam [f36], on foot, by The Harl Way [f22]: 187.2 miles, 13.4 days.
- Kessmar [f19] to Venn Notch [f21], on foot, by The Venn Track [f23]: 71.5 miles, 5.1 days.

## What the old tell (added creation night 3b, 2026-11-22)
Written from `deep-history.md`. What is told, and where the telling is wrong. Nothing agreed on Night 3a is changed.
They tell that they have always been in the vale and that the first Dathri fitted the oldest walls. Wrong: they came up about 1,500 years before the god came, and the oldest walls of Kessmar [f19] were standing then, the work of the road-builders. They tell that Gathol Door [f24] shuts in a king who would not die and that digging there lets him out. Wrong about the king. Right that someone lies behind it, though not at the door and not a king. They say the great light stands a little nearer the south wall of the vale than it did in their grandmothers' day, and they argue about it.
