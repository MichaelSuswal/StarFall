# Report to the Keeper, Kiln, Year 1

**Touching others.**
- Timber people (north): an 80-person party went in force, haggled nine days, bought beams and planks for a hall, no blows struck. The people dislike them more than before. Nothing to roll. If the Keeper wants the north to answer the shoving, that is the Keeper's.
- Pass people: toll paid in pots by both parties; nothing further.
- Hill people: the Kiln are angry at being turned back and told no stranger goes down to the river without leave. The Quarrelers talk of going to ask leave with spears. The council forbids it this year. The Keeper should decide whether the hill people notice the angry talk or send word. No roll needed from me.

**The people report to Ashwake at year's end.**
Population 5,015 (182 born, 167 dead). Grain bought for the winter ran thin and must be bought again. The great kiln is finished and was fired once. Timber for the hall is bought and stacked, to be raised next year. The coal custom holds. The western watch saw only sky-lights. Mood warming, two factions.

The walkers, in their words:
- The potter: "We found nothing, lord, but your coals lived the whole road."
- The charcoal-burner: "It pulls at the chest. Bring more pots next time, the toll-takers are hungry."
- The clay-digger: "The hill men had notched poles by their houses and would not say why. They turned us back."
- The young burner: "The old man smiled when he took our pots. I did not like it."
