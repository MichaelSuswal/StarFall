> SPOILER WARNING. Keeper release to the Kiln civ agent, Year 1, pass one.

Season opens in early spring. Ordinary weather in the burning hills: a wet spring that floods the dry riverbed below the town for two weeks, a hot dry summer, an ordinary autumn and winter. The vents breathe as they always have. Clay and scrub are as always; timber must be bought from the timber people to the north, who will sell for charcoal and pots and haggle hard.

Things the people see: a sky-light at midsummer, a single steady pale light rising slowly from below the horizon to the west, climbing for about as long as it takes to walk two hundred paces, dimming and gone high up; a second at midwinter, also to the west. (Two others this year rise too far west to be seen from the burning hills.)

The god's decisions for the year are in SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/dryrun/gods/ashwake/decisions-001.md. Carry them out as the people are able. Of the two parties sent west (decision 3): the Keeper has resolved their journeys. Both reached a pass town of stone-walled toll-takers about twenty days out and paid toll in pots. Both walked on into hill country held by a hill people. Party one (the potter and the charcoal-burner) was never approached; they turned at thirty days as told and came home in early summer with nothing found and the coals still alive. Party two (the clay-digger and the young burner) was stopped about day 27 at a hill steading by hill people who questioned them, took two pots as a gift, and turned them back, saying no stranger goes down to the river from their hills without the hill people's leave. The clay-digger noticed tall poles by the hill steading's houses, notched from top to bottom, and did not learn what they were for. Both parties are home by early summer. Report what they tell the god and the people, in the people's words, and how the people take it.
