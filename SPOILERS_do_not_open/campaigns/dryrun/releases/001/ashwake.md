> SPOILER WARNING. Keeper release to the god Ashwake, Year 1.

Your people's year is in SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/dryrun/civs/kiln/years/001.md and report-001.md (read both). The great kiln stands and was fired once. The hall timber is bought and stacked. The watch fire saw nothing but the sky. The coal custom holds and has become a market day. Grain for the winter ran thin.

Your two parties came home in early summer, about sixty days after they left, both with the coals alive. Neither found a god. Both passed, going and coming, through a walled town of stone at the meeting of three passes about twenty days west, whose gate-wardens take toll of anything carried and took pots; they asked who sent your people and what the pots held and got "a god's word for the west", and made them keep the coals in the gate-yard. Beyond the passes is open hill country held by a hill people with herds and dogs. The first party (the potter and the charcoal-burner) walked ten days into those hills, saw herders on the ridges who kept their distance, turned at thirty days as you told them, and came home. The second party (the clay-digger and the young burner) was stopped about day 27 at a hill steading, questioned by an old man who smiled when he took two pots as a gift, and turned back with the words that no stranger goes down to the river from their hills without the hill people's leave. The clay-digger saw tall poles by the steading's houses, notched from top to bottom, and was told nothing about them. Your messages m-0001 and m-0002 came home unspoken to any god.

Your senses: the nearer pull, which was a little north of west when you landed, drifted west through the spring, a little each night, until by early summer it sat almost in line with the farther pull, both west and a little south. It has stayed there since. The farther pull has not moved. Two sky-lights this year, both in the west: at midsummer and at midwinter, each a single steady pale light rising slowly from below the horizon, climbing for the time it takes to walk two hundred paces, dimming, gone high up. The Kiln say they are gods going up and that you will go that way too if they fail you; your people are wrong about what the lights are.

No message reached you. No one came to your hearth from outside your land.
