> SPOILER WARNING. Keeper release to the god Thornmere, Year 1. Your year is held at a moment of contact; your decisions for Year 2 wait until the scene is played in the day session.

You walked west from the first days of spring, alone, by night where the ground was open, walking every notch, ford and fork (your record may keep them: eleven notches and nine fords between the passes and the river valley, and the brook crossings along the hill foot). The Carn hold that country in stone-and-turf steadings with more dogs than people. As far as you know, no one saw you.

At the start of the fifth week you came over the last hills and saw the river valley below: water meadows and scattered oak, a brown river running south to a wide marsh and a grey sea, a dozen villages along it, smoke. The pull was in the nearest village under the hills (its people call it Wellbrook). You crossed a brook above the village in the sixth week at night and went north along the hill foot, and kept level with the pull as it moved upriver, about a village a week, along the hills and then the wood's edge, until in the thirteenth week it came to the top of the valley, where the river leaves the wood over a falls beside the village they call Fallstead, and stayed there.

What you learned from the herders' talk at the hill foot, overheard, never spoken to: the river people call themselves the Riverfolk and their god the Weir-King. He woke on a shingle bar at the river's mouth at the start of spring and walked upriver wetting his hand at every staithe; he says little; a woman died at the village they call Aldford while he stood in the river and said nothing, and that village is cool to him; he ordered a weir built at the mouth; in the fourteenth week two men went up past the falls into the wood on his errand, and you saw them carry a boat round the falls at first light. You watched him from the shoulder above the falls more than once at dusk; as far as you know he did not see you.

Your senses: one sky-light this year before the hold, in the tenth week, over the wood to the north, a single steady pale light rising slowly, climbing a quarter of the way up the sky in about the time it takes to walk two hundred paces, dimming, gone. (A light rose over the far western sea in the first week, too low to be seen from the passes where you then were.) The far pull to the east has not changed its bearing. The near pull is at the falls.

Week 16, dusk: you came down the east bank path from the wood's edge to the stones at the pool below the falls, openly, a little below Fallstead and away from the houses, the fishers gone in. The god is standing knee-deep in the river at the foot of the falls, facing you: a tall man, river-grey eyes, never dry, water beading on him though he stands in spray. Two children on the village staithe three hundred paces downstream have seen you. You stopped at the water's edge ten paces from him on the stones. He has not spoken and neither have you. The scene is the day's; when the DM brings you in, speak as yourself from your decisions (one sentence of who you are; ask what it is and what it wants of this valley; nothing of your power; no oath, no bargain; leave if hostile; do not stay long if welcomed).

Nothing reached you from the Gatefolk this year; you have not been back.
