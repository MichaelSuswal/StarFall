# Year 1

## The frame
Season opens in early spring. No clocked event fires (first: the Ald flood, Year 2 late summer). Random event rolled: none (odds 10, roll 34). Sky-lights: 4, the true count from the sealed record, at the moments fixed in world-events.md (early spring over the sea; late spring over the wood; midsummer over the eastern hills, the night of week 17; midwinter over the sea). Weather: ordinary year as set in world-events.md; the Ald high for three weeks in early spring; dry summer; two autumn gales in the week of the fish run; ordinary winter.

## The Weir-King and the Riverfolk (player's god; thread held from week 23)
Cause of every act: player/decisions-001.md, carried out under standing conduct (observe only, avoid strangers, retreat if seen, defend but do not kill).

- Weeks 1 to 23: the god walks the Ald upriver from the shingle bar, touching the water at every village, several days at each: Wellbrook about week 5, Aldford about week 9, Fallstead by week 22. The Riverfolk leave a reed bundle on the shingle the first day; he takes it as meant (standing conduct, observe).
- Week 5, Wellbrook: the headman Hobb and fishers ask him for a law of giving at the weir. He observes and says nothing (conduct). The question is a pending question for the player (DESIGN 12.9); the world did not wait: Maren of Oxmouth set the giving gate and the tenth herself (civ year). Keeper ruling on the rule: a petition from the god's own people, answered in the moment by the god's standing silence while the world goes on, is a pending question, not a held encounter (ruling ledger r-001).
- Week 6: Thornmere, walking at night, crosses the Wellbrook brook a mile above the village. River-sense: the god knows within the day that a single walker he has never sensed before crossed the brook from the hills, going north along the hill foot. God-sense: the nearer pull, which moved toward him through weeks 4 and 5, has stopped in the hills east of Wellbrook and stays there, then shadows him up the valley all summer, never nearer than the hills.
- Week 9, Aldford: a net-maker, Tam, begs the god at the water to save his wife Ede in a hard labor. The god has no power that bears on it (power card: river-sense only); conduct: cautious, no power used in a new way. He comes to the door of the house and stands there through the night and does nothing else. Roll "Ede comes through alive": odds 45, roll 88, failed; she dies before dawn. Roll "the child lives": odds 45, roll 19, success; a daughter lives. New fixed facts: Tam, net-maker of Aldford, about 30; Ede his wife, dead week 9 Year 1 in childbed, aged about 25; their daughter, unnamed at year's end. The Riverfolk deaths this year become 169 and the births 183 (civ count plus this birth and death); population at year's end 5,014 stands (both added).
- Weeks 11 to 13: a dugout built at Fallstead (30 worker-days). Week 14, about day 94: Hask of Fallstead and Brin of Aldford pole it round and above the falls, cache it in a pool, and go east on foot into the North Wood (7.5 miles a day laden, forest), two and a half days to the hills' edge, then into the Carn's hill country at 10.5 miles a day. Trigger fired: boats passed the first falls.
- Weeks 15 to 17: the boatmen reach Black Mere on about the sixth day out, having passed below the old earthworks on the crest (the King's Fort) on the fifth. Roll "Carn stop and question the boatmen": odds 70, roll 40, stopped. On the seventh day, at a high steading above the mere, Carn herders surround them with dogs and take them to an elder. The Carn keep them two nights, feed them, and ask about the river people, the new god who woke on the bar, and whether the river people count the lights. The boatmen answer plainly (Hask literal, Brin talkative). By the houses stand tall poles notched from top to bottom; the Carn do not say what they are for, and the boatmen do not ask twice. The elder, a woman the Carn call Grandmother Ceth (new fixed fact: aged about 70, one eye clouded), sends them back the way they came with the words: the river people are to come no farther than the wood's edge; the Carn will come down to see the river themselves. Roll "boatmen return unhurt within time": odds 45, roll 42, success. They reach Fallstead about day 115, week 17, unhurt. Map knowledge: North Wood reported, Drover's Hills reported, Black Mere reported, King's Fort reported, all by the boatmen (player/map-log.md). They also bring the name of the hill people, the Carn, and the words about the poles and the warning.
- Weeks 16 to 19: the weir at Oxmouth, built as the civ year says (two rows of 60 oak posts, stone and brush, wicker face, trap-pen, giving gate, a tenth returned). River-sense: the god, at Aldford and on the road to Fallstead, feels the Ald's flow at the mouth change over those days as the posts go in and the fill is packed, and feels the gate opened on the dark of the moon.
- Week 17: the old posts under the Oxmouth mud struck by the mauls (mystery Short 1, clue "posts seen at low water"; cause: the weir work the player ordered). Two rows, black, square-cut. Maren says the god's first weir; Hobb says a drowned house and a grave. Nobody digs. The truth (sealed): the drowned village of the salt-road migrants, 150 years under. Both headmen are wrong.
- Summer: roll "the god catches sight of the hidden watcher": odds 50, roll 13, success. Once, in week 19 at dusk from the Aldford bank, he sees a figure standing on the hill crest to the east against the last light, too far for a face, for the time it takes to draw a net; when he looks again it is gone. The pull is there.
- Weeks 22 to 25: harvest, good (civ year). The god reaches Fallstead about week 22 and stands at the falls; Hask's mother asks him to swear the boatmen will come back (they are already back; her ask was made before, in late summer when the god arrived, by the civ year; the Keeper fixes it: she asked him, on his arrival about week 22, to swear that if her son is sent again he will come back; standing conduct forbids promises; he said nothing; pending question). Hask reports to him at Fallstead what the boatmen found.
- Week 23, evening, the pool below the falls: MOMENT OF CONTACT, HELD. Thornmere comes down the east bank path openly toward the god (her decision 5). Board in releases/001/dm.md. Everything in the god's own thread after this moment is not resolved.

## The Riverfolk after the hold (resolved for the world; told to the player after the scene)
- Week 27: council of headmen at Oxmouth (civ year).
- Week 32: autumn run and two gales. Roll "weir holds": odds 45, roll 39, success. The weir holds through both gales and the run; the trap-pen takes about four tons of fish over the run, dried and salted; a tenth returned alive at the gate. Stores: add about 4 tons of dried fish to the Riverfolk's state.
- Winter: ordinary; the fourth sky-light at midwinter over the sea. Barley holds a full winter and a tenth over. Population 5,014.
- Year's end mood: warming to the Weir-King, still wary; Oxmouth and Wellbrook divided over the posts (First-Weir and Graveward); Aldford reserved, and Tam's house grieving; Fallstead relieved.

## Thornmere (NPC god; thread held from week 23)
Cause: her decisions-001.md. She walks west alone from the first days of spring, 1.6 times a mortal's day on rough hill ground, walking every notch and crossing (her goal one), hiding by day where the ground is open. Roll "crossing the Carn hills unseen": odds 50, roll 86, failed: Carn herders see a lone tall traveler walking at night in the fourth week of spring, going west; they do not stop her and she does not know she was seen. She reaches the hills above Wellbrook about week 5, crosses the brook in week 6, and spends the summer in the hills east of the valley, watching the Riverfolk and the god from a distance, walking the hill tracks and brook crossings by night. She learns from herders' talk overheard at the hill foot that they call their god the Weir-King, that he woke on the bar at the river's mouth and wets his hand at every staithe, and that he is walking upriver. Roll "herders notice her": odds 35, roll 94, not noticed. In week 19 the god sees her once on the crest (see above); she does not know it. In week 23 she comes down to the falls pool openly to speak (her decision 5). HELD at contact.

## Ashwake and the Kiln
Cause: his decisions-001.md. The great kiln built (civ year), fired once. Timber bought north with shoving and no blows. Watch fire kept; coal custom holds and becomes a market day. Two parties west: see messages m-0001 and m-0002 (both returned undelivered; Carn stopped party two; the clay-digger saw the notched poles). God-sense for Ashwake: the nearer pull drifted west through the spring until it sat almost in line with the farther one. Sky-lights seen from the burning hills: the midsummer and midwinter ones, in the west (the other two are below the horizon from there). Kiln population 5,015; grain thin; two factions (Hearth-keepers, Quarrelers).

## The Gatefolk
Cause: Thornmere's decision 6 (left to themselves). Ordinary year; tolls; two pairs of Kiln walkers passed west and back, paid in pots, told nothing; council told no one. Population 5,009. Two voices about the absent god. Sky-lights seen: midsummer and midwinter, in the west.

## The Carn (godless, Keeper-run)
Saw Thornmere pass in week 4 (did not stop her). Watched Kiln party one, stopped and turned back party two about day 27. Stopped and questioned the boatmen in week 15 to 16, learned of the river god and the Riverfolk, sent them back with a warning and the word that the Carn would come down to see the river. Trigger fired: the Carn will send a watcher down the river. Keeper fixes: the watcher is chosen at the Carn autumn gathering, sets out in the last weeks of winter with a notched staff, and reaches Fallstead in the first weeks of Year 2 (mystery Long 1, clue "watcher carries a notched staff, seen Year 2"). Their poles gain four notches this year.
