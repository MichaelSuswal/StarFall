# Year 1

## The frame
Season opens in early spring. Weeks: spring 1 to 13, summer 14 to 26, autumn 27 to 39, winter 40 to 52. No clocked event fires (first: the Ald flood, Year 2 late summer). Random event rolled: none (odds 10, roll 34). Sky-lights: 4, the true count from the sealed record, at the moments and visibilities fixed in world-events.md (week 1 over the sea, low; week 10 over the wood; week 20 midsummer over the eastern hills, high; week 46 midwinter over the sea, high). Weather: ordinary year as set in world-events.md; the Ald high for three weeks in early spring; dry summer; two autumn gales in the week of the fish run; ordinary winter; clear skies on the four light nights.

## The Weir-King and the Riverfolk (player's god; thread held from week 16)
Cause of every act: player/decisions-001.md, carried out under standing conduct (observe only, avoid strangers, retreat if seen, defend but do not kill). Decisions 1 (walk to the falls) and 4 (watch each night from the shingle bar) could not both be carried out; the Keeper followed 1 and read 4 as watching the sky each night from wherever he stood. This is said plainly in the DM's release for the DM to raise in (oog).

- Weeks 1 to 13: the god walks the Ald upriver from the shingle bar, touching the water at every village, about a week at each of the twelve, walking between at 1.6 times the foot rate: Wellbrook in week 4 to 5, Aldford in week 9 to 10, Fallstead in week 13. From week 13 he stays at the falls, his decided walk finished, learning the water (his last decided course). The first Riverfolk to reach him on the bar say nothing and leave a reed bundle; he takes it as meant.
- Week 1, fourth night: sky-light over the sea, seen from the bar. Week 10: sky-light over the wood to the north, seen from Aldford.
- Week 5, Wellbrook: the headman Hobb and fishers ask him for a law of giving at the weir. He observes and says nothing (conduct). Pending question for the player (DESIGN 12.9); the world did not wait: Maren of Oxmouth set the giving gate and the tenth herself (civ year). Keeper ruling r-001 (scoped to this dry run, sent to Michael for his yes): a petition from the god's own people answered in the moment by standing silence while the world goes on is a pending question, not a held encounter.
- Week 6: Thornmere crosses the Wellbrook brook a mile above the village at night. River-sense: NOT sensed. Ruling r-002: river-sense covers only running water the god has himself touched; a tributary is its own water; the god touched the Ald at the staithe, not the brook. God-sense (direction only, power card): one of the two pulls has changed bearing night by night through weeks 4 and 5, and from Wellbrook on it lies due east of him and stays east of him as he walks north, instead of falling behind to the south-east as a thing standing still would; the other pull is due east and does not change. He knows one of the two moves with him. Nothing more.
- Week 9, Aldford: Tam, a net-maker, comes to the god at the water at nightfall and begs him to save his wife Ede, in a hard labor. The god has no power that bears on it (power card: river-sense only; the Keeper's reading of the card, recorded in r-002's margin, not a ruling on a new case). Conduct: observe only; he stays in the river and says nothing. Tam goes back. Roll "Ede comes through alive": odds 45, roll 88, failed; she dies before dawn. Roll "the child lives": odds 45, roll 19, success; a daughter. New fixed facts: Tam, net-maker of Aldford, about 30; Ede, his wife, dead week 9 Year 1 in childbed, about 25; their daughter, unnamed. Aldford cools toward the god (civ year). Riverfolk counts: births 183, deaths 169, population 5,014.
- Weeks 11 to 13: a dugout built at Fallstead (30 worker-days). Week 14, about day 94, first light: Hask of Fallstead (38) and Brin of Aldford (22) carry it round the falls in the god's sight, pole a day above, hide it in a pool, and go east on foot into the North Wood, ten days out and ten back as decided. Trigger fired: boats passed the first falls.
- Week 15, dusk, from the pool below the falls: roll "the god catches sight of the hidden watcher": odds 50, roll 13, success. He sees a figure standing on the rock shoulder at the top of the falls on the east side, against the last light, upright and still, too far and too dark for a face, for about the time it takes to draw a net; when he looks again it is gone. The moving pull's bearing at that moment is north-east, toward the shoulder.
- Week 16: the weir at Oxmouth begun (civ year: weeks 16 to 19). River-sense: from the falls the god feels the Ald's flow at the mouth begin to change over several days as the first posts go in, as if the river were being narrowed and held.
- Week 16, dusk, the east bank a little below Fallstead, away from the houses, the fishers gone in: MOMENT OF CONTACT, HELD. Thornmere comes down from the wood's edge openly (her agent's answer, recorded in keeper-log.md: week 16, at the falls, a threshold; she has watched him settle and wants to speak before her envy grows). Board in releases/001/dm.md. Everything in the god's own thread after this moment is unresolved.

## The Riverfolk after the hold (resolved for the world; told to the player after the scene)
- Weeks 16 to 19: the weir finished as the civ designed (two rows of 60 oak posts, stone and brush, wicker face, trap-pen, giving gate, a tenth returned). Maren set the gate and the tenth as the code's answer.
- Week 17: the old posts under the Oxmouth mud struck by the mauls at low water (mystery Short 1, clue "posts at low water"; cause: the weir work the player ordered). Two rows, black, square-cut; the line moved eight yards down so none are touched. Maren says the god's first weir; Hobb says a drowned house and a grave. Nobody digs. Truth (sealed): the drowned salt-road village, 150 years under. Both are wrong.
- Week 17: the boatmen home unhurt (roll "return within time": odds 45, roll 42, success). Their walk: two and a half days east through the wood to the hills' edge; day 5 below the old earthworks on the crest (the King's Fort); day 6 the shore of Black Mere; day 7 stopped at a high steading above the mere (roll "Carn stop and question the boatmen": odds 70, roll 40, stopped). Herders with dogs took them to the elder Grandmother Ceth (new fixed fact: about 70, one eye clouded). The boatmen said first who they were and that a god had woken on the bar at the river's mouth; the Carn then asked about the river people, that god, and whether the river people count the lights. Tall poles notched top to bottom stand by the houses; the Carn said nothing of them. The Carn fed them two nights and sent them back with the words: the river people are to come no farther than the wood's edge; the Carn will come down to see the river themselves. Hask tells the village the day they come home; Brin tells Aldford in week 18. Whether and when the god hears it depends on where the scene leaves him (encounters.md 6: the god's thread is unresolved from week 16); the dependency is noted in the DM release part C, and the map reveals from the boatmen's telling wait on it.
- Week 20: midsummer sky-light over the eastern hills. Oxmouth links it to the posts three weeks before (civ year), a mortal error.
- Weeks 22 to 25: harvest, good. Week 27: council of headmen at Oxmouth (civ year), which also set a watch at the last ash grove above Fallstead because of the Carn's words.
- Week 32: autumn run, two gales. Roll "weir holds": odds 45, roll 39, success. About four tons of fish taken, a tenth returned alive. Stores: dried fish about 8 tons.
- Week 46: midwinter sky-light over the sea. Winter ordinary. Population 5,014 at year's end. Mood: warming and wary; Oxmouth and Wellbrook divided over the posts; Aldford cool; Fallstead relieved and uneasy.
- The god's own actions after week 16 depend on the scene and are unresolved.

## Thornmere (NPC god; thread held from week 16)
Cause: her decisions-001.md and her agent's answer on timing. She walks west alone from the first days of spring at 1.6 times a mortal's day on rough hill ground, walking every notch and crossing (eleven notches, nine fords; fixed), hiding by day where the ground is open. Roll "crossing the Carn hills unseen": odds 50, roll 86, failed: Carn herders see a lone tall traveler walking at night in week 4, going west. Roll "Carn stop and question her": odds 45, roll 51, not stopped. She does not know she was seen. She reaches the hills above Wellbrook at the start of week 5, crosses the brook in week 6 (unsensed, r-002), and shadows the god along the hills and the wood's edge as he walks north, about a week per village. Roll "herders notice her": odds 35, roll 94, not noticed. She learns the people's name for him and his doings from herders' talk overheard at the hill foot. She sees the week 10 light over the wood (the week 1 light was below the hills from the passes; her count this year before the hold is one). In week 15 the god sees her once on the shoulder above the falls; she does not know it. Week 16, dusk: she comes down openly to the bank below Fallstead. HELD at contact.

## Ashwake and the Kiln
Cause: his decisions-001.md. The great kiln built and fired once. Timber bought north with shoving and no blows (roll "reprisal": odds 35, roll 43, none). Watch fire kept; coal custom holds and becomes a market day. Two parties west: m-0001 and m-0002, both returned undelivered; party one watched by the Carn, not stopped (odds 70, roll 90); party two stopped at a Carn steading about day 27 (odds 70, roll 39), two pots given, notched poles seen, turned back with the words that no stranger goes down to the river without the Carn's leave. Hard-country carriage rolls both failed (odds 40, rolls 56 and 100): party one home about day 75 (late spring) with a sprained ankle; party two home about day 80 (late spring), the young burner's arm broken. The factor "dry summer +5" in those two rolls was mistaken (the return leg fell in late spring, which the weather calls fair); the results stand (encounters.md 3) and the error is logged. God-sense for Ashwake: one of the two western pulls changed bearing through the spring until both lay west and a little south. Sky-lights seen from the burning hills: week 20 and week 46 (the others too low or hidden). Kiln population 5,015; grain thin; two factions.

## The Gatefolk
Cause: Thornmere's decision 6. Ordinary year; tolls; two pairs of Kiln walkers passed west about day 20 and back in summer (one limping, one splinted), paid in pots, told nothing; council told no one. Population 5,009. Sky-lights seen: weeks 20 and 46.

## The Carn (godless, Keeper-run)
Saw the night-walker in week 4 (did not stop her). Watched Kiln party one; stopped and turned back party two about day 27. Stopped and questioned the boatmen on day 7 of their walk, learned of the river god from the boatmen's own words, sent them back with a warning. By autumn, word through traders that the pass people's new god had walked west in spring (roll odds 55, roll 27, yes); the Carn connect it with the night-walker. Trigger fired: the Carn send a watcher down the river. Fixed: chosen at their autumn gathering, sets out in the last weeks of winter with a notched staff, reaches Fallstead in the first weeks of Year 2 (mystery Long 1, clue "watcher carries a notched staff, seen Year 2"). Their poles gain four notches this year.
