r-001 | 2026-11-18 | Year 1 | Whether a petition from the god's own people, made in person in unplayed time, is a held encounter (encounters.md 6, "a pending question reaching the god in person") or a pending question (DESIGN 12.9). Cases: Wellbrook week 5 (a law of giving at the weir), Aldford week 9 (a plea to save a wife in labor), Fallstead week 22 (a mother asks an oath). | For holding: encounters.md lists a pending question reaching the god in person as major. Against: DESIGN 12.9 says open questions to the god are tracked as pending items, put to the player during the day, and if the world cannot wait the other side meets the god's silence; holding the year at every petition would stop play at week 5 of every year a god walks among its people; the god's standing conduct (observe only) already answers the moment. | Ruled: a new question put to the god by its own people in unplayed time, which standing conduct answers in the moment by silence and inaction, is a pending question for the DM to put to the player, not a held encounter. The world goes on as it would. A question that is already pending when its asker reaches the god in person, a stranger, another god, a threat, or a chance to use a power the god has, is still held. | Binds: this dry run, Year 1 only. The checker (flags.md 1) found that settling encounters.md 6 against DESIGN 12.9 for every campaign is a rule change needing Michael's yes and a Change List line. The Keeper keeps the ruling for this turn so the year can close and puts it to Michael in the morning notice as a proposal. If declined, the year is reworked with the Wellbrook ask of week 5 as the first held encounter.
r-002 | 2026-11-18 | Year 1 | Whether river-sense ("knows the state of any running water it has touched") covers a tributary the god has not touched: Thornmere crossed the Wellbrook brook a mile above the village in week 6; the god had touched the Ald at the Wellbrook staithe, not the brook. | For: the brook's water becomes Ald water at the mouth, and the player's intent was to know his river. Against: the card says "water it has touched"; a brook is its own running water until it joins; the player chose the words "touching the water at every village" and did not say every brook. | Ruled: river-sense covers only running water the god has himself touched. A tributary is its own water until he touches it; the stretch of the Ald below the brook's mouth is covered, the brook is not. Crossing of the brook not sensed. | Binds: the Weir-King's river-sense; precedent for the power card (the DM adds it with the morning). Margin: the Keeper also read the card as giving no power that bears on a childbirth (Aldford, week 9); that is a reading of the card, not a ruling on a new case.
