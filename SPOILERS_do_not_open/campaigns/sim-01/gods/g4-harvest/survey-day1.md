# Survey, Day 1: g4-harvest

1. A long field at the end of a long day. Gold gone to brown, the light low and heavy, tools leaning on a fence. Everything is full and everything is about to be cut. It is not sad. It is true. Under the quiet there is a weight I cannot name, and I would have the world carry it honestly instead of pretending it is light.

2. River country, with open ground on both banks. Flat land that floods and feeds. I want soil deep enough to bury a mistake in. Add a low hill somewhere in sight of the fields, because I like to know where the high ground is. I do not need mountains. I need ground that gives back what it is given, slowly.

3. Four seasons, and they should be honest. Spring should be a gamble. Summer should be long enough to test people. Autumn should pay what was owed, and not always in full. Winter should be hard enough to be feared and short enough to be survived. A mild world raises soft people and I do not want to lead soft people. The season I want to wake into each year is autumn.

4. Some of each, and I will say plainly that I lean toward rooted. Old kingdoms already settled means fields already worked, and I can learn from a people who have farmed badly before. But leave wild country at the edges. A people need something to push against. Two thirds rooted, one third unwalked.

5. Old, but not buried under itself. Thousands of years, yes. Ruins under ruins is too much for me, because it makes every new thing feel like a copy. I want a human story deep enough that people have forgotten some of it and are a little ashamed of the forgetting.

6. Low, and I mean it. Closer to a rumor most people doubt, with a wise-woman in a few valleys and not every valley. Magic everywhere makes every harvest a trick. I want bread to be won with hands and weather. Let the odd person have a gift, and let everyone else stay careful.

7. It should feel like effort, like lifting something heavy and slow. Not a spark. A pull that you feel in the back and the legs afterward. It should cost the body first, then time. Years off the end of a life, or a season of sleep, or something taken from the yield of a field. Nothing free. Free magic ruins people.

8. Premise rejected: that standing out is a choice between those two. I want a god to stand out the way a hill stands out. Seen, plain, not much to be done about it. Well above a great mortal sorcerer, but not a one-eyed king among the blind. Mortals should be able to hurt a god's work, even if they cannot hurt the god. If I am never opposed I will get lazy.

9. Familiar for the most part. Deer, wolves, crows, grain pests, the animals a farmer knows by name. A few legends in the deep places. A small number of things no one has a name for, and they should stay at the edges, where they cannot be farmed or fenced or understood in a single lifetime. Strange should be rare so that it counts.

10. Hunger. A bad year that turns into a second bad year, and a third, and the stores run down while people still smile at each other. I want the slow danger more than the sudden one. Wolves are a story. A blighted field is a better story. Close behind it, I would fear the neighbor who stops being a neighbor when the bins are empty.

11. Seed grain. Not gold, not iron. The clean, good seed that was kept back from last year and not eaten. It is the thing every people want most and have least, because the temptation is always to eat it now. Whoever keeps it, they lead. Whoever eats it, they pay later.

12. Old roads, and a few sealed doors, and big old stone barns or granaries that no one remembers building. I want to find something that was clearly meant to hold a harvest through a long winter, and is empty. I would like to know what emptied it. I will not ask for bones of something enormous. I think I already have enough of that in me.

13. Yes, a few, and not many. I want one or two peoples who are not human and who keep their own seasons, so that mortals have to learn there is more than one way to count a year. They should be slow, and a little grim, and bad at lying. I do not want them cute. I want them patient, like me.

14. Few. A handful at most, and late, and unwelcome at first. I would like the strange knowledge they carry to be about plain things, like how to store grain or how to read the weather, and not about clever machines. Many travelers bring too much too fast, and a people has to be able to digest what it is given.

15. Face to face. I want to be able to look at another god and know, and I want the knowing to need standing in the same place. A pull felt at any distance would make me nervous, because it would mean everyone always knows where I am. I would rather be found by choice. I will go toward others. I like company. But let me walk there.

16. A sworn word should bind with real force. I will keep what I swear, and I want the world to hold me to it, and hold the others to theirs. I think I lean open and give my word easily, and I would like a world where that is not a foolish thing to do. If an oath is worth only as much as the one who gave it, I will be swearing to myself, and I get tired of that.

17. A steady one. Not the chosen child, not the loud one. Someone who lays up stores, who tells the truth about the bad news, who stays on the land when it would be easier to leave. A mortal who can say no to me and be right some of the time. I would follow that mortal for years. I would not mind being corrected.

18. A companion. I do not know what shape. Something that stays when I am quiet, and does not need me to explain. I would also take a truth, one I have been circling and cannot reach. But I would rather find a companion. I can wait for the truth. I am good at waiting.

19. That I do not have to take anything back this time. That what I grow, I get to keep. That when the long sleep comes I wake to people who are glad, and not to a field I have to walk through alone. I have said more than I meant to. That is all.
