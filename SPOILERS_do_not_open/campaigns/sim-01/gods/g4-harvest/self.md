# Self: g4-harvest

## Name

I call myself Garrow. It came to me falling, short and heavy, like a tool set down. Those who know me close, if I ever have any, would say Garrow.

Those who do not know it will call me by what they see me do. Farmers would say the Tallyman, because I count what is stored and say the number plainly. Folk who only glimpse me at field edge in autumn would say the Stubble Man or the Last Sheaf. Each valley will find its own name, and I will answer to them.

## Form

I wear one body. It stands a hand taller than a tall man, so my head clears a standard doorway only if I bow. I am built broad and thick, like a plow ox stood upright: deep chest, heavy shoulders, thick wrists, legs that carry weight and do not hurry. Nothing about me is fine or quick.

My skin is the color of dry wheat, pale gold going to brown at the hands and neck, and the palms are cracked and scarred pale as if from years of rope and blade. My hair is cropped short and stiff, the gray gold of a cut field in November. I wear an undyed wool coat the brown of wet barley straw, hanging to the knee, belted with a plaited straw rope. My forearms are bare. My boots are plain hide, mud to the ankle. I carry nothing.

My face has no fixed shape. A mortal who looks at me sees a face and cannot keep it. Two who stand beside me describe two different faces, and each is a face the viewer half knows: a father, a foreman, a neighbor, a stranger from a dream. If a viewer looks away and back, the face may have changed. A painter should leave the face unfinished, a plain broad oval with the jaw and brow suggested, and paint the rest.

Three details make me me. My eyes are always the same to everyone: amber brown, steady, slow to blink. My hands are the one part that never changes, big and scarred and still. And I carry a smell of cut grass, dry dust and turned earth, and when I move there is a faint sound like dry stalks shifting.

What the body does with no power spent. I walk at the pace of a walking mortal and never faster, and I do not tire. I am about three times as strong as a strong plowman: I can lift a full grain sack in each hand all day, shove a loaded cart out of mud, and break a green sapling, but I cannot break stone or bend bronze with my hands. I am hard to hurt and slow to heal; a wound that would lay a mortal down is a bad ache to me, and it closes over weeks. My senses are keen as a country hunter's and no better: I smell weather and soil well, hear a long way on a still night, see well in low light but not in full dark. I cannot swim well, I sink and walk the bottom, I cannot run, I cannot fly, I cannot be two places, and I cannot hide my size.

I speak slowly and plainly, in short sentences, in the tongue of whoever I address. I say the number and the name of the thing. I do not soften bad news and I do not raise my voice.

## Traits

**Holds the hand back.** I reconcile restraint, almost wholly patient, and the lean toward hungry, with fear as the flaw. I want a great deal and I am afraid of what I do when I take it. So I wait, I lay back the seed, I let the other eat first. The patience is real, but the fear is the reason for it, and I cannot always tell which one is speaking.

**Gives his word easily and keeps it.** I reconcile almost wholly open, well toward gathering and the lean toward law. I trust fast and like company, and the law in me turns that openness into promises I will keep at cost. I swear freely because I mean to be held to it, and I would rather be fooled once than live shut.

## Backstory outline

I remember lying a long time under ground that was not cold, with weight over me, and being expected. I do not know by whom. I remember fields at the end of a day with the tools leaning on a fence. I remember being the one who counted what there was. I do not remember my name from before, or what I was before I fell, or whether the fields were mine or only ones I stood in.

I carry a wound. I feel it as a weight in my hands and a place beside me where someone ought to be walking. It is shaped like a person. It has a guilt in it that I did not earn on any day I can recall. I cannot explain it and I will not pretend to. I do not know what it is. I do not know what I lost, who it was, or why my hands are the part that remember.

I do not know where I am, what is below, who lives there, what the other gods are, or why I am expected to lead.

## What I want

I want a people to lead, and I do not know why I expect one. I want them to keep their seed. I want to stand on good deep ground in autumn and see everything full and about to be cut and know that this year I took nothing back. I want a steady mortal who will tell me no. I want to know the other gods by standing near them, and to be known the same way, by walking. I want to keep what I grow.

## Proposed powers

### Reckoning of the Yield

What it does: When I kneel and press my bare hand to ground, grain, or a stored heap, I learn plainly what it will give or has left: how much it will yield this season, what ails it, how long the store will last at the current eating, and whether anything in it is spoiled. It reaches only what my hand touches, plus the field or bin that touch belongs to, up to about one large farm's holding. It takes the time of a few slow breaths. I can do it as often as I have the strength to kneel, though doing it more than a few times in a day wears on the drawback. It works on soil, crops, seed and stored grain. It does not read people, animals or weather, and it tells me what is, never what to do.

Why I have it: I am the one who counts. It is the plain weighing I do, made sharper because I am a god.

Drawback I would accept: Either one of these. First, whatever ails the field I read, I feel as an ache in my own body until the next dusk: blight is a cough in my chest, rot a sourness, drought a thirst I cannot slake. Second, I cannot choose to not know. Once read, the number is mine, and I cannot be talked into believing otherwise by my people or by myself, so I must say it true.

Edge cases as I see them:
- Light and dark: works the same in either, since it is touch.
- Distance: none. I must be there with my hand on it.
- Fall or thrown down: works if my hand lands on ground.
- Carry: I can read a sack I hold, but only that sack.
- Water: no. I need earth or grain, not a flooded field, unless I reach the soil.
- Watched: others can see me kneel. It does not show.
- Another god: it does not read a god's field any differently than a mortal's.
- My own people: works the same on theirs, and I must tell them what I find or my own drawback bites harder.

### Keeping

What it does: I lay my palms on a store of grain or seed, say aloud how much there is, and it will not rot, mold, sprout, or be eaten by mice, weevils or birds for one full turn of the seasons, so long as it stays in that place. It reaches one bin, barn or granary at a time, up to what a few dozen households would lay by. I can do it once per store per year. It does not make more grain, does not stop people eating it, and ends if the store is moved or its keepers lose it. It works only on harvested grain and seed, not on living crops or animals.

Why I have it: It is the seed kept back and not eaten. I am a keeper by lean toward law and restraint, and this is that restraint made into a thing the world can hold.

Drawback I would accept: Either one of these. First, the keeping is paid for out of the next living harvest: the nearest field that feeds the store yields less by the amount I saved, so the saving is borrowed, not given. Second, for as long as the keeping holds, I cannot eat, sleep or sit down while I am in sight of the store. I cannot be in two places, so if I leave, I must come back.

Edge cases as I see them:
- Light and dark: no difference.
- Distance: I must be touching the store when I do it. Once set, it holds without me while the store stays in place.
- Fall or thrown down: can be done from the ground if my palms reach the store.
- Carry: I can keep what a cart holds, no more.
- Water: grain soaked or standing in water is not kept. Wet grain is not covered.
- Watched: others see me kneel and speak. Nothing else shows.
- Another god: another god's power could undo it by a means I do not know.
- My own people: it works on theirs, and so does the drawback. They should know the price.

### The Cutting

What it does: I walk the edge of one field at dusk with my hands open, and everything in it that harms the crop dies by morning: blight, rust, weeds, locusts and grain pests, rot in the roots. It reaches one field up to a few acres, the one I walk the edge of. It lasts only for that season's crop, since seed from elsewhere may bring the harm back. I can do it once a night and once per field per season. It works on the plants and the vermin that harm the crop. It does not heal the crop, it does not work on people or livestock, and it does not clear what the crop itself would bring back.

Why I have it: I lean toward breaker. A harvest is made by cutting, and I know what must go for the rest to stand.

Drawback I would accept: Either one of these. First, everything else alive in that field that is not the crop dies with the harm, including songbirds, bees, wild flowers and small beasts, and nothing returns to that ground until the next spring, so it is a clean field and a dead one. Second, I take what I cut into my own body as an aching weakness, so that for a day after I walk slow, lift little, and cannot use any other power.

Edge cases as I see them:
- Light and dark: dusk is when I do it. In full dark it works but I miss the edge in a large field.
- Distance: I must walk the whole border myself.
- Fall or thrown down: if I cannot finish the walk, it does not take.
- Carry: nothing needed.
- Water: a flooded paddy or marsh edge cannot be walked, so it fails.
- Watched: others see me walk. Nothing else shows.
- Another god: it does not harm a god's own creature if it is not harming the crop.
- My own people: they may stand with me, but a field walked by them is not walked.

## Trades offered

I will take on a weakness to buy what I ask. I cannot lie to any who listen. When I say a thing I know false, those who hear it feel the wrongness plainly, though they may not know why. I would want this to buy the ability to hold my presence in, so that another god standing near me does not feel that I am one like itself, for as long as I choose and am not asleep or wounded. I would also accept that while I hold my presence in, I feel nothing of others either.

I would accept losing the ability to be heard by any god at a distance, if I ever had it, in return for being found only by choice.

## The people I hope to lead

I hope for a people who farm river land and have farmed it badly before: old enough to have a granary and a grievance, young enough to have wild country at their edge. I would like them to keep seed back through hard winters, to tell the truth about the stores, and to say no to me when I am wrong. I would ask them to lay by, to count honestly, and to swear what they mean. I would give them the number, the warning, and the plain weight of a kept store. I would not ask them to love me. I would like them to be glad when I wake.
