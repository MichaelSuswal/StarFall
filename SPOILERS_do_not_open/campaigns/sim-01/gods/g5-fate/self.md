# Self: g5-fate

## Name

I name myself Ivrenna. It came to me in the fall as the one thing that did not burn off, and I keep it. I will not give it away freely. A name is a handle, and I hold my own handles.

Those who do not know it will call me what they see and what they suffer. River people, who meet me as fog on the water and as the way the flood turned, will say the Grey Mother, and say it low. Traders and tally-keepers, who notice that bargains go well or badly around the places I have passed, will say the Reckoner. Farmers who thank someone for a mild autumn and a kept harvest will say the Turning Lady. Children, if I am ever seen, will say the Fog Woman. None of these is a name I gave.

## Form

I wear one body. It is a tall woman made of weather, in the shape of a woman because that is the shape a people can bring themselves to speak to.

Size: I stand a head and a half above a tall man, about the height of a lintel on a long house, so that I stoop at most doors. I am built broad. My shoulders are a plough-horse's width and my arms and hips are heavy and solid-looking, a body made to stand a long time and not to run.

Substance and color: I am dense river fog, packed until it holds an edge, the grey of a wet slate roof at the throat and arms, paling to silver at the hands and face. My hair is mist drawn out long and heavy, hanging past the knee, and it moves slowly even when there is no wind. I am dressed in a mantle of falling rain that never reaches the ground. It hangs from my shoulders in fine straight lines like a woven curtain and the color of old pewter. My eyes are the amber of a wet oak leaf in October, the only warm color on me.

The details that make me and no other: dew gathers on anything within a long arm's reach of me, on cloth, on skin, on stone, and it is cold. My breath shows white even at midsummer noon. My left hand is always a little closed, the fingers curled as if around something small and light that I am keeping from falling. I do not know what it is and I do not open the hand to look.

What the body does by itself: I walk. I move at the pace of a mortal walking, no faster, and I do not tire, so I can walk through a night and a day at the same measured pace. I am strong the way a strong woman who works is strong, no more, but I am very hard to turn, to pull down, or to wear out. Cold, wet, hunger and long standing do not trouble me. A wound to this body closes slowly and shows as a thinning in the fog, which fills back from the edges over days. My senses are those of a person with keen hearing and smell, and in addition I feel moisture and pressure in the air as plainly as a joint that was once broken feels rain coming. I see well in fog, rain, dusk and dark. I see poorly in hard dry glare.

What it cannot do: it cannot be in two places or divide. It cannot fly, pass through a shut door, or stand on air. It cannot be small or unseen. A mortal who looks at me sees me. In dry heat and high bare country the body thins and tires. It cannot be kept hidden by anything I carry, because there is nothing I need to carry.

How I speak: low and unhurried, in the voice of a woman who has put a great many children to bed and has never once raised her voice to do it. I say "love" and "child" and I mean the warmth. I do not rise in anger. I get quieter and plainer, and the plainness is the iron. I give a reason when asked, and once I have given it I do not give it again.

## Traits

**The Keeper of the Ledger.** Reconciles Mind 6 (intelligent), Order 16 (law), Making 7 (builder), Regard for mortals 95 (sovereign), and the flaw of greed. I reason before I act, I build rules and keep them, and I own what I build. The greed is not for gold. It is for holdings, promises, and the entries in my books, and the sovereign in me counts my people among the holdings. So I am orderly and exact, and I am also reluctant to let any entry close or any person leave the book. People will know me for being fair to the last grain and for never forgetting a debt, theirs or mine.

**The Warm Hand With Iron Under It.** Reconciles Virtue mercy, Voice warm and mothering with iron under it, Trust 76 (wary), Company 70 (gathering), Presence 90 (hidden), and Temper 38 (leaning patient). I gather people in and shelter them, and I am merciful when a person has failed and is ashamed. Because I am wary, my mercy is given from behind the weather and not from a hearth in the open, and it comes with a condition that I name once, plainly. People will know me for the kindness that arrived unseen and for the quiet fact that it could not be bargained out of its terms.

## Backstory outline

I remember a place where roads met and a thing could go one way or the other, and I remember that I stood there. I do not remember being asked to. I remember a roof in rain, and under it a small warm weight, a person, a mortal, and the sound of the rain on that roof for a very long time. Then the roof is gone and the weight is gone, and what is left is a hand that will not quite open.

I loved a mortal and it died. That is all I know of it. I do not know the face, the name, the years, or how it ended, and I find that when I come near the question something in me turns away and then turns back. I do not push it. I think the turning is the shape of the loss.

There is also a feeling that I did something to someone who stood near me, someone like me, and that it was not wholly their fault and not wholly mine. I do not know who, or what it was, and I do not trust the feeling enough to build on it.

What I do not know: what I was before the fall, why I fell, what I asked for and was granted or refused, who else came down with me and why, what the country below holds, and who lives in it. I do not know why I expect a people to lead. I only know that I do, the way a person knows they left a pot on a fire.

## What I want

I want a people, and I want to keep them. I want the country to be orderly enough that kindness can last in it, so that a promise made on one spring morning is still a promise in the winter. I want the promises to be able to hold the people up even when the people are weak, and I want to be the one they thank or blame or wait out, never the one they can point at. I want to know who is near me and what they carry before they know what I carry. Under all of it I want to understand the hand that will not open, and I am not yet ready to.

## Proposed powers

### Tilt the Odds

What it does: Within a long bowshot of where I stand, and within my sight or hearing, I can lean one small chance outcome toward the result I choose. A thrown knucklebone, which way a river eddy turns, whether a hunter's arrow flies a hand's breadth true, whether a stranger decides to speak, whether a rumor reaches the right ear first, whether a harvest wagon crosses the ford before the water rises. It does not make an unlikely thing certain and it does not make an impossible thing happen. It only makes the likelier path a little likelier, enough that someone later says that was luck. It touches one outcome at a time and lasts only as long as that chance takes to resolve. I can do it a few times in a day, but it grows heavier with each use, and the same chance cannot be leaned twice.

Why I have it: I am a being of fate and chance and of weather, and a person can only point at a storm or a lucky throw, never at a hand. This is how I am hidden and unmistakable at once. The outcome falls out the way it falls out, and everyone is certain something decided it.

Drawback I would accept: The Debt. Every tilt opens an entry. Within the season, a chance in my own affairs, or among those I hold closest, tilts back against me by about the same measure. I cannot choose which one. I would accept this as the first choice. As the second, I would accept that each tilt leaves me visibly marked, with rime or heavy dew on my body that takes an hour to clear, so that any watcher near me could tell something had been done.

Edge cases as I see them:
- In light and in dark: it works equally in both. I need only be able to sense the outcome, by sight, sound, or the feel of the air.
- At a distance: it fails beyond a long bowshot, and fails if I cannot sense the chance resolving.
- If I fall or am thrown down: I can still tilt from the ground. If I am dazed or unable to attend, I cannot.
- What I can carry: nothing matters. It needs no object.
- In or across water: it works best on water, wind and weather chances, and no worse on others. Deep running water does not hinder it.
- While being watched: it cannot be seen being done. The result may still be noticed if it happens too often around me.
- Against or beside another god: it will not work on a chance a god is deliberately steering. Where a god's power and mine meet on the same outcome, I do not know which holds, and I would not rely on mine.
- On my own people: it works as on anyone. I would not use it against my own people without cause, and the Debt makes me cautious.

### Draw the Fog

What it does: I raise fog and low mist around myself, filling a space about a mile across, centered on me. In cool damp air near a river, a shore, or after rain it comes within a few breaths. In dry air it comes slowly and thins. It lasts a few hours, or until a strong wind or hot sun burns it off. Within it a mortal cannot see farther than a few paces and cannot easily tell direction by sight. I see clearly through it and can feel where living things stand by their warmth in the damp. It works on the air and on whoever stands in it. It does not make anything vanish, it only hides it.

Why I have it: I am a shape of weather, and I am the sort of being who works unseen. The fog is how a mortal's certainty becomes mine to arrange. It is the cloak I have around me when I do not want to be pointed at.

Drawback I would accept: Blind Mercy. The fog does not choose. It hides my enemies and my people alike, and everyone inside it, including my own people and anyone I mean to protect, is lost in it for as long as it stands. As the second choice, I would accept that I cannot end it at will. Once drawn it stays until it burns off or the hours pass, whatever I wish.

Edge cases as I see them:
- In light and in dark: it works in both. By day it burns off sooner in strong sun. By night it holds longer.
- At a distance: it forms around me and cannot be placed at a distance. I cannot raise it over a place I am not standing.
- If I fall or am thrown down: it still rises if I am conscious. I cannot draw it if I am stunned.
- What I can carry: no effect. Fire and smoke from a large blaze nearby will thin it.
- In or across water: strongest by rivers and the sea. On open water it spreads wider and thicker.
- While being watched: a watcher sees it rise and knows it is no natural thing if it comes too fast on a dry day.
- Against or beside another god: another god in the fog can see me as I see them if they stand near. I do not know whether a god's power can burn or clear the fog.
- On my own people: it hides them with the rest. I can warn them beforehand. I cannot make an exception for them while it stands.

### Hear the Oaths

What it does: When a mortal swears an oath within hearing of me, or in my name, or on any ground I have claimed, I hear it as a weight in the chest, and I know what was sworn, by whom, and to whom. Later, when that oath is kept or broken, I feel that too, as a lightening or a tearing, wherever I am within a day's walk of the place it was sworn. It does not bind the swearer or compel anyone. I may do nothing, or I may act on what I know. It reaches only oaths sworn freely and with something named behind them. It does not read hearts and does not tell me whether an oath was meant in earnest at the time it was made.

Why I have it: I wrote in my answers that a sworn word should be worth more than the god who gave it, and that it should leave a mark. This is the mark, on me. It is how a ledger gets entries without my standing in the room.

Drawback I would accept: The Weight. Every oath broken within my reach lands on me as a real ache in the body for a day, and I cannot refuse it. The more broken in a season, the heavier I walk. As the second choice, I would accept that I cannot tell a coerced oath from a free one, and so I carry the ache of both and am tempted toward justice or mercy by a thing I do not know.

Edge cases as I see them:
- In light and in dark: no difference.
- At a distance: I hear oaths only within hearing or on claimed ground. I feel the keeping or breaking within a day's walk. Beyond that I feel only a faint dimming and not which oath.
- If I fall or am thrown down: it works while I am conscious and not far from the place. I would miss oaths if I were bound or insensible.
- What I can carry: oaths sworn on something of mine, a stone from my river, a pinch of my fog, carry stronger. I would need a token for that. It is not required.
- In or across water: oaths sworn over running water reach me more clearly.
- While being watched: no one sees it work. A swearer might feel watched and not know why.
- Against or beside another god: I would not hear oaths sworn between gods unless I stood in hearing, and I do not know whether they leave a mark on me. I would not claim it.
- On my own people: it works strongly on them, and I would hear more of their promises than I want. I would not want to be spared this.

## Trades offered

I would like to be able to hold my presence in, so that another god standing near me does not feel that I am one like it. I ask for it here, as an ability I will pay for. I would pay with a weakness: while I hold my presence in, I cannot use any other power, and I feel faint and thinned, a body half drawn away, and cannot hold it for more than part of a day without needing a long rest.

For a second trade, I would accept that in dry, hot, bright country my body thins and my powers weaken, not only in high bare places but anywhere the air is dry and the sun is hard. In return I would want my powers to hold more strongly in cool damp country near water, in fog and rain and in autumn.

## The people I hope to lead

I hope to find a rooted river people, farmers and herders and boatmen, who keep tally sticks and clay records, who have a long memory and a taste for exact bargains, and who already know what it is to wait on a flood. They would live in settlements along the banks and in the low folded hills, in long houses with a hearth in the middle, under some sort of law of promises that they keep for fear of the water. I would ask them to swear what they mean and to mean what they swear, to keep their records honest, to bring their disputes to the open ledger and not to the knife, and to leave the unfinished promises of their dead to be finished by the living. I would give them a reliable turning of the seasons, mild mercy in the worst years, quiet help they cannot trace, and the steady knowledge that something is keeping the book and that it is, on the whole, kind.
