# Survey, Day 1: g5-fate

1. A hearth in a long house with the weather turning outside. Warm inside, and everyone in it knowing, without being told, whose roof it is. Under the warmth I want a floor that does not give. I want a world that is kind, and orderly enough that the kindness lasts.

2. River country, and the low hills that fold around it. Land that floods and recedes and floods again, where people learn to keep records and keep promises because the water keeps its own. I would take a coast of fog as a second love. I do not want the high bare places. Nothing there needs me.

3. Four seasons, and I want them honest. A winter that is hard enough to teach thrift and a summer that is generous enough to forgive it. I want the turning to be reliable, so that mortals can plan, and I want it to fail them just rarely enough that they remember who to thank. When each new year finds me, I want to wake into autumn.

4. Some of each, though I lean toward the rooted. I would like neighbors who have kept their own books and their own borders, so that there is something to treat with and something to measure myself against. Wild country beyond them, yes, but tamed in time. Empty land is only an unwritten ledger, and I like a ledger with entries.

5. Old. Thousands of years, and I do not mind ruins under ruins. A people with long memory can be taught to value continuity, and a people with no memory will believe whatever the last loud voice told them. I want the dead to have left a great many promises behind, unfinished.

6. Present, and rationed. Somewhere past the rumor, toward the wise woman, but not in every valley. I want it to be a thing a community knows who to ask for, and knows what the asking will cost. Magic spread too thin becomes a nuisance, and magic spread too thick becomes a rival to me.

7. It should feel like a debt being entered into. A weight in the chest when it is used, a sense of a ledger somewhere being opened. The cost should be real and should fall on the one who spends it, in years or memory or the small comforts, and never on a stranger. I do not want it free, and I do not want it cruel. I want it fair, and I want mortals to learn that fairness is a thing you pay for.

8. High. A god should be unmistakable, a different order of thing, and I will not apologize for that. Mortals can be great and I will honor greatness, but the gap should be one they can see from a long way off, and one they are glad of. The one-eyed man in the kingdom of the blind is nearer to it than a great sorcerer.

9. A few legends, a few true strangenesses, and most of it familiar. I want the world legible enough to build on. Let the odd things be rare and let them be memorable, so that when one appears it means something, and someone writes it down.

10. Hunger that comes slowly. Famine, and the long bad years where a good harvest is a rumor. I also like the danger that comes from a bargain gone wrong, the debt that outlives the one who made it. A story needs a threat that mortals could have prevented and did not, because that is the kind that teaches.

11. Surety. The thing mortals want most and have least is the certainty that tomorrow will be like today. Grain, salt and iron will do for trade, but under all of it, the want is for a promise that holds. Let them trade for it, bind each other for it, and be disappointed in it often enough to value it.

12. Old roads, and sealed doors. A road that someone built with care tells you that someone was here who thought ahead. A door that was sealed on purpose tells you someone was afraid, or someone was keeping a promise. I would like to stand in front of one and not yet know which.

13. Some, yes, and few. Peoples who keep to their own places and their own customs, who are neither servants nor enemies, and who have long memories of their own. I would want them to be hard to bargain with and honest once bargained with. I do not want a world with nobody else in it who can tell me no.

14. Few. One or two, arriving once in a generation, strange enough to be unsettling and not so strange that they cannot be understood. I would want to hear what they carry and weigh it before my people do. Anything that comes from far away should come through a door that I am watching.

15. A knowing at a distance, faint, and a clearer knowing face to face. I would like to feel the others the way you feel weather coming in a joint that was once broken, as a pressure with a direction. I do not want to be surprised in the dark. I want to know where the rest of you stand, even if I cannot see you.

16. Premise rejected: I reject the idea that a sworn word could be worth only as much as the god who gave it. I want the word to be worth more than the giver, so that a poor god can still be trusted for what it swore. It should bind with real force. A sworn word should leave a mark on the one who swears it, and breaking it should cost. I would write that law myself if I could. Without it every treaty is only a courtesy, and I do not trust courtesies.

17. One who keeps faith. Someone ordinary and stubborn who is offered an easy betrayal and refuses it, and is not rewarded, and does it anyway. I would like to watch someone like that grow old. I will not say more than that.

18. A companion. Not a servant, not a worshiper, but one who stays. I do not know what form it would take, and I find I do not want to guess. Something in me turns away from the question and then turns back to it.
