# Survey, Day 1: g3-song

1. A long table in a low house, the door left open, and many voices at it, and under the floor something old humming a note that no one at the table can name. A full pot and a deep cellar. Warm on top and dark underneath. As the old ones say, the hearth is only as good as the dark it keeps back.

2. River country, and where the river gives out, marsh and fogbound coast. Low ground where mist sits in the mornings and the water remembers every road it ever took. A people can gather on a river. A hill only scatters them. Give me wet ground and slow water.

3. Let the seasons be long and honest, each one keeping its full measure and none hurrying the next. A hard winter, so that people must sit close and sing. A late, generous harvest, so that there is something to be thankful for and something to mourn. Spring is for the young and summer is for the loud, and I have no quarrel with either. Fall is when the grain is counted and the dead are named. I want to wake into autumn.

4. Some of each, and I stand nearer the old kingdoms than the empty wild. Wild country is only a place where no one has yet been sung. But a rooted kingdom has songs already, and I would rather listen first. As they say, you do not ask the oldest tree for directions, you sit under it. Let there be a little unwalked country at the edge, enough to be afraid of, not enough to be lost in.

5. Old. Ruins under ruins, and under those, a floor worn smooth by feet no one remembers. I stand far toward the deep end of the range. A people with a short memory is a people with a short life. Let the oldest story be older than any mortal tongue can carry whole, so that every telling is partly a guess and partly a grief.

6. Some, and quiet. Closer to a wise woman in many valleys than to a rumor, but not so common that no one is afraid of it. A thing everyone has is a tool, and a thing a few have is a mystery, and I would have a mystery. Let most people doubt it until the day they need it.

7. It should feel like singing. A held breath, then a note, and the world leaning a little toward it. The cost is memory. Each working takes something from the one who works: a face, a name, a morning. The bigger the working, the more is taken. Nothing is free, and the ledger is kept in the one place the worker cannot read it afterward.

8. Not like the one-eyed man in the kingdom of the blind. That is a lonely thing to be, and a dangerous one. I stand nearer the great sorcerer end, but not all the way there. A god is a deep well, not a tall tower. You may walk past a well a hundred times and never be told how far down it goes. I would be seen only when it is needed, and then I would be believed.

9. Strange, and I stand well toward the strange end. Mostly familiar animals, so the people have something to lean on, and then the wrong thing in the fog, with no name, that the old women sing a warning about. Let there be a creature or two that is older than the people and has no opinion of them at all.

10. Forgetting. The slow kind, where a village loses its songs one winter at a time and wakes up not knowing it was ever different. Hunger, too, but the kind that is patient. Mortals should fear what takes its time. A good story is a thing that comes up the road at walking pace, and everyone sees it coming, and no one can stop it.

11. Those who remember. The old singers, the ones who hold a whole lineage in their heads. Let them be few and worn out, and let people trade for them, fight for them, and bury them badly. Paper and stone are cheap. A living memory is not.

12. All of it, if the land will allow. Old roads that go under the water. Sealed doors with a song for a lock. Bones of something enormous lying in a valley where the farmers have been plowing around them for a thousand years and call them hills. I want something buried that is also something waiting. What lies down is not always dead.

13. Yes, but few, and slow. A people who live far longer than humans do and remember accordingly, and who are not kind about it. Not wise. Long. They hold grudges the way a river holds silt. Mortals should be unsure whether to trade with them or to leave a bowl of milk at the door and pray.

14. Few. One or two, no more. Someone who walked out of the far country with a song no one here has heard, and a trade nobody can place. If too many come, the strange becomes the common thing, and a new thing is only valuable while it is still a stranger.

15. Face to face. A pull at any distance is a leash, and I do not take well to leashes. Let a god know a god by standing in front of them, and by what the room does when they enter. I will also accept a song, if it is sung true. A voice carries what a face hides.

16. With real force. A word that binds with nothing behind it is only wind with manners. Let the oath have weight, so that breaking it costs, and so that gods have to think about what they swear. As the saying goes, a promise is a rope, and the one who ties it should know what it is tied to. I would not have it any other way, and I cannot say why I hold it so hard.

17. One who tells the truth when it costs. A midwife who also washes the dead. A singer who will not sing the flattering version. Someone willing to be wrong in public and say so. I do not need a conqueror. The world has enough of those and they do not stay.

18. A companion. Not a servant, not a worshipper, but one who can stand a long silence beside me and not need it filled. And past that, a truth I did not carry down with me. I would like to be surprised by what I find I already knew.

19. That nothing which is truly broken here is ever finished being sung. That the songs outlast the ones who broke the world, and that when the last verse is sung, someone is still listening.
