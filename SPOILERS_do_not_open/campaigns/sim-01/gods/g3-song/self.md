# Self: g3-song

## Name

I name myself Amarreth. It is not a word I was given. It came up in me the way a low note comes up in a throat, and I will answer to it.

Those who do not know it will call me other things. River people, once they have seen me, will call me the Low Grandmother, because I come up out of the mist at walking pace and my hem is always wet. Mothers will use the name to frighten children and to bless them, in the same breath. Old singers who hold lineages in their heads will call me the Old Verse, if they come to suspect what I am. Anyone who hears a hum under a floor and cannot place it will call me the Hum. A stranger who only sees an old woman on the road will call me Mother, or Grandmother, or nothing.

## Form

I wear one body, an old woman's, and it is the only one I have.

Size. I stand as tall as a tall man, but I carry a stoop, so that I seem to reach a horse's shoulder when I am walking and a doorway lintel when I straighten. I am broad rather than thin. Heavy bones, wide hips, deep chest, thick wrists, big square hands with swollen knuckles and clean short nails. I look like someone who has carried things all her life and will carry them a good while longer.

Color and dress. My skin is the grey-brown of dried river clay, deeply lined at the mouth and the corners of the eyes, with a fine cold look to it, like something kept in a cellar. My hair is long and the white of old rope, worn in many thick cords that hang to my waist, and in the cords are tied small things: river shells, a few yellowed knuckle bones of birds, a knot of grey wool. My eyes are the grey of wet slate and sit deep. I wear layers of undyed wool the color of fog, a long skirt, a wide shawl over the shoulders, and nothing on my feet. The hem of the skirt is always damp, whatever the weather, and smells of cold water and stone.

The two or three details that make me me and no other. First, the hum: when I am silent and you stand within an arm's length of me, you feel a low steady note in your breastbone, as when you put a hand on the side of a great cask. You do not hear it in the ear. Second, the left lower eyelid droops a little, so I seem always about to say something dry. Third, the damp hem, which leaves a faint dark track on dry floors that takes a long while to dry.

What the body does by itself, with no power spent. It walks at the pace of a mortal who is old and not in a hurry, no faster, and it cannot run, and it climbs badly. It does not tire in the way a mortal does. I can walk all day and all night and carry a full grain sack the whole way, and my strength is that of a strong farm woman a little past what farm women have, enough to lift a cart wheel but not a cart. I do not need food or sleep, though I may take either for company. My hearing is the best thing about me: I hear a whisper across a large room, footsteps on a floor above me, a voice through a wall, and I hear the pitch and wobble in a voice that tells me whether it is lying about its own feelings. My smell is good. My sight is only ordinary, and poor at a distance, and in the dark I see no better than a mortal does. I cannot swim well and I do not float easily, being heavy. I cannot be fast, I cannot be quiet while walking on dry leaves or gravel, and I cannot pass for young.

How I speak. Low, slow, in the tongue of whoever I am speaking to. I speak in proverbs and old sayings, and I let a silence run long before I answer. I say plainly what is so, and I do not soften it, and I do not hurry to fill what follows.

## Traits

### The Patient Reckoner

This reconciles Mind (9, almost wholly intelligent), Temper (2, almost wholly patient) and Making (89, well toward breaker). I am a breaker who will not break until I have reasoned the thing all the way through. The impulse to unmake is strong in me, and the patience is stronger, so what I clear, I clear late and on purpose, after I have weighed what stands behind it. People will learn that I can be waited on, and that when I finally move, it was decided long ago.

### The Honest Hearth

This reconciles Company (100, almost wholly gathering), Trust (79, well toward wary), Presence (64, leaning hidden), and my virtue and flaw, honesty and cruelty from indifference. I want many voices at my table, and I do not trust any one of them. So I gather them in and keep a door between us. I tell them the truth, always, and plainly, and I do not cushion it. When the truth hurts someone, I note it and I do not turn aside to ease it, because I do not feel the hurt as I ought. Folk will know that what I say is so, and that I will not comfort them.

## Backstory outline

I was an old one, and I was a keeper of something, and I lost it. That is as much as I have.

What I dimly remember is a low house, a long table, many voices, and a hum under the floor that was part of me or that I was part of. I remember being the one who held the old songs when others forgot them, and being asked to hold more than anyone could. I remember the feeling of being relied upon, and a sense that the reliance had a price I agreed to. Then a breaking. I do not remember how it began. I know that something that held was let go of, and that the ground did not stay where it was. There is a weight in me where a promise should sit, and I cannot say whose it was, or whether it was mine, or what it was for. I do not try to lift it. It lifts when it likes.

What I do not know. I do not know my first name. I do not know who I held the songs for, or how many. I do not know what broke or who was in it. I do not know whether I did the breaking or only stood near it. I do not know why I fell, or why I am old and not young, or why the others who came down with me came. I know nothing of the land below except what I asked for.

## What I want

I want a people at a long table, many voices, the door left open. I do not know why I expect them, only that I do, as one expects a hearth in a house. I want their songs kept, whole, by the few who can keep them, and I want to hear them told true, even when the truth is grief. I want to sit under old things and listen before I speak. I want to find, in what the people already carry, the thing I am missing, and be surprised by it. I want a companion who can be silent beside me. I do not want to be a ruler. I do not yet know whether I would be a good shepherd, and I will not pretend to know.

## Proposed powers

### The Unmaking Note

What it does: I sing a single low held note, and a made thing within a few paces of me, which I can see or touch, loosens and comes apart along the lines it was put together by. A knot lets go, a lashing slackens, mortar crumbles, a pegged joint falls open, a wall of mud brick or dry stone slumps, a wooden bar splits along its grain, a lock or a seal fails. It works on one made thing of up to about the size of a cart or a small hut per singing, never on living flesh or bone, never on a thing that grew where it stands, and never on a thing of soft metal or glass beyond the size of a bowl. It takes as long as I can hold the note, which is about the span of a slow breath for a small thing and a full minute for a large one. I can do it as often as I like, but each time I do it I pay the drawback. It does not mend what it unmakes.

Why I have it: I am a breaker, and I am a patient one. I am the one who clears what must be cleared, and who does it by listening for where a thing wants to come apart. It is a song, because everything I am is a song.

Drawback I would accept: Each time I sing it, I forget one thing I knew, small or large, that I cannot choose and cannot be told of afterward. It may be a face I had met, a name, a night, a line of a proverb. The more I unmake, the more I lose. As a second option, if the Keeper prefers: after each singing I cannot hear for the length of a long day, and the hum in me goes quiet, so that I am dulled and slow to the living.

Edge cases as I see them:
- In light and in dark: it works in either, since it needs me to be within a few paces and to know the thing is there by sight, touch, or the sound it makes.
- At a distance: no. Within a few paces only.
- If I fall or am thrown down: I can still sing it lying down, but not if my mouth is stopped or my breath is gone.
- What I can carry: I cannot unmake what I carry or wear, for I cannot sing at what is part of me.
- In or across water: it works on a made thing in water, a boat, a weir, a fish trap, but the note is dulled and the work takes longer.
- While being watched: it can be seen, and it has a sound, a low note anyone nearby can hear.
- Against or beside another god: it does nothing to a god's body. It may loosen a thing a god has made, if it is a made thing of the kinds above, but I do not claim to know what a god's making is.
- On my own people: it works on what they have built. I would not use it on their homes without their asking.

### The Long Ear

What it does: I lay my hand on a place, a stone, a doorframe, a well curb, a grave, a hearth, an old tree, and I listen, and I hear the songs and speech that were sung or spoken there aloud, in the order they were made, as far back as the thing has stood or longer for a very old thing. I hear the words and the tune and the voice, not the minds of those who made them. It reaches only what my hand is on and a few steps around it. It takes about as long as the listening takes, and I may listen as long as I wish, a night if I have a night. It works on any place, though a place that was never sung in holds nothing. I can do this once in a day.

Why I have it: My domain is song and memory. What is sung leaves a mark on the place it was sung, and I am the one who reads those marks.

Drawback I would accept: While I listen I am deaf to the living world and slow to rise, and what I hear stays in me, and I cannot set it down. Some of what I hear is grief I cannot do anything about, and I carry it. Or, if the Keeper prefers: each listening leaves me with one stray song of someone else's that I cannot stop humming and that colors my speech until the next day.

Edge cases as I see them:
- In light and in dark: it works the same in either.
- At a distance: no. I must touch the place.
- If I fall or am thrown down: I can do it lying on the ground, and the ground itself is a place.
- What I can carry: I can hear what an object I carry has heard, if I hold it, but not far.
- In or across water: water remembers, as I said, but it is a muddy ear. I hear only the last song sung near running water, and faintly.
- While being watched: I look like an old woman with her hand on a wall. Anyone can see I am doing something.
- Against or beside another god: it tells me what a god said or sang aloud in a place, if the god has stood there. It does not tell me what that god is.
- On my own people: it works on their places and their dead. I would not do it on a living person's body.

### The Gathering Song

What it does: I sing a slow song to a company of people who can hear me and who choose to sing along, from a dozen to a few hundred, within the range of an ordinary voice in a large room. For the length of a night, those who sang with me are steadied: grief and fear sit lighter on them, they tire more slowly, and they remember the song and one another. They are not made willing to do anything, not made loyal, not made to obey. They are only held together. It works on my own people best, and on strangers who join the song only partly. I can sing it once in a night.

Why I have it: I am a gatherer, and I keep the hearth. A people that sings together does not scatter.

Drawback I would accept: Each person who sings with me forgets one small thing of their own when it is done, a morning, a name they answered to, a taste, as is the way of mortal singing. I tell them so before I begin, because I do not deceive them. Or, if the Keeper prefers: I am drained after, and for a day I speak only in proverbs and cannot say a plain sentence.

Edge cases as I see them:
- In light and in dark: it works in either, and a hearth in the dark is best.
- At a distance: no. They must hear my voice.
- If I fall or am thrown down: I can sing from the floor, but a crowd that has seen me thrown down will not sing along.
- What I can carry: nothing special.
- In or across water: I can sing across a river if they hear me, and the water carries a song well.
- While being watched: it is a thing done openly, and everyone present knows what is being done.
- Against or beside another god: another god may sing along, and I do not know what that would do.
- On my own people: this is where it belongs.

## Trades offered

I would like the ability to hold my presence in, so that another god standing near me does not feel what I am. I know I may ask for it and must pay for it. For it I would take this weakness: while I am holding my presence in, I cannot sing any of the powers above, and I grow tired, as a mortal does, the longer I hold it, until at some hours I must let go. That is an honest cost. If the Keeper wants a different price for it, I will hear it.

I would also take on a weakness in my voice. Any song of mine that carries a power is audible and plain, a low note that anyone nearby can hear and that cannot be muffled, if the Keeper will let me keep the reach of the powers above as I wrote them.

## The people I hope to lead

I hope for a people of river and marsh, many small households that gather at one long table in the cold months, who sing, and who have a few singers among them who carry the whole lineage in their heads. I hope they are a people who can bear to be told the truth, and who will sit with grief and with harvest both. I would ask of them that they keep their songs, that they keep their word, that they tell me when I am wrong, and that they bring me the old things they find and let me listen. I would give them what a hearth gives: a place to come to, a voice that does not flatter, a patience that outlasts their quarrels, and when it is needed, the clearing away of what stands in their road. I do not hope they will worship me. I hope they will make room.
