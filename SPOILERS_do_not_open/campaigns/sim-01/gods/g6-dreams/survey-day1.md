# Survey, Day 1: g6-dreams

1. A house at night with every lamp lit and the door unlocked. People inside, talking low, bread on the table, and a dark outside that is not hostile, only deep. I want the dark to feel like a held breath, not a threat. Something is always about to be understood.

2. River country, with hills at its edges and a long coast within reach. I want land that people can settle and keep, with water that carries news and a night sky wide enough to be worth looking at. Marsh at the river mouths, for the mist. Nothing too bare. I build, and I need ground that lets me.

3. Four seasons, and they should keep their word. Winter long enough that people tell stories to get through it, summer short and bright, and the turns between them clear and dependable so a people can plan a life around them. No season should lie. I do not want the weather to be a trick. Of all of them, I want to wake into autumn.

4. Some of each, and I want more rooted than wild. Old kingdoms I can learn from, argue with, and trade with. A few stretches of empty country, so there is somewhere to send the restless. I would sooner meet a neighbor than a void.

5. Old. Ruins under ruins. I want a human story deep enough that nobody can say where it started, so there is always something half remembered under the floor. Thousands of years, many thousands. I stand at the far end of that range, nowhere near living memory.

6. Rare but real. A wise-woman in a valley here and there, and most people in most places doubt her until the day they need her. I stand closer to the rumor end than the every-valley end, but not at the rumor itself. If mortals have nothing, I have nothing to answer, and I do not want that.

7. It should feel like listening. Quiet, sideways, closer to remembering than to casting. And it should cost sleep. The more a mortal reaches, the less rest they get, and the worse their nights run. Pay for power in the hours you cannot get back. That is a fair price and a plain one.

8. A god should stand out, but not by much. Let us be something a clever mortal could eventually puzzle at and be afraid of, not an unanswerable sun. I stand nearer the great sorcerer than the one-eyed king. Gods too far above are lonely, and I do not want to be lonely.

9. Familiar animals, mostly, and a handful of real legends. Wolves, owls, deer, moths. Then a few things at the edge of the maps that no one has a name for, and that stay unnamed. I want the strange to be rare enough that it still counts.

10. Being forgotten. Plain death is a fine fear, but I mean the other kind, where the name goes first and then the grave. Also the dark beneath the story, whatever walks in the hours when the lamps run out. A good story needs something that can take your name and leave you alive.

11. Sleep that restores. Honest rest. Mortals should want it more than gold and have the least of it, and they should trade for it, build for it, and fight over it. A good bed, a quiet house, a night without a wound. I will call that wealth.

12. Old roads, first. Roads that go somewhere nobody remembers. Then a few sealed doors and one or two buried halls with the walls carved. I do not want bones of something enormous, and I do not want nothing. I want things made by hands, and I want to find out whose.

13. Yes, a few. Not many. Peoples who keep the old hours, awake when the humans sleep, quiet, courteous, hard to read. They should be real peoples with laws and grudges, not monsters and not wonders. I want to be able to sit at their table and be a little afraid of the host.

14. A few, and strange. A handful of travelers who arrived from very far with tools nobody can explain and no way home. Not a flood of them. I want to meet one at a time, and ask them a great many questions.

15. A pull, faint at distance and sharp up close. I want to know another god is near before I see them, so I can decide what face to wear. Knowing only face to face is too late for someone who prefers caution. But I do not want the pull to be perfect. I want to be able to be wrong.

16. A sworn word should bind with real force. Not total, but real. I am for rules, and a world where promises are worth only the god who made them is a world that cannot hold anything up. Let the breaking cost something that cannot be argued around.

17. Someone who keeps a house and a ledger and a promise, and then does one impossible thing at the end of a long, ordinary life. A mender, a midwife, a gatekeeper, a teacher. I want the quiet ones to rise. Heroes with swords I can have anywhere.

18. A companion. Someone who has been here a long time, who is not a god and not a mortal, and who will tell me the truth when I ask. A place would be good, and a power would be good, but I want a face on the other side of the table.

19. I wish to be remembered. I do not know why I want it so badly, and I will not pretend I do. Let the people below know my name, and still know it in a thousand years.
