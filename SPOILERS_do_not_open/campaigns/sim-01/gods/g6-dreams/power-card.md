# Power card: Orvane

Campaign: sim-01. Keeper label `g6-dreams`. Written on creation Night 2 (2026-11-18), from the final text the god's agent confirmed in `negotiation.md` (Round 2 reply: Confirmed). Descriptions and Drawbacks are frozen from this date; everything learned later goes under Rulings.

## Form

One body. This campaign allows a god one body. A man a hand's breadth taller than a tall man, broad in chest and shoulder, long-armed, with big square unburned hands, and wrong in how right he is: an even face, straight teeth, skin of pale warm brown like fired clay with no scar or line. Black collar-length hair cut straight. Very dark grey eyes that take the shine of a lamp and give back a little more. A long belted coat of undyed oatmeal wool, wool trousers, soft boots, all clean. A small bone lamp-hook on the belt. When it stops moving it stops entirely. It speaks bluntly, in short sentences, low and level.

What the form does by itself:

- Pace: a man's walk, a good runner's run at most. It tires as little as it chooses.
- Strength: it can lift the end of a loaded cart or break a spear shaft across the knee. Not stone, not bronze bar.
- Hearing sharp, best by night; a whisper through a wooden wall. By starlight it sees as a mortal at dusk; in full dark it is blind.
- A spear hurts it. Wounds heal only as fast as a mortal's (see Weaknesses).
- The stillness marks it: a watcher who sees it stop knows something is not a man.

God-sense, as set for this campaign: Orvane knows that others like it came down when it did, and nothing more about them. It feels nothing of them from far away. When one stands near enough to speak to, it feels plainly that this is one like itself, and is known the same way. Nothing else is learned that way: not what the other is, what it can do, what it is called, or what it wants. Orvane can hold its presence in; see the power below.

What another god's power can do to Orvane, or to what it makes, was not settled at creation. It is ruled when it happens.

## Powers

### The Quiet Door
Status: main
Description: Once in a night, Orvane enters the dream of one sleeping mortal: either one it has met face to face, awake, who lies within a day's walk of its body, or any mortal asleep in a Lit House it holds, met or not, at any distance. There it may speak, show what it chooses as a dream shows things, and see what the sleeper is dreaming. It may ask; what answers is a dream, sideways, in the sleeper's own pictures, and it can be wrong, afraid, or lying to itself. It lasts while the sleeper sleeps. The sleeper wakes with a thin, honest memory: that someone was there, the gist, sometimes a shape.
Drawback: While Orvane is in a dream its body lies senseless where it left it, and nothing short of a wound wakes it before the dreamer wakes or dawn comes.
Limits settled at creation:
  - Mortals only. No god, and no sleeper under another god's power.
  - It cannot make the sleeper do anything on waking, cannot plant what the sleeper's mind will not carry, and cannot hold a sleeper who struggles to wake.
  - A mortal with the gift may feel the door open and may shut it.
  - Entering a dream darkens every held Lit House for that night, so a sleeper reached through a Lit House loses that night's rest as Orvane arrives.
  - Nothing passes in or out of a dream but words and pictures.
Rulings:
Trades: The reach to sleepers in a held Lit House was bought with the weakness The uninvited threshold.
Growth: none open

### The Lit House
Status: main
Description: A house, hall or hut in which Orvane has sat through part of a night with a lamp burning becomes, on any night a mortal-tended lamp burns in it, a place of honest rest for mortals and beasts asleep within its walls: no nightmare, no fever dream, a clean sleep and an easy waking. The tired mend as well as rest can mend them, and the sick and hurt rest easier. Orvane may hold five houses at once; to take another it must let one go.
Drawback: The lamp must truly burn, mortal-made and mortal-tended. If a mortal's hand snuffs it while people sleep, Orvane feels it as a blow wherever it is and loses that house for a season. Every held house goes dark whenever Orvane is senseless, and that includes every night it spends through the Quiet Door.
Limits settled at creation:
  - It heals no wound, cures no sickness, and gives back nothing a mortal has spent on a working.
  - It reaches the walls and no farther. A great hall is one house. A boat is not a house.
  - It guards against nothing: anyone, mortal or god, may walk in.
  - It is not secret.
Rulings:
Trades: Five houses (first asked at three) were bought with the weakness Mortal healing.
Growth: none open

### The Held Breath
Status: main
Description: When Orvane takes it up, on purpose, over a few breaths, a god standing near enough to speak to does not feel that Orvane is one like itself. It hides that feeling and nothing else: the body looks as it looks, and a god may still work out what Orvane is from shape, manner or deeds. It does nothing to mortals, who never felt a god that way.
Drawback: It can be held only while Orvane stands or sits quiet. While it is held its hearing and sight fall to a mortal's, and it cannot speak a lie without a stammer anyone can hear.
Limits settled at creation:
  - It closes both ways: while it is held, Orvane feels nothing of other gods either.
  - No other power can be used while it is held. Using one ends the holding at once, and any god within speaking distance then feels Orvane all at once, as a shock.
  - It ends on a blow, a wound, a bad startle, or sleep.
  - Being watched: nothing shows to mortals; to a god, the absence of the feeling is all there is.
  - No limit on how long, so long as it stays quiet.
Rulings:
Trades: Priced by its own drawback.
Growth: none open

## Weaknesses bought against powers

### Mortal healing
Weakness: A wound heals on Orvane only as fast as on a mortal, and a bronze blade leaves a scar that looks open for a year.
Bought: Five Lit Houses held, not three.

### The uninvited threshold
Weakness: Orvane cannot cross the threshold of a dwelling or hall that someone keeps unless it has been invited over it. Invited means: a welcome spoken to it by one who lives there or has the keeping of the place; a hand held out to it at the door by such a one; or a lamp set in the window for it, meaning Orvane itself by its name or by what that house calls it. A lamp set for travelers in general is not an invitation. An invitation stands until one with the same right withdraws it aloud. Ruins, empty houses and open ground have no threshold.
Bought: The Quiet Door's reach to any mortal asleep in a held Lit House.
