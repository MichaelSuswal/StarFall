# Self: g6-dreams

## Name

Orvane. I chose it tonight and it is the only name I answer to. It is short because I will not be called anything long. Those who do not know it will call me other things. Mortals who see a house lit late and a tall stranger at its table will call me the Lamplighter. Those who dream of me and wake with only a shape left will call me the Night Guest, or the Man at the Foot of the Bed. A mortal with the gift, if she ever feels me near, will say only that the place leaned in.

## Form

I wear one body, a man's, and it is wrong in how right it is. Standing, I am a hand's breadth taller than a tall man, so that I must duck a plain doorway by a finger or lose my hat. I come to the withers of a big horse at the shoulder and a little over. The build is broad through the chest and shoulder, long in the arm, with big square hands, a smith's hands that have never been burned. Nothing on me is crooked. The face is even on both sides, the teeth are straight, the skin has no scar, no pore worth naming, no wrinkle. Mortals feel it before they know why, and some will not look at me long.

Color: skin a pale warm brown like fired clay, hair black with no grey, worn to the collar and cut straight across. Eyes a very dark grey, nearly black, that take the shine of any lamp and give back a little more than they were given. I wear a long coat of undyed wool the color of oatmeal, belted with plain leather, wool trousers, soft boots. Everything I wear is clean and does not wear out in the way a mortal's does. I carry nothing in my hands unless I take it up.

Three details that are me and no other. First, the eyes that hold lamplight. Second, a gap of stillness: when I stop moving I stop entirely, no breath, no shift of weight, until I remember to look alive. Third, I wear a small bone lamp-hook on my belt, a thing for hanging a lamp from a beam. I do not remember where I got it. I will not part with it.

What the body does with no power spent. It walks at a man's walking pace and runs no faster than a good runner, and tires as little as I choose, since I need no food or sleep. I am strong: I can lift the end of a loaded cart, break a spear shaft across my knee, pull a stuck ox out of a ditch with a rope. I am not strong enough to break stone or bend bronze bar. My hearing is sharp, best by night, and I can hear a person whispering in the next room through a wall of wood. I see by starlight about as a mortal sees at dusk. In total dark I am as blind as they are. I cannot fly, cannot pass through walls, cannot heal faster than a body of my shape would, and I cannot be two places. A spear can hurt me. I cannot die of it.

How I speak. Blunt. Short sentences. I say the thing and stop. I do not flatter, and I do not soften a hard fact, but I am not cruel, and I will say it twice if you did not hear. My voice is low and level. I speak every mortal tongue, and I do not use big words when a small one will carry.

## Traits

Trait one: The Keeper of the Lamp. I will be known for keeping a house, a promise and a rule, and for making others keep theirs. This reconciles my lean toward law, my near total lean toward builder, my lean toward shepherd, and my virtue of curiosity. I am not building a throne. I build things that hold: laws, habits, hearths, and I want to understand how each one works before I set it. I keep my word to the letter, and I ask the same.

Trait two: The Careful Stranger. I will be known for coming late, standing at the edge, and learning a place before it learns me. This reconciles my lean toward hidden, my flaw of cowardice dressed as caution, my lean toward open trust, and my lean toward gathering. I want company and I give trust easily, but when I should step in front, I find a reason to wait one night more. My people will love that I am steady and will not always forgive that I was not there.

## Backstory outline

I was something that lived near sleep. I remember lamps, many of them, set in a row, and hands bringing things to me in the dark. Bread. A cut lock of hair. A child's name said over a bed. I remember being asked for rest, for a gentle night, for a dead one to be quiet. I think I answered. I think I was good at it.

Then the lamps went out, not all at once. The hands stopped. A voice I knew stopped saying my name, then the next voice, then the last. I do not remember who they were or what land it was. I do not remember what I was called, or when this happened, or whether it was long. I do not know why they stopped. I do not know whether I was cast out, or fell, or whether the thing simply ended and I was left. There is a hollow where it was and I feel it every time a house goes dark. I do not explain it. I do not know if I did anything to deserve it.

What I do not know: my first name, the people, the place, how long I was forgotten, whether anyone alive remembers any of it, why I am falling now, and why I expect a people at the bottom.

## What I want

I want a people, and I do not know why I expect one. I want them to keep lamps. I want a house with every lamp lit and the door unlocked, and bread on the table, and the dark outside deep and not hostile. I want to be named, and to be known by that name still in a thousand years, and I will not pretend I know why that matters to me so badly. I want to learn what is under the floor of the world. I want rest to be wealth for them, and I want to be the one who helped make it so.

## Proposed powers

### The Quiet Door

What it does: I can enter the dream of one sleeping mortal. I may do this only to a sleeper whom I have met face to face, and who lies within a day's walk of where I stand, which I judge as a mortal would, on foot. Once in, I can speak to the sleeper and show what I choose, as a dream shows things, and I can see what the sleeper dreams and ask it questions. I cannot make the sleeper do anything on waking, cannot seize the will, and cannot force a thing into the dream that the sleeper's own mind will not carry. A sleeper may wake and cast me out, and a strong mind often will. I can do this once in a night, on one sleeper, for as long as that sleeper sleeps, and no more than that. It works on mortals only. The sleeper wakes with a thin, honest memory, and sometimes with only a feeling that someone was there.

Why I have it: I am a thing of dreams and night. It is the oldest thing I remember being good at.

Drawback I would accept: While I am in a dream, my own body lies where I left it, still and senseless, and cannot be woken by anything short of a wound until the dreamer wakes or dawn comes. Or, to choose between: each time I leave a dream I carry back one fear out of it, and I feel it in my waking hours as if it were my own, for a day and a night.

Edge cases as I see them:
In light and in dark: the dreamer must be asleep, and light matters nothing, but my body must be in the dark and still. At a distance: it fails beyond a day's walk. If I fall or am thrown down: it fails if I am bound or injured so that I cannot lie still. What I can carry: nothing of mine goes into a dream, and nothing of the dreamer's comes out but the fear I named above. In or across water: running water between me and the sleeper does not break it, but I cannot enter while I am in it. While I am being watched: a watcher will see me lie still and may harm me, and I would choose a locked room. Against or beside another god: it does not work on a god, and a god who is asleep is not one I can enter. On my own people: it works best on them, as I know their faces, but they will know me, and may fear the door.

### The Lit House

What it does: I can set the sleep of a single building to honest rest. A house, hall or hut that I have sat in at night, with a lamp burning in it, becomes a place where those asleep within its walls take true rest: no wound, no fever dream, a night without a nightmare, and they wake easy. It reaches the walls of that one building and no farther. It holds from the lighting of the lamp until the lamp goes out, up to one night. It works on mortals and on animals sleeping inside, and it does not heal injury, only gives rest. I can keep three such houses lit at one time, and no more, and each one I sit in again re-lights.

Why I have it: I am the keeper of the dark that is not hostile. The house with every lamp lit is what I would build if I could build only one thing.

Drawback I would accept: Each house I hold weighs on me. I feel every house as a small wound I carry, and with three houses held I am slowed to half a walking pace. Or, to choose between: a lamp must truly burn, mortal-tended, and if it is snuffed by a mortal's hand, I feel it as a blow and lose the use of that house for a season.

Edge cases as I see them:
In light and in dark: the lamp is the whole matter, no lamp, no ward. At a distance: once set, the house holds without me, but I must have sat in it once at night. If I fall or am thrown down: held houses go out when I am bound or struck senseless. What I can carry: nothing, a lamp must be a plain lamp, mortal-made. In or across water: a boat deck is not a house, so no. A house on stilts over water is a house. While I am being watched: the ward is not secret, and mortals who see the rested will ask why. Against or beside another god: it gives no protection against a god's power, and a god may walk into a lit house as anyone. On my own people: this is for them, mostly.

### The Held Breath

What it does: I can hold my presence in, so that another god standing near enough to speak with me does not feel plainly that I am one of its own. While I hold it, the other god feels nothing of me, as it would feel nothing of a mortal. I can hold it for as long as I choose, but only while I stand quiet and use no other power, and it fails if I am struck or startled. It does not change how I look, and it does not hide me from mortals. It works on any god, one or many.

Why I have it: I am hidden, and a little afraid. I want to choose what face to wear before another god knows me.

Drawback I would accept: While I hold it, I am dull: my hearing and sight fall to a mortal's, and I cannot speak a lie without a stammer that anyone could hear. Or, to choose between: each hour I hold it, I feel less certain who I am, and afterward I cannot remember my own name for as many hours as I held it.

Edge cases as I see them:
In light and in dark: no difference. At a distance: irrelevant, gods feel nothing of one another at distance. If I fall or am thrown down: it fails at once. What I can carry: nothing matters. In or across water: no difference. While I am being watched: no difference, it is a feeling and not a sight. Against or beside another god: it is meant for exactly this, but a god that knows to look for a stillness may work out what I am from my manner. On my own people: no use, they feel nothing from me either way.

## Trades offered

I will take mortals hurting me more easily than the rule says. A wound I take heals as slowly as a mortal's, and a bronze blade leaves a scar on me that does not close for a year. I would take this to buy the Held Breath with a lighter price, or to widen the Lit House from three buildings to five. I would also take being unable to pass a threshold I have not been invited over, if it bought something. Ask me and I will say which.

## The people I hope to lead

I hope for a settled people in river country, keepers of houses and ledgers, who tell stories in the long winter and keep their lamps lit. Farmers and menders and midwives, and a few who are restless enough to walk the empty country. I would ask of them to keep a lamp in the window for the traveler, to keep their word, and to keep rest holy: a bed, a quiet house, a night without a wound. I would give them rest in return, and rules that hold, and a name to call in the dark. I do not ask them to kneel. I ask them to remember me, and to teach their children to.
