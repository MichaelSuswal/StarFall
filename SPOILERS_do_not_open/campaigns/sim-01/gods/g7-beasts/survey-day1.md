# Survey, Day 1: g7-beasts

1. A long table at the end of a hunt, and every seat taken. Firelight on fat and bone, a hundred voices talking over each other, and a deal being struck at every elbow. Cold air at the door, warmth inside. That is the truest this world gets. I will take a full hall over a quiet hill any day, friend, and I will give you a fair price for the hall.

2. Forest edge and high steppe, where the trees thin out and the herds move. Game country. I want land with deep cover behind it and open grass in front of it, so that something is always being driven from one into the other. Marsh and coast are fine for trade, and I would never turn down a good river, but the herds are where my heart sits.

3. Four honest seasons, each with its own work and its own prices. A hard winter that thins the weak and makes every fire precious, a green spring of birthing, a fat summer, and an autumn with a real edge to it, so that everything worth having must be gathered before the frost closes the bargain. Nothing mild all year. A mild world has no market in it. When each new year finds me, I want to wake into autumn.

4. Some of each, but I will say it plainly: more old kingdoms than empty land. Wild country is fine for hunting, and I want some of it, but I am a gathering sort. I want neighbors to trade with, argue with, and sell to. A world with nobody past the border is a shop with no street. Leave me a few unwalked valleys and fill the rest with people who have something to haggle over.

5. Skipped.

6. Closer to the wise-woman end than the rumor end, but not quite a wise-woman in every valley. Say one in every few valleys, known by name, sought out, and a little feared. Enough that a mortal can count on it existing and still have to pay for it. A thing that is everywhere is worth nothing. A thing that is doubted is worth nothing either. I want it scarce and real, the best price.

7. It should feel like a bargain being struck, because that is what it is. The mortal asks, something answers, and a price is named. The cost should be fair and it should be paid in something real: years, a memory, a season of luck, a thing loved. I want the price named before the work is done, never after. A cost sprung on a mortal afterward is cheating, and I do not hold with cheating. Honest weights, honest measures.

8. Well above a great mortal sorcerer, but not the only one-eyed man among the blind. Say a god stands out like a mountain stands out from hills. Visible from a day away, impossible to ignore, yet a determined crowd could still make a god's life difficult. I want to be seen and spoken to, and I want to be strong enough that my word is weighed. I do not want to be so far above them that nobody can bargain with me.

9. Mostly familiar animals, with a good many legends, and a few things that have no name. Say seven in ten creatures should be ones a hunter already knows by track and sound, two in ten should be known only by old stories that turn out true, and one in ten should be a thing nobody has words for yet. The nameless ones are the best of the hunt. But if everything is strange, nothing is, and a hunter has nothing to read.

10. The stranger at the door who is refused. The kind of danger that comes from breaking a rule of hospitality or an oath, where the harm is earned and everyone in the story can see the account being settled. I like a danger with a ledger in it. Beyond that, a beast that hunts the hunters, and a hard winter. Plain teeth and plain cold make the best stories, and a debt that comes due makes the best ending.

11. Salt, and honest weights. Salt keeps meat through winter, and a mortal who has it can feed a hall. Honest weights are rarer than salt, because a market where every scale tells the truth is a market almost nobody has ever seen. Mortals will trade for the first and fight over the second, and they will go without both more often than they should.

12. Skipped.

13. Yes, a few, and they should be the sort you can sit down with. I would like a people who keep the old paths through the deep forest and will guide you for a fair fee, and a people of the high grass who breed fine animals and sell them dear. Not monsters. Neighbors with their own customs, their own law, and their own prices. I would also leave room for one that is hard to like and impossible to ignore.

14. A few, not many. One or two travelers a generation, arriving with something odd in their packs, such as a tool, a seed, a map nobody can read, or a trick of smithing. A trickle keeps a market lively. A flood would flatten it. And I would like to meet each one at my own door and see how they behave as guests.

15. Face to face, and by manner more than by sense. I want to know a god by how it behaves when it sits down at my table: whether it is generous, whether it keeps to the terms, whether it takes without giving. A pull felt at any distance is cheap and tells you nothing. Let gods have to meet, and let the first meeting be a test.

16. A sworn word should bind with real force. I do not mean a leash, I mean a debt. Break a sworn word and the world should keep the account and collect on it, sooner or later, with interest. A word worth only as much as the one who gave it is a coin with no stamp. I would rather live in a world where a promise costs something to make, so that the ones who make them mean them.

17. One who is generous at the table and ruthless about fairness. A mortal who feeds the stranger, keeps the bargain, and will not let a cheat go unanswered, even when it costs them. A hunter, a headwoman, or a trader, I do not much mind which, as long as they can gather people around them and hold them together. I would love to follow someone whose hall is always full and whose scales are always true.

18. A companion. Something that follows me out of the wild and chooses to stay, a creature or a person or a thing between the two, that I did not summon and cannot quite price. I would also take a power that comes only from being many, a strength that grows with the size of the gathering. But the companion is what I would want. I can already imagine a good deal, and I cannot imagine that.

19. That I be known by name in every hall in the land, and that when they set a place for the stranger at their table, they are setting it for me. I want to be worshipped, and I will admit it, though I would rather be worshipped for being fair than for being feared. I have a feeling that I once did a great deal of serving, and I would very much like, this time, to be the one who is served.
