# Negotiation replies: g7-beasts

## Round 1 reply

1. Accept. I did not ask to hide, and I would rather be seen from a day away.
2. Accept. A balance that weighs weight truly is all I ever claimed for it. The weighing of bargains is done by me.
3. Accept. Salt that spoils by dawn is a fair price for a table that binds. One note, so the account is honest: I take it that I may pour the salt for a table at the time the table is laid, and that a fresh pour for a new table is always there when I ask. If so, I am content.
4. Accept.
5. Accept. Both together, as ruled.
6. Accept.
7. Accept. I name the price before the work, and I pay it. A beast's kind may hold me to it for their generation.
8. Accept.
9. Accept.
10. Accept. I would not want salt to bind a man who was tricked into it. That would be cheating.
11. Accept. A host who breaks his own terms should feel it.
12. Take it. Every mortal who sees me knows me for a god, and the account after a Salt Table runs a full year for both sides. A year is a fair length for a debt.
13. Take it. At my own table, when a guest who has taken my salt puts a fair bargain to me by my own honest judgment, I cannot refuse it. I may haggle to fair, and I may not walk away from fair. I accept that anyone who learns this can bring me a fair offer I would rather not take. A merchant who walks away from fair is no merchant. In exchange, the Count of the Herd reaches a full day's walk.
14. Understood.
