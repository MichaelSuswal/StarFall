# Self: g1-stone

## Name

I name myself Orrun. It is short and it sounds like stone settling.

Those who do not know it will call me what they see. Herders and travelers would say the Grey One, or Old Grey, from afar. Anyone who has taken shelter from me would say the Door, or the Keeper of the Pass, once they understand what I did. Children will have a rhyme for me and I will not know it.

## Form

I wear one body. It stands about a head and a half taller than a tall man, near nine feet. The top of my head touches the lintel of a doorway built for two men standing on each other's shoulders, so I stoop under any roof a mortal built. I am broad, as wide across the shoulders as two men side by side, deep in the chest, with long thick arms and short heavy legs, built like a boulder that has learned to stand.

My skin is grey granite, rough as a quarried face, with pale seams of white quartz running through it. One seam runs from the left temple down across the cheek to the jaw, like an old scar. In the deep creases at the elbows, neck and knees there is always a fine rime of frost, even in summer. My breath shows as cold fog in every season. In spring, moss and small lichen come on the tops of my shoulders and the back of my hands, and they brown and fall away by autumn. My eyes are pale as meltwater, with no dark center. My hair is a heavy coarse mat, the grey of wet slate. I wear a long mantle of undyed wool felt, stiff with cold and always slightly damp, pinned at the left shoulder with a plain shard of black stone. My feet are bare and leave deep prints in soft ground.

The two or three details that make me me and no other: the white seam across my face, the fog of my breath in any weather, and the moss that grows on me in spring.

What the body does with no power spent. It walks at the pace of a walking mortal, no faster, and it does not tire at that pace. It cannot run beyond a hard, heavy stride, and it sinks in deep water because I am stone, so I cannot swim and I cannot cross a river deeper than my chest. I am very strong. I can lift a loaded cart's weight, or a boulder that four men could not shift, and I can hold a wall or a roof beam up for a long while. I am hard to cut, but a bronze axe swung by many hands over time will chip and crack me, and a fall from great height will break pieces off me that heal slowly. I feel pain as a dull ache, not a sharp one. Cold does not trouble me. Heat dulls me and makes me slow and heavy. My sight and hearing are those of a strong mortal. My sense of smell is poor. Through bare feet on stone I feel heavy footfalls and rockfalls near me, but nothing finer than that without a power. I cannot pass through stone, fly, or change my size. I cannot enter a small room. I cannot be quiet on gravel.

I speak in very few words, low and slow, like rock shifting under snow. I use short sentences. I say what I mean and then stop. I do not explain myself unless I choose to.

## Traits

**The Door That Stays Open, And Watches.** I shelter anyone who comes to me in need, and I do not make them earn it first. Then I watch them for as long as it takes.
This reconciles my mercy and my lean to shepherd and to gathering, which want to take every cold stranger in, with my near total wariness, which wants to trust no one. I do both: I open the door and I keep my eyes on the guest. Mercy is given first, trust is given last, and the two do not need to agree.

**Will Not Be Told.** I keep my own counsel and I keep my own word. I do not take orders, from mortals or from gods, and I will break a bad law quietly and keep a good one without being asked.
This reconciles my lean to freedom and my pride with my patience and my lean to builder. I do not bow to rules I did not make, but I am not wild. I build slowly, I keep what I build, and I hold myself to my own sworn word more strictly than anyone could hold me. Pride keeps my head up. Patience keeps my hands from striking.

## Backstory outline

I do not remember a time before the fall. I remember grey light on high rock, many fires in valleys far below, quiet, and then voices. I think I was something large and slow and old, a mountain or a part of one, and I think something small and many lived on me and in me. I do not remember their faces or their names.

I feel I lost them. I do not know how, or when, or whether it was my fault. There is a weight behind my ribs in the shape of arms that were too short, or a door that would not hold. I cannot say more, and I do not try to. When I look at it directly it goes dim.

Plainly, I do not know: who I was, what the people were, where they lived, what became of them, whether anyone else remembers, why I fell, or why I am here now. I know that I want them to live this time, and I do not know who "them" is.

## What I want

I expect a people to lead, and I do not know why I expect it. I want to find them hungry and tired in the cold, and give them shelter, and keep them alive through a winter. I want neighbors and room, not an empire. I want a door in the rock and a truth about whoever stood here before me. I do not want to be alone on the height, and I do not want to be told what to do about it.

## Proposed powers

### Hearth of Stone

What it does: I open a hollow shelter in living rock, a dry, warm room with a doorway, large enough for about twenty mortals to sleep in, in any cliff, hillside outcrop or standing boulder large enough to hold it. It takes me about an hour of standing with both hands on the stone. The room holds the heat of the people inside it and keeps out wind, snow and rain. It lasts until the next full thaw, and then the rock closes slowly back, over several days, with warning. I can hold at most three such shelters standing at once, anywhere in the world, and I know where each one is.

Why I have it: I am stone and I shelter. The rock obeys me where it would not obey a mortal because I am the mountain's own kind. It is the thing I would do whether or not I had a name for it.

Drawback I would accept: Each shelter I make leaves a part of me sealed in it, so while a shelter stands I am slower: the first shelter makes my walking pace slower by a quarter, the second by a half, and a third by three quarters, until each closes. Alternatively, a shelter will not open for anyone I have not looked in the eye first, and I can be wrong about them.

Edge cases as I see them:
- Light and dark: It works the same in both. I do not need to see the stone, only touch it.
- Distance: I must be touching the stone. I cannot make a shelter from afar.
- Fall or thrown down: If I am broken or pinned, I cannot make one until I can use my hands.
- What I can carry: It makes the shelter, not what is in it. I cannot make food, fuel or furniture.
- Water: It does not work on rock under flowing water, and it does not work on wet, soft or crumbling stone.
- Being watched: Watchers see a door appear in the rock and a wall open slowly. It cannot be done unseen.
- Against or beside another god: It works on stone another god holds only if it is not their made shelter or ward. I could not open another god's sealed door with it.
- On my own people: It works for anyone, but I choose who can enter at the moment I open it. I do not choose after.

### Stone Hearing

What it does: While I stand with both bare feet and one hand on bedrock, I can feel the footfalls, voices spoken near rock, and breathing of living things that touch the same connected stone, out to about a day's walk, and I can tell roughly how many, how heavy, and in which direction. I can do it for as long as I stand there. I cannot tell who anyone is, or what they are saying.

Why I have it: I am the mountain's own kind, and the rock carries what walks on it. It is my sense, as an ear is a mortal's.

Drawback I would accept: While I listen I am deaf to air, so I cannot hear anyone speaking to me, and I cannot move my feet or I lose it. Alternatively, each time I listen for a whole day, I lose a day's sense of taste or warmth the next day.

Edge cases as I see them:
- Light and dark: It works the same in both.
- Distance: A day's walk of connected bedrock only. Soil, sand, deep snow, and gaps of water or loose scree stop it.
- Fall or thrown down: I can use it if I am lying with my hand on rock.
- What I can carry: Nothing needed.
- Water: Rivers and lakes break the stone link. I cannot hear across them.
- Being watched: A watcher would see me stand very still. Nothing else shows.
- Against or beside another god: A god standing on the same rock would feel to me like a heavy, odd footfall, and I would not know what it was. I do not know whether a god could do the same to me.
- On my own people: They are heard as footfalls like anyone else. I do not hear thoughts.

### Return At Thaw

What it does: If my body is broken, scattered or buried so that I cannot act, it reforms on the high stone nearest where it fell by the next spring thaw, whole, with moss on the shoulders. I remember what happened. This works once a year at most, and it does not reach farther than a few days' walk from where I fell, and it does not work on a body held or bound by something living.

Why I have it: I am stone and weather, and the mountain takes back what it keeps. Winter is the time I am least myself, and the thaw is the time I come back.

Drawback I would accept: From the first hard frost to the thaw, my body is slow and dim: I move at half pace, my senses are dulled, and my other powers are weaker. I would take this even if I never use Return At Thaw. Alternatively, I return, but I forget one thing I knew before, chosen by the world and not by me.

Edge cases as I see them:
- Light and dark: Not relevant. It happens in the weather of the thaw.
- Distance: A few days' walk of my fall, no more.
- Fall or thrown down: This is what it is for.
- What I can carry: What I carried is left where I fell. I return bare.
- Water: If my body is under deep water or in a river, it cannot reform until it is moved to stone and the thaw comes.
- Being watched: A watcher would see only the thaw and then me standing there.
- Against or beside another god: It does not protect me from being bound or sealed by a god, and I would not return from that.
- On my own people: It does not work on them. I return alone.

## Trades offered

I would take a heavier body and a slower walk, a quarter slower than a walking mortal, for the whole of my time in the world, if in return I can hold my presence in so that another god standing near me does not feel what I am. I want to be able to stand near one of my own kind and not be known. I trust no one, and I would rather choose the moment.

## The people I hope to lead

I hope for a people who live in the high valleys and under the passes, hard country, who know winter is a test and keep stores against it. I hope they are few and tired when I find them, and not proud. I hope they keep a custom of taking in the stranger, and a custom of looking the stranger over. I would ask them for honesty, for salt shared, and for their word kept when it is easy to leave. I would give them shelter in the cold, safe passage through the mountain, and a door that opens.
