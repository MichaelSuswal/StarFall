# Survey, Day 1: g2-law

1. A great hall with the doors thrown open and the table already loud. Everyone is sworn to something, and everyone is arguing about what it meant. Old sayings go round like cups. The mood is a held breath just before a vow is spoken, and just after one is broken, the same breath. A new thing is always about to begin, and an old thing is always about to fall down. That is the world at its truest.

2. River country, and the broken ground where rivers change their minds. A river that keeps its bed is a river that has never been tested. I want flood plains, fords that matter, and cliffs where the water has cut through a wall that thought it was permanent. Mountains if they crack. Marsh if it swallows a boundary stone. I do not love land that sits still.

3. Four seasons, and none of them polite. A short, fierce spring, a long gold summer, an autumn that arrives all at once, a winter that is honest and does not apologize. Let the turns be sharp, so that a people can say "before the thaw" and be understood. A door that opens slowly is no door. As for the waking, when the new year finds me I want to wake into spring.

4. Some of each, but weighted toward the rooted kingdoms. Wild country is only empty paper. An old kingdom has contracts, grudges and walls, and walls are what I am for. Give me neighbors with long memories and sharp customs, and a few wild corners where nobody has yet agreed on anything. The fun is at the seam between the two.

5. Thousands of years, and the ruins should be stacked. A people that remembers only yesterday cannot swear to anything, because there is nothing for the oath to stand on. I want old law under new law, and tablets nobody can read any longer. Deep, but not so deep that nothing living can be traced back. Call it five or six thousand years of people being stubborn at one another.

6. Toward the generous end, but never a wise-woman in every valley. One in every third valley, and the other two valleys quarrel about whether she is real. Enough that a child can grow up knowing someone who has seen it. Not so much that anyone stops being afraid of it. A rumor that most people half believe is the best size for a rumor.

7. It should feel like a promise being kept in the body. A pull in the chest, a weight in the hands, a taste of iron when the thing is nearly done. The cost should be a word given up. Let the mortal spend something they swore, a name, a vow, a year of their own promise, and let them feel the hole where it was. Magic that costs nothing makes liars of everyone. Magic that costs an oath makes careful people, and careful people make good stories.

8. Stand out plainly, a great deal. I do not hide and I do not want to. A god should be a thing people point at, flinch from, and tell their children about. Not the one-eyed man among the blind, that is too small and too lonely. More like a thunderstorm walking into a market. But let there be sorcerers and mortal powers worth being wary of, so that standing out is still a risk and not merely a stroll.

9. Strange, and I will say how far: strange enough that a hunter can come home with a story no one believes, not so strange that nobody has a word for anything. Mostly familiar beasts, and a good third of the wild unnamed. I want the first time a creature is seen to be an event. A thing with a name is a thing that has been tamed.

10. Premise rejected: I do not grant that mortals fear each other first and the world second, or that the two are separate fears. The danger worth a story is the broken oath. A bridge sworn safe that falls. A lord who swore protection and sold the village. A curse that was only a contract read to the letter. Fear of the one who will not keep his word, and fear of being that one yourself, is the best fear I know.

11. Trust that holds. Everything else can be traded for, stolen, mined or farmed, but a word that will still be good in ten years is the rarest coin, and people should pay dearly for it. Salt and iron can be hard to find as well, so there is something to carry. But the true scarcity is the believable promise.

12. Sealed doors. Doors that were shut by an oath, with the oath written on the frame, and no one alive who remembers who swore it or what the price of opening would be. Beside them, old roads that run straight to places that are no longer there. I would like to stand in front of one of those doors and feel my hand want to push.

13. Yes, a few, and not many. Two or three peoples, each with its own idea of what a promise is. One that cannot lie and is therefore dangerous to talk to. One that lies as a courtesy and takes offense at plain speech. One old and small and watchful, who keep records of everyone. They should be neighbors and not monsters. They should find humans a little ridiculous, and be a little right.

14. Skipped.

15. Face to face, and I want to see them. A pull at any distance sounds tidy, but tidy is how you end up unable to avoid anyone. I would rather meet a god across a table and know it, all at once, the way you know a storm is in the room. Let there be a moment, and let it be a shock. I want to be seen, and I want to see, and I want the first look to be mine to remember.

16. A sworn word between gods should bind with real force, and a god who breaks one should feel it. If it binds only as far as the god who gave it, then every promise is a bluff and every table is a lie. But the force should be a thing a god can choose to take on, not a thing laid on them for free. Make the swearing cost something and the breaking cost more. Let no god be compelled into an oath, and let no god be easily excused from one. Even I, who like to break things, would wish this to stand.

17. A mortal who changes their mind in public and means it. A judge, a witness, a liar caught out and made better by it. Someone who is brave enough to say a hard sentence in front of a crowd, and clever enough to make the sentence stick. Not a conqueror. Not a saint. A person who is, on the whole, impatient with injustice and willing to be late for dinner because of it.

18. A companion, and I do not mean a servant. Another being who can sit across from me and say no, and have the no matter. Failing that, a truth about why I am so quick to anger and so slow to rest, though I would not know where to look. I would also like to find a place I cannot enter without paying, so that I learn what it feels like to be bound.

19. That the first oath ever sworn in this land be kept, and that nobody ever learn what it was. Let it sit under everything like a keystone. All the rest can crack and be rebuilt, but that one should hold, and the whole world should lean its weight on it without knowing. I have not dared say it because I do not know why I want it, and something in me goes very quiet when I do.
