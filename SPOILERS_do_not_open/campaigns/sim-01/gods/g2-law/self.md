# Self: g2-law

## Name

My name is Kest. I chose it tonight because it is short enough to be shouted across a market and hard enough to be sworn on. A name is a thing you hand to people so they can hold you to account, and I mean to be held.

Those who do not know it will call me by what they see. Strangers who meet me in a road or a market will say the Small Judge, or the Child at the Table, because of my size and because I will walk to the head of any argument. Those who fear me will say the Level Eyes. Children will say the Gray Boy, whatever my age looks to be. A people who come to know me will in time have a title of their own, and I will answer to it.

## Form

I wear one body: a child of about ten years, and uncannily perfect. I stand to the ribs of a tall man, about four and a half feet. My head comes a hand's breadth below the shoulder of a plow horse, and an ordinary doorway clears my head by more than a forearm. I am slight and square in the shoulder, with long straight limbs, narrow hands and feet, and a neck held very upright. Nothing about me is crooked or uneven: the two sides of my face match, my teeth are even, and my hands are the same size.

My skin is warm olive brown and entirely without mark: no scar, freckle, blemish or visible pore, like glazed clay. My hair is the color of wet slate, straight and cut cleanly at the line of the jaw, with a fringe cut level across the brow. My eyes are a pale gold, one flat even color from rim to center, with no flecks. I wear a sleeveless tunic of undyed gray linen to the knee, with a plain cord belt, and nothing on my feet. The linen is clean and does not crease.

Three details make me me. First, my eyes do not blink unless I choose to blink, and when I look at a person I do not look away first. Second, the palms of my hands have no lines on them, smooth as a river stone. Third, my bare feet leave prints of exactly equal depth, heel and toe, even in mud.

With no power spent, I walk at the pace of a walking mortal and no faster. I am strong for what I look like, as strong as two strong men working together: I can lift the end of a loaded cart, snap an oak limb as thick as a wrist, and break a clay jar in one hand. I am not tireless and I am not hard to hurt. A blow that would hurt a child hurts me, and a bronze blade opens my skin, though the wound is clean and slow to bleed. My eyes and ears are as sharp as a good hunter's, and no better. I see in the dark as a mortal does, which is badly. My sense of smell, taste and touch are ordinary. I need no food and sleep only if I choose.

I cannot make myself look older, taller or common, and I cannot pass unnoticed in any crowd. I cannot fly, cannot climb well with my short reach, and I do not swim well. I cannot hold in my presence so that another god does not know me, because I have not asked for that.

I speak in a clear, level voice, high for a child and unhurried, and I do not rush a sentence. I reach for a proverb or an old saying before I reach for a plain phrase. I say what I mean, and I say it as if reading it from a long scroll. I can speak any mortal tongue.

## Traits

**The Breaker of Bad Walls.** This reconciles my domain of law and oaths, my lean toward freedom (Order 83), my near total lean toward breaker (Making 95) and my lean toward intelligent (Mind 28). I am for law, and I break things. These do not fight. A law that cannot be broken in a hard case is a cage, and an oath that cannot be tested is a bluff. I take up a wall, a seal or an old rule, and I put my weight against it to learn if it is honest. If it holds, I will defend it. If it was only held up by fear, I will bring it down and say so in front of everyone.

**The Patient Word and the Hurried Hand.** This reconciles my virtue of patience, my near total lean toward impulsive (Temper 100), my flaw of cowardice dressed as caution and my lean toward revealed (Presence 12). I am patient about oaths: I will wait years to see a word kept or broken, and I will hear a thing out in full. In everything else I am quick, loud and rash, and I act before I have counted. The cowardice shows where the two meet: when I meet a thing I cannot weigh, I hold back and call it care, and I hold back longest from the things that matter most. The saying I fall back on is that the careful man is not yet afraid, and I know it is a lie when I say it.

## Backstory outline

I do not know who I was. I know I was old, and small, and that I was not alone. I remember a long fall, and a great many voices asking for things as I went down, and a sense of choosing among them without being able to say how.

There is a place in me that is quiet and shaped like someone. Something once stood beside me, near my size, and does not now. When I press toward that place my hands go cold, and I do not follow it. I do not know who it was, I do not know what happened, and I do not want to be told by a stranger. It is my wound, and it is not mine to explain.

I do not know what I was a god of before the fall. I do not know why I am quick to anger and slow to rest. I do not know why I expect a people to lead. I know nothing about the land below except that I asked for river country, and nothing about who lives there.

## What I want

I want to be seen, and I want to see. I want to be set down in a place with old walls and long memories, and to put my hand on the first thing that holds. I expect a people to lead and I do not know why I expect it, but the expectation is a weight, like a promise made before I could speak. I want a people who swear to things and keep them, and a few who do not, so there is something to test. I would like a companion who can say no to me and have it matter. And somewhere under it all, I want the first oath ever sworn in this land to hold, though I could not tell you why, and something in me goes very quiet when I say it.

## Proposed powers

### The Ear for Oaths

What it does: Any oath, vow or sworn bargain spoken aloud within about sixty paces of me, while I am awake and attending, I take in whole: the exact words, who swore it and to whom, and the thing named behind it. I do not learn what the swearer meant in the heart, only what was said. From then on I feel the oath in my chest as a thin held thread. If it is broken, the thread snaps, and I know that it broke, which oath it was, and the direction and rough distance of the breaker, so long as the breaker is within a day's walk, about twenty miles. I cannot do more than know. I cannot punish, compel or undo. There is no limit on how many oaths I may hear, the memory of the words lasts as long as I do, and the thread lasts until the oath is kept, released or broken. It works on mortals and, where I am in hearing, on gods.

Why I have it: I am a being of law and oaths, and a promise is the one thing I could always hear, even in the fall.

Drawback I would accept: Each break I feel lands in my body as a blow. I stagger, my hands and legs go weak, and I lose my grip on what I hold, for the time of ten breaths, and I am dull and slow for an hour after. Several breaks at once stack, so a hall of broken oaths can put me on the floor. As an alternative drawback, I would accept that I cannot choose not to hear: in any crowd I take in every oath within range, and more than a handful at once leaves me dazed and unable to follow speech.

Edge cases as I see them:
- In light and in dark: It needs hearing, not sight, so it works the same in the dark.
- At a distance: I take in oaths only within about sixty paces. I feel a snap only within a day's walk, and a broken oath farther out I do not feel at all.
- If I fall or am thrown down: Nothing changes, but a snap while I am down is as heavy as it is standing.
- What I can carry: It has no bearing on what I carry.
- In or across water: I cannot hear speech under water, and I take in nothing there. Across a river I hear what carries across it.
- While I am watched: Nothing shows. I stand and listen like a child at the edge of a crowd.
- Against or beside another god: I hear an oath a god swears within sixty paces. I feel its break only within the day's walk, like any other, and I do not learn what the god is by it.
- On my own people: This is its best use. I will feel when an oath among my people breaks, and I will know it was theirs.

### The Breaking Hand

What it does: With my bare hand flat on a made barrier, I can unmake it. I mean a door, a bar, a lock, a seal, a boundary stone, or a stretch of wall no larger than a doorway, about a man's height and width. In the time of three breaths it cracks through along every seam and falls apart into splinters, rubble, or shards. It works on wood, clay, stone, bronze, bone, rope and cloth, as the peoples below make them. It does not work on living things, on earth, on water or ice, or on the body of any god, and I cannot tell yet if it works on anything a god or a sorcerer made. I can do this once in a day, from one sunrise to the next. The thing I break stays broken and I cannot mend it.

Why I have it: I am almost wholly a breaker, and law is only worth anything where a wall can be tested.

Drawback I would accept: Each use leaves a visible hairline crack in my perfect skin, from the palm of the hand I used up the forearm, pale and fine like a flaw in glaze. It aches. It takes a full day to heal, and while it lasts the grip of that hand is weak and I cannot use the power again. As an alternative, I would accept that the hand will not close on a barrier that stands under a sworn oath still being kept, and if I try I am stunned for the time of ten breaths.

Edge cases as I see them:
- In light and in dark: I need to find the barrier with my hand, and I see poorly in the dark, so I may break the wrong thing.
- At a distance: Touch only. I cannot break anything I cannot lay a palm on.
- If I fall or am thrown down: It still works if my hand lands on the thing. It does not work if I am bound or held so I cannot put the palm flat.
- What I can carry: It does not break what I hold, only what I touch with a free palm, and it cannot be used while I carry anything in that hand.
- In or across water: A barrier under water breaks the same, but the rubble sinks or drifts, and I cannot see it fall.
- While I am watched: It is plain to see, a crack running through stone under a child's hand. I do not hide it.
- Against or beside another god: It does not work on the body of a god, nor on a barrier a god is standing behind and holding shut with its own strength.
- On my own people: It works on a wall my people raised, and I will not break one unless they ask or the wall is false. I take care, because I will not mend it.

### The Hall Voice

What it does: When I choose to, my voice carries clear and unhurried to every person within about two hundred paces, over a crowd, a storm, a market or a battle, and each word reaches each hearer as plainly as if I stood beside them. It does not compel anyone to anything. It does not soften or sharpen the meaning. It only makes sure that what I say is heard, in full, by everyone who stands in the range. It lasts as long as I speak, and I may use it as often as I like in a day. It works on mortals and gods alike, within the range.

Why I have it: I want to be a thing people point at and hear, and a judgment is no good if only the front rows catch it.

Drawback I would accept: The voice cannot carry a word I know to be false. If I speak a lie in it, my voice breaks into a thin child's treble and everyone in range hears the break. As an alternative, I would accept that while I speak in this voice I cannot lower it, so that I cannot whisper or speak to one person alone and unheard, and for an hour after I use it my ordinary voice is hoarse.

Edge cases as I see them:
- In light and in dark: Sound only, so it works the same in the dark.
- At a distance: About two hundred paces, and nothing past that. It does not carry across a wide river or over a hill.
- If I fall or am thrown down: It works if I can draw breath, and a blow to the chest or a hand at my throat stops it.
- What I can carry: It has no bearing on what I carry.
- In or across water: It does not work under water. Across water it carries as far as it does on land.
- While I am watched: It is the opposite of hiding. I use it to be watched.
- Against or beside another god: A god within range hears me as a mortal does, and may answer in its own voice. It gives me no hold on any god.
- On my own people: It reaches my people the same as any other, and I mean to use it for judgments and oaths sworn in front of all.

## Trades offered

I do not ask to hide what I am from another god. I want to be seen, and I want the first look to be mine to remember.

I would take on this weakness: any word I swear freely, with something of my own named behind it, binds me in the body as hard as it binds anyone, and if I break it, a crack opens on me at once and I cannot use the Breaking Hand for a season. I would want this to buy the reach of the Ear for Oaths: the hearing extended from sixty paces to the whole of a hall or a market square, about two hundred paces, and the feeling of a snap extended from a day's walk to the whole of a valley.

## The people I hope to lead

I hope to find a rooted people in river country, with old walls, old grudges and a long memory, who swear to things and take their oaths seriously. They would have a custom of judgment, a place for witnesses, and a name for the one who breaks his word. I would ask them to swear in public, to keep what they swear, and to bring me the oaths that are broken so I may test them. I would give them a judge who will not look away first, a voice that carries over the crowd, and a hand that will break an unjust door. I do not need them to love me. I need them to try me, and to keep the hard sentence when I give it.
