# The Cutfolk: record

Campaign: sim-01. The people of `g3-song`. The agreed record, written by the Keeper on creation Night 3 from the god's hope (`gods/g3-song/people-hoped.md`), fitted to what the land and its past hold. Names of people and places are the world's.

## Who they are
Human. The people of the cuts of the lower river Sarn, at the landward edge of the Sarnmoss. They call themselves the Cutfolk. About forty steadings of one to three households on raised ground, joined by causeways and boats. They gather in the cold months at the Long Roof on the island of Eelholm.

## How many
About 1,500, as the god hoped.

## How they live
Peat, eels, geese, wildfowl, reed, flat boats. Bronze is traded for. Salt is their hardest want: the Murk is too wet to boil it, and what they have comes from Sarnmouth, four days by boat, which has it by ship from the far south-east, at a price, so they smoke more than they salt.

## Their code
- They tell the truth to those they eat with. A liar sits at the door end until it is mended.
- They name their dead aloud every autumn, all of them, in order.
- A sworn word is kept or paid for, and they swear rarely.
- No one is turned from the Long Roof in winter.
- No lament is sung for gain and no verse to flatter.

## Skills and culture
Boat-craft, causeways, eel and fowl craft, peat-cutting, smoking. Songs for work, the dead, the harvest, the road. Quarrels are sung by each side and the table decides which sounded true. Ordinary. Not priced.

## Priced edge: the lineage singers
The edge: three at a time (at the lock: Old Wenna Marl, Tobin Sedge, and the girl Isk) can hold the whole lineage, law and list of the dead word for word. Few peoples have it.
The price, as the god offered and the Keeper accepted without change: each singer is worn thin. They are short-lived (none in memory has passed fifty), sickly, and poor at holding anything outside song, so that a singer may forget a debt, a face or the road home. The people must feed and watch them. When one dies before teaching a successor, the part only she held is gone. This is a trained skill and its price, not mortal magic, and it is priced as a skill.

## Their magic
One worker: the woman they call Reed-Hanna, on the outermost steading, who can call a lost boat in out of fog. She cannot taste. Respected, not owned.

## What they believe about gods
Old powers in the water and fog that do not care for people. A bowl is left out; no one prays.

## Their history in the land
The Sarn country has been drowned and drained more than once. The Cutfolk have been on the cuts about 700 years, and their oldest lineage verses name steadings that are now open water. The squared stones that run into the water are far older than they are; the Cutfolk call them the Drowned Road and do not follow it.

## Hierarchy
None, by the god's refusal and their own habit. Household heads decide at the table; singers advise.

## How the god meets them
Already there, not waiting. Reconciled with the deep history. The god lands in autumn, the households beginning to move stores to Eelholm.

## What they know of the land

By `map.json` id. Released to the god as it is told; raised on its map only then.

Reported: cuts, sarnmoss, sarnvale, the-ribs, drowned-road, sarn, sarnmouth-town. Rumored: Murkhaven and Westhaven; the red hills north (low-breaks); a walled ford town far upriver (corrford-town); the black plain and its city east (wester-plain, maundsea-city); "a lintel under the water" (singing-door).
