# The Lavefolk: record

Campaign: sim-01. The people of `g6-dreams`. The agreed record, written by the Keeper on creation Night 3 from the god's hope (`gods/g6-dreams/people-hoped.md`), fitted to what the land and its past hold. Names of people and places are the world's.

## Who they are
Human. River farmers and herders of the lower Lave, in hamlets and single steadings along both banks and up the side valleys, with a market cluster at the old bridge, called Piers. They call themselves the Lavefolk.

## How many
About 2,000; some 300 at Piers.

## How they live
Plough, hay, pollard willow, sheep and cattle, fishing, reed cutting downstream. Mending, weaving, midwifery, bronze and wood. Winter tales told house to house.

## Their code
- A lamp is kept lit in the window of every house through the dark hours, and a traveler who knocks is let in and fed.
- A promise is kept to the letter. A broken oath is said aloud and paid for.
- Sleep is not interfered with. No hand is laid on a household at night.
- A name is said over every child at bed, and the dead are named at the hearth once a year.

## Skills and culture
Thatch and timber, marsh knowledge, ledgers on notched sticks and clay, good beds. The mounds beside the houses, where older houses stood, are tended and not dug. Ordinary. Not priced. The lamp custom is theirs and older than the god; under ruling L-0002 a lamp set for travelers in general is no invitation.

## Edges
None proposed. None had.

## Their magic
One worker: Bryn Tolley, a woman in the side valley of Sallowbeck, who can quiet a fever by sitting with it. She pays in sleep and has not slept a night through in years.

## What they believe about gods
That powers walk the night and a stranger at a lit door might be one. They tell of a tall guest who sat at a table once and left the lamp burning with no oil in it. They have had no god of their own; the tale is from the lake kingdom's time.

## Their history in the land
The longest unbroken habitation on the sheet: hearth on hearth for 3,000 years, the house mounds the proof. They were the south-east march of the lake kingdom at its height and have had no master since it drew back, 500 years ago. The free salt town of Whitepans, nine days east along the coast, sells them salt; Maundsea claims the right to and cannot enforce it.

## Hierarchy
Elders in each hamlet. A lamp-keeper at Piers for the bridge lamp, chosen by the town: at the lock, Odo Marrin. The god refused a priesthood.

## How the god meets them
Already there, not waiting. Reconciled with the deep history. The god lands at dusk in autumn and watches before it comes to any door.
