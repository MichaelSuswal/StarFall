# The River Households: record

Campaign: sim-01. The people of `g5-fate`. The agreed record, written by the Keeper on creation Night 3 from the god's hope (`gods/g5-fate/people-hoped.md`), fitted to what the land and its past hold. Names of people and places are the world's.

## Who they are
Human. The River Households of the middle Ishe, in the low hills called the Folds. About thirty long houses and their dependents along both banks and up the side valleys, each house several generations under one roof. Their meeting house and the flood post stand at Postlanding, at the crossroads on the south bank.

## How many
About 3,000.

## How they live
Terraces and orchards on the south-facing slopes, hill pasture, flat boats, the ferry and the towpath.

## Their code
- A sworn word is kept, and names what stands behind it.
- Disputes go to the open count, never to the knife.
- A debt does not die with the debtor. The living finish the promises of the dead.
- No one builds below the highest mark on the post, and no one cuts the marks.
- One who has failed and says so plainly is given terms, once.

## Skills and culture
Boat building, orchards, herding, weaving, a little bronze. The unfinished promises of the dead are recited at the winter solstice. Ordinary. Not priced.

## Priced edge: exact books
The edge: split tallies, clay tokens and trained memory kept so well that old debts and boundaries can be settled generations later without a fight. A skill, nothing ahead of the age.
The price, as the god offered and the Keeper accepted without change: they are grudge-bearing and litigious. A wrong once counted is never struck out. Feuds over inherited debts run for generations and tie households up in the count: at any time about one house in five is in open dispute with another and will not work beside it.

## Their magic
One worker: Corwen Ashe, an old man in the side valley of Thornfold, who can find a drowned body. He has lost the names of his own dead. The Households ask what he gave, not what he can do.

## What they believe about gods
That the water and the weather keep a book and that it is, on the whole, fair. No named face. They have had no god.

## Their history in the land
The longest unbroken tally in the east: the flood post at Postlanding is the seventh post, each copied from the last, and carries 640 years of marks. Towns on this river have moved up and down the slopes as the river moved for 2,500 years; the green banks on the hilltops are their ancestors'. The Households have no master. The Tarsk clans north of the Folds trade them salt and horses and have raided them twice in living memory.

## Hierarchy
A council of household heads at the crossroads, and the Reckoner, chosen by them, who keeps the common count. At the lock the Reckoner is Hesper Vane, a woman of sixty. The office is older than the god and is not the god's.

## How the god meets them
Already there, not waiting, and by the god's own wish not met: it means to stay unseen. Reconciled with the deep history. The god lands mid-harvest in morning fog.
