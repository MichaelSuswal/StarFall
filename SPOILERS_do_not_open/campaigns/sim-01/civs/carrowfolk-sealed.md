# The Carrowfolk: what the public record does not hold

Campaign: sim-01. The public record is `P/civs/carrowfolk/record.md`. This file holds the world's side of it, fixed at the lock.

- "The ones before" were the Hollin (deep history, Layer 8). The oldest people in the three upper hamlets know the outline: that there was a sickness, that the dead were found on the hill and buried in the fold above Tophouses, and that it was agreed not to speak of it. They do not know where the sickness came from. They believe the Hollin "turned on each other", which is half of it. No one living knows the name Hollin except as a word in one lullaby.
- The two bands. The three upper hamlets descend from the pass band, the rest from the Wend band. Each tells it that they came first. Both came within two summers.
- The stone-wife is Aud Tarn, seventy-three, of Tophouses. Her mother's line runs back to Mab Hollin, who stayed. She does not know it. Her knack and its price are as the DM ruled; under the world's rule each year she works it she loses a little more of her way (S3).
- The girl who comes up for the ewes on the first morning is Edda Marle, fifteen, Aud Tarn's great-niece.
- The small cairn by the worn way at the valley head was begun eleven years ago for a shepherd, Coll Marle, Edda's uncle, who died there in a spring storm. Tophouses people add a stone when they pass.
- The Sallow's pan fever flares in Year 4 (event schedule). The Carrowfolk have never met it; the Hollin's hardening died with them.
- Ninth-of-the-Door (cast) looks down into the valley from the Old Men one night each ninth month. Three living herders have seen a tall grey figure on the pass at night and each thinks it was one of the Old Men, seen wrong.
