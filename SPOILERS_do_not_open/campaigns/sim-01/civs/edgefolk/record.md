# The Edgefolk: record

Campaign: sim-01. The people of `g7-beasts`. The agreed record, written by the Keeper on creation Night 3 from the god's hope (`gods/g7-beasts/people-hoped.md`), fitted to what the land and its past hold. Names of people and places are the world's.

## Who they are
Human. Herders and hunters of the line where the Dimmet forest meets the High Grass. They call themselves the Edgefolk. Seven steadings along the forest edge, each a few households round a timber hall, and the great hall, Antlerhall, where all gather at autumn's end. Herd camps go out on the grass in summer.

## How many
About 2,000.

## How they live
Cattle, horses and sheep on the grass; hunting with dog and bow in the forest; salting, smoking, hides, timber; trade along the edge track.

## Their code
- A stranger at the door is fed before he is asked anything.
- A bargain named is kept, and a broken word is told aloud in the hall.
- Weights are true. A cheat at the scale is put out of the trade.
- No one is turned away without a cause that can be said at the table.
- The winter store is shared before any household starves.

## Skills and culture
Herding, tracking, salting, hide work, tally cords. Accounts are reckoned at the long table at autumn's end. Ordinary. Not priced.

## Edges
None proposed. None had.

## Their magic
One worker: Halla Dun, who rides with the summer camps and can turn a stampede by standing in front of it. Each time she has lost a year's memory of one person. Paid in beasts.

## What they believe about gods
They tell of a stranger who comes to the door in autumn and remembers how he was treated. They have had no god.

## Their history in the land
On the edge about 500 years, pushed north off the open grass when the Tarsk clans came. They buy their salt from the Tarsk, who hold Lake Sere, and pay in hides and timber; twice in the last sixty years the Tarsk have shut the salt to press them. The mounds on the grass are older than both peoples.

## Hierarchy
The headwoman of Antlerhall holds the head of the long table; at the lock she is Marra Voss, fifty-one, a hunter. Steading elders advise her. The god refused a priest class and hopes she keeps her seat.

## How the god meets them
Already there, not waiting. Reconciled with the deep history. The god lands in autumn, eight days before the gathering at Antlerhall.

## What they know of the land

By `map.json` id. Released to the god as it is told; raised on its map only then.

Reported: edge, wester-edge, easter-edge, hallwood, sere-grass, serewell, sere, edge-mounds, farhall, tarrow. Rumored: the deep wood north (deep-dimmet); a bear's ground west in the wood (marrowden); a dry grey mountain north-west (vorn-peak); river people far south who grow apples (folds); grass with no kinds in it, far east (off the sheet).
