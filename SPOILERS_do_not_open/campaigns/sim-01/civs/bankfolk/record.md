# The Bankfolk: record

Campaign: sim-01. The people of `g4-harvest`. The agreed record, written by the Keeper on creation Night 3 from the god's hope (`gods/g4-harvest/people-hoped.md`), fitted to what the land and its past hold. Names of people and places are the world's.

## Who they are
Human. Farmers of the middle Maund on the Black Plain. They call themselves the Bankfolk. Ten villages on raised banks and old mounds along both sides of the river, the largest Stonesteading, beside the roofless stone ruin they call the Old Bins. The lone hill a morning's walk north-east is the Howe.

## How many
About 3,000.

## How they live
Barley, flax and fallow in rotation, ox teams, shared dykes and ditches, granaries on staddle stones. Scrub and wood, the Westwild, begin a day's walk west.

## Their code
- Seed is laid back first. No household eats the seed.
- The count is said aloud and true. A false count is worse than theft.
- A sworn word is kept at cost.
- A stranger is owed one meal and one night. After that he is counted among the eaters.
- No one cuts the last sheaf alone.

## Skills and culture
Plain farming done well. A midwinter meal where each house names what it has left. Village moots with a reckoner of precedent. Stories of the Old Bins, and some shame at not knowing who built them. Ordinary. Not priced.

## Edges
None proposed. None had.

## Their magic
One worker: Aldith Crane of Reedditch, who can tell from a handful of soil what it will bear. She has not dreamed in twenty years. Treated like a hard winter: with care.

## What they believe about gods
That something walks the stubble at the year's end, the Stubble Man, and is not to be thanked loudly. They have had no god.

## Their history in the land
The Black Plain has been the granary of whoever held it for four thousand years. The Bankfolk are the plain's own farmers under their fourth set of masters. For 190 years they have owed Maundsea, the city at the river's mouth, a levy of one sheaf in eight, taken by the king's tallymen each autumn and carried down by barge. In three bad years out of the last twenty the levy took seed. That is the grievance. They built their own bins after the second.

## Hierarchy
A moot in each village. The god hopes for one Counter to each village; four villages already keep such a person, called the bin-reeve, to stand against Maundsea's tallymen.

## How the god meets them
Already there, not waiting. Reconciled with the deep history. The god lands at the end of autumn harvest, eleven days before Maundsea's levy barges are due.

## What they know of the land

By `map.json` id. Released to the god as it is told; raised on its map only then.

Reported: bankland, upper-plain, low-plain, wester-plain, maund, howe, old-bins, maundsea-city, barrow-down. Rumored: the Gap and the lake people upriver (the-gap, ennerhold, enner); the Westwild; three roofs on the east plain (three-bins); the sea; a salt town far east along the coast (whitepans).
