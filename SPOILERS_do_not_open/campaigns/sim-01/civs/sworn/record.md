# The Sworn of Corrford: record

Campaign: sim-01. The people of `g2-law`. The agreed record, written by the Keeper on creation Night 3 from the god's hope (`gods/g2-law/people-hoped.md`), fitted to what the land and its past hold. Names of people and places are the world's.

## Who they are
Human. The people of Corrford, the walled town on the bluff above the ford where the river Corr leaves its gorge, and of about thirty holdings along a day's walk of river either way. They call themselves the Fordmen; in law, and when they mean to be taken seriously, the Sworn.

## How many
About 3,000: some 1,800 in and under the walls, the rest in the holdings.

## How they live
Flood-plain barley and flax, hill flocks, fishing in the channels, stone hauling, and the ford itself: tolls, marker posts, guides in spate. The town holds the market, the hall and the tally posts.

## Their code
- A sworn word is sworn aloud, before witnesses, with something named behind it.
- An oath-breaker is named in the hall and carries the name; he may earn it off.
- Boasts and promises in passing are not oaths.
- A seal, door or boundary stone set by oath is not touched in anger.
- The guest at the ford is not robbed there.

## Skills and culture
Stone and timber, bronze, ferrying and flood lore, tally cords, notched posts, long genealogies. Old law and new law stacked, and argument over which governs. Many proverbs. Ordinary. Not priced.

## Edges
None proposed. None had.

## Their magic
One worker: Maddoc Rill, a ferryman's widow living at the gorge mouth, who can say where the river will cut next spring. She no longer recognizes her own sons' voices. Sought out at the thaw.

## What they believe about gods
That gods are real and late, and are judged like neighbors, by whether their word holds. They half remember that the lake kingdom far to the east had a god. The lower courses of the bluff wall are older than the Fordmen, and they argue whether a god laid them. No god did (deep history).

## Their history in the land
The river is one water with three names: the Wend above the gorge, the Corr at the ford, the Sarn below the red hills. The ford has been held by nine kingdoms in three thousand years; the Fordmen are the tenth holders and have had it about 420 years, since the last of the Corr kings died without an heir and the town's guilds swore the Ford Peace among themselves. That oath is kept. They pay no tribute, but their salt comes two ways, both long: sea salt up the river from Sarnmouth of the Three Havens, three weeks by boat and portage, and brine salt down the Wend from the Sallow, four weeks by pack. Sarnmouth presses them on tolls.

## Hierarchy
A council of the guilds and holdings, called the Hall, with a speaker chosen yearly. The god hopes for a Bench of witnesses and wardens; the Hall is the nearest thing that exists and is not the god's.

## How the god meets them
Already there, not waiting. Reconciled with the deep history. The god lands in spring spate, with the lower holdings cut off and the ford guides busy.

## What they know of the land

By `map.json` id. Released to the god as it is told; raised on its map only then.

Rumored: the Sallow and Brinehall up the Wend; Sarnmouth and the Havens down the water (sarn, sarnmouth-town); the lake kingdom east over the hills (wester-ennerwold, enner, ennerhold); the dark wood north-west (holt); herders at the water's head (carrow, as "the high valleys").
