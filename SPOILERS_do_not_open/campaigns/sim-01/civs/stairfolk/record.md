# The Stairfolk: record

Campaign: sim-01. The people of `g1-stone`. The agreed record, written by the Keeper on creation Night 3 from the god's hope (`gods/g1-stone/people-hoped.md`), fitted to what the land and its past hold. Names of people and places are the world's.

## Who they are
Human highlanders of the valley of Stair, the head of the river Enn on the south face of the Skarn. They call themselves the Stairfolk, from the stepped track up to the pass they call the High Stair. Six steadings of stone houses built against the rock, strung down eleven miles of valley: Topsteading, Gullet, Sheerwall, Middle Stair, Pinehouse and Low Stair. No town.

## How many
About 800. The god hoped for about eight hundred and that is what the valley holds.

## How they live
Terraced barley and beans on the sunny side, goats and sheep taken up with the thaw, felt, cheese, a little bronze bought with wool. Each steading has a granary dug into the hill. Salt comes along the foot of the Skarn from the Sallow in the open months, a four-week road, two or three pack trains a summer, and is dear. The High Stair goes north over the range into country they call the Backlands and do not visit.

## Their code
- A stranger who comes cold to the door is fed and housed, and then watched openly until he is known.
- Salt is shared, and a bargain sealed over salt is kept.
- Stores before pleasure. The granary is counted by two people from two houses.
- No steading is left to a bad winter. Those who can walk take in those who cannot.
- A bad order is turned aside quietly.

## Skills and culture
Drystone walling, terracing, herding, felt, reading snow and slope. The winter-count is kept by notches on a standing stone at Middle Stair; it has 311 notches and the oldest are worn nearly smooth. Short songs, slow speech. Ordinary. Not priced.

## Edges
None proposed. None had.

## Their magic
One worker: Ysolt Garr of Gullet, about fifty, who can tell a day ahead which gully will slide. She has lost her sense of cold and has twice been found frostbitten without knowing it. The steadings feed her. She is the only known worker between the Sallow and Vorn.

## What they believe about gods
That the mountain listens and old stones are owed courtesy. They have never had a god. They know by trade talk that the lake people far downriver once had one and lost it, and they think that a warning about lowland pride.

## Their history in the land
In Stair about 310 winters by the stone, come up the Enn from the Ennerwold's north slopes when the lake kingdom's levies grew heavy. The next valley east, Hask, held their kin until the Winter of the Shut Stair, 140 years ago, when slides closed both its ways out in the eleventh month and no one of its four steadings was alive at the thaw. Stairfolk reached Hask in spring and buried 212. No one has lived in Hask since. Every Stair child is told it. This is why no steading is left to a bad winter.

## Hierarchy
Elders in each steading. The god refused a throne and a priesthood and hopes for keepers of the door chosen by each steading. None exists yet; whether the steadings choose any is theirs.

## How the god meets them
Already there, with their own history, not waiting. Reconciled with the deep history: nothing in it forbids the meeting. The god lands in early spring above Topsteading, the passes still shut, the granaries at their lowest of the year.

## What they know of the land

By `map.json` id. Released to the god as it is told; raised on its map only then.

Reported (they go there): stair, hask, upper-ennvale, stair-heights, high-stair, hask-steadings, all six steadings. Rumored: the Underskarn track west to the salt people (underskarn, sallow, brinehall); Ennvale and "the lake people who had a god" (ennvale, fallwater, enner); the Backlands over the High Stair (off the sheet); a dry mountain far east that ravens will not land on (vorn-peak); a deep wood east (skarnwood).
