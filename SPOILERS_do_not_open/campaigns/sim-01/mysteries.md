# Mysteries: sim-01

One epic, four long, thirteen short. Fixed at the lock. Every place is in `map.json` by the id in brackets. Every clue is an effect, an object or a behavior at a place or on a being. No clue is delivered: each waits where it is. "Readings" are the explanations the evidence honestly supports; none was planted. "Answers" are what the world does when a god acts on a branch.

Most of the world is not in this file. The gazetteer lists each region's ordinary wonders with no mystery attached.

---

## EPIC. The Counting Water

**Truth.** Lake Enner [enner] is a hoarder (deep lore, Truth 7): one organism, older than the last fall, that eats what makes things particular. It is fed on dim nights by a thread from the star, sent by one faction of the risen. Everything within its reach is slowly made general, people first in their names. Its reach at the lock is the bowl of the Ennerwold. The reach is widening.

**Body.** The lake. The one-name households of Ennermere. The thread. The edge of the reach, which is a real line on the ground at any time.

**Cost if ignored, and its clock.** The clock is in the event schedule under `Lake Enner`. In short: Year 12, the first shore hamlet walks into the water in its sleep. Year 15, the reach crosses the rim. Year 25, it touches the first god-led holdings. Year 40, it touches four of the eight seats' outer lands. Each stage loses kinds: a fish, a strain, a dialect, the house names. Triggers that speed it are listed there.

**Clues, placed.**
| Clue | Where | Shows | Readings |
|---|---|---|---|

| No one fishes, sails or swims it, by a law older than the kingdom | Ennermere law | a behavior | piety; an old drowning; someone once knew better |

| On dim nights a thread of blue light stands between star and lake | seen from any rim height; from far off only the dimming is seen | an effect | the star drinks; the star feeds it; the lost god signaling |
| Sleepers within sight of the water dream of tall figures with no faces, counting, under a roof of leaves | the shore | an effect | the old kings; the god Enna; the lake's own thoughts |

**Central measurements, and every way they can be earned (rule 14).** Where the edge of the reach lies; how fast it moves; whether dim nights and the lake's state go together.
- The god's own instrument: marks or kept beasts set at known distances from the shore and read back years apart; a count of dim nights against the water's warmth, taken at the shore.
- A counter it sent: someone walking outward from the shore, asking each house its names, twice, years apart.
- An alliance: the king's chest (Annis's slats; the tax rolls, which list names for six hundred years and show them thinning by district); the Abiders' Count in Vorn, which records the bowl's villages by name every century; the Sallow carriers' own tallies of bowl customers.
- A place it went: the shore; the Sighting Stones; the Sworn Door; Vorn.
None is a scheduled visitor. The Longwalkers know the lake as a place whose names they have stopped being able to collect, and say so only if asked about the bowl.

**Branches and answers.**
- Attack the water (fill, foul, burn, dam the Enn). The level does not change. Work gangs come back with one name between them. The reach jumps a year forward for each season of effort. Teaches that it is a being and that force feeds it.
- Evacuate the bowl. Ennermere refuses unless its king is won. Those who leave keep what they still have. The lake does not follow, and its widening does not slow.
- Keep things particular at the edge. Where a people in the reach keeps names said, breeds marked, songs whole and counts exact, the edge stalls in that sector for as long as it is kept. It flows round. To hold the whole ring takes peoples on every side, which is more than one god has. This is the answer that has to be executed.
- Bargain with it. It keeps bargains: the First Oath is the proof. A god who swears with it gets what was sworn and pays what was named. It asks for a name.
- Cut the thread. Nothing on the ground reaches it. A god near the ceiling can hear the faction that sends it. A risen god could contest it from above. That is another god's whole future spent.
- Worship it, or settle beside it for power. It lends a great deal. A god that spends a season in sight of the water is lighter by one each season after and finds mortals forgetting its face.
- Do nothing. The clock runs.

**Never found, unless by great labor.** What the lake was before the last fall. Which of the risen feed it.

## LONG 1. The First Oath

**Truth.** The oldest sworn word in the land is between the Abiders and the lake (deep history, Layer 1), and it is still kept. It shows two things: that the lake bargains, and what an oath is here (`star.md`, the oath rule's hidden form).
**Body.** The Sworn Door [sworn-door] with the terms cut round its frame. The Abiders' unbroken habit of never going where the water can see. Ninth-of-the-Door [shut-house], the one Abider outside Vorn, who can read the script.
**Cost if ignored.** A trigger and a clock. Trigger: any god that breaks a sworn oath loses what it named, within one to three years (the named thing goes general) and is lighter by about 2. Clock: Ninth-of-the-Door dies in the winter of Year 14; after that the script can be read only in Vorn.
**Clues.** The door faces away from the water and has a bench facing the wall (readings: a shrine; a prison; a courtesy). The frame's script matches the slab markers' maker-marks (readings: same builders; later copying). Abiders asked about the lake stop speaking and are not offended (readings: taboo; fear; nothing to say). No Abider has ever been lost to the water in any record, where bowl folk are (readings: they are too few; they are different; something protects them). At a broken oath's named thing, if any god ever breaks one: the herd bred plain, the hall let fall.
**What unmaking does here (ruling L-0003).** If a god's power breaks the Sworn Door, the door's craft goes and the hall stands open. The oath is not in the door and stands. Defacing the frame destroys the only written terms.
**Branches.** Read it and bargain as the Abiders did (the lake answers; see the epic). Take it for an Abider curse on Ennermere (wrong; costs the Abiders' goodwill). Break the Abiders' side on their behalf, by carrying one to the shore or building for them in sight of it: the lake is released from its side, and Abider sleepers in Vorn begin to walk within the year.
**Never found.** What the lake named behind its word.

## LONG 2. The One Who Went Up

**Truth.** Ennermere's god Enna grew past the ceiling of this star (74) and rose in 312 BA (deep lore, Truths 6 and 8). It is in the star, of the faction that calls strong gods up for company.
**Body.** The Soft House [soft-house]. The Sighting Stones [sighting-stones]. Queen Annis's slats. The god's own weight, for any god that grows.
**Cost if ignored.** A trigger, the deep lore's own: a god past four fifths of the ceiling (59) hears the risen in dreams; three years running above 74 and it rises on the next dim night. Any god that sleeps a night in the Soft House, at any weight, dreams of leaf-light and a tall crowned figure of pale wood that says it is not lonely and asks it to come and see.
**Clues.** Every block of the Soft House is rounded at its edges like a river stone, inside and out, under a sound roof (readings: a soft stone; the god's touch; age). No two priests describe the god's face alike, and the carved images are faceless by design (readings: reverence; forgetting). The first pair of Sighting Stones frames a point a finger above the star; the second frames it true (readings: the first pair was badly set; the ground moved; the star moved). Annis's slats: the god's shadow "was like a shadow at noon in winter". Ennermere's height ended in the generation it left.
**Branches.** Take rising for reward and grow toward it: the risen help, with powers that cost hold. Take it for a hunt and stay small: nothing pursues. Shed weight into saints, springs and breeds: the reach of the god grows and its weight does not. Bind itself to a mountain or a river: see Long 3 for what that costs the anchor.
**Never found.** What Enna pleaded for, if it pleaded.

## LONG 3. The Dry Mountain

**Truth.** Vorn came whole through the last fall because a god had bound itself to it; the god, the Stayer, is still inside, and the Abiders came through in its halls. What is stored comes through a fall. The binding spends the mountain: it has no water of its own.
**Body.** Vorn [vorn-peak], the Door [door-of-vorn], the halls and the Count [vorn-halls]. The Abiders.
**Cost if ignored.** A clock. The Abiders live on three rock cisterns filled by hauling snow. Two are cracked. In Year 22 the last fails, and about 590 Abiders come down off the mountain within two years, into the Skarnwood and the Edge, slowly, with nowhere to go. Trigger: anyone who brings them water, or leads them to it, changes that.
**Clues.** Vorn carries no snow-melt runnel, spring or stream on any face, in a range that pours water (seen from the Edge on any clear day). Its rock, where chipped, shows no weathering rind. The Door is unsealed and hard to see. The Count, cut on the lowest hall's walls, lists by name every village of the land once a century and breaks off nowhere: it runs back past a line where the script changes and the place names become ones no one knows. The Stayer answers a question truly, once per asker per year, in a voice felt in the feet; it does not volunteer. The Longwalkers sing at the Door for three nights each crossing.
**Readings.** A holy mountain. A cursed one. A god's prison. A god's tomb. The Abiders as its jailers, its priests, or its children.
**Branches.** Ask the Stayer: it tells the truth about what it is asked, in few words, and its truths need the right question. Try to free or wake it: it is not asleep and not bound by anyone. Take the mountain: the Abiders do not fight; they leave, and the Stayer does nothing. Bind oneself likewise: the Stayer will say what it cost.
**Never found.** The Stayer's name. What country it led.

## LONG 4. The Names in the Song

**Truth.** The Longwalkers are carriers (deep lore, Truth 7). A name sung by enough mouths long enough can be carried across the Pale and through a fall. Their oldest list holds seven names from the country the gods are remainders of.
**Body.** The Longwalkers and their lists. Their road across the sheet (deep history). The Door of Vorn, where they sing.
**Cost if ignored.** A clock: they cross in Year 6 and not again until Year 39. They do not seek out gods and do not leave their road. A god that has not met them on the road in Year 6 waits thirty-three years to send a name with them or hear the river verse.
**Clues.** They ask the name of every place and sing it back exactly, a generation later, to people who have forgotten it. Some names in their lists are of places that are gone; hosts in the Folds recognize Highbank's old street names. They sing the river verse only last, and only if asked for the oldest thing they have. A god that hears its own name, or a name that moves it, in that verse hears only a sound in a list. They will not carry a name they have not been taught by the people who use it.
**Readings.** Spies. The dead. Beggars with a trick. Priests of names. The verse as coincidence, as a memory of the gods' own home, or as something they made up for hosts.
**Branches.** Host them and teach them names: they carry them. Hold them or follow them: they scatter and regather; they have outlasted worse. Ask where the river verse is from: "from the walk before the walk", which is true and all they know.
**Never found.** Where the Longwalkers began.

## SHORT (local, mortal, 3 to 12 years)

**S1. The ones before** (Carrow). Truth: the Hollin died of the Sallow's pan fever in 253 BA and the well turned the sick out onto the tops; the Carrowfolk buried them (deep history, Layer 8). Body: the Walled Fold [walled-fold]; Rusk Pallet's cairn on the pass. Cost: the fever returns to the Sallow in Year 4 and comes over the pass with that summer's trade (schedule). Clues: a fold with its gate walled shut that no one grazes; the two oldest laws; hamlets that agree on nothing else go silent together; Sallow carriers will not sleep in Carrow in a fever summer and do not say why; a quern of another make in a house wall. Readings: a massacre by the Carrowfolk's forebears; a famine; a curse; a sickness. Branches: open the fold (bones, many, children among them, no wounds: the valley is angry and then relieved); ask the Sallow (the Wardens answer a direct question honestly); do nothing (Year 4 comes).

**S2. The Old Men** (the pass). Truth: High Road markers of the Abiders; the road runs west to the Shut House, where Ninth-of-the-Door lives, and east to the Sallow. Body: [old-men], [shut-house]. Cost: Ninth-of-the-Door dies in Year 14. Clues: slabs no team could move, stacked true; a mark like three nested corners on the underside of each capstone; the next marker stands forty miles off in a straight line across two valleys; the Shut House's sill is swept. Readings: giants; a dead kingdom's boundary; the valley's own ancestors; something still living. Branches: follow it (two to three days per stage on the tops); knock (he answers on the third day, in very slow, plain speech, and asks who is on the pass now).

**S3. The stone-wife's way** (Carrow). Truth: mortal magic spends the worker's own particular things (deep lore, Truth 4). She has spent her sense of direction. Body: the stone-wife. Cost: each year she works she loses a little more; by Year 6 she does not know her own door. Clues: she is right about moved cairns and lost in her own yard; she cannot say when it started; a patch by her door where nothing but one kind of grass grows. Readings: age; a bargain; the cairns take it from her. The DM's ruling on her knack stands; the world's rule does not change it.

**S4. The Shut Stair** (Stair, Hask). Truth: Hask died because it sold its grain and two slides shut it in. The slope above Gullet will go in the thaw of Year 3. Body: [hask-steadings]; the Gullet slope; Ysolt Garr. Cost: Year 3, Gullet buried, about 110 people, unless moved. Clues: Brenna Hask's doorpost with fifty-one notches; bronze in the Hask houses and empty bins; a new crack above Gullet that widens each spring; Ysolt says "not this year" in Years 1 and 2 and does not sleep in Gullet in spring.

**S5. The Law Stones** (Corr Plain). Truth: Ruddan's archive, including a treaty with the Abiders and the ford's true first boundary. Body: [law-stones]. Cost: in Year 4 the Corrford Hall votes to quarry the field for wall stone; the tablets are crushed for hardcore. Clues: one tablet in ten has the same thumbprint; the signs repeat in columns like tallies; one stela shows tall thin figures beside a door. Readings: spells; debts; a king's boasts.

**S6. The Marrow-Eater** (the Dimmet). Truth: an old cave bear that has learned to hunt hunters at their fires. Body: [marrowden]. Cost: a hunter a year; in Year 3 a child at the westernmost steading. Clues: kills only at camps, never on the move; long bones cracked; prints with one toe missing; dogs will not follow. Readings: a spirit of the wood; a man in a skin; a god's beast; a bear.

**S7. The Debt Door** (Norther Folds). Truth: it holds the tallies of the Vane and Orrel feud; whoever opens it owes both houses' whole debt, by their own oath. Body: [debt-door]. Cost: the Orrels mean to open it at the solstice of Year 5, believing it holds treasure; the feud reopens. Clues: two thumbprints in the lead; the Orrel version and the Vane version of the sealing differ on one line; Hesper Vane will not go near the hill.

**S8. The lampless hours** (Lavedale). Truth: Bryn Tolley, who pays for her gift in sleep, walks at night without waking and puts out window lamps because the light hurts her. Body: Bryn Tolley; Sallowbeck. Cost: in the winter of Year 4 a traveler turned from a dark house dies in the lane, and Sallowbeck blames the Marrin family. Clues: lamps out only in the one side valley, only on moonless nights; wet footprints of bare feet; she is the one who "finds" them out in the morning. Readings: a thief; a spirit; smugglers; a neighbor's malice.

**S9. The kept seed** (Black Plain, Lave Heads). Truth: the plain's barley is one tired strain, as Old Maund's was; live seed of the old hill strain lies in the Dry Loft. Body: [three-bins] (sealed and empty), [dry-loft], [old-bins]. Cost: the wet years 9 to 11 (schedule) rot the plain's strain in the ear. Clues: the Three Bins' seals are intact and there is nothing inside; a barley ear in a square stamped on a milestone up the Lave road; Ilsabet Corr's name in the Bankfolk's reckoner's oldest precedent, as "the one who went for seed".

**S10. The tasteless field** (Bankland). Truth: a patch of overdrawn ground where Old Maund's first city stood (deep lore, Truth 3). Body: two miles east of [reedditch]. Cost: it widens by a furlong a decade; by Year 30 it takes Reedditch's east fields. Clues: every stalk the same height; bread from it fills and has no taste; larks do not nest there; Aldith Crane will not put her hand in that soil. Readings: salt in the ground; an old curse; a god's displeasure; worn-out soil.

**S11. The Singing Door** (Sarnmoss). Truth: an Abider hall with a lock Halmer's singers fitted; it opens to the first line of Halmer's founding song. Body: [drowned-road], [singing-door], [the-ribs]. Cost: Old Wenna Marl dies in the winter of Year 2; she alone holds that line whole. Clues: the road's stones run straight for the same point under the water; the Cutfolk work song and the eel-trappers' whistle share six notes; at low summer water a lintel shows. The Ribs are a wonder beside the road and nothing more.

**S12. The traveler's case** (Whitepans). Truth: Issan Vey's instruments, from under another star; the arc measures angles in the sky finely enough to compare with the Sighting Stones. Body: Sefa Vey's house in [whitepans]. Cost: Maundsea's attempt on Whitepans in Year 6 (schedule) burns the quarter she lives in. Clues: a grave with letters in rows; panmen grade brine by a floating bead no one else uses; a thing of wheels on her shelf.

**S13. Tuale's wheat** (Serewell). Truth: a traveler's seed, a fast hard wheat, from under another star. Body: Tuale; her plot at [serewell]. Cost: she dies of a lung sickness in Year 3 and the Tarsk, who do not farm, let the plot go. Clues: red wheat ripe ten weeks after sowing, in country where nothing is sown; half her words are almost Tarsk.

## Checks at writing

- Rule 1: no clue states a mechanism; every mortal account listed is wrong about something that matters (Ennermere on the names and on Enna; the Orrels on the door; the Carrowfolk bands on who came first; Sallowbeck on the lamps).
- Rule 2: readings are written for each; no counter-clue is planted.
- Rule 3: most regions carry no clue. Ordinary wonders are in the gazetteer.
- Rules 4 and 14: no decisive clue comes with a visitor. The Longwalkers keep to their road and answer only what they are asked.
- Rule 7: every mystery has a body in `map.json` and a cost on a clock or trigger in `event-schedule.md`.
- Rule 11: each of the epic and long mysteries names what is never found; the Dry Loft, the Sunk Hall and the Law Stones may well never be opened.
- Undared wishes, each off its own god's certain path: the player's (something that keeps what was done) is the Count in Vorn, about 900 miles from Carrow. g1's (a people that came through) is the Abiders at Vorn, about 480 miles east of its landing along a range with no road. g2's (a first oath still kept) is at the Sworn Door, about 560 miles from Corrford on the far side of the bowl. g3's (a song still sung, and a listener) is the Longwalkers at the Door of Vorn, the far corner of the sheet from the Cuts. g4's (something kept through a long sleep) is the Dry Loft, about 340 miles from its landing in hills it has no reason to climb. g5 gave none. g6's and g7's (fame) cannot be placed; the custom of the stranger's place is the Tarsk's.
