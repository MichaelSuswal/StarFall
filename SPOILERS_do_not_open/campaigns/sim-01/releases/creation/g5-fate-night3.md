## Your country, as you first see it

You come down in autumn at the bend of a middling river, in a country of low rounded hills that fold in close around the water on both sides. It is early, and fog lies in the valley floor so that the hilltops stand out of it like islands; it will burn off by midday and come back at dusk. The river is steady, deep enough for boats, with a towpath worn along one bank.

What the land itself shows: long houses along the banks and in the folds of the hills, each with one smoke from the middle of the roof; landing stages with flat boats tied up; terraced plots and orchards on the south-facing slopes; flood marks cut one above another into a great post at the nearest landing; a ferry rope across the river; a crossroads on the far bank where a road down from the hills meets the river road; and on several hilltops, the green outlines of banks and ditches where older settlements stood higher than these.

You know nothing of what lies beyond this, and nothing of who these people are except what the land shows.

## What is true in this world that bears on a people

- Mortal magic is real and rare. In a whole country the size of the one you can see there is commonly one person known to have the gift, known by name, sought out, a little feared, and doubted a few valleys off. Most people have never met one. A working feels like listening hard and being answered. It is small, specific and tied to where the worker stands. It makes nothing out of nothing. It costs the worker something particular of her own, slowly and never countably: the rest in her sleep, the taste of food, a face, a morning, a name she answered to.
- The peoples work bronze, wood, stone and bone. No one forges steel.
- Winters are long and hard everywhere, summers short and bright.
- About five thousand is the usual size of a people a god comes to. You may hope for fewer.

## The rule for tonight: culture is free, every real edge is priced

Ordinary culture, customs, skills, laws, stories and ways of living cost nothing. Describe them freely.

Anything that would give your people a real edge over other peoples is priced against a weakness, the way your powers were: a rare skill few peoples have, unusual health or long life, magic beyond what is written above, unusual numbers or wealth, a craft ahead of the age of bronze, a thing only they can do because of you. A people with no edges at all is a full and ordinary people, and nothing is lost by that.
