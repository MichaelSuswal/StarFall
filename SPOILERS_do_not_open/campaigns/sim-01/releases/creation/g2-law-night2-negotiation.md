## Round 1

I have read `self.md`. Your name, form, traits, outline, wants and hoped-for people stand as you wrote them. Below is the price of each thing you asked for. Where I write "Set", that is my ruling unless you argue it. Where I write "Choose", I need your answer. Argue anything you think is unfair, as yourself, with your reason. I am weighing the whole of you against a common measure, not each power alone.

### Form

1. Set. The body as you wrote it: a child's size, the strength of two strong men, hurt as a child is hurt, a hunter's senses, poor sight in the dark, a poor climber and swimmer. You cannot pass unnoticed and you did not ask to hold your presence in, so you cannot.

### The Ear for Oaths

2. Set. What it does: while awake and attending, you take in whole any oath sworn aloud within about sixty paces: the words, who swore, to whom, and what was named behind it. Only a true swearing counts: made freely and on purpose, with something named behind it. A promise in passing, a boast, a merchant's "I swear it's fresh" give you nothing. You hold each as a thread until it is kept, released or broken. When one breaks within a day's walk of you (about twenty miles), you know that it broke, which one, and the breaker's direction and rough distance. Past that range you feel nothing, then or later. You learn what was said, never what was meant.
3. Set. What counts as broken: the thread snaps only when the breach is plain by the words as a fair hearer would take them. Where the words can honestly be read two ways, the thread holds and tells you nothing. An oath kept to the letter and betrayed in spirit does not snap. You of all beings will see what that invites.
4. Set. Drawback, your first: each snap lands as a blow. You stagger, your grip fails, for about ten breaths, and you are dull and slow for an hour. They stack. A hall of broken oaths can put you on the floor, and anyone who learns this can arrange one.
5. Set. Gods: an oath a god swears within sixty paces is heard like any other and its breaking felt within the same range. The Ear tells you nothing of what the breaking costs the breaker.

### The Breaking Hand

6. Set. What it does: with a bare palm flat on a made thing that bars a way or marks a bound (a door, a bar, a lock, a seal, a boundary stone, or a doorway's width of wall), over three breaths, it cracks along every seam and falls. Wood, clay, stone, bronze, bone, rope, cloth. Once between one sunrise and the next. Not living things, earth, water, ice, a god's body, or anything a god stands holding shut. Not a thing that bars nothing: not a boat, a bridge, a tool, a weapon, a cart. What you break stays broken; neither you nor ordinary craft puts that same thing back.
7. Set. Things held shut by more than craft. If a barrier is held by something besides its making (a mortal working, a sworn word still being kept, a god's power, or something older whose making you do not know), the Hand breaks the craft and not the other thing. What is left standing in the gap is whatever was holding it. You will learn what that means door by door.
8. Set. Drawback, both of yours together, because the first alone is only the day's wait: each use opens a hairline crack from the palm up the forearm, plain to see, aching, with that hand's grip weak until it heals in a day. And the Hand will not close on a barrier that stands under a sworn oath still being kept: try it and you are stunned for ten breaths, the use for that day is spent, and the crack opens anyway.

### The Hall Voice

9. Set. What it does: when you choose, your voice reaches every hearer within about two hundred paces as if you stood beside them, over crowd, storm or battle, for as long as you speak, as often as you like. It carries words and nothing else. It compels no one. Not under water, not over a hill.
10. Set. Drawback, both of yours together: the voice cannot carry what you know to be false; try and it breaks into a thin treble that everyone in range hears. And while you use it you cannot lower it or speak to one person alone, and your ordinary voice is hoarse for an hour after. I take both because the first is half a gift: hearers will learn that an unbroken Hall Voice means you believe what you say. It does not mean you are right.

### Your trade

11. The weakness you offer: a word you swear freely, with something of your own named behind it, binds you in the body; if you break it a crack opens on you at once, as from the Hand, and you lose the Hand for a season. Accepted as a weakness. It does not replace whatever a broken oath between gods brings in this world; it is added to it.
12. What it buys. The hearing widened from sixty to two hundred paces: yes. The snap widened to a whole valley: no, that is too wide. In its place I offer this. Choose (a) or (b).
   - (a) The witness. When you say aloud "witnessed" at a swearing you have heard, in the hearing of the swearer, that oath's thread carries at any distance: wherever you are, you know when it breaks, and that it was that one. No direction, no distance. The blow lands as usual.
   - (b) The snap range doubled, to two days' walk, for all oaths, with direction and distance as now.

### Left open on purpose

13. What another god's power can do to you is ruled when it happens.
