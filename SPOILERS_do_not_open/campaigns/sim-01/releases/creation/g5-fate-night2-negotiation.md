## Round 1

I have read `self.md`. Your name, form, traits, outline, wants and hoped-for people stand as you wrote them. Below is the price of each thing you asked for. Where I write "Set", that is my ruling unless you argue it. Where I write "Choose", I need your answer. Argue anything you think is unfair, as yourself, with your reason. I am weighing the whole of you against a common measure, not each power alone.

### Form

1. Set. The body as you wrote it: packed fog in a tall woman's shape, a working woman's strength and no more, very hard to turn or wear out, slow to close a wound, seeing well in fog, rain, dusk and dark and poorly in dry glare, thinning and tiring in dry heat and on high bare ground. Feeling damp and pressure in the air is the form's own sense. The dew on everything within reach, the white breath and the closed left hand are form. They also mark where you have stood: wet stone on a dry day is a track.

### Tilt the Odds

2. Set, and made exact. What it does: within a long bowshot, when you can sense a chance about to fall, you lean it a little toward the outcome you choose. It works on chance only: a throw, an eddy, an arrow's last hand's breadth, a wheel in a ford, which of two riders arrives first. It does not work on a choice. Whether a stranger decides to speak is a mind deciding, and no power here rules a mind. It makes nothing certain and nothing impossible happen. You must know the chance is coming and attend to it before it falls. One chance at a time, never the same chance twice, at most three in a day, and the third is felt: you are dull and heavy until you have stood an hour in damp air.
3. Set. Its size, plainly: where the world would roll for a thing, your lean is a moderate step on that roll, for or against. It is never the largest factor in a fight.
4. Set. Drawback, your first, The Debt: every lean opens an entry. Within the season, a chance in your own affairs or among those you hold closest leans against you by the same measure. You do not choose which and you are not told when; you may recognize it. The Debt is never forgiven and never deferred past the season.
5. Set. It does not work on a chance another god is deliberately steering, and where two gods lean the same chance, neither lean takes.

### Draw the Fog

6. Set. What it does: once between one sunrise and the next, you raise fog around yourself, about a mile across, centered where you stood when you drew it. It does not follow you. In cool damp air it comes within a few breaths; in dry air it comes slowly and thin; in dry heat it may not come. It lasts a few hours, less under strong sun or wind, longer at night. In it a mortal sees a few paces and cannot hold a direction by sight. You see through it and feel where warm living things stand. Sound carries in it as in any fog. It hides; it removes nothing.
7. Set. Drawback, both of yours together: it does not choose. Your own people in it are as lost as anyone and you can make no exception. And you cannot end it. Once drawn it stands until it burns off or its hours pass, whatever has changed. A fog that came too fast on a dry day is known for no natural thing by anyone who saw it rise.
8. Set. Another god in the fog is as blind as a mortal unless its own form or powers say otherwise, and you feel it there only as a warm shape, or as a god if it is within speaking distance.

### Hear the Oaths

9. Set, and trimmed. "Any ground I have claimed" has no edge I can rule on, so it goes. What it does: you hear any true oath (sworn freely, on purpose, with something named behind it) that a mortal swears within plain hearing of you, and any such oath sworn in your name within a day's walk of where you stand. You know what was sworn, by whom, to whom. When it is kept or broken, you feel it, a lightening or a tearing, if you are then within a day's walk of where it was sworn. It binds no one. It does not tell a free oath from a forced one, or an honest one from one never meant.
10. Set. Drawback, your first, The Weight: each oath broken within your reach lands as an ache in the body for a day, and you cannot refuse it. They stack through a season. A people who learn to swear in your name and break lightly will bow your back.
11. Set. Oaths between gods you hear only if you stand in hearing, like anyone with ears. They leave no weight on you.
12. Tokens. You asked that an oath sworn on something of yours carry stronger. I will give this much for nothing more: an oath sworn with a hand on a stone you yourself took wet from a river and gave to the swearer is heard and felt at two days' walk and not one. The stone is a stone; it can be lost, stolen or shown to others.

### Your trades

13. Holding your presence in. Granted, at a price. What it is: when you take it up, on purpose, over a few breaths, a god standing near enough to speak to does not feel that you are one like itself. Terms that always come with it:
   - It closes both ways. While you hold it you feel nothing of other gods either. One could stand at your elbow and you would not know it.
   - It hides the feeling and nothing else. Your body looks as it looks. A god may still work out what you are from your shape, your manner or what you do.
   - You can use no power while you hold it. Using one ends the holding at once, and any god within speaking distance then feels you all at once, as a shock.
   - It ends if you are struck, wounded, badly startled, or asleep.
   - It does nothing to mortals, who never felt you that way.
   - Your price, as you offered it: while you hold it you are faint and thinned, a body half drawn away, visibly paler, and the dew stops. About a quarter of a day is the most you can hold, and then you need a night and a day before you can take it up again.
   - Choose: take it as priced, or withdraw it.
14. Your second trade. The weakness: in dry, hot, bright air anywhere, not only the heights, your body thins, you tire, the fog may fail and the lean reaches only half as far. Accepted. What it buys: in fog, rain, or the autumn season near water, your fog comes at once and stands until the next sunrise. Nothing more; this is a cold country and dry heat is the rarer weather. Choose: take it or leave it.

### Left open on purpose

15. What another god's power can do to you or your fog is ruled when it happens.
