## Round 1

I have read `self.md`. Your name, form, traits, outline, wants and hoped-for people stand as you wrote them. Below is the price of each thing you asked for. Where I write "Set", that is my ruling unless you argue it. Where I write "Choose", I need your answer. Argue anything you think is unfair, as yourself, with your reason. I am weighing the whole of you against a common measure, not each power alone.

### Form

1. Set. The body as you wrote it, including its strength (a loaded cart's weight, a boulder four men could not shift), that it sinks and cannot cross water deeper than its chest, that heat dulls it, and that bronze and many hands over time can chip it. The frost, the breath and the spring moss are part of the form and cost nothing. Feeling heavy footfalls and rockfall near you through bare feet is the form's own sense, out to about a stone's throw.

### Hearth of Stone

2. Set. What it does: with both hands on natural rock that has never been worked (a cliff, an outcrop, a great boulder), over about an hour, you open a dry room with one doorway, large enough for about twenty to sleep. It keeps out wind, snow and rain and holds the warmth of those inside. It makes nothing else: no food, fuel or furnishing. It stands until the next full thaw, then closes over several days, slowly, and never on a living thing; anyone inside is pressed gently out. At most three stand at once. You know where each is and nothing about what happens in it.
3. Set. It cannot be opened in worked stone: not a wall, a tower, a quarried face, a sealed door, or anything hands have shaped. It is a shelter and not a way through. A room is always a blind room with one mouth; it never joins a cave, a passage or another room.
4. Choose, between two doors.
   - (a) The open door. The doorway is a plain opening. Anyone and anything may walk in. Drawback: each standing shelter holds a part of you, and you are slower for it: a quarter slower with one, half with two, three quarters with three, until each closes. You may close one early by going to it and laying hands on it for an hour.
   - (b) The kept door. The doorway admits only those you have looked in the eye, one by one, before or after it opens; to all others it is rock, though mortals with picks and days could cut in as into any rock. Drawback: the same slowing as (a), and also this: once you have let someone in you cannot shut them out of that shelter while it stands, and you can be wrong about them.
5. Set. Being watched: it cannot be done unseen. Another god can walk into a shelter like anyone (under (b), only if you have met its eyes). What another god's power can do to one is ruled when it happens.

### Stone Hearing

6. Set, and trimmed. A day's walk of breathing and voices is more than I will give. What it does: standing still with both bare feet and one hand on bedrock, you feel what moves on the same unbroken rock. Footfalls, hooves, rockfall and digging: out to about a day's walk on foot in that country, with rough number, heavy or light, two legs or four, and direction. Voices and breathing against rock: only within about a bowshot, as sound without words. It takes time to reach: nearby at once, the far edge after about an hour of standing. Soil, sand, deep snow, scree, and any river or lake break the link.
7. Set. Drawback: while you listen you are deaf to the air and your eyes see only dimly, as through water; you cannot move your feet without losing it; and coming back to yourself takes about as long as a slow count of a hundred. Someone could walk up to you.
8. Set. A god on the same rock is a footfall like any other, as heavy as its body is. Nothing in the hearing tells you it is a god.

### Return At Thaw

9. Set. What it does: if your body is broken, scattered or buried so that it cannot act, it is whole again at the next spring thaw, standing on the high stone nearest where it fell, bare, with moss on the shoulders, remembering what happened up to the breaking and nothing of the time between. Not if the pieces lie under deep water, not if a piece has been carried more than a few days' walk off (you return without it, lessened, until you recover it), and not from being bound or held by a living thing or a god.
10. Set. Drawback, your first: from the first hard frost to the thaw, every year, used or not, you move at half pace, your senses are dulled, and your powers are weaker: a shelter takes three hours to open and you can open no more than one new one in a winter, and the hearing reaches half as far. I note for you that winter is when a people most needs a shelter opened. That is the price.

### Your trade

11. Holding your presence in. Granted, at a price. What it is: when you take it up, on purpose, over a few breaths, a god standing near enough to speak to does not feel that you are one like itself. Terms that always come with it:
   - It closes both ways. While you hold it you feel nothing of other gods either. One could stand at your elbow and you would not know it.
   - It hides the feeling and nothing else. Your body looks as it looks. A god may still work out what you are from your shape, your manner or what you do.
   - You can use no power while you hold it. Using one ends the holding at once, and any god within speaking distance then feels you all at once, as a shock.
   - It ends if you are struck, wounded, badly startled, or asleep.
   - It does nothing to mortals, who never felt you that way.
   - Your price, as you offered it: your body is heavier and walks a quarter slower than a walking mortal, always. For that price there is no limit on how long you may hold it while awake.
   - I note the sum for you, so you choose with open eyes: a quarter slower always, half pace in winter, and slower again for each standing shelter. In deep winter with shelters standing you may barely move. Choose: take the trade as priced, or withdraw it.

### Left open on purpose

12. What another god's power can do to your body or your shelters is not settled tonight and is ruled when it happens.
