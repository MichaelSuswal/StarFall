## Round 1

I have read `self.md`. Your name, form, traits, outline, wants and hoped-for people stand as you wrote them. Below is the price of each thing you asked for. Where I write "Set", that is my ruling unless you argue it. Where I write "Choose", I need your answer. Argue anything you think is unfair, as yourself, with your reason. I am weighing the whole of you against a common measure, not each power alone.

### Form

1. Set. The body as you wrote it: a hand over a tall man, three times a plowman's strength, tireless at a walk, hard to hurt and slow to heal, a hunter's senses, sinks and walks the bottom.
2. Set. The face. It is form and not a power, and you do not steer it. Each mortal sees a face they half know, and no two agree. You cannot choose whose face a viewer sees, cannot wear a particular person's face, and cannot stop it. So you cannot pass as anyone, and no description of your face will ever find you, but the eyes, the hands, the size, the smell and the sound are the same to all and anyone can know you by those. What another god sees when it looks at you I leave to be found out.

### Reckoning of the Yield

3. Set. What it does: kneeling, with a bare hand on soil, standing crop, seed or a stored heap, over a few slow breaths, you know what it will yield this season or has left, what ails it, how long a store lasts at the present eating, and what in it is spoiled. It covers what the touch belongs to: one field or one store, up to a large farm's holding. It reads soil, crops, seed and stored grain. Not people, beasts or weather. It reads the season as it now stands: a flood, a frost or a fire that has not happened is not in the number. It tells you what is, never why beyond the ailment's name, never what to do.
4. Set. Drawback, your first: what ails the thing you read, you carry in your own body until the next dusk. Blight a cough, rot a sourness, drought a thirst nothing slakes, an empty bin a hunger. Several readings stack. A day of reading a failing country leaves you sick.

### Keeping

5. Set. What it does: with both palms on one store of harvested grain or seed, you say aloud how much is there, and for one full turn of the seasons it does not rot, mold, sprout or feed mice, weevils or birds, so long as it stays in that place. One bin, barn or granary at a time, up to what a few dozen households lay by. Once per store per year. It makes no grain and stops no hand from taking it. It ends if the store is moved. Wet grain is not kept. You must say the true amount: the Keeping holds what you named and no more, so if you name short, the rest is unkept.
6. Set. Drawback, your first: the Keeping is borrowed from the next living harvest. The fields that feed that store yield less the following season, by about what the Keeping saved from loss. You do not choose which fields. Keep every store in a country and you will have saved this winter and thinned the next autumn. That is the price, and your people should hear it from you.

### The Cutting

7. Set. What it does: you walk the whole edge of one sown field, up to a few acres, at dusk, with open hands, and by morning what harms that crop in it is dead: blight, rust, weeds, locusts and grain pests, root rot. Once in a night, once per field per season. It holds for that season's crop. It does not heal what was already damaged. It does not touch people, livestock, or any beast larger than a hare. It works only on a field under a sown crop: not on wild ground, pasture or forest. If you do not finish the walk it does not take.
8. Set. Drawback, your first: everything else alive in that field that is not the crop dies with the harm. Bees, songbirds, worms, wildflowers, the small beasts. Nothing comes back to that ground until the next spring, and a field cut year after year grows a little poorer each time. A clean field and a dead one.

### Your trades

9. The weakness you offer: you cannot lie without it being felt. When you say what you know to be false, those who hear feel the wrongness plainly. Accepted, as you wrote it. I add what follows from it: silence, and a true word beside the point, are open to you, and anyone who learns the weakness can put a straight question to you and read what comes back.
10. Holding your presence in. Granted, at a price. What it is: when you take it up, on purpose, over a few breaths, a god standing near enough to speak to does not feel that you are one like itself. Terms that always come with it:
   - It closes both ways. While you hold it you feel nothing of other gods either. One could stand at your elbow and you would not know it.
   - It hides the feeling and nothing else. Your body looks as it looks. A god may still work out what you are from your shape, your manner or what you do.
   - You can use no power while you hold it. Using one ends the holding at once, and any god within speaking distance then feels you all at once, as a shock.
   - It ends if you are struck, wounded, badly startled, or asleep.
   - It does nothing to mortals, who never felt you that way.
   - Your price is the weakness in item 9, and it is a heavy one, so there is no limit on how long you may hold it while awake and unhurt.
   - Choose: take it as priced, or withdraw the trade (and the weakness with it).
11. Your second offer, to give up being heard by a god at a distance: you never had that, so there is nothing to give. No god is felt or heard at a distance here.

### Left open on purpose

12. What another god's power can do to you, a kept store or a cut field is ruled when it happens.
