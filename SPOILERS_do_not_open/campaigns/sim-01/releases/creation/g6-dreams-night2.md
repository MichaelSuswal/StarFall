## What is true of every god in this world

You would know these of yourself. They are not open to bargaining tonight. Build inside them.

- One body. A god here has one body, and only one.
- Knowing another god. You know that others like you came down when you did, and nothing more about them. You feel nothing of them from far away. When one stands near enough to speak to, you feel plainly that this is one like yourself, and it feels the same of you. You learn nothing else that way: not what the other is, what it can do, what it is called, or what it wants. That must be worked out. A god may try to hold its presence in so that it is not known; whether you can is a thing to ask for tonight and to pay for, like any other ability. If you do not ask, you cannot.
- A word given. A word sworn between gods, freely and on purpose, with something of the swearer's own named behind it, is a real thing here. Nothing stops a god from breaking one. What comes of breaking one is not written anywhere a god can read. Reputation follows a god besides.
- How far a god stands above mortals. Plainly, and not beyond all answer. To a mortal who meets you, you are a different order of thing; the first feeling is awe and wariness, not helplessness. A god can be wrong about things. Mortals can oppose a god and can damage its work, and enough of them with time and tools can trouble a god's body.
- Mortal magic. It is real and uncommon. Across the whole world a mortal with the gift is found in something under half of the valleys, known by name where she is, sought out and a little feared, and doubted a few valleys off. It is spread unevenly. To the one who works it, it feels like listening hard and being answered: a held breath, a reach, the place leaning in. It is small, specific, and tied to where the worker stands. It makes nothing out of nothing. It costs the worker something particular of her own, slowly and never countably: the rest in her sleep, the taste of food, a face, a morning, a name she answered to. She often cannot say afterward what went.
- What you can do is not that. A god's powers are its own, as eyes or legs are. They are not learned magic and do not run on its cost.
- The body's plain facts. You need no food. Sleep is a choice. You cannot die; you can be wounded, bound, driven out, cut off from your people, or forgotten. You move at the pace a body of your shape would move, no faster, unless a power you pay for says otherwise. You can speak the tongues of mortals.
- The age of tools. The peoples below work bronze, wood, stone and bone. No one forges steel.
- No power arrives finished and free. Every power comes with a drawback that always travels with it. There is no set number of powers: you may have one great one or several smaller ones, and the Keeper weighs the whole of you against a common measure, so one enormous power costs what several small ones cost. Nothing over the top. A power that rules minds outright, kills at a word, or undoes the rules above will be refused or cut down.

### File 1: `SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/sim-01/gods/g6-dreams/self.md`

Begin with the line `> SPOILER WARNING: Keeper material. Players must never read this file.` then the heading `# Self: g6-dreams`, then these sections in this order, each under its own `##` heading with exactly these names:
