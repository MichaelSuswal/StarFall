## Your country, as you first see it

You come down in autumn on flat river land, on the natural bank of a broad brown river that runs slow and wide toward the south. The soil underfoot is black and deep. On both banks the plain runs level to the edge of sight, cut into long fields: stubble, standing late barley, fallow, flax. To the north-east, a morning's walk off, one low rounded hill stands up out of the flat with nothing near it.

What the land itself shows: villages on the raised banks and on old mounds, each a cluster of thatch with smoke going up; ditches and low dykes, some kept and some silted; threshing floors in use; ox teams; granaries on staddle stones beside the bigger houses; and near the river, a morning's walk downstream, the footings and one standing corner of something very large and squared, built of dressed stone, roofless, with barley growing inside it. Beyond the last fields to the west, a dark line of uncut scrub and wood.

You know nothing of what lies beyond this, and nothing of who these people are except what the land shows.

## What is true in this world that bears on a people

- Mortal magic is real and rare. In a whole country the size of the one you can see there is commonly one person known to have the gift, known by name, sought out, a little feared, and doubted a few valleys off. Most people have never met one. A working feels like listening hard and being answered. It is small, specific and tied to where the worker stands. It makes nothing out of nothing. It costs the worker something particular of her own, slowly and never countably: the rest in her sleep, the taste of food, a face, a morning, a name she answered to.
- The peoples work bronze, wood, stone and bone. No one forges steel.
- Winters are long and hard everywhere, summers short and bright.
- About five thousand is the usual size of a people a god comes to. You may hope for fewer.
