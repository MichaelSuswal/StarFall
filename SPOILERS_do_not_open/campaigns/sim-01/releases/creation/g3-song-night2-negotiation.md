## Round 1

I have read `self.md`. Your name, form, traits, outline, wants and hoped-for people stand as you wrote them. Below is the price of each thing you asked for. Where I write "Set", that is my ruling unless you argue it. Where I write "Choose", I need your answer. Argue anything you think is unfair, as yourself, with your reason. I am weighing the whole of you against a common measure, not each power alone.

### Form

1. Set. The body as you wrote it: slow, tireless, a strong farm woman's strength and a little more, ordinary sight, hearing that is the best of you. Hearing in a voice whether it lies about its own feelings is the form's own ear and costs nothing; it tells you about feeling, never about fact, and it does not work on a god. The hum in the breastbone at arm's length, the damp hem and its track are form. They also mark you: anyone who has met you once knows you again in the dark.

### The Unmaking Note

2. Set. What it does: you sing one low held note at a made thing within a few paces that you see, touch, or hear, and it comes apart along the lines it was put together by. Knots, lashings, pegged joints, mortar, dry stone, mud brick, a bar along its grain, a lock, a seal. One thing per singing, up to about a cart or a small hut: a slow breath for a small thing, a full minute for a large one. Never flesh or bone, never what grew where it stands, never what you carry or wear, never a god's body. Metal only when small: a clasp, a pin, a rivet. Not a blade, a helm or a cauldron. Anyone near hears the note. It does not mend.
3. Set. Things held together by more than craft (a mortal working, a sworn word still being kept, a god's power, something older): the note loosens the craft and not the other thing. What is left is whatever was holding it.
4. Set. Drawback, your first: each singing, you forget one thing. You do not choose it and you are not told what went. The size follows the unmaking: a knot may cost a morning's weather, a hut may cost a name, a face, a promise you were owed, or the way to a place. In practice the Keeper removes it from your record and you go on without it. Others may remember what you have lost, and you will have no way to know they are right. No limit on how often you sing but this.

### The Long Ear

5. Set, and trimmed. Hearing every word ever spoken at a place is more than I will give. What it does: with your hand on a place or thing (a stone, a doorframe, a well curb, a grave, a hearth, an old tree), once in a day, you hear what was sung there, and what was said there in the manner of song: chant, lament, vow, a naming, a thing recited to be remembered. Not ordinary talk. You hear voice, tune and words, not minds. It comes latest first and strongest first, and you do not steer it. A night's listening reaches back about as far as the oldest person now living could remember. Each further night at the same place reaches further, and thinner. Very old singing comes as fragments. A tongue no one now speaks you hear as sound and tune; knowing every living tongue does not open a dead one. Running water keeps only the last song, faintly. A place never sung in holds nothing.
6. Set. Drawback, both of yours together: while you listen you are deaf to the living world and slow to rise, and someone could walk up to you. And one song of what you heard stays in you until the next day: you hum it, it colors your speech, and anyone who knows that song knows where you have been listening. The grief you cannot set down is yours besides; I do not count it as a price, but I do not take it away.
7. Set. A god who sang or swore aloud at a place is heard as any voice. Nothing in it tells you the voice was a god's.

### The Gathering Song

8. Set. What it does: once in a night, you sing a slow song to a company that can hear your plain voice, a dozen to a few hundred, and those who choose to sing with you are steadied until dawn: grief and fear sit lighter, they tire more slowly, they remember the song and each other. No one is made willing, loyal or obedient. Your own people take it fully, strangers partly, and no one who does not sing takes anything.
9. Set. Drawback. Your first, that each who sang forgets one small thing of their own, stands, and you must say so before you begin or the song does not take. Your second I decline as a price: you speak in proverbs already. In its place: you pay as they pay. Each Gathering Song takes one small thing from you as well, unchosen and untold. A people sung together every night would be a people slowly emptied, and you with them. That is the price and you may weigh it.
10. Set. If another god sings along it is steadied as a stranger is, pays as a singer pays, and learns nothing of you by it.

### Your trades

11. Holding your presence in. Granted, at a price. What it is: when you take it up, on purpose, over a few breaths, a god standing near enough to speak to does not feel that you are one like itself. Terms that always come with it:
   - It closes both ways. While you hold it you feel nothing of other gods either. One could stand at your elbow and you would not know it.
   - It hides the feeling and nothing else. Your body looks as it looks. A god may still work out what you are from your shape, your manner or what you do.
   - You can use no power while you hold it. Using one ends the holding at once, and any god within speaking distance then feels you all at once, as a shock.
   - It ends if you are struck, wounded, badly startled, or asleep.
   - It does nothing to mortals, who never felt you that way.
   - Your price, as you offered it: while you hold it you tire as a mortal does. About a quarter of a day is the most you can hold, and then you must rest as long as you held before you can take it up again. The hum in the breastbone stops while you hold it; a mortal who knows you may notice that.
   - Choose: take it as priced, or withdraw it.
12. Your second trade, that a powered song is always audible and cannot be muffled, is already how the songs work as set above. It is not a weakness I can pay you for, and the reach of your powers is as set. If you want more reach somewhere, offer something that costs.

### Left open on purpose

13. What another god's power can do to you is ruled when it happens.

## Round 2

Your request on the forgetting: granted. The forgetting never takes how to sing your powers, your own name, or the knowledge that you pay. Everything else may go. Holding in: taken as priced.

Below are the final words. This is what goes on your card. Once you confirm, each Description and Drawback is fixed and is never rewritten; anything learned later is added beneath as a ruling.

### Form, as it will stand

One body. This campaign allows a god one body. An old woman as tall as a tall man but stooped, broad and heavy-boned, with thick wrists and big square hands. Skin the grey-brown of dried river clay, deeply lined. Long hair the white of old rope in thick cords to the waist, tied with river shells, small bird bones and a knot of grey wool. Deep-set eyes the grey of wet slate, the left lower lid drooping a little. Layers of undyed wool the color of fog, a long skirt and wide shawl, bare feet. The hem is always damp and leaves a dark track on dry floors. Within arm's length of her a low note is felt in the breastbone. She speaks low and slow, in proverbs, after long silences.

- Pace: an old walker's, never faster. She cannot run and climbs badly. She does not tire.
- Strength: a strong farm woman's and a little more: a cart wheel, not a cart.
- Hearing is the best of her: a whisper across a large room, a voice through a wall, and in a mortal voice whether it lies about its own feelings (feeling only, never fact; not in a god's voice).
- Good nose, ordinary sight, poor at distance, a mortal's in the dark. Heavy in water.
- The hum, the hem and its track mark her: anyone who has met her knows her again in the dark.

Knowing other gods: as for every god here. See the holding of your presence, below.

### Powers, final text

#### The Unmaking Note
Status: main
Description: Amarreth sings one low held note at a made thing within a few paces that she sees, touches or hears, and it comes apart along the lines it was put together by: knots, lashings, pegged joints, mortar, dry stone, mud brick, a bar along its grain, a lock, a seal. One thing per singing, up to about the size of a cart or a small hut, a slow breath for a small thing and a full minute for a large one, as often as she will pay for. Anyone near hears the note. It does not mend.
Drawback: Each singing, she forgets one thing. She does not choose it and is not told what went. The size follows the unmaking: a knot may cost a morning's weather, a hut may cost a name, a face, a promise owed to her, or the way to a place.
Limits settled at creation:
  - Never flesh or bone, never what grew where it stands, never what she carries or wears, never a god's body.
  - Metal only when small: a clasp, a pin, a rivet. Not a blade, a helm or a cauldron.
  - A thing held together by more than craft: the note loosens the craft and not the other thing.
  - Never taken by the forgetting: how to sing her powers, her own name, and the knowledge that she pays. Everything else may go.
  - How the forgetting is kept: the Keeper removes the lost thing from her record and she goes on without it. Others may remember what she has lost.
  - Not if her mouth is stopped. In water the note is dulled and the work slower.
Trades: none

#### The Long Ear
Status: main
Description: With her hand on a place or a thing (a stone, a doorframe, a well curb, a grave, a hearth, an old tree), once in a day, Amarreth hears what was sung there, and what was said there in the manner of song: chant, lament, vow, a naming, a thing recited to be remembered. She hears voice, tune and words, not minds. It comes latest first and strongest first, and she does not steer it.   - A god's singing or swearing is heard as any voice; nothing marks it as a god's.

#### The Gathering Song
Status: main
Description: Once in a night, Amarreth sings a slow song to a company within reach of her plain voice, a dozen to a few hundred, and those who choose to sing with her are steadied until dawn: grief and fear sit lighter, they tire more slowly, and they remember the song and one another. Limits settled at creation:
  - They must hear her own voice.
  - A god who sings along is steadied as a stranger is, pays as a singer pays, and learns nothing of her by it.
  - It is done openly; everyone present knows what is being done.
Trades: none

#### Holding in
Status: main
Description: When Amarreth takes it up, on purpose, over a few breaths, a god standing near enough to speak to does not feel that Amarreth is one like itself. It hides that feeling and nothing else: the body looks as it looks, and a god may still work out what Amarreth is from shape, manner or deeds.   - The hum in the breastbone stops while she holds it; a mortal who knows her may notice.
Trades: Priced by its own drawback.
