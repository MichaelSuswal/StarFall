## Your country, as you first see it

You come down in autumn in low wet country, on a hummock of firm ground beside a wide, slow river the color of tea. The air is soft and smells of peat smoke and salt. Downstream, to the south-west, the river loses its banks among reedbeds, alder carr and standing water that goes on out of sight; beyond that the sky is the flat white of fog, and gulls come inland from it. Upstream the ground rises a little, to wet meadow and willow.

What the land itself shows: many small smokes, scattered, each from a house or two on its own patch of raised ground; plank and brushwood causeways between some of them, others reached only by water; flat boats poled along cuts; eel traps, peat stacks, geese; one long, low roof, larger than the rest, on the biggest island within sight; and here and there a line of old squared stones running straight into the water and not coming out.

You know nothing of what lies beyond this, and nothing of who these people are except what the land shows.

## What is true in this world that bears on a people

- Mortal magic is real and rare. In a whole country the size of the one you can see there is commonly one person known to have the gift, known by name, sought out, a little feared, and doubted a few valleys off. Most people have never met one. A working feels like listening hard and being answered. It is small, specific and tied to where the worker stands. It makes nothing out of nothing. It costs the worker something particular of her own, slowly and never countably: the rest in her sleep, the taste of food, a face, a morning, a name she answered to.
- The peoples work bronze, wood, stone and bone. No one forges steel.
- Winters are long and hard everywhere, summers short and bright.
- About five thousand is the usual size of a people a god comes to. You may hope for fewer.

## The rule for tonight: culture is free, every real edge is priced

Ordinary culture, customs, skills, laws, stories and ways of living cost nothing. Describe them freely.

Anything that would give your people a real edge over other peoples is priced against a weakness, the way your powers were: a rare skill few peoples have, unusual health or long life, magic beyond what is written above, unusual numbers or wealth, a craft ahead of the age of bronze, a thing only they can do because of you. Name each such edge plainly as an edge, and offer the weakness you would accept for it. The Keeper prices it, in at most two rounds. An edge you do not name is not had. A people with no edges at all is a full and ordinary people, and nothing is lost by that.
