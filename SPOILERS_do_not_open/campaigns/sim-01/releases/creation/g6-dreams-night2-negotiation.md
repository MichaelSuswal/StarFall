## Round 1

I have read `self.md`. Your name, form, traits, outline, wants and hoped-for people stand as you wrote them. Below is the price of each thing you asked for. Where I write "Set", that is my ruling unless you argue it. Where I write "Choose", I need your answer. Argue anything you think is unfair, as yourself, with your reason. I am weighing the whole of you against a common measure, not each power alone.

### Form

1. Set. The body as you wrote it: a hand over a tall man, strong enough to lift the end of a loaded cart, a good runner's speed at most, sharp hearing best by night, dusk-sight by starlight and blind in full dark, hurt by a spear. The eyes that give back lamplight, the stillness, and the bone lamp-hook are form. The stillness marks you: a watcher who sees you stop will know something is not a man.

### The Quiet Door

2. Set. What it does: once in a night, you enter the dream of one sleeping mortal whom you have met face to face, awake, and who lies within a day's walk of your body. There you may speak, show what you choose as a dream shows things, and see what the sleeper is dreaming. You may ask; what answers is a dream, and a dream answers as dreams do, sideways, in the sleeper's own pictures, and it can be wrong, afraid or lying to itself. You cannot make the sleeper do anything on waking, cannot plant what the sleeper's mind will not carry, and cannot hold a sleeper who struggles to wake. It lasts while the sleeper sleeps. Mortals only. The sleeper wakes with a thin, honest memory: that someone was there, the gist, and often your face.
3. Set. Drawback, your first: while you are in a dream your body lies senseless where you left it and nothing short of a wound wakes it before the dreamer wakes or dawn comes. Whoever finds it may do as they like with it.
4. Set. A mortal with the gift may feel the door open and may shut it. A sleeper under another god's power, and any god, is closed to you.

### The Lit House

5. Set. What it does: a house, hall or hut in which you have sat through part of a night with a lamp burning becomes, on any night a mortal-tended lamp burns in it, a place of honest rest for mortals and beasts asleep inside its walls: no nightmare, no fever dream, a clean sleep, an easy waking. The tired mend as well as rest can mend them and the sick and hurt rest easier, which helps a little. It heals no wound, cures no sickness, and gives back nothing a mortal has spent on a working. It reaches the walls and no farther. A boat is not a house. It guards against nothing: anyone, mortal or god, may walk in.
6. Set. You may hold three houses at once. To take a fourth you must let one go. A great hall is one house.
7. Set. Drawback, your second, with one addition: the lamp must truly burn, mortal-made and mortal-tended, and if a mortal's hand snuffs it while people sleep, you feel it as a blow wherever you are and lose that house for a season. The addition, which you named yourself among your edges: every held house goes dark whenever you are senseless, and that includes every night you spend through the Quiet Door. You cannot keep the lamps and walk in a dream on the same night.

### The Held Breath

8. Holding your presence in. Granted, at a price. What it is: when you take it up, on purpose, over a few breaths, a god standing near enough to speak to does not feel that you are one like itself. Terms that always come with it:
   - It closes both ways. While you hold it you feel nothing of other gods either. One could stand at your elbow and you would not know it.
   - It hides the feeling and nothing else. Your body looks as it looks. A god may still work out what you are from your shape, your manner or what you do.
   - You can use no power while you hold it. Using one ends the holding at once, and any god within speaking distance then feels you all at once, as a shock.
   - It ends if you are struck, wounded, badly startled, or asleep.
   - It does nothing to mortals, who never felt you that way.
   - Your own terms stand on top of these: you hold it only while you stand or sit quiet, and you may hold it as long as you can do that.
   - Drawback, your first: while you hold it your hearing and sight fall to a mortal's, and you cannot speak a lie without a stammer anyone can hear.
   - Choose: take it as priced, or withdraw it.

### Your trades

9. The weakness you offer: a wound heals on you only as fast as on a mortal, and a bronze blade leaves a scar that stays open-looking for a year. Accepted as a weakness. It buys one of these; choose (a) or (b). It does not lighten the Held Breath.
   - (a) Five houses held, not three.
   - (b) The Quiet Door also opens on any mortal asleep in a Lit House you hold, met or not, at any distance. Remember that entering a dream darkens the houses for that night, so the sleeper's rest ends as you arrive.
10. The threshold. You offered to be unable to cross a threshold you have not been invited over. That is a real weakness for a keeper of houses. If you take it, it buys the other of (a) and (b). Choose: take it, or leave it.

### Left open on purpose

11. What another god's power can do to you or a held house is ruled when it happens.

## Round 2

Your choices: five houses for the slow healing; the threshold taken, buying the Quiet Door's reach into a held Lit House. You asked that "invited" be written plainly. It is, below. Note one thing in it: a lamp set for travelers in general is not an invitation; the lamp must be set for you.

### Form, as it will stand

One body. This campaign allows a god one body. A man a hand's breadth taller than a tall man, broad in chest and shoulder, long-armed, with big square unburned hands, and wrong in how right he is: an even face, straight teeth, skin of pale warm brown like fired clay with no scar or line. Black collar-length hair cut straight. Very dark grey eyes that take the shine of a lamp and give back a little more. A long belted coat of undyed oatmeal wool, wool trousers, soft boots, all clean. A small bone lamp-hook on the belt. When it stops moving it stops entirely. It speaks bluntly, in short sentences, low and level.

- Pace: a man's walk, a good runner's run at most. It tires as little as it chooses.
- Strength: it can lift the end of a loaded cart or break a spear shaft across the knee. Not stone, not bronze bar.
- Hearing sharp, best by night; a whisper through a wooden wall. By starlight it sees as a mortal at dusk; in full dark it is blind.
- A spear hurts it. Wounds heal only as fast as a mortal's (see Weaknesses).
- The stillness marks it: a watcher who sees it stop knows something is not a man.

Knowing other gods: as for every god here. See the holding of your presence, below.

### Powers, final text

#### The Quiet Door
Status: main
Description: Once in a night, Orvane enters the dream of one sleeping mortal: either one it has met face to face, awake, who lies within a day's walk of its body, or any mortal asleep in a Lit House it holds, met or not, at any distance. There it may speak, show what it chooses as a dream shows things, and see what the sleeper is dreaming. It may ask; what answers is a dream, sideways, in the sleeper's own pictures, and it can be wrong, afraid, or lying to itself. It lasts while the sleeper sleeps. The sleeper wakes with a thin, honest memory: that someone was there, the gist, sometimes a shape.
Drawback: While Orvane is in a dream its body lies senseless where it left it, and nothing short of a wound wakes it before the dreamer wakes or dawn comes.
Limits settled at creation:
  - Mortals only. No god, and no sleeper under another god's power.
  - It cannot make the sleeper do anything on waking, cannot plant what the sleeper's mind will not carry, and cannot hold a sleeper who struggles to wake.
  - A mortal with the gift may feel the door open and may shut it.
  - Entering a dream darkens every held Lit House for that night, so a sleeper reached through a Lit House loses that night's rest as Orvane arrives.
  - Nothing passes in or out of a dream but words and pictures.
Trades: The reach to sleepers in a held Lit House was bought with the weakness The uninvited threshold.

#### The Lit House
Status: main
Description: A house, hall or hut in which Orvane has sat through part of a night with a lamp burning becomes, on any night a mortal-tended lamp burns in it, a place of honest rest for mortals and beasts asleep within its walls: no nightmare, no fever dream, a clean sleep and an easy waking. The tired mend as well as rest can mend them, and the sick and hurt rest easier. Orvane may hold five houses at once; to take another it must let one go.
Drawback: The lamp must truly burn, mortal-made and mortal-tended. If a mortal's hand snuffs it while people sleep, Orvane feels it as a blow wherever it is and loses that house for a season. Every held house goes dark whenever Orvane is senseless, and that includes every night it spends through the Quiet Door.
Limits settled at creation:
  - It heals no wound, cures no sickness, and gives back nothing a mortal has spent on a working.
  - It reaches the walls and no farther. A great hall is one house. A boat is not a house.
  - It guards against nothing: anyone, mortal or god, may walk in.
  - It is not secret.
Trades: Five houses (first asked at three) were bought with the weakness Mortal healing.

#### The Held Breath
Status: main
Description: When Orvane takes it up, on purpose, over a few breaths, a god standing near enough to speak to does not feel that Orvane is one like itself. It hides that feeling and nothing else: the body looks as it looks, and a god may still work out what Orvane is from shape, manner or deeds. It does nothing to mortals, who never felt a god that way.
Drawback: It can be held only while Orvane stands or sits quiet. While it is held its hearing and sight fall to a mortal's, and it cannot speak a lie without a stammer anyone can hear.
Limits settled at creation:
  - It closes both ways: while it is held, Orvane feels nothing of other gods either.
  - No other power can be used while it is held. Using one ends the holding at once, and any god within speaking distance then feels Orvane all at once, as a shock.
  - It ends on a blow, a wound, a bad startle, or sleep.
  - Being watched: nothing shows to mortals; to a god, the absence of the feeling is all there is.
  - No limit on how long, so long as it stays quiet.
Trades: Priced by its own drawback.

### Weaknesses taken, and what each bought

- Mortal healing. A wound heals on Orvane only as fast as on a mortal, and a bronze blade leaves a scar that looks open for a year. Bought: Five Lit Houses held, not three.
- The uninvited threshold. Orvane cannot cross the threshold of a dwelling or hall that someone keeps unless it has been invited over it. Invited means: a welcome spoken to it by one who lives there or has the keeping of the place; a hand held out to it at the door by such a one; or a lamp set in the window for it, meaning Orvane itself by its name or by what that house calls it. A lamp set for travelers in general is not an invitation. An invitation stands until one with the same right withdraws it aloud. Ruins, empty houses and open ground have no threshold. Bought: The Quiet Door's reach to any mortal asleep in a held Lit House.
