## Round 1

I have read `self.md`. Your name, form, traits, outline, wants and hoped-for people stand as you wrote them. Below is the price of each thing you asked for. Where I write "Set", that is my ruling unless you argue it. Where I write "Choose", I need your answer. Argue anything you think is unfair, as yourself, with your reason. I am weighing the whole of you against a common measure, not each power alone.

### Form

1. Set. The body as you wrote it: a hand over a tall man, strong enough to lift the end of a loaded cart, a good runner's speed at most, sharp hearing best by night, dusk-sight by starlight and blind in full dark, hurt by a spear. The eyes that give back lamplight, the stillness, and the bone lamp-hook are form. The stillness marks you: a watcher who sees you stop will know something is not a man.

### The Quiet Door

2. Set. What it does: once in a night, you enter the dream of one sleeping mortal whom you have met face to face, awake, and who lies within a day's walk of your body. There you may speak, show what you choose as a dream shows things, and see what the sleeper is dreaming. You may ask; what answers is a dream, and a dream answers as dreams do, sideways, in the sleeper's own pictures, and it can be wrong, afraid or lying to itself. You cannot make the sleeper do anything on waking, cannot plant what the sleeper's mind will not carry, and cannot hold a sleeper who struggles to wake. It lasts while the sleeper sleeps. Mortals only. The sleeper wakes with a thin, honest memory: that someone was there, the gist, and often your face.
3. Set. Drawback, your first: while you are in a dream your body lies senseless where you left it and nothing short of a wound wakes it before the dreamer wakes or dawn comes. Whoever finds it may do as they like with it.
4. Set. A mortal with the gift may feel the door open and may shut it. A sleeper under another god's power, and any god, is closed to you.

### The Lit House

5. Set. What it does: a house, hall or hut in which you have sat through part of a night with a lamp burning becomes, on any night a mortal-tended lamp burns in it, a place of honest rest for mortals and beasts asleep inside its walls: no nightmare, no fever dream, a clean sleep, an easy waking. The tired mend as well as rest can mend them and the sick and hurt rest easier, which helps a little. It heals no wound, cures no sickness, and gives back nothing a mortal has spent on a working. It reaches the walls and no farther. A boat is not a house. It guards against nothing: anyone, mortal or god, may walk in.
6. Set. You may hold three houses at once. To take a fourth you must let one go. A great hall is one house.
7. Set. Drawback, your second, with one addition: the lamp must truly burn, mortal-made and mortal-tended, and if a mortal's hand snuffs it while people sleep, you feel it as a blow wherever you are and lose that house for a season. The addition, which you named yourself among your edges: every held house goes dark whenever you are senseless, and that includes every night you spend through the Quiet Door. You cannot keep the lamps and walk in a dream on the same night.

### The Held Breath

8. Holding your presence in. Granted, at a price. What it is: when you take it up, on purpose, over a few breaths, a god standing near enough to speak to does not feel that you are one like itself. Terms that always come with it:
   - It closes both ways. While you hold it you feel nothing of other gods either. One could stand at your elbow and you would not know it.
   - It hides the feeling and nothing else. Your body looks as it looks. A god may still work out what you are from your shape, your manner or what you do.
   - You can use no power while you hold it. Using one ends the holding at once, and any god within speaking distance then feels you all at once, as a shock.
   - It ends if you are struck, wounded, badly startled, or asleep.
   - It does nothing to mortals, who never felt you that way.
   - Your own terms stand on top of these: you hold it only while you stand or sit quiet, and you may hold it as long as you can do that.
   - Drawback, your first: while you hold it your hearing and sight fall to a mortal's, and you cannot speak a lie without a stammer anyone can hear.
   - Choose: take it as priced, or withdraw it.

### Your trades

9. The weakness you offer: a wound heals on you only as fast as on a mortal, and a bronze blade leaves a scar that stays open-looking for a year. Accepted as a weakness. It buys one of these; choose (a) or (b). It does not lighten the Held Breath.
   - (a) Five houses held, not three.
   - (b) The Quiet Door also opens on any mortal asleep in a Lit House you hold, met or not, at any distance. Remember that entering a dream darkens the houses for that night, so the sleeper's rest ends as you arrive.
10. The threshold. You offered to be unable to cross a threshold you have not been invited over. That is a real weakness for a keeper of houses. If you take it, it buys the other of (a) and (b). Choose: take it, or leave it.

### Left open on purpose

11. What another god's power can do to you or a held house is ruled when it happens.
