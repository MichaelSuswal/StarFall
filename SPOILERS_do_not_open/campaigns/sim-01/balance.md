# Balance: sim-01 (Night 2)

A rough weight of each god's total power at arrival: main powers, drawbacks, trades, hidden powers. The scale is the Keeper's own, 1 to 10, where 5 is the middle of this campaign's eight. It is a judgment, not a measure, and it is not the deep lore's density scale. Roughly equal is the aim, not exactly (DESIGN 3.15).

Spread: 4 to 5.5. No god is far out of line. No card was adjusted after confirmation, and none needed to be.

## God by god

The Cairn (player). The DM's lean note: below the middle at arrival, rising toward the middle and possibly a little above as the line grows. The Keeper agrees. Nothing on the card works on the day it lands; it has no power that harms, compels, hides, heals, feeds or moves fast; it took two heavy weaknesses for small returns. The voice is the one strong thing and is the only fast word in this world. Hidden powers were set at the fuller end for that reason: the cool one lets it trace lost roads marker by marker, the minor one counts added stones. On the DM's question about the felt weight of its word reaching other gods: the Keeper has no concern. Two NPC gods carry something of the same kind at a lighter price (a voice that cannot carry a known falsehood; a lie that hearers feel), so the Cairn's version, paid for with a total ban on falsehood, is not out of line. No question goes to the DM.

g1-stone. Three modest powers: a shelter, a long sense on bedrock, a return by spring. All defensive. It is the slowest god in the campaign by its own choices: a quarter slower always, half pace in winter, slower again per shelter. Its winter drawback bites in the season its people need it most. Holding in with no time limit is its one sharp tool. Hidden powers are small and merciful. Slightly below the middle.

g2-law. A wide ear for oaths with a witnessed thread at any distance, a hand that breaks one barrier a day, a voice that fills a square. Its body is a child's and is hurt as one. Every power has a lever an enemy can pull: staged oath-breaking floors it, a kept oath stuns the hand, and it cannot whisper. At the middle.

g3-song. The strongest raw kit: unmaking at will, hearing a place's sung past, steadying a company. Each is paid for in memory, hers and her people's, and the forgetting is real: the Keeper strikes things from her record. She can hold her presence in for a quarter day. A little above the middle, and the only god whose power use shrinks her.

g4-harvest. No power that touches a contest directly. A true number, a kept store paid for by the next harvest, a clean field that is also a dead one. In a world whose first fear is slow hunger this is worth more than it looks, but only with a people farming, and only over years. It cannot lie unfelt. Holding in with no time limit. The hidden cool power is large in effect and takes it out of the world while it runs. Slightly below the middle at arrival.

g5-fate. A lean on chance worth a moderate step, three a day, paid back in kind within the season; a mile of fog she cannot end and that blinds her own; oaths heard and their breaking felt. The fog is the best tactical tool any god has at arrival. The Debt makes the lean close to a loan. A little above the middle.

g6-dreams. Reaches one sleeper a night, holds five houses of honest rest, and cannot do both on one night. Cannot cross an uninvited threshold; heals as a mortal. The gentlest kit, with the widest quiet reach into mortal minds, which could matter a great deal over years. Slightly below the middle.

g7-beasts. The strongest body, a day's-walk sense of every beast, speech and binding bargains with beasts, a table that stops a blow. No hiding, by choice. Its horn was ruled so it is never a source of salt. It cannot walk away from a fair bargain at its own table. A little above the middle at arrival, mostly from the body and the Count.

## What the Keeper watches

- Three gods hear oaths in some form (g2, g5, and the player's felt word). They were built apart and were not harmonized. Where they meet, the cards rule.
- Five gods can hold their presence in. Two refused it. The player was offered it and did not ask. So in any meeting, either side may be hidden, and a hidden god is blind to gods in turn. This shifts first contacts toward caution and toward mistakes. It is a fact of this campaign now and the world's politics will deal with it.
- g2 and g3 both unmake made things. Sealed doors are few in this world (Night 1). What their powers do at one is ruled by the same sentence on both cards: the craft goes, whatever else was holding it stays.
- No NPC god stays noticeably stronger. Nothing is logged under DESIGN 3.15.
