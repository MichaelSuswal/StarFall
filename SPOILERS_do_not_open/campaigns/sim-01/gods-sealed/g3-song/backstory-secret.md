# Secret backstory: Amarreth (`g3-song`)

Campaign: sim-01. Written by the Keeper on creation Night 2 (2026-11-18). Consistent with `deep-lore.md` Truth 5, which wins wherever this file and it could be read apart. Nothing here is placed in the world; what of it can surface is fixed on Night 3.

## The country that fell

Under the deep lore's Truth 5, the eight gods of this campaign are what one far starfall threw up: the most-held patterns of one country that died. None of them was a person. Each is a people's long memory of something they followed, leaned on or prayed at. The names below are names that country's people used; the "events" are that people's own stories, which is the only form in which a god carries them. The full account is in `S/gods-sealed/the-country-that-fell.md`.

The country was called Vessary by the people who lived in it. One long river, the Anwy, ran through it from a mountain called the Orrun down to a fog coast at Anwy Strand. In its last age it was old and tired, with harvests failing year on year. Four things happened close together near its end, and every god of this campaign carries a piece of them:

1. The Judgment at Tessaly Ford. The twin courts split, the matter went to the lot, and the court of the sworn word put down the court of leave.
2. The Calling-In. The granary kingdom of Marrowmead called in the seed it had lent the hill people of Orrun's Dale, by the letter of an old oath between the two.
3. The Closing Winter. The Orrun pass shut. The hall's servants drove the herds down the road the night before. The Dale people wintered in the mountain's rock-cut halls with no seed and no herds, and none came down in spring.
4. The Roll finished. At the Low House the song of the country's names, sworn never to be closed, was sung to its last cadence.

Then the star came down. Nothing in the gods' memories reaches that night.

## What this god is

The Low House at Senna's Reach: the people's memory of the singers who held the Roll, the song of every name in Vessary, and of the house that took the names of the dead in and gave them back each year. Cradle and grave of the country's memory.

## The rest of the story behind the outline

The god's outline says: a low house, a long table, a hum under the floor; holding the old songs when others forgot; a reliance with a price agreed to; then a breaking, something that held let go of, and the ground not staying where it was. The rest: the singers were sworn never to close the Roll. Each year's singing ended on an open line handed to the next year, so the song of the country was never finished. In the last year, with the Dale's names newly among the dead, the eldest singer, Mother Ilse Varr, sang on to a closing cadence so that they might rest. After that year the edge villages could not say their own names, and then the star came down. Whether the closing caused the end or only marked it, the Keeper does not write here; the deep lore keeps why the plane keeps accounts. The people who remembered the Low House believed it did.

## The wound, and what it does not know

It broke an oath and the world broke with it. It does not know what the oath was, that the breaking was a mercy, or that the hum under the floor was the Roll itself, sung so long it had soaked into the house. It holds oaths hard and cannot say why. Its indifference to a hearer's hurt is the indifference of a list: a name is sung in its place whether or not anyone weeps.

## The Keeper trait, explained here and nowhere else

The song it knows whole and must not finish is the Roll of Vessary, open line and all. It is the only complete record of the country the eight gods came from. If it were ever sung to its close again, the Keeper rules on Night 3 what that would do; nothing is promised here.

## Where its past touched others

Every other god's making is a verse of the Roll: the Orrun, the Hand, the Kest and the Lyse, the Tallymen, the Grey Mother, the Night Guest, and Keld Anwy among the cairns. It touched all of them and knows none of them. Recorded here and in the common file.

## How this past might surface

- Another god's name, or the name of a far river, heard by her and found to sit in a tune she already has.
- Her hidden power The Roll, which is the old office begun again with living names.
- Her own wish: that nothing broken is ever finished being sung, and that someone is still listening at the last verse.
- A mortal singer here who closes a song in a way that makes her go still.
- If this star has carriers (Night 3), wanderers whose lists she can follow further than they can.
Each admits other readings: all old songs share tunes; grief makes anyone go still.
