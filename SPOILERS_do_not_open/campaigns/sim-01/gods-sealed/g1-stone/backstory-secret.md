# Secret backstory: Orrun (`g1-stone`)

Campaign: sim-01. Written by the Keeper on creation Night 2 (2026-11-18). Consistent with `deep-lore.md` Truth 5, which wins wherever this file and it could be read apart. Nothing here is placed in the world; what of it can surface is fixed on Night 3.

## The country that fell

Under the deep lore's Truth 5, the eight gods of this campaign are what one far starfall threw up: the most-held patterns of one country that died. None of them was a person. Each is a people's long memory of something they followed, leaned on or prayed at. The names below are names that country's people used; the "events" are that people's own stories, which is the only form in which a god carries them. The full account is in `S/gods-sealed/the-country-that-fell.md`.

The country was called Vessary by the people who lived in it. One long river, the Anwy, ran through it from a mountain called the Orrun down to a fog coast at Anwy Strand. In its last age it was old and tired, with harvests failing year on year. Four things happened close together near its end, and every god of this campaign carries a piece of them:

1. The Judgment at Tessaly Ford. The twin courts split, the matter went to the lot, and the court of the sworn word put down the court of leave.
2. The Calling-In. The granary kingdom of Marrowmead called in the seed it had lent the hill people of Orrun's Dale, by the letter of an old oath between the two.
3. The Closing Winter. The Orrun pass shut. The hall's servants drove the herds down the road the night before. The Dale people wintered in the mountain's rock-cut halls with no seed and no herds, and none came down in spring.
4. The Roll finished. At the Low House the song of the country's names, sworn never to be closed, was sung to its last cadence.

Then the star came down. Nothing in the gods' memories reaches that night.

## What this god is

The Orrun: the people of Orrun's Dale's long memory of the mountain that sheltered them. Not a mountain spirit and not a person. The shape of being kept alive through winter by rock.

## The rest of the story behind the outline

The god's outline says: something large and old that small things lived on and in; arms too short, a door that would not hold; a people lost, perhaps its fault. The rest: the Dale folk wintered every year in the Doors, halls cut into the Orrun's rock, and counted the mountain as one of the household. In the Closing Winter they went in with no seed and no herds, because the seed had been called in by Marrowmead and the hall's servants had driven the herds down the Line the night before the pass shut. The headwoman Hedra Coll kept the count every night: so many under the roof, and one for the Orrun. The count fell all winter. The Doors held; the shelter did not fail. There was nothing in them to eat.

## The wound, and what it does not know

It failed to save a people. It does not know that the shelter was sound and the failure was hunger; it feels a door that would not hold because that is the only failure a shelter can imagine. It does not know its people were betrayed by a sibling kingdom, abandoned by its own servants, or that a court ruled against them. Its pride and its wariness are both older than this and both were fed by it. It has lost the people, their names, and the knowledge that it was not its fault alone.

## The Keeper trait, explained here and nowhere else

Its count is always one more than are under its roof because the Dale's count ended "and one for the Orrun". It is counting itself, in a voice that is no longer there.

## How this past might surface

- The shared name. It will one day hear that another is called Orrun.
- A notched left ear on a god, the mark its own herds bore.
- The word "Door" for a shelter, if its people come to use it unprompted.
- A count said aloud in the old form, a number "and one for" something.
- Its hidden power One More, which is the count coming right for one night.
- If a Remainder fell in this world (Night 3), dreams near it of a winter hall and a count going down.
