# The country that fell: Vessary

Campaign: sim-01. Written by the Keeper on creation Night 2 (2026-11-18). The common ground of the eight secret backstories. Consistent with `deep-lore.md` Truth 5. This is a far country under another star, gone. Nothing of it stands in this campaign's world. What reaches this world is the eight gods, and whatever else Night 3's star rolls allow.

## The land

Vessary lay along one river, the Anwy. At its head stood the Orrun, a mountain with one high pass, and under it the high valleys of Orrun's Dale. Below the hills the river crossed the grain plain of Marrowmead, with a single low barrow hill, the Garrow, in sight of the fields. At the middle crossing stood the law town of Tessaly Ford. Lower, in the marsh at Senna's Reach, stood the Low House of the singers. The river ended in fog at Anwy Strand. One road ran the whole length, from the pass to the Strand. The people called it the Line. It was marked by cairns, each with a name, and broken into stages by lamp-houses where a light was kept for travelers. The last lamp-house stood at Anwy Strand.

## The founding story, as Vessary told it

Two brothers, Orrun and Marrow, came down the river. One took the mountain and one the plain. They swore the Brothers' Oath: the plain lends seed to the hills in bad years, the hills give salt and keep the pass open, and what is lent may be called in. The hill folk called the mountain by the first brother's name and counted it as one of the household. The plain buried its kings, the Tallymen, in the Garrow, and said each one slept there and would wake at need.

## The institutions the gods are made of

- The Orrun: the sheltering mountain, with rock-cut winter halls called the Doors. Its people's long memory of being kept alive by it. This is g1-stone.
- The Orrun's Hand: the bonded stewards of the pass hall, who drove the herds, kept the salt and the lamp tallow, served at table and were never seated at it. They carried the master's name, as servants there did, and were notched in the left ear like the herd. This is g7-beasts.
- The Kest and the Lyse: the twin courts of Tessaly Ford, each headed by a child judge chosen young, one left-handed and one right. The Kest, the left hand, held the sworn word to its letter. The Lyse, the right hand, could give the Hearth's Leave, releasing a breach when the wronged forgave it. This is g2-law, and the sibling it lost.
- The Tallymen of Marrowmead: the granary kings who counted the stores, said the number plainly, and slept in the Garrow. This is g4-harvest.
- The Grey Mother of the Ford: the keeper of the lots at Tessaly Ford, where anything the courts could not settle, and every levy, was drawn for. Fog on the water was hers. This is g5-fate.
- The Low House at Senna's Reach: the singers who held the Roll, a song made of the names of every village, ford, hall and line of the dead in Vessary, sung each year and sworn never to be closed: each year's singing ended on an open line handed to the next. This is g3-song.
- The Night Guest: what travelers prayed to at each lamp-house on the Line for a quiet night, and what kept the last lamp at Anwy Strand. This is g6-dreams.
- The cairns of the Line: touched and added to by every traveler. The one below the pass on the low side, where those who had come down out of the mountain added a stone for having got through, was called Keld Anwy, which meant something near "go on, the river is ahead". This is the player's god.

## The end, in order

1. The Judgment at Tessaly Ford. In a run of bad harvests Marrowmead moved to call in the seed lent to the Dale. The Dale asked the Lyse for leave. The twin judges split one to one, as they often did, and as custom had it the matter went to the lot at the ford. The lot fell for the Kest. The people's story says the Grey Mother leaned it, because the plain was her own river people and they were hungry. The court of the word then ruled that a stay of a sworn debt was itself oath-breaking and dissolved the court of leave. The right-hand child, the last Lyse, was put out of the hall that night. The story does not say what became of the child, only that the left-hand judge sat alone afterward and used the right hand for everything.
2. The Calling-In. The seed came down from the Dale in the autumn, carried by a levy drawn by lot at the ford. The story says one lot, a ferryman's boy named Tam Oly, was palmed by the Grey Mother, who loved him, and another went in his place. Tam Oly drowned at the ford in the next spring's flood. The last Tallyman, Edric Vane, counted the seed into the Marrowmead granaries and said the number plainly. He is remembered as saying, when he was laid in the Garrow, that he would not have to take anything back the next time.
3. The Closing Winter. The pass shut early. The night before it shut, the last Orrun's Hand, a steward called Brask, drove the herds down the Line with the salt and the bondsmen, past Keld Anwy, where each of them added a stone. The Dale's headwoman, Hedra Coll, took her people into the Doors for the winter with no seed and no herds. She kept the count each night in the old form, so many under the roof, and one for the Orrun. The count went down through the winter. No one came down past Keld Anwy in spring. No hand ever added a stone to it again.
4. The lamps. With the Dale dead and the drovers scattered, the Line emptied. The lamp-houses went dark one stage at a time from the mountain end. The keeper of the last, Old Dorn Hale at Anwy Strand, hung his lamp on a bone hook in the window every night for whoever was still coming down the road, until he did not.
5. The Roll. News of the Dale came to the Low House. The eldest singer, Mother Ilse Varr, sang the Dale's names in their place, and then, out of pity or exhaustion, sang the Roll on to a closing cadence, so that they might rest. It was the one thing the singers were sworn never to do. After that year the villages at the edges of Vessary could not say what they were called.
6. The fall. Not remembered by anything.

## How the eight pasts touch

- g1-stone and g7-beasts: master and servant of one house. They carry one name because the servant bore the master's. The servant's flight and the people's death are the same winter. Logged.
- g1-stone and g4-harvest: the two brother-kingdoms. What g4 took back is what g1's people starved for. g4's "sibling" is the Dale. Logged.
- g2-law and g4-harvest: the Kest's judgment is what let the Calling-In go forward.
- g2-law and g5-fate: the lot that ended the Lyse was leaned by the Grey Mother. g5's dim sense of having done something to one like herself is this. Logged.
- g3-song and all: the Roll held every name the others are made from. Its closing is the last thing that country did.
- g6-dreams and the player: the Line's cairns led from lamp to lamp. What waited at the end of the player's road was the lamp at Anwy Strand. Logged.
- g7-beasts and the player: the last hands that ever added a stone to Keld Anwy were the fleeing drovers'. Logged.
- g1-stone and the player: Keld Anwy stood under the Orrun pass, waiting for a people who never came down.

## What no god will ever be told

That these were institutions and not persons. Whose remainder each is. Under the deep lore a god may learn in one campaign that it is a remainder of a far fall, and never learn whose. Nothing in play states any of this. It surfaces only as marks, words and likenesses, and each admits more than one reading.
