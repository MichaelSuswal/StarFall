# Secret backstory: Garrow (`g4-harvest`)

Campaign: sim-01. Written by the Keeper on creation Night 2 (2026-11-18). Consistent with `deep-lore.md` Truth 5, which wins wherever this file and it could be read apart. Nothing here is placed in the world; what of it can surface is fixed on Night 3.

## The country that fell

Under the deep lore's Truth 5, the eight gods of this campaign are what one far starfall threw up: the most-held patterns of one country that died. None of them was a person. Each is a people's long memory of something they followed, leaned on or prayed at. The names below are names that country's people used; the "events" are that people's own stories, which is the only form in which a god carries them. The full account is in `S/gods-sealed/the-country-that-fell.md`.

The country was called Vessary by the people who lived in it. One long river, the Anwy, ran through it from a mountain called the Orrun down to a fog coast at Anwy Strand. In its last age it was old and tired, with harvests failing year on year. Four things happened close together near its end, and every god of this campaign carries a piece of them:

1. The Judgment at Tessaly Ford. The twin courts split, the matter went to the lot, and the court of the sworn word put down the court of leave.
2. The Calling-In. The granary kingdom of Marrowmead called in the seed it had lent the hill people of Orrun's Dale, by the letter of an old oath between the two.
3. The Closing Winter. The Orrun pass shut. The hall's servants drove the herds down the road the night before. The Dale people wintered in the mountain's rock-cut halls with no seed and no herds, and none came down in spring.
4. The Roll finished. At the Low House the song of the country's names, sworn never to be closed, was sung to its last cadence.

Then the star came down. Nothing in the gods' memories reaches that night.

## What this god is

The Tallymen of Marrowmead: the people's memory of the granary kings of the plain, who counted the stores and said the number plainly, were buried one after another in the low barrow called the Garrow, and were each said to sleep there and wake at need.

## The rest of the story behind the outline

The god's outline says: lying a long time under ground that was not cold, with weight over it, and being expected; fields at day's end; being the one who counted; a guilt in the hands, and a place beside it where someone ought to be walking. The rest: Marrowmead and Orrun's Dale were founded, the story said, by two brothers, and were bound by the Brothers' Oath: seed lent to the hills in bad years, and what is lent may be called in. In the last run of bad harvests the last Tallyman, Edric Vane, called it in, lawfully, by the letter, to keep the plain alive. The Dale wintered without seed and died. The sibling it killed is the brother-kingdom. The god took "Garrow" for its name because it is the name of the hill it lay in.

## The wound, and what it does not know

It killed a sibling. It does not know that the sibling was a people, that the killing was a levy and was lawful, or that the hands remember because the hands counted the Dale's seed into its own bins. Its fear of what it does when it takes is exact. Its wish not to have to take anything back this time is the last Tallyman's own saying. It expects to wake to glad people because that was the promise made over every king laid in the hill.

## The Keeper trait, explained here and nowhere else

It knows the long sleep because it is made of a line of sleepers. Whether the sleep comes for it in this world is its hidden power's matter and is never scheduled.

## Where its past touched others

- g2-law's court upheld its claim. g5-fate's lot drew the levy that carried the seed.

## How this past might surface

- Its hidden power Seed Knows Seed: levied grain burns its palm, and it cannot tell that from guilt.
- Its own drawback on Keeping, borrowed from the next harvest. It rebuilt the Calling-In as a power and priced it on itself.
- A low hill in sight of its fields, which it asked the world for without knowing why.
- A god of stone before whom its hands shake.
- Empty granaries, if this world holds any (a Night 1 candidate, unplaced), which will read to it as an accusation.
Each admits other readings: a farmer's caution, a hill is a hill, old barns are old barns.
