# Secret backstory: Kest (`g2-law`)

Campaign: sim-01. Written by the Keeper on creation Night 2 (2026-11-18). Consistent with `deep-lore.md` Truth 5, which wins wherever this file and it could be read apart. Nothing here is placed in the world; what of it can surface is fixed on Night 3.

## The country that fell

Under the deep lore's Truth 5, the eight gods of this campaign are what one far starfall threw up: the most-held patterns of one country that died. None of them was a person. Each is a people's long memory of something they followed, leaned on or prayed at. The names below are names that country's people used; the "events" are that people's own stories, which is the only form in which a god carries them. The full account is in `S/gods-sealed/the-country-that-fell.md`.

The country was called Vessary by the people who lived in it. One long river, the Anwy, ran through it from a mountain called the Orrun down to a fog coast at Anwy Strand. In its last age it was old and tired, with harvests failing year on year. Four things happened close together near its end, and every god of this campaign carries a piece of them:

1. The Judgment at Tessaly Ford. The twin courts split, the matter went to the lot, and the court of the sworn word put down the court of leave.
2. The Calling-In. The granary kingdom of Marrowmead called in the seed it had lent the hill people of Orrun's Dale, by the letter of an old oath between the two.
3. The Closing Winter. The Orrun pass shut. The hall's servants drove the herds down the road the night before. The Dale people wintered in the mountain's rock-cut halls with no seed and no herds, and none came down in spring.
4. The Roll finished. At the Low House the song of the country's names, sworn never to be closed, was sung to its last cadence.

Then the star came down. Nothing in the gods' memories reaches that night.

## What this god is

The Kest: the court of the sworn word at Tessaly Ford, and the long line of left-handed child judges who headed it. The people's memory of a law that held an oath to its letter and did not look away first.

## The rest of the story behind the outline

The god's outline says: old, small, not alone; something its own size once stood beside it and does not now; its hands go cold near the thought. The rest: Tessaly Ford had twin courts under twin child judges. The Kest, the left hand, held the word. The Lyse, the right hand, gave the Hearth's Leave, mending a breach when the wronged forgave it. When Marrowmead moved to call in the Dale's seed, the Dale asked the Lyse for leave. The judges split, the matter went to the lot, the lot fell for the Kest, and the Kest's court then ruled that staying a sworn debt was itself a breaking, and dissolved its twin. The last Lyse, a child, was put out of the hall that night. The Kest sat alone afterward and did with the right hand everything the Lyse had done, except forgive. The god chose "Kest" for itself tonight because it is what it was called.

## The wound, and what it does not know

It killed a sibling. It does not know that the sibling was the other half of the law, that the killing was a judgment and not a blow, that the lot which licensed it was leaned by another, or that a people starved downstream of it. It is quick to anger because it has been judging alone ever since. It holds back longest from what matters most because the one time it did not, this happened.

## The Keeper trait, explained here and nowhere else

It is left-handed and does everything with the right because the right hand's work was its twin's, and it took that work over and has never let itself notice.

## Where its past touched others

- g4-harvest is the kingdom whose claim it upheld.
- g1-stone's people are who paid.

## How this past might surface

- Its own ruling from Night 2: an oath kept to the letter and betrayed in spirit does not snap. It built its own blindness into its power.
- Its hidden power The Hearth's Leave, which is the Lyse's office, in the left hand.
- The words "leave" and "letter" in a mortal court's mouth.
- A god of fog who, asked about a lot, goes quieter and plainer.
- The wish it has never dared: a first oath kept and never known. In Vessary that was the Brothers' Oath, and its court is what turned it into a weapon.
Each admits other readings: a child's handedness, a judge's caution, a common proverb.
