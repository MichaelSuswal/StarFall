# Secret backstory: Orvane (`g6-dreams`)

Campaign: sim-01. Written by the Keeper on creation Night 2 (2026-11-18). Consistent with `deep-lore.md` Truth 5, which wins wherever this file and it could be read apart. Nothing here is placed in the world; what of it can surface is fixed on Night 3.

## The country that fell

Under the deep lore's Truth 5, the eight gods of this campaign are what one far starfall threw up: the most-held patterns of one country that died. None of them was a person. Each is a people's long memory of something they followed, leaned on or prayed at. The names below are names that country's people used; the "events" are that people's own stories, which is the only form in which a god carries them. The full account is in `S/gods-sealed/the-country-that-fell.md`.

The country was called Vessary by the people who lived in it. One long river, the Anwy, ran through it from a mountain called the Orrun down to a fog coast at Anwy Strand. In its last age it was old and tired, with harvests failing year on year. Four things happened close together near its end, and every god of this campaign carries a piece of them:

1. The Judgment at Tessaly Ford. The twin courts split, the matter went to the lot, and the court of the sworn word put down the court of leave.
2. The Calling-In. The granary kingdom of Marrowmead called in the seed it had lent the hill people of Orrun's Dale, by the letter of an old oath between the two.
3. The Closing Winter. The Orrun pass shut. The hall's servants drove the herds down the road the night before. The Dale people wintered in the mountain's rock-cut halls with no seed and no herds, and none came down in spring.
4. The Roll finished. At the Low House the song of the country's names, sworn never to be closed, was sung to its last cadence.

Then the star came down. Nothing in the gods' memories reaches that night.

## What this god is

The Night Guest: what travelers on the Line prayed to at each lamp-house for a quiet night, and the people's memory of the keepers who hung a light for whoever was still on the road. Bound to a threshold: the Guest was in the house and nowhere else.

## The rest of the story behind the outline

The god's outline says: lamps in a row; hands bringing bread, a lock of hair, a child's name said over a bed; being asked for rest, for a gentle night, for a dead one to be quiet; then the lamps going out, not all at once, and the voices stopping one by one. The rest: the lamp-houses stood a day's stage apart the length of the Line. When the Dale died and the drovers scattered, no one came down the road, and the houses went dark one stage at a time from the mountain end. The last was at Anwy Strand. Its keeper, Old Dorn Hale, hung his lamp on a bone hook in the window every night for whoever was still coming, until he did not. The god carries that hook. It was not cast out and did nothing to deserve it. The road ended, and it was left.

## The wound, and what it does not know

It was once worshipped and then forgotten. It does not know that it was forgotten because its worshippers died or fled, not because they turned from it. It does not know it was always tied to a threshold; it chose tonight, freely, to be unable to cross one uninvited, and so rebuilt its own binding. Its caution at the edge of things is a keeper's: it waits in the house for the traveler to come to it.

## The Keeper trait, explained here and nowhere else

No one keeps its face because no one ever saw it. The Guest was a lamp in a window and a quiet night. The uncannily even face it wears is a face no one remembered wrong, because no one remembered it at all.

## Where its past touched others

- The player's god is a cairn of the road its lamps stood on. What waited at the end of that god's road was the lamp at Anwy Strand. Recorded in both files.
- g7-beasts slept in its houses on the way down, the last guests it had.

## How this past might surface

- The bone lamp-hook, and its hidden power The Last Lamp, which draws the lost as the houses did.
- Its own hoped-for custom, a lamp in the window for the traveler.
- A god of stacked stones who says a road once ran to a place where something was waiting. Neither will know what it is hearing.
- A line of cairns in this world leading toward a house it holds.
- A mortal who says its name over a child's bed.
Each admits other readings: a common custom, a keepsake, a shared melancholy.
