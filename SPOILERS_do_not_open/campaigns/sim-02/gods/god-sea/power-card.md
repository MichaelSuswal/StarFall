# Power card: the Factor (god-sea)

Campaign: sim-02. Written on creation Night 2, 2026-11-19, from the final text of round 2 as the god confirmed it, with the wording corrections of its round 2 reply. Description and Drawback text below is frozen. Limits settled at creation were settled at the Night 2 table; the arguments are in the sealed ledger.

## Form

Filed under the Factor; it holds no name. Those who do not know it call it the Factor, the Broker, or the one in the damp coat.

A broad, middling figure, a little under a tall man's height, heavy through the shoulder and loose in the limb, built to stand on a deck in a swell. It walks with a slight roll, feet set wide. The outline is steady: anyone can say "a stout someone in a long coat." The face is not: each person who looks sees a different face, a stranger's, plausible and forgettable, and a different one the next time; the face never frightens and never flatters. The coat's hem is always wet, however dry the day, and wets what it touches no more than any damp coat would. The hands are ordinary and warm. The voice is low, quick and friendly, and carries across a crowded room without being raised.

Bodies: one. A god in this world has one body and is in one place.

What the form can and cannot do: the face no two see alike is a face, not a hiding. The god is always hungry and food does nothing for it.

Senses: taste and smell keen, far past a mortal's; hearing good, best for a crowd; sight ordinary in light. Touch and any sense not named are unsettled and asked in play, never inferred.

Pace: mortal pace on land, by the pace table. In water, see the Bottom Walk.

God-sense, as set for this campaign: a god knows another god only face to face, in person, within sight and speaking distance, each seen by the other; the knowing is mutual, immediate and sure, and tells only that the other is a god. At a distance there is nothing to feel. Every god knows from landing only that others like it came down when it did.

When the body is broken. If the body is broken or taken, the god is gone from that place. Where it fell it leaves one thing, a single salt-crusted coin from nowhere. After one turn of the moon the god returns, up out of the nearest open water to where it fell, wherever that is; it cannot choose where. If no open water lies near, it comes up at the nearest sea. If someone keeps the coin dry and away from water, the god does not return until the coin is wet again; the coin can be found, taken, spent or lost, and the god cannot tell where it is. A god who sees the god broken sees it happen; a god's hand on the coin is unruled until it happens.

### Definitions

- Open water: water open to the air. Not water in a vessel or a barrel; not frozen water.
- In sight: what the god can see by any light; for Slack Water, also water it can hear at its feet in the dark.
- A night held: one night or any part of one in which the god holds Slack Water.
- "Done": the word said aloud by the god over terms another has heard.

### Things carried

- A long, heavy coat, always damp at the hem. A coat.
- A leather satchel.
- A small brass pan scale with a set of plain bronze weights.
- A purse of mixed coins from nowhere it can place. The coin left behind when the body is broken comes from the purse and is a coin.

## Powers

### Slack Water
Status: main
Description: Within sight of the god, on water open to the air, the god can still a surface to glass or raise a chop or a surge, along the whole stretch of coast in its sight, for as long as it holds its attention on it, up to a night. Boats feel a calm or a squall and no one can trace it to the god, since it looks like weather turning. It moves water only: no storm from a clear sky, no holding up a wave the wind has made, no reach to water out of sight.
Drawback: Each night held takes something small from the god, up front, that it did not agree to give. The god is not told what went.
Limits settled at creation:
  - The god's first wording, kept as intent: "a bay or a harbor mouth at the widest." The whole coast in sight was bought by the weakness (Trades).
  - How the drawback is enacted: for each night held (Definitions) the Keeper removes one item from the god's records: one entry of its record of other gods (which holds its debts, promises and guesses) while that record has an entry to give, and one line of its goals only when it does not. The last line of the goals is never taken. The god does not choose the item and is not told what went.
  - Light and dark: in the dark it works while the god can see the water by any light or hear it at its feet.
  - Distance: only what is in sight.
  - Falling: not while falling. Carrying: nothing.
  - Not water in a vessel or barrel; not frozen water. It moves salt water only and makes none sweet. In an estuary where river and salt are mixed, the water counts as salt while the god can taste or smell the salt in it.
  - Being watched: mortals see weather.
  - Other gods: another god in sight sees the god and what it does; water another god has acted on, or a god's power in sight, is unruled until it happens. A warded hull is unruled until it happens.
  - The god's own people thank the weather.
Rulings:

Trades: the coast was bought by the weakness (Weaknesses taken). Recorded both ways.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### Keeping What Sinks
Status: main
Description: Standing in water or touching it, the god feels what has sunk in the water around it: what lies there, how many, how deep, where, and roughly how long it has been down. It learns nothing of who lost it, why, or what it meant. It reaches a fair way across a bay or river mouth, no farther than a long walk.
Drawback: The use dulls the god's taste for days; nothing has flavor, and the deeper or heavier the find the longer the dullness.
Limits settled at creation:
  - The dullness runs from one day for a small thing (a pot, an anchor stone) to ten days for a ship or anything heavier, set by the Keeper for each find.
  - The great old things of this world: it does not reach them. Anything that is more than a sunk thing of mortal making is felt only as weight at a depth, nothing more. The god asked for this limit.
  - Touch and not sight: light and dark are nothing.
  - Distance: only water the god is in or touching; a well or cistern if touched.
  - Falling: falling into water, it works on the way down.
  - Carrying: what the god carries is not felt.
  - Being watched: nothing shows.
  - Other gods: not felt in water, nor what they carry.
  - The god's own people: it may tell them where a wreck lies.
  - It shows a place and an age, never a cause.
Rulings:
  - (none yet)
Trades: none.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

### The Bottom Walk
Status: main
Description: The god can walk into water and keep walking. It breathes water, sees dimly in it, and moves through it on the bed or off it, to any depth, without harm from cold or pressure: at half again a mortal's walking pace in the first ten fathoms, and at a mortal's walking pace below that.
Drawback: Each time the god comes up from deep water it carries out a cold that sits in the joints for a day, and for as long as the cold lasts it cannot speak a lie.
Limits settled at creation:
  - While the cold lasts the god may be silent, and may say a true thing that misleads; only a false statement is barred.
  - Deep water: below ten fathoms.
  - Light and dark: dim sight in water, none in total dark.
  - Distance: no reach; it is walking.
  - Falling: a soft landing in water, a hard one on land.
  - Carrying: a mortal in the god's arms breathes nothing; no one is carried below.
  - Being watched: anyone who sees the god walk in sees it go.
  - Other gods: face to face, as always.
  - The god's own people: their water does not call it.
  - On land the god is a mortal walker; that is form.
Rulings:
  - (none yet)
Trades: the quicker pace in the first ten fathoms was bought by the weakness (Weaknesses taken). Recorded both ways.
Growth: Possible. Not shown.

## Weaknesses taken

### The Bound Word
What it is: when the god says "done" aloud over terms that another has heard, its body cannot act against those terms until they are met, or the other releases it, or the terms lapse by their own words (a bargain "until the tide turns" ends when the tide turns). It cannot walk off, vanish, break a window, or use a power against them. It binds the body, not only the god's word.
How it can be used against the god: a mortal who learns the word can hold the god to a bad bargain made in haste; a rival can bait it into terms; and it binds hardest at the moment the god would most want to leave. The god chooses when it says the word.
What it bought: Slack Water's reach along the whole coast in sight at one item a night, and the Bottom Walk's quicker pace in the first ten fathoms.
