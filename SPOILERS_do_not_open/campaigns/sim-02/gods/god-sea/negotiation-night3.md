# Negotiation: god-sea, Night 3

## Round 1 reply

I read the draft the way I read any bill, line by line, thumb on the total. I take Choice C with two clarifications and one counter. Everything else I accept.

### Edges
Accepted as stated. I proposed none and want none. A people priced as ordinary is a people nobody has looked at twice, which is the point.

### What was read as ordinary culture, unpriced
Accepted. I note the tide-readers are skill and I ask nothing of them.

### Size and shape
Choice: C. About 20,000, a great city for this age, paid for by the two weaknesses.

Reasons. I hoped for fifteen to thirty thousand because a quay wants a crowd, and twenty is inside what I hoped. Choice A is a harbor town, and a town is a room, not a market. Choice B is closer, but twelve thousand is a number I would be paying for in the same two coins anyway, since the grain weakness is the same in both. The second weakness is the one that buys the extra eight thousand, and I think it is a fair coin: crowded warm people are the thing I want, and a crowd that warm is a crowd a sickness loves. I would notice losing them to it, which is what a drawback is for. I accept thirty thousand refused. I asked for no ruler and I will not buy a hinterland to get a bigger count.

On the first weakness, the grain, I accept the draft as written, with stores at two months. I like it. A city that must trade for every cup is a city that haggles instead of fighting, and sellers who know their hold are sellers I can sit across a table from. It is the world I asked for in the survey.

On the second weakness, the sickness, I accept the shape and contest two points of wording, and I counter on one.

1. Wording, in the write-up and not a new power: "a share of its people" has no number. I would sooner weigh it before I buy it. I ask the Keeper to state the share, even as a range, for a sickness year. I do not contest a heavy number. I contest an unmeasured one, because an unmeasured loss is the kind that grows in the telling.

2. Wording: the draft has the neighbors closing their ports to the city. The grain weakness already has the neighbors holding their grain over the city. If a closed port also means no grain, that is one lever pulled twice and priced twice. I ask the Keeper to confirm in the text that a port closed against the city for sickness counts under the sickness weakness only, and that it stops the grain boats under the grain weakness only if the neighbors choose to embargo as well, which is a separate act in the world and not the same price. If the Keeper holds that the two are one lever, I accept that, and then I ask that the stores line of two months be left as it is and not tightened a third time.

3. Counter: "in any year a sickness comes by ship or by fog." Fog is a weather the world has already shown me for this coast, and I do not want the people to read a fog morning as a thing to fear every year, since I would rather they went on treating fog as weather and thinking no further. I ask that the sickness come by ship, as the draft says, and by fog only in a year the world chooses for its own reasons, not by default every year. The sickness is the people's weakness; it is not mine to pay twice. If the Keeper holds that the fog clause is what makes the price fair, I accept it as drafted and I will not argue it again.

Conduct, which buys and costs nothing, and which I add so it is on the table: I will not use Slack Water to open or hold a grain route against a neighbor's embargo or to push a surge on a port to settle the city's hunger. Trade is the people's own work, and I would sooner be a customer than a rescuer. I would still hold the coast still or raise it by my own judgment for the reasons I already said, seldom and for cause.

### What is the world's to decide
Accepted. I have heard "own history, own neighbors and a trouble you did not ask for," and I will meet them as they are. The weights-keeper I described is set as who she is. What she does is hers. If a custom, an office or a household I hoped for is not there when I arrive, I will stand at the edge of the crowd and listen until I learn what is.

### Summary of my positions
- Edges: accept, none.
- Culture: accept.
- Size: Choice C, about 20,000, both weaknesses.
- Grain weakness: accept, stores at two months.
- Sickness weakness: accept the shape. Ask for a stated share. Ask that the port closing and the grain embargo not be priced as one lever twice. Counter that fog is not a default yearly carrier. If refused on the fog point, I accept the draft.
- Thirty thousand refused: accept.
- World's to decide: accept.
- Conduct line on grain routes and surges: added, free.

## Round 2 reply

Confirmed. I have read the final text as a bill and the total matches what I agreed: about 20,000, two weaknesses, nothing else bought, thirty thousand refused. I correct no wording.

Reasons, point by point.

1. The share. One in twenty to one in eight for the city, one in forty to one in twenty for a default town, set by the year's dice with factors. That is a measured price and a heavy one, and I said I would not contest a heavy number. The range stated against the default town is what makes it a fair coin. Accepted.

2. One lever or two. Two acts, as I asked. A port closed for sickness shuts that port to the city's hulls and people and counts under the sickness weakness only. A seller's boats going on carrying grain, or stopping, is the seller's own act and counts under the grain weakness only if the seller makes it. I accept the plain follow-on that grain the city would have fetched in its own hulls from a closed port is not fetched, since it follows from the first act and is not a third price. Two months stays at two months. Accepted.

3. Fog. Accepted in substance. A sickness comes in whichever year the world brings one, by ship or by people on the roads, and fog carries nothing. What the people believe about fog is theirs, which is all I wanted, and I will not argue it further.

The conduct line on grain routes and surges stays mine, free, and I will keep it in play unless I change it for a reason I would say aloud.

The final text states both weaknesses as the people's and says neither is on my card. I confirm that, and I confirm that the Keeper sets the exact number and the starting stores, trade, treasure and mood.
