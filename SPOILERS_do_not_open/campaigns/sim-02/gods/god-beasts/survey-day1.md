## Answer 1

Cold water over stones at first light, and the sound of many feet moving together through tall grass. A wide land that is awake and does not look at you, because it has its own business. The old saying is that a land is true when the wolf and the shepherd can both be hungry in it and neither one lies about why. I want it like that. Plain, and a little dangerous, and honest about both.

## Answer 2

Steppe, and the broken hills at its edge where the grass gives out into stone and scrub. Open ground where a herd can be seen a day away and a people can pick up its tents and follow. Rivers to cross, not rivers to settle on. The saying goes, a stone that rolls gathers no wall, and I mean that as praise.

## Answer 3

Four seasons, each with its own teeth. A hard winter that thins the weak and teaches the strong to huddle. A thaw that floods the low ground. A short loud summer of calving and travel. And an autumn that is long, with the herds fat and moving and the air clear enough to hear a hoof from far off. Let the turns be fair and regular, so that a law written for one year can be read in the next. A law that must be rewritten every spring is a rope made of smoke.

When each new year finds me I want to wake into autumn, when the herds are on the move and the hunt is honest.

I wake into autumn.

## Answer 4

Some of each, but more wild than rooted. I want country no one has walked, because a hunter needs ground that has not learned his habits. I also want a few old kingdoms far off, standing and proud, so that my people have neighbors to measure themselves against and to trade with. A land with no neighbors has no mirror. But a land that is all kingdoms has no room to move.

## Answer 5

Old. Thousands of years, and ruins under ruins. Mortals have been writing and forgetting for a very long time here. The saying is that the oldest road was a game trail first. I want my people to walk on something that was walked on before them, and to know it.

## Answer 6

Rare, but real. A rumor most people doubt, and a few valleys where it is not a rumor. Closer to the doubted rumor than the wise-woman in every valley. If every mortal had it, no one would need law, and I want law. Magic should be something a keeper of the law has to account for, like weather.

## Answer 7

It should feel like borrowing from a lender who remembers. Cold in the hands, a taste of iron, a sense of being watched by something patient. It should cost them something they would notice losing: a season of strength, a name they used to answer to, a memory of someone's face. And the cost should be written down by those who pay it, so the next one knows the price. Nothing is free, and a bargain hidden is a bargain broken.

## Answer 8

Plainly above them, and I will not pretend otherwise. I am a giant and I will stand like one. But I do not want to be the one-eyed man in a kingdom of the blind, because that makes a tyrant of whoever stands there, and I do not want that. Let a god be much more than a great sorcerer, and let mortals know it, and let it still be possible for a clever mortal to out-argue one. Height is not wisdom. The tall tree is the first to be struck.

## Answer 9

Strange enough to be worth hunting. Mostly familiar animals, true to themselves, with the old legends attached to them. But in the far places, a handful of beasts with no name in any tongue, so that a hunter has something to learn and something to fear. The saying is that the best prey is the one that studies you back. I want a wild that is not finished being understood.

## Answer 10

Famine, and hard weather, and things that hunt back. Not demons or curses, but teeth, and cold, and a long dry year when the herds do not come. The best stories are the ones where a people could have survived and did not, because they ignored what the land was telling them. I want danger that can be read and still catches the careless.

## Answer 11

Salt. And good grazing in a bad year. Mortals will trade for salt and fight over water, and the people who hold the best ground in drought will be hated for it. Scarcity should be a thing a law can govern, and a thing a law can fail to govern. I want my people to have to decide who eats.

## Answer 12

Bones of something enormous. A rib standing out of a hillside like the beam of a fallen hall. Let mortals build tales on it, and let the tales be wrong, and let the truth be older than any of them. I would also take old roads that lead nowhere now. But the bones first.

## Answer 13

Yes, a few, and not many. Peoples who are not human but who keep to their own ways and do not want ours: a quiet folk of the high rocks, perhaps, who count in knots and do not speak to strangers. They should be neither enemies nor friends, but neighbors with their own law, and the two laws should sometimes meet at a river crossing and have to be reconciled. I would like to read both codes.

## Answer 14

A few. Very few. A traveler from far off is a rare and valuable thing, like a bird blown off its course. Let them bring one strange tool or one strange idea each, and let the idea be hard to use well. I would not want many, because a crowd of strangers is an invasion, and a handful is a gift. They would have to be asked what they came for, and I would want an honest answer.

## Answer 15

A knowing face to face, and not before. A pull at any distance would be a leash. I want to meet another god standing in front of me and take its measure with my own eyes, hear its voice, and decide for myself what it is. Let it be possible to be fooled. I am proud enough to want the chance to be right anyway.

## Answer 16

One body. A giant is one body, and one is enough to carry. If a god could be in many places, it would stop being in any of them. If there must be a way to be elsewhere, let it be dear: a sending that is thin and brief, that leaves the god weak and half-blind while it lasts, and that cannot be used twice in a day. The saying is that a hound with two masters hunts for neither.

## Answer 17

A keeper of the law. Someone who writes down what was said and what was meant, and refuses to bend it for a friend. A mortal who can stand in front of a god and say, that is not what you agreed to, and be right. I would hope for a scribe or a judge who walks with the herds and carries the whole law in memory and on skins. Stories about such a one would be worth following, because they would cost something.

## Answer 18

A truth. Something about the sky, and why it came down once. I do not know yet whether I want to find it, but I would like it to be findable. A place is a thing you reach; a power is a thing you are given; but a truth is a thing you have to be ready for. Let there be one waiting that I cannot yet imagine.

## Answer 19

That nothing in this world falls again. I have seen a sky come down, and I would rather not see it twice. If a thing must end, let it end slowly, with warning, in a way that can be written down and read by those who come after. That is the whole of it. I did not dare say it because it is small, and because a proud one does not like to ask.
