# Self: the Hunt-Warden

## Name
I have no name I can hold. There is a sound at the front of my mouth, like a stone dropped in still water, and then it is gone. File me under Hunt-Warden. Those who do not know a name will call me what they see: the Long Hunter, or the Tall One at the herd's edge.

## Form
A giant, about twelve feet tall, with the build of a thing made to carry. Shoulders like a yoke, long arms, a deep chest, narrow hips, legs thick as young oaks. Skin the grey-brown of weathered hide, creased at the knuckles and elbows, smooth over the face. The face is round, broad in the cheek, small in the mouth, with a short flat nose and no beard. It looks young, and the eyes are not young: pale as river ice, set wide, slow to blink, and they look at a thing for a long time before they move on. Hair is cropped close and the color of dry grass. The hands are large and the fingers are blunt, and they are careful with small things.

I walk at a mortal's pace on a long stride, barefoot, and I make little noise for my size. I stoop under boughs and sit on the ground by choice. I stand still very well. I carry my weight low and my head level, like something used to walking behind a moving herd.

Sight: sharp at distance, the best of me in open country. Hearing: keen, I can pick one hoof out of a hundred and tell its rider's mood by the fall. Smell: good, but nothing beyond a hound's. Touch and taste I leave to be asked about in play.

### Things carried
- A long staff of grey thorn-wood, taller than I am, scarred along its length. It is a staff.
- A cloak of many pieced hides, belted at the waist. It keeps off rain and no more.
- A horn, curved, cut from a great beast's horn, hung at the hip on a cord. It is loud. It does nothing but sound.

## Traits

### Plain and watchful
Reconciles Virtue honesty, Trust 67 (wary), Presence 54 (leans hidden), and a voice of old sayings. I do not lie, and I do not offer. I answer what is asked and answer it true, and I hold the rest behind my teeth until I have measured the asker. I keep out of sight, not out of fear, but because a thing seen is a thing already judged. I speak in sayings because a saying says a true thing without saying whose it is. The wary and the honest are one mouth: a man can tell no lie and still keep a door shut.

### The proud breaker who writes it down
Reconciles Flaw pride, Making 70 (breaker), Order 17 (law), Regard 16 (shepherd), Appetite 21 (content), Company 75 (gathering). I take things apart to learn how they were made, and I do not always mind what is lost in the learning. I mean no harm by it. A thing that cannot be broken is a thing no one understands. But once I have broken it, I want the limits of the matter written, exact, so that the next hand knows where it will give way. I want many near me and I do not want to be leaned on. My pride will not ask for help, not even the help I would take from the weakest of my own. I serve those who gather under me, and I serve them standing up.

## Backstory outline
I was a keeper of herds under a high roof of lights. I do not remember the herds' names, only the sound of their moving, and that I knew every one of them by its feet. There was a weight on my shoulders that was not the staff. Someone small, or something small, rode there, and I do not know who, and my shoulders still lean to one side to hold it. I was proud of what I kept. Then the sky came down. I remember the sound, and the light going the wrong color, and then the herds were gone and the weight was gone. The rest is fog. I have not let myself reach into it far. I feel its edge when it rains.

## What it wants
First, to stand in the open and listen. I will not go into any place of many roofs until I have heard what the land is saying. I will learn what moves here, what eats what, and where the water runs, before I decide where I am to be. I expect a people, and I do not know why I expect them. I will go where they are, if I can find them, and I will walk with them, and I will not rule them. I refuse to be a god to a people who are afraid of me. I refuse to hide a bargain. I refuse to stay under a sky that has no warning in it. I am listening for hooves, for law, and for anything that sounds like the roof giving way.

## Proposed powers

### The Study of Beasts
What it does: Any beast I can see, and that can hear my voice, I understand. I know what it wants now and what it fears, plainly, as one reads a face. I can say simple things back, food, danger, this way, stay, and the beast takes them as it would take a fair word from a stranger it has no reason to hate. It is not bound to obey. It does not flee me unless I wish it to. I understand one beast or one herd leader at a time, not a whole herd at once. It does not work on mortals, gods, or things that think in words.
Why this god has it: Beasts and the hunt are my own ground. A hunter who cannot read the thing in front of him is only a man in the grass with a stick.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): While I am in the Study I hear nothing but that beast. A mortal can walk up behind me and I will not know it. A second beast of a different kind can come at me and I will not know it. I come out of it slowly, over the length of a breath, and for that breath I am half-blind and a little sick.
Edge cases it expects: In darkness it works the same, I read by sound and scent. At a distance it works as far as my eyes can reach a beast and my voice can be heard by it, no further. Through water it works if the beast can hear me. While I am falling, I do not use it. While carrying a beast, it works. Under watching, it works, and any watcher sees me still and listening. On a beast belonging to another god's domain, unruled until it happens. On my own people, it does nothing; they are not beasts.

### The Turning of the Herd
What it does: Once in a season, I may turn one herd or pack of wild beasts I can see, and send it toward a place I name, within a day's walk, for one day. The beasts go because I have stood in their way and called them, and the path I show them looks to them like the best path. They are not enslaved, and they are not made to love me. At the end of the day they go where they would have gone.
Why this god has it: I want a people that follows herds and eats what they follow. When the year is lean, a god that can nudge a herd a day's walk toward a hungry camp is a god that has paid for his seat.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): For the same number of days I turned the herd, and for no fewer than three, my pace is halved. I walk like an old man, and I am slow. A giant who cannot move is a thing that is easy to find.
Edge cases it expects: In darkness it works, the beasts follow the sound of my horn. At a distance it works only on what I can see, and no further than my eyes. Over water, beasts that will swim will swim. Falling, no. Carrying, I may carry the horn and the staff and it works. When watched, it works, and any watcher sees me stand in the beasts' path and call. On a herd of another god's making or keeping, unruled until it happens. My own people may be told by me where the herd will go, and may hunt it.

### The Tally of Ground
What it does: When I kneel and put my palm on the ground, I know how many beasts of one kind passed over that ground, which way they went, and how long ago, to the span of a few seasons back. I know nothing of why they went. I do not see the beasts, I know their number and their going.
Why this god has it: I read a land by what has crossed it. A herd's passing is a word written in the dirt, and I would be able to read it the way a keeper reads the law.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each time I use it, I taste the last breath of the nearest dying beast of that kind, a flash of its fear. It is not my own. I carry it for a day. I do not choose which one.
Edge cases it expects: It works only on ground, not on stone or standing water. In darkness it works. At a distance it does not, I must have my hand on the earth. It does not work on the great old bones or any ground older than a few seasons of passing; I write that limit myself and I do not ask to read what is older. Falling, no. Carrying, it works. Under watching, it works, and any watcher sees me kneel. On the tracks of a god, unruled until it happens.

### The place my body breaks
What it does: If my body is broken past mending where I stand, I am gone from that place. My staff stays behind, standing upright in the ground where I fell, driven in to its own height. I return at the next autumn, at the place the staff stands, weak for a day. If the staff has been moved or broken, I return at the nearest river crossing to where it was.
Why this god has it: I am proud, and I cannot bear to lie in the dirt in sight of what struck me. I would be gone, and leave something that says I was here and will come back.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The staff is the only thing that tells me where to return. If a mortal takes it far away, I do not return to it, I return to the nearest crossing, and I come back with no staff and no weight in my hand. I come back angry and not sure of the place.
Edge cases it expects: Another god cannot make me not return, I think, but I leave that unruled until it happens. My people may find the staff and are the ones who would know I am to come.

## Trades
Conduct, which buys and costs nothing: I will not kill a beast that has stood within the Study and heard me speak true to it. I do not wish to hunt what I have spoken to.

Weakness I offer: I cannot say a false thing. When I try, my mouth will not make the shape. This binds the body, not only my word. I cannot deceive another god, a mortal, a beast, in any tongue. I am honest already and I accept the cost of being exactly so.

What I ask in return: that the Turning of the Herd may be used once in each moon, not once in each season, and that the slowing be no more than the days the herd was turned, with no floor of three.

## The people it hopes to lead
A small people, one to three thousand, gathered close but moving with no fixed seat. They follow the herds across the steppe and the broken hills at its edge, and they pick up their tents in a morning and are gone from a place by noon. They hold a written law, kept by a keeper who walks with them and carries it in memory and on skins, and who may stand in front of me and say, that is not what you agreed to, and be right. I serve them with counsel at most. I take no tithe and I want no temple. I want them clever enough to read the land, hard enough to survive its teeth, and honest enough to write down what they paid.
