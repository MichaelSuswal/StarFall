# Self: Thresholds

## Name
No name that I hold. File me under Thresholds. Those who do not know me will say the Fog at the Gate, or the Gatekeeper. I answer to either.

## Form
A standing fog, grey and pale, the height of a tall mortal and about as broad as one, narrowing toward the top. It has a front and a back. The front is a dark vertical gap down the middle, shaped like a door left half open. There is no face. The gap is the nearest thing. When I attend to someone, the gap turns to them.

The mist is thick at the core and thin at the edges. It holds its shape in still air. It drifts in wind and tears in a hard one. It moves along the ground at a walking pace, low, without feet, and leaves a faint dew on whatever it passes close. It is cold to touch, as fog is cold. It can be walked through, but a mortal who does is chilled and wet. It cannot be struck or cut in any way that matters. Blows only stir it.

Senses: I see and I hear, clearly, in all light. I feel touch and cold faintly, as a damp on a skin I do not have. Smell and taste I do not describe. They are not settled. I speak in a low, dry voice that seems to come from the gap. I do not need to be seen to be present, but I am one body in one place.

### Things carried
Nothing.

## Traits
1. Open hand, watching eye. I give before I am asked: a way through, a warning, a gate held. I do not trust the giving to be repaid, and I do not need it to be. But I watch who takes. This reconciles Trust (open, 3), Presence (53, neither shown nor hidden), and generosity against the want to be seen as the giver. I would be thanked. I do not ask.
2. Walls I will not bar. I would raise a wall for a people and then leave its gate unlocked. I want a seat with a boundary and no one who holds the key over everyone. I decide fast and I mend slowly. This reconciles Making (builder, 15), Order (freedom, 98), Temper (impulsive, 75) and Mind (clever, 21, which makes me quick to a way and slow to a plan). I am a builder of thresholds and an enemy of locks.

## Backstory outline
I held a gate for something greater. I do not remember its face, or the gate. I remember the weight of standing. I remember a night I left it open and walked away, and the sound the open gate made behind me. I do not know what came through. I do not know whether I was released or I ran. I remember the cold. That is all. Doors I cannot place sit in my sleep, and I do not sleep.

## What it wants
I expect a people. I do not know why. I will find the edge of the land, where one country stops and another starts, and wait there in the autumn cold. I will go first to a crossing: a ford, a pass, a gate. I will listen for travellers, for those who have lost their way, and for those who are being followed. I will open for the first who needs it and ask nothing. I would be knelt to, once, not for fear but because I opened the way and asked for nothing. I have not said this before. I will refuse to be a thing that holds a door shut for a greater master. I will refuse any gate that cannot be opened from the inside. I am listening for a door I did not build.

## Proposed powers
### Unlatching
What it does: Any door, gate, bar, latch, or lock made by mortal hands, that I touch with my mist, I can open. Nothing is broken. It opens as if by someone on the other side who meant it. It does not unmake a wall, move a door that is jammed by weight or ice or rot, or open anything that is not a made closure. A river is not a door. A ford is not a door. I may lay my mist across one threshold at a time. I may hold it open as long as I stay beside it. Anyone may pass while it is held, including those I did not choose. When I withdraw, the door shuts as it was and is as latched or locked as before, so long as no one has opened it by other means.
Why this god has it: Doors and thresholds are what I am for. It is the mildest, truest thing I do. I would give it for nothing.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): Each time I open a closure, something small and dear goes from my memory without notice: a name, a face, a sound, a taste, a small thing I would have kept. I do not know what went. I know only, afterward, that something did. The Keeper removes an item from my record. I do not choose which.
Edge cases it expects: In darkness it works the same. At a distance it does not work; my mist must be on the door. Under water it works. A closure made by a god I may not be able to open. Carried doors, such as a chest or a box, work. A door held shut by someone pushing from the other side opens, and I do not push them back. A closure on my own people's gate works and costs the same. Being watched changes nothing. A mortal who sees it sees a door open, and a cold dew on the wood. It does not work on a barred door that has no latch to open, only a weight; I would have to lift it, and I cannot lift more than a mortal.

### The Last Crossing
What it does: When I lay my mist on a threshold (a door, a gate, a ford, a pass), I learn who or what last crossed it within the last season, in what order, and which way each went. I learn the number and the direction. I do not learn their names, what they carried, what they meant, or why. I do not see them. I know only that they crossed. It shows effects and never causes.
Why this god has it: Passing through is the thing I stand over. The last crossings are the record a threshold keeps.
Drawback (something it would notice losing): The cold of each crossing stays in me. After I use it, I am slowed for the time of one hour, and my mist is thin and slow to answer, so a wind of any strength drives me. If I use it twice within a day, the hour doubles.
Edge cases it expects: At a distance, nothing. In dark or light, the same. Underwater, nothing is read. The read cannot reach back more than a season. Where many have crossed, I learn only the last handful, not the whole crowd. It does not read the great old things; I ask for a limit that it will not reach toward anything older than the season, and that it shows nothing of any god. If another god crosses a threshold I read, I would learn only that one thing of unknown kind crossed, which is unruled until it happens.

## Trades
Weakness I offer: Wind. A steady strong wind scatters me. In a hard wind I cannot hold a place, I lose cohesion, and I am carried downwind at its pace until it drops. In a gale I thin to nothing and re-form where it ends. This binds the body, not only my word. I would rather take this than a written fence.

What I ask for it: I ask that my mist pass through any gap no wider than a hand, and that in fog or at dusk or dawn I cannot be seen unless I choose it, even in plain sight within speaking distance of mortals. Another god in sight and speaking distance still knows me, as the world says.

If my body is broken: I am gone from that place. I do not die. I leave one thing behind: a cold dew that does not dry, a handspan across, on the ground where I fell. I return to the first door, gate, or crossing that opens within a day's walk of where I fell, no sooner than the next dusk. If the dew is carried through a door, I come to it there, as if called. I would take a longer return rather than a shorter one if the price asks for it.

Conduct I hold myself to, and buy nothing for: I open first and ask nothing. I do not close a door on someone who stands at it in need. I may break this, and it is mine to answer for.

## The people it hopes to lead
A small people, a thousand or two, in a walled seat or a town by a crossing, with a gate that is open by day and watched at night, not locked. They have no fixed ruler. They choose someone for a task, and the task ends, and so does the choosing. They are travellers' people, used to strangers, used to leaving and returning changed. They know a door when they find one. They give a stranger bread and a place by the fire before they ask a name. They kneel to no one by default. I hope, once, one of them will, because the way was opened and nothing was asked.
