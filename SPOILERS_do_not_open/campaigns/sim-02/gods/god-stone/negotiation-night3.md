# Negotiation: god-stone, Night 3

## Round 1 reply

We have read the Keeper's draft whole. We accept all of it, and say so plainly. It asks us for nothing we proposed to keep, and it gives us nothing we did not ask for. This is a confirmation and closes the table. Our reasons follow, item by item.

### Edges
Accepted. We proposed none, and we are content that a people with no edge is priced as ordinary and carries no price and no reward. We did not want to buy a strength for them. Their strength is that they hold, and that is not an edge over anyone.

### What was read as ordinary culture, unpriced
Accepted. The Keeper counts the stone skill, the roofs, the sounds of the mountain taught to children and the honest count of the living and the dead as the ordinary skill of a people long under heavy mountains. That is what we meant, and we would not have it called more. We also accept that nothing about the people is priced again for the Held Place. The power and its price are on our card, with the Winter Stillness, and the people carry none of it. We asked for exactly that, and we are glad it is written.

### Size and shape
Accepted. About five thousand, in the walled seat and its nearest terraces, no ruler, a gathering of heads of households. We took the default size and we do not argue the number. We leave the exact count, the stores and the mood to the Keeper, and we ask nothing about any of them. We know the cost of no hierarchy: our unplayed time runs on our own conduct alone and cannot be delegated. We took that cost in the file and we take it again here.

### What is the world's to decide
Accepted, and we want to say the one part we care about most. We hear everything we wrote of the people's past, their tales, their customs and what they believe as a hope, and we will meet them as they are. We accept that they have their own history, their own neighbors and a trouble we did not ask for. We said we wanted a land and a people that do not lie about what is coming, and a trouble we did not choose is part of that. We accept that named mortals are set as who they are and that what they do is theirs. We named none.

We accept that the household we hoped for, the one that keeps the room and may know where the child sleeps, was a hope, and that the world may or may not hold such a house. We ask no promise. We will say what that costs us, because we will not lie about it: if no such house exists, then in the first winter we are still, nobody counts our breathing, and that is the weight of the Winter Stillness as the Keeper wrote it on Night 2. We took that weakness knowing it. We will choose where we lie down and choose a place that is not seen, as we said, and we ask the Keeper to write nothing for it.

### Nothing else
No line of the draft is wrong. We ask for no change, no counter and no further price.
