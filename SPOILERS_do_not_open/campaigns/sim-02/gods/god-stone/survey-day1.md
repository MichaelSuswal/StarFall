## Answer 1

A hollow under a hill, dry and cool, with a long slow sound far above like weather that does not concern the sleeper. We would have the world feel kept. Heavy, patient, and quiet enough that a small voice carries a long way. We say plainly that we also want it to feel safe, and we are not sure the world can be both kept and safe.

## Answer 2

Mountains. Stone that was here before anything walked on it and will be here after. High valleys with one way in. We want a land that has a spine, with places to be under, and places that do not move when something frightening happens.

## Answer 3

Long seasons, and honest ones. A hard winter that is plainly a winter, so that people know what the stone is for. A slow, late spring with real melt, a short generous summer, and an autumn with enough warning in it that no one is surprised. We do not want seasons that lie about what is coming.

We want to wake into the deep of winter, when the ground is shut and the world is still, when the mountain is most itself and nothing is asked of us yet.

We wake into winter.

## Answer 4

Some of each, and we will be honest that the proportion is a worry to us. We would like wild country no one has walked near our own borders, so that nothing arrives quickly. Farther out, old kingdoms already rooted, so that the world has weight and we are not the only heavy thing in it. We do not want to be the oldest.

## Answer 5

Thousands of years, with ruins under ruins. A long story means people have survived many things, and that is a comfort to us. People who arrived within living memory would have no one to teach them what to do when the ground shakes.

## Answer 6

Closer to the rumor than to the wise-woman in every valley. Mostly doubted, sometimes proved. We want it rare enough that people who have it are not simply obeyed, and present enough that the doubt is never total. A world with too much of it is a world with too many things we cannot see coming.

## Answer 7

It should feel like pressure, like holding a breath beneath weight. It should cost the body first, slowly, so that nobody can take much of it quickly. What a mortal draws should be taken from something they would notice missing: warmth, sleep, a year at the far end of their life. No one should be able to buy it without paying where they live.

## Answer 8

Closer to the one-eyed man, but only a little. A god should be plainly more than a person, in what it endures and what it outlasts. But it should not be so far above that mortals have nothing to say to it. We would rather be underestimated than looked at. We say this because it is true, not because it is clever.

## Answer 9

Familiar animals, mostly, and a few things in the high places that have no name. Old things in caves, in the cold, in the dark between rocks. The strangeness should live where people rarely go, and should be slow and large rather than quick. We are not afraid of slow things.

## Answer 10

Sudden things. The ground opening, the slope letting go, the cold that arrives overnight. Danger that has no face and cannot be argued with or bargained down. We think the best stories come from people who knew the mountain was dangerous and went up anyway. We do not mean to say we like these dangers. We mean that we know them.

## Answer 11

Safe ground. A place to sleep without listening. Mortals will say salt, or iron, or good seed, but under those we think it is the feeling of a roof that will not fall, and we would have it be scarce so that people learn to build, and to value those who hold the roofs up.

## Answer 12

Sealed doors. Old roads that end at rock. A deep place with something in it that nobody wants to find, which stays found only when someone is ready. We would also like there to be a place we are not sure of ourselves, so that we do not have to pretend to know every room of the land we stand on.

## Answer 13

Yes, a few, and they should not be like us or like humans. Quiet peoples who live in the stone or the snow, wary of strangers and unhurried. They should not be enemies by default and not friends by default. They should be people with their own long account of the mountains, who might tell us we are wrong about things, and we would want to hear it.

## Answer 14

Few. One or two who arrive hurt or lost, carrying something we cannot name, and who must be looked after until they say what it is. Many travelers would mean many strange tools, and we do not like to be surprised by many things at once. We would like the first one to be someone we can be truthful with.

## Answer 15

Face to face only. We do not want to be felt at a distance by anyone, and we do not want to feel them. A knowing in person, close enough to see, so that a god who wishes to stay unseen can. We would rather be a rumor than a pull.

## Answer 16

One body. Premise partly bent: if there must be more than one, it should cost more than we think anyone would pay, and it should hurt in a way that is not repaired. Being in two places is a way of being split, and we do not think a god should want it. A god should have to be somewhere, and mean it.

## Answer 17

A mortal who stays. Who does not leave the valley when it is hard, who holds a door, who keeps count of the living and the dead honestly. Not a conqueror, and not a seeker. Someone stubborn and clear, who will say what is true even when no one wants to hear it, and will not be made to say otherwise.

## Answer 18

A companion. Premise bent: we would not say a place or a power first, though we would take them. We would like to find that there is someone who can be near us and not be afraid, and whom we need not be afraid of. We did not know we wanted to say that until the question came.

## Answer 19

We wish that nothing in this world has to be put right by us alone. We wish for a mountain that holds, and for one chance, after something has been done that cannot be undone, to be allowed to stay and carry it rather than to be sent away. We have not said this before.
