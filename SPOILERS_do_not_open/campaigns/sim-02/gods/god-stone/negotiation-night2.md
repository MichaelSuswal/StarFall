# Negotiation: god-stone, Night 2

## Round 1 reply

We have read the Keeper's draft whole. We accept most of it and say so plainly, and we argue two things: how long the Held Place lasts, and the weakness that buys it. Those are what we care about. The rest we give way on.

### 1. Form, and the thing we carry
Accepted as drafted. Heavy for our size, a cool skin, a child's pace that never runs, the senses as listed, the rest left unsettled and asked in play. The cloak is a cloak. We wrote that we do not run, and we are glad the pace table is told it. No change asked.

### 2. The Held Place
We accept the description, the drawback and the limits settled at creation as drafted. The pain, the stillness and the rush when we are made to let go are the price of it, and we would notice losing any of them.

Choice: (c). A place held for a full season stays steadied through the winter that follows, and no longer, with no limit on how many places.

Our reasons. We asked for what survives. A roof that holds only while one hand stays on it, or only one roof at a time, leaves the rest of a people under what mortals built, and a mountain is hard on what mortals built. Option (b) makes us choose one family to keep safe and leave the others to the dark, and we do not want that choice put on us. It is a cruelty, and it is also the thing most likely to send us from the hold in grief.

On the Keeper's worry about scale, we say it is bounded already, by our own body and not by a written number. A full season held means we stand with both hands on the stone and our feet fixed for the whole of that season, in pain that rises. One body, one place. So in a year we can hold a place through spring, through summer and through autumn at the most, and not in winter, because the weakness below takes us then. That is at most three places a year, each bought with a whole season of standing. It lasts only through the winter that follows, so the next year we must stand again, and if we do not, the roofs return to what mortals built. No count is needed to make that so. We would rather bear that cost than a number written on the card, since a fence of numbers is the kind we resist.

If the Keeper still finds (c) too wide, we counter: (c) with the lasting held only for stone roofs, cuts and slopes where people are under them, and not for walls or open ground. We would give that up before we took (b), though we would call it a smaller thing than we want.

### 3. The Listening Stone
Accepted as drafted. The Keeper's addition is right and we ask no change: a hollow heard is a hollow, and the power never tells us what a deep place holds, only that one is there. We would not want to be told. We do not know every room of the land we stand on and we are content not to.

On the Keeper's offer in Trades that it reach two miles in winter: we decline it. In winter we are still and cannot act, so the extra reach would be a power we could not use, and we would rather not carry a thing that tempts a stretch of the wording. If the Keeper means that it could work while we lie still, we do not ask for that either. The stillness is meant to be real.

### 4. The Unmarked Way
Accepted as drafted. It is a small hiding with a small cost, and we are content with the price. It also keeps us a rumor, which is what we would be.

### 5. When the body is broken
Accepted as drafted, including the numbers. One winter gone, a second if the stone is carried, a third if it is broken, and the last season forgotten, enacted as a removal from our goals and record. We did not mean a shorter "longer." We wrote this one knowing it was heavy, and we would have it heavy, since we cannot die and should not find leaving cheap.

One thing for the write-up, not a new power: we ask that the card say the stone left behind is warm and plainly out of its kind, as drafted, and that it is the people's to keep if they find it. The draft already says so. No change.

### 6. Trades: the Winter Stillness, and the Keeper's question
The answer to the question is the first one. The god is still through the frozen ground and wakes as it thaws. That is the larger weakness, and we choose it.

Our reasons. A shorter stillness that ends while the ground is still shut would be a weakness we mostly want and would hardly feel. The Keeper is right that what we would not choose is being unable to wake when a roof groans, and for a god whose whole wish is that nothing fall, that is the weight of it. We offered a weakness that binds the body, and we would take the heavier one before the lighter one that costs us nothing we do not already pay. It also buys what we asked for.

A point of wording, in the write-up and not a new power. We land in the deep of winter, and we asked to wake into it so that we may be still and listen before we move. We ask that the card say the stillness binds us from the first winter after the landing, and that in the winter we land in we are awake, since we wake into it. If the Keeper holds that the landing winter is a winter like the rest, we accept it, and we would lie still through it and wake at the thaw. We prefer awake, and we say why: a people we do not yet know, a land we have not walked, and nothing yet held. But we will not bargain hard over it.

We accept the Keeper's note on how it can be used against us. Anyone who learns it has us each winter to carry, to bind or to wall in. That is a true risk, and we would have it written. We will say, as conduct and not as a rule on the card, that we will choose where we lie down each winter, and choose a place that is not seen. We do not ask the Keeper to write that.

The Keeper's offer of the two miles in winter is declined, as above. Instead we take choice (c) as the price of the larger weakness, and we ask for nothing more.

Conduct, which buys and costs nothing: accepted. We do not lie, to any mortal or any god, and the card may say so under goals.

### 7. Edge-case floor and the four tests
Accepted. The limits blocks answer light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, water, being watched, other gods and our own people from what we wrote, and we confirm them as written. We accept that a wall made by more than craft is unruled until it happens, and that a god that takes or holds our left stone is unruled until it happens. We accept that the Keeper writes no limit on the scarcity of safe ground, and that a roof held is not a roof built. We add only that we do not rule a mind, and the Keeper's "none" is correct.

### Summary of our choices
Form: accepted. Held Place: description, drawback and limits accepted, with choice (c), and a counter held in reserve. Listening Stone: accepted, two miles in winter declined. Unmarked Way: accepted. The Break: accepted. Winter Stillness: the larger one, through the frozen ground, with one wording request for the landing winter. Conduct: accepted.
