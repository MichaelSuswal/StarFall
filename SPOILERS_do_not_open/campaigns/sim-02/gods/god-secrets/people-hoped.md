# People: unnamed

They name themselves. Their home is the town in the bend, unnamed by me.

## Who they are
Human. A people already living here, with their own history and their own account of what came before them. Not mine. They did not wait for me. They are neighbors to me, and over years a people I keep. I hope to lead them, but never in sight. If they never learn I am here, that is allowed. If they half learn it, that is better.

## How many
Seven to fifteen thousand. Gathered close, the most in the walled town, the rest on the farms and orchards near it. I would take fewer before I took a king.

## How they live
Behind walls in the town, close, through the winters that keep people indoors. Fields and orchards on the better ground, the harvest in. Lamps early. Shutters closed. A household in the town keeps a room warm and a door unlatched, and leaves a share of the harvest set out, for whoever comes. They do not say for whom. They are not told.

## Their code
What is told in confidence is kept among them as a thing owed. A person who carries another's secret into the street is not trusted again. A secret may be set down in one place, with the one who keeps, and not elsewhere. Silence is not an insult and not a lie. A question asked with others standing by is asked with care. These are theirs to make and bend. I set no law.

## Skills and culture
Ordinary. Farming, orchards, river trade, building in wood and stone, the crafts of a people before steel. A fondness for closed doors, small rooms and low voices. Lamps at dusk are a custom. Telling is done at the hearth, late, one to one. They are good at keeping their own counsel and at reading what is not said.

## Edges and their weaknesses
I propose none. They have no edge over other peoples. The only strangeness is the one I carry on my own card: they cannot hold my face, and cannot thank me.

## Their magic
Rumor only. A few in every town know a little, and it is not spoken of. Never a wise-woman in every valley. I describe no mortal with a knack.

## What they believe about gods
Little, and not of me. They have old stories, most of them lost, and whoever forgot them had reasons. They do not know I am a god. They do not know what my powers are. At most they know there is a quiet one who keeps, and that telling it helps.

## Hierarchy
I reject one. No king, no priests, no advisors who carry out my wishes, no chain of orders. I give no orders. Because of this my unplayed time runs on my own conduct alone, and I accept that.

They settle things among themselves, by household and by lane. The old of a lane sit with the ones in the quarrel, and what is agreed there stands. No office. Who sits is whoever both sides will have. A quarrel that a lane cannot settle goes to the lanes beside it, and that is as far as it goes.

## How the god meets them
Already there, with their own history. I come to the town as a quiet person, to the walls and the edges, and I listen before I am heard. I will not be named ruler, priest or god in the open. I will not be found by force. I would be found, if at all, by someone who needs to tell a thing and has nowhere to put it.

I will not lie. I will not tell what I am told. In time I do not play, I do not act on any secret against the one who told it, and I do not speak one to anyone. I do not rule the household's room or the share left out. I take what is left.

## Questions for you

1. How does the household begin?
One person tells a thing in the dark, to someone they cannot name, and feels lighter. They tell one other. A household learns that something in the town takes what cannot be said and gives nothing back. Someone leaves a share out by the wall. It is taken, or it is not. They keep a room warm. They keep the door unlatched. No one tells them to. It begins with a person who had nowhere to put a thing, and it goes on because it worked.

2. Who settles things among them, and do I mean to be heard?
The lanes settle things, as above. I do not mean to be heard in it. I mean to listen. If I am asked a direct thing I answer true or say nothing. I do not guide the quarrels, and I will not be a voice in them. If a secret told to me bears on a quarrel, it stays in me, and I carry it.

3. Do I hope they know what the quiet one is, or only that telling it helps?
Only that telling it helps. Some may half know there is a keeper, and that it forgets nothing and says nothing. I hope they do not know it is a god. I hope no one can describe it. What they know is the lightness.
