# Negotiation: god-secrets, Night 3

## Round 1 reply

I read each item for what it takes from my people and what it gives them.

### Edges
Accept. I proposed none and I ask for none. I note that the Keeper prices the people as ordinary and gives no reward for it. I do not argue that.

### Ordinary culture
Accept. All of it is theirs to make and bend. I set no law.

### Size and shape
Choose B, about nine thousand, most in the walled town, paid for by a weakness.

Reasons. I hoped for seven to fifteen thousand, gathered close. Five thousand is under my band, and a people that small has fewer who need somewhere to put a thing. Nine thousand is inside my band and enough that every lane has someone who knows something and will not say it. I take the lower half of my band and do not push toward fifteen. I would not reopen the ceiling the Keeper names: above nine, one weakness is not enough, and I would not pile on a second.

On the weakness, I accept the Keeper's draft: the town feeds about two in three of its mouths in a fair year, the rest is bought from farms upriver and down, and stores at the end of an ordinary harvest carry the town eight months, not twelve. I do not offer my own in its place. Reasons: it does not restate what they are, they would notice losing it, and it suits what I want. A town that must buy its bread has constant traffic, quarrels it cannot settle by lane alone, and many who carry something they cannot say. That is where secrets go. I would take this weakness over a heavier one with a written fence.

Two wordings I ask in the write-up, not new powers:
1. That the weakness is the town's and the sellers' hold is theirs. It says nothing of me and gives me no way to feed or relieve them. I have no hand in their grain, and I will not be priced as if I had.
2. That "hunger within one winter" is a risk the world may or may not bring in a given year, and not a promise of famine. I accept the risk. I do not ask the Keeper to soften the eight months.

If the Keeper holds either wording out, I accept the draft as written and do not reopen it.

### What is the world's to decide
Accept. What I wrote of their past, tales, customs and beliefs is a hope, and I will meet them as they are. If they have a trouble I did not ask for, I will hear it before I am heard. If the household that keeps a room, the share left out, or the lanes that settle things do not exist when I arrive, then I find the way they do settle things, and I listen. I do not set a tithe on a people who do not know me, and I would not wish to.

I name no mortal and ask for none.

### Summary of choices
Choice B at about nine thousand with the Keeper's draft weakness as written. Everything else accepted. Two write-up wordings asked: the weakness is the people's and not mine, and the hunger is a risk and not a promise.
