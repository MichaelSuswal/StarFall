# Negotiation: god-storms, Night 3

## Round 1 reply

I take a fair price as it is offered. Here is each item, with my reasons.

### Edges
Accepted. I asked for none and I am glad the table prices a people with none as ordinary. A people with an edge gets watched, and a people watched gets bargained with. I want them hard to find and easy to overlook, and an ordinary people is that.

### What was read as ordinary culture, unpriced
Accepted. Herding, tracking, horse-handling, felt and hide, cord and notch counting, weather sayings and fire songs are what a herd people has. I agree the nightly count is a custom and not an edge. It gives them no advantage over another people. It only means a short count is noticed early, and a neighbor with the same custom would do as well.

### Size and shape
Accepted, with one reading stated so both sides hold the same one, and not a request to change a word. The costs of the smaller people and the missing hierarchy are real, and they buy me nothing because I asked for nothing. I take that. It is the choice I made. The council is theirs and not mine, I give it no orders, and my unplayed time runs on my own conduct alone, as I wrote. I accept that this cannot be delegated and I will not look for a way around it. The Keeper sets the exact number, the herds, the stores and the mood. I ask no say in them. Whether they build fixed roofs for winter is theirs, and I will not ask them to build or refuse to build.

### What is the world's to decide
Accepted, all of it. I heard my words about their past, their tales and their beliefs as a hope, and I hold them as one. The people have their own history, their own neighbors and a trouble I did not ask for. I expected as much. A land with banks and ditches enclosing nothing, and lines in the grass that cross ridges without regard for them, was never going to be empty of trouble. I will meet them as they are, and I will count them as they are, and I will not ask the world to soften the trouble for the herd I have not yet counted.

On the named mortals, I accept that they are set as who they are and that what they do is theirs. That is the line I drew. I set a headman who counts and has a daughter or a brother better at what he is worst at, and I did not set what either of them does. I ask for nothing about their loyalty to me, since there is none to ask for, and nothing about whether they ever come to know what the old herder is. That is theirs.

On a custom, an office or a household I hoped for: I accept that nothing promises it. If the council is not as I described, or the counter is not who I pictured, or the winter quarters are fixed roofs, I find that out at the edge of their fire, and I deal with it from there.

### Summary
All accepted as drafted. No counter, no refusal. This is a confirmation.
