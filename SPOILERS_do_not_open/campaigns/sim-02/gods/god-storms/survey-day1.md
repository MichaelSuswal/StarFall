## Answer 1

Wind over grass before the rain, and every head in the herd turned the same way. A wide sky with weather moving across it, and under it small fires that do not go out. They say a storm is only the sky counting its own. I want a world where the counting is kind, and where whoever is counted never sees the hand.

## Answer 2

Steppe, and the hill country where the steppe breaks into ridges. Open ground that gives a storm room to be seen coming, and gives a people room to move. Grass that moves is a river that will not drown you. I would take no fixed seat for anyone I love.

## Answer 3

Four seasons, and each of them honest. A hard winter that thins the weak plans and keeps the strong ones, a wet spring with real thunder, a long dry summer of heat lightning, an autumn of herds coming down. The old saying is that the year is a herd, and you count it at the gate. Let the weather be big and the change between seasons be sudden enough to be felt.

When each new year finds me I want to wake into the turn of late spring, when the first great storms break and the herds are on the move.

I wake into spring.

## Answer 4

Some of each. Wild country no one has walked, because a people that moves needs ground that is not yet owned, and old kingdoms rooted somewhere far enough off to be a rumor and a market and a danger. A closed gate and an open road make a good pair. Let the kingdoms be far and the grass be near.

## Answer 5

Thousands of years, with ruins under ruins. A world that has forgotten more than it knows is a world that stays humble. Dig anywhere and find someone who thought they would last. The old saying is that every road was first a rumor of a road, and I like a land that remembers none of its builders.

## Answer 6

Nearer the wise-woman end than the rumor end, but never in every valley. Perhaps one in a hundred hands has it, and most of those do not know. A little magic that people half believe is worth more than a great magic that people plan around. Let it be common enough to be a tale and rare enough to be a gift.

## Answer 7

It should feel like weather in the body: a pressure behind the eyes before it comes, a prickle in the hands, a taste of metal. It should cost them something they would feel losing. Warmth, or sleep, or a season of memory, or the steadiness of the hands that did the work. No magic for free. What is borrowed from the sky is paid back in the blood, and the sky is a patient creditor but not a forgiving one.

## Answer 8

Barely more than a great mortal sorcerer. Premise bent: I would go further, and say a god should be easy to mistake for weather, for luck, for an old shepherd with a strange gift. A god that stands out is a god that gets watched, and a god that gets watched gets bargained with, and a god that is bargained with is soon owned. The quiet one outlives the loud one. Let us be one tall tree among many, and let most travelers pass without looking up.

## Answer 9

Mostly familiar animals, with a few legends, and a rare thing at the edge that has no name. Herds, wolves, hawks, horses, and then once in a lifetime something that stops a man's breath. A world with too many strange beasts has no room left for the ordinary ones, and I am fond of the ordinary ones. Count the herd and notice the one that is not in the count.

## Answer 10

Weather that does not care, and winters that outlast the grain. Hunger that comes slowly. Sickness among the herds. The stranger who arrives with a smile and a long memory. Let the danger be the kind that a council of old heads can argue about by the fire all night and still get wrong. The best fear is one with a reasonable explanation.

## Answer 11

Salt and good iron. Mortals can graze without them for a while, but a people that moves must have something to trade and something to cut with, and salt keeps the meat through the winter. Let those who have it be far and those who need it be many. Trade breeds more quarrels than war does, and quarrels are how a people learns its own shape.

## Answer 12

Old roads, half swallowed by grass, that no one built on purpose for the people now walking them. Maybe a sealed door in a hillside. Bones of something enormous that the herds avoid without knowing why. Anything that makes a child ask who left it there. Let it not be a treasure. Let it be a question.

## Answer 13

Yes, a few, and not many. Peoples who are not hostile and not kind, only older, and keeping their own counsel. Let them live in the high places and the deep woods, and let them trade only when they choose. A neighbor you cannot read is a good neighbor to have. Let them have long memories and short patience with fools, and let each of them know something my people do not.

## Answer 14

A few, yes, and rarely. One traveler in a generation, carrying a tool or a tale that does not fit the land. Too many would turn the world into a market for novelties. One stranger by the fire does more good than a caravan. Let them come from far enough that no one can check their stories, and let them be a little afraid of what they have lost by leaving.

## Answer 15

Something I can ignore. A knowing only face to face, and even then only when I choose to be seen. A pull felt at any distance would make me easy to find, and I would rather be a rumor than a landmark. Let the knowing be like seeing a familiar horse in a strange herd: a flicker, then certainty. Let it be possible to be wrong. Let it be possible to hide.

## Answer 16

One body at a time, and shifting between two forms is price enough. The old saying is that a man who is in two places is nowhere. If a god must be in more than one place, let it cost them the thread of their own thinking, so that each body knows less than the one that stayed, and let the farther body be slower and clumsier and more easily broken. I do not ask for it. Premise bent: I would take the limit gladly, and keep the second form for the one that moves with the herd.

## Answer 17

One who counts. Someone who can hold a herd or a household or a quarrel in their head and say what it is worth, and who is too stubborn to stop when it hurts. Clever, loyal, not noble, and a little drunk on a good night. Not a king. A headman who is right too often to be loved. Let them have a daughter or a brother who is better at the thing they are worst at.

## Answer 18

A companion that is not a god and not a mortal, one that has been here longer than I have and does not mind being counted among my herd. Or a place where the sky is lower. Or a truth about the thing I failed before. I cannot imagine it yet, which is the point. Let it be a thing I stumble on while looking for something else.

## Answer 19

Premise rejected: I have said what I wish aloud. What I have not dared say is simple. I wish that this time the count comes out even, and that no one of mine is missed at the gate. The old saying is that the herd you save is the herd you remember, and the one you lose is the one you count forever. Let me be allowed to save this one.
