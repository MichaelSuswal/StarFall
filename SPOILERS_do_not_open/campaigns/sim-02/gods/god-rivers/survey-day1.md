## Answer 1
Water finding its way around something that thought it was a wall. A world that moves. Wet stone at dawn, a ford with a few people crossing it who have no idea what is under their feet, and somewhere upstream a thing laughing that nobody hears.

## Answer 2
River country. Springs high in the hills, small and cold and honest, and then the long slow widening to the mouth. I want a land that is mostly edges and crossings. Marsh at the bottom of it is fine. Anywhere a thing must be crossed, I am interested.

## Answer 3
Four seasons, and each one should mean something. A hard winter that freezes the shallows and shuts the fords, so that people remember water can refuse them. A loud spring thaw with flood in it, because a river that never overruns its banks is only a ditch. A long warm summer with low clear water, for the mercy of it. A grey autumn of rain and rising brown water, for the memory of it.
I want the world to be able to take things back. Let the floods wander a little from year to year, so no one's map stays true for long.
I would wake into the spring thaw. I wake into spring.

## Answer 4
Some of each, but more old than wild. I want to find kingdoms already rooted, because a rooted kingdom has fords, tolls, ferries and bridges, and every one of them is a gate. I also want a stretch of wild country at the sources, where no one has walked, because that is where I would go to be left alone and to be missed.

## Answer 5
Thousands of years, and ruins under ruins. Long enough that there were once people who knew who I was, and long enough that they stopped. I find that very funny, and I would like the joke to have plenty of room.

## Answer 6
Closer to the rumor than the wise-woman, but never gone. A few people in a few valleys who can do a small strange thing and cannot explain it. Enough that a mortal might stand at a spring and feel watched and be right. Not so much that anyone can prove it.

## Answer 7
It should feel like cold water on the wrist, a tug from below, a sense of being carried and not quite steering. The cost should be loss by erosion: each working wears something away, a memory, a name, a small ease, slowly, so no one notices the day it crosses over. Mortals will pay it gladly, which is the part I would watch.

## Answer 8
Closer to the one-eyed man, but only just, and only if the blind do not know it. I want to stand out less than I could. A god who is obviously a god gets worshipped, and a god who gets worshipped gets forgotten. Let me be a great and strange presence that most people mistake for weather.

## Answer 9
Familiar animals mostly, with legends at the water. Big old fish no one has ever caught. Things in the deep pools with no name that are probably only fish. A few creatures that are not fish. The strangeness should live near water and be allowed to stay vague.

## Answer 10
Drought, first. Then a thing that comes up out of the river at night. Then the quiet kind: a neighbor's ford closed, a spring that goes bad, a debt that follows you downstream. I like a danger that arrives as an ordinary inconvenience and is understood too late.

## Answer 11
Clean water, in the places that matter. Salt. Perhaps both. Good water should be scarce enough to be bargained over and not so scarce that it is only cruelty. Mortals will make a great deal of themselves around a thing they cannot do without.

## Answer 12
Old roads that end at rivers, with the far half missing. A sealed door beside a spring. A drowned town under a lake that someone once made on purpose. Bones, if they are bones of something that drank here. I would like to be able to wade over something that was once an altar and not be told.

## Answer 13
Yes, a few, and mostly near water. Quiet, wary peoples who keep to the reedbeds and the cold headwaters, who are not much interested in us or in humans. I would like to be unremarkable to them, and I suspect I would be.

## Answer 14
A few. One or two, not many, who came a very long way and are tired and strange. Let them carry knowledge that does not belong here and tools that no longer work properly. Travelers are another sort of crossing, and I like the ones who do not know where they are.

## Answer 15
Face to face only. A pull at any distance would let others find me, and I would rather not be findable. If we must recognize each other, let it be a knowing at arm's length, as one knows a stranger is also wet from the same rain. After that, whatever we make of it.

## Answer 16
Premise bent: one body, but with a river's habit. I am in one place and I flow through many, so let me feel the length of my own water without being standing at each bend. A true second body should cost something I would notice losing. I would pay with a stretch of my own memory, and be sorry only if I remembered it.

## Answer 17
One who stands at a crossing and decides who passes. A ferryman, a toll-keeper, a gatekeeper of some small stone gate, who is neither kind nor cruel and whose judgment turns out, over a lifetime, to matter. I would like to watch someone be fair by accident, or unfair on purpose, and see which of those the world remembers.

## Answer 18
A source. The very first spring, the one every river here begins from, and whatever is down there. I would also like to find that some old thing here still has my name in its mouth, and does not know it.

## Answer 19
That someone remembers me, correctly, once. Not worship. I do not want worship again, I have had it and I know what follows. Only to be recalled by a person who has no reason to flatter me, and who laughs.
