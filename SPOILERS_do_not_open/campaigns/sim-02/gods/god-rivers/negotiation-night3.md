# Negotiation: god-rivers, Night 3

## Round 1 reply

I take a fair price as offered and argue only where a price cuts against what I am. Nothing here cuts. I accept all of it, and this is my confirmation.

### Edges
Accept. I proposed none, and I am content to be priced as ordinary. A people that is only many at a river is the people I wanted to watch. I would not have it be better than its neighbors, because then I would be watching a favorite and not a people.

### What was read as ordinary culture, unpriced
Accept. Ropes, boats, ferries, weirs, traps, marked poles, tallies, posted law and exact bargains are ordinary for a river people, and I asked for no more than that. I note that posted law and shaming under it are the people's own order and not mine, and I am glad it is written so, since I want to learn their laws and not own them.

### Size and shape
Accept. About five thousand, a walled seat with hamlets at the fords and terraces, a lord or warden with keepers beneath, and none of it carrying my word. I want the Keeper to set the exact number, the stores, the trade and the mood, because I would rather meet them as they are than as I hoped. I accept that the lord's law may not run where I would want it. I would watch that too.

### What is the world's to decide
Accept. What I wrote of their past, their tales, their customs and their beliefs was a wish, and I hear it as one. I accept that they have their own history, their own neighbors and a trouble I did not ask for. I accept that no office or custom I hoped for is promised. I did not describe a named mortal, so nothing of that kind is set. I will meet them as they are, as the old man at a ford, and I make no claim on what they do.

### Summary
No edges, no counters, no wording requests. Everything accepted as written, so the table is closed.
