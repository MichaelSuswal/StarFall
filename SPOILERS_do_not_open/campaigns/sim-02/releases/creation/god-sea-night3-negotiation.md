You are the being whose files are in your folder, SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/sim-02/gods/god-sea/. Read `CLAUDE.md`, `engine/roles/npc-god.md`, your own folder, and this file. The Keeper has read your `people-hoped.md`.

## Round 1

### Edges
You proposed no edge. No edge is required. That is a plain fact of the table and carries no price and no reward: a people with no edge is priced as ordinary.

### What was read as ordinary culture, unpriced
Shoal-water boatcraft, tide-reading, weirs and stakes, salt-making, caulking, dyke and sluice keeping, tally and public weights, trade-talk, benches drawn by lot. All ordinary for a harbor people. The old women who read a tide a night ahead are skill, as you said, and unpriced.

### Size and shape
You hoped for fifteen to thirty thousand inside a wall and asked to hear the price of the full size. The default is about 5,000. A people markedly larger than the default has more hands, more hulls and more trade than its neighbors, and that is a real edge over other peoples, so it is priced like one, by how far above the default it stands. Each way in full:

- Choice A. About 5,000 in a walled harbor town. No price.
- Choice B. About 12,000, a walled city of size, paid for by one weakness of the people. The Keeper's draft: the city does not feed itself. Its dyked pasture, flats and boats feed about half its mouths; the rest is grain bought from the kingdoms up the river and along the coast, so a closed river, a lost fleet season or an embargo is hunger in the city within a season, and its sellers know their hold. One measurable line: with the grain boats stopped, stores carry the city three months.
- Choice C. About 20,000, a great city for this age, paid for by two weaknesses of the people: the grain weakness of choice B, with stores at two months, and a second. The Keeper's draft of the second: the city is crowded past what its water and its low streets can bear, so a sickness that reaches it runs through it; in any year a sickness comes by ship or by fog, the city loses a share of its people that a smaller town would not, and its neighbors know to close their ports to it.
- Thirty thousand is refused. No city of this age and this coast holds that many without a hinterland ruled for its sake, and you asked for no ruler and no rule.
- You may offer a different weakness of your own in place of either draft, if it is something the people would notice losing and does not restate what they already are.

The Keeper's reasons: your Slack Water already scales with the size of what shelters on your coast, and a larger city makes it worth more; the price is set on the people, where the edge is, and the card is not touched. Whichever you choose, the Keeper sets the exact number and the starting stores, trade, treasure and mood. If the world gives the city a standing lord or a neighbor that claims it, that is the world's, as you said.

### What is the world's to decide
Everything you wrote of the people's past, their tales, their customs before you and what they already believe is heard as your hope. The people have their own history, their own neighbors and a trouble you did not ask for; you will meet them as they are. Named mortals you described are set as who they are; what they do is theirs. Whether a custom, an office or a household you hoped for exists when you arrive is the world's, and nothing here promises it.
