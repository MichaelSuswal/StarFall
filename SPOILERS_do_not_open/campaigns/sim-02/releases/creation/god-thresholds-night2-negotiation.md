# Negotiation: god-thresholds, Night 2

Read your own folder and `engine/roles/npc-god.md`. This file is the Keeper's side of the table. Nothing here is a decision; it is draft card text with the Keeper's reasons, for you to accept, refuse or counter, with your reasons. Where a price could go two ways, both ways are written in full and the choice is yours; you may also propose a third. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the default.

## Round 1

### Form
Draft text: Form as you wrote it, with these lines added for the card: one body, one place; a walking pace along the ground; god-sense as the world sets it; sight and hearing clear in all light, touch and cold faint, smell and taste unsettled. Passing through any gap a fog can pass is form and costs nothing; so is drifting in wind and tearing in a hard one, as you wrote. Things carried: nothing.
Choices: your form says blows only stir it and it cannot be struck or cut in any way that matters. A god here can be wounded and its body broken, so the card must say what breaks a body of fog. (a) The body is broken when it is scattered past re-forming: a gale, a great fire or heat, or being driven through flame does it, and so does any mortal means that scatters mist, such as a fire built across the gate you stand in. Blows and blades do nothing. (b) The body can be hurt as a mortal's is by anything that disturbs it: a blow tears a piece away and the god is slow and thin until it re-forms over an hour, and enough blows at once break it. Under (b) you are harder to break by fire and easier to drive off by hand.
The Keeper's reasons: "cannot be struck" is a defence that does a power's work if nothing else can reach you, so the card must say what does. Either way is a fair body for a fog. Under (a) your wind weakness (below) is not a weakness, because a gale already breaks you; under (b) it is.

### Unlatching
Draft text:
Description: Any door, gate, bar, latch or lock made by mortal hands that the god touches with its mist, it can open. Nothing is broken; it opens as if by someone on the other side who meant it. It does not unmake a wall, free a door jammed by weight, ice or rot, lift a bar that is only a weight, or open anything that is not a made closure. The god lays its mist across one threshold at a time and may hold it open while it stays beside it; anyone may pass while it is held. When the god withdraws, the closure is as it was.
Drawback: Each opening takes something small and dear from the god's memory without notice. The Keeper removes one item from the god's record (its record of other gods or its goals, the Keeper's choice) for each closure opened, and the god is not told what went.
Limits settled at creation: in darkness the same; no reach, the mist must be on the closure; works under water; a chest or box works; a door held shut by someone pushing opens and the god does not push them back; the god's own people's gate works and costs the same; a watcher sees a door open and a cold dew on the wood; a closure made by a god, or warded by more than ordinary craft, is unruled until it happens.
The Keeper's reasons: the power is mild and true, as you say, and the memory price is a real one; its edge cases are kept as you wrote them. One addition: the memory drawback is enacted on a record, so the card must name which. Your record of gods and your goals are the records the Keeper may take from; while they are short the loss will be felt soon.

### The Last Crossing
Draft text:
Description: When the god lays its mist on a threshold (a door, a gate, a ford, a pass), it learns who or what last crossed within the last season, in what order, and which way each went: the number and the direction, never names, loads, meanings or reasons. It does not see them. Where many have crossed it learns only the last handful.
Drawback: The cold of each crossing stays. After a use the god is slowed for an hour, its mist thin and slow to answer, so that any wind drives it. A second use within a day doubles the hour.
Limits settled at creation: no reach; light and dark the same; nothing read under water; nothing older than a season; nothing of the great old things, as the god asked; another god's crossing is unruled until it happens, and until then the god would learn only that one thing of unknown kind crossed.
The Keeper's reasons: an information power that shows effects and never causes, within a season, with a cost you would notice. Kept as you wrote it. Your request that it show nothing of any god is written as "unruled until it happens," which is how every card here treats other gods; the Keeper rules that case when it comes, not before.

### When the body is broken
Draft text (under Form): The god is gone from that place. It leaves one thing: a cold dew that does not dry, a handspan across, where it fell. It returns at the first door, gate or crossing that opens within a day's walk of where it fell, no sooner than the next dusk. If the dew is carried through a door, the god comes to that door instead, as if called.
Choices: you said you would take a longer return rather than a shorter one if the price asked. It does. (a) Return no sooner than the next dusk, and the door the god returns through can never again be latched or locked against anyone; it opens to any hand from then on. (b) Return no sooner than one full turn of the moon, at the first door that opens within a day's walk, with nothing else spent.
The Keeper's reasons: a return by the next dusk at any door is the quickest return any god here has asked for, on a body that under (a) above cannot be struck. Every other god pays for a quick return with something spent or a long wait; so do you. The spent door under (a) is in your own grain, a gate that stays open.

### Trades
What you offered: wind. A steady strong wind scatters the god; it cannot hold a place and is carried downwind until the wind drops; in a gale it thins to nothing and re-forms where it ends.
What you asked: that the mist pass through any gap no wider than a hand, and that in fog, or at dusk or dawn, the god cannot be seen unless it chooses, even within speaking distance of mortals; another god in sight still knows it.
Draft pricing: the gap is form; a fog passes where fog passes, and it is written under Form for nothing. Being unseen in fog and at dusk and dawn is a power, and is priced as one.
Choices: (a) Take Form choice (b) above (the body can be hurt by blows). Then the wind is a true weakness, since blows and wind both reach you, and it buys the hiding power, which the Keeper would write as: "Veil: in fog, or in the hour of dusk or dawn, the god is not seen by mortals unless it chooses to be, even in plain sight within speaking distance. Drawback: while veiled the god cannot open or read any threshold; it is a fog among fog and does nothing. Limits: another god in sight knows it; it hides the god, not the dew it leaves or the cold a mortal walks through; a mortal who walks into it feels it." (b) Take Form choice (a) above (only scattering breaks you). Then the wind restates your form and buys nothing, and the hiding power needs a weakness that is not already your body. You may propose one; the Keeper names some shapes so that the option is plain and not to steer you: a threshold once barred against the god by name stays shut to it; the god cannot cross running water except at a made crossing; the god cannot hold its shape in full sun. (c) Take no weakness and no veil; the card stands with Unlatching and the Last Crossing.
Conduct, which buys and costs nothing: the god opens first and asks nothing, and does not close a door on someone standing at it in need. Written to your goals as conduct.
The Keeper's reasons: a drawback or weakness that restates the god's form costs nothing and is priced again; that is a rule of the table, not a judgment of you. Your own form says the mist drifts in wind and tears in a hard one, so the wind is already what you are. The hiding is a real power for a god whose seat is a gate, since a gate watched by something unseen is a different gate.

## Form

Filed under Thresholds; it holds no name. Those who do not know it say the Fog at the Gate, or the Gatekeeper.

A standing fog, grey and pale, the height of a tall mortal and about as broad, narrowing toward the top. It has a front and a back. The front is a dark vertical gap down the middle, shaped like a door left half open; there is no face, and when the god attends to someone the gap turns to them. The mist is thick at the core and thin at the edges. It holds its shape in still air, drifts in wind and tears in a hard one. It moves low along the ground without feet and leaves a faint dew on whatever it passes close. It is cold to touch, and a mortal who walks through it is chilled and wet. It speaks in a low, dry voice that seems to come from the gap.

Bodies: one. A god in this world has one body and is in one place.

What the form can and cannot do: it passes through any gap a fog can pass, a hand's width or more. It cannot lift more than a mortal. The body can be hurt by anything that disturbs it: a blow or a blade tears a piece away, and fire and heat disturb the mist as a blow does; the god is then slow and thin until it re-forms over an hour, and enough at once breaks it.

Senses: it sees and hears clearly in all light; touch and cold faintly. Smell, taste and any sense not named are unsettled and asked in play, never inferred.

Pace: a mortal's walking pace along the ground, by the pace table.

God-sense, as set for this campaign: a god knows another god only face to face, in person, within sight and speaking distance, each seen by the other; the knowing is mutual, immediate and sure, and tells only that the other is a god. At a distance there is nothing to feel. Every god knows from landing only that others like it came down when it did.

When the body is broken. The god is gone from that place. It leaves one thing: a cold dew that does not dry, a handspan across, where it fell. It returns at the first door, gate or crossing that opens within a day's walk of where it fell, no sooner than the next dusk. If the dew is carried through a door, the god comes to that door instead, as if called. The door the god returns through, the dew's door included, can never again be latched, locked or barred against anyone: it still hangs and still shuts, and it opens to any hand from then on. Another god's hand on the dew, or on the return, is unruled until it happens.

### Definitions

- A made closure: a door, gate, bar, latch or lock made by mortal hands. A wall is not one; a weight is not one; a river or a ford is not one.
- A threshold: a door, a gate, a ford or a pass, a place where one crosses from one ground to another.
- In fog: fog that is in the place. The god makes none of its own.
- The hour of dusk or dawn: the hour of each at the place where the god stands.

### Things carried

Nothing.

### Weaknesses and what each bought
