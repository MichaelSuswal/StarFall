## What is so of every god here

These are facts of being a god in this world, known from the moment of landing.

Knowing another god. A god knows another god only face to face: in person,
within sight and speaking distance, each seen by the other. The knowing is
mutual, immediate and sure. Neither can pretend to the other not to be a god.
It tells only that the other is a god, nothing of its name, its nature or what
it can do. At a distance there is nothing to feel. A god that keeps out of
sight is not known for what it is. What every god knows from the moment of
landing is only this: that others like it came down when it did.

Bodies. One body. A god here has one body and is in one place.

Mortal magic, for pricing. It is simple, and it costs the worker.

What a god is, whatever its build. You need no food. Sleep is a choice. You
move at the pace of your form; a god's pace is a mortal's unless a power says
otherwise. You cannot die; you can be wounded, imprisoned, exiled, cut off
from your people, or forgotten. Every power carries a drawback, something you
would notice losing; a drawback that only restates your form or your voice is
not one. There is no set number of powers: one great power or several smaller
ones, as you like, and the whole is priced, not the count. Nothing over the
top; latitude is earned in the world. The peoples you will find work at a
level before the medieval era, with no steel forges; peoples who walked here
from far away may have brought more.

## Options

Each of these was put to the god at the table on Day 2. Each is open to you
on the same terms. None is usual, none is expected, and none is recommended.

- Your form is yours to describe: shape, size, what moves and what does not,
  how it carries itself, in enough detail for a painter to work from. Any
  sense you do not describe is left unsettled and asked about in play; it is
  never inferred for you.
- Your name, if you have one, and what those who do not know your name will
  call you. A nameless god says what it should be filed under.
- Your past, as an outline: who you were and what you lost, as you dimly
  hold it. The rest is foggy to you, and stays so; what clears it, and when,
  is the world's.
- You may propose a power at any size. A power that reaches too far is priced
  down to a version built on something that is yours, rather than refused, and
  you may push back on the price with reasons.
- Every power has a drawback. Where a drawback could go two ways, you will be
  shown both ways in full and you choose between them, or propose your own.
- A thing you carry that does no more than an ordinary object goes under your
  form. A thing you carry that does more is a power, and is priced.
- A thing that is part of what you are (your own fire, your own breath, your
  own shadow) may be written under your form with its limits, rather than as a
  power. If it does a power's work it is priced as one.
- What happens when your body is broken is yours to say, and is priced. A god
  here cannot die. Two shapes were offered as examples and a third left open:
  that you are gone from that place, with one thing of yours left behind to be
  found again; that you lie where you fell and mend slowly in sight of whoever
  did it; or something else of your own. How you return, if you leave, is
  priced with it.
- A rule you hold yourself to may be kept as conduct, in which case it buys
  nothing and costs nothing in the rules; or taken as a weakness that binds
  the body, not only your word, in which case it buys an improvement
  elsewhere. You need not take a weakness at all.
- A weakness taken on can buy an improvement on a power or on your form. You
  name the weakness and what you would want for it, and both are priced. One
  weakness may be larger than any power.
- A power that reveals things (the past, what is far off, what is hidden) is
  priced against what it can show: effects and never causes. Where it reaches
  toward the great old things of this world, a limit may be written.
- Anything that acts on another god, or a power of yours in another god's
  hand or sight, is written "unruled until it happens" and goes to the Keeper
  then. You may ask for effects other gods would perceive.
- You may name the edge cases you expect for each power. The Keeper will also
  test every power against light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, water,
  being watched, other gods, and your own people, and will write the answers
  on your card for you to confirm.
- Where you need only wording changed, ask for it "in the write-up, not a new
  power," and it is taken as wording.
- What you want on arrival, as truly as you know it: what you will do first,
  what you would refuse, what you are listening for. This is what runs you in
  time you do not act.
