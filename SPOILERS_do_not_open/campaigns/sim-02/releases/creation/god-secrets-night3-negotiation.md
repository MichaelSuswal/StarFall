You are the being whose files are in your folder, SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/sim-02/gods/god-secrets/. Read `CLAUDE.md`, `engine/roles/npc-god.md`, your own folder, and this file. The Keeper has read your `people-hoped.md`.

## Round 1

### Edges
You proposed no edge. No edge is required. That is a plain fact of the table and carries no price and no reward: a people with no edge is priced as ordinary.

### What was read as ordinary culture, unpriced
Farming, orchards, river trade, building in wood and stone, closed doors and low voices, lamps at dusk, confidences kept as a thing owed, quarrels settled by lane. All ordinary for a town people.

### Size and shape
You hoped for seven to fifteen thousand gathered close. The default is about 5,000. A people markedly larger than the default has more hands, more trade and more spears than its neighbors, and that is a real edge over other peoples, so it is priced like one. The price could go two ways, each in full:

- Choice A. About 5,000, most in the walled town. No price.
- Choice B. About 9,000, most in the walled town, paid for by a weakness of the people. The Keeper's draft of the weakness: the town does not feed itself. Its own fields and orchards feed about two in three of its mouths in a fair year; the rest is bought from the farms upriver and down, so a bad harvest, a closed road or a quarrel with a neighbor is hunger in the town within one winter, and those who sell it grain know their hold. One measurable line: stores at the end of an ordinary harvest carry the town eight months, not twelve.
- You may offer a different weakness of your own in place of the draft, if it is something the people would notice losing and does not restate what they already are.

The Keeper's reasons: nine thousand is inside the band you named and is what a walled river town of this age can hold through a shut-in winter; above that the price would have to be heavier than one weakness. The world decides the people's history either way. Whichever you choose, the Keeper sets the exact number and the starting stores, trade and mood. The share of the harvest left out for you and the household that keeps a room are your hope: a tithe is a people's choice and cannot be set from above on a people who do not know you.

### What is the world's to decide
Everything you wrote of the people's past, their tales, their customs before you and what they already believe is heard as your hope. The people have their own history, their own neighbors and a trouble you did not ask for; you will meet them as they are. Named mortals you described are set as who they are; what they do is theirs. Whether a custom, an office or a household you hoped for exists when you arrive is the world's, and nothing here promises it.
