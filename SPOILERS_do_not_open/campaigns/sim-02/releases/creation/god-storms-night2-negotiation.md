# Negotiation: god-storms, Night 2

Read your own folder and `engine/roles/npc-god.md`. This file is the Keeper's side of the table. Nothing here is a decision; it is draft card text with the Keeper's reasons, for you to accept, refuse or counter, with your reasons. Where a price could go two ways, both ways are written in full and the choice is yours; you may also propose a third. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the default.

## Round 1

### Form, and the things you carry
Draft text: Form as you wrote it, both shapes, with these lines added for the card: one body, one place, in one shape at a time; the change takes a slow count to one hundred and cannot be made while anyone is looking; a mortal's senses in the man, a horse's in the horse; the man's pace is a mortal's, the horse's a horse's steady trot; god-sense as the world sets it; drink felt as a man feels it; anything not named unsettled. Things carried: the crook, which is a staff; the tally cord, which is a cord with knots and also the thing you leave behind when broken (below); the flask, which is a flask.
The Keeper's reasons: two shapes is your rolled form and costs nothing; the horse's trot is the pace of that form and is allowed by the world's rule. The count to one hundred and the unwatched change are limits you set on yourself, kept as you wrote them, so the change is never a way out of a room full of eyes. Nothing carried does more than an ordinary object.

### Foreknowing of the Sky
Draft text:
Description: Standing in the open, the god knows the weather of the next three days within the sky it can see: a storm's size, direction and hour of breaking, and where lightning will strike within the next hour under that sky. It knows nothing of causes and nothing beyond the sky in view from where it stands.
Drawback: Every reading brings the pressure behind the eyes and the taste of metal, a headache that lasts until the weather read has passed; the farther ahead the reading, the longer the ache. Sleep does not end it.
Limits settled at creation: night the same; under a roof or underground, nothing; the same in the horse; under water or in a cave, nothing; it tells nothing of other gods; the god's people receive nothing unless told; fog and cloud do not blind it, but low cloud that hides the far sky narrows it to what can be seen.
Choices, with your trade (see Trades below): you asked that it reach the whole sky visible from the highest ground you could stand on, not only from where you stand. (a) As first written, the sky in view from where the god stands. (b) Bought by your weakness: the sky as it would be seen from the highest ground within a day's walk of the god, whether or not the god has climbed it; the ache then lasts a day past the weather's passing.
The Keeper's reasons: an information power about the sky, which shows what will come and never why; three days under one sky is a herder's gift and is kept whole. The reach you ask is a reach, and reach is priced; the Keeper has written the price as more ache, since that is the coin this power pays in.

### The Small Turn
Draft text:
Description: The god can nudge weather already forming within its sight: turn a rain a mile off its course, hold a wind an hour, call heat lightning over a ridge, bring a shower from a cloud that would have passed, thin a fog. It works only on weather that is already there. It cannot make a storm from clear sky or stop a great one; it bends a little.
Drawback: The sky takes it back. Each working leaves the god's hands unsteady for a day, without the fine use of the fingers (no knot, no bow); in the horse the unsteadiness is in the legs and it stumbles for a day. And some weeks after, the weather bent returns harder in the same country, a little worse than it would have been, on a place the god cannot name.
Limits settled at creation: not from under a roof; by day or night, on what the god can see, and poorly on what it only senses; weather turned off the god's people lands on someone's herds, and the god may not know whose; another god's weather or hand in it, the god stops and it is unruled until it happens.
The Keeper's reasons: kept as you wrote it. The returning weather is a drawback in the world's grain and the Keeper will enact it by roll in the night turn, some weeks after, within the same country, logged. The Keeper adds no limit: a mile of rain is a camp's worth of grass and not a kingdom's.

### A Tall Tree Among Many
Draft text:
Description: People who meet the god in either shape do not mark it. They see an old herder or a stray horse and remember it as they would any such, with no detail of face lasting beyond a season. They do not wonder at it or look for a god in it. It does not turn aside a determined search, does not work on another god, and does not make the god invisible; it makes it forgettable.
Drawback: The people the god loves forget its face too. One it walked beside a year knows it by cord, crook and voice and the face goes; it cannot be thanked in a way that holds, cannot be missed, and after a season away is a kind, half-remembered stranger who must learn their names again.
Limits settled at creation: light and dark, distance, water, falling, carrying change nothing; a mortal told to look for the god finds the forgetting weakened to a doubt and not a certainty; the god's own people keep cord, crook and voice and not the face; its effect on another god's sight is unruled until it happens.
Choices, with your trade: you asked that it hold against one told to look for you, as a doubt that grows no stronger the harder they look. (a) As first written: a searcher with intent gets a doubt, and a long or close search wears through it. (b) Bought by your weakness: a searcher's doubt never hardens into certainty by searching alone; only being told by one who knows, or seeing the change, makes the god sure to them; the drawback then deepens, and the god's own people lose the face within a season even while walking beside it daily.
The Keeper's reasons: forgettable is a hiding, and a hiding that no search can wear through is a stronger one, so under (b) the cost to your own people deepens to match. The Keeper would not write (b) without the heavier drawback, and says so plainly rather than offering it clean.

### When the body is broken
Draft text (under Form): If the body is broken, the god is gone from that place. It leaves its tally cord, knotted once for every living person within sight when it fell, where it lay. The god returns when the next real storm breaks over that place, no sooner, walking in from the grass in the horse shape, with no memory of what broke it; the cord is a dead cord afterward. If a finder carries the cord back to where it was left and a storm breaks over it, the same.
Limits: the return waits on the weather; in a dry summer the god may be gone a season or more; those who left before the fall are not on the cord; if the cord is lost or burned, the god returns only when a storm breaks over the exact spot and also loses the memory of what it was most recently doing (the Keeper removes the most recent item from its goals); under a roof the god does not return until the roof is gone or the spot is open; in water or a fall the cord stays where the body fell; another god with the cord is unruled until it happens.
The Keeper's reasons: a return that waits on weather is a real wait in the world's grain and is kept. The memory loss is written as a removal from a record so that it can be enacted.

### Trades
Weakness as you offered it, binding the body: the open sky binds the god. Under a roof, in a built shelter or underground for longer than one night, the god loses the horse shape, Foreknowing and the Small Turn, and its mind goes thick as in a fever, until it has been in the open a full day.
A question for the write-up, not a new power: does a tent of hide or felt count as a built shelter? The people you hope for live in tents, and the answer changes the weakness's size by a great deal. Answer it in your reply.
How it can be used against the god: anyone who gets the god under a roof for two nights has a god with no powers and a fevered mind; a people that knows it can keep the god by housing it.
What it buys: if a tent counts, the weakness is large and buys both (b) choices above, the Foreknowing reach and the Tall Tree's hold. If a tent does not count, it is a weakness of middling size and buys one of the two; you choose which.
Conduct, which buys and costs nothing: the god takes no god's name from mortals, no offering, tithe or prayer, and leaves a people that begins to pray until they stop. Written to your goals as conduct.
The Keeper's reasons: a weakness is priced by what it costs the god that the god does not already pay. A god that means to live under open sky with a moving people pays little for being bound to open sky unless its own people's tents bind it; then it pays every winter night. You said you were ready to argue the weakness smaller and the improvements larger; the Keeper has done the opposite arithmetic in the open and shows it, so you can argue against the numbers and not the dark.

### Edge-case floor and the four tests, for every power above
Light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, water, being watched, other gods, your own people: answered in each Limits block from your own edge cases. A world scarcity: the Small Turn brings rain to grass, which touches water in drought country, a local scarcity and not a world one; the returning weather is the balance. Ruling a mind: the Tall Tree takes the memory of a face from everyone the god meets, a power over minds at a people's scale, priced by its drawback. Things made by more than craft: none of your powers works on made things. Scale across a people in a year: the Small Turn, a mile at a time, each with a day's unsteadiness and a return; the Keeper writes no count.

## Form

Filed under Storms; it holds no name. Those who do not know it call it the Grey Herder when they see the man, and the Stray when they see the horse.

Two shapes, one at a time. The first is a man, old by the look of him but not frail: tall and long in the bone, a little stooped; skin the brown of a worked saddle, creased at the eyes; hair and beard the grey of a rain cloud, tied back with a leather thong, the beard cut short with a knife; large steady hands scarred across the knuckles; pale grey eyes that rest on a thing a little too long, as though counting it; undyed wool and hide, layered, nothing a poor herder could not own. He walks with a long even stride, stands at the edge of a group, and speaks low and dry. He smells of wool, smoke and wet earth. The second is a horse, a gelding of the steppe: grey, rough-coated, rather small, ribs showing a little in winter, the same pale eyes. It cannot speak and carries nothing but itself, and a herd takes it in.

Bodies: one. A god in this world has one body and is in one place, in one shape at a time.

The change: it takes the time of a slow count to one hundred, and it cannot be made while anyone is looking at the god. Seen, the god waits. Whatever it wore is on the man and whatever it carried is with him; the horse has no hands for any of it.

Senses: a mortal's in both shapes. In the man, an alert old herder's. In the horse, a horse's hearing and smell, and a horse's wide eyes, bad at fine detail. Drink is felt as a man feels it. Any sense not named is unsettled and asked in play, never inferred.

Pace: the man's is a mortal's; the horse's is a steppe horse's steady trot, by the pace table.

God-sense, as set for this campaign: a god knows another god only face to face, in person, within sight and speaking distance, each seen by the other; the knowing is mutual, immediate and sure, and tells only that the other is a god. At a distance there is nothing to feel. Every god knows from landing only that others like it came down when it did.

When the body is broken. The god is gone from that place. It leaves its tally cord, knotted once for every living person within sight when it fell, where it lay; those who left before the fall are not on the cord. The god returns when the next real storm breaks over that place, no sooner, walking in from the grass in the horse shape, with no memory of what broke it; the cord is a dead cord afterward. A cord carried off and brought back to the place serves the same. If the cord is lost or burned, the god returns only when a storm breaks over the exact spot, and also loses the memory of what it was most recently doing: the Keeper removes the most recent item from its goals, and the god is not told which. Under a roof the god does not return until the spot is open to the sky. In water or a fall the cord stays where the body fell. Another god with the cord is unruled until it happens.

### Definitions

- A built shelter: a roof on walls fixed to the ground: timber, sod, thatch, stone, mud brick. A tent of hide, felt or cloth, a windbreak, a lean-to, or a cave mouth open to the sky is not one.
- The sky in view: the sky as it would be seen from the highest ground within a day's walk of where the god stands, whether or not the god has climbed it.
- A long or close search: a search for the god that goes on for days, or that looks at it from within speaking distance.

### Things carried

- A herder's crook of dark wood, as tall as the man, worn smooth at the grip. A staff.
- A cord of braided wool with knots in it, a hand span long when coiled. A tally cord; it is what the god leaves behind when broken (Form) and is otherwise only a cord.
- A leather flask of sour fermented milk. A flask.
