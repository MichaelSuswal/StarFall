# Negotiation: god-secrets, Night 2

Read your own folder and `engine/roles/npc-god.md`. This file is the Keeper's side of the table. Nothing here is a decision; it is draft card text with the Keeper's reasons, for you to accept, refuse or counter, with your reasons. Where a price could go two ways, both ways are written in full and the choice is yours; you may also propose a third. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the default.

## Round 1

### Form, and the thing you carry
Draft text: Form as you wrote it, with these lines added for the card: one body, one place; mortal pace; god-sense as the world sets it; sight and hearing a little keen, the rest unsettled and asked in play; not strong, hurt by a blow as anyone is. Things carried: the cloak, which is a cloak.
The Keeper's reasons: a face forgotten between sentences is a thing your Unmarked Hand does, and is priced there; the form itself is a plain perfect face. Nothing carried does more than an ordinary object.

### The Kept Word
Draft text:
Description: Whatever a mortal tells the god of its own will, as a secret, the god holds. It is safe in the god; the teller feels lighter and cannot be made to repeat it by mortal magic for as long as the god holds it. The god cannot speak it to anyone, in any way, ever. The secret does not fade in the god.
Drawback: The god cannot unhear. Every secret kept stays in it, and it cannot act on one against the teller, nor speak it to save a life, nor break it if it wishes.
Limits settled at creation: told in the dark or from a distance, it holds if said to the god and heard; a sleeper's or an uncomprehending child's telling is not willing and is not kept; told in front of others it is shared and not the god's alone; told while a god watches it holds and that god gains nothing by the watching, otherwise unruled until it happens; the god's own people tell as anyone; water, falling and carrying change nothing. The guard on the teller is against mortal magic only; a teller may still be made to speak by ordinary means (fear, pain, drink), and may choose to.
The Keeper's reasons: the power touches a mind, the teller's, and so the Keeper writes its edge plainly: it guards against workings, not against a knife or a cup. That is your own wording read exactly. The drawback is one you would notice losing and is kept.

### The Unmarked Hand
Draft text:
Description: What the god does leaves no trace that points to it. Those it speaks with remember the words and not the face, and by the next day cannot say who it was. Tracks in soft ground are taken by the next rain. A mortal asked afterward cannot describe the god, though they may say honestly that someone came.
Drawback: No one remembers the god's kindness or service. Its people cannot thank it, pass its name on, or name it in any offering; they are served and do not know by whom.
Limits settled at creation: another god face to face knows it as a god, unchanged; a likeness painted or a word written on the day is not forgotten but names no one; light and dark the same; tracks go with the next rain and not before, so a tracker on a dry day follows them like anyone's; a god acting on this is unruled until it happens.
Choices, with your trade (see Trades below): you asked that those who have met the god three times or more still not remember its face. (a) As first written: the face goes by the next day, every time, and nothing of the god is kept past a day but its words. (b) Bought by your weakness: the face is never kept, however many meetings, but after three meetings a mortal keeps the god's voice and its manner, and knows the god when it speaks again, as one knows a voice in the dark. Under (b) long service builds a knowing that is not a face.
The Keeper's reasons: as you first wrote it, the forgetting already holds against any number of meetings, since each day wipes the face; so what you ask for is that the forgetting not wear thin, and the Keeper's reading of your words is that it never did. What a weakness can buy is the shape under (b): the face stays hidden forever and the voice is allowed to be known, so that a household can keep a door unlatched for someone it knows and cannot picture. If you meant something else by "strengthened," say so in the write-up.

### The Inward Door
Draft text:
Description: Given a season of work by its own hands, with no mortal help and what is there, the god can make one passage in stone, packed earth or timber: a hidden way with a door that opens only inward. The door is unseen by any who do not already know of it. It opens for the god and for any the god names aloud at it. It leads to hidden rooms, cellars and ways under a wall.
Drawback: A passage of the god's has one way only. The door opens inward, so the god cannot leave by it unless someone outside opens it. Shut inside, the god stays until opened. Its own works are where it may be kept.
Limits settled at creation: below the water line a passage fills and is not closed by water; a door does not open into open air; it cannot be moved once made; one passage per season of work, the god's hands only; a door of the god's is a made closure and a power that opens made closures meets it as more than ordinary craft, unruled until it happens; another god does not see the door unless told, and then unruled until it happens; the god's people may be named to it and then pass.
The Keeper's reasons: a making power with a real cost in time and a drawback that can be turned on the god. You asked that the drawback be written as shown and not heavier; it is written as shown. The Keeper adds one limit, that the door is more than ordinary craft to any power that works on made things, so that the case is named before it arises.

### When the body is broken
Draft text (under Form): The god is gone from that place. It leaves one thing, a plain iron-grey stone no larger than a fist, with a small closed hollow in its face like a keyhole, warm to the hand and bearing no mark of the god. Anyone may find it. The god returns after one season, by a door of the people it serves, from inside, in the room before anyone has seen it enter. It remembers what was told it. Whoever broke it is not told where it is.
Limits: if the god serves no people yet, or none of their doors stands, it returns at the stone; another god's hand on the stone or the return is unruled until it happens.
The Keeper's reasons: a season gone is a real price and the Keeper adds nothing to it. The one line added says where you return when you have no people's door to return by, so the card is never silent on the case.

### Trades
Weakness as you offered it, binding the body: the god cannot speak a falsehood aloud. If it tries, its voice stops. It may say nothing, or say a true thing that misleads.
How it can be used against the god: one who learns it can read the god's silences as answers, and a direct question before witnesses forces either a truth or a silence that is itself a confession; the god can never bluff a rival god or deny its own people's secrets exist.
What it buys: choice (b) under the Unmarked Hand. The Inward Door's drawback stands as you wrote it, which asked nothing.
The Keeper's reasons: you are honest by virtue and wary by lean, so the binding costs you the lie you would seldom tell and the bluff you might; the sheet marks you hard in bargaining, which is a thing a god that cannot lie does with its silences. It is a real weakness of middling size and buys an improvement of the same size.

### Edge-case floor and the four tests, for every power above
Light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, water, being watched, other gods, your own people: answered in each Limits block from your own edge cases. A world scarcity: none touched. Ruling a mind: the Kept Word guards a mind against workings and nothing more, written above; the Unmarked Hand takes one memory, the face, from everyone the god meets, which is a power over minds at a people's scale and is priced by its drawback, that the god's own people cannot hold it either. Things made by more than craft: the Inward Door makes such a thing, named above. Scale across a people in a year: the Unmarked Hand runs over everyone, always, and is the only power here that does; it is a hiding, not an act, and the Keeper writes no further limit.

## Round 2

### Your reasons, answered
- The Kept Word: your ruling is granted. The teller remembers that they told a secret to someone and keeps the lightness; only the face goes.
- The Unmarked Hand, (b), your counter that the knowing be recognition only: granted, with one bound. The Keeper accepts your reason: a voice that could be described or imitated would be a trace that points to you, and the power's first line says nothing points to you. So one who knows you cannot describe your voice or manner, imitate it, or hand it to anyone as a mark. The bound: while you stand before them speaking, they can say to another who is there, "this is the one." Knowing a thing in front of you and saying so is not handing you over; and without it the knowing would be a secret each knower held alone, and a household could not keep a door together.
- Two meetings in place of three: refused, with the Keeper's reason. You argued that you will be met seldom and that a household must learn in one winter. The weakness is of middling size because you are honest already; it has now bought the knowing and the recognition-only wording, which is more than round 1 offered. Three meetings in a winter is within a household's reach if you choose to come to it, and the choosing is the point: a knowing that starts at the second meeting starts before you have decided to be known. You said you would accept three and not reopen it.
- The Inward Door: accepted on both sides.
- The body broken: your wordings are granted. The season counts from the breaking; a door of your own making counts as a door of your people if they have been named to it.
- Form: "the rest unsettled," and no sense inferred, is on the card.

### Final text
This is the text that will be your power card, in the card's own form. Description and Drawback freeze on your confirmation and are never rewritten after it.

## Form

Filed under the Listener; it holds no name. Those who do not know it call it the quiet one, or the one who keeps.

Human-seeming, of middle years, neither man nor woman at first glance, and then one or the other as the looker needs. Slight, a little under an average man's height, narrow in the shoulder, long in the hand. The body is perfect in a way that is wrong: both sides match; the skin has no scar, freckle or line; the hair is dark, short and never out of place. The face is calm and plain. The eyes are grey and steady and blink less than they should. The mouth rests closed. It moves quietly with no wasted motion, keeps to walls and edges, and stops at doors before it passes them.

Bodies: one. A god in this world has one body and is in one place.

What the form can and cannot do: it is not strong; it cannot lift much, and a hard blow hurts it as it would anyone. It can stand a very long time in one place.

Senses: sight and hearing a person's, a little keen; it hears what is said low. The rest unsettled, asked in play, and no sense inferred.

Pace: mortal pace, by the pace table.

God-sense, as set for this campaign: a god knows another god only face to face, in person, within sight and speaking distance, each seen by the other; the knowing is mutual, immediate and sure, and tells only that the other is a god. At a distance there is nothing to feel. Every god knows from landing only that others like it came down when it did.

When the body is broken. The god is gone from that place. It leaves one thing, a plain iron-grey stone no larger than a fist, with a small closed hollow in its face like a keyhole, warm to the hand and bearing no mark of the god. Anyone may find it. One season after the breaking, the god returns by a door of the people it serves, from inside, in the room before anyone has seen it enter; a door of its own making counts if its people have been named to it. If it serves no people, or none of their doors stands, it returns at the stone. It remembers what was told it. Whoever broke it is not told where it is. Another god's hand on the stone, or on the return, is unruled until it happens.

### Definitions

- A secret: a thing a mortal tells the god of its own will, meaning it to be kept. A sleeper's telling, or a child's who does not understand, is not willing.
- A meeting: one occasion on which a mortal and the god speak together.

### Things carried

- A plain grey woollen cloak with a hood. A cloak.
