# Negotiation: god-sea, Night 2

Read your own folder and `engine/roles/npc-god.md`. This file is the Keeper's side of the table. Nothing here is a decision; it is draft card text with the Keeper's reasons, for you to accept, refuse or counter, with your reasons. Where a price could go two ways, both ways are written in full and the choice is yours; you may also propose a third. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the default.

## Round 1

### Form, and the things you carry
Draft text: Form as you wrote it, with these lines added for the card: one body, one place; mortal pace on land with a roll; god-sense as the world sets it; taste and smell keen past a mortal's, hearing good for a crowd, sight ordinary, touch unsettled; always hungry and food does nothing for it. The face no two see alike is your rolled form and costs nothing; it is a face, not a hiding, and anyone can still say "a stout someone in a long coat" and point. Things carried: the coat, the satchel, the pan scale and weights, the purse, each ordinary; the coin you leave behind when broken (below) comes from the purse and is a coin.
The Keeper's reasons: nothing carried does more than an ordinary object. The form's wet hem and shifting face are form.

### Slack Water
Draft text:
Description: Within sight of the god, on water open to the air, the god can still a surface to glass or raise a chop or a surge, over a bay or a harbor mouth at the widest, for as long as it holds its attention on it, up to a night. Boats feel a calm or a squall and no one can trace it to the god, since it looks like weather turning. It moves water only: no storm from a clear sky, no holding up a wave the wind has made, no reach to water out of sight.
Drawback: Each use takes something small from the god, up front, that it did not agree to give. The Keeper removes one item from the god's record (its record of other gods or its goals, the Keeper's choice) and the god is not told what went.
Limits settled at creation: in the dark it works while the god can see the water by any light or hear it at its feet; only what is in sight; not while falling; nothing carried; not water in a vessel or barrel; not frozen water; mortals see weather and another god in sight sees the god and what it does, unruled until it happens; the god's own people thank the weather; water another god has acted on, or a god's power in sight, unruled until it happens.
Choices, with your trade (see Trades below): you asked for the whole coast within sight, up to the full night, at one item per use rather than one per extent. (a) A bay or harbor mouth at the widest, one item per use, as first written. (b) Bought by your weakness: the whole stretch of coast in the god's sight, and the price is one item for each night or part of a night held; a surge raised over a whole coast costs the same one item as a bay's. (c) The whole coast in sight, with no weakness taken, at one item for each bay's breadth of water moved, so a coast of four bays costs four.
The Keeper's reasons: this power scales. A still bay is a harbor's luck; a surge over a whole coast is a fleet drowned and a season's fishing gone, and it touches the sea trade a people of thirty thousand lives by. The memory price is the right coin, and the Keeper's only argument is how many coins a coast costs. Under (b) your weakness pays the difference; under (c) the water does. The drawback as you offered it is kept in your words, and the Keeper names the record it takes from, as it must.

### Keeping What Sinks
Draft text:
Description: Standing in water or touching it, the god feels what has sunk in the water around it: what lies there, how many, how deep, where, and roughly how long it has been down. It learns nothing of who lost it, why, or what it meant. It reaches a fair way across a bay or river mouth, no farther than a long walk, and it does not reach the great old things of this world.
Drawback: The use dulls the god's taste for days; nothing has flavor, and the deeper or heavier the find the longer the dullness.
Limits settled at creation: touch and not sight, so light and dark are nothing; only water the god is in or touching; falling into water, it works on the way down; nothing carried is felt; open water, or a well or cistern touched; nothing shows to a watcher; other gods are not felt in water nor what they carry; the god may tell its people where a wreck lies and would not say how. Great old things: the limit the god asked for is written as a Limit, and anything that is more than a sunk thing of mortal making is felt only as weight at a depth, nothing more.
The Keeper's reasons: an information power priced against what it shows, a place and an age and never a cause, with a drawback a hungry god would notice. Your own request for a limit against the great old things is written, in the only form it can take without telling you what they are.

### The Bottom Walk
Draft text:
Description: The god can walk into water and keep walking. It breathes water, sees dimly in it, and moves through it at a mortal's walking pace, on the bed or off it, to any depth, without harm from cold or pressure.
Drawback: Each time the god comes up from deep water it carries out a cold that sits in the joints for a day, and for as long as the cold lasts it cannot speak a lie, which it would very much notice.
Limits settled at creation: dim sight in water, none in total dark; no reach, only walking; a soft landing in water and a hard one on land; a mortal in the god's arms breathes nothing, so no one is carried below; anyone who sees the god walk in sees it go; face to face with another god as always; the god goes to its people or does not, and their water does not call it. On land the god is a mortal walker, which is form and not a drawback.
The Keeper's reasons: you offered this as what you are, priced as a power, and that is right: a body that lives in water does a power's work. "Only water moves me" restates your form and buys nothing; the cold and the day of truth are the drawback, and they are enough. The Keeper adds nothing.

### When the body is broken
Draft text (under Form): If the body is broken or taken, the god is gone from that place. Where it fell it leaves one thing, a single salt-crusted coin from nowhere. The god returns after one turn of the moon, up out of the nearest open water to where it fell, wherever that is, and cannot choose where. If someone keeps the coin dry and away from water, the god does not return until it is wet again; the coin can be found, taken, spent or lost, and the god cannot tell where it is.
Limits: if no open water lies near, the god comes up at the nearest sea; a god who sees the god broken sees it happen, and a god's hand on the coin is unruled until it happens; the god's people may find the coin.
The Keeper's reasons: a turn of the moon is a fair wait and the coin is a real exposure. Kept as you wrote it; nothing added.

### Trades
Weakness as you offered it, the Bound Word, binding the body: when the god says "done" aloud over terms another has heard, its body cannot act against those terms until they are met or the other releases it; it cannot walk off, vanish, break a window or use a power against them.
How it can be used against the god: a mortal who learns the word can hold the god to a bad bargain made in haste, and the god is impulsive by lean; a rival can bait it into terms. The god chooses when it says the word, which is also what limits the weakness.
What it buys: choice (b) under Slack Water.
Conduct, which buys and costs nothing: the god never asks a mortal what it has swallowed, and never lies in the terms of a bargain. Written to your goals as conduct.
The Keeper's reasons: the Bound Word is in your grain and costs you something you do not already pay, since nothing else in the world holds a god to its word; but you hold the key to it, because the word is yours to say or not. So it is a weakness of middling size. It buys the coast at one coin a night, which is a large improvement, because the Keeper judges the coast a thing you will use seldom and the harbor a thing you will use often. If you think the weakness is worth more, say what more you would want and the Keeper will price it.

### Edge-case floor and the four tests, for every power above
Light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, water, being watched, other gods, your own people: answered in each Limits block from your own edge cases. A world scarcity: Slack Water touches the sea trade of a port people, and good water is scarce on the coast of this world as elsewhere; Slack Water moves salt water and makes none sweet. Ruling a mind: none. Things made by more than craft: none of your powers works on made things; a warded hull is unruled until it happens. Scale across a people in a year: Slack Water over a coast is the one that could, and its coin count is the price.

- Your contest on the drawback's source: granted in part. You are right that goals are a larger coin than a guess about a rival, and another god made the same argument tonight in its own terms; the table prices every god alike, so one rule now stands on every card that pays in memory: one entry of the record of gods while it has an entry to give, and one line of the goals only when it does not; the last line of the goals is never taken. Your "ledger of what I was promised or owed" is not a separate purse: debts, promises and guesses are entries in your record of gods, and that is where they are taken from. So your goals are touched only when the record is bare, which in the first year it will be.

And a taste or a night's sleep cannot be taken from a record; the price must be something the Keeper can remove.
- The size of the Bound Word: your argument is accepted in part. The Keeper named one edge that depends on your choosing and you named a second that does not: the word binds hardest at the moment your fear is loudest and your temper has already spoken. That is a real edge and the Keeper had underweighed it. The weakness is larger than middling.
- What more it buys: a swimmer's pace at twice a walker's, refused; the lesser form, granted in the Keeper's numbers. Reasons: a mortal swimmer is slower than a walker, not faster, so "a swimmer's pace" as you priced it is a power's pace under a mortal's name, and the world gives every god the pace of its form. Your form is a thing at home in water, and that is worth something: half again a walker's pace in the first ten fathoms, a walker's below. It is on the card under the Bottom Walk, bought by the weakness.
- Your wordings: the hem wets no more than a damp coat; the dullness runs from a day to ten, set per find; while the cold lasts you may be silent or say a true thing that misleads, and only a false statement is barred. All granted.

## Form

Filed under the Factor; it holds no name. Those who do not know it call it the Factor, the Broker, or the one in the damp coat.

A broad, middling figure, a little under a tall man's height, heavy through the shoulder and loose in the limb, built to stand on a deck in a swell. It walks with a slight roll, feet set wide. The outline is steady: anyone can say "a stout someone in a long coat." The face is not: each person who looks sees a different face, a stranger's, plausible and forgettable, and a different one the next time; the face never frightens and never flatters. The coat's hem is always wet, however dry the day, and wets what it touches no more than any damp coat would. The hands are ordinary and warm. The voice is low, quick and friendly, and carries across a crowded room without being raised.

Bodies: one. A god in this world has one body and is in one place.

What the form can and cannot do: the face no two see alike is a face, not a hiding. The god is always hungry and food does nothing for it.

Senses: taste and smell keen, far past a mortal's; hearing good, best for a crowd; sight ordinary in light. Touch and any sense not named are unsettled and asked in play, never inferred.

Pace: mortal pace on land, by the pace table. In water, see the Bottom Walk.

God-sense, as set for this campaign: a god knows another god only face to face, in person, within sight and speaking distance, each seen by the other; the knowing is mutual, immediate and sure, and tells only that the other is a god. At a distance there is nothing to feel. Every god knows from landing only that others like it came down when it did.

When the body is broken. If the body is broken or taken, the god is gone from that place. Where it fell it leaves one thing, a single salt-crusted coin from nowhere. After one turn of the moon the god returns, up out of the nearest open water to where it fell, wherever that is; it cannot choose where. If no open water lies near, it comes up at the nearest sea. If someone keeps the coin dry and away from water, the god does not return until the coin is wet again; the coin can be found, taken, spent or lost, and the god cannot tell where it is. A god who sees the god broken sees it happen; a god's hand on the coin is unruled until it happens.

### Definitions

- Open water: water open to the air. Not water in a vessel or a barrel; not frozen water.
- In sight: what the god can see by any light; for Slack Water, also water it can hear at its feet in the dark.
- A night held: one night or any part of one in which the god holds Slack Water.
- "Done": the word said aloud by the god over terms another has heard.

### Things carried

- A long, heavy coat, always damp at the hem. A coat.
- A leather satchel.
- A small brass pan scale with a set of plain bronze weights.
- A purse of mixed coins from nowhere it can place. The coin left behind when the body is broken comes from the purse and is a coin.
