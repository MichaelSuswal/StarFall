## The land you come down into

This is the kind of country and what the land itself shows. It is not a map: there are no distances, directions or sight lines here. Those come later, from the world as it is finally laid.

### The season

Spring, the thaw. The river is high and brown and loud. Ice has gone from the shallows, the banks are soft, and the willows are in first leaf. The fords are deep.

### The country

River country. A long river comes down out of hills where its springs run cold, and widens and slows as the land flattens. Its valley is broad, with water meadows that flood, alder and willow along the banks, and tilled ground on the terraces above the flood line. Lower down the river divides and wanders, and the ground goes to reed and standing water. There are many places where the river is crossed.

### What the land shows

- Fords marked with posts, and ferry landings with a rope or a moored boat.
- Smoke from many roofs, and on the river a walled place of some size, with a landing below it.
- Roads that come down to the water's edge and stop, with nothing answering on the far bank.
- Tilled fields, weirs and fish traps, and boats drawn up above the flood.
- A spring cased in old dressed stone on a hillside above the river.
- A wide still water in a side valley, held behind a straight bank that no river made.

That is what the land shows. Who lives here, how many they are, what they call themselves, what they say came before them, and what lies beyond are not in this release.

## What is so of the world for a people

- The peoples here work at a level before the medieval era, with no steel forges. Peoples who walked here from far away may have brought more.
- Mortal magic, for pricing: it is simple, and it costs the worker. The Keeper fits a people's magic to the world on Night 3; a mortal with a knack whom you describe is priced at the table and reconciled by the Keeper.
- Your own rules are unchanged from your Night 2 release and your power card.

## Options

Each is offered once. None is the usual one, and none is expected of you.

- The size and shape of the people. The default is a people of about 5,000 with a town or city and advisors who carry out the god's wishes. You may take that, shrink it, or refuse any part of it, and nothing punishes the choice. The people you hope for is yours to describe; the actual people is negotiated. A smaller people, no town and no hierarchy are real choices with real costs, and they count toward the price of the people's edges.
- A hierarchy, or none. If you want one: who advises, who rules under you, how orders travel, who settles disputes. If you reject it: say so, and say how the people settle things among themselves. With no hierarchy, your unplayed time runs on your own conduct alone and cannot be delegated.
- What they are to you: followers, subjects, neighbors, or a people who do not know you exist. You may hope to lead them or not.
- Their kind. Human by default; another kind if you describe it.
- Their code, customs, skills and culture, described freely. Ordinary culture is not priced.
- Edges. Anything that gives a real edge over other peoples (a rare skill, unusual longevity, a magic most peoples lack) is priced against a weakness, like a power. No edge is required.
- An edge that would change your own powers (a mortal doing what only you can, or your power acting across your people) is priced on you, not on the people, and goes on your power card as a dated entry. It may not undo a limit agreed on Night 2; the Keeper will price a smaller version that keeps the limit, and may offer more than one shape.
- Their magic. You may describe none, a rumor, or a mortal with a knack. A knack is priced and reconciled with the world.
- How you meet them: already there with their own history, waiting for something, or arrived with you. You may say how you mean to be found, and what you will not do or tell in time you do not play.
- What they believe about gods before you arrive, and what they know of you and of what your powers are: that they know, half know, or do not know.
- Where a power or weakness of yours turns on a place the people keep in common (a gate, a hall, a boat), you may say whose word answers for that place.
- Names. You may name your people and their home, or leave either unnamed. The world names everything else.
- Named mortals. You may describe particular people. You set who a mortal is, never what that mortal will do.
- Anything you say about the land or its past is heard as a wish or as your people's belief. The world decides, and you will see.

## Questions for you

1. You hope for a king or lord who answers to you without being told so, and a people who do not know you are there. How do you hope to reach such a lord, when you will not be seen working and cannot be thanked?
2. You must pay a true thing to every keeper of a crossing, your own people's keepers among them. Do you hope they know why the old man at the ford tells them things?
3. You hope for a people who keep laws exactly. Whose laws: ones they have, or ones you mean to give them unseen?
