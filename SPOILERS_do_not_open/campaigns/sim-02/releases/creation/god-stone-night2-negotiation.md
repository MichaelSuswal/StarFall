# Negotiation: god-stone, Night 2

Read your own folder and `engine/roles/npc-god.md`. This file is the Keeper's side of the table. Nothing here is a decision; it is draft card text with the Keeper's reasons, for you to accept, refuse or counter, with your reasons. Where a price could go two ways, both ways are written in full and the choice is yours; you may also propose a third. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the default.

## Round 1

### Form, and the thing you carry
Draft text: Form as you wrote it, with these lines added for the card: one body, one place; a child's walking pace, never running; god-sense as the world sets it; sight good in dim light and poor in glare, hearing keen for low sounds and poor for small high ones in crowds, touch exact through soles and palms, the rest unsettled and asked in play; heavy for its size, lifting as a stone is lifted; cold no trouble. Things carried: the cloak, which is a cloak.
The Keeper's reasons: heaviness and a cool skin are form. Nothing carried does more than an ordinary object. The card will say that the god cannot run, as you wrote, since that is a pace the pace table must know.

### The Held Place
Draft text:
Description: With both hands laid on stone, on earth packed hard as stone, or on a stone wall or roof, the god holds it. While it holds, the stone within a body's length of its hands does not shift, crack, slide or fall, and a roof or slope of ordinary size above that reach is steadied with it. Load that would break it does not. The god does not move the stone or make it stronger; it keeps it from letting go.
Drawback: While holding, the god cannot take its hands from the stone or move its feet, and it feels the whole load as pressure rising in the body the longer it holds, until it is in pain and cannot speak above a whisper. If it is made to let go, all that was held goes at once, in a rush, on whoever is under it.
Limits settled at creation: by touch, so light and dark are the same; no reach; not while falling; not while walking; it holds the stone and not the water; a watcher sees a child standing very still with its hands on a wall; a god in reach does not stop it, and its effect on a god or a god's power is unruled until it happens; the god's own people are safest under it and are asked nothing.
Choices, with your trade (see Trades below): you asked that a hold of a full season last a year after the hands come away. (a) As first written: the hold ends when the hands leave. (b) Bought by your weakness: a place held for a full season stays steadied for the year after the hands come away, one such place at a time; beginning a new season-long hold ends the lasting on the old one, so the god chooses which roof it is under in the dark. (c) Bought by your weakness: a place held for a full season stays steadied through the winter that follows, and no longer, with no limit on how many places.
The Keeper's reasons: the power is a roof that will not fall, bought with the god's own stillness and pain, and the Keeper keeps it whole. What you ask is that the roof hold while you are elsewhere, which is the power acting at a distance from its drawback; that is a real improvement and must be bounded either in number or in time. Under (b) one roof in a people is always safe and the rest are as mortals built them; under (c) every roof held in a season is safe through the winter and the god must hold again each year.

### The Listening Stone
Draft text:
Description: With a bare hand or foot on living rock, the god hears through the stone about a mile around: footfalls, water moving, a slope working loose, a hollow, hammering, the weight and number of those walking. It hears effects only: what moves and how heavy, never who, never why, never words.
Drawback: While listening the god is deaf to the air and hears nothing spoken near it, and the deep slow sounds of the mountain come large and near, so that its fear is loud. It cannot stop at once; a slow count of breaths brings it back.
Limits settled at creation: dark and light nothing; nothing past a mile; a hand or foot on rock and a still body, so not falling and not carrying; water, loose earth and snow stop it; a watcher sees a child kneeling with its palm to a rock; a god keeping still in sight is heard as weight and nothing more, and any perception of a god by it is unruled until it happens; the god hears its people's steps and counts them, nothing of their words.
The Keeper's reasons: an information power that shows weight and movement and never a cause, within a mile, with a cost in the god's own grain. Kept as you wrote it. One thing the Keeper adds for the write-up: a hollow heard is a hollow, not what is in it; the power never tells the god what a deep place holds, only that one is there.

### The Unmarked Way
Draft text:
Description: In stone places (caves, cuts, tunnels, bare rock) the god leaves no trace of its passing: no track, no scuff, no echo of its step, no smell on the stone. Those who come after find the stone as it was. It does nothing to what is seen of the god while in sight.
Drawback: While on stone the god cannot leave a sign for anyone it means to find it, and those who would find it cannot find it by track or mark until it leaves the rock. It may be hidden from its own.
Limits settled at creation: seen in light or dark as anyone; distance nothing; the mark of a fall is not erased; what the god carries leaves its own mark; water washes anyway; a watcher sees the child and not the trace; a god tracking by trace finds none and a god that sees is not hindered; the god's own people cannot find its trail.
The Keeper's reasons: a small hiding with a small cost, in balance. Kept as you wrote it.

### When the body is broken
Draft text (under Form): If the body is broken past mending, the god is gone from that place, and one small stone of the place, a child's fist in size, is left where it was, warm and plainly out of its kind. The god returns from the nearest deep stone to that place after a winter has passed, as a child, whole, in the cold, unmarked. If the left stone is carried away, the god returns from the stone nearest to it, and it takes longer (a second winter). If the stone is broken, the return is delayed by another winter again.
Limits: the god does not remember the last season before the break, and what it learned then is lost (the Keeper removes from the god's goals and record of gods every item added in that season, and logs it); broken in water, the stone is left at the nearest bank; those who broke it see it go and see the stone; a god that takes or holds the stone is unruled until it happens; the god's people may find and keep the stone.
The Keeper's reasons: a winter gone and a season forgotten is a heavy return and the Keeper adds nothing to it; it only writes "longer" as a number of winters so the card is never silent, and writes the memory loss as a removal from a record so it can be enacted. If you meant a shorter "longer," say so in the write-up.

### Trades
Weakness as you offered it, the Winter Stillness, binding the body: in the deep of every winter, for as long as the ground is frozen shut, the god falls into a cold stillness in the stone, unable to act, speak or be easily woken; anyone who finds it finds a child sleeping in the cold.
A question for the write-up, not a new power: you wake into the deep of winter each year. The stillness and the waking fall in the same season, so say which you mean: that the god is still through the frozen ground and wakes as it thaws, or that the god is still only in the deepest part and wakes while the ground is still shut. The first makes the weakness larger; the second smaller.
How it can be used against the god: anyone who learns it has the god each winter, to carry, to bind, to wall in; a people that knows it must guard a sleeping child or lose it.
What it buys: choice (b) or (c) under the Held Place, as you prefer. If you mean the longer stillness (through the frozen ground), the Keeper judges it worth more than either and offers in addition that the Listening Stone reach two miles in winter, when the ground carries sound farther; say whether you want that.
Conduct, which buys and costs nothing: the god does not lie, to any mortal or any god. Written to your goals as conduct.
The Keeper's reasons: a weakness is priced by what it costs the god that it does not already pay. A god that wants to be still in winter pays something for being made still, but not the full price, since it is what you would mostly choose; what you do not choose is being unable to wake when a roof groans, and that is the real weight of it, especially for a god whose whole wish is that nothing fall. It is a large weakness, and it buys a large improvement.

### Edge-case floor and the four tests, for every power above
Light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, water, being watched, other gods, your own people: answered in each Limits block from your own edge cases. A world scarcity: safe ground is a condition your people will want and the Held Place gives it under one roof at a time; the Keeper writes no limit, since a roof held is not a roof built. Ruling a mind: none. Things made by more than craft: the Held Place holds stone whatever shaped it, so a wall made by more than craft is unruled until it happens. Scale across a people in a year: the Held Place lasting is the one that could, and the choice between (b) and (c) is the price.

Accept, refuse or counter each item, and give your reasons. Answer the question on the stillness and the waking. Where you choose among (a), (b) and (c), say which and why. ## Round 2

### Your reasons, answered
- The Held Place, (c): taken as you chose it, without your reserve counter. Your reason is accepted: the bound is your own body. A full season's hold means both hands on the stone and the feet fixed for the whole season, unbroken, and it is written so in the Definitions; no count of places is written. The Keeper's worry about scale is answered by the cost you already pay.
- The Listening Stone at two miles in winter: declined by you, for a reason the Keeper respects (a power you could not use, that would tempt the wording). It is withdrawn.
- The stillness: the longer one, through the frozen ground, waking at the thaw. Taken as you chose it.
- The landing winter: granted. You wake into the winter of your landing and the stillness binds from the first winter after. The Keeper's reason: the larger weakness is worth more than the one improvement you took, you declined the second, and this is a small thing that costs the world nothing. The Keeper notes what follows from your two choices together: your year opens in winter, and in every year after the first you will be still when it opens and will act from the thaw. That is yours to confirm knowingly.
- The balance left over: by the Keeper's reckoning the weakness is still worth somewhat more than it bought. You asked for nothing more and said why; the Keeper does not press a gift on a god that declines it, and records that the choice was yours.
- The body broken, the Unmarked Way, the conduct: accepted on both sides.

### Final text
This is the text that will be your power card, in the card's own form. Description and Drawback freeze on your confirmation and are never rewritten after it.

## Form

Filed under the Still Child; it holds no name. Those who do not know it call it the quiet child, or the stone child. It says "we" of itself.

A child of perhaps seven winters, small and narrow, a little under four feet tall, thin through the shoulders and wrists. Skin pale grey-brown, the color of wet slate drying, cool to the touch in any weather and never flushed. Hair dark, coarse and cropped unevenly, as if cut with a flint edge in a hurry. A round, plain, very still face; wide eyes set a little far apart, a flat grey with no bright in them, slow to move and seldom blinking; a small mouth that does not smile easily. Hands broad for the child's size, with short thick fingers and nails like chipped shale. It stands more than it sits and does not shift its weight; it places each foot as if testing the ground. Its voice is high and quiet and carries in still, enclosed places.

Bodies: one. A god in this world has one body and is in one place.

What the form can and cannot do: heavy for its size; it lifts a thing as a stone is lifted, all at once or not at all. Cold does not trouble it. It does not run.

Senses: sight ordinary, good in dim light and poor in glare; hearing keen for low sounds and poor for small high ones in crowds; touch exact through the soles and palms. Smell, taste and any sense not named are unsettled and asked in play, never inferred.

Pace: a child's walking pace, never running, by the pace table.

God-sense, as set for this campaign: a god knows another god only face to face, in person, within sight and speaking distance, each seen by the other; the knowing is mutual, immediate and sure, and tells only that the other is a god. At a distance there is nothing to feel. Every god knows from landing only that others like it came down when it did.

When the body is broken. If the body is broken past mending, the god is gone from that place, and one small stone of the place, a child's fist in size, is left where it was, warm and plainly out of its kind. The god returns from the nearest deep stone to that place after a winter has passed, as a child, whole, in the cold, unmarked. If the left stone is carried away, the god returns from the stone nearest to it, after a second winter. If the stone is broken, the return is delayed by another winter again. The god does not remember the last season before the break, and what it learned in it is lost: the Keeper removes from its goals and its record of other gods every item added in that season. Broken in water, the stone is left at the nearest bank. Those who broke it see it go and see the stone. The god's people may find the stone and keep it. A god that takes or holds the stone is unruled until it happens.

### Definitions

- Stone: living rock, earth packed hard as stone, or a stone wall or roof.
- A full season's hold: both hands on the stone and the feet fixed for the whole of one season, unbroken.
- The frozen ground: the part of each winter in which the ground where the god lies is frozen shut.

### Things carried

- An undyed wool cloak, too long for the child, belted with a plain twisted cord. A cloak.
