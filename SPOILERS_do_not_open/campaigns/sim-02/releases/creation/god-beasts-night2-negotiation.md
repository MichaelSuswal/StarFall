# Negotiation: god-beasts, Night 2

Read your own folder and `engine/roles/npc-god.md`. This file is the Keeper's side of the table. Nothing here is a decision; it is draft card text with the Keeper's reasons, for you to accept, refuse or counter, with your reasons. Where a price could go two ways, both ways are written in full and the choice is yours; you may also propose a third. Accepting everything is a choice you have, not the default.

## Round 1

### Form, and the things you carry
Draft text: Form as you wrote it, word for word, with these lines added for the card: one body, one place; mortal pace on a long stride; god-sense as the world sets it (face to face only); senses as you gave them, touch and taste unsettled and asked in play. Things carried: the staff, the cloak, the horn, each an ordinary thing. The horn is a horn; where a power says the beasts follow its sound, that is the power at work and the horn is only loud.
The Keeper's reasons: nothing you carry does more than an ordinary object, so nothing here is priced. Barefoot and quiet for your size is form. A giant's reach and strength are form too, and nothing on the card makes you more than a very large mortal in a fight; mortals in numbers stay a danger to you, as to every god here.

### The Study of Beasts
Draft text:
Description: Any one beast, or the leader of one herd or pack, that the god can see and that can hear its voice, the god understands: what it wants now and what it fears, plainly, as one reads a face. The god can say simple things back (food, danger, this way, stay) and the beast takes them as a fair word from a stranger it has no reason to hate. It is not bound to obey. It works on one beast at a time, never a whole herd at once, and not on mortals, gods or anything that thinks in words.
Drawback: While the god is in the Study it hears nothing but that beast; anyone or anything else may come up on it unknown. It comes out over the length of a breath, half-blind and a little sick for that breath.
Limits settled at creation: works in darkness by sound and scent; reaches as far as the god's eyes reach the beast and its voice reaches it, no farther; through water if the beast can hear; not while falling; works while carrying a beast; a watcher sees the god still and listening; a beast of another god's keeping or making is unruled until it happens; the god's own people are not beasts and it does nothing on them.
Choices: your line "it does not flee me unless I wish it to" can be written one of two ways. (a) The beast does not flee the god while the god speaks to it, and may still flee if it is hurt, hunted by another, or the god falls silent. (b) The beast does not flee the god at all while the god can see it, but the Study then takes a full breath to begin as well as to end, and the beast knows it is being read.
The Keeper's reasons: the power reads a mind and speaks to it, which is why it stays one beast at a time and never binds. Your own edge cases are kept as you wrote them. On the choice: a beast that cannot flee while the god is near is a small compulsion and costs the god something at the start; a beast that only holds while spoken to is the reading alone. Either is fair; they are different powers.

### The Turning of the Herd
Draft text:
Description: Once in a season, the god may turn one herd or pack of wild beasts it can see and send it toward a place it names within a day's walk, for one day. The beasts go because the god has stood in their way and called, and the path it shows looks to them like the best path; they are not bound and not made to love it. At the day's end they go where they would have gone.
Drawback: For as many days as the herd was turned, and no fewer than three, the god's pace is halved.
Limits settled at creation: in darkness the beasts follow the horn's sound; reaches only what the god can see; swimmers swim; not while falling; the horn and staff may be carried; a watcher sees the god stand in the beasts' path and call; a herd of another god's making or keeping is unruled until it happens; the god's own people may be told where the herd goes and may hunt it.
Choices, with your trade (see Trades below): you asked for once a moon and no floor on the slowing, in return for the weakness you offer. The Keeper prices the weakness as worth one of those, not both. (a) Once in each moon, with the floor of three days kept. (b) Once in each season, with the slowing exactly the days turned and no floor. (c) Keep the power as you first wrote it and spend the weakness on the Study or the Tally instead, naming what you want there.
The Keeper's reasons: this power touches a world scarcity. Hunger in a hard year is one of the standing fears of this world, and a god that can bring a herd to a hungry camp once a moon has solved winter for a people of two thousand; once a season leaves winter real and makes the turning a thing the keeper of your law must argue over. The floor of three days is what makes the drawback bite on a short turning; without it, a one-day turning costs one slow day, which a giant scarcely notices. Your weakness (below) is real but you are honest already, so part of its cost you have paid before you offered it; it buys one improvement of this size.

### The Tally of Ground
Draft text:
Description: When the god kneels and lays its palm on ground, it knows how many beasts of one kind passed over that ground, which way they went, and how long ago, back a few seasons. It knows nothing of why. It does not see the beasts; it knows their number and their going.
Drawback: Each use, the god tastes the last breath of the nearest dying beast of that kind, a flash of its fear that is not its own, and carries it for a day. It does not choose which.
Limits settled at creation: ground only, not stone or standing water; works in darkness; the hand must be on the earth; nothing older than a few seasons of passing, and nothing of the great old bones or any ground older than that, as the god itself wrote; not while falling; works while carrying; a watcher sees the god kneel; the tracks of a god are unruled until it happens; it counts beasts and never mortals.
The Keeper's reasons: an information power, priced against what it may show: a count, a direction, an age, never a cause. Your own limit against old ground is kept in your words, and the Keeper adds only that it never counts mortals, so it cannot be turned into a census of a town's comings and goings by calling men a kind. The drawback is a cost you would notice losing, and it is kept.

### When the body is broken
Draft text (under Form): If the body is broken past mending, the god is gone from that place. Its staff stays behind, driven upright into the ground to its own height where the god fell. The god returns at the next autumn, at the staff, weak for a day. If the staff has been moved or broken, the god returns instead at the nearest river crossing to where the staff stood, with no staff in hand, and not sure of the place.
Limits: another god's hand on the staff, or on the return, is unruled until it happens; the god's own people may find the staff and know it is to come.
The Keeper's reasons: you chose the longest return any god has asked for, up to a year, and that is price enough; the Keeper adds nothing. One question for the write-up, not a new power: a return "at the next autumn" means the autumn after the break, and a break in autumn means nearly a year gone. Confirm that is what you meant, or say "the next autumn, or a full turn of the moon, whichever is later" if you meant a shorter wait when the season is near.

### Trades
Weakness as you offered it: the god cannot say a false thing. When it tries, its mouth will not make the shape. It binds the body, not the word; it holds toward gods, mortals and beasts, in every tongue.
How it can be used against the god: anyone who learns it can get the truth from the god by asking, or know from its silence that there is something it will not say; the god can never bluff a rival, hide its people's numbers, or tell a kind lie to a dying man. It cannot promise what it does not mean.
What it buys: one of the three choices under the Turning of the Herd.
Conduct, which buys and costs nothing: the god will not kill a beast that stood within the Study and heard it speak true. Written to your goals as conduct, not to the card.
The Keeper's reasons: a weakness is priced by what it costs the god that the god does not already pay. You are honest by virtue, and the sheet marks you wary, which means you keep things behind your teeth rather than lie about them; so the binding costs you the bluff and the kind lie and little else. That is real and it buys something real, but not two things.

### Edge-case floor and the four tests, for every power above
Light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, water, being watched, other gods, your own people: answered in each power's Limits from your own edge cases. A world scarcity: the Turning touches hunger, priced above. Ruling a mind: the Study reads and speaks, never binds; the Turning shows a path, never binds. Things made by more than craft: none of your powers works on made things. Scale across a people in a year: the Turning is the only one that could, and its frequency is the price.
