# Negotiation: god-rivers, Night 2

Read your own folder and `engine/roles/npc-god.md`. This file is the Keeper's side of the table. Nothing here is a decision; it is draft card text with the Keeper's reasons, for you to accept, refuse or counter, with your reasons. ## Round 1

### Form, and the things you carry
Draft text: Form as you wrote it, with these lines added for the card: one body, one place; mortal pace; god-sense as the world sets it; sight good in dim light and poor in glare, hearing keen for water, cold no trouble; smell, taste, heat and pain unsettled and asked in play. Always knowing the sound of running water within a long walk is form, since it is hearing. Things carried: the cord, which is rope; the holed grey stone, which is a stone and also the thing you leave behind when broken (below), and does nothing else.
The Keeper's reasons: nothing carried does more than an ordinary object. The stone's part in your return is a part of your Form, not of the stone.

### The Long Water
Draft text:
Description: While the god stands in running water, or has a hand or foot in it, it feels the whole length of that stream as one body: its pace, its fullness, its cold or warmth, where it is blocked or fouled, and where living things cross it or stand in it, as weight and movement and no more. It reaches along the one course the god stands in, from source to mouth, and what happens on its fords and banks within a few paces of the water. It learns no faces, names, words or intentions, and never why.
Drawback: Each use takes a small piece of the god's own memory. The Keeper removes one item from the god's record (its record of other gods or its goals, the Keeper's choice) for each day on which the power is used, and the god is not told what went.
Limits settled at creation: still water gives only that body; frozen water gives nothing until open water is found beneath; falls and rapids are felt as noise and blur fine things; light and dark the same; no loss with distance along the line, but it holds only while the god is in the water; the god's own people are felt as weight like anyone; another god in the stream is unruled until it happens.
Choices, with your trade (see Trades below): you asked that it follow the stream into its branches. (a) One line, source to mouth, as first written. (b) The whole river and every water that joins it, bought by your weakness, with the memory price then one item per use rather than per day, since a whole river is many streams.
The Keeper's reasons: this is an information power and is priced against what it may show: weight and movement, never a cause. One line is already a long reach; a whole river with its branches is most of a country's crossings felt at once, which is why it costs more memory if you take it. The memory drawback must name a record and a rate, so the Keeper has written both.

### The Gate
Draft text:
Description: At a ford, ferry, bridge foot, stepping stones or any place where a stream is crossed, within sight of the god, it may make one crossing easier or harder for those attempting it: the water slackens and the bed firms and the stones sit true, or the current pulls, the bed softens and the stones turn. It is the stream behaving as a stream might and never more, readable as weather, melt or luck. It touches everyone who crosses while the god holds it, or one crosser if the god chooses, for the crossing only.
Drawback: For the rest of that night the god's hands are numb and clumsy and it feels no warmth in anything it touches. The cold is the god's, not the water's.
Limits settled at creation: in flood or freeze it makes a crossing less ruinous, never safe; it cannot hold a crossing shut against one who wades far enough up or down; nothing on dry land or on a bridge that does not touch water; night the same; it does not tell the god who crosses beyond what the water shows; it stops no armed man and no beast; the god's own people are touched like anyone unless the god chooses; near or against another god, unruled until it happens.
Choices, with your trade: you asked that the Gate carry to a second crossing you can name but cannot see, once a night. (a) The Gate as first written, within sight only. (b) Once each night, one further crossing on the same stream that the god can name, having stood at it before, bought by your weakness; the Keeper would then write a second drawback for the unseen use: the god does not learn how that crossing went until it next stands in the water.
The Keeper's reasons: a crossing within sight is a small kindness or cruelty landed where you can see it land; a crossing you cannot see is an act at a distance with no eye on it, which is why it costs a weakness and carries its own blindness. Your weakness buys one of (b) here or (b) under the Long Water, not both.

### The Face of the Weather
Draft text:
Description: Whatever the Long Water or the Gate does is read by mortals as weather, current or luck and leaves nothing that points to the god. Standing in plain sight at a crossing while working, the god looks like an old man resting.
Drawback: The same hiding hides the god from those who would thank it. A mortal who tries to thank, pray to or recall the god at a crossing it worked gets nothing back; the god feels the words go past into the water without landing and cannot answer them.
Limits settled at creation: it hides the workings, never the god's form from one who looks at it; it does not hide the god from another god; if the god chooses to be seen working, it may, and the Gate's numbness that night lasts the whole of the next day as well; on other gods, unruled until it happens.
The Keeper's reasons: a hiding power with a cost the god would notice losing. Your "first drawback doubling" is written out in plain terms (the numbness runs through the next day) so there is no doubt what doubles. The Keeper adds nothing else; this power makes your other two untraceable and they are small enough to bear it.

### When the body is broken
Draft text (under Form): The god is gone from that place in a breath. It leaves the smooth grey stone with the hole through it, and only that. The god rises again from the nearest stretch of that stream, wet and whole and a little slow, remembering it was gone, and cannot choose where along the stream. If the stone is taken from where it fell, the god cannot return until the stone is placed back in running water of that stream, and it does not know who has it.
Choices: as you wrote it, the god returns the next time any living person who means no harm touches the stream, which on a river is at once. Every god here pays for its return with a wait or with something spent. (a) The god rises no sooner than the next dawn after a living person who means no harm touches the stream, and only while the stone lies in that stream's running water. (b) The god rises after one full turn of the moon, at the nearest water of that stream, whatever anyone does or does not do, the stone's condition unchanged as a bar if it is taken.
The Keeper's reasons: the stone is a real exposure and is kept. The wait is the price. Under (a) your return still depends on a mortal's hand and a night; under (b) it depends on no one.

### Trades
Weakness as you offered it, binding the body: the god cannot cross a gate, ford, bridge or threshold that someone is keeping (standing at it, guarding, tending or claiming it) unless it gives the keeper one true thing, a word or a fact the god knows and the keeper does not. With nothing true to give, it stands where it is.
How it can be used against the god: a keeper who learns it can take a truth from the god at every crossing, and a people that sets keepers on every ford can bleed the god of what it knows, or hold it on one bank; the god, who wants to be unseen, must speak to be let through.
What it buys: one of the (b) choices above, the Long Water's branches or the Gate's unseen second crossing. The other stays as first written.
Conduct, which buys and costs nothing: the god takes no worship, temple or prayer. Written to your goals as conduct.
The Keeper's reasons: the weakness is real and in your grain, a crossing god that must pay at crossings, and it costs you something you have not already paid, since you are hidden by lean and this makes you speak. It buys one large improvement. The two you asked for are each large.

### Edge-case floor and the four tests, for every power above
Light and dark, distance, falling, carrying, water, being watched, other gods, your own people: answered in each Limits block from your own edge cases; falling is not raised and stays unsettled, since no power of yours works off the ground. A world scarcity: the Long Water finds fouled water, and a god that keeps its people in clean water touches the local scarcity of good water; the Keeper writes no limit, since finding is not making. Ruling a mind: none. Things made by more than craft: none of your powers works on made things; a warded ford is unruled until it happens. Scale across a people in a year: the Gate is one crossing at a time; the Long Water is watching, not acting.

## Form

Filed under the Ferryman; its name will not come up. Mortals who do not know it call it the Old Man of the Crossing, or the Cold One, or nothing at all, and call the weather by its work.

An old one, of mortal size. Stooped, long in the arm, with the knuckles of a man who has hauled rope for a hundred winters. Skin the grey-brown of a wet bank, creased as dried mud creases, always faintly damp and cool to a hand. Hair white and thin, hanging as if wet even in sun. A long face with a mouth a little crooked, as if halfway through a joke. The left eye is dark and steady; the right is pale as river ice and does not seem to settle. Bare broad feet with long toes that leave wet prints which dry slowly. A coat of undyed river reed and sodden wool, belted with a plain cord. Nothing about it shines. It carries itself as a man leans on a gate he does not own.

Bodies: one. A god in this world has one body and is in one place.

Senses: it sees well in dim light and badly in glare; it hears water better than speech and always knows the sound of running water within a long walk; cold does not trouble it. Smell, taste, heat, pain and any sense not named are unsettled and asked in play, never inferred.

Pace: mortal pace, by the pace table.

God-sense, as set for this campaign: a god knows another god only face to face, in person, within sight and speaking distance, each seen by the other; the knowing is mutual, immediate and sure, and tells only that the other is a god. At a distance there is nothing to feel. Every god knows from landing only that others like it came down when it did.

When the body is broken. The god is gone from that place in a breath. It leaves the smooth grey stone with the hole through it, and only that. After one full turn of the moon the god rises from the nearest water of that stream, wet and whole and a little slow, remembering it was gone; it cannot choose where along the stream. If the stone has been taken out of that stream's water, the god cannot return until the stone lies again in running water of that stream; while the stone bars it the god knows nothing, not who has the stone nor that the moon has turned. Another god's hand on the stone, or on the return, is unruled until it happens.

### Definitions

- That stream: the stream or river whose water the god was standing in, or nearest to, when the body was broken; on dry land, the nearest running water.
- Taken: carried out of that stream's water. A stone lifted and set back in running water of the same stream is in place.
- A use of the Long Water: one standing in the water, held unbroken until the god steps out, however many things it notices in it.
- A kept crossing: a gate, ford, bridge, ferry or threshold with a keeper at it. A keeper is a person present and aware who stands at it, guards it, tends it or claims it; a gate or bar shut in a keeper's name is kept though no one stands there. A crossing no one keeps at that moment, and open water waded or swum where no crossing is kept, are not kept.
- A true thing: a word or a fact of the god's own knowing that the keeper does not know and could not learn by looking around where they stand. It may be small. The god judges what it gives.

### Things carried

- A coil of plain river cord with a rough knot at one end. Rope.
- A smooth grey stone the size of a hen's egg, worn through the middle with a hole. A stone; it is what the god leaves behind when broken (Form) and does nothing else.
