# Secret backstory of the player's god (Vesper)

Written on creation Night 2, step 2.5, after `S/gods-sealed/common-past.md` and tied to it. The god's own agent never receives this file, and neither does the DM. The past reaches the god only through play, as effects, never as a statement of what it is (mystery standard, rule 1).

## The rest of the story

The outline the player gave: a keeper of a place of passage, where things came through to be sorted, taken in or turned away; fair and not kind; hospitality the only law; the place lost, the people let through remembered only as lit windows, and the reason it was the one at the door lost worst of all. Its fear: that the place was being locked as much as kept, and that part of the fog is a mercy.

The rest. Vesper is the lamp and the law of the great door at Hesp, the gate town on the one road from the heart of Anmere to its provinces. For most of that country's life the door was a welcome: a lamp was lit at the evening hour, whoever stood in its light was heard, and the law of hospitality said who was owed a roof. The name it still holds is the old word for that hour. The lit windows are the houses of Hesp as the door saw them each evening, and the faces behind them are every traveler the door ever let in, pressed into one remembering.

Its fear is true. About ninety years before the end the Varrach, the regency that ruled Anmere, shut the provinces out, and the great door became the wall of a quarantine. The law was not repealed. It was reread. Those who came from the plain provinces, who could speak and could not say what their mothers called them, were sorted by the same lamp and turned away, in numbers that in the last years matched those taken in. In the last autumn the droves from Auren were counted through the door, and some of those missing from the count were drovers the lamp had turned away as plain.

Chosen, or chose. Neither. The office was never one person. A thousand keepers stood at that door over the country's life, some appointed and some who simply stayed, and the pattern that fell here holds all of them at once. That is why the question has no answer in the fog, and it is the thing the god lost and cannot remember: that it was many.

## What it lost and does not remember

The shape of the place (a gate with two doors in a wall across a road). The faces. That the same lamp welcomed and refused. That it was an office and not a self. What the low door beside its own was for.

## How this past might surface

Never as a statement. A mortal turned from a door who looks at the hood-flame and flinches at being looked at by a lamp. The god's own ease, which it may notice and dislike, at saying who may not come in. A second god who, face to face, seems to have stood at the same gate. A dream beside the warm stone of an evening street of lit windows seen from a doorway, which is the god's own remembering dreamed by someone else. The word for the evening hour in a tongue no people here speaks, met carved on something old only if the night 3 history places such a likeness, logged. The fog clears by pieces through what the god does at doors, as the player asked; nothing is scheduled.
