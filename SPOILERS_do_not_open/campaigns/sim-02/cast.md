# Cast: sim-02

Written on creation Night 3b. A few figures of the world's own, with nature and history fixed and a place on the map. Each people's named mortals are in its record and are not repeated here. Everyone else rises from play. What a figure does in play is its own; this file fixes who it is.

## Beings and whole peoples

- The Sleeper [f27], on the Bare Floor [r14]. The beast under the star. About forty miles long, asleep through every age on record, breathing about once in an hour. It does not speak, hear, wake or answer. Nothing now on the land can wound it.
- The people of the rim, on the North Ashring [r12] and the South Ashring [r13]. About 9,000, without names for one another. They answer questions.
  Limit: they know only what they have themselves seen from the rim: the beast's shape, that it breathes, that a thread of light comes down to it on dim nights, that some of them walk down. They do not know what it is, how old it is, why they have no names, or anything of the star's height. They answer any plain question from anyone, at any time, truly and in few words; they cannot say when a thing happened or who did it; "that is not known" is their commonest answer.
- The cord-keepers of the pass folk, at Kinhold [f17] and the lesser kin-houses of the Deep Scarrow [r04]. Eldest among them is Urru, about 104, who keeps the long count. They answer questions.
  Limit: a cord-keeper knows what the cords count: winters, their own number, which rooms were closed and when, what crossed the passes, and in which winters the Lamp first stood below the notch and below the notch's foot. The cords hold no causes and no measure of how far. They do not know what the star is, what the stone in the Kettle [f08] is, or what any god is. A keeper answers only one who has traded fairly at a night stone for three winters, only at night, only at a stone, one question a meeting, and may decline without a reason.
- The thing in the deep channel of the Reedsea [r27]. One animal, very old, as long as six boats. It does not answer anything.

## Rulers of the godless kingdoms

- Maerwen Sark, queen of the Corrant, at Gullwick [f21]. Fifty-one, twenty years on the bench of the sea. Exact about weights, patient about debts, and tired of being told she is wrong in public by ship-holders whose grandfathers were richer than hers. She wants the gap's toll because her pans yield less each year and she can count.
- Aldous Thessa, king at Vessany [f50]. Sixty-three, a clerk's king, who doubled the Loweth's due because his granary is two thirds full and he fears a third bad year more than he fears a river people.
- Irmel Thessa, queen at Ardmoor [f51]. Thirty-four, keeper of the land's best archive and the only one of the three who reads in it. She believes the kingdom's decline is a failure of blood and is looking for the lost heir's line to mend it.
- Cadoc Thessa, king at Talcross [f52]. Forty-seven, whose oldest quarter is emptying under him. He has sent for forty years of arrears from the city on the tide because it is the only large sum anyone owes him.

## Others the great matters pass through

- Joss Tarn, salt carter, of no fixed home; winters at Dunmarrow [f18]. Fifty. He works the Salt Road [f23] from Gullwick [f21] to the fair on the Wetfoot [f05] and has done for thirty years. He carries news honestly and prices dishonestly, knows every gate-warden between the sea and the dales by name, and has never once asked why the old road goes on past the fair.
- Marget Tessel, head of the lending house of Tessel, at Vessany [f50]. Seventy. She keeps every promise and remembers every pledge, the bend town's bronze key among them, and has been waiting for a reason to call it.
- Perrin Marrin, clerk of arrears to the coast crown, at Talcross [f52]. Thirty. Careful, underpaid, raised on a story of a share that never came.
- Bryn Hale, eldest shepherd of the east end of the Greyspur [r10]. Eighty. He counts sheep in the wardens' old words and knows perhaps two hundred of them, which is more than anyone alive. He does not know they are a language.
- Ikk, a knotter of the pass folk who lives alone below the tree line on the east side of the Low Scarrow [r05]. About seventy, young for her kind. She was put out of her kin-house for teaching a charcoal burner's child a counting knot. She speaks to lowlanders, which no other of her people does, and is not a cord-keeper: she knows her own house's count and nothing of the long one.
- Sisk, a guide of the reed folk, in the western channels of the Reedsea [r27]. Twenty-six. She has dived the Drowned Streets [f53] deeper than anyone living and has seen the long wake twice. She sells passage to anyone and trusts no one on dry land.
- The girl at Goatsteps [f28]. About nine. She has begun giving the goats names, and then the other children, and the hamlet has not yet stopped her.
