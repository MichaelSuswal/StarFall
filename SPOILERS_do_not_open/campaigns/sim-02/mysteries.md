# Mysteries: sim-02

Written on creation Night 3b in the runbook's template 7.8. One epic, five long, eleven short. Everything here exists from the lock and is findable from the first morning; nothing is held back and nothing is added. Rows named C or T are in `event-schedule.md`. Kinds of clue: SEEN (the god or its people witness it), REPORTED (a named mortal tells what they saw), RUMORED (secondhand or older).

## E01 The Lowering and the One Beneath
Kind: epic. Span: the whole campaign, to Year 118. Related to: L01, L02, L03, L04, L05.

### Truth
The star over this land is in its last age. It is coming down, slowly and faster each decade, and by the clock no one controls it reaches the ground in Year 118, on the Bare Floor [r14]. What grew under it is then pressed flat and written over, and new country pours out of it. The Sleeper [f27] lies exactly beneath, older than the last such night, grown on what the land has lost and fed from above on the nights the star dims; it will be under the fall and will take a great share of what comes out. Only what is kept densely enough comes through as itself: a name said yearly by many mouths, a breed kept true, a god bound to a mountain or a river. The rooms the Measurer sealed hold little, because no one has held them in mind for three thousand years. Nothing one god can do stops the fall. What can be done has to be done: things kept by many peoples at once, the beast's share cut by slowing the land's losses in its reach, a god anchored or shed. Most of this needs more than one god.

### Body
The star, which hangs over the Sleeper [f27] and never moves against the land. The Sleeper [f27] on the Bare Floor [r14]. The Holed Stone [f25] above Old Orsa [f24] and the cliff-top stone at Westerhythe [f10]. The star tables in the Measure House [f26]. The Far Woman's rule in the weighing house at Saltstairs [f41]. The cords at Kinhold [f17]. The rim people, who watch.
Limit: the rim people know only what they have themselves seen from the rim: the beast's shape, that it breathes about once in an hour, that a thread of light comes down to it on dim nights, that some of them walk down. They do not know what it is, how old it is, why they have no names, or anything of the star's height. They answer any plain question from anyone, at any time, truly and in few words; they cannot say when a thing happened or who did it; "that is not known" is their commonest answer.
Limit: a cord-keeper of the pass folk knows what the cords count: winters, their own number, which rooms were closed and when, what crossed the passes, and in which winter the Lamp first stood below the notch of the peak they sight it over. The cords hold no causes and no measure of how far. A keeper answers only one who has traded fairly for three winters at the Leaving Stone [f67] or the Night Table [f68], only at night, only at that stone, one question a meeting, and may decline without a reason.

### Cost if ignored
The fall, in Year 118, which ends every people and place on the sheet that has not been kept. Before it: the Sleeper [f27]'s reach widens. From Year 14 people outside the rim walk toward the Bare Floor [r14] in their sleep, first from the Hollow March [r11] and the Rivermeet [r15]; from Year 30, from the Underfell [r06] and the upper hamlets of the Middle Reach [r26]. A god that never measures the star is surprised by everything that already knows.

### Clock or trigger
Clock. Rows C01 (the fall), C02 (the lowering made plain), C03 (the Sleeper [f27]'s reach), and the recurring dim nights, in `event-schedule.md`. Triggers T01 and T02 can bring C01 forward; T03 can hold C03 back.

### Clues
#### The hole that no longer holds the Lamp
Location: the Holed Stone [f25]
What is shown: SEEN, from Year 1 by anyone who looks through it at night. The hole was plainly bored to frame the star from a worn foot-hollow in the rock behind it. From the hollow, the star now sits a full hand below the hole's lower edge.
Readings: the star is lower than when the stone was set; the slab has leaned (it does lean); the hole was bored for something else, a sunrise or a peak; the foot-hollow is not the place the sighter stood.

#### The margin note of 600
Location: the archive at Ardmoor [f51]
What is shown: REPORTED, from Year 1 to anyone the scribes admit. A survey of the old heartland made for Queen Halla notes that from the slab above the dead city "the Lamp stands a finger under the hole, which the rim men say it filled in their fathers' fathers' time."
Readings: a finger then and a hand now means it is quickening; the scribe measured carelessly; the rim men were flattering a queen's surveyor; a finger and a hand are both the slab's lean.

#### The Lamp in the old songs
Location: Gullwick [f21], among the Corrant, in the midwinter naming songs
What is shown: RUMORED, from Year 1. The oldest songs place the Lamp "above the mast-top at the turn of the quay" on the longest night. From that quay it now stands at the mast's cross-yard.
Readings: poetry; the quay was rebuilt; masts are taller now; the Lamp has come down.

#### The winter of the notch
Location: the cord-keepers of the pass folk, at the Leaving Stone [f67] or the Night Table [f68]
What is shown: REPORTED, from the third winter of fair trade at the earliest. A keeper asked will find the knot: the winter the Lamp first stood below the notch. It was 211 winters ago. A second knot, 60 winters ago, marks the winter it stood below the notch's foot.
Readings: two marks and their years give a rate and a quickening; the peak has been worn by slides; the cords mark a custom of sighting from a different ledge; it counts fog years.

#### The Far Woman's rule
Location: the weighing house on the long quay at Saltstairs [f41]
What is shown: SEEN, from Year 1. A staff of dark wood with two sliding pegs and a scale of fine cuts along it. Held to the eye and set on the star and the horizon, it gives a number. Along its back are four older sets of cuts with tally marks beside each, in a hand not hers.
Readings: a tool for the height of a light above the sea; a weaver's or shipwright's rule; the older cuts are a record of another light's height, taken long apart, each lower; the older cuts are a child's game.

#### The breath
Location: the crest of the North Ashring [r12] or the South Ashring [r13], looking in
What is shown: SEEN, from Year 1 by anyone who watches an hour on a clear evening. The dark shape at the floor's middle has a head laid down and a flank, and the flank rises and falls.
Readings: a beast asleep; a hill with heat-shimmer over it; a god; a thing that will wake.

#### The thread
Location: any place within sight of the Bare Floor [r14] on a dim night; best from the rim crest
What is shown: SEEN on dim nights (their count is in the phenomena record). The star dulls for part of the night and a thin line of light stands between it and the Sleeper [f27].
Readings: the star feeds the beast; the beast feeds the star; the star speaks to it; a trick of dust over the floor, like a sun pillar.

#### Those who walk down
Location: the inner slopes of the North Ashring [r12] and the South Ashring [r13]; Goatsteps [f28]
What is shown: SEEN and REPORTED, from Year 1. Some of the rim people walk down onto the floor at night and are found, by any who look, a day or two out, lying on their sides facing inward, unmarked. The hamlets do not stop them.
Readings: the beast calls them; they go to die as their custom is; a sickness of the rim; despair in a people with nothing to want.

#### What is in the jars
Location: any of the five sealed rooms: the Measure House [f26], the Stairfoot Door [f62], the Lintel Door [f15], the Underkeys [f57], the Springdoor [f47]
What is shown: SEEN, by whoever opens one. Seed jars three thousand years old. Sown, a few kinds sprout and most do not. The kinds that sprout are the ones still named in some living people's speech.
Readings: old seed mostly dies; the sealing failed in places; what is still spoken of keeps; the Measurer's keeping has worn thin unevenly.

### Branches
#### The god sets its own mark and sights the star each year
What the world does: after six years two careful marks differ by a width a patient eye can see; after twelve the yearly change itself has grown. The Keeper gives each year's sighting as it is. Nothing in the world remarks on it.

#### The god decides the beast is drinking the star down, and attacks or starves it
What the world does: nothing mortal or divine now on the land can wound it. A great working against it repeats Orsand's: the workers are hollowed, the ground they stood on goes dead, row T01 fires, and the beast does not wake. Driving the rim people away from its sight does slow its growth a little (row T03).

#### The god decides the beast is a guardian and feeds or honors it
What the world does: offerings left on the floor lie there. People brought to live in sight of it lose the use of names over years, as the rim did. The reach in row C03 comes sooner by the number of years the god's people have faced it.

#### The god decides the lowering is a trick of the eye and does nothing
What the world does: the world goes on. Rows C02 and C03 arrive on their years. By Year 8 old people in every country say the Lamp is larger than it was.

#### The god keeps something on purpose: a name said yearly by many mouths, a breed, a rite
What the world does: nothing visible for decades, except that the kept thing does not go plain when its neighbors do (rows of L02 skip it). This is the only branch whose result outlasts Year 118.

#### The god seeks the other gods to act together
What the world does: each answers as its own agent decides. A name or rite kept by several peoples at once is kept more surely than by one, and the Keeper counts it so.

### Never found
What the star is. Why the beast was not crushed by the last such night. Whether the beast knows anything. What the First Crowns are now. The exact night: even the Measure House [f26] tables, read with every other mark, give the fall only to within twenty years.

## L01 The Dross of the Throw
Kind: long. Span: about 60 years. Related to: E01, L03.

### Truth
On the night the gods came down, a piece of the same throw that did not become a god fell into the Kettle [f08]: a blue-grey stone the size of a hay cart, warm to the hand, heavier than it should be. It holds, thinly, what the gods are made of, and dreams it at anyone who sleeps near: a crowd kneeling at a ford, a river no dreamer has seen, a gate with two doors and a lamp over the taller, a child riding on a tall one's shoulders, a count that comes out short, a room that opens only inward, a harbor with a deep in it. A god that sleeps there dreams its own part sharper than the rest. No dream says what any of it was.

### Body
The stone in the Kettle [f08], in the Deep Scarrow [r04], 295 miles from the Hald Valley [r01] landing and 287 from the Underfell [r06] landing. The pass folk's two watchers. The lintel of the Lamp Hut [f61], on the Far Capes [r30], which carries two words and a gate cut by a man whose line came from the gods' own country.

### Cost if ignored
If no god reaches it, the watchers become its keepers. By Year 20 a small priesthood of the heights exists that belongs to no god and chants what the stone dreams. It lends them a little of what it holds, and their workings are stronger than any on the land. If one god reaches it and others do not, that god holds the shape of every other god's wound.

### Clock or trigger
Trigger and clock. Row T04 (a god sleeps at the stone) and row C04 (the kneelers, Year 20 if no god has taken the stone into its care), in `event-schedule.md`.

### Clues
#### The watchers
Location: the rim of the Kettle [f08]
What is shown: SEEN, from Year 1 by anyone who climbs there. Two of the people of the heights sit above the hollow, day and night, relieved at dusk. They do not go down to the stone and do not stop anyone who does.
Readings: they guard it; they fear it; they are waiting to see who comes for it; it is a grave of theirs.

#### The melt ring
Location: the Kettle [f08]
What is shown: SEEN. In deep winter the hollow's floor is bare of snow for twenty paces round the stone, and a small spring runs from under it that was not there before. It is the highest water of the Withy [f12].
Readings: a hot spring broke out that night; the stone is hot from its fall and will cool; it is alive; it is a piece of the star.

#### The watchers who have begun to face it
Location: the rim of the Kettle [f08]
What is shown: SEEN, from about Year 3 by one who watches the watchers for a season. Those who have sat many nights no longer sit looking outward. They sit facing the stone, and come down at dusk slowly, and one has been seen on its knees.
Readings: cold and weariness; a new order from their elders; the stone does something to those who sleep near it; they have decided it is theirs.

#### The lintel
Location: the Lamp Hut [f61]
What is shown: SEEN, by whoever goes there. Two words in an unknown script, a gate with two doors and a lamp over the taller, and a line of smaller writing. A god can read any mortal tongue: the words are Maunt and Issel, and the line is a joke about a ferryman who charged the river for crossing itself.
Readings: a hermit's home country, real and far; a madman's invention; the same gate some dreamers describe; a coincidence of gates.

#### Habits no god chose
Location: on the gods themselves, wherever one is watched for a season
What is shown: SEEN, by any patient watcher, mortal or god. Each of the gods has a habit it did not choose and does not explain: one knots a cord without deciding to, one counts the breathing in a room, one kneels to speak to anyone smaller, one keeps count of doors. A watcher who has also slept at the stone finds each habit in the dream. A meeting between gods tells neither of them any of this; the world's rule gives only that the other is a god.
Readings: every god is strange in its own way; they are kin; they are pieces of one thing; the stone makes its dreams out of whoever comes near.

### Branches
#### A god goes to the stone and sleeps beside it
What the world does: row T04. It dreams, its own part sharpest. The Keeper gives the dream as images only. The watchers knot that a god came.

#### A god takes the stone away or walls it in
What the world does: it can be moved by forty people with rollers, or by a god with time. Moved to a god's seat, it dreams at that people instead and they begin to kneel to it, not to the god. The pass folk remember who took it.

#### A god tells its people the stone is holy, or cursed
What the world does: pilgrims, or a ban, as the people's own agent decides. Either way more mortals sleep near it sooner than Year 20, and row C04 comes early or late by their number.

#### The god never goes
What the world does: row C04 runs on its year.

### Never found
Whose country it was. Why a throw makes gods of some of what it carries and stone of the rest. The name of the river in the dream, unless the lintel is read by one who has also dreamed.

## L02 Kind by Kind
Kind: long. Span: about 80 years. Related to: E01, L05.

### Truth
The land is spending more than the star's thin rain returns, and it shows first where the ground is oldest in use, where great workings were done, and where the Sleeper [f27]'s reach lies. A place that has overdrawn does not wither. It goes general: water with no taste, reeds of one kind, calves of one color, speech that has lost the words that were only its own, people who answer to any name. Each decade the worst places lose another kind for good. A place where things are kept on purpose, named, bred true, told over, loses them more slowly. What is lost below is gained above.

### Body
The oldest quarter of Quillan [f55], by the first wall. The old quarter of Talcross [f52]. The Eastflat [f54]. The easternmost pasture of the Rope Ridges [r20]. The salt pans at Panmouth [f22] and on the east flats of the Pell Mouth [r23]. The nine wells of Stonemeet [f66]. The wild herds of the Hornfoot Grass [r16]. The Bare Floor [r14], which went first.

### Cost if ignored
Every people on the sheet loses kinds, each decade a few more, and the peoples nearest the worst places lose their own particular ways and then their names. The schedule gives the years. In the Hald Valley [r01] the first loss falls about Year 22 if nothing changes.

### Clock or trigger
Clock. Row C05 and its table of losses by year and place, in `event-schedule.md`. Trigger T01 brings every later row of that table forward ten years.

### Clues
#### The old lanes' way
Location: Quillan [f55], the lanes by the first wall
What is shown: SEEN, from Year 1. People there answer to any name called. The lane's own turns of speech are gone. Its well tastes of nothing, where every other well in the town tastes of iron.
Readings: age; a slow sickness in that well; inbreeding in old families; something under the oldest quarter, where the doors are.

#### Sixty years of tallies
Location: the panmasters' hall at Panmouth [f22]
What is shown: REPORTED, from Year 1 to anyone the panmasters trust. The yield of salt per pan has fallen a little every decade for sixty years, with the same sun and the same sea.
Readings: wetter summers; lazy panmen; the sea is fresher than it was; the salt is the same weight and salts less.

#### Brine that does not keep
Location: the east flats of the Pell Mouth [r23]
What is shown: SEEN, from Year 1. Meat packed in the east pans' salt spoils in half the time it used to. The pan-wives say the brine has gone thin.
Readings: bad barrels; river water getting into the pans; the same thing as at the northern pans; a seller's lie to cover short measure.

#### One reed
Location: the Eastflat [f54]
What is shown: SEEN, from Year 1. A reach of marsh wider and shallower than it was forty years ago, with one kind of reed where there were nine, and no eels.
Readings: silt from the crowns' fields; salt creeping in; overfishing; the thing in the deep channel has fouled it.

#### The dun calves
Location: the easternmost pasture of the Rope Ridges [r20]
What is shown: SEEN, from Year 1. Calves and foals born all one dun. The families that summer there come back quieter each year and slower to answer to their names.
Readings: one bad bull; the blood of the Dun Walkers showing; poor grass; whatever lies east of the ridges.

#### The red-flanked kind
Location: the Hornfoot Grass [r16], in the old men's talk
What is shown: RUMORED, from Year 1. A kind of wild cattle not seen since their fathers' time, and fewer wild herds each decade.
Readings: overhunting; the herds have moved east; a murrain; the land is tired.

#### Wells that are only wet
Location: Stonemeet [f66]
What is shown: SEEN, by any who go. Nine wells with water in them that stock will drink and not thrive on. It tastes of nothing at all.
Readings: a mineral gone from the ground; a curse on the town; the same as the old lanes' well; nothing, old wells go stale.

#### The spring that kept its taste
Location: Wardwell [f48]
What is shown: SEEN, from Year 1. A cased spring whose water tastes exactly as the oldest Loweth songs say it does, sharp and cold, in a country where three wells within a mile have gone flat in living memory.
Readings: a deeper source; a holy spring; something was done to it long ago; chance.

### Branches
#### The god blames a sickness and treats it
What the world does: nothing treats it. The loss goes on at its rate.

#### The god blames a person, a people or another god
What the world does: the accused answer as they would. The losses continue on their years, in the accuser's country too.

#### The god moves its people off the worst ground
What the world does: the people keep what they have a decade or two longer than they would have. The ground left behind goes on by row C05.

#### The god has things kept on purpose: named, counted, bred true, told over yearly
What the world does: those things miss their row in the table for as long as the keeping lasts. Nothing announces it; the god sees it only by a neighbor losing what its own people still have.

#### The god forbids the workings of the grip in its country
What the world does: its country's rows fall two years later than written, and row T01 cannot fire there.

### Never found
Where what is lost goes. Why the oldest ground goes first. Whether anything gone plain has ever come back.

## L03 The Weight No God Carries
Kind: long. Span: about 70 years. Related to: E01, L04.

### Truth
Gods came down here once before, four of them, about 3,900 years ago, and made Orsand. Three grew as their people grew, passed a weight no god can carry on the ground, went light and rose into the star, where they are still: not gods and not dead. They are most of whatever mind the star has, they are very old, and they speak as weather and want almost nothing. The fourth saw it, refused to grow, gave away what it had into springs and sealed rooms, and was at last prayed over the same weight by a frightened people. The weight is the same for the gods now on the land: 79, on a scale where a newly landed god stands near 20. A god past 63 begins to hear them. A god three years past 79 rises on the next dim night. A god may refuse to grow; may give itself away into people and places; or may bind itself to a mountain, a river or a people's one sung name and carry more.

### Body
The voices that come in weather to a sleeper on the rim crest and to a god past 63.
Limit: the voices know weather, counting, and the old images of where they were first kept. They do not know the year of the fall, the rate of the lowering, the weight any god stands at, what the star is or what the beast is, and they say so by saying nothing. They cannot be questioned: a god may speak to them and gets no answer to anything asked. They speak unasked, rarely, a few words at a time, to count or to name a weather; once or twice in decades one names a power to a god past 63, and never says what it costs.
The carvings of Old Orsa [f24]: four crowned figures with their faces worn smooth and everything else sharp; the Reaper King a hand's breadth above a threshing floor. The feast lists in the archive at Ardmoor [f51]. The houses of ancestors in Vessany [f50], Ardmoor [f51] and Talcross [f52]. Wardwell [f48] and the other kept springs. The thread on dim nights. A god's own shadow.

### Cost if ignored
A god that grows without knowing rises and is gone from the ground for good. The warnings come three times: its shadow pales; the stones of its own house go soft at their edges; voices come in weather. There is no fourth.

### Clock or trigger
Trigger. Rows T05 (a god passes 63: the voices begin) and T06 (three years past 79: it rises on the next dim night), in `event-schedule.md`.

### Clues
#### The worn faces
Location: Old Orsa [f24]
What is shown: SEEN, from Year 1. On every carving of the four, the face is smooth as a river stone and the hands, robes and tools are crisp. No chisel did it.
Readings: later people defaced their old kings; weather; a custom of touching the faces; the faces were never cut.

#### The king above the floor
Location: Old Orsa [f24], the relief on the granary wall
What is shown: SEEN. One crowned figure stands with a clear gap carved between its feet and the threshing floor. The threshers are looking at the gap.
Readings: a boast of holiness; a carver's way of showing rank; a thing that was seen; a warning.

#### Three feasts and no fourth
Location: the archive at Ardmoor [f51]
What is shown: REPORTED, from Year 1 to anyone admitted. Orsand's feast lists, copied and recopied, keep three "crownings in the Lamp", about 130 years apart. For the fourth Crown there is a last entry and no feast.
Readings: three were taken up as a reward and one was not worthy; three were taken and one escaped; the fourth list was lost; the fourth still walks.

#### The old kings will be asked
Location: the houses of ancestors in Vessany [f50], Ardmoor [f51] and Talcross [f52]
What is shown: SEEN and REPORTED. The Thessend keep lamps before nameless crowned figures and say the old kings went into the Lamp and will be asked for an accounting.
Readings: ancestor worship; a memory of something that happened; a crown's claim to old blood; the Lamp is where the dead go.

#### Weather that counts
Location: the rim crest of the North Ashring [r12] and the South Ashring [r13], in sleep; and in the sleep of any god past 63
What is shown: SEEN, by the sleeper. Tall figures without faces, in rain or a moving sky, counting something over and over. In this star's telling the sky is wide and there is shallow water under houses on legs. They rarely speak. When they do it is to count, or to name a weather.
Readings: the dead; the star's own mind; the old kings; the rim's sickness reaching the sleeper.

#### The paling shadow
Location: on the god itself, wherever it stands
What is shown: SEEN, by the god, past 63 and more each year. Its shadow is lighter than a mortal's beside it. It can be looked at longer. It finds anger harder.
Readings: a blessing; a wasting; what happens to every god that is loved; the first of the old kings' signs.

### Branches
#### The god takes rising for a reward and seeks worship
What the world does: its weight climbs with its people and their fervor. Rows T05 and T06 fire on their numbers.

#### The god takes it for a hunt and hides from worship
What the world does: its weight falls as it is forgotten, and its powers with it. It never hears a voice.

#### The god gives itself away into springs, saints and breeds
What the world does: each gift lowers its weight and makes one thing that keeps. The gifts outlast it.

#### The god binds itself to a mountain, a river or one sung name
What the world does: it can carry more than 79. The thing it is bound to spends faster: springs first, then the river's bends, then everything in its people that is not the god. Its neighbors see the springs go.

#### The god answers the voices
What the world does: they have little to say and mean it. Once or twice in decades one names a power; it costs weight to hold and brings the god nearer 79.

### Never found
What the three are now. Whether the fourth meant to go. Why a god's weight is what it is.

## L04 The Measurer's Rooms
Kind: long. Span: about 50 years. Related to: E01, L03.

### Truth
The Measurer had nine rooms cut and sealed between about 3,250 and 2,951 BA and filled each with the same things: slate rolls of names, jars of seed, the kingdom's bronze weights and measures, and a slate naming the other eight. Five lie unopened on the sheet. The Measure House [f26] also holds nine hundred years of star tables. The rooms were meant to keep what the kingdom was. They keep less than was hoped, because a kept thing needs living minds as well as a locked door; but what is in them is still more than any living people has of that age, and some of the seed is of kinds now lost above.

### Body
The Measure House [f26] under Old Orsa [f24], behind a plastered blocking with a handprint. The Stairfoot Door [f62], under slide rubble of 1,900 BA. The Lintel Door [f15], blocked with fitted stone from outside. The Underkeys [f57], behind two locks whose keys were one family's. The Springdoor [f47], under forty feet of the Bankwater [f45]. The two broken rooms under Gullwick [f21] and under the Tide Stumps [f43].

### Cost if ignored
Rooms are lost one at a time. The Springdoor [f47] is scoured out if the bank breaks (row C08, Year 7). The Underkeys [f57] passes in part to a lender's claim (row T09). As kinds go plain above (L02), the only true stock of some of them is in the jars, and dies there unsown.

### Clock or trigger
Trigger and clock. Rows C08 and T09, and row T07 (a room is opened), in `event-schedule.md`.

### Clues
#### Doors where cellars end
Location: the Underkeys [f57]
What is shown: SEEN, by the keyholders; REPORTED by anyone of the old quarter. Three fitted doors of stone on bronze pins, each with two keyholes. The family opens none of them and has one key.
Readings: tombs; a treasury; a way out of the town; something shut in.

#### A doorway blocked from outside
Location: the Lintel Door [f15]
What is shown: SEEN, from Year 1 from the terraces of Undercrag [f13]. A dressed doorway high on the slope, filled with fitted blocks whose faces are flush on the outer side.
Readings: a tomb; a mine closed after a death; a store closed by its owners, who meant to come back; a prison.

#### The slate in the empty room
Location: the cellars of the harbor hall at Gullwick [f21]
What is shown: SEEN, by anyone let into the cellars. A looted chamber with a stair that ends in a blank wall, and on the floor a slate in Orsand script, cracked across, with nine short lines. A god can read it: nine places, named by road and distance from the dead city.
Readings: a list of forts; a list of tombs; a merchant's stations; a list of rooms like this one.

#### Under the stair a door
Location: the trimming rhyme of the Corve family, at Ostrey [f01]
What is shown: REPORTED, by Anwen Corve if she is asked for it or heard at the lamp. A counting rhyme for filling a lamp that names nine lights, "one at the stones", and "under the stair a door".
Readings: a nursery rhyme; a list of the old road's lamps; a memory of one real door up the road; nothing, rhymes say such things.

#### The cased spring's twin
Location: the Bankwater [f45]
What is shown: SEEN, in a dry autumn from a boat. Under the still water, roofs; and beside what was a spring house, a squared doorway with no door in it but a single slab.
Readings: a drowned shrine; a cistern; one of the old doors; a cellar.

### Branches
#### The god opens a room by force
What the world does: the room is as it was left. Row T07. Whoever claims the ground, the keyholders, the Hodd, the pass folk, the warden of Kessary [f44], learns of it and answers as they would to a broken door.

#### The god opens a room by leave

## S11 The Untaken Eel
Kind: short. Span: 5 to 9 years. Related to: L02.

### Truth
The thing in the deep channel of the Reedsea [r27], which the reed folk give the first eel of each year and have never seen, is one of the land's few nameless beasts: a single animal, very old, as long as six boats. It has lived in the east channel. As the Eastflat [f54] goes plain it is moving west, toward the main mouth of the Lowe [f03].

### Body
The beast. The Eastflat [f54]. The reed folk's first-eel custom. The barge channel below Ardmoor [f51].

### Cost if ignored
In Year 6 it reaches the barge channel. Barges are lost in still weather. Ardmoor [f51] blames the reed folk, and by Year 8 the crown is hunting them again.

### Clock or trigger
Clock. Row C15, in `event-schedule.md`.
