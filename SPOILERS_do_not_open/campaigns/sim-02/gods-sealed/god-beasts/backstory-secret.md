# Secret backstory of god-beasts (the Hunt-Warden)

Written on creation Night 2, step 2.5, after `S/gods-sealed/common-past.md` and tied to it. The god's own agent never receives this file, and neither does the DM. The past reaches the god only through play, as effects, never as a statement of what it is (mystery standard, rule 1).

## The rest of the story

The outline it holds: a keeper of herds under a high roof of lights; a small weight that rode its shoulders; pride in what it kept; then the sky came down.

The rest. It is the herd warden of Auren, the high summer pastures on the shoulder of the Keld in Anmere, and the written law of the autumn drive. The roof of lights is the star of that country seen from the high grass, low and large in its last centuries. Each autumn the warden led the droves down to the gate town of Hesp, and by a custom older than the regency one of the country's child heirs rode down on the warden's shoulders at the head of the drive, as the small king of the year. That is the weight its shoulders still lean to hold. In the last decade the child stopped coming: the twins were hidden under the Keld, and the warden walked the last drives with nothing on its shoulders.

In the last autumn the counters at the edge of the herd read weather no one had seen before, and on their word the warden brought the droves down early. The count at the gate came out short. Then the star fell, which is the sky it remembers: the light going the wrong color, the sound, the herds gone.

Its pride is the office's pride. The warden's law was the one thing in Anmere the Varrach never rewrote, and the pattern remembers being the last honest thing in a country that had started rereading its laws.

## What it lost and does not remember

The names of the herds, which were the names of Auren's families. Who rode its shoulders, and that the rider was taken from it to be hidden, not lost. That it brought the droves down early on another's word, and that the count was short.

## How this past might surface

A child who climbs onto its shoulders without asking, and the god's body knowing exactly how to carry one. Its flinch under a low bright sky. After it has dreamed beside the warm stone, a second god in whom it knows a figure from the dream, one who walked at the edge of the same herd. A dream beside the warm stone of a drive coming down a mountain with a small figure riding high at its head. Nothing is scheduled.
