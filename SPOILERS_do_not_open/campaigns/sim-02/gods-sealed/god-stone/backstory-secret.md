# Secret backstory of god-stone (filed under Stone)

Written on creation Night 2, step 2.5, after `S/gods-sealed/common-past.md` and tied to it. The god's own agent never receives this file, and neither does the DM. The past reaches the god only through play, as effects, never as a statement of what it is (mystery standard, rule 1).

## The rest of the story

The outline it holds: a hollow in the dark, dry and cool, a slow sound far above; a second small breathing beside its own, and then the quiet after it; a weight that was its own and that it did not set down; not being allowed to stay; waiting for someone to say it may stay somewhere.

The rest. It is the child under the Keld. In the last decade of Anmere the regency hid the country's two child heirs, twins, in a cut room under the mountain, to be kept through whatever was coming. The room was closed by the regency's hidden works with a door that opened only inward. Two went in. In the dark, at some point no record fixes, one child's breathing stopped. One came out. The country told the story both ways for the few years it had left: that the one who lived had smothered the other for the air, and that the one who lived had held the other all night and could not keep it breathing. The pattern that fell here holds both tellings at once, pressed together, and cannot separate them. That is the weight, and the "we."

Before the hiding, one of the twins had ridden each autumn on the shoulders of the herd warden at the head of the drive down from Auren, as the small king of the year. The pattern does not know whether it is the one who rode or the one who watched.

The one who came out was not let stay in the country's heart. The regency sent the surviving heir away, out by the gate town, and that is the last thing in the fog before the landing.

## What it lost and does not remember

Which child it is. Whether it killed or failed to save. The rider, and who carried the rider. Who made the door that would not open from inside.

## How this past might surface

Its counting of breaths in any room. A tall one, met face to face, whose shoulders it looks at too long. A plain perfect face before which it will not stand in a closed room. A sleeper beside it who lives through a night they should not have. A dream beside the warm stone of two children in a dark room, one counting. Nothing is scheduled.
