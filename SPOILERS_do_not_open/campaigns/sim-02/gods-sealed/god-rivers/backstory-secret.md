# Secret backstory of god-rivers (the Ferryman)

Written on creation Night 2, step 2.5, after `S/gods-sealed/common-past.md` and tied to it. The god's own agent never receives this file, and neither does the DM. The past reaches the god only through play, as effects, never as a statement of what it is (mystery standard, rule 1).

## The rest of the story

The outline it holds: people came to it; there was water and a place where things were set down for it, and voices; then the voices stopped and no one told it why; a habit of listening for a sound that does not come.

The rest. It is the Issel, the long river of Anmere, as it was prayed to at its fords. For most of that country's life every crossing had a shrine, travelers set a small thing in the water before they crossed, and the river had a name that was said aloud there. The two faces it half shows are the two banks: the river that let you over and the river that did not. About sixty years before the end the Varrach, the regency, forbade the offerings. Its quiet works stripped every ford shrine in one season and carried off the stone that bore the river's name. The stone was taken down to the port of Maunt and sunk in the harbor deep. Within two generations the fords were only fords, and no one on the river could say what it had been called. Then, for sixty years, nothing. That silence is longer in its memory than the worship was.

It was not betrayed by anyone it would recognize. An order was written by people who thought prayer was a spending, and they were not wholly wrong. Its laugh is the laugh of a thing that was thanked for ten thousand years and then filed.

## What it lost and does not remember

Its name, which the harbor deep still holds. That the forgetting was ordered. Who carried the order out. That it ran to a sea.

## How this past might surface

A mortal who sets something in the water at a crossing for no reason they can give. A word the god cannot say. Another god, met face to face, in whose mouth something sits that the river god leans toward without knowing why. Another whose hands it mistrusts on sight. A dream beside the warm stone of a crowd kneeling at a ford on a river none of the dreamers has seen, which is its own river. Nothing is scheduled.
