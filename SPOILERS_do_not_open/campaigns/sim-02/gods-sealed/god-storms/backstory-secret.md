# Secret backstory of god-storms (filed under Storms)

Written on creation Night 2, step 2.5, after `S/gods-sealed/common-past.md` and tied to it. The god's own agent never receives this file, and neither does the DM. The past reaches the god only through play, as effects, never as a statement of what it is (mystery standard, rule 1).

## The rest of the story

The outline it holds: a wide place with many animals and people moving across it under weather; standing at the edge; a hard season; a gate, or the shape where a gate was; counting, and counting again, and the number being wrong; a taste of metal.

The rest. It is the count at the gate, and the weather-reading of the drovers who lived by it. In Anmere the great herds summered on Auren, on the shoulder of the Keld, and came down each autumn to be numbered through the great door at the gate town of Hesp, people and beasts together, by counters with knotted cords who stood at the edge of the drove and did not join it. The counters were also the ones who read the sky for the drive. In the last autumn they read weather no one had seen before, pressure behind the eyes and a taste of metal, and on their word the herd warden brought the droves down early. At the gate the count came out short. Some had been lost on the mountain in the haste. Some had been sorted under the lamp at the great door and turned away as plain, and so were never counted in. The counters counted again. It was still short. Before a third count the star fell.

The people it failed to save are the ones missing from that count. It did not lose them by carelessness. It called the weather rightly and they were lost anyway, some of them to a door it did not keep.

## What it lost and does not remember

Who the missing were. That it was right about the weather. That the count was short partly by another's sorting. That it was many counters and not one.

## How this past might surface

Its cord, which it knots without deciding to. A count of its own people that will not come out the same twice. After it has dreamed beside the warm stone, a tall one it knows from the dream as walking the edge of the same herd, and a lamp in a hood it knows from the dream as the light by which someone was not let in. A dream beside the warm stone of a drove passing a great doorway while someone at the edge says a number again. Nothing is scheduled.
