# People: the Corrant (the kingdom at the north river's mouth)

## Who they are
Human. A sea-and-river kingdom on the northern coast, where the river that runs through the northern gap reaches the sea. One queen, a council of ship-holders, a hard-working and unsentimental people who think of the uplands as where wool and trouble come from.

## How many
About 35,000: one port city of about 8,000, a dozen towns, fishing villages along the north coast.

## Where
The northern lowlands from the sea up the river to the foot of the gap in the hills. Finished from the map in the section at the foot of this record.

## How they live
Fishing, salt pans on the north shore, oats and rye, sheep, coastwise trade in broad sewn boats. They are the uplands' source of salt and they know it. Timber halls on stone footings older than the halls.

## Their code
What is weighed is honest; what is promised at the salt pans is kept. The sea's dead are named aloud every midwinter. The queen may be told she is wrong by any ship-holder, once, in public.

## Their history in the land
Founded about 500 years before the gods came by coast peoples who took over the works of an older harbor. They took the toll of the gap in the hills about 300 years before, lost it about 120 years before in a winter rising of the hill towns, and have claimed it ever since without marching. Their salt pans have yielded a little less each decade for sixty years; the panmasters blame the weather.

## What they believe about gods
The sea gives and keeps accounts. They sing to it and expect nothing. The star is "the Lamp" and its lowering in old songs is taken as poetry. A claimed god would be asked what it can do about the pans.

## What they want
The gap's toll back. A market for salt that does not depend on the hill towns' goodwill. Lamp oil: they render fish and seal for it and sell it dear.

## How they touch the great events
They hold the north's salt and much of its winter light. Their pans are one of the places where the land's loss can be counted by anyone who keeps tallies, and they have sixty years of tallies.
