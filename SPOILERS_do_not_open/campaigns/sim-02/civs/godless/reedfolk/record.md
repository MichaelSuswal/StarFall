# People: the reed folk (the Keeper's working name; the river people call them the marsh neighbors, or the wet ones)

## Who they are
Not human. A lean, long-toed, mottled people of the great southern marsh, who swim as easily as they walk, hold their breath a long while, and are at ease in cold water. Wary, quick, soft-spoken, fond of barter and bad at trust. They live about fifty years.

## How many
About 4,000, in floating and stilted villages that move with the channels.

## Where
The reedbeds, creeks and standing water of the delta marsh where the long southern river meets the sea. Finished from the map in the section at the foot of this record.

## How they live
Fish, eels, waterfowl, reed bread, woven reed boats and houses. They guide strangers through the marsh for a price and drown none of them. They burn little: fire is hard to keep on water, and they see in the dusk better than people do.

## Their code
The water is everyone's road and no one's ground. A guide's word holds until dry land. Nothing is taken from the drowned stones. Children are taught to dive before they are taught to speak to strangers.

## Their history in the land
They were in the marsh before the river kingdom rose and were hunted by it at its height, 600 years before the gods came, when it wanted the delta drained. The marsh won. Since the kingdom split they have been left alone and sell eels and passage to all three crowns. Their oldest villages stand over worked stone that runs out under the water, and their divers know its streets by touch. In the last forty years the eastern reach of the marsh has gone slack: wider, shallower, the reeds all of one kind, the eels gone from it. They have moved west and do not talk about the east.

## What they believe about gods
Something large lives in the deep channel and is owed the first eel of the year. They have never seen it. They would take a god for a dangerous trader.

## What they want
The marsh, whole. A fair price. To know why the east reach went quiet.

## How they touch the great events
They live on one of the places where the land is visibly going plain, and they have moved because of it. They know the drowned stones better than anyone living.
