# People: the pass folk (the Keeper's working name; neighbors call them the knot people or the night traders)

## Who they are
Not human. A small-statured, long-armed, grey-skinned people of the high range, with large dark eyes that see well by starlight and poorly at noon. They live long, about a hundred and twenty years, breed slowly, and speak little to outsiders. They count and remember with knotted cords and trade when they choose, by night, at stones set for the purpose, leaving goods and taking goods without meeting.

## How many
About 2,500, in some forty kin-houses.

## Where
The high valleys, caves and old cut rooms of the western range, above the tree line, between and above the countries at its foot. Finished from the map in the section at the foot of this record.

## How they live
Goats of their own small breed, lichen bread, hunting, cut stone, a fine cord of goat hair, a little worked copper. They winter inside the mountain in rooms their ancestors cut. They keep the high passes in the sense that nothing crosses without their knowing it.

## Their code
What is counted is true. What is not asked is not told. A trade left at a stone is honored or returned whole. The lowlanders' dead and the lowlanders' doors are not touched.

## Their history in the land
They are the oldest people on the sheet. Their ancestors cut the first rooms in the range about 6,000 years before the gods came, when humans were few and low. They once held the foothills too and were pressed upward over three thousand years by every human kingdom that climbed. Their cords carry a count of winters that long, and a count of their own number, which has halved in five hundred years. They closed several of the deep rooms from outside themselves, long ago, and their cords say which and not why.

## What they believe about gods
Nothing that they will say. Their cords have a knot for "came down" that is older than this arrival. They watched something fall into one of their high hollows on the night the gods came, and they have set a watch on it and told no one.

## What they want
To be let alone above the trees. To trade for salt and bronze on their own terms. To know what the warm stone in the hollow is before any lowlander does.

## How they touch the great events
The warm stone that fell with the gods lies in their country. Their cords hold the longest honest count in the land, of winters and of their own decline. They are few, slow to trust, and no one's allies.
Limit: a cord-keeper knows what the cords count: winters, their own numbers, which rooms were closed and when, what crossed the passes. The cords do not hold causes, and a keeper asked why will say the cord does not say. They do not know what the star is, what the stone is, or what any god is. They answer only one they have traded with fairly for at least three winters, only at night, only at a trading stone, one question a meeting, and they may decline without giving a reason.

### What they know of the land

They know everything above the trees and see a great deal below from above. They tell none of it.
