# People: the people of the rim (the Keeper's working name; they have no name for themselves)

## Who they are
Human. The people who live in the worn hills around the great bare basin at the middle of the land, in sight of the thing that sleeps there. They have words for everything except each other: they point, touch a sleeve, or say "that one". They are gentle, slow, incurious and hard to anger. Strangers find them restful and then frightening.

## How many
About 9,000, in hamlets of thirty to two hundred along the inside of the rim. There were more: half the hamlets stand empty.

## Where
The inner slopes of the hills ringing the basin, and nowhere beyond them. Finished from the map in the section at the foot of this record.

## How they live
Goats, a plain grey barley, gathered roots. Houses of reused stone from the ruins thick along the rim. They make almost nothing a neighbor would trade for and want almost nothing. They do not go down onto the basin floor and do not say why. They sit facing it in the evenings.

## Their code
Do not go down. Do not wake it. Share the pot. They keep no count of days, of kin or of the dead.

## Their history in the land
Their forebears were the last holders of the old heartland, the rim towns of the road-making kingdom that fell about 2,900 years before the gods came, and then tenants of every later power that claimed the rim. Records in the south name rim families, with pedigrees, as late as 400 years before the gods came. Names for people went out of use among them over about four centuries, a hamlet at a time, nearest the basin first. No one among them marks it as a loss. In the last thirty years some have walked down onto the floor at night and not come back; the hamlets do not speak of it and do not stop them.

## What they believe about gods
The sleeper is the oldest thing and the only thing worth facing. They do not pray to it. They look at it. A god who came among them would be looked at, fed, and not named.

## What they want
Nothing they can say. To be left where they are.

## How they touch the great events
They are the body of what the sleeper does to those who live in its sight. They can be asked what they have seen, and they answer plainly, and they have no word for when.
Limit: they know only what they have themselves seen from the rim: the sleeper's shape, that it breathes slowly, that the light comes down to it on dim nights, that some walk down. They do not know what it is, how old it is, or why they have no names. They answer any plain question from anyone, at any time, truly, in few words, and "that is not known" is their commonest answer. They cannot say when a thing happened or who did it.
