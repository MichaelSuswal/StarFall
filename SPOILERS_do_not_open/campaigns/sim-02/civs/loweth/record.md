# People: the Loweth

God: god-rivers (the people do not know this on the day it lands). Agreed on creation Night 3a from the god's `people-hoped.md` and its table. The people named themselves; the god left them and their home unnamed.

## Who they are
Human. A crossing people on the long river's middle course: ferrymen, toll keepers, boatwrights, weir and trap tenders, traders, and the farmers of the terraces who feed them.

## How many
About 5,200: about 1,800 in the walled seat, the rest in hamlets at the fords and on the terraces.

## How they live
By the water and by crossing it. Boats, weirs, traps, tilled terraces, water meadows left to the river in spring. They mark the river's height each thaw on poles.

## Their code
Law, written plainly and posted where it applies. A bargain struck in exact words is kept even when it hurts. A crossing keeper who takes more than the posted toll is shamed in the open. No fouling of a spring or of a stream above a landing.

## Skills and culture
Rope, knots, boats, ferries, weirs, marked poles, weights and tallies, bargaining, landing jokes, river songs. Ordinary and unpriced.

## Edges and their weaknesses
None. The god proposed none and the table closed in round 1. The default size, with the people's own lord and keepers, none carrying the god's word.

## Their magic
Fitted to the world, as for every people: about one in a few hundred has the grip, and most of those do not know it. A working is attention paid; it does small things (warmth from cold, a bleed stopped, a thing found, weather or a beast read); it takes from the worker something small and specific that she does not choose and notices only afterward. Among the Loweth: perhaps fifteen with the grip. One ferryman's mother can find a drowned body in a day; she cannot now recall her own wedding.

## What they believe about gods
They honor the river as a thing: a handful of grain, a word at the ford. Every ford has its own old man of the crossing in story, and the stories are jokes. No temple, no priest.

## Hierarchy
Their own, and none of it the god's. A warden of the crossings holds the walled seat and the landing; ford and ferry keepers answer to the warden for their crossings. Disputes are heard at the landing by the warden and three keepers under the posted law, and the judgment is posted.

## How the god meets them
Already there. The god means to be found as an old man resting at a ford who talks too much. It comes to itself at the thaw, with the fords deep.

## Their history, their neighbors, and a trouble their god did not ask for
The walled seat stands on the footings of a river fort of the old lower kingdom, which held this reach six hundred years ago and still claims it. The warden pays a yearly due in boats and grain to the nearest of the three crowns downriver. In a side valley above the seat lies a wide still water behind a straight bank: the crown of six hundred years ago dammed it for a canal and drowned a town that would not sell. The Loweth fish it and do not swim in it. Roads come down to the river and stop, with nothing on the far bank: they are older than the fort, and where the river has moved the far half lies under meadow. Their trouble: the crown downriver doubled the due this year and sent a clerk to count the fords.

## What the god hoped, and what the people hold
Held: a walled seat and landing; a warden and keepers; posted law; habits at the water; the old man of the crossing as a common tale. Not held: a lord whose law happens to run where the god would want it. The warden is exact and fair by the letter, and also in the habit of pleasing the crown.

## What they know of the land
Finished from the traced map (step 3a.6), in the section at the foot of this record.

## Named mortals
- Marda Ferrow, warden of the crossings, about sixty-five, exact, dry, twenty years in the office.
- Tobin Ashe, keeper of the upper ford, about thirty, the loudest voice against the doubled due.
