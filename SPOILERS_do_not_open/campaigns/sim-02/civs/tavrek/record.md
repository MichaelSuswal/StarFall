# People: the Tavrek

God: god-beasts (the people do not know this on the day it lands). Agreed on creation Night 3a from the god's `people-hoped.md` and its table. The people named themselves; the god left them and their home unnamed.

## Who they are
Human. A herd people of the grass and the broken hills at its edge, in six bands that travel apart and gather in autumn. Wary of strangers, fair once they have measured one, fond of sayings.

## How many
About 2,100, in six bands of 250 to 450.

## How they live
They follow the wild herds and keep some cattle and horses of their own. No fixed seat; hide shelters struck in a morning. They cross rivers and do not settle on them. They trade hides, horn and dried meat for salt, which they never have enough of.

## Their code
A written law on skins, every limit named with the cost of breaching it. A hidden bargain is a broken bargain. Whoever pays a cost says aloud what it was, and it is written. Quarrels go to the law and its keeper, never to the strongest at the fire.

## Skills and culture
Tracking, reading weather far off, moving camp fast, butchery, hide, bone and horn, honest count, sayings. Ordinary and unpriced.

## Edges and their weaknesses
None. The god proposed none and the table closed in round 1. Smaller than the default, no town, no hierarchy under the god: the god's choices, which bought nothing.

## Their magic
Fitted to the world, as for every people: about one in a few hundred has the grip, and most of those do not know it. A working is attention paid; it does small things (warmth from cold, a bleed stopped, a thing found, weather or a beast read); it takes from the worker something small and specific that she does not choose and notices only afterward. Among the Tavrek: perhaps seven with the grip. One is known, a bone-reader of the second band who can tell where a herd watered; she has lost the names of two of her own children and the band covers for her.

## What they believe about gods
Tales, half believed, of a Tall One at the herd's edge. In their telling he is long dead and the pale curve on the hillside is his rib. They leave a strip of the first kill there in autumn, by custom. They do not expect him.

## Hierarchy
None under the god. Each band has elders. One office spans the bands: the keeper of the law, who carries the skins. The old keeper died at midsummer, and two claim the skins: his apprentice, and the eldest elder of the largest band, who holds that the bands should choose. The autumn gathering now assembling is to decide it.

## How the god meets them
Already there. The god means to stand at the herd's edge in the open and wait to be seen. It lands as the bands draw together for the autumn gathering.

## Their history, their neighbors, and a trouble their god did not ask for
They have been on the grass about nine hundred years and have never built in stone. Their oldest law skin is a copy of a copy; its first lines are in a speech no one now reads, kept because the law says nothing may be scraped. Their southern neighbors on the same grass are another herd people, and the middle of the steppe between is no one's. Their trouble: the keeper's office is in dispute, and without a keeper there is no one to say who a turned herd is for. A second, slower trouble they have not named: the wild herds are fewer each decade, and the old men say the red-flanked kind has not been seen since their fathers' time.

## What the god hoped, and what the people hold
Held: the written law and its keeper (the office exists and is disputed); bands with no seat; the tale of a Tall One (as a dead one). Not held: any knowledge of the god or its nature; any rule in their law about a turned herd, which the law has never had to cover.

## What they know of the land
Finished from the traced map (step 3a.6), in the section at the foot of this record.

## Named mortals
- Hesk Orrun, the dead keeper's apprentice, about thirty, exact, unloved, carries the skins now.
- Dama Veth, eldest elder of the largest band, about sixty, who wants the bands to choose the keeper.

## The land, from the traced map

Written on creation Night 3a after the tracing, from `map.json`, never from the plan.

Home: the Hornfoot Grass [r16], D1, the grass under the east end of the Greyspur [r10], running north to a low shore. Their autumn gathering ground lies on the grass under the spur's last hillside, where the Rib [f29] stands, about fifty miles and four days north of the fords of the Hornwater [f31]. Their god lands there.

### What they know of the land

What the bands know among them. Distances are straight lines measured on the traced map. Days are on foot along that line, each stretch at the pace table's rate for the region it crosses (rough ground 14 miles a day for valley, hill, plain, grass and shore; forest 10; mountain 8; marsh 6), with the kinds of ground named in order of their share; a line that follows a used road is given at the road rate and says so. Grades: seen (they have been there), reported (told by one who has), rumored (a name and a rough place).

- The Hornfoot Grass [r16] (seen): their own grass, every ford and hollow of it. The gathering ground lies in it.
- The Rib [f29] (seen): the pale curve on the hillside, the dead Tall One of their tale. A strip of the first kill is left under it each autumn. From the gathering ground, lies about 2 miles west.
- The Twin Spring [f30] (seen): the wet saddle where two rivers begin; they water there. From the gathering ground, lies about 100 miles east, 7 days on foot, at the pace table rate for each stretch of ground crossed (open grass by share of the way).
- The Hornwater [f31] (seen): forded every autumn. From the gathering ground, its nearest reach lies about 50 miles south, 3 days on foot, at the pace table rate for each stretch of ground crossed (open grass by share of the way).
- The Longloop [f32] (seen): forded in spring going out and in autumn coming in. From the gathering ground, its nearest reach lies about 110 miles east, 8 days on foot, at the pace table rate for each stretch of ground crossed (open grass by share of the way).
- The Hardway [f35] (seen): the straight stretch where the grass grows thin over something hard. They ride along it and have never asked where it goes. From the gathering ground, lies about 1 mile south.
- The Greyspur [r10] (seen): the broken hills; shepherds there trade with them at the hill fords. From the gathering ground, the middle of it lies about 280 miles west, 20 days on foot, at the pace table rate for each stretch of ground crossed (hill country by share of the way).
- The Windward Grass [r17] (seen): summer hunting ground. From the gathering ground, the middle of it lies about 510 miles east, 37 days on foot, at the pace table rate for each stretch of ground crossed (open grass by share of the way).
- The Noman Grass [r19] (reported): the middle grass, no one's; hunters go into its edge. From the gathering ground, the middle of it lies about 550 miles south-east, 39 days on foot, at the pace table rate for each stretch of ground crossed (open grass by share of the way).
- The Rope Ridges [r20] (reported): where the other herd people are; headmen have met at the Dunwater [f33]. From the gathering ground, the middle of it lies about 940 miles south-east, 67 days on foot, at the pace table rate for each stretch of ground crossed (open grass, then hill country by share of the way).

- The Rivermeet [r15] (reported): the farm villages where bronze is had. From the gathering ground, the middle of it lies about 340 miles south, 24 days on foot, at the pace table rate for each stretch of ground crossed (open plain, then open grass by share of the way).
- The North Ashring [r12] (rumored): brown hills in the south where the people do not use names. From the gathering ground, the middle of it lies about 720 miles south, 52 days on foot, at the pace table rate for each stretch of ground crossed (open plain, then hill country, then open grass by share of the way).

### The god's hope and the ground

The god hoped for steppe with broken hills at its edge and rivers to cross, and has it. The paint put the broken hills on the grass's western side, as a grey spur, and not along the north; the people's ground is under the spur's east end. Changed in this record: nothing but place.
