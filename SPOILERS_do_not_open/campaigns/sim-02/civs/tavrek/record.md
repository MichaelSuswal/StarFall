# People: the Tavrek

God: god-beasts (the people do not know this on the day it lands). Agreed on creation Night 3a from the god's `people-hoped.md` and its table. The people named themselves; the god left them and their home unnamed.

## Who they are
Human. A herd people of the grass and the broken hills at its edge, in six bands that travel apart and gather in autumn. Wary of strangers, fair once they have measured one, fond of sayings.

## How many
About 2,100, in six bands of 250 to 450.

## How they live
They follow the wild herds and keep some cattle and horses of their own. No fixed seat; hide shelters struck in a morning. They cross rivers and do not settle on them. They trade hides, horn and dried meat for salt, which they never have enough of.

## Their code
A written law on skins, every limit named with the cost of breaching it. A hidden bargain is a broken bargain. Whoever pays a cost says aloud what it was, and it is written. Quarrels go to the law and its keeper, never to the strongest at the fire.

## Skills and culture
Tracking, reading weather far off, moving camp fast, butchery, hide, bone and horn, honest count, sayings. Ordinary and unpriced.

## Edges and their weaknesses
None. The god proposed none and the table closed in round 1. Smaller than the default, no town, no hierarchy under the god: the god's choices, which bought nothing.

## Their magic
Fitted to the world, as for every people: about one in a few hundred has the grip, and most of those do not know it. A working is attention paid; it does small things (warmth from cold, a bleed stopped, a thing found, weather or a beast read); it takes from the worker something small and specific that she does not choose and notices only afterward. Among the Tavrek: perhaps seven with the grip. One is known, a bone-reader of the second band who can tell where a herd watered; she has lost the names of two of her own children and the band covers for her.

## What they believe about gods
Tales, half believed, of a Tall One at the herd's edge. In their telling he is long dead and the pale curve on the hillside is his rib. They leave a strip of the first kill there in autumn, by custom. They do not expect him.

## Hierarchy
None under the god. Each band has elders. One office spans the bands: the keeper of the law, who carries the skins. The old keeper died at midsummer, and two claim the skins: his apprentice, and the eldest elder of the largest band, who holds that the bands should choose. The autumn gathering now assembling is to decide it.

## How the god meets them
Already there. The god means to stand at the herd's edge in the open and wait to be seen. It lands as the bands draw together for the autumn gathering.

## Their history, their neighbors, and a trouble their god did not ask for
They have been on the grass about nine hundred years and have never built in stone. Their oldest law skin is a copy of a copy; its first lines are in a speech no one now reads, kept because the law says nothing may be scraped. Their southern neighbors on the same grass are another herd people, and the middle of the steppe between is no one's. Their trouble: the keeper's office is in dispute, and without a keeper there is no one to say who a turned herd is for. A second, slower trouble they have not named: the wild herds are fewer each decade, and the old men say the red-flanked kind has not been seen since their fathers' time.

## What the god hoped, and what the people hold
Held: the written law and its keeper (the office exists and is disputed); bands with no seat; the tale of a Tall One (as a dead one). Not held: any knowledge of the god or its nature; any rule in their law about a turned herd, which the law has never had to cover.

## What they know of the land
Finished from the traced map (step 3a.6), in the section at the foot of this record.

## Named mortals
- Hesk Orrun, the dead keeper's apprentice, about thirty, exact, unloved, carries the skins now.
- Dama Veth, eldest elder of the largest band, about sixty, who wants the bands to choose the keeper.
