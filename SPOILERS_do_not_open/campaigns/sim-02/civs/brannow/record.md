# People: the Brannow

God: god-secrets (the people do not know this on the day it lands). Agreed on creation Night 3a from the god's `people-hoped.md` and its table. The people named themselves; the god left them and their home unnamed.

## Who they are
Human. A close-mouthed town people in the great bend of the south-western river, with farms and orchards around. They read what is not said and keep their own counsel.

## How many
About 9,000: about 6,500 inside the walls, the rest on the farms and orchards near it.

## How they live
Behind walls, close, through winters that keep people in. Fields and orchards on the better ground, river trade, lamps lit early behind shutters.

## Their code
What is told in confidence is owed keeping. One who carries another's secret into the street is not trusted again. Silence is neither an insult nor a lie. A question asked before others is asked with care.

## Skills and culture
Farming, orchards, river trade, building in wood and stone, small rooms and low voices, telling done late and one to one. Ordinary and unpriced.

## Edges and their weaknesses
One edge, priced at the table (ledger L-0022). Edge: a people markedly larger than the default, about 9,000. Weakness that pays for it: the town does not feed itself. Its own fields and orchards feed about two in three of its mouths in a fair year; the rest is bought from farms upriver and down. Measurable line: stores at the end of an ordinary harvest carry the town eight months, not twelve. This is the people's weakness and the sellers' hold, no part of the god's card. Hunger within one winter is the risk in a year the world brings a bad harvest, a closed road or a quarrel; a risk and not a promise.

## Their magic
Fitted to the world, as for every people: about one in a few hundred has the grip, and most of those do not know it. A working is attention paid; it does small things (warmth from cold, a bleed stopped, a thing found, weather or a beast read); it takes from the worker something small and specific that she does not choose and notices only afterward. Among the Brannow: perhaps thirty with the grip, a handful known and not spoken of. A midwife of the east lanes stops bleeding; she has lost the taste of anything sweet.

## What they believe about gods
Little, and nothing of this god. Old stories, most lost. They do not know a god has come, and no one keeps a room or leaves a share for anything unnamed.

## Hierarchy
None under the god, and no king. Quarrels are settled by lane: the old of a lane sit with those in the quarrel, then the lanes beside it. One family, the keyholders, has kept the keys of the town's gates and of the old doors beneath it for longer than the town's records run; the keys open and close and the family rules nothing.

## How the god meets them
Already there. The god means to come to the walls and the edges as a quiet person and listen before it is heard.

## Their history, their neighbors, and a trouble their god did not ask for
The town is older than its own records and is built over rooms no one has opened: the cellars of the oldest quarter end in fitted doors the keyholders hold keys to and do not use. Old banked roads leave the used roads and run into the woods. The mounds in the bottomland are older than the town. Its grain sellers are the free farms upriver and the hill villages toward the range. Their trouble: the keyholder's heir, a man of thirty, has begun to say that a town of nine thousand that cannot feed itself needs one hand on its gates, and some lanes agree. A second trouble, unnamed: in the oldest quarter, by the first wall, people have grown vague. They answer to any name, the lane's own way of speaking has gone flat, and its well no longer tastes of the iron the other wells taste of. The town calls it the old lanes' way and thinks it age.

## What the god hoped, and what the people hold
Held: a walled town of size in the bend, no king, settling by lane, the code of confidences. Not held: a household that keeps a room and a door unlatched; a share of the harvest left out; any knowledge of a quiet one. Those begin in play or do not.

## What they know of the land

- Old Sabeth of the east lanes, the midwife with the grip, about seventy.

## The land, from the traced map

Written on creation Night 3a after the tracing, from `map.json`, never from the plan.

Home: the Bendlands [r29], B4, and in it Quillan [f55], the walled town on the rise inside the great bend of the Sarn [f56]. The fitted doors under the oldest quarter are the Underkeys [f57]. The mounds in the bottomland are the Bottom Mounds [f58]. The old banked road into the woods is the Banked Way [f59]. Their god comes to the town's walls.

### What they know of the land

What a close town knows: its own bend, its sellers, and hearsay. Distances are straight lines measured on the traced map; days are at the pace table's rate for the ground named. Grades: seen (they have been there), reported (told by one who has), rumored (a name and a rough place).

- Quillan [f55] (seen): the town. At Quillan [f55].
- The Sarn [f56] (seen): from the hill villages to the sea. From Quillan [f55], lies about 4 miles north-west.
- The Underkeys [f57] (seen): by the keyholders only; everyone else knows that the cellars end in doors. At Quillan [f55].
- The Bottom Mounds [f58] (seen): the mounds; no one digs there. From Quillan [f55], lies about 10 miles south-east, 1 day on foot over open ground.
