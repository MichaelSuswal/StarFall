# People: the Senn

God: god-thresholds (the people do not know this on the day it lands). Agreed on creation Night 3a from the god's `people-hoped.md` and its table. The people named themselves; the god left them and their home unnamed.

## Who they are
Human. A travellers' people at the crossing in the northern gap. Used to strangers, used to leaving and coming back.

## How many
About 1,600: about 1,100 inside the wall, the rest on the terraced slopes.

## How they live
A walled town at the crossing with two gates, one on each road. They farm the old terraces, keep goats and sheep, mend the road, keep the cairns, feed and lodge those who pass, and take a toll at the crossing.

## Their code
Bread and a place by the fire first, a name after or never. A stranger is not turned from the gate. Whoever leaves is welcome back. A task is given to one chosen for it and the choosing ends with the task.

## Skills and culture
Dry-stone and timber walling, road and path keeping, cairns, guiding, reading fog, hospitality, songs for leaving and returning. Ordinary and unpriced.

## Edges and their weaknesses
None. The god proposed none and the table closed in round 1. Smaller than the default and no hierarchy: the god's choices, which bought nothing.

## Their magic
Fitted to the world, as for every people: about one in a few hundred has the grip, and most of those do not know it. A working is attention paid; it does small things (warmth from cold, a bleed stopped, a thing found, weather or a beast read); it takes from the worker something small and specific that she does not choose and notices only afterward. Among the Senn: perhaps five with the grip. One guide is said never to lose the way in fog; he no longer knows the faces of travelers he led last year.

## What they believe about gods
Little. They tell of the open morning: a gate found standing open in fog, in their great-grandparents' day, and what came of it. Most say a traitor inside lifted the bar. A few say the fog did. No priests, no shrine.

## Hierarchy
None under the god, and no fixed ruler. Tasks are given at the gate or the fire. The nearest thing to an office is the toll-reeve, chosen each autumn to hold the toll chest and answer to strangers for the town; this year's is new to it. Disputes go to neighbors who are asked.

## How the god meets them
Already there. The god means to come to the crossing in fog at the turn of the day and be found by an opened door, not a name.

## Their history, their neighbors, and a trouble their god did not ask for
The toll at the gap has been taken by three peoples in three ages. The Senn took it about a hundred and twenty years ago, in a winter rising against the garrison of the kingdom at the river's mouth, whose queen has claimed it ever since without marching. The two dressed stones on the open ground beside the road are a gate with no walls, far older than the town, and the Senn hang nothing on them and do not say why. Their trouble: since the rising, both gates are barred at dusk and opened at first light, and anyone outside waits in the gatehouse lee until morning. The custom is a hundred and twenty years old and the Senn are proud of it. A second trouble: the queen's envoy came this summer with a new tally of what is owed.

## What the god hoped, and what the people hold
Held: a walled seat at a crossing, no fixed ruler, hospitality as code, a story of a fog and an opened gate (told mostly as treachery). Not held: a gate left unlocked at night. It is barred.

## What they know of the land
Finished from the traced map (step 3a.6), in the section at the foot of this record.

## Named mortals
- Ilse Brandoch, this year's toll-reeve, about forty, a carter's widow, careful with strangers and short with envoys.
