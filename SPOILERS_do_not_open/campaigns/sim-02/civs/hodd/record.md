# People: the Hodd

God: god-stone (the people do not know this on the day it lands). Agreed on creation Night 3a from the god's `people-hoped.md` and its table. The people named themselves; the god left them and their home unnamed.

## Who they are
Human. A stubborn, plain-spoken, unhurried people of the high country, who have buried many and lost roofs and learned from it. They stay.

## How many
About 5,100: about 3,600 in the walled seat, the rest on the nearest terraces.

## How they live
In houses of laid stone with deep roofs, through a long winter, a late spring and a short summer. Terraces, flocks, stores put up for the cold. They go to the heights for stone, timber and pasture, and leave the old cut openings alone. The gate is not shut on a stranger in deep snow.

## Their code
Honest count of the living and the dead, spoken aloud and set down. Say what is so. Look after the small, the lost and the hurt before asking whose they are. Do not abandon a roof, a cellar or a shaft with someone under it. Worship no face and ask no being to rule.

## Skills and culture
Dry-laid stone, lintels, reading a slope, roofs re-laid every few years, the sounds of the mountain taught to children, slow songs. A little is left at the lowest hearth stone of each house, and each winter-house keeps a room for a guest who does not come. Ordinary and unpriced.

## Edges and their weaknesses
None. The god proposed none and the table closed in round 1. The default size, no ruler. Nothing about the people is priced for the god's Held Place.

## Their magic
Fitted to the world, as for every people: about one in a few hundred has the grip, and most of those do not know it. A working is attention paid; it does small things (warmth from cold, a bleed stopped, a thing found, weather or a beast read); it takes from the worker something small and specific that she does not choose and notices only afterward. Among the Hodd: perhaps fifteen with the grip. An old woman of the upper terrace is said to know when a slope will go; she has twice been right, and she no longer knows her dead husband's face.

## What they believe about gods
A custom and no creed: something old and quiet in the stone that does not like to be thanked. No name, no image, no story that it is a child. Some say it is only a custom.

## Hierarchy
None under the god, and no ruler. Each household speaks for itself. Matters between households go to a gathering of heads of households at the gate in the dry cold. The count-keepers say who is alive, who is owed and who owes, and rule nothing.

## How the god meets them
Already there. The god means to come down unseen from the heights in deep winter, be still and listen, and be found if at all as a child at the gate in the snow.

## Their history, their neighbors, and a trouble their god did not ask for
They have been at the mountain's foot about seven hundred years and say the wall was there when they came, which is nearly true: its lowest courses are older work. The squared openings in the cliff and the blocked doorway high on the slope are not theirs; they trade by night, without meeting, with the people of the heights, at a flat stone above the terraces, the Night Table [f68], and have done so for as long as they have counted. Their trouble: above the upper terrace a slope has a crack across it that was not there three winters ago, and the gathering cannot agree whether to move forty households. A second trouble: the count-keepers' rolls show that in each of the last five winters one or two people who went up toward the high hollows after strayed goats came back slow and mild and unable to say what they saw. The rolls list them as living.

## What the god hoped, and what the people hold
Held: the walled seat, the gathering, the count-keepers, the custom of the lowest hearth stone, the guest room in every winter-house. Not held: a household that knows where a child sleeps in winter. No one knows of any child.

## What they know of the land
Finished from the traced map (step 3a.6), in the section at the foot of this record.

## Named mortals
- Garran Holt, eldest of the count-keepers, about sixty, slow, exact, trusted with the count and nothing else.
- Mother Ansa of the upper terrace, the old woman with the grip, about seventy-five.

## The land, from the traced map

Written on creation Night 3a after the tracing, from `map.json`, never from the plan.

Home: the Underfell [r06], B2, the high trough between the Deep Scarrow [r04] and the back of the rim hills, and in it Undercrag [f13], the walled seat, with the Withy [f12] running down the trough. The squared openings in the cliff are the Squared Doors [f14]. The blocked doorway high on the slope is the Lintel Door [f15]. The high hollows where the strayed goats go are toward the Hush [f16]. The flat stone above the terraces where the silent trade is done is the Night Table [f68]. Their god comes down onto the slope above the seat.

### What they know of the land

What a people who stay know: their valley well, the heights by avoidance. Distances are straight lines measured on the traced map. Days are on foot along that line, each stretch at the pace table's rate for the region it crosses (rough ground 14 miles a day for valley, hill, plain, grass and shore; forest 10; mountain 8; marsh 6), with the kinds of ground named in order of their share; a line that follows a used road is given at the road rate and says so. Grades: seen (they have been there), reported (told by one who has), rumored (a name and a rough place).

### The god's hope and the ground

The god hoped for mountains, a high valley with one way in, and places to be under, and has them. The paint set the range's foot against the back of the rim hills, so the valley is a trough between the two, open to the south. Changed in this record: nothing but the valley's shape.
