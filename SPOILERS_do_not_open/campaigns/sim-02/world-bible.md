## The rules made final

- God-sense: face to face only; mutual, immediate and sure; it tells only that the other is a god. Nothing at a distance. It is never a tally. One rule for the whole world.
- Bodies: one.
- Mortal magic: level 4 of 10 under a thin rain. About one person in a few hundred has the grip, most unknowing; workings are small; each takes from the worker something small and specific, unchosen, noticed afterward. One rule for the whole world; no place makes it read differently.
- How gods stand out: 5 of 10. Plainly more than mortal in what they endure and perceive; mistakable for weather, luck or a strange old person; numbers of mortals remain a threat.
- The wild: 2 of 10. Familiar beasts, and three nameless kinds, each rare: the slow grazers of the High Scarrow [r03], the thing in the deep channel of the Reedsea [r27], the thing on the bottom of the Drowned Shelf [r35].
- Climate 5; the land past the borders 6; the age of the human story 9; non-human peoples 2; travelers from afar 3.
- Scarcities: salt, and light for the dark months.
- Technology: before the medieval era; bronze; no steel forges.
- The gods came down on one night in late autumn; each god comes to itself in its own season within the year after.

## The land

One continent on one sheet, about 2,560 miles across, traced on the painted sheet into `map.json` (34 regions, 66 features) and described region by region in `world/gazetteer.md`. A range down the west; dales under it; a pale dead basin at the middle ringed by worn hills, with the beast at its center; grass in the north-east and east; a long river from the dales round the basin to a southern delta, with an arm to an eastern estuary; a great wood in the south; a looping river in the south-west; sea on the west, north and south; the east edge in fog. No underground sheet. The Pale lies beyond the sheet.

## Where each Night 1 candidate went

- Feel: dusk, lamplight, wet stone, an old indifferent world with something patient under it: the whole land, and the Sleeper [f27]. The hollow under a hill where weather does not reach: the Underfell [r06]. The crowded warm harbor: Saltstairs [f41].
- Land wishes: each god's country, as the keeper log's 3a.5 entry measures.
- Magic's minority understandings, kept as local beliefs: that the lender writes it down (the Tavrek); that the sky is the creditor (the Ushkai); that the cost is a secret spoken (the Brannow); that it costs years at life's end (the Hodd). Each is wrong about something that matters.
- Fears: winter and hunger (row C18 and the random table); the slow nameless sickness (S02; the plainness, L02); the quiet debt (S03, S06, S07, S10); doors that should stay shut (L04; the Haldren's stories); a thing that waits (the Sleeper [f27]); a thing that comes up out of the river (S11); the slope letting go (row C16); fog and ship-borne sickness (the random table, by ship and road, never by fog).
- Scarcities: salt (the Corrant's pans, the Salt Road [f23], the Pellim pans); light (lamp oil from the Corrant; thin everywhere).
- Buried things: sealed doors (the five rooms); old roads that end at water or rock (the eight Orsand roads); bones of something enormous (the Rib [f29]); a drowned town under a made lake (Sellnot [f46]); a sealed door beside a spring (the Springdoor [f47]); wrecks and sealed holds where the sea drew back (the Dry Hull [f42]; the looted room under the Tide Stumps [f43]); a passage with no exit (the stair under Gullwick [f21]); a deep place with something in it no one wants to find (the Hush [f16]); gates with no walls (the Bare Gate [f19]); the blue-grey glass (under the North Ashring [r12] and on the Bare Floor [r14]).
- Non-human peoples: the quiet counting folk of the high places (the pass folk); the wary people of the water's edge (the reed folk). Left out: a tall pale singing folk of cold coasts and plain-faced watchers who tell only the truth, as separate peoples; the scale allowed two. The rim people, who are human, carry the watchers' plain truthfulness.
- Travelers' gifts: one strange tool hard to use (the Far Woman's rule); something carried that they could not name (the Dun Walkers' disc); arriving unable to say how (the walker of row C20); locks and the means to open them, and cargo no one can price, were not given to a traveler: the locks are Orsand's and the cargo is in the rooms.
- Mortals worth following: a keeper of what was said (each people's own: the Tavrek keeper, the Hodd count-keepers, the Ushkai counter, the Pellim keeper of weights); one at a crossing who decides who passes (the Loweth ford keepers, the Senn toll-reeve, the Haldren gate-warden); a trader with a late conscience (the Pellim keeper of weights; Joss Tarn); no chosen ones.
- Discoveries: what the gods are and what came before (L01, L03, the deep history); a door no one built (the sea-cut arch of the Tern Shore [r18], an ordinary wonder); the first spring every river begins from (the Twin Spring [f30], an ordinary wonder from which two rivers run); a sea with a floor (the Drowned Shelf [r35]); a place where the sky is lower (the Bare Floor [r14], under the star); companions (Ikk; Sisk; the girl at Goatsteps [f28]; the rest rise from play).
- Undared wishes: see the keeper log's 3b.5 entry for which were placed, where, and how far from each wisher's seat.
- Left out for failing the star or the lore: carriers and thinners, which the star's roll removed; nothing was invented in their place.
