# Ruling ledger: campaign sim-02

Every ruling the Keeper or the DM makes, with its arguments. Every agent checks this file before ruling on anything already ruled on (DESIGN 12.7). Ids are `L-NNNN`, in the order entered. Each entry: real date, campaign year, god, power or matter, the arguments on each side, the ruling, what it binds. The player sees a ruling only on its card and in play.

## Creation table, Day 2 (the player's god, Vesper), entered Night 2

All ten were made at the Day 2 table by the DM and agreed by the player ("Yes. The card is right, and the night can fall."). Entered 2026-11-19 Pacific, Night 2, step 2.3. None was settled against the player; each is a table ruling recorded for precedent. The id is written back beside each on `P/player/power-card.md`.

### L-0001
Date 2026-11-19, Year 0 (creation). Vesper, Form: the god's own fire.
Arguments: the player offered the lantern as "only a lantern" that "holds fire without fuel and burns until I put it out." The DM: a lamp that burns on nothing would make the god a free light for a whole people, against the shape of a scarcity the player wished for; the fire is a piece of the god and belongs under Form with a limit. The player did not object.
Ruling: the god's flame burns without fuel only in the lantern, and only while the lantern is in the god's hand, within its hood-light, or set down in a room (Hearth-light). Passed to a wick or set farther off, it is ordinary fire from then on: the god's by the chain, but fueled like anyone's and out when the fuel is. The god can give a hearth its flame; it cannot give it fuel.
Binds: Form; every power that runs on the god's flame; Hearth-light's one-room widening.

### L-0002
Date 2026-11-19, Year 0. Vesper, Form: when the body is broken (absence).
Arguments: the player chose absence ("I'm absent, which is closer to what a door does than to what a body does") and asked the return to be priced. The DM priced it: the hearth returned through is spent, because a door a god came through broken does not stay a door; lanternless, no kindling and no stepping; the lantern in a stranger's hand is the exposure, not softened at the player's request.
Ruling: as written on the card under Form, five lines: hood-flame out, coat empty, lantern left; return at the next local dusk through a flame of its own, without the lantern, the hearth spent and out of the chain; until the lantern is in hand no kindling and no Door, the Window still works; if no flame of its own burns, the god stays gone until someone lights the lantern from any fire, which makes that fire the god's again, and the god returns through the lantern at the next local dusk in front of whoever holds it, spending nothing; whoever holds the lantern holds the source of every flame of the god's.
Binds: Form; the Door (the single lanternless return); Definitions (a flame of mine).

### L-0003
Date 2026-11-19, Year 0. Vesper, the Window: the sign.
Arguments: the player asked that the pale-flame sign be "subtle, not invisible": a mortal must be watching that fire and must learn what the sign means, or it is a free counter. The DM: the sign is present every time or it is not a drawback; it is seen only while the god looks and only by eyes on that fire; its meaning is known to no one on the day of landing and must be learned; countermeasures come from the world.
Ruling: the sign is present every time, only while the god is looking, and only to someone whose eyes are on that fire at that moment. What it means is known to no one in the world on the day the god lands. The DM promised no one would be prevented from learning it.
Binds: the Window. Precedent: a drawback's tell is never removed, but its meaning is the world's to learn.

### L-0004
Date 2026-11-19, Year 0. Vesper, the Window: a hand over the flame.
Arguments: raised by the DM as the natural countermeasure once the sign is read. The player accepted.
Ruling: a hand over the flame shuts the window while the hand is there.
Binds: the Window.

### L-0005
Date 2026-11-19, Year 0. Vesper, the Door: local dusk.
Arguments: the player: dusk must be local dusk at the exit flame, or the Door cannot be used across half the world; the player accepted that it means picking the exit by the sun. The DM: granted on fairness to the player's intent ("arrive at any fire without crossing the distance").
Ruling: local dusk means dusk at the exit flame, not at the one left. One step a day: the hour comes once and the god goes through once.
Binds: the Door; Definitions (local dusk).

### L-0006
Date 2026-11-19, Year 0. Vesper, the Door: carrying.
Arguments: the DM wrote the floor edge case; the player accepted without objection.
Ruling: the god carries what is in its hands. Nothing living comes through; a child in the god's arms stays on the near side.
Binds: the Door.

### L-0007
Date 2026-11-19, Year 0. Vesper, the Door: the lantern as a flame of the god's.
Arguments: the player asked whether the lantern counts, since it changes what the god does first. The DM: yes for going in, since it is the god's fire; no for coming out, since the lantern comes with the god; as a window it shows only what the god already sees.
Ruling: the god may step into its own lantern flame from wherever it stands. It cannot come out of the lantern. The god has nowhere to arrive until a first hearth is its own and kept by someone.
Binds: the Door; the Window (the lantern as a window shows what the god already sees).

### L-0008
Date 2026-11-19, Year 0. Vesper, the Door: without the lantern.
Arguments: follows from L-0002 and L-0007; the DM wrote it, the player accepted.
Ruling: without the lantern in hand the god cannot step at all, except the single return from absence.
Binds: the Door; Form (absence).

### L-0009
Date 2026-11-19, Year 0. Vesper, Hearth-light: where the drawback applies.
Arguments: the player asked the reading be agreed, "not as a change": the drawback holds wherever the god stands while the lantern is down and lit in a room, "the price of giving the room my light." The DM: that is what was meant.
Ruling: while the lantern is set down lighting a room and the god is not holding it, wherever the god stands, its hood-flame burns low and past its own few paces it sees in the dark as a mortal does.
Binds: Hearth-light.

### L-0010
Date 2026-11-19, Year 0. Vesper, Weaknesses taken: the vow, its limits and edge cases.
Arguments: the player took the vow as a weakness binding the body and asked for Hearth-light in return. The DM priced it: the vow is large and the gift real but bounded; wrote the vow in full with threshold, asked over, withdrawal, and the hearth as an invitation already given, and how it can be used against the god.
Ruling: the body does not pass an unasked threshold by any means, walked, carried, pushed or dragged; a threshold is the way into a kept place and a place no one keeps has none; a withdrawn invitation shuts it and the god leaves by the nearest door; a hearth of the god's burning inside, kept by someone, is an invitation already given. A house or town that learns what the god is can keep it out forever. Bought Hearth-light.
Binds: Weaknesses taken; Definitions (threshold, asked over); Hearth-light (the trade recorded both ways).

## Keeper reviews at creation, Night 2

Not rulings against anyone; recorded so the review is on the record.

- The Window against the mystery standard (powers.md section 1; the DM's request in `power-lean.md`). The Window shows what a flame of the god's lights and what is said within that light. It shows effects and never why; it cannot show the star, the sky or anything the flame does not light; it hears mortals, who under rule 1 of the standard are never wholly right about a cause; a being that answers questions carries its own limit under rule 16 wherever it speaks. No central measurement of this world's epic can be read from firelight. No limit added. Logged in the keeper log, 2.3.
- The absence return through a relit lantern (the DM's second question). Weighed under 2.7: a mortal act that brings the god back in front of the one who did it is an exposure, not a strength, and the player asked for it not to be softened. No question to the DM.

## Creation, Night 2: the NPC gods

Entries L-0011 onward are added as each negotiation settles a request against a god in whole or in part, or binds more than one card.

All entries below: date 2026-11-19 Pacific, Year 0 (creation), Night 2, steps 2.3 and 2.4. Each was put to the god in round 1 with the Keeper's reasons, answered by the god in its round 1 reply, and settled in the round 2 final text. The god's confirmation, or its objection, is noted under each entry when it arrives. Arguments in full are in `S/releases/creation/<label>-night2-negotiation.md` (the Keeper's side) and `S/gods/<label>/negotiation-night2.md` (the god's).

### L-0011
All seven NPC cards and the creation table: how a price is set.
Arguments: several gods offered a weakness or drawback that was already part of their form or lean (a fog scattered by wind, an honest god that cannot lie, a water-god moved only by water) and several asked two improvements for one weakness. The Keeper: a drawback or weakness that restates the god's form or voice costs nothing and is priced again (powers.md section 6); a weakness is priced by what it costs the god that the god does not already pay; a weakness of middling size buys one improvement of that size, a large one may buy two.
Ruling: as argued. Applied alike to every god whatever its bargaining stance.
Binds: every NPC card; every later trade at Night 3; the DM's Day 3 pricing is not bound by it but may be read against it.

### L-0012
Cards that pay in memory (god-thresholds, god-rivers, god-sea; and the fixed removals on god-storms and god-stone): how a memory-loss drawback is enacted.
Arguments: round 1 wrote "one item from the record of gods or the goals, the Keeper's choice." god-thresholds: its record of gods is one entry long and its goals are what runs it; take an entry or a line, never a file; the record first, the goals only when the record is bare; never the last goal. god-sea: goals are a larger coin than a guess about a rival and it would not offer them. god-rivers accepted the Keeper's choice and said it would rather not lose its goals. The Keeper: the price is real only if it takes a piece and not the whole; the table prices every god alike.
Ruling: one item is one entry of the record of other gods or one line of the goals. The Keeper takes from the record of gods while it has an entry to give, and from the goals only when it does not. The last line of the goals is never taken. The god does not choose and is not told. Rate: per closure opened (thresholds), per use, a use being one unbroken standing in the water (rivers), per night held (sea). god-storms loses the most recent item of its goals if its cord is lost or burned before its return; god-stone loses every item added to its goals and record in the season before a break.
Binds: the five cards named; any later memory-loss drawback on any card.

### L-0013
god-beasts, the Turning of the Herd, and its trade.
Arguments: the god asked that its weakness (cannot say a false thing) buy both once-a-moon use and no floor on the slowing. The Keeper: the Turning touches hunger, a standing fear; once a moon solves winter for a small people; the weakness costs the god only the bluff and the kind lie; it buys one. The god accepted, chose the slowing with no floor, and gave its own reason for keeping once a season (it wants its people to have to decide who eats). It offered a floor of two days; the Keeper declined it as unneeded.
Ruling: once in a season; slowing exactly the days turned. The Tally of Ground counts beasts and never mortals. Settled against the god in part (one improvement, not two).
Binds: god-beasts' card.

### L-0014
god-thresholds, Form, the wind, the Veil and the return.
Arguments: the form said it "cannot be struck or cut in any way that matters." The Keeper: a god can be wounded (DESIGN 7.8); a body nothing reaches does a power's work, so the card must say what breaks it; under a body only scattering can break, the wind weakness restates form and buys nothing. The god chose the body that blows and fire can hurt, so the wind is a true weakness and buys the Veil. The hand-wide gap is form and free. The return by the next dusk was priced: the door returned through can never again be latched, locked or barred (the god's choice over a turn of the moon).
Ruling: as on the card. Settled against the god in part (the form's immunity; a price on the return).
Binds: god-thresholds' card. Precedent for any form that claims it cannot be harmed.

### L-0015
god-rivers, its trade, the toll's wording, and the return.
Arguments: the god asked that its weakness buy both the Long Water's branches and the Gate at a second, unseen crossing. The Keeper: each is large; the weakness buys one. The god chose the branches. In its reply it asked that a keeper be only a person present and aware, that a true thing may be small and its own to judge, and that a ford with no gate or bridge not be a crossing under the weakness. The Keeper: the god's first wording named fords and claimed crossings; narrowing it so far would free a river god at most crossings of the river the weakness bought. The return "the next time a living person touches the stream" was priced as a wait; the god chose a turn of the moon.
Ruling: a keeper is a person present and aware who stands at, guards, tends or claims the crossing; a gate or bar shut in a keeper's name is kept; a ford with a keeper is a kept crossing; open water waded or swum where no crossing is kept is not bound. A true thing may be small, is the god's to choose, and must be of its own knowing and not learnable by the keeper looking around. Settled against the god in part.
Binds: god-rivers' card.

### L-0016
god-secrets, the Unmarked Hand and the Kept Word.
Arguments: the god asked that the forgetting hold after three meetings; the Keeper read its first wording as already holding and offered instead a knowing by voice after three meetings, bought by the weakness. The god countered: recognition only, not describable, not a mark; and at two meetings, because it will be met seldom. The Keeper: recognition only follows from the power's own first line and is granted, with the bound that a knower may say "this is the one" while the god stands before them; two meetings refused, since the weakness is middling, has now bought more than round 1 offered, and a knowing at the second meeting starts before the god has chosen to be known. The Kept Word guards against mortal magic only, not ordinary means; the teller keeps the lightness and the memory of having told.
Ruling: as on the card. Settled against the god in part (three meetings; pointing in presence).
Binds: god-secrets' card.

### L-0017
god-storms, its trade.
Arguments: the god asked that its weakness (the open sky) buy both Foreknowing's wider reach and the Tall Tree's hold against searchers. The Keeper: the weakness's size turns on whether a tent is a built shelter; both if it is, one if not; and the Tall Tree's stronger hold would not be written without a deeper cost to the god's own people. The god answered against its own interest that a tent is not a built shelter, took one improvement (Foreknowing's reach, at a day more of ache), and declined the Tall Tree's stronger form because of its cost.
Ruling: a built shelter is a roof on walls fixed to the ground; a tent, windbreak, lean-to or cave mouth open to the sky is not. One improvement bought. Settled against the god in part, by its own account.
Binds: god-storms' card.

### L-0018
god-sea, Slack Water's coin, the Bound Word's size, and pace in water.
Arguments: the god chose the whole coast in sight at one item a night, bought by the Bound Word. It contested the coin's source (no goals; or, in the first year, an item of its own choosing) and the weakness's size (it binds at the moment of fear, a second edge that does not depend on its choosing), and asked for a swimmer's pace in water at twice a walker's, with a lesser fallback. The Keeper: goals granted in part under L-0012; a coin of the god's choosing refused, since its own drawback says the coin is not agreed to and a taste cannot be removed from a record; the weakness's size accepted as larger than middling; twice a walker's pace refused, since a mortal swimmer is slower than a walker and the world gives each god its form's pace; half again a walker's pace in the first ten fathoms granted, a walker's below. "Only water moves me" restates form and bought nothing; the god withdrew it.
Ruling: as on the card. While the Bottom Walk's cold lasts only a false statement is barred. Settled against the god in part.
Binds: god-sea's card.

### L-0019
god-stone, the Held Place's lasting, and the Winter Stillness.
Arguments: the god asked that a place held a full season stay steadied for a year after. The Keeper: the power acting away from its drawback must be bounded in number or in time; one place for a year, or every place through the following winter. The god chose the winter, argued that its own body bounds the number (a full season's unbroken hold, one body), and the Keeper accepted that in place of a count. The god chose the longer stillness (through the frozen ground), declined a second improvement, and asked to be awake in the winter of its landing; granted.
Ruling: the lasting runs through the winter that follows a full season's unbroken hold, with no count of places. The stillness binds from the first winter after landing. The return's "longer" is a second winter, and a third if the stone is broken. Settled against the god in part (a winter, not a year). The weakness is recorded as worth somewhat more than it bought, by the god's own choice.
Binds: god-stone's card.

## Night 3a (2026-11-20): rulings settled at the Day 3 table, and the Keeper's review

### L-0020
The player's god, the first night (a people's edge priced on the god, Day 3).
Arguments: the player asked that no Haldren sheltered at a hearth of the god's in winter could die of cold or hunger. The DM: as asked it undoes L-0001 (the god gives a hearth its flame and cannot give it fuel; "I don't ask to feed hearths") and reaches every hearth the god ever kindles; two smaller shapes offered (the first night; the long room). The player declined the long room (its price would cost "windows I couldn't choose to lose") and countered on the first night that its first draft "punishes me for ordinary hospitality"; the DM accepted the counter on fairness: the hearth shuts only when a life was truly at stake that night.
Ruling: as on the card's dated Day 3 entry, status main. Keeper's review, since it works across a people. It touches neither world scarcity (the hearth burns on its own fuel and the power gives none). It rules no mind. It does nothing through things made by more than craft. Across a whole people in a year it carries at most one life per hearth per winter and shuts that hearth to the god until the thaw, so it shrinks the god's reach as it is used; it does not feed, heal or warm, and at dawn the person is in mortal hands. No limit added. One reading fixed for every later year: "first hard frost" and "thaw" are called by the Keeper for the place where the hearth stands, from the year's weather as resolved, and written in that year's record before any hearth is ruled on. Whether a life "was truly at stake" is the Keeper's finding from the resolved night, never the god's guess; the god feels only that the hearth closed, and which.
Binds: the player's card. Settled against the god in part (the shape asked was refused; a smaller one agreed).

### L-0021
The player's god, the vow at a town's gate (Day 3).
Arguments: the vow (L-0010) holds at "a walled town at its gate" and asked over means a word from one who lives in the place or keeps its door. The player asked that at its people's town one keeper's word answer, "so that the vow has a name to answer to": a gate-warden chosen by the old houses for a year. The DM added, and the player agreed, that with no warden the moot's word through its speaker stands in, so the gate is never kept by no one.
Ruling: as on the card's dated Day 3 entry. It narrows L-0010 at that one gate, at the player's own asking, to the god's cost. The world's side: the town has a low wall and one gate (placed on Night 3a), and the office of gate-warden exists among its people, so the reading applies from the first morning. At any other walled place the Day 2 definition stands unnarrowed until play settles whose word answers there.
Binds: the player's card.
