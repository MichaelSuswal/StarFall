# Placement draft: campaign sim-02, creation Night 2

Provisional. Written at step 2.8 from each god's answer 2, the Night 1 terrain list and the world draft. The placement plan on Night 3a is written from this draft and must honor the player's Day 3 brief; the paintings then decide, and the data follows the paint. Working names here are plain descriptions and get map ids when traced. No distance or direction in any release comes from this file.

## The region

One continent on one sheet, provisionally about 2,600 miles across and 2,600 from top to bottom (a square sheet at about two and a half miles to the pixel), wholly inside the star's living radius; the sheet's edges stay in fog. A long folded mountain range runs down the western third, highest in its middle. Hill country falls from it eastward into wide river lowlands. A grass steppe fills the north-east and east, breaking into ridges where it meets the hills. The south is a long drowned coast with flats, an estuary and a shallow shelf. Marsh lies in the low ground of the two largest rivers. No desert, no ice.

## The center

The star's center lies near the middle of the sheet, in a wide shallow basin where the eastern hills, the river lowlands and the steppe's edge meet. The center beast lies there, a sleeping beast that is visibly a beast, swollen. The surface painting must show its ground. No seat is placed in the basin. The history this ground will carry: the oldest and most spent country on the sheet, ruins under ruins thickest around the basin's rim, the oldest roads all bending toward it and stopping short.

## The eight landing countries

Spaced evenly in rough terms, as a loose ring around the center, each many weeks' walk from the next, none in the basin.

- The player's god: the north-west. Upland river valleys, each shut in by its own ridges, rising westward through hill country to the high folded mountains; forest on the slopes; marsh where the valley river reaches low ground far downstream; the coast very far and seldom spoken of. It wakes in late autumn. History this land will carry: valleys settled, emptied and resettled several times, each with its own speech; an old paved way under the turf running up-valley; the high passes above held by no one the valley people will name.
- god-stone: the west, in the heart of the high range. High valleys with one way in, cut rooms and deep places under the mountain. It wakes in deep winter. History: the oldest worked stone on the sheet; rooms closed from outside; a people at the mountain's foot who build well and do not leave.
- god-thresholds: the north, where the range drops to hills and a river runs through a gap between two countries. Narrow passes, a crossing, border ground where no one is quite in charge. It wakes in autumn. History: a gate with no walls on an old road; tolls taken by three different peoples in three different ages.
- god-beasts: the north-east. Steppe and the broken hills at its northern edge, rivers to be crossed and not settled. It wakes in autumn. History: game trails become roads become game trails; a rib of something enormous standing from a hillside; herders who have never built in stone.
- god-storms: the east. The same steppe at its far side, breaking into long ridges under an open sky. It wakes in late spring. History: half-swallowed roads no one built for the people now walking them; wintering grounds fought over in dry years.
- god-rivers: the south-center. A long river from cold springs in the eastern hills to a slow mouth, marsh at the bottom, fords, ferries and a walled town on it. It wakes in spring. History: a river with rooted, tired kingdoms on its lower course and unwalked wild at its sources; roads that end at the water with the far half missing; a made lake with something under it.
- god-sea: the south-east coast. Drowned coast, tidal flats, an estuary and a shelf, and a walled city with its feet in the tide. It wakes in autumn. History: the lowest ruins under the waterline; wrecks on the shelf and on land the sea left; old kingdoms across the water in the telling of the port.
- god-secrets: the south-west. River country below the range's southern end, a walled town on a bend, fog in the mornings. It wakes in autumn. History: a town older than its own records, built over rooms no one has opened; old roads that lead nowhere anyone remembers going.

Four gods asked for river country and receive four different rivers. Two asked for steppe and receive its two sides. The player's far coast and god-sea's coast are the same sea at two very different distances.

## Between the seats

Three to five godless peoples, to be set on Night 3a from the Q4 scale at 6, holding a little over half the usable land: old kingdoms on the lower rivers and behind the coast, nearer to god-rivers and god-sea as they asked, far from most others; real wild country at the sources, on the heights and across the steppe's middle. The two non-human peoples are unplaced: the quiet folk of the high places in the range, between and above the western seats; the wary people of the water's edge in the reedbeds and shallows of the south.

The warm stone that fell with the gods lies in godless country between two seats. Which two is decided on Night 3a when the plan is written, by a roll among the adjacent pairs, so that the choice is not the Keeper's.

## Kept from the wishes, and not

Every god lands in the kind of country its answer 2 named. The player's wish to land in "winter country" at "a house with a fire that's about to go out" is a wish: the land is upland and the season late autumn, with winter coming; who is there and what fire burns is the world's and is set on Night 3. No desert and no ice anywhere on the sheet.
