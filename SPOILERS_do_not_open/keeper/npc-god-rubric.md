# NPC God Creation Rubric (draft 3)

> SPOILER WARNING: Keeper material. Players should not read this file.

All rolls are 1 to 100 unless stated. The agent for the god sees its rolls, then answers the world survey and creation questions from inside the character. Nothing from the archetype table is ever named in play.

## 1. The ten leans
Each is a lean between two ends. 1 = fully the first, 100 = fully the second.
1. Mind: intelligent (reasons it through) / clever (finds the trick)
2. Body: strong (overwhelming force) / enduring (outlasts anything)
3. Presence: revealed (wants to be seen) / hidden (works unseen)
4. Temper: patient (plays for centuries) / impulsive (acts now)
5. Regard for mortals: shepherd (serves its people) / sovereign (its people serve it)
6. Order: law (builds rules) / freedom (breaks or ignores them)
7. Making: builder (creates and keeps) / breaker (unmakes and clears)
8. Company: solitary / gathering. Roll +25 toward gathering, capped at 100.
9. Appetite: content / hungry
10. Trust: open / wary

On the sheet each lean is printed with its number, both ends named, and one plain sentence that says which way it leans, so that no god reads its scale from the wrong end (runbook template 7.1). Company's sentence is read from its number with the +25.

| Lean | The sentence |
|---|---|
| 1 to 15 | Leans hard toward <the first end>. |
| 16 to 35 | Leans toward <the first end>. |
| 36 to 45 | Leans a little toward <the first end>. |
| 46 to 55 | Sits between <the first end> and <the second end>. |
| 56 to 65 | Leans a little toward <the second end>. |
| 66 to 85 | Leans toward <the second end>. |
| 86 to 100 | Leans hard toward <the second end>. |

## 3. Domain (roll d20; reroll duplicates within a campaign)
Also reroll a domain that matches what the player has said its own god is about, in its Day 1 answers or in any remark kept with them (runbook 1.3), so that the player's god stays somewhat its own. The reroll is logged with its reason and the roll it replaces, and no one is told.

A match is the same domain as the player's god's, or a domain the player named as its god's center. A domain that only neighbors the theme (the player's manner rather than its matter, a thing it hopes to discover, a season it names) is not a match. This is the narrowest reading that keeps the player's god its own and the table whole; the Keeper fixes which entries match, from the player's words, in the log before the first domain roll.

1 fire, 2 harvest, 3 war, 4 secrets, 5 roads and borders, 6 the dead, 7 the sea, 8 storms and sky, 9 stone and mountains, 10 craft and making, 11 beasts and the hunt, 12 healing and rot, 13 law and oaths, 14 trade and counting, 15 song and memory, 16 dreams and night, 17 rivers and springs, 18 ice and winter, 19 thresholds and doors, 20 fate and chance

## 4. Form (roll d12)
1 human-seeming, uncannily perfect; 2 human-seeming, visibly wrong in one way (too tall, no shadow, wrong joints); 3 a great beast; 4 a small beast; 5 a shape of weather or element; 6 a thing of many parts (a swarm, a flock, a court of voices); 7 a giant; 8 a child; 9 an old one; 10 a half-thing (half beast, half person); 11 shifting between two forms; 12 something with no fixed face that others see differently

## 5. Virtue and flaw that pull against each other (roll each, d10)
Virtue: 1 mercy, 2 patience, 3 generosity, 4 courage, 5 honesty, 6 loyalty, 7 curiosity, 8 restraint, 9 justice, 10 humor
Flaw: 1 pride, 2 wrath, 3 envy, 4 greed, 5 fear, 6 despair, 7 lust for being worshipped, 8 cruelty from indifference, 9 obsession, 10 cowardice dressed as caution

## 6. The wound (roll d12, from the foggy past; the god feels it but cannot explain it)
1 it was betrayed by one it made; 2 it failed to save a people once before; 3 it was once worshipped and then forgotten; 4 it killed a sibling; 5 it was bound for an age; 6 it lost its name; 7 it ate something it should not have; 8 it was the servant of a greater thing and fled; 9 it loved a mortal who died; 10 it broke an oath and the world broke with it; 11 it was born of a death; 12 it remembers a sky that fell

## 7. Voice (roll d10)
1 speaks in questions; 2 speaks in very few words; 3 speaks in proverbs and old sayings; 4 courtly, formal, elaborate; 5 blunt as a hammer; 6 warm and mothering, with iron under it; 7 laughs at the wrong moments; 8 never says a thing directly, always sideways; 9 speaks of itself in the third person or plural; 10 speaks like a merchant, everything a bargain

## 8. Traits
After seeing all rolls, the god's agent names two traits for itself (what it will be known for). The Keeper assigns one more, known to the god from arrival.

The Keeper trait is a new fact, one the god's rolls and its own two traits did not supply, that presses on a contradiction already on its sheet. It adds nothing that resolves the contradiction and nothing that invents a second one; it makes the existing pull harder to live with or harder to ignore. Written in one line.

## 9. Rules
- No two gods in one campaign share an archetype seed or a domain.
- The agent must reconcile contradictions among its rolls rather than ignoring them; a contradiction is where the character lives.
- Nothing from the archetype table is ever named or quoted in play.
- The rubric forbids nothing else. Two gods may share a form, a virtue, a wound, a voice, or a name they choose for themselves.

Principle from run 01 (Night 2, step 2.2): when gods working apart arrive at the same choice the rubric does not forbid, such as the same name, the choice stands and neither agent is told. The Keeper accounts for it in the sealed files, and every record tells the two apart by label or byname. Nothing is changed that would tell one god something about another.

## 10. Labels
- Each NPC god's folder, and its name in every Keeper file until it names itself, is `god-<domain word>`: the first noun of its domain, lowercase, articles dropped. Stone and mountains is `god-stone`; roads and borders, `god-roads`; the dead, `god-dead`; beasts and the hunt, `god-beasts`.
- Domains never repeat within a campaign, and no two domains on the table share a first noun, so labels never collide.
- No number appears in any label, file name or sheet, and no roll id appears on a sheet: ids run in rolling order and would let a god count the others. The order in which the gods were rolled, and every roll id, live in the keeper log only. A god reading its own folder can learn its domain and nothing about how many others there are.

## 11. Bargaining stance
How hard a god argues over the price of its powers (Night 2) and its people's edges (Night 3). Worked out from the leans when the sheet is written, and written on the sheet in words, as part of the god's character. The Keeper prices the same way whatever the stance; the stance shapes how the god answers.

Stance score: the mean of Appetite, Trust and Regard for mortals, rounded.

Rounding, for every score in this rubric: to the nearest whole number; a mean that falls exactly on a half (from two or four leans) rounds up, toward 100. A mean of three whole numbers never falls on a half. The rounded score is written on the sheet with the leans it came from.

| Score | Stance | On the sheet |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 35 | yielding | It takes a fair price as offered and argues only where a price cuts against what it is. |
| 36 to 65 | measured | It argues for what it cares about most, gives way on the rest, and wants reasons for any price it thinks high. |
| 66 to 100 | hard | It contests every price it thinks too high, offers its own drawbacks in place of the Keeper's, and accepts a counter only for a reason it respects. |

Leans that make a god argue harder: Appetite toward hungry (it wants more); Trust toward wary (it suspects the price); Regard toward sovereign (it expects to be served, not priced). Leans that make it yield: content, open, shepherd.

Then, whatever the score, these shape how it argues (each from 1 to 35 or 66 to 100 on that lean; between, no mark):

- Mind. Intelligent: argues from reasons and consistency with what was already agreed. Clever: hunts for wordings, loopholes and the case nobody priced.
- Body. Strong: bargains for force and reach. Enduring: bargains for duration and for what survives.
- Temper. Patient: willing to spend all three rounds to get a thing right. Impulsive: decides fast and may accept to be done.
- Order. Law: wants every limit written exactly. Freedom: resists limits and would rather take a heavier drawback than a written fence.
- Presence. Revealed: wants powers others can see work. Hidden: prefers powers no one can trace to it.

Every god gives a reason for each thing it asks and for each price it accepts. Accepting everything is a choice open to any stance, never the default the release suggests.

## 12. The people it leans toward
Worked out from the leans when the sheet is written, and written on the sheet in words, so that seven gods do not come out alike. It is the god's own lean, as a lean is: the god builds within it, and may hope otherwise if it gives its reasons in `people-hoped.md`.

Every god comes down to lead a people, to build, rule and spread (the premise of The Between). The leans below decide the form that takes, never whether. Every NPC god starts with a people and with helpers who run things in its absence (the `## Helpers` section of the people record); the rule lean decides the form they take. The god may refuse them, dismiss them or never meet them in play, always for a reason it gives, logged; once dismissed they are not forced back.

The helpers serve the god from landing only if its meeting says its people already know it. A god that comes to a people who do not yet know it finds the offices there and filled; each becomes its helper as it hears the god, in play, and until then runs on the people's own custom, as the player's designed helpers do before they know their god. The world listens and does not obey.

### Size
Size score: the mean of Company, Appetite and Regard for mortals, rounded. (Company carries its +25.)

| Score | The people it leans toward |
|---|---|
| 1 to 25 | a few hundred: a household, a band, a single steading's worth |
| 26 to 45 | small: one to three thousand |
| 46 to 65 | the usual number, about five thousand |
| 66 to 80 | large: seven to fifteen thousand, with a town of size |
| 81 to 100 | very large: fifteen to thirty thousand, with a city |

A people larger than the default is not a priced edge. Its costs are practical and come from the world (more food and fuel, slower growth, harder to move, a longer gestation for a larger kind of people), argued case by case and open to argument, and the Keeper applies them in play (runbook 3a.1). More than a few large peoples in one campaign is possible.

### Settlement
One result, from Making and Company together. Find the row by Making and the column by Company (with its +25); the cell is the result, and the two leans never give two results that contradict each other.

| Making \ Company | 1 to 35, solitary | 36 to 65 | 66 to 100, gathering |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 to 35, builder | a walled seat with steadings scattered round it | a walled seat or town | a walled town, gathered close |
| 36 to 65 | scattered steadings | the default: a town | gathered close in one place |
| 66 to 100, breaker | scattered and moving, no fixed seat | moving, no fixed seat | a close band on the move, no fixed seat |

### Rule lean
How the god's people are ruled, and who holds rule under the god. Rule score: the mean of (101 minus Order), Regard for mortals and Appetite, rounded. Law, sovereign and hungry push toward a ruling god; freedom, shepherd and content push toward a loose one.

| Score | Rule lean | On the sheet |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 12 | a lone god that refuses its helpers | It starts with helpers like every god (a council's or elders' offices, by default), and its rolls lean it to refuse them: it gives its reason in `people-hoped.md`, the refusal is logged with the date, and its people then go their own way. Rare; it comes only on an extreme roll. |
| 13 to 39 | a council of elders or chieftains | The people is ruled by a council of its own (elders, headmen, chieftains); the god is heard there, and the council's helpers carry out what is agreed. |
| 40 to 54 | a sacred monarch behind a court | A mortal line holds the throne in the god's name and is held sacred for it; a court stands between the throne and the people, and the god is reached through it. |
| 55 to 69 | a mortal ruler under the god | One ruler answers to the god and rules for it: a priest-king, a warlord or a merchant-prince, by the variety table below (those three only). |
| 70 to 100 | a god-king with a court and priesthood | The god holds the crown itself, with a court and officers; its word is law, carried by them. A priesthood stands in it only as the worship lean gives one. Its manner is set by the variety table below (all five). |

Odds, for the Keeper's reference: about 1 in 130 for the lone god that refuses its helpers, a quarter for a council, a third for a sacred monarch, a quarter for a mortal ruler, one in seven or eight for a god-king.

**Variety table.** For each kind, take the lean value given; the highest wins; a tie is settled by one roll at even odds, logged.

| Kind | Value | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| tyrant | Regard for mortals (toward sovereign) | rules by fear and its own will; the people serve it |
| priest-king | 101 minus Presence (toward revealed) | rules through rite and its own visible presence |
| warlord | 101 minus Body (toward strong) | rules by force, through its war band and captains |
| merchant-prince | Mind (toward clever) | rules by bargain, debt, toll and trade |
| sacred monarch | Presence (toward hidden) | the god is the monarch, seldom seen; its court speaks for it |

In the 55 to 69 band the kind names the mortal ruler (priest-king, warlord, merchant-prince only); in the 70 to 100 band it names the god's own manner as king (all five).

### Worship lean
How the god is worshipped, if at all. Worship score: the mean of (101 minus Presence), Regard for mortals and Appetite, rounded. Revealed, sovereign and hungry push toward a state cult; hidden, shepherd and content push toward none.

| Score | Worship lean | On the sheet |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 39 | none | It asks no rites; what the people do about it is theirs. |
| 40 to 57 | local shrines | Household and village shrines, kept by whoever keeps them; no order of priests. |
| 58 to 71 | a priesthood | Keepers of rite with standing among the people, offerings brought, no tithe by law. |
| 72 to 100 | a state cult | Temples, tithes and a priesthood under the throne. |

Odds, for the Keeper's reference: about a quarter none, two in five local shrines, a quarter a priesthood, one in nine a state cult. Shrines or none is the common result. The worship lean alone decides whether a god has priests: a god-king's court (rule lean) carries a priesthood only if its worship lean gives one.

Calibration, for the rubric's spread: the gods real players ran at length in the original game all had a governing apparatus (a throne with council and advisors, a crown with noble houses, a record-keeping state, a state of schools and roads, a state of trade and expeditions, a war god), none ruled by hiding or by nothing, and none put priests in view. What they wanted was knowledge, trade, justice for offences and security against a shared threat; their wars began from an offence against kin, an ultimatum between courts, a shared threat or a suspicious death. So rule is common and "no one" rare, worship is often light or absent, and a god's wants are not only land and worship (runbook template 7.5, `goals.md`).

### On the sheet
Write the result as four short lines in the sheet's `## The people it leans toward` section, for example:
"Size: large, seven to fifteen thousand, with a town of size. Settlement: a walled town, gathered close. Rule: a god-king with a court and priesthood, a merchant-prince in manner. Worship: a priesthood."
or
"Size: a few hundred. Settlement: a close band on the move, no fixed seat. Rule: a council of chieftains. Worship: local shrines."

Each god builds within its result. It may hope for something else, and then it says why, in its own voice, in `people-hoped.md`; the Keeper reads the reasons against the sheet as on Night 2 (a hope that leaves the lean with no reason given breaks character and is sent back once). The Night 2 and Night 3 releases carry the rule lean and the worship lean in one line each and ask the god to build within them; the Night 3 release carries the size lean too, in one line beside them, with the usual number (about five thousand) given as the middle of the range and never as what is expected (runbook template 7.7).

Principle from run 02 (Night 1, step 1.3): where two leans on one sheet seem to give two results that cannot both hold, the rubric's table gives one result and the contradiction is the god's to live in (section 9); the Keeper does not pick one lean over the other by judgment.

## 13. Test overrides

A test run may force lean results to test the engine. The rubric never changes for it.

- The override lives only at `SPOILERS_do_not_open/campaigns/<name>/state/test-overrides.md`, created by Michael before Night 1, since it names NPC god results. The Keeper never looks for one on a public path. A campaign without that file has no override. Night 1 step 1.1 makes the rest of the sealed folder around it.
- The file names each forced result: which lean (size, settlement, rule, worship), which gods (all NPC gods, or by label once labels exist), the result or family of results forced, how a variety within it is chosen (by this rubric's tables, from the god's rolls), and the date Michael approved it.
- The Keeper rolls every lean as usual and works out the rubric's own result first. Then it applies the override and logs both, in the keeper log, at step 1.3: the rubric's result with its score, the forced result, and the override file's line. The sheet carries the forced result in the same words as any result, with no mark that it was forced; the god builds within it like any lean. The forced line carries no score on any god's sheet, forced or not, so the absence marks no one; the scores stay in the keeper log (runbook template 7.1). A forced rule lean is written with its name and sheet words as section 12 gives them, the words on priests included: priests come only from the worship lean.
- An override may force one NPC god only, to a lean that invites refusal (the lone god that refuses its helpers, a very large people, a people on the move), so that refusal and size are priced for an NPC as for the player. Since labels do not exist when the file is made, it names that god as the first god rolled; the roll order stays in the keeper log only.
- Only what the file names is forced. Every other lean, table and rule stands, and nothing else bends to make the forced result fit.
- The override is a fact of the test run, kept in the keeper log. It is never written into this rubric, the runbook or any engine file, and it ends with the run.

Example, for a run that forces every NPC god's rule lean to a god-king:
```
# Test overrides: <campaign>
Approved by Michael: <date>
Rule lean, every NPC god: a god-king with a court; its manner by the variety table (all five kinds), from its own rolls.
```

## 14. Luck

Every NPC god has a luck modifier, as the player's god does, earned the same way: by taking heavier prices than it needs, or by refusing powers. The Keeper sets it on Night 2 (runbook 2.4) and again on Night 3a for edges (3a.1), keeps it in the sealed `S/state/luck.md`, and uses it in the god's rolls and in the weight of the world's choices that fall on it (runbook standing rules; `SPOILERS_do_not_open/keeper/sealed-factors.md`, with the 5 to 95 cap and, in a contest between two gods, only the difference in their luck counting). It is never on the sheet, never in any release, and never told to the god's agent; the god's bargaining stance is the same with it or without it, and no release suggests that a heavier price earns anything. A god that volunteers a heavier price than its power needs has it written as asked.
