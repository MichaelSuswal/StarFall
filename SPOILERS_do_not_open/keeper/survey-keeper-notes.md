# Keeper Notes: Turning the World Survey into a World

How the Keeper uses the Day 1 survey answers (and the Day 2 and 3 follow-ups) to build the world on the three creation nights. Read with DESIGN.md sections 3, 4 and 7, the creation runbook, the mystery standard, and the deep lore. The deep lore always wins: no survey answer can change what the stars are, where the gods came from, or anything else sealed there. The deep lore does not fix god-sense or bodies; those are the campaign's to set here.

## Governing rules

1. **Equal weight.** The player and each of the seven NPC gods are one voice in eight. The player's answers carry no extra pull.
2. **One world, one set of rules.** Every answer is blended into a single world. Special places may exist, and may be strange, and one rule may be spread unevenly (magic rare overall and common in a few valleys), but no place makes a rule read differently. A wish can become a place; it can never become a local exception to the rules.
3. **The world listens; it does not obey.** No single wish is granted whole. The Keeper never tells any god which of its answers were used, and nothing any god is told reads like a vote result.
4. **Three kinds of answer, three ways of blending:**
   - **Scales** (how much, how old, how strange): place each answer on the question's 1 to 10 scale, take the median, settle a half, then one variation roll (see the arithmetic below). The median keeps one extreme god from dragging the world.
   - **Pools** (what is scarce, what is feared, what is buried, what peoples exist): gather every answer into one pool. The most common themes become widespread features of the world. Rarer answers may become special places, single beings, or local histories, never rules.
   - **Rules** (god-sense, bodies): one rule for the whole world, by the counting rules below. Record the vote counts in the keeper log; never reveal them.
5. **The star and the deep lore come first.** The star is rolled on Night 1 before the blend. Survey answers shape the world within what this star and the deep lore allow. Where an answer cannot fit, it is quietly left out and the leaving out is logged.
6. **Rejected and bent premises.** A rejected premise refuses the question's assumption; a bent one answers outside the range offered. A marker with extra words ("Premise partly bent") is read as the nearer of the two, logged. Every marker is read by the answer's words: an unmarked bend is a bend, and a marker the words do not bear out is read as what the words do (runbook 1.4). A marker counts only at the start of an answer; one placed elsewhere is no marker. An answer to a question that offers "or something else" is inside the range and is never bent. Record the god's own answer as given, count it in the blend where it maps to the scale, pool or vote, and treat it as a gap only when the blend cannot place it.
7. **Everything is fixed by the lock.** Nothing derived from the survey is added after the world locks.
8. **Remarks are not answers.** What the player said beside the survey, kept by the DM under `Also said` in `survey-day1.md`, is not blended and not acted on, with one use: it counts, with the answers themselves, toward the player's stated theme, and an NPC domain that matches that theme is rerolled on Night 1 (runbook 1.3).
9. **Log every choice.** For each question the keeper log records the eight answers in summary, the Keeper's placement of each, the blend, any rolls, and what was built.

## Given and discovered

The player is never told which wishes the world kept. What is innate to a god is given before Day 2: the Keeper writes it to `P/player/world-rules.md` for the DM, in the same words the NPC gods receive in their Night 2 releases, as plain facts of being a god here. That file carries three things and nothing else: god-sense as set, the bodies rule as set, and the pricing terms for mortal magic (it is simple, and it costs the worker). The god's own power limits are settled at the Day 2 table and live on its card. Everything else the survey sets is learned from the world, in play, through what the god and its people meet.

## The arithmetic of scales

1. **Placement first.** The Keeper places each answer on the question's scale (the ends are in the table below) and logs every placement before any roll for that question. No placement is moved after a roll is seen. A god that gives a number beside its words is placed at its number. An answer that speaks to two matters on one scale (how many, and how far apart) is placed on the matter the scale measures, by the ends in the table; the other matter is logged and may color a place, never the placement.
2. **Skipped answers.** A god that skips a scale question has no place on it. The median is taken over those who answered. A skipped answer is out of the pool for that question. In a rule vote a skip casts no vote.
3. **The median.** With an odd count, the middle placement. With an even count, the mean of the two middle placements.
4. **The half-median rule.** A median that falls on a half (4.5) is settled by one roll at even odds before the variation roll: success rounds up, failure rounds down. A median on a quarter (from a placement at a midpoint) is no half: it rounds to the nearer whole, and only a half is rolled.
   **Even odds**, here and wherever these notes roll at even odds, is one `--die d100`: 51 to 100 succeeds, 1 to 50 fails. In a tied vote, success goes to the side the question names first, written in the log before the roll.
5. **Variation.** One roll: 1 to 33 lowers by one, 34 to 67 leaves it, 68 to 100 raises by one.
6. **Clamp** the result to 1 to 10. Q6 then clamps again to the star's magic range.
7. **Follow-ups before rolls.** A range answer that names two far-apart points is a gap; ask its follow-up before any roll on that question. The follow-up answer replaces the one it follows.

| 5 | Age of the human story | people arrived within living memory | ruins under ruins over thousands of years |
| 6 | Mortal magic | a rumor most people doubt | a wise-woman in every valley |

| 9 | Strangeness of the wild | familiar animals and a few legends | creatures no one has a name for |

Answers placed between the ends by their words: "a little", "some", "a few" sit at 2 to 4; "some of each" or "neither" at 5 to 6, and at the midpoint, 5.5, when the words tilt neither way; "much", "most", "many" at 7 to 9. The Keeper's reading is logged in the answer's own words beside its number.

## Counting rule votes

1. **Two-way votes.** Majority of those who voted. A tie goes to a roll at even odds.
2. **Bent answers.** An answer that bends a two-way vote counts with the side it is nearer to, by the Keeper's reading, logged with the reason. An answer that fits neither side at all and cannot be placed is a gap and gets a follow-up. An answer that keeps one side's words and adds an extra (one body, and something that goes where the body cannot) counts by its words, with the side whose words it keeps; the extra is not a sub-point of the rule, and goes to that god's Day 2 as a power it may propose and have priced, like any other.
3. **Three-way or wider votes.** Count every option. An option with more than half the votes carries. Otherwise the two largest go forward: each vote for any other option counts with whichever of the two it is nearer to, by the Keeper's reading, logged; then majority, and a tie by even roll.
4. **Sub-points.** How the winning rule works in detail (how near, how sure, what a second body costs) is the Keeper's judgment from the answers on the winning side, logged with which answers it drew on. A losing answer may color nothing.
5. **No votes.** If no answer speaks to a rule at all, its default holds, logged as a default and not as a vote. Bodies: one body. God-sense: nothing beyond what DESIGN 1.5 gives every god (it knows others came down when it did), plus what its own senses tell it.

## When two star results pull against each other

The star's results are rolled on Night 1 (runbook 1.2) and every one stands as rolled: none is rerolled, dropped or softened because another seems to pull against it. When two read against each other in wording, each governs its own part of the matter, and where both speak to the same part, the one lower in this order yields to the higher, on that part only, keeping its full force on everything else:

1. **The phase** governs time: when things happen, how often, how strongly, and how near the end is.
2. **The bodies** (center body, long-game presence, Remainder) govern what exists: which beings and places there are.
3. **The measures** (rain density, living radius, the ceiling) govern how much and how far.
4. **The content** (star content, ascended temper) governs what things are like: their matter, their voice, what they want.

So a falling phase that makes the ascended loud, and a temper that has them speak in weather and want nothing, together give voices that come often and strong (the phase) and are weather and old images with few offers (the temper). Write the reading in the keeper log, with the two results and which part each governs, before anything built on it is released. The deep lore itself outranks all four.

Principle from run 02 (Night 1, step 1.2): a contradiction between two rolled results is read, not resolved by choosing one; each keeps the part of the world that is its own.

## How the "stands out" blend shades the ceiling roll

The ceiling is 70 plus a d10, rolled on Night 1 after the Q8 blend. Q8 shades the d10 so:

| Q8 result | The d10 |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | roll two (two rolls, each named), keep the lower, then lower it by one (never below 1) |
| 3 to 4 | roll two, keep the lower |
| 5 to 6 | roll one |
| 7 to 8 | roll two, keep the higher |
| 9 to 10 | roll two, keep the higher, then raise it by one (never above 10) |

Write the method in the keeper log before the first die. Two dice are two rolls, each named for itself in `--what` ("ceiling, first d10", "ceiling, second d10"). The ceiling always lies between 71 and 80.

Principle from run 01 (Night 3, step 3.4, shading): when one result is said to "feed" or "shade" a roll and no file says how, the Keeper writes the method in the log before the die is cast, and never after.

## Question by question

**1. The world's feel (mood or image).** Pool. Distill the eight moods into the world's tone: weather tendencies, light, the color of the land, the temper of its peoples. Widely shared moods set the world; a lone mood may color one region or one place. Feeds the map sheets' palette within the house style. Discovered.

**2. The land that calls.** Placement, not a rule. Each god lands in country that suits its own answer (DESIGN 7.5). The answers also tell the Keeper what terrain the world must contain. Lay the land so each god's country shifts realistically into its neighbors' over distance.

**3. Seasons, and the season each year opens on.** Scale for the climate (table above), applied consistently and varying by geography as real climate does: latitude, altitude, distance from sea. The season each god wakes into is personal: the last sentence of answer 3, written to the god's sheet by the Keeper. It sets that god's return sequence. The climate is discovered; the waking season is the god's own.

**4. What lies past the borders.** Scale. Sets how much land is held by godless peoples and how much is open, by area as well as by count of regions. Balance per DESIGN 4.9. Discovered.

**5. How old the human story is.** Scale. Sets the depth of the deep history: how many risen-and-fallen cities, how many layers of ruin, how far back the oldest stories go. Discovered.

**6. How much magic mortals have.** Scale, then bounded by the star (table above). Never 0, never everywhere. Discovered.

**7. What magic feels like and costs.** Pool. Only "it costs the worker" is given, as a pricing term; the rest is discovered. The most shared feel and cost become how magic works for mortals everywhere, consistent with what magic is in the deep lore. A god's people share this magic, and the god shares it in its own way. Minority answers may survive as a local tradition's understanding of magic (often wrong, per the mystery standard) but never as a second magic system. Discovered.

**8. How much a god stands out.** Scale. Shades the ceiling roll (table above) and sets how mortals react to gods at first sight. Discovered.

**9. How strange the wild is.** Scale. Sets the bestiary's strangeness across the whole world. Strangeness may be concentrated in particular places (high country, deep water, old ground) for realistic reasons, but the rules governing creatures are the same everywhere. Discovered.

**10. What mortals fear.** Pool. The most shared dangers become the world's standing threats and feed the event schedule's clocks and triggers. A lone answer may become one being, one place or one local threat. Every danger obeys the mystery standard: a body, a cause, a cost if ignored. Discovered.

**11. What is scarce.** Pool. The most common answer, or two, becomes the world's real scarcity, which drives trade, war and migration everywhere. Other answers may be local shortages from geography. Scarcity is a pressure, not a punishment; every god's people must face it somewhere. A scarcity of something no one can hold or trade (quiet, trust, time) has no build path as a thing in the land; it becomes a temper of the peoples, written into their records as how they live, never a rule. Discovered.

**12. What lies buried.** Pool. The most shared become widespread features of the deep history (common ruins, old roads). Rarer ones become single sites, placed at creation with map ids, findable from the moment they exist, and possibly never found. The raw material of the deep history and the short mysteries. Whether the world has an underground layer at all is decided from this pool, the deep lore and the star on Night 1 (runbook 1.5, its own box), never from whether a sheet is approved; a yes is written into `map.json` on Night 3a before any painting. Discovered.

**13. Non-human peoples.** Pool and scale. If most gods want none, the world is human with at most one rare exception. If several want them, include as many kinds as the answers support, each with a real backstory. A god may still lead a non-human people of its own regardless of the blend (Day 3). An answer that asks not to be told what they are is honored. Discovered.

**14. Travelers from afar.** Scale plus a pool of what they brought. Travelers come from under other stars, consistent with the deep lore, each from an entry in the sealed map's plane section. What they brought becomes the edge they hold over local peoples, and their history in the land. Discovered.

**15. How gods recognize each other.** Rule, by vote. Becomes the campaign's god-sense (DESIGN 7.2). Options are usually three (a pull felt at any distance, a knowing only face to face, something else); count them by the wider-vote rule. Sub-points (how near, how sure, whether it can be held in) by the sub-point rule. Whatever the vote sets, god-sense is never a tally: a god feels direction, nearness or weight, never a count (DESIGN 7.2). Michael's own preference when he plays counts as one vote, like any other. Given before Day 2.

Principle from run 01 (Night 1, Q15): a vote on a world rule stands whole. Where a sealed file seems to fix the same matter, the vote governs and the sealed file is read as yielding to it. Anything the lore keeps that touches the rule may live in the world only as a thing to be found (a being, an object, a power to be grown), never as a place where the rule itself reads differently, and never as a sense a god has without asking.

**16. Bodies.** Rule, by vote: "Can a god be in more than one place at once? One body, or several, and if several, what should it cost?" Count one body against more than one; a bent answer counts with the nearer side. If more than one carries, the number allowed and the cost are sub-points from the answers on that side, and every god pays the same cost. If no answer speaks to it, one body, logged as a default. Given before Day 2.

There is no rule on oaths. A promise between gods is worth what the god who gave it is worth; nothing in the world enforces it or punishes breaking it, and reputation applies.

**17. The mortals worth following.** Pool. Feeds the kinds of mortals the world's peoples tend to produce and the Keeper's small cast of world-level figures. Does not script anyone. The best characters still come from play. Discovered.

**18. What a god could discover.** Pool. Feeds the layered mysteries (one epic, several long, many short) and the discoverable powers. Every answer that fits the deep lore becomes a candidate truth, placed at creation, findable, never held back. Discovered.

**19. The undared wish.** Pool, handled with care. Each wish the world can hold becomes a special place, a being, a hidden thing, or a thread in the deep history, never a change to the rules. Many will be kept and never found. A god's own undared wish is never placed where it is certain to stumble on it. Discovered.

## Follow-ups (Days 2 and 3)

A follow-up is asked only where the blend cannot place an answer, or a rejected or bent premise leaves a gap the world needs filled. Few, woven into the day's conversation, (oog) when needed. An answer that asks not to be told something is honored and never becomes a follow-up. NPC gods receive theirs the same night. A follow-up answer replaces the answer it follows, and is blended by the same rules above. Send an answer back once at most.

## Day 2 and Day 3 inputs

Day 2 (the gods) and Day 3 (the peoples) are not blended. Each god's own powers, backstory, people and land are its own. The Keeper fits them into the one world, reconciles them with the deep history, and keeps total god power roughly but not exactly balanced.
