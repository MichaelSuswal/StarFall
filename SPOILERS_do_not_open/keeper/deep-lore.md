# Deep lore (v2)

Keeper material. Settled truth unless a section says it is rolled per star. Nothing here is ever stated to a player, who meets the world only through effects, objects, behavior, and the consequences of acting on a theory.

Each truth has three layers: what peoples believe, what investigation reveals, what is true. The gap between the second and third is the game. Each also names its bodies, its cost if ignored with the clock or trigger that runs it, and the several readings the evidence honestly supports. Those readings are not decoys. The world answers only when the god acts on one.

## The one mechanism under everything

The plane is infinite and has no edge, but it keeps accounts.

Everything that is a particular thing rather than a general thing is particular because it holds a measure of something. This river with these bends. This bird with this song. This woman with this name. The Keeper calls that something hold. Mortals have no word for it, though they have a hundred words for its absence. Hold is spent by being specific. A stone spends almost none. A river spends a little, keeping its bends against each flood. A living thing spends more. A thing that remembers and names spends most of all.

Spent hold does not vanish. It rises. It leaves the ground as the thinnest exhalation and gathers in the thin above, where it condenses, and condensed hold is blue. The blue of deep ice. The blue in a cave of old bone. The blue at the heart of a knotter's working when she borrows too hard. The blue of the stars.

That is the whole engine. Every truth below is this engine seen from another side.

## Truth 1. What the stars are

**What peoples believe.** They are stars, though not like the stars in the oldest songs, which were many and small and white. They are the eyes of a sleeping thing. They are lamps hung by the first gods. They are pure mana falling slowly over thousands of years, and life sprang from the last one that fell. They are eggs. They are where the dead go. Each people names its own star and knows roughly that others exist, because travelers have seen a second light low on the horizon after a hundred days of empty country.

**What investigation reveals.** The star never moves against the land. A plumb line at one fixed place points straight at it. It rains. Not water: on still moonless nights a fine falling glimmer can be seen under it by anyone who lies on their back long enough, thickest at the center and gone some ninety days' walk out. Everything particular is richer under the center: more kinds of beetle, more dialects in a day's ride, more springs that taste of something. The empty country past the rain is not empty of ground. It is empty of kinds. Travelers who cross it come back unable to say which village they are from. Two sightlines fixed in stone a generation apart show it lower.

**What is true.** A star is condensed hold: the gathered particularity of a vast country that spent itself. Not souls. Patterns, compressed without order: the grammar of a species, the habit of a river, the law of a city, the way a people said a word. It hangs above the place where the plane is exhaling hardest, because that is where it formed and is still fed. It is not sealed. It leaks. The slow rain of hold from it is why life gathers under a star and thins toward the edges, and why beyond its reach lies country the Keeper calls the Pale, where things are still things but no longer particular things. Nearby stars are similar because they drew from the same kind of country. Distant ones differ because the country differed.

**Bodies.** The star. The center point under it, a strange place in every world. The Pale. The oldest sightline stone.

**Cost if ignored.** None directly. Ignoring the stars costs only the ability to understand the other clocks when they run out.

**Readings the evidence supports.** A reservoir that feeds the land (partly true). A predator draining the land, since the oldest ruins lie under it (false, but the correlation is real: old lands spend fastest). A being watching the center (the ascended make this partly true). A seed that will hatch (near enough to the fall that acting on it is not wasted). A god can sit at 50 between reservoir and predator for most of a campaign.

## Truth 2. What a starfall does

**What peoples believe.** Two stories, usually both told in the same village. In one, the star fell and life sprang from the crater and the first people climbed out of the blue mud. In the other, the star fell and the first world ended, and the people before us are the bones under the hills. Some say they are the same night.

**What investigation reveals.** The star is lower than it was. A god who sets two marks and waits can measure the rate, and it is slow and not steady: it is quickening. Under old ruins near the center lie older ruins of a different kind, and under those a layer of blue-grey glass that rings when struck. Beasts near the center grow restless on nights the star dims. Something at the center is eating.

**What is true.** A star hangs while the country beneath it exhales more hold than the star rains down. When the country is nearly spent, its oceans gone to salt plain, its species to a few general beasts, its cities to mounds, the balance tips and the star sinks. Centuries at first, then decades, then one night. At impact the star unpacks. Everything it carried returns as particularity poured into spent ground: new rivers that cut hard courses in a season, beasts of forms no one has seen, minerals, weathers, and in time new peoples, because hold is what life needs and there is suddenly more of it in one place than anywhere on the plane. The old story is right. Life springs from the fall.

But the fall lands on what grew beneath it, because life clusters under the rain. Everything particular there is crushed into the general and overwritten by the star's contents, which are not the shapes that rose. The second story is also right. A fall renews the plane and ends a world. Destroy or renew is answered: both, always, and who it was good for depends on where they stood and what they had stored.

Stored particularity survives a fall. A thing dense enough in its own hold, a name sung yearly by a thousand mouths, a breed kept pure for forty generations, a god anchored to a mountain, comes through as itself and wakes in the new country. This is the whole secret of the long game, and no mortal knows it in those terms.

**Bodies.** The blue-grey glass under the deepest ruins. The center point. The star's measurable descent. The oldest Longwalker song, a list of names and nothing else.

**Cost if ignored.** The fall itself, in late worlds. In the test world the fall is centuries off, but the descent becomes measurable within the campaign, and a god who never measures it will be surprised by everything that already knows.

**Readings the evidence supports.** The fall destroys and the center thing is a devourer waiting for it. The fall renews and the center thing is a gardener preparing for it. The fall is the star hatching. The fall is ten thousand years off and the lowering is a trick of the eye. All four fit until the god stores something and sees whether it is treated differently from what is not stored.

## Truth 3. The fading

**What peoples believe.** The lake is drying because the rains have failed. The sea left because a god offended it. The old kingdom in the north fell to war. Apples do not taste like they did, which is what grandmothers always say.

**What investigation reveals.** The lake is not drying. It is the same breadth on the map and shallower everywhere at once, and the water has lost its cold and its weed and its particular fish, and is becoming water in general. The salt plain where the sea was has no shells. The sea did not go away; it stopped being a sea. Dialects at the edges have flattened to one. Cattle at the edges have lost their markings. People who go far enough out come back plain: they can speak, and cannot say what their mother called them. The oldest and most storied places fade first. The empty north with its mounds was the heart of the region once.

**What is true.** Fading is overdraw: a place that has spent more hold than the star's rain replaces. It is patchy, not a tide from the edge. It strikes first where the accounts are deepest in the red: the oldest cities, the most-magicked ground, and the far edges where the rain is thinnest. It is the exhale that feeds the star. Every particular thing lost below is gained above, which is why the star brightens as the land dims. The fading is the star seen from underneath. A late region fades faster every decade, because particularity is what resists fading and there is less of it each year.

**Bodies.** The shallowing lake. The salt plain with no shells. The returned traveler who cannot say her name. The mounds in the north.

**Cost if ignored.** A clock. Each decade the overdrawn places lose a visible kind: a fish, a strain of grain, a dialect, a color of cattle. By the second half of a campaign the god's own valley loses kinds unless the god has slowed the spending (fewer great workings, more things kept particular). Ignored entirely, the valley's second river goes general around Year 40 of the test world: slower, wider, no particular depth, a marsh.

**Readings the evidence supports.** The land is sick and the star is the sickness. The land is dying of age and the star is unrelated. A thing in the north or at the center is drinking the land. Magic is draining the land (true as a part). The gods' arrival began it (false, but the god arrived late and will be blamed).

## Truth 4. What magic is

**What peoples believe.** Some are born with a gift, from the star, or the blood, or a bargain. The old woman who ties knots can bring heat from cold and stop a bleed. She is strange and she forgets things. Magic is thin here and thicker in the south, where the sea left and the people had to bargain hard.

**What investigation reveals.** No working creates anything. A knotter who warms a hearth has made something nearby colder, or duller, or less itself. Great workings leave ground where nothing particular grows again. The oldest knotters cannot say their names. Magic is strongest at the center and nothing in the Pale. The south, where magic is strongest, is where the sea stopped being a sea. There is a blue flicker at the heart of a hard working.

**What is true.** Mortal magic is the moving of hold from one thing to another by hand. The mage borrows particularity from whatever is nearest and lends it elsewhere. Nothing is made. The cost falls on the nearest most-particular thing, and the most particular thing any mage has is herself: her name, her face, the way she says a word, which of her children she loved best. Magic is fading done fast and on purpose, and every tradition of magic is, without knowing it, a tradition of spending the region into the star. It exists everywhere under a star because hold rains everywhere under a star. It is never ubiquitous because most people cannot find the grip, and those who can lose themselves using it.

God power is not this. A god does not borrow. A god is dense hold, and acts by spending itself and by being lent hold from every mind that keeps its shape. A god can watch a knotter work and realize that what she does with a cord, the god does by wanting. That realization is one way a god discovers a new power.

**Bodies.** The knotter who has lost her name. The dead patch where a great working was done. The southern tradition and its bargaining songs. The blue flicker.

**Cost if ignored.** A trigger. If a god permits a school of magic past a threshold (in the test world, more than a dozen knotters under patronage, or any single working that moves a river or breaks a siege), the valley gains a dead patch and its accounts tip into overdraw, pulling the Truth 3 clock forward a decade. The school also produces the hollow: people who worked too hard and are now general. Not dangerous. A cost.

**Readings the evidence supports.** A gift from the star with a price (true in effect). A theft from the land. A theft from the mage. What the star eats, and mages feed it (true in a way no mortal would phrase it). Simply dangerous, and old people simply forget.

## Truth 5. Where the gods came from and why

**What peoples believe.** The gods came down from the star. The gods were always here and have woken. The gods are the old kings returned. The gods are from beyond the empty country.

**What investigation reveals.** The gods arrived in the same night, and that night a stone fell in the hills between two of them, warm, blue-grey, heavy for its size. People who sleep near it dream of a crowd kneeling and of a river none of them has seen. The gods' own memories are of being followed, of being wanted, of a wound they cannot place. Every god's power grows when more mortals hold it in mind and shrinks when it is forgotten. What the gods sense of each other is set for each campaign (DESIGN 7.2); the lore fixes no sense between gods, and investigation finds whatever that campaign's god-sense gives and no more. None remembers being a child, or anything but a god.

**What is true.** Gods are what a far starfall throws up. When a star lands somewhere across the plane, the impact drives the densest packets of hold clear off the ground, and some are thrown so far that they pass through the Between and come down under another star. These packets are not people. They are the most-held patterns of the country that died: a people's long memory of being led, a river's habit of being followed by roads, a dynasty's shape, a law that outlived its city. They land as gods. That is why they arrive expecting a people to lead: they are made of having been followed. That is why their pasts are foggy: they carry no single person's memory, only a people's memory of them. The wound each god feels is real and belonged to no one in particular. Several gods arrive at once because one fall throws them together, the remainder of a single world.

The Between is the long arc above the plane. Gods cannot die because hold does not vanish; a scattered god regathers over years and wakes with a longer fog. God power is innate because a god is a source. It grows with worship because every mind that keeps the god's shape lends it hold, which is why a forgotten god diminishes and why a god's name matters more to it than it can explain.

Some throws do not compress into gods. They land as dross: warm blue-grey stone that holds the same memories at lower density and dreams them at anyone nearby. The Keeper calls this the Remainder. It is the god's own origin in a form that can be touched.

**Bodies.** The Remainder. The gods' shared arrival night. The dream of the kneeling crowd and the unseen river.

**Cost if ignored.** A trigger. The Remainder lies in godless country between two gods. If neither reaches it, the people around it begin to worship it, it lends them hold in return, and by Year 20 of the test world a small priesthood exists that is no god's and that chants the god's own wound. If a rival god reaches it first, the rival learns the shape of the other gods' pasts, which is a lever.

**Readings the evidence supports.** The gods came from the star (false, but the stone is star-colored and warm). The stone is a god that failed to wake (near enough to be useful). The stone is a piece of where the gods came from (true). The gods are the risen dead of the old kingdom in the north (false, but the dreams of being followed fit). Discoverable in one campaign but never fully: a god can learn that it is a remainder and never learn whose.

## Truth 6. The ascended

**What peoples believe.** Some gods become greater gods. There are gods in the star. The star is a god. On certain nights the star dims and a thread of light comes down to the center: the star drinking, or feeding its child, or speaking.

**What investigation reveals.** The thread is real and always lands on the center. Dreams near the center are of tall figures with no faces, counting. A god grown very strong begins to feel light. Its shadow pales. Its temple stones go soft at the edges. Prayers return as echoes. Mortals remember the god and forget its face. Stronger still, and one night it is not on the ground anymore.

**What is true.** A god that grows past the ceiling (Truth 8) lightens and rises into the star, as all dense hold does when the star's pull exceeds the ground's. Those who have risen are inside it. Not dead, not gods anymore. They are the most coherent part of the star's content, and because they were patterns of leading, they are what gives the star a mind, insofar as it has one. They are not one being and they do not agree. Some want the fall soon, because hanging is long and they will unpack first as the first shapes of the new country. Some want company and call gods upward. Some are so old they have become the star's weather and want nothing.

They can reach down. The thread on dim nights is one faction feeding the center being, its agent. They can speak into dreams near the center and into the mind of any god near the ceiling. They can teach: a god who listens learns powers no god below has imagined, which is one of the three ways powers are discovered. They cannot lie as mortals lie, but they have no perspective but their own, and their own is that the fall is good.

**Bodies.** The thread. The dreams at the center. The lightening of a strong god. The star itself.

**Cost if ignored.** A trigger. When any god passes 80 percent of the ceiling, the ascended begin speaking to it in dreams. The offer is always a power, the power always costs hold, and taking it brings the god nearer the ceiling. A god who ignores the offers loses nothing. A god who takes them without understanding rises within a decade.

**Readings the evidence supports.** The star is a single god that eats gods. The star is heaven and rising is reward. The star is a prison. The thread feeds the center thing, or drinks from it. The voices are the old kings of the north. All five fit until the god rises or refuses, and sees what the voices do.

## Truth 7. The long-game creatures

**What peoples believe.** There are old things. Something lives in the still water at the center and the people there do not fish it. There are herds in the empty country all one color, and no one has seen one born. There are wanderers who come once in a generation, sing lists all night, and want nothing but to be told the names of things.

**What investigation reveals.** The center being is the lake. Not in the lake. The water does not freeze, does not move in wind, and is warm at the bottom. The thread lands on it. The herds walk toward the center across a cycle of years and turn back before the rain's edge; they have no scent, leave no dung, and the one that was killed had no particular organs inside, only general meat. The wanderers' songs are lists of names, some of places that no longer exist and some of places that do not exist yet. They ask the names of the god's villages and sing them back exactly. They have been seen under other stars.

**What is true.** Three strategies have evolved for surviving falls, and each has at least one body in every world.

The hoarders eat hold directly and store it. The Still, the lake being at the center of the test world, is one: an organism the size of a lake, older than the last fall, grown under the thickest rain and fed from the star's thread by one ascended faction. It wants the fall. It will be directly under the impact, dense enough to survive and swollen enough to eat a great share of what unpacks. It is patient beyond any god. It encourages fading in its reach, because fading hastens the fall: the godless people on its shore have no names for each other, and it arranged this over four hundred years by being the thing they look at.

The carriers hold particularity in portable form and walk it across the Pale. The Longwalkers are a people, few and human-seeming, who have survived at least two falls by singing names. A name sung by enough mouths for enough years is dense enough to cross faded country and survive an impact, and the Longwalkers carry names from dying regions to living ones and plant them. They are why some things come back. Not kind, not cruel. They will carry the god's valley's names if asked and taught, and will carry them unasked if they decide the valley is worth it.

The thinners shed particularity ahead of the fall and ride it out as nearly general things. The pale herds are this: beasts evolved to hold almost no hold, that cannot fade because they have already faded, and that will be the first grazers in the new country because the fall has nothing in them to crush. Not intelligent. The plane's own hedge.

**Bodies.** The Still. The Longwalkers and their songs. The pale herds. The shore people with no names.

**Cost if ignored.** The Still's clock: it grows while the land fades, and at a size (Year 30 in the test world) it begins drawing people into the water in sleep, first the nameless, then the god's own edge villages. The Longwalkers' trigger: they visit once in the first decade and once in the fourth; a god who does not engage them cannot send anything through. The herds have no clock.

**Readings the evidence supports.** The lake is a god, or a wound in the world, or the star's child. The wanderers are spies, or the dead. The herds are cursed, or holy, or merely strange. A god will not know these are one question seen three ways until it has acted on at least two of them.

## Truth 8. The ceiling and beyond

**What peoples believe.** Gods grow with worship. The greatest gods are in the star. Our god will become one of them if we are faithful.

**What investigation reveals.** The god's own sense of weight. Past a point, more worship brings less ground. A god who notices finds its shadow paled, that it can be looked at longer, that it is harder to be angry. A rival grown fast may be seen to lift a hand's breadth off the ground one night and set down again, shaken. The ascended begin speaking.

**What is true.** Under any star there is a threshold of density past which the star's pull exceeds the ground's, and a thing that holds more than that lightens and rises. For gods this is the ceiling. It is rolled per world on a scale where a newly arrived god sits near 20 and a god ruling 50,000 who have held it in mind for a century sits near 70. The roll is 70 plus d10, so 71 to 80. The test world's ceiling is 76. Nothing states the number. The god learns it by approaching it.

Past the ceiling there are four things, none stated by anyone.

The god can rise. Ascension is permanent, and the campaign continues from inside the star, reaching down only in dreams and threads, waiting for the fall. Some players will want this. It is not a loss. It is a different game, and a short one.

The god can anchor. By binding itself to a body with great particularity of its own, a mountain, a river, a people's single sung name, the god holds more than the ceiling without rising, because the anchor carries part of the weight. The anchor spends faster: the mountain's springs go first, the river's bends soften, the people forget everything that is not the god. An anchored god can pass 100 and will survive the fall as itself, and so will its anchor, and nothing else.

The god can shed. By giving power to mortals and places, saints and holy springs and blessed breeds, the god stays below the ceiling while its reach grows. Slow, and the only path that leaves a valley full of particular things, which is also how a god makes things that survive the fall without it.

The god can refuse to grow, which the world honors. A god at 60 under a 76 ceiling can rule two hundred years and never hear the ascended.

**Bodies.** The god's own shadow. The rival who lifted. The saint's spring.

**Cost if ignored.** A trigger. Three consecutive years above the ceiling and the god rises on the next dim night. The world warns three times, in the shadow, the stones, and the voices. It does not warn a fourth time.

**Readings the evidence supports.** The star rewards great gods by taking them up. The star hunts great gods. Gods have a natural size, like trees. Worship is poison past a dose. The rival who lifted was struck for pride. The god sits at 50 between reward and hunt until it rises, anchors, or sheds.

## Per-star variation

Roll at world creation. Record in the campaign's Keeper file only.

**Phase** (d100). 1 to 40 young: small, high, pale star; thin rain, fresh land, fading rare, fall tens of thousands of years off. 41 to 80 mature: medium star, strong rain, fading in the oldest places only, descent not yet measurable. 81 to 95 late: large, low, bright; fading visible in a dozen places; descent measurable within forty years by a god with marks and patience; fall centuries off. 96 to 100 falling: descent visible to the eye within the campaign, fall within 100 to 300 years, the hoarder swollen, the ascended loud. The test world rolled 88.

**Ceiling** (70 plus d10). Between 71 and 80.

**Rain density** (d6). How common mortal magic is. 1 one knotter in a region. 6 a wise woman in every valley and a school in the south.

**Living radius** (40 plus d8 times 10 days' walk). Where the rain gives out and the Pale begins. The test world rolled 90 days.

**Center body** (d6). What the hoarder is. 1 to 2 a lake. 3 a warm hill. 4 a forest of one tree. 5 a stone circle that is one organism. 6 a sleeping beast that is visibly a beast.

**Long-game presence** (d6). 1 to 2 hoarder only. 3 to 4 hoarder and carriers. 5 to 6 all three. The test world rolled 6.

**Remainder** (d10). 1 to 7 fell in godless country. 8 to 9 fell into a god's own territory, unrecognized. 10 none fell.

**Star content** (d10, secret, shapes the ascended dreams and what unpacks at a fall). 1 canals. 2 one enormous forest. 3 cities on stilts over a shallow sea. 4 a country of law, every stone a boundary. 5 herders with no walls. 6 ice and long memory. 7 islands that moved. 8 one city. 9 a country where the dead were kept. 10 a fall's new ground that remembered the one before. The test world rolled 3.

**Ascended temper** (d6). 1 to 2 mostly those who want the fall soon. 3 to 4 mixed. 5 to 6 mostly the very old, who want nothing and speak in weather. The test world rolled 3.

## What stays mysterious across campaigns

Why the plane keeps accounts. What is above the thin above. Whether the Between is a place. Whether the star's mind is one or many, which even the ascended cannot answer. What a risen god becomes after a fall: a god, a country, or a people. Where the first star came from. Whether anything has ever not faded. Why the thrown remainders of dead countries are shaped like leaders, which is to say why the plane keeps making gods. Never answered. The Keeper may hint through the ascended, who do not know either.

## What may be solved in one campaign

That the star is lower and quickening. That fading is overdraw and the valley can be spent or kept. That magic costs the mage's particularity and the land's. That the god is a thrown remainder of a far fall, and so are the others, and so is the stone. That the center being wants the fall and is fed from above. That names sung long enough cross the Pale and survive a fall. That there is a ceiling, where it sits, and at least two ways past it. That the fall both destroys and renews, and what is stored comes through. A careful player reaches 80 on three or four of these by Year 60 and sits at 40 to 60 on the rest, which is the target.
