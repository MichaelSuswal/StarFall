# The sealed factors: luck

> SPOILER WARNING: Keeper material. Players should not read this file.

The public engine says only that, where a roll is made, the Keeper applies "the sealed factors" after the stated odds. This file is what that means. It is read by the Keeper and the continuity checker, and by no other agent. Its rules come from change list item 90 (run 03), Michael's decisions of 2026-11-21, run 04 items 23, 43, 52 and 66 (approved 2026-11-22), run 05 items 20, 31, 58 and 63 (approved 2026-11-23), the priced bend (Michael, 2026-11-24), run 06 items 17 and 53 with Michael's decisions of 2026-11-25 on played scenes and clues (build 7), and his decisions after the build 7 check (the overrule of DM rolls removed; searches for a placed thing).

## Secrecy (Michael, 2026-11-21)

Luck is a hidden mechanic and is never stated. The player and the NPC gods must be unaware it exists. Its existence appears in no file the player, the DM, an NPC god agent or a civ agent reads: no engine file, release, card, sheet, morning story, public file or roll line they can see. The DM is never told it and never mentions it. The odds stated to the player, and argued with the DM, leave it out. Since the scan cannot know an ordinary word is sealed, every campaign's `S/sealed-terms.txt` carries the factor's name from Night 1, `!luck` and `!Luck`, each on its own line, the `!` marking a term the scan never exempts (runbook 1.1 and template 7.12). It never comes off the list, even where the player says the word at the table: that conversation stays sealed (Michael, 2026-11-22). The creation transcripts are the player's own words, kept verbatim, and the scan does not check them for it; it checks every other public file, so no agent ever writes it (runbook section 4).

## The modifier

Every god, the player's and each NPC's, has a luck modifier, sealed in `S/state/luck.md` (runbook template 7.14): one line per god (the player's under `player`), a whole number from 0 (ordinary) to +5, with a dated history line for every change and its reason.

- **Earned.** A god earns it by taking heavier prices than it needs, or by refusing powers: each priced step taken beyond what it bought adds one; each power offered and refused, or a god taking none, adds two per power. The Keeper sets it at the Night 2 close (runbook 2.4) and again on Night 3a for edges (runbook 3a.1), and logs each count.
- **What counts.** A power a god shrinks of its own accord, keeping it under its name, is not a power refused: the rule counts powers, not parts of one. A choice between prices the table itself offered as equal is the price, not a step beyond it, whichever is taken; so is the rung of a standing scale the god chooses. A slower, costlier return than the standing rungs, which a god (the player's or an NPC's) may take, priced by the DM or the Keeper, is a heavier price taken willingly and counts as a priced step beyond what was bought (Michael, 2026-11-23). Time alone makes a return costlier: a slower return priced at a standing rung's item cost, with only the time grown, counts the same way (run 06 item 17). Choosing the heavier side of a pair the table offered, a price against a narrower power, does not count: the pair is the table's own choice. A stricter wording a god asks for itself, beyond the price agreed, counts as a priced step. Bending or refusing a default (its helpers, most often) counts only when it is priced: in its negotiation round, on Night 2 (runbook 2.3) or Night 3a (runbook 3a.1), or at the player's Day 3 table; unpriced, it counts nothing. A priced bend counts by what the default does (Michael, 2026-11-24). The Keeper judges each one: whether the bend makes the god's life harder, by how much, and net of any price paid for it elsewhere. A bend that makes the god's life harder, and is not already paid for, counts as priced steps beyond what was bought, as many as the Keeper judges the net hardship worth. A bend that makes the god's life easier, or is already paid for elsewhere, counts nothing. The same rule for the player's god and every NPC god, each judgment logged with its reason. "Designed, not yet serving" is never priced and counts nothing. "Taking none" counts only powers that were offered: a god offered nothing (one that proposed no edge, or whose ask was no power) has refused nothing and earns nothing for it.
- **Bounds.** Never below 0, never above +5.
- **Moved back.** It moves one step toward 0 when a god's growth reaches a tier in a god power (`engine/rules/powers.md`), with a dated history line, in pass two of the night turn. Craft, technology, wealth and numbers never move it.

## Use

- **On a roll.** Before any roll the Keeper makes for a god or its people's acts, it works out the stated odds from the open factors (DESIGN 12.6), then applies luck after them: 5 points in the god's favor on a d100 per step, or one face per step on a smaller die. Luck is named in the roll's `--what` as a factor at its step, in the sealed roll log (`tools/roll.py --campaign <name>`), with the stated odds and the odds rolled both written there.
- **The clamp (Michael, 2026-11-21).** Odds with luck stay capped at 5 to 95, as every roll is (DESIGN 12.6). On a smaller die the shifted target leaves at least one face that fails and one that succeeds. Luck never makes a roll certain, and a thing certain or impossible by the open factors is not rolled at all.
- **A contest between two gods (Michael, 2026-11-21).** Where a roll sets one god or its people against another god or its people, the two gods' luck nets out and only the difference counts, toward the god with more. Equal luck shifts nothing.
- **Weighting.** In pass one, where a chance event chooses among targets (a weather year, a murrain, a schedule roll that chooses which people a natural harm falls on), the Keeper weights the choice away from a lucky god's harm by the same steps, logged as a factor. Where two lucky gods' peoples are among the targets, each is weighted by its own steps. The method, cast as one weighted draw over the hundred, `tools/roll.py --campaign <name> --who keeper --what "<the event>, which people" --weight <label>=<share>` with one `--weight` per target (the tool writes the bands in the log with the draw): each target starts with an even share of the hundred, in whole points, the remainder one point each to the targets in the order the log lists them, by label, alphabetically (run 06 item 53); each step of a target's god takes 5 points from its share (never below 1); the points freed are spread evenly over the targets whose gods have no luck, or, if every target's god has some, over those with less; the shares are written in the keeper log before the roll. Rounding: the freed points are divided by the number of targets that take them, in whole points; the remainder goes one point each to those targets in the order the log lists them, by label, alphabetically, so the bands always sum to a hundred. A people the same harm has already struck is removed before the cast (runbook 3b.6).
- **A fixed target.** A natural harm whose target the schedule fixes (one place, one people) and whose year is rolled takes no luck on the year: a year is not a choice among targets, and luck never moves a harm sooner or later. When the harm comes, any roll for how hard it falls on the people, or for what they save, is a roll for their acts and takes luck as any roll does.
- **Not acts.** The table rolls that build a god or the world (leans, seeds, the star, the plane) are not acts and take no luck.
- **A search.** A search by a god or its people for a thing that came about in the game is a roll for their acts and takes luck after the odds the evidence sets (`mystery-standard.md` rule 17). A search for a thing the Keeper placed is rolled only under pressure or a race (rule 17), and then takes luck like any roll for their acts; with no pressure or race it works out and is not rolled, so luck has nothing to shift. A world fact the search turns up that is not the thing sought (a near miss) is the world's question and takes no luck, since the god's favor has no side in it; the search's own success takes it (run 07, `tests/creation-sim/run-07/rulings-to-rules.md` rule 14).
- It is a factor on the record, never a judgment in the moment.

## DM rolls in play (Michael, 2026-11-25)

The DM rolls in the open on played days, with the stated odds, as the public engine gives it, and never knows of this file. Its roll lines in `P/state/rolls.jsonl` are never edited. The Keeper never changes the result of a roll the DM made in play: no overrule, no sealed re-roll, no consequence carried as though the roll had gone the other way (Michael, 2026-11-25, removing the overrule of 2026-11-22). Luck works only through the odds the Keeper itself sets:
- the Keeper's own rolls for a god's or its people's acts, as above;
- the weighting of chance events among targets, as above;
- the chance ranges on a board it writes for a held moment or prepared scene (`year-turn.md`, the day sheet): where a range turns on chance for the god or its people (how far a figure will move, how many come), the Keeper sets it as it would set a roll's odds, shifted by the god's steps, before the board is written, and logs the shift in the keeper log only. Every chance range on a board is set so, and given to the DM as its outcome by branch; the DM rolls only the player's own attempts (run 07 item 18). A range is never reshaped after its roll. The odds the sheet gives the DM to state are stated odds and leave the factor out, as every stated odds does; the reasons never name it.

## The Keeper's record

The neutrality self-check of every night and every turn names every roll, weighting or board range where luck was a factor, by roll id or board and step. The keeper log's counts match `S/state/luck.md`.

## The checker's check

The continuity checker reads `S/state/luck.md`, the sealed roll log and the keeper log, and checks:

1. Every roll for a god with luck above 0, or for its people's acts, names luck in its `--what` at its logged step, with the stated odds and the odds rolled; every contest between two gods uses the difference only; no odds outside 5 to 95.
2. Every pass-one weighting of a chance event among targets names luck where it applied.
3. Every change in `S/state/luck.md` has its dated reason, matches the keeper log, and stays within 0 to +5; growth to a tier in a god power moved it one step toward 0, and nothing else moved it down.
4. No word of luck in any file the player, the DM, an NPC god agent or a civ agent reads: every public file, every release, card and sheet, every engine file, the creation transcripts excepted, which are the player's own words kept verbatim and are not scanned for it. A hit is a flag, fixed by rewording (the Keeper's own replacement in a DM-owned file, runbook section 4), never by an allow line. The factor's name stands on the campaign's sealed term list on `!` lines.
5. No result of a DM roll was changed, in the record, the year or any release; no DM roll line in the public roll log is changed; every board range shifted by luck is logged in the keeper log with its board and step.
