# Cold Reader Log: deep-lore blind test

Reader: a cold god-king of the Ossel. Only the packet was read, one block at a time. Theories and confidences are written before the next block was opened.

---

## After Block 1 (Years 1 to 10)

### Theories

1. **The Lamp is not a star. It is something that drinks water.** Every water in the land is falling (ford at the knee, wells deepened, Thornwell spring dead, western lake now a marsh) except the Still, which is rising. The rhyme "The Lamp drinks the lake, one sip, two sips," the shimmer standing straight up from the Still toward the Lamp, and the Lamp riding higher in the notch all point the same way: water is being pulled from the land into the Still and from the Still up to the Lamp. Confidence: 70.

2. **The Still is a crater, and the "flood and fire where the lake is now" was an impact or a landing.** The unbroken bowl rim, the black layer with long-skulled bones under it, the far southern story of the sky burning for a season right after the glowing oxen walked out "to the fall." Something came down there long ago. Confidence: 75.

3. **The blue lights, including the Lamp, are things like us. We fell. Others fell before.** "Fall story" is the word a far people use for their own origin. We also fell through "something thinner than air." The Hob's Barrow saying, "No god has entered the Barrow and come out the same," means gods are a known category in this world and have been here before. I think the blue lights are other arrivals, or the vessels of arrivals, some of them still in the sky and one of them (the Lamp) hanging overhead and doing something to the land. Confidence: 45.

4. **Power here is paid for with memory and self, and the Still is where the exchange is strongest.** Mother Reed sold her name for a harvest and forgot a child she delivered. The Still healers are young and strong and forget their husbands. The rain-caller cannot taste water anymore. The Still villages have "the youngest forgetting workers." The elder's saying, "A wise-woman buys with what she has," is the rule. Whatever is at the bottom of the Still (the blue from underneath, growing wider) is what the workers are drawing on, and it is taking something back. Confidence: 65.

5. **The glowing oxen are tied to the fallen thing.** They glow, they will drink only at the Still, and a whole herd once walked to a fall site before the sky burned. They are either made by the fallen thing or feed it. Confidence: 50.

6. **The Hem is what the land becomes when the Lamp finishes with it.** "The star does not love the Hem." The north slopes nearest the Hem lose grass first. The dry pale country is the end state and it is spreading south toward us. Confidence: 55.

7. **Where we gods came from: we are the same kind of thing as the blue lights, or were carried by one.** Confidence: 35. I do not yet know whether we are a second instance of the problem or a response to it.

### What I would do next

- Send a trusted party with the Tallow herders on the spring walk of the glowing oxen to the Still, with orders to watch what the oxen do at the water and to go out on the lake at night and look at the blue from underneath.
- Send a second party to the east ridge spot at dawn to map exactly where the shimmer stands and whether it moves with the Lamp.
- Stop granting land to Thornwell's refugees at Oss Ford and instead settle them upriver on higher ground, and begin digging deep cisterns at Oss Ford now while the ford is still at the knee.
- Ask Mother Reed, directly and privately, what exactly she sold her name to and whether the thing she bought from is the same thing under the Still.
- Do not go to Hob's Barrow yet. Note it.

### Clues: nothing, pointing, or handed

- Felt like nothing (texture only): goats blamed for the north slopes; the ferrymen's remark on its own; the tax-counter's remark on its own. Each is small, but they add up, which is right.
- Pointed somewhere without saying: the oxen that drink only at the Still; the bowl rim seen from the pass; the long skulls under the black layer; the far southern fall story; Mother Reed forgetting the child; the rain-caller losing taste for water. These were the good ones. Each is a worldly detail with a hidden reason behind it.
- Felt close to handing me the answer: the counting rhyme "The Lamp drinks the lake" and the shimmer "straight up toward the Lamp" in the same block. Either alone is a strong hint; both together nearly state the mechanism. The Hob's Barrow saying also reads as a signpost placed for me rather than a thing people would say. The Year 8 line "while every other water in the land is lower" is the narrator summarizing for me; a pilgrim would not phrase it so completely.

## After Block 2 (Years 11 to 20)

### Theories

1. **The Lamp and the blue under the Still are one body. We gods are made of the same stuff.** The Lamp flared and the blue under the lake flared the same night. A god cut to pieces bled blue, the blue ran uphill-flat toward the Still, and the god walked out whole three days later. The Still is where our substance lives and where it goes back when we are broken. The Lamp is the part of it in the sky. Confidence: 85.

2. **Working power spends water. The Still is the account.** A healer closed a wound and the water dropped a hand's width. Every worker in the land, every god, every glowing ox, is drawing on the same pool, and the pool draws on the land's water. The drying of the land is us. The Hem is the ledger of past spending. Confidence: 80.

3. **The Lamp feeds on people, not just water. "The star loves a crowd."** The stone-people will not build over large towns. The Still villages are the most crowded and have the youngest forgetting workers. Where people gather, the Lamp draws harder, and what it draws is memory and self along with water. Confidence: 65.

4. **We are not new. We are a repeat. The long-skulled people were the last cycle's people, and the ring-hill vaults are their record of the gods that came to them.** Faces, hands, a crown, a tool. Five figures on the oldest stone. My face in the fourth vault. The same gods, or the same molds of gods, fall each cycle. The flood and fire at the Still was the last fall. The far southern sky burning for a season was a fall before that. Confidence: 70.

5. **The cycle ends with the land dried and the Lamp leaving or moving on.** The Lamp has started moving sideways toward the southeast. The Tallow oxen now graze in a line toward the southeast. The glowing herd in the old southern story walked "to the fall" and then the sky burned. I think the Lamp is beginning to go where the oxen are pointing, and that a fall happens where it goes. Greenhollow, to the east, is green and has two harvests and no old stories. Either Greenhollow is where the next fall lands, or it is a fresh place the last Lamp has not yet drunk from. Confidence: 55.

6. **Gods lose themselves the same way workers do, and faster when they are named by a title and used.** The Lord of the Ford has grown slow and no longer gives its own name. Mother Reed sold her name. Names are the thing that gets spent first. Confidence: 60.

7. **Hob's Barrow is a place made to shut the Lamp out.** Water goes around it, never under. The garden is green inside while the Hem is grey. My power dimmed at the door. Whoever built it knew how to make a place the Lamp cannot drink from, and the price of that is that gods cannot work there either. The stone-people quarry only in the finished Hem because that stone has already been fully drained and is "safe." Confidence: 70.

8. **There are five of us meant to be here. Three I know, a fourth revealed at the headwaters. One more is missing or hidden.** Confidence: 60 that a fifth exists; where it is, 20.

9. **Where we came from: we were sent or shed by the Lamp itself, or by whatever the Lamp is a piece of, to this land, and we have fallen before without remembering it.** All three of us expected a people waiting. That expectation feels planted. Confidence: 50.

### What I would do next

- Stop all healing and weather work in the crowded Still villages by decree, and move the pilgrim season away from the lake edge. Test whether the water stops dropping against the steps when no one works there.
- Send the two gods I know to walk the Tallow herd line southeast with the oxen, carrying no title, and find what the herd is walking toward. I do not go myself; I am the one whose face is in the vault.
- Dig one more vault at the ring hills, the fifth, to find the fifth face.
- Pay the stone-people whatever they ask for one answer: how the Barrow was built. Not what is inside. How.
- Have the priest make a new stone at Oss Ford with the Lamp's position cut into it every midsummer from now on, so the drift is measured and not remembered.
- Send a party to Greenhollow with orders to count wells, ask the oldest person what their grandparents said, and look for a bowl rim in the hills.
- Tell no one my name again. Begin letting the people call me by the river.

### Clues: nothing, pointing, or handed

- Felt like nothing: the miners' remark about quarrying in the deep Hem on its own. In hindsight it supports the Barrow theory, but it read as filler at first.
- Pointed somewhere without saying: the healer and the water dropping (a quiet, perfect clue, and the fishermen not looking up is the best line in the packet); the Tallow herd grazing in a line; the Lord of the Ford answering only to a title; Greenhollow having no old stories; the clay-digger dreaming of the face. These made me want to act.
- Felt like the narrator handing me the answer: "In the fourth vault, the face is yours." That is a large reveal delivered flat. It works as a gut-punch, but it is the one moment where I felt the design showing me a card rather than me finding it. The Year 20 fight (blue blood running toward the Still, god walks out whole) is also very direct, though it earns it as a payoff for the Year 6 and Year 12 blue-under-the-lake clues. The Year 16 count of five figures with three known gods is clearly a planted arithmetic hook, and I did not mind it.

## After Block 3 (Years 21 to 30)

### Theories

1. **The Lamp is a thing that falls, feeds, and seeds. A new one just fell in the far southwest.** The glow that stayed all season and became a ring in the sky, then warm rain carrying blue dust that tastes like the Still. The dust brought the heaviest harvest in memory and three new workers, one a child. This is how the far southern story's "sky burned for a season" looks from a distance. The blue lights at the horizons are other Lamps over other lands. Confidence: 85.

2. **Everything we grow, the Lamp drinks. Building is feeding it.** The Lamp flared the night after my canal opened. The Dun god built faster and the Dun failed first. The Barrow thing said the last ones "built high, and the Lamp drank them with the rest." The stone-people will not build over crowds. Civilization is the bait. Our instinct to build a people is the mechanism by which the Lamp harvests a land. Confidence: 85.

3. **We gods are the Lamp's seed and its organs.** We fell the same night, from the same sky. We bleed blue and reheal in the Still. The faces, hands and crown hanging in the fissure at the lake floor are the gods of past cycles, kept, not dead. The ring-hill vaults are the long-skulls' casts of the same faces. My face is down there too, or will be. The "wall against my growing" is the sky's wall: I am allowed to grow only as far as the Lamp wants, because my growth is its growth. Confidence: 75.

4. **The price of every working is memory, paid by whoever is nearest, and gods pay with their names last.** The festival village lost one year collectively. The eastern hamlets lost their dye craft when the fields were stripped. I have lost what I was called before I was king and no one noticed. The Lord of the Ford is further along. Confidence: 85.

5. **The Barrow is a third thing's refuge. There are three ways to end: the floor of the Still (be drunk and kept), the Barrow (shut yourself away from the Lamp, powerless, in a green pocket), and a third way the Barrow thing would not take.** The elders' saying about "a land with no star, where no one dies of thirst and no one is remembered" is a description of the third way: leave the Lamp's reach entirely, and be forgotten. I think the third way is to walk out from under the sky, as the glowing herd once did. Confidence: 55.

6. **The glowing oxen are the Lamp's walkers. Something huge, slow and blue-lit walks the Hem and finishes the drying.** The Hem-walker dried a spring overnight and paled the stone. The Tallow oxen glow under the hide and drink only at the Still. They are small versions of the same kind of thing, or they are what the Hem-walker is made from. They turned east when the southwest fall happened. They are steering toward something and the Lamp follows them, or they follow the Lamp. Confidence: 60 that they are the same kind; 40 on which leads which.

7. **Where we came from: we were in the Still, or in a Lamp, before. We do not remember because we have already paid.** Confidence: 60.

8. **The fifth god is in the Barrow.** It said it was like me. It came in one night with others. It chose the Barrow. Confidence: 65. (Alternative: it is from the previous cycle, and the five on the stone are the previous five. Confidence: 30.)

### What I would do next

- Close the canal. Let the eastern fields go. Issue a standing order that no god, worker, or crowd works at the Still.
- Cut three marks into the ford stone every autumn and send a runner to the Dun to compare their failure timeline against mine, year for year, so I know how long Oss Ford has.
- Go back to the Barrow door and ask one question: what is the third way, and why would you not take it. Pay in whatever the stone-people want except my people's names.
- Send a party east along the Tallow herd line with a glowing ox on a rope and a month of water, with orders to turn back when the ox stops.
- Begin moving the youngest families out from under the Lamp, northwest across the Hem toward the stone-people's country, in small groups, with the dye-makers and bee-keepers first. Not the workers. The workers stay here where they will be drunk.
- Write down everything I remember about myself and give the tablet to someone who is not a priest.

### Clues: nothing, pointing, or handed

- Felt like nothing: "The Oss at the Ford comes to the ankle in autumn" as a solitary line (good as a pulse, but inert); the first hunger in the north (expected, no new information).
- Pointed somewhere without saying: the blue dust tasting like the Still; the Dun god building faster and failing first; the clay-bees swarming and the dye craft lost together; the festival village losing exactly the twelve months of the harvest; the Hem-walker; the diver who did not come up. These were all strong and I wanted to act on each one.
- Felt like the narrator handing me the answer: the Barrow speech in Year 25. It is a good scene, but it states the whole cycle in four sentences: others came in one night, built high, the Lamp drank them, three ways to end, the wall is the sky's. After twenty-four years of oblique clues it is a sudden lecture. I would want it split: one sentence per visit, three visits, each paid for. The Lamp flaring the night after the canal opened is also very direct cause-and-effect; it is fine as confirmation but I had already concluded it.

## After Block 4 (Years 31 to 40)

### Theories

1. **The Lamp is a feeding body tethered to the land through a ladder at the floor of the Still. The shimmer at dawn is the ladder. It can be cut.** The stone-people elder says it has been done twice: Hem stone in quantity, every worker a land could muster, and a god at full height, at the floor of the lake. After the cut the land stopped dying and the star went dark and hangs there still. The faint dark lights, if any, at the horizons would be dead Lamps. Confidence: 85.

2. **The "shepherds" are what gods become when they take the third way: they walk out from under their Lamp, lose their names entirely, and become the huge, slow, blue-lit things that lead a Lamp to a fresh land.** The glowing oxen followed one east for eleven years and crossed into Greenhollow, where something very large sleeps under a hill that is not a hill, warm, and the people leave bread without knowing why. The Hem-walker was a shepherd finishing a land. The shepherd in Greenhollow is lying in the new pasture waiting for the Lamp to arrive. "Once like you and are not now." This is the third way the Barrow thing would not take: become the Lamp's dog and survive by leading it to the next people. Confidence: 75.

3. **The Lamp is preparing to move. It has stopped drifting against the notch, the herds have gone, and the Still is now dropping.** The Still rising was the Lamp filling its cistern. The Still falling, with Thornwell's spring returning and springs near the Still failing, means the Lamp is pulling in the last of the water and lifting its weight off the land, or the ladder is weakening because so much has been drunk. Either way the land is in its last stage. The Lamp will go to Greenhollow, and Greenhollow's two harvests and new workers and refugees will be its next meal. Confidence: 70.

4. **Gods end in three ways, now seen in full.** Be drunk: build and press and flicker and repeat yourself until the Lamp keeps your face in the fissure (the god who built most is flickering now; the Lord of the Ford before it). Hide: shut yourself in a Barrow, powerless, with a green garden, and sell water for labor (the Barrow thing is doing exactly that; the forty-year labor is its price, and I notice forty years is my own tenure). Become a shepherd: walk out and lead. There is a fourth way the stone-people know and the Barrow thing did not mention, or mentioned only as "the wall is the sky's": cut the ladder, which spends everything a land has and leaves a dark star and a living land. Confidence: 85.

5. **We gods are the Lamp's own substance, seeded into a land to raise a crowd and build high so the Lamp can drink.** The expectation that a people would be waiting was built into us. Our hunger to build is the Lamp's hunger. The height a god reaches under its sky is a measure of how much the Lamp has invested in it, which is why the cutting must be done by a god at full height: it takes the Lamp's own stored strength to cut it. Confidence: 80.

6. **The long-skulled people were the last cycle here. They tried to record us, and maybe to cut the ladder, and failed.** The vaults with faces, hands, a crown, and a six-holed tool are their casts of the gods and of the implements of that attempt. Confidence: 55. (I now think the black layer is the fall that made the Still, and the long-skulls were already there to be drunk. The Still was not their lake; it was their crater.)

7. **The land with no star where no one is remembered is a land where the ladder was cut.** No star, no thirst, and no memory, because the memory was the price paid to cut it. Both the hill elders and an unrelated far-southern people have the same saying, which means it is true somewhere. Confidence: 70.

8. **Names are the first coin and the last coin.** Mother Reed's successor at the end has no name and no one can give it back. I have lost mine too. The Lamp drinks names before water. Confidence: 90.

### What I would do next

I now believe there is one real choice and it is a bad trade either way. I would try to cut the ladder.

- Stop all growth. No more canals, no temple, no new villages. Hold the people at the size they are. This is a holding move, not a solution; the Lamp is going to leave for Greenhollow regardless and finish us as it goes.
- Send every spare laborer the stone-people have not taken, with every goat and cart, into the deep Hem to quarry pale stone and haul it to the Still. Pay the stone-people in grain, not names, not labor-levies.
- Recall the god who flickers and the god who walked out of the Still whole. The fourth god too, if it can be found. Tell them what the elder said. Ask them to give me their height for one working, at the floor of the lake, at dawn, from the east-ridge line where the shimmer stands. I am the one whose face is already cast; I have the least to lose and I am at the top of my wall.
- Before that, send every worker in Oss Ford to Greenhollow with the refugees and tell them not to work there, ever, and to teach their children the saying about the land with no star. Someone has to carry the memory the cutting will cost.
- Write my own name on the tablet if I can still find it anywhere. Give it to the Greenhollow party.
- Do not do the cutting until the Still is at its lowest and the Lamp is about to leave. The elder said the land "stopped dying," which means the cut was made while the Lamp was still attached. If the Lamp leaves first, there is nothing to cut.

### Clues: nothing, pointing, or handed

- Felt like nothing: "The Lamp brightened for one night" in Years 33 and 40 without any paired event. After Year 12 and Year 26 paired the flare with a cause, an unpaired flare reads as a drumbeat, which is fine, but I kept hunting for the cause and found none. If the cause is meant to be hidden (something happened in Greenhollow, say), that is good; if it is just rhythm, it is noise.
- Pointed somewhere without saying: Thornwell's spring returning while springs near the Still fail (excellent reversal clue); the flickering god repeating a ruling word for word; the herds leaving the land; the bread left at the hill that is not a hill; the forty-year labor contracts; the far-south stranger repeating the hill saying independently.
- Felt like the narrator handing me the answer: the Year 40 elder's speech. It is the second lecture in the packet, and it hands me the win condition directly: what to use, where, who, and what happens after. I would have preferred to get "it has been done" from the elder and to assemble the how from the six-holed tool, the Hem stone, and the Barrow thing's hint about a third way. Year 36 "a shepherd does not remember its name either" is also direct, but it lands as a confirmation of a theory I had half-formed, which felt earned. The Mother Reed successor ending is not a reveal but an image, and it is the right last line.

## Overall note

**Subtlety.** The first two blocks are close to right. Block 1 is quiet and worldly and I had to work for every inference; the three or four clues that nearly stated the mechanism (the counting rhyme, the shimmer, the "every other water is lower" line) could each be made one degree more oblique and the block would still carry. Block 2 is the best block: the healer dropping the water while the fishermen do not look up, the herd grazing in a line, the Lord of the Ford answering only to a title. Blocks 3 and 4 shift mode: two speeches (the Barrow in Year 25 and the elder in Year 40) do in one line each what the first twenty years did by accretion. They are good scenes but they are exposition. I would split each across two or three paid visits and withhold at least one piece (the how of the cutting, or the "star went dark" outcome) so the reader has to gamble.

**Pull to act.** Strong throughout. I wanted to act from Year 8 (the Still rising while all else falls) and never stopped. The design does the key thing: nearly every clue has an obvious verb attached (go there, dig that, pay them, follow the herd, stop the canal). The world did not feel plodding. The ten-year block pacing works because the water lines (knee, waist, ankle, a third lost) give a felt clock. If anything I felt the lack of consequences for my own actions; the packet is passive by design, but a god who has concluded "building feeds it" by Year 25 would want to see the Lamp respond to restraint as well as to building. One line where the Lamp dims because something was not built would be strong.

**Confusing or unmotivated.** A few:
- The five figures on the oldest stone are set up in Year 16 and never paid off. I guessed the fifth is in the Barrow, but nothing confirmed or denied it, and the fourth god vanishes after Year 20.
- The six-holed tool is placed but never used. If it is the cutting tool, one more sighting (in the fissure, or in the stone-people's hands) would close the loop.
- The Hem-walker (Year 28) and the Greenhollow sleeper (Year 38) are clearly the same kind of thing, but the Year 28 walker was drying the Hem while the Year 38 sleeper is warming a green valley. I could not decide whether shepherds finish old lands or prepare new ones, or both. One clue tying the walker's path to the herd's path would settle it.
- The forty-year labor term is a lovely rhyme with the forty-year packet but I could not tell if it is meant to mean something (the Barrow thing buying bodies for its own ladder cut?) or is just a number.
- Nothing in the packet ever made me doubt the central read after Year 20. Once "the Lamp drinks us through what we build" was in place, every later clue confirmed it. A single clue that cut against the grain (a land that built high and did not dry, say, or a Lamp that drank a land of hermits) would have kept the reader off balance into the last block.

Verdict: the mystery is a touch too generous in the back half and about right in the front half. The world pulled me forward. The core idea, that the god's own drive to build is the harvest, is strong enough that it should be protected by making the player discover it rather than be told it.
