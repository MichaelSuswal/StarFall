## f) Verdict on the five failure modes

- Plodding: Not present. The reader felt a clock from Block 1 and the water-line style markers gave a felt sense of time; the ten-year block pacing held.
- Mysteries in documents: Partly present in the back half. Blocks 1 and 2 deliver by accretion of worldly detail; Blocks 3 and 4 each contain one reported clue (Year 25, Year 40) that functions as a lecture and resolves in four sentences what the earlier blocks earned by inference.
- Invented drama: Not present. The two large seen events (Year 14, Year 20) are gated and paid for, and the reader accepted both; nothing in the log reads as manufactured incident.
- Too subtle: Not present. Nothing was missed; the one late solve (Mystery 4) was late because a rival theory crowded it out, not because the clues were faint. The only "noise" complaint was the unpaired Year 33 and Year 40 sky events, which the reader hunted a cause for and found none.
- Too obvious: Present, moderately. Four mysteries fell a block early, the reader's central read was fixed by Year 20 and never shaken, and the two speech clues hand over conclusions the design wanted assembled.

## g) Three recommended changes

1. Split the Year 25 reported clue into three paid visits (Years 23, 25, and 28), one sentence of substance each, and make the speaker wrong about one load-bearing detail in the first visit, to be corrected only on the third. This restores the design's own three-row structure and its "blurting mortal is wrong about something" rule.

2. Hold the Year 40 reported clue to "it has been done, twice" plus the cost and the outcome, and withhold the method. Carry the method instead through a seen clue in Year 37 or 38 that re-sights the object introduced in the Year 12 to 14 vault clues, placed where the Year 40 speaker would have no reason to explain it. The reader explicitly asked to assemble the how from pieces and had already named those pieces.

3. Add one counter-grain clue and one clock-correcting clue. In Block 3 (Year 27 or 29, reported), give the reader a case that cuts against its density rule, so the central read stays in doubt into Block 4. In Block 4 (Year 39 or 40, reported), give the Year 39 seen clue a witness frame that distinguishes "settled" from "about to leave," since the reader's final plan is timed to the wrong one of those two readings. Pair the Year 33 and Year 40 unpaired sky events with a hidden cause in the far valley so a careful reader can find it.

A fourth, smaller fix: rephrase the Year 8 seen clue through its witness and move the Year 10 seen clue to Year 11 or 12, so Block 1 ends with Mystery 2 at suspicion rather than at 70.

## Other notes for the Keeper

- The reader asked for one line where the Lamp responds to restraint. The C8 design row ("in the year the god raises its first great work, the orb pulses out of schedule") has no inverse. A line in Block 3 such as "the Lamp did not brighten on the seventh year for the first time anyone remembers, the year the Dun stopped building" would give a reader who concluded "stop" a way to test it, and would also complicate the reader's belief that the pulse is purely a feeding rhythm.
- The reader could not place the Hem-walker (Y28) against the Greenhollow sleeper (Y38). Per the key these are the same ascended; the walker's spring-death in the Hem and the sleeper's warm green valley read to the reader as "finishing old lands" versus "preparing new ones." One clue tying the walker's path to the Y24 herd turn would settle it.
- The forty-year labor contracts at the Barrow were read as a possible purchase of bodies for a ladder cut. If this is not intended, consider a different number.
- The Y33 and Y40 unpaired pulses: the Y33 pulse is on the Keeper's seven-year schedule; the reader did not know the schedule and hunted a cause. Either give the schedule away through the priest's stone by Block 3 (the reader itself proposed cutting the Lamp's position into a stone yearly, so it would have discovered the rhythm) or pair them.
- Counter-grain clue for C8: the design has no row that cuts against density. A reported clue in Y27 or Y29 (a land that built high and did not dry because it built under a roof; or a land of hermits that dried anyway because of workers) would keep the reader off balance into Block 4 and would also seed C10's roof logic.
- The direction of the gods' origin (up from the lake, not down from the sky) has no clue in the packet that disambiguates it from the reader's seed theory. If the Keeper wants C7 fully reachable by Y32, one clue is needed: for instance, Y30 lake floor shows an empty socket among the hanging faces, in the shape of the god's own face from Y14.
