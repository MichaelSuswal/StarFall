# Cold Reader Packet: deep-lore blind test

Instructions for the reader: you are a god-king of a river-valley people. Below is only what you plausibly noticed, heard, or were told, year by year, in the words the morning story would use. Nothing is explained. At the end of each block, write down what you think is going on and what you would do. Do not read the answer key until you have finished all four blocks.

## Setting (all the reader is given)

You arrived with the others in a single night, falling through something thinner than air, and woke on a hillside above a river. The people here call themselves the Ossel. They farm the valley of the river Oss, fish it, and herd goats on the slopes. They have bronze and no iron. The Oss runs south out of the hills for two months' travel to a great still lake at the center of the land, which they call the Still and visit for pilgrimage. North of the hills is a dry pale country they call the Hem. Overhead hangs the star they call the Lamp, a pale blue-white, the largest light in the sky. Other, fainter blue lights sit low at the horizons. You rule from a hill town called Oss Ford where the river is shallow enough to cross.

=== BLOCK 1 ===

## Years 1 to 10

**Year 1**
- The Ossel took you as king within the year. Their old wise-woman, called only Mother Reed, blessed the crowning. When asked her name she laughed and said she had sold it for a good harvest a long time ago.
- An elder's saying, offered at the feast: "A wise-woman buys with what she has."
- Goatherds mention that the north slopes toward the Hem have less grass than their fathers described. They blame the goats.

**Year 2**
- Mother Reed set a child's broken leg with her hands and a word. That evening at the feast she asked whose child it was. She had delivered it.
- A proverb from the north villages, when the pale country is spoken of: "The star does not love the Hem."

**Year 3**
- At the autumn crossing, the ferrymen at Oss Ford remark that the water came to the knee this year. In their fathers' time it came to the waist. The rains were ordinary.
- A goatherd from the south says the great slow oxen that the Tallow herders keep, the ones that glow faintly under the hide at night, will drink only at the Still and are walked there every spring. He thinks it is a Tallow superstition.

**Year 4**
- A trader from the far west edge, selling salt, says the lake his grandmother swam in is a reed marsh now, and the village moved twice to follow the water.
- Children at the Still villages sing a counting rhyme, a pilgrim reports: "The Lamp drinks the lake, one sip, two sips."
- From the high pass, on a clear day, you can see that the hills ringing the Still make a single unbroken curve, like the rim of a bowl.

**Year 5**
- The night the Lamp brightened. Shepherds on the night watch say it flared for one night, brighter than a full moon, and was ordinary by morning.
- The priest who keeps the old standing stone above Oss Ford says the Lamp no longer sits in the notch it was carved to sit in on midsummer night. It rides a finger's width higher.
- Pilgrims returning from the Still tell a story the lake people tell: of a flood and a fire "where the lake is now," in the grandfathers' grandfathers' time.

**Year 6**
- A pilgrim says that at the Still the healers are young and strong, and forget their own husbands.
- Fishermen at the Still say the water there is blue at night from underneath, and that the blue is wider than their fathers said.

**Year 7**
- Well-diggers report that two villages a week apart both had to deepen their wells this spring.
- Men digging a grave in the ring hills struck a black layer and under it bones and tools "of the wrong kind." The skulls were long. Nobody wanted to keep them and they were reburied.

**Year 8**
- Your own visit to the Still, if you made one: the water stands higher against the pilgrim steps than the oldest pilgrims remember, while every other water in the land is lower.
- A traveler from a far southern people, who have their own fall story, says that in the age of her grandmother's grandmother a whole herd of the glowing oxen walked out of her land in a line and never came back, "to the fall," and then the far sky burned for a season.

**Year 9**
- The spring at Thornwell, a northern village under your rule for twenty years, stopped in a single season. Forty families have come down to Oss Ford asking for land.
- A worker in the north villages who called rain for the fields in the dry year says plainly she can no longer taste water. Her family asks you whether this is a punishment.
- Traders who deal with the stone-people of Hob's Barrow, in the Hem, repeat a saying from that country: "No god has entered the Barrow and come out the same."

**Year 10**
- Hunters on the east ridge say that at dawn, from one spot and no other, a shimmer stands in the air over the Still, straight up toward the Lamp, like heat over a road.
- A tax-counter remarks that the Still villages, the most crowded in the land, have the deepest wells and the youngest forgetting workers.

## Years 11 to 20

**Year 11**
- At the Still, during the pilgrim season, you watch a healer close a wound at the water's edge. The water drops a hand's width against the steps as she does it. The fishermen do not look up.

**Year 12**
- The Lamp brightened again for one night. Fishermen at the Still, asked afterward, say the blue under the lake brightened the same night.
- A clay-digger at the ring hills broke into a hard pale vault in the black layer. In its wall was a face, pressed in perfect detail, in a style no one makes, with a long skull. The digger has dreamed of it every night for a month and will not go back.

**Year 13**
- You went to Hob's Barrow. Water runs around the hill and never under it. The garden inside the door is green when the Hem around it is grey. At the door your strength dimmed like a lamp in wind. Inside you could not do what you do.
- The stone-people would not speak of the Barrow's inside. They asked what you would pay.

**Year 14**
- You opened three more vaults at the ring hills. Faces, hands, a crown, a tool with six holes. None of the faces belong to any people now living in the land.
- In the fourth vault, the face is yours.

**Year 15**
- A stone-person trader, asked about roofing a town, says their people do not build over towns of more than a few hundred. "The star loves a crowd," he said, and would not explain.

**Year 16**
- The Tallow herds, which have grazed a circle south of the Still for as long as the Tallow remember, began this season to graze in a line, all heads toward the southeast.
- You have spoken privately with the two other gods you know of in the land. Both arrived the same night you did. Both expected a people waiting. Neither remembers more than you do.
- Your priest counted the figures carved on the oldest standing stone at the ring hills. There are five. Including yourself, you know of three gods in the land.

**Year 17**
- Miners who followed the stone-people say they quarry only in the deep Hem, where the drying is finished and the stone has gone pale.

**Year 18**
- The priest who keeps the stone above Oss Ford says the Lamp has moved against the notch for the first time not up, but sideways, toward the southeast.
- Travelers from the river Dun, two months east, say the god there is called only "the Lord of the Ford" by its priests, and has grown slow, and answers to that title and no other, and no longer gives its own name when asked.

**Year 19**
- Refugees from the east say they passed through a valley a month beyond the Dun, which they call Greenhollow, where nothing has gone grey and the harvests are two a year, and whose people have no stories older than their grandparents.

**Year 20**
- A fourth god, until now unknown to you, fought one of the two you know at the headwaters. Witnesses say the loser was cut in pieces and the pieces bled blue, and the blue ran downhill toward the Still across level ground. Three days later the loser walked out of the Still, whole, onto the pilgrim steps.
- A dream of a sky falling, which you have had before, now has the long-skulled face in it.

## Years 21 to 30

**Year 21**
- The Oss at the Ford comes to the ankle in autumn.

**Year 22**
- A glow rose in the far southwest horizon in spring and stayed all season, blue-white, brighter than any of the low horizon lights. By summer a ring of light stood in the night sky there.
- In autumn the rains came warm and left a fine blue dust on every surface. Mother Reed's successor says the dust tastes like the water at the Still.

**Year 23**
- Harvests across the valley are the heaviest in memory.
- Three new workers have declared themselves in Oss Ford alone. Their workings are strong. One of them is nine years old.

**Year 24**
- The Tallow herds, which had walked southeast for eight years, turned east in a single season, all at once. The herders can find no reason.
- Refugees from the Dun say their river failed last year. Their god built faster than you did: a walled city, canals, a great temple. Their wells were deeper than yours a decade ago.

**Year 25**
- You have paid the stone-people what they asked, and spoke through the Barrow door with what is inside. It said it was like you. It said the last ones came in one night too, and built high, and the Lamp drank them with the rest. It said it chose the Barrow over the floor of the Still, and that there was a third way it would not take. It said the wall you have begun to feel against your growing is the sky's, not yours.
- The Oss has lost a third of its flow at the Ford against the marks you cut in year 8.

**Year 26**
- The Lamp brightened for one night. It brightened again, out of season, the night after your canal from the Oss to the eastern fields was opened.
- Elders in the hill villages say: "There is a land with no star, and no one dies of thirst there, and no one is remembered."

**Year 27**
- A whole village of workers at the Still, celebrating the harvest, worked together to raise the lake's edge for a festival. Everyone in the village over the age of twelve cannot now recall the same twelve months, the year of the great harvest. They remember before it and after it.

**Year 28**
- The clay-bee hives near the eastern fields swarmed for the first time in living memory. The eastern fields are stripped. Everyone in the eastern hamlets has forgotten how to make the dye they were known for.
- Edge villagers in the Hem say something huge and slow walked the pale country at night, blue-lit from within. Where it passed the stone went paler and a spring they had used for generations stopped the next morning.

**Year 29**
- First hunger in the north villages. Thornwell's descendants ask for grain.
- You pressed against the wall again. You cannot now recall what you were called before you were called king. Your priests do not notice.

**Year 30**
- Divers you paid at the Still went down to the fissure at the lake's floor and came up saying there were faces hanging in the blue below, turning slowly, and hands, and a crown. One diver did not come up.

=== BLOCK 4 ===

## Years 31 to 40

**Year 31**
- The stone-people have begun taking Ossel laborers north for forty-year work in exchange for Barrow water. The laborers' families say the pay is fair.

**Year 32**
- A traveler from the far south, a people who were never in contact with the hill villages, says: "There is a land with no star. No one dies of thirst there, and no one is remembered."

**Year 33**
- The Lamp brightened for one night.
- A spring at Thornwell, dead for twenty-four years, began to run again. The same month, two springs near the Still failed.

**Year 34**
- The god you know best in the land, who built the most and pressed hardest, was seen by its own people to flicker during a judgment and to give the same ruling twice, word for word. Its people whisper that it repeats itself.

**Year 35**
- The Tallow herds, walking east for eleven years, have left the land. The last of them crossed the ring hills toward Greenhollow.

**Year 36**
- A stone-person elder, paid in a year's grain and the promise of a laborer-levy, named "the shepherds" with fear and precision. She said one sleeps in Greenhollow. She said they were once like you and are not now. She said a shepherd does not remember its name either.

**Year 37**
- Pilgrims report the Still is lower this year than any year since you came.

**Year 38**
- Your emissary returned from Greenhollow. The valley is as reported: green, two harvests, no grey anywhere, no old stories. In the valley's head, under a hill that is not a hill, something very large sleeps and the ground around it is warm. The Greenhollow people leave bread there and do not know why. Half the Ossel refugees from the east have settled in Greenhollow and will not come back.

**Year 39**
- The priest at the Oss Ford stone says the Lamp has not moved against the notch this year for the first time in twenty-one years. The herds have gone. The Still is lower.

**Year 40**
- The Lamp brightened for one night.
- The stone-people elder, pressed with what the Barrow told you, said: it has been done twice, the cutting of the ladder, at the floor of the lake, with Hem stone in quantity and every worker a land could muster, and the god who did it was as high as a god can go under that sky. She said the land stopped dying and the star went dark and hangs there still. She would not say where either land is, at any price.
- Mother Reed's successor, now very old, asked to be called by her name. No one in Oss Ford could tell her what it was.
