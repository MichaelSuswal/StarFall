# Clue Design

Rules this file obeys:
- Three-clue rule. Every conclusion has at least three independent clues of three kinds: **seen** (the god or its people directly observe it), **reported** (a mortal tells the god something they observed), **rumored** (hearsay, travelers' tales, old sayings, which may be distorted). No single clue states the answer.
- Signs reach a god only through who noticed. Every clue below names the kind of mortal who would notice it. If no such mortal exists in the god's people, the clue does not arrive until one does, or until the god goes and looks.
- Quiet opening by distance. Years 1 to 7 carry only the clues marked "quiet": things at the edge of awareness, with no cost attached yet.
- Each conclusion opens an action. The answer to a mystery is a verb.
- Earliest years assume mid fullness (31 to 70). Shift earlier by a quarter for late fullness, later by a third for early.

Conclusions run small to large. Later conclusions assume earlier ones.

## C1. The drying is not weather. Water is leaving toward the center of the land.

| Clue | Kind | How it surfaces | Earliest year | Opens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A ford the people cross every autumn is knee-deep where it was waist-deep; the rain this year was ordinary | Seen (quiet) | Herders or ferrymen remark on it during a seasonal crossing | 3 | Begin keeping a water record |
| A trader from the far edge says the lake his grandmother swam in is a reed marsh, and the village moved twice to follow it | Reported (quiet) | Arrives with trade, if the people trade outward | 4 | Send someone to look at the edge |
| The far country is grey "because the star does not love it," said as a proverb by the old | Rumored (quiet) | A wise-woman or elder, any season | 2 | None yet; a frame to test later |
| Wells in two villages a week apart both needed deepening the same spring | Reported | Well-diggers, if the people dig wells | 7 | Dig deeper, store water, move |
| At the crater lake the water is higher than ten years ago while every other water is lower | Seen | Fishermen at the center, or the god's own visit | 8 | Hypothesis: the water goes to the center |

Solvable: year 8. Verb opened: **move the people toward the center or store against the drying**, which is also the trap, since the center is where the lift happens.

## C2. The orb overhead is growing, and it and the ground glow at the center are one thing.

| Clue | Kind | How it surfaces | Earliest year | Opens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The orb no longer sits in the notch of an old standing stone it was carved to sit in | Seen (quiet) | A priest or elder who knows the stone; the god standing where they stand | 5 | Begin measuring the orb against fixed marks |
| Fishermen at the crater lake say the water is blue at night from beneath and the blue is wider than their fathers said | Reported | Fishermen, if the people fish the center | 6 | Go and look |
| "The star drinks the lake" is a child's rhyme in the lake villages | Rumored | Children, or a nurse | 4 | None yet |
| On the pulse night (the Keeper's schedule) the orb brightens and that same night the lake's blue brightens, both seen by different people | Seen + Reported, two witnesses in two places | Night-watch or shepherds for the sky; fishermen for the lake | first pulse year (3 to 11) | Hypothesis: sky and ground are connected |
| At dawn, a shimmer stands in the air directly between the orb and the lake, like heat over stone, seen only from a ridge at a certain angle | Seen | Hunters on the ridge, or the god who goes to find out | 10 | Name the column; begin to think of the star as a thing with a body |

Solvable: year 12. Verb opened: **treat the star as a physical thing that can be studied, approached, and possibly interfered with**.

## C3. Magic costs the worker a piece of self of the same kind as the working, and it is stronger near the center.

| Clue | Kind | How it surfaces | Earliest year | Opens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The people's old worker mended a child's leg and that evening did not know the child's name, though she had delivered it | Seen (quiet) | Anyone in the house; reported to the god as sad, not strange | 2 | None yet |
| A traveler says that in the lake villages the healers are young and strong and forget their own husbands | Reported | Trader or pilgrim | 6 | Compare workers by where they live |

Solvable: year 11. Verb opened: **ration or forbid magic, or use it deliberately as a lever**.

## C4. The herds walk where the star leans. A straight-walking herd means the orb is leaning toward leaving.

| Clue | Kind | How it surfaces | Earliest year | Opens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Great slow glowing beasts drink at the crater lake and nowhere else; herders know this and think nothing of it | Reported (quiet) | Herders, if the people herd near the center | 3 | None yet |
| A traveler says that in the age of her grandmother's grandmother a whole herd walked out of the land in a line and never came back, "to the fall," and then the far sky burned | Rumored | Traveler from a people with an old fall story | 8 | Watch the herds |
| A herd that has grazed in a circle for generations begins, in one season, to graze in a line, all heads one way | Seen | Herders, scouts | 16 | Record the direction; it points where the orb leans |
| On the night the herd starts walking, the orb's position against a fixed mark has shifted for the first time | Seen | Priest who keeps the stone | 16 to 18 | Hypothesis: the beasts follow the star |

Solvable: year 18. Verb opened: **read the herds as a clock for the lift; decide whether to follow them out of the land**.

## C5. A people lived here before the fall. This land is where a star came down.

| Clue | Kind | How it surfaces | Earliest year | Opens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The ring of hills around the land's center is a single curve, which a surveyor or a god from a height can see | Seen (quiet) | The god, from a mountain, or a mapmaker | 4 | Ask what made the ring |
| Diggers at the rim hills strike a black layer and under it bones and tools "of the wrong kind," which nobody wants to keep | Reported | Miners, grave-diggers, well-diggers at the rim | 7 | Dig further |
| Every people within a month's walk has a flood-and-fire story set "where the lake is now" | Rumored | Any neighbor people's elders | 5 | Compare the stories |
| Pale blind insects build hard vaults in the black layer; a broken vault shows a face in its wall in perfect detail, in a style no one makes, and the breaker dreams of it for a month | Seen | Whoever broke it; reaches the god as a wonder or a curse | 12 | Open more vaults, carefully |
| The faces in the vaults are not the faces of any people now living in the land, and in one vault, a face is the god's own, or another god's | Seen | The god, standing before it | 14 | Hypothesis: the gods were here before |

Solvable: year 14 (for the fall site) with the god's-face clue pointing forward to C7. Verb opened: **excavate the rim and the vaults; treat the pre-fall people as the god's own past to be recovered**.

## C6. Gods are made of the blue. Shielded stone cuts them off. Names hold them.

| Clue | Kind | How it surfaces | Earliest year | Opens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A god is badly wounded (any god-on-god or god-on-beast fight) and bleeds blue, and the blue runs toward the lake across level ground, and the god feels itself pulled after it for a day | Seen | The god itself, or witnesses who report it | whenever the first deep wound happens, typically 8 to 15 | Avoid wounds; or ask what the blue is |
| The stone-people's hill at the edge is said to be a tomb, yet "no god has ever entered it and come out the same," a saying among the stone-people's neighbors | Rumored | Traders who deal with the roof-makers | 9 | Go to the hill |
| At the hill's door the god feels its powers dim like a lamp in wind, and inside it cannot do what it does | Seen | The god, at the door | 13 | Learn what the hill is made of |
| A neighboring god's priests, after a generation of calling it only "the Lord of the Ford," report that it has grown slow and answers to that title and no other, and no longer remembers its own name | Reported | Travelers, or the other god's defectors | 18 | Guard your own name |
| A god cut to pieces in battle re-forms three days later at the lake shore, not where it fell | Seen or Reported | The combatants, the lake fishermen | 20 | Stop trying to kill gods; think about prisons |

Solvable: year 20. Verb opened: **guard your name, treat roofs as the only prison and the only refuge, and stop wasting years on killing gods**.

## C7. The gods were the leaders of the pre-fall people, released by the star, and will be taken back when the orb leaves.

| Clue | Kind | How it surfaces | Earliest year | Opens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The god's own face in a hive vault (from C5) | Seen | The god | 14 | Ask who that was |
| A stone-people elder says, for a price, "You are not the first. The last ones came in one night too. They built high and the star drank them with the rest. We watched from inside." | Reported | Roof-maker elder, after payment | 22 | Pay more; ask what "drank them" means |
| Every god in the land, when asked privately, arrived in the same hour and expected to find people waiting; a tally of the figures carved on the oldest rim stone equals the number of gods now in the land | Seen (the tally) + Reported (the other gods' accounts) | The god compares notes; a priest counts the stone | 16 | Hypothesis: the arrival was one event with a count |
| The bound god in the hill, if reached, says plainly what it is and what the god is, and that the lift will take them both unless they are under stone | Reported | The bound god, through the door or after release | 25 | The three verbs: refuse, ascend, cut |
| At the lake floor, reached by divers or draining or a fissure, faces and hands hang turning in the blue, and one of them is from the god's dreams | Seen | The god, at great cost | 30 | Certainty |

Solvable: year 32 (the roof-maker's testimony plus the stone count plus the hive face is enough for a careful reader; the lake floor is confirmation). Verb opened: **choose how to meet the lift: refuse (roof or name-anchor), ascend (anchor to the body), cut (sever the column), or walk the people out**.

## C8. Density speeds the fading. Every great work, every city, every working, is a payment to the star.

| Clue | Kind | How it surfaces | Earliest year | Opens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The lake villages, most crowded in the land, have the deepest wells and the youngest forgetting workers | Reported | Well-diggers, tax-counters, anyone who travels between villages | 10 | Compare density to drying |
| In the year the god raises its first great work (city wall, canal, temple), the orb pulses out of schedule, or the ford drops two hands instead of one | Seen | Priest of the stone; ferrymen | the year after the first great work | Suspicion |
| The stone-people say "the star loves a crowd" and will not build a roof over a town of more than a few hundred | Rumored then Reported | Traders, then the roof-makers directly | 15 | Ask them why |
| A neighboring god's people, who built faster, are drier; their river failed a decade before the god's own | Reported | Refugees from the neighbor | 24 | Hypothesis: the works are the cause |

Solvable: year 26. Verb opened: **choose between greatness and time: thin the people and slow the lift, or build and bring it on**.

## Pacing spine for a 60-year campaign (mid fullness)

| Beat | Year | Note |
|---|---|---|
| First sign | 3 | A shallow ford, noticed by whoever crosses it. Costs nothing. May be ignored. |
| Quiet years | 1 to 7 | Random events weighted down. Only "quiet" clues above arrive. The god's own problems (mortals, neighbors, the other gods) are the story. |
| First pulse night | first scheduled year, 3 to 11 | Two witnesses in two places. The god hears about the sky one, and the lake one only if someone fishes there. |
| First physical pressure | 9 | A spring-death at an edge village in the god's awareness. Refugees arrive. The drying has a face. |
| C1 solvable | 8 | Water goes to the center |
| C3 solvable | 11 | Magic's cost |
| C2 solvable | 12 | Sky and ground are one thing |
| C5 solvable | 14 | Fall site and pre-fall people; first hive face |
| C4 solvable | 18 | Herds read the lean |
| C6 solvable | 20 | What gods are made of, roofs, names |
| Far starfall (if rolled) | rolled d60, typical 22 | Horizon glow for a season, warm dust rains, then a decade of heavy harvests and cheap magic. The communal forgettings follow within five years wherever workers multiply. |
| River loses a third of its flow | 25 | The reclaim reaches the god's people's water directly |
| C8 solvable | 26 | Density is the price |
| First famine | 30 | Unless the god acted on C1 by year 15 |
| C7 solvable | 32 | What the gods are and what the lift does |
| Swarm pressure | 28 onward | Hive-carriers near towns swarm; crops and shared memories go |
| C9 solvable | 38 | The ascended |
| C10 solvable | 44 | Roofs, the ceiling, the cutting |
| Lift possible | not before 60 at mid fullness; 45 to 60 at late; 20 to 40 at leaning | The god knows from C4 and C2 whether it is coming |
| Endings available | 45 onward | Refuse, ascend, cut, walk out, or do nothing and be drunk with the rest |

Notes for the Keeper:
- The spine is a ceiling on speed, not a schedule. A god that never fishes the lake, never trades outward, never climbs a mountain, and never visits the stone-people's hill gets none of the seen clues and solves nothing. The costs arrive on schedule regardless.
- A god that goes looking early can pull every "earliest year" forward to the year it goes, except the ones gated by events (pulse night, first great work, the far fall, the herd's turn), which stay fixed.
- Never let a single clue state a conclusion. If a mortal would, in character, blurt the whole truth, the mortal is wrong about one load-bearing detail.
- Reported clues should pass through the reporter's own frame: the fisherman who says the star drinks the lake is right, and thinks he is being poetic.
