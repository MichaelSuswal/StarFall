# StarFall Deep Lore (Keeper only)

Keeper vocabulary used below, never spoken in play: **quick** (the blue substance), **the body** (a star's buried mass), **the orb** (a star's sky mass), **the draw** (the pull between them), **the release** (how gods arrive), **the reclaim** (the fading), **a roof** (a shielded place). Mortals have their own words for all of these and get them wrong in useful ways.

All of this is set at creation. Nothing below is ever discovered to "have always been there." The Keeper rolls the per-star table at world creation and writes the results into the world ledger before Day 1.

---

## 0. The plane and the cycle in one paragraph

The plane is infinite, flat, and old without beginning. It is not uniformly alive. Life exists in patches, each patch tended by one star. A star is a single organism with a two-part body and a very long life, and life on the plane is what a star grows. The orb mortals see in the sky is the star's upper body. Buried beneath the land it hangs over is its lower body, the fallen mass from its last descent. Between them runs the draw. Over thousands of years the lower body gives up water, warmth, and quick to the land and life swarms over the patch. Then the draw reverses and the orb slowly reclaims what was lent, taking it back up through the land. The land fades. When the orb is full and the ground is empty, the orb lets go, drifts over barren plane toward ground that has been dead longest, hangs over it for a few generations while leaking enough quick to seed it, and when the seedling life has taken, it falls. The impact kills most of what grew under it and buries the new lower body. Life rises from the crater. The cycle begins again. A starfall is both ruin and renewal because it is neither. It is a planting.

Gods are part of this. They are the star's tools for making a crop grow dense. That is the darkest truth in the game and it is a mechanism, not a villainy. The star does not think about gods or people any more than a root thinks about a worm.

## 1. Truth: What the stars are

**(a) What peoples believe.** Stars are lights hung by the first makers, or the eyes of dead kings, or holes into a brighter sky. Nearly every people has a flood or fire story about a star that fell "in the grandfathers' time" and a belief that the star overhead keeps them safe or keeps them watched. Useful because it makes mortals watch the sky and record its changes.

**(b) What investigation reveals.** The star overhead is the same object as the thing under the ground. Both glow the same blue. The orb has grown within living memory (old sightlines carved on standing stones no longer frame it). Ground glow rises at night from a region at the center of the land, strongest at the deepest crater lake. Water, over decades, moves from the land toward the ground glow region, then vanishes there. Birds and migrating beasts orient by the orb, not the sun.

**(c) What is actually true.** The star is one organism in two parts. The orb is an immense mass of quick held aloft because quick is lighter than air when it is dense and pure, and heavier than stone when it is thin and mixed with water and matter. The body is the same substance saturated with everything it has absorbed: water, warmth, soil, the fine structure of living things. The draw is a slow column of exchange between them, invisible except as a faint shimmer at dawn directly between orb and body, like heat over a road. Mortals have no word for the column. Some call that shimmer "the ladder."

**Bodies.**
- Place: the crater lake at the center of the land (keeper: the Mouth), the deepest body of water in the region, blue at night, with the body beginning a few hundred paces beneath its floor. Every campaign land has one. Also: any standing stone with a sightline carved toward the orb's old position.
- Being: cistern-beasts (section 8), who drink only at the Mouth and were born there.
- Event: the orb visibly brightening or dimming in the course of a single night (a "pulse") when the draw shifts strength. Happens on a fixed schedule from the per-star roll, not at random.

**Cost if ignored.** None on its own. This truth is the frame. Its clock is the reclaim (section 3).

## 2. Truth: What a starfall does

**(a) Belief.** A fall is the end of the world, or a gift of the gods, or a punishment. Peoples who have an old fall story disagree violently about which, and the disagreement is a real political fault line the Keeper should use. Two traditions recur across the plane: the fall is a seed (farmer peoples), the fall is a judgment (herder peoples).

**(b) Investigation.** The land's crater is a fall site. The oldest layer of human remains in the land is thin, scattered, and found under the ash layer at the crater rim, and shows a people who lived there before the fall, with their own tools, which do not match anything made now. Above the ash the record thickens fast. Life after the fall was richer and stranger than life before. Far falls can be seen from the land as a horizon glow lasting a season and a year of warm ashy rain afterward, and the following decade's harvests are heavier.

**(c) Truth.** A fall has three phases that mortals confuse into one.
1. The hang. The drifting orb stops over dead ground and leaks. Life seeds within two generations. This is the "life grew under it" of the old stories.
2. The descent. When the seedling life has reached a threshold of density (the star reads this through the quick in living things), the orb descends. Descent takes between one and five years, visible as the orb growing night by night. The last stage is a fall of hours. Those who grew under it die unless they walk away, and anything that drank enough quick to be part of the star's accounting is pulled down with it. This is why the two beings in the old story wanted to eat a falling star: certain creatures breed only inside a fresh body (section 8).
3. The garden. The buried body gives for thousands of years. The land around the crater becomes the richest on the plane.

So the question "does a fall destroy or renew" has the answer: it destroys the seed-life to plant the body, and everything that comes after is the body's gift. The seed-life is a crop grown to be ploughed under.

**Bodies.**
- Place: the crater rim, where the ash layer and the pre-fall remains can be dug. Every land has one, always within 3 to 8 years' walk of the Mouth.
- Being: the hive-carriers (section 8), whose hives are built into the ash layer and contain preserved shapes of pre-fall things.
- Event: a far starfall on the horizon. The Keeper rolls its year from the neighbor-star table. It is seen as a blue-white glow at the horizon for a season, a bright ring at night, then warm rains carrying fine blue dust, then heavy harvests for ten years.

**Cost if ignored.** The far fall's dust is quick. Wherever it lands, magic gets cheaper for a decade and more mortals try it. The costs of magic (section 4) multiply across the people. A god who does not understand why its wise-women are suddenly powerful and suddenly forgetting their children's names will lose its magical class in a generation.

## 3. Truth: The fading (the reclaim)

**(a) Belief.** Droughts come from an angry star or a failed sacrifice. The far country has always been grey. Grandmother's stories of a wide lake are exaggeration.

**(b) Investigation.** The drying is not weather. Rain still falls but the ground does not hold it. Wells deepen every decade at the same rate everywhere in the land. Water moves toward the Mouth and vanishes there. The ground glow is widening. The orb is bigger. Old high-water marks on cliffs sit a man's height above the present shore. Beasts leave. Far from the Mouth, at the land's edges, stone itself has gone pale and crumbly, colors have leached, and nothing takes a dye well.

**(c) Truth.** The draw has reversed. The body is sending quick back up and quick carries water, warmth, and fine structure with it. The reclaim takes centuries from first reversal to lift. The Keeper's per-star roll sets how far along the star is at Day 1 (the fullness roll). The fading is not uniform. It begins at the land's edges, where the body's roots are thinnest, and closes toward the Mouth. The Mouth is the last water in the land to go. Peoples therefore crowd toward the center over generations, which is exactly where the orb will lift from and exactly where the new seedling life will be ploughed under if the orb ever came back down here, which it will not for ten thousand years. The real danger is the lift: when the body is empty the orb detaches, the ground glow goes out in a single night, every spring in the land fails inside a year, and the quick in living things thins until magic is impossible and gods are pulled up with the orb (section 5).

Density of life speeds the reclaim. Cities, irrigation, metal, cattle, writing, all of it concentrates quick in living pattern, and the orb takes from concentration faster than from scatter. A god who builds a great civilization is building a faster lift.

**Bodies.**
- Place: the land's edges, where the pale country begins. Called by mortals the Grey, the Hem, the Bone Country, whatever fits the people. A god who walks there sees a cliff with high-water marks, a dead forest standing without rot, a village of standing houses with no one in them and no bones. Also the Mouth itself, where the water goes.
- Being: the roof-makers (section 8), who shield pockets of land from the draw and have outlived three reclaims.
- Event: a spring-death. A named spring the people have used for generations stops in a single season. The Keeper places the first spring-death between year 6 and year 12 depending on fullness, in a settlement at the edge of the god's awareness, and reports it through whoever noticed.

**Cost if ignored.** The reclaim clock. Per-star fullness roll sets its pace; at mid-fullness the god's river loses a third of its flow by year 25 and the first famine comes by year 30. At high fullness the lift itself can fall inside a 60-year campaign (section 10 for rolling it). Actions opened at each layer: (a) sacrifice and rain-prayer, which do nothing but keep people together; (b) move the people toward the Mouth, dig deeper wells, store water, which buys decades; (c) starve the star by thinning the people's density, build a roof, bargain with an ascended to steer the drift, or cut the draw at the Mouth (section 9).

## 4. Truth: What magic is

**(a) Belief.** Magic is a gift of the star, or of spirits, or of the dead. It is inherited, or it comes to those who sleep at the Mouth. Wise-women forget things because they are old.

**(b) Investigation.** Magic is stronger near ground glow and after far-fall dust. Every worker of magic pays in self: a memory, a sense, a color, the ability to recognize a face. The payment is in the same category as the working. Heal a bone and lose a childhood year. Call rain and lose the taste of water. Mend a pot and forget your mother's hands. Workers who work often go grey early, not in hair but in the way they stand and speak, and the very old ones no longer remember their names and answer to whatever they are called. The worker's cost and the land's fading look alike because they are alike.

**(c) Truth.** Quick carries pattern. The body is a library of every shape that has lived in the land, held as structure in the substance. A mortal with the knack pulls a shape out of ambient quick and presses it into the world: an unbroken bone, a full cloud, an unbroken pot. The star's accounting is strict. Pattern pressed into the world is paid for with pattern taken from the worker, which goes up the draw. Magic is a small local reclaim. Every working feeds the orb. A land of many sorcerers lifts sooner.

Magic is everywhere because quick is everywhere, in trace. It is never ubiquitous because the knack is a matter of how much quick a person's own body holds, which is partly birth and partly where they grew up and what they drank. Children raised at the Mouth have the knack more often. Children raised under a roof never do.

God power is innate and separate. A god does not borrow pattern. A god is pattern, a self-holding knot of quick. Gods never pay the worker's cost for their powers. They pay a different cost (section 9).

**Bodies.**
- Place: the Mouth, where workers are made and where a working done at the water's edge is visibly stronger and leaves the water lower.
- Being: a nameless old worker in the people's own land, who has forgotten her name and answers to a title, and who can still work and does not know why she forgets. Every campaign land has one such within the god's people or a neighbor's.
- Event: a far-fall dust decade (section 2), or more locally, a working gone large: a worker calls rain and a village's whole memory of one year goes with it. Everyone over a certain age cannot recall the same twelve months.

**Cost if ignored.** The god's magical class erodes and the people's memory erodes. Specific trigger: when the number of active workers in the people exceeds a threshold set by the Keeper (roughly one in two hundred), communal forgettings begin within five years. Actions opened: (a) honor and fear the workers; (b) ration magic, protect workers from themselves, forbid working at the Mouth; (c) use magic deliberately as a lever on the reclaim (forbid it to slow the lift, or encourage it to bring on something you want sooner), or teach workers to pay with the god's own pattern instead, which is a power past the ceiling (section 9).

## 5. Truth: Where the gods came from and why they arrive

**(a) Belief.** The gods came from the sky, from beyond the stars, from the first makers. They came because the people deserved them, or because the people were about to be tested. Gods have always existed. A god's foggy past is divine mystery.

**(b) Investigation.** All the gods arrived in the same hour. All of them expected to find people to lead. None remembers more than fragments, and the fragments are of ruling, judging, building, and losing. A god wounded badly enough bleeds blue and the blue runs toward the Mouth. A god inside a roof is weak as a strong mortal and can be held there. The hive-carriers' preserved shapes include faces, and some gods' faces are among them, in a pre-fall style. The oldest standing stones at the crater rim carry a count of figures that matches the number of gods now in the land.

**(c) Truth.** The gods are the released. When the body of this star received its last fall, everything in the seed-life that held enough quick was pulled down with it: the beasts bred for it, and the leaders of the seed-life's people, the ones who had drunk most deeply from the leak as kings, judges, priests, and sorcerers. Their pattern was held in the body for thousands of years, dissolving slowly. Memory went first. The knot of self went last and did not finish dissolving. When the body's reclaim passes a threshold, the star releases those knots up the draw and into the air above the land, where they re-form and fall. From inside the knot this is The Between: a long descent from a place you cannot remember toward a world you do not know. The release always lands the gods in the same hour, scattered over the land. The gods expect a people because they were rulers once, under another sky, and the shape of ruling survived when the content did not.

Why does the star release them? Because a released knot gravitates to people and makes them dense. Gods build cities, raise armies, order irrigation, demand monuments, teach writing. Density speeds the reclaim. The star does not intend this. It is simply that stars whose bodies released their knots early have lifted sooner and planted more often across the endless plane, and so the plane is full of stars that do. The gods are a selected trait. They are the star's hands, and they were once the previous crop's best heads.

The wound table in the god rubric is the surviving shape of the god's real past life. Wound 12 ("it remembers a sky that fell") is literal. Wound 3 ("worshipped and then forgotten") is the dissolution in the body. Wound 8 ("servant of a greater thing and fled") is a god that half-knows.

Whether the gods were ever human in the modern sense is deliberately unanswered: the pre-fall remains at the crater rim are human-shaped but with differences the Keeper rolls per star (section 10). The gods' forms (rubric section 4) are what pattern does when it re-forms without flesh to remember.

**Bodies.**
- Place: the Mouth's floor. A god who descends into the crater lake and below its floor (through a fissure, with mortal divers' help, or by draining it, each a campaign in itself) reaches the body's upper surface and sees, suspended in the blue, the unfinished knots that were not released: faces, hands, a crown, slowly turning. Some are fresher than others. One or two will be recognizable from the god's own foggy dreams.
- Being: a roof-maker elder who witnessed the previous release, two thousand years ago, and remembers what those gods did and how it ended. Also: the ascended (section 7), who know exactly what they are.
- Event: a god's own deep wound, when the blue runs out of it toward the Mouth and the god is drawn after it for a day before the knot re-forms. Any god badly wounded in the first fifteen years experiences this. The Keeper does not explain it.

**Cost if ignored.** The lift (section 3) takes the gods with it. A god who does not know what it is cannot refuse. Actions opened: (a) seek your origin as a divine mystery, through prophecy, which gets fragments right; (b) stop feeding the star blindly, and understand that every great work is a payment; (c) three verbs: refuse (anchor yourself in a roof or in a people who hold your name, and remain when the orb lifts, as a god over a dead land), ascend (go up willingly and become part of the star's next planting, section 7), or cut (break the draw at the Mouth, section 9). None of these is the right one.

## 6. Truth: Why gods cannot die

**(a) Belief.** Gods are immortal because they are gods. A dead god is a story told by the god's enemies.

**(b) Investigation.** A god cut to pieces re-forms within days, closer to the Mouth than where it fell. A god inside a roof weakens to a strong mortal and heals slowly. A god nobody names for a long time grows thin, slow, and vague, and answers to the wrong name. Gods inside roofs can be held indefinitely.

**(c) Truth.** A god is a knot of quick, and quick does not dissipate under the star's own sky; the ambient leak re-supplies the knot and the body re-forms it. Death is not available because the body holds a copy. Five things are available. Wounding: the knot frays and takes time to re-form; a badly wounded god is weak for a season. Imprisonment: a roof cuts the leak, so a god inside one cannot re-form and cannot draw; the roof-makers have held gods for centuries this way. Exile: outside the land, under another star, the god's knot is slowly rewritten by a different body's accounting and the god changes nature over decades (this is the one way a god's rubric rolls can shift). Cutting off: a god who is severed from the draw by a mortal working of great size (it has been done twice in the plane's history that the roof-makers know of) is a knot with no re-supply; it survives but cannot heal and cannot grow. Forgetting: names are pattern, and a god whose name is held in many living minds is re-formed faster and holds its ceiling better; a forgotten god is re-formed slowly and sinks toward the Mouth over years. "Forgotten" is a condition gods fear for a physical reason.

**Bodies.**
- Place: a roof (section 8). The people's land has at least one, often mistaken for a tomb or a sealed hill.
- Being: a bound god. Every campaign has one, held in a roof by the roof-makers or by the ascended or by a previous age's mortals, from the previous release or the one before. It is sane, strange, patient, and knows everything in sections 1 to 5. Freeing it is one of the campaign's great choices.
- Event: a god's re-forming after a battle, witnessed by mortals, who report it as a miracle. Or a god's thinning, when its own priests begin calling it by a title and cannot recall the name.

**Cost if ignored.** The trigger is any god-on-god violence: a god who does not understand re-forming wastes years on a war that cannot be won by killing, and the only victory in such a war, imprisonment, is one the roof-makers will offer to broker, at a price. A god who does not guard its name loses its ceiling (section 9).

## 7. Truth: What the ascended are

**(a) Belief.** Some gods became greater by virtue or sacrifice and now live in the sky or beyond it. They can be prayed to. They are kind or cruel according to the people telling the story.

**(b) Investigation.** Herds change course in a single season as if driven. The far-fall glow a decade ago fell exactly where a traveler's people had just abandoned a dying land, which suggests falls are aimed. Something enormous and slow was seen walking the Grey at night, blue-lit from within, and the pale country where it passed went paler. Roof-makers refer to "the shepherds" with fear and precision and will not say more without payment.

**(c) Truth.** A god reaches the ceiling (section 9) and finds its knot fraying at every attempt to grow. The ascended are gods who stopped trying to be a larger knot and anchored instead. There are two ways. One is to descend into the body and bind the knot to it. The other is to go up the draw during a lift and bind to the orb. Either way the god ceases to be a person in the usual sense and becomes a function of the star: a body-anchored ascended is a steward of the garden, able to direct where the leak goes and so where life flourishes in the land, and it walks the land as a slow blue giant, mostly asleep, waking when the star's accounting changes. An orb-anchored ascended rides the star through its drift and can bias where it falls. They are the closest thing the plane has to intent inside the cycle, and they are not united; each has what is left of a personality, with the virtue and flaw of its rubric rolls amplified and its memory of being a person almost gone. They do not remember their names. They remember a single purpose each, which is the rubric's domain roll grown huge.

An ascended can mentor. This is one of the three ways a god learns a new power. It will do so for a price, and the price is always a step toward anchoring, because an ascended's only remaining drive is for the star's cycle to go well, and a god who anchors serves the cycle.

The number of ascended is small. One per star at most within walking distance, often zero. Roll per star.

**Bodies.**
- Place: where the ascended sleeps. For a body-anchored one, a valley in the land where nothing fades and the harvests are impossible and the people there have no stories older than their grandparents. For an orb-anchored one, there is no place; it is seen only at a far fall or at the lift.
- Being: the ascended itself. It can be found, spoken to, fought (uselessly), bargained with, and learned from.
- Event: the steering. Herds turn in a season. A spring at the edge revives while two at the center fail. The leak has been moved by hand.

**Cost if ignored.** An ascended's steering ignores the god's people. If the ascended is body-anchored, its valley thrives while the god's river dies, and the god's people migrate toward it and become the ascended's people. Clock: begins when the reclaim first touches the god's river. Actions opened: (a) worship the far god; (b) find the valley and understand it is being chosen over you; (c) bargain (trade a step toward anchoring for a decade of water), fight (bind it in a roof, which requires the roof-makers), or follow it (anchor yourself and become a steward alongside it, which is the ascension ending).

## 8. Truth: The long-game creatures

Three lineages have evolved to the cycle rather than to the land. They are not allies of each other. All three exist in every campaign land, in numbers set by the per-star roll.

### 8.1 Cistern-beasts
Large, slow, long-lived grazers, the size of oxen to the size of houses, with bodies that store quick in a bladder along the spine, glowing faintly through the hide at night. They drink only at the Mouth. They breed only inside a fresh body, which means they breed once per cycle, at a fall, and the herd must be present at the fall site to do it. So the herd has evolved to read the star: they feel the draw's strength and the orb's drift and begin walking toward where the orb will fall generations before it falls, following the orb's slow lean. The two beings who wanted to eat a falling star in the old story were cistern-beasts. A herd walking in a straight line out of the land is the earliest reliable sign that the orb is leaning toward a lift. A herd that stops walking has felt the lean end. Mortals herd them for their hide and the blue in their bladders, which is quick and can be sold to workers at terrible cost to the buyer.
- Belief: holy oxen, or omens, or just meat.
- Investigation: they go where the star goes.
- Truth: they are the star's own livestock, bred to carry its seed to the next fall. A herd that reaches a fall site and is ploughed under seeds the new body with their pattern, which is why cistern-beasts are the first life out of every crater.
- Cost: herds cross the people's fields on their walk and nothing stops them. A people in the herd's path loses a harvest.

### 8.2 Hive-carriers
Colony insects, finger-long, pale, blind, that build hives in the ash layer at crater rims and in the clay around the Mouth. Their hives are vaults of hardened secretion in which they preserve shapes: a leaf, a hand, a face, a tool, pressed into the hive wall in perfect detail and held for thousands of years. When a body is drawn up, the hive-carriers go up with it, inside the orb, dormant. When the orb falls, they hatch and begin preserving. They are the plane's memory across falls, not because they mean to be but because they build from what is nearby and the quick in their secretion holds structure. Their hives contain pre-fall faces. Some faces belong to the gods. Mortals who break open a hive breathe the dust and dream the preserved shapes for weeks. The swarm, when disturbed or when the draw strengthens, moves and feeds on living pattern: a swarmed village loses crops and the villagers lose the same memories.
- Belief: tomb-builders, ghost-bees, the dead's craftsmen.
- Investigation: the hives hold things from before the fall, including people who look like the gods.
- Truth: a mechanism that carries pattern across the cycle. The only "documents" of the previous age are hive walls, and they are not documents; they are a place and a being.
- Cost: the swarm clock. The swarms grow with the draw. By mid-campaign a hive near a town is a famine and a forgetting.

### 8.3 Roof-makers
A non-human people, the only one that has survived three reclaims in place. Short, heavy, slow of speech, long-lived (four centuries), few (one settlement per land, two at most, each a few hundred). They learned long ago that dense stone of the right kind, mined from the Grey where the draw has already leached it of quick, blocks the draw. They build roofs: hills of leached stone over pockets of land, under which water does not go, magic does not work, gods go weak, and the reclaim never comes. Under their roofs they have kept gardens, animals, and themselves through the lift of two previous stars and the fall of this one, which they watched from inside. They are not wise and they are not kind. They are survivors with a trade: they sell roof-space and bound gods, and they will build a roof for a people who pay, which takes forty years. They know what the gods are and they say it only for a price. They have held gods in roofs as currency since before this fall.
- Belief: stone-dwarfs, tomb-folk, a people who worship stone.
- Investigation: their hills are not tombs, water runs around them and never under, nothing inside fades, and a god feels weak at the door.
- Truth: the one thing on the plane that the cycle cannot take, built by craft, not magic. Roofs are also prisons for gods, and the roof-makers have the only key.
- Cost: they take mortals as labor for forty-year builds and they bind gods who threaten them. Clock: when the god's people first approach a roof for water, the roof-makers set a price, and the price rises with the reclaim.

## 9. Truth: The power ceiling and what is past it

**(a) Belief.** Gods grow as their people grow. Great worship, great power, forever.

**(b) Investigation.** Past a certain point every new power frays something: the god forgets, flickers, re-forms wrong for a day. Mortals notice their god repeating itself. Other gods in the land hit the same wall around the same decade. A bound god in a roof says, when asked, that the wall is the sky's and not the god's.

**(c) Truth.** A god is a self-holding knot of quick in an ambient of thinner quick. The knot can hold only so much pattern before the ambient's accounting begins to take from it, exactly as it takes from a worker. The ceiling is set by the ambient density of the land, which is set by the star's fullness and leak, which the Keeper rolls per world. On a ten-step scale of god power, the ceiling falls between 6 and 8, rolled once per world and never revealed. A god pressing past the ceiling pays in self: memory, name, a rubric trait. Each attempt above the ceiling costs one permanent thing. A god at the ceiling for a long time stays there safely. Growth up to the ceiling comes three ways, all set at creation: a mentor (an ascended, a bound god, a roof-maker elder with a stolen trick), a realization (reaching layer (c) of any truth above opens one power tied to it), or a witnessed example (seeing another god, an ascended, or a great working do something the god did not know could be done).

Past the ceiling is anchoring, which is not more power but a change of kind. Steps 9 and 10 belong to the ascended. Step 9: anchored to the body, steward of where life goes in the land. Step 10: anchored to the orb, able to lean the drift. An anchored god has given up being a person and gives up its name entirely. There is a cheaper, uglier path past the ceiling which the bound god knows: pay with other people's pattern. A god who learns to route the accounting through its worshippers can grow past the ceiling and the worshippers pay with memory, exactly as workers do. This is the tyrant's ascension and it is available in every campaign and it works. It is also what the star wants, since it accelerates the reclaim.

**Bodies.**
- Place: the wall itself, met wherever the god attempts its next growth. And the Mouth's floor, where anchoring is done.
- Being: the bound god, who hit the wall and chose a roof over anchoring, and the ascended, who chose the other way.
- Event: another god in the land visibly fraying and re-forming wrong after an overreach, witnessed by the god or reported by its people.

**Cost if ignored.** A god that keeps pushing loses its rubric piece by piece until it is a title with power and no self; the Keeper tracks this. Actions opened: (a) seek worship as the path to power; (b) recognize the wall and stop, and seek mentors and examples for lateral growth instead; (c) anchor, route through worshippers, or cut the draw. Cutting the draw: a working at the Mouth of enormous size, requiring a god at the ceiling, the roof-makers' leached stone in quantity, and hundreds of workers who will each pay everything, that severs the column. The land stops fading. Magic in the land dies within a generation. The gods in the land become cut-off knots that cannot heal or grow. The orb above goes dark and hangs, dead, forever. The land is saved and becomes the only place on the plane with no star, which the roof-makers say has been done twice and will not say where. This is an ending.

## 10. Per-star variation

Roll these at world creation. Write results into the ledger. Neighbor stars are rolled as the home star's values shifted by a small delta, with the delta growing with distance.

| Property | Range | Roll | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fullness | 1 to 100 | d100 | How far the reclaim has run. 1 to 30: early, fading only at the far edges, lift not possible in 60 years. 31 to 70: middle, river losses by year 20 to 30, first famine 25 to 35. 71 to 90: late, lift possible in years 45 to 60 if density is high. 91 to 100: the orb is already leaning; lift in years 20 to 40 regardless. |
| Leak (magic density) | 1 to 100 | d100 | Sets how common the knack is (1 in 2000 at 10, 1 in 150 at 90) and how fast the swarm clock runs. |
| Ceiling | 6, 7, or 8 | d3 + 5 | The god power cap. Never revealed. |
| Hunger | 1 to 100 | d100 | How strongly density speeds the reclaim. At high hunger a city of ten thousand advances fullness by a point a decade. |
| Pulse schedule | every 3 to 11 years | d9 + 2 | Years in which the orb visibly brightens for a night. Fixed from Day 1. |
| Tint | pale ice to deep violet-blue | d6 | Cosmetic, but neighbors share tint, so travelers' descriptions of "a greener star" are evidence of distance. |
| Pre-fall people | d6 | d6 | 1 to 3 human as now; 4 taller with long skulls; 5 smaller with six fingers; 6 not human-shaped at all. Determines what the crater-rim bones and hive faces look like, and so what the gods see of themselves. |
| Cistern-beast herds | 0 to 3 | d4 - 1 | Zero means the walking sign is absent and the lift comes with less warning. |
| Hive sites | 1 to 4 | d4 | Always at least one at the rim. |
| Roofs | 1 or 2 | d2 | At least one, always, since the bound god needs a prison. |
| Bound gods | 1 or 2 | d2 | From which release: d2, this star's previous release (2,000 to 4,000 years ago) or the one before. |
| Ascended nearby | 0 or 1 | d2 - 1 | If 1, roll d2 for body-anchored (present in the land) or orb-anchored (present only at lift or far fall). |
| Neighbor stars | 2 to 4 within sight | d3 + 1 | For each: distance 100 to 300 years' walk (d3 x 100), fullness as home ± d20, phase check: if neighbor fullness over 95, it lifts and falls within the campaign; roll the far-fall year d60. |
| Gods released | equals player gods plus NPC gods | fixed by setup | Always the same hour. |

Hard rules: no two stars within sight share all of fullness, tint, and pulse schedule. Neighbors never differ in tint by more than one step. Every land has exactly one Mouth and one crater rim.

## 11. What must stay mysterious across campaigns

- Whether the stars think. The Keeper never confirms or denies. The ascended believe they do and the roof-makers believe they do not and both have evidence.
- Why the plane does this at all. There is no origin of the cycle. "Endless in time" is literal.
- What is past step 10. Orb-anchored ascended that have ridden through many falls stop speaking. Something happens to them. The Keeper does not know and does not invent it.
- Where the two severed lands are. The roof-makers know and never say. No campaign may be set there.
- Whether a released god is the same person as the pre-fall leader whose knot it was, or only the shape of one. Two gods can look at the same hive face and reach opposite conclusions, and both are defensible.
- What happens to a god who is exiled far enough that it passes out of every star's sky. It has never been reported back.

## 12. What may be fully solved in one campaign

- What the orb and the body are and that they are one thing.
- What the fading is, where the water goes, and that density speeds it.
- What magic is and what it costs and that it feeds the star.
- Where the gods came from, why they arrived together, and what the lift will do to them.
- Why gods re-form, what a roof does, and what forgetting does.
- What the three long-game lineages are doing and why.
- That the ceiling is the sky's and not the god's, and that there are three ways past it (anchor, route through others, cut the draw), each with a known price.
- Whether this star will lift within the god's horizon, from the signs.
- A full ending: anchor, refuse and remain, cut the draw, or walk the people out of the land toward a neighbor star, which takes a century and is a different campaign.
