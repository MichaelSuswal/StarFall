# God 1: Sill

## God sheet

**Name it calls itself:** Sill. "The bottom of a doorway. You step over me and don't look down."

**Domain:** thresholds and doors.

**Form:** a tall woman, plain-faced, dressed in undyed wool, human in every way but one: her hands are always wet. Water runs off her fingers onto any floor she stands on and never dries, never pools, never leaves a mark once she has gone.

**Bearing:** she is almost never seen, and when she is seen she is standing in a doorway, never in a room. She does not knock and does not lock. No door closes to her, and she will not put a bolt on anything she makes. She is patient to the point of stillness and will wait a lifetime in one frame. She is strong, not endless; she lifts gates off hinges rather than climbing walls. She likes company and keeps it the way a doorframe keeps a crowd, present at every gathering and remembered by no one. She trusts easily and openly, which has not gone well for her. She wants little for herself. What she wants is to be looked at, and the hunger for that is the only hunger she has. She envies the gods who stand in daylight and is honest enough to say so. Her curiosity is the engine: she opens every door to learn what is behind it. Her envy is the brake: she stays to want what she finds there instead of leaving.

**The work she falls into without choosing it:** sitting with those who are about to cross over, washing what they will wear, saying plainly that they will not come back. She does not decide who dies. She waits at the last door and keeps it unlocked.

**The wound she feels and cannot explain:** the sound of a bolt sliding home makes her stand very still. She cannot make a thing without feeling it will turn and shut her out. She has made nothing with a face in longer than she can recall, and she does not remember why she stopped.

**Voice:** blunt as a hammer. Short declaratives. No softening.

**Traits she names for herself:**
1. Nothing Stays Shut.
2. Last One in the Room.

## Survey answers, in character

**1. Mood or image.** The hour before dawn when the boots are laced and nothing is decided yet. Cold floor. One door standing open to the dark and no one has looked through it.

**2. Land.** River country cut by fords and mountain passes. Land that has its own doors. I want places a traveler must step through, not walk around.

**3. Seasons.** Hard winter, real snow, roads that close. Wake me on the first thaw day, when the ice breaks on the ford and the pass opens. That is a threshold and I want to stand on it.

**4. Past the borders.** Old kingdoms, walled, with gates. Wild country has nothing to open. Give me walls and I will find the way in.

**5. Age of the human story.** Ruins under ruins. Thousands of years. I want doors so old nobody remembers what they were shut against.

**6. How much magic.** A wise-woman in every valley, but weak. Enough to find lost things and ease a death. Not enough to break a gate.

**7. What magic feels like and costs.** It feels like cold water on the hands. Every working leaves the worker a little harder to notice. Do it enough and your own family stops seeing you at table. That is the cost. Nobody gets it back.

**8. How much a god stands out.** Barely more than a strong sorcerer. A god should be able to stand in a doorway and be taken for a servant. Make it so the mortals have to earn knowing us.

**9. How strange the wild.** Strange past the passes, familiar inside them. Wolves and deer in the valleys. Things with no names in the high country, and they do not come down unless someone opens the way.

**10. What mortals fear.** The thing you let in. Not the monster outside, the one you opened the door to because it knocked politely. Every good story here should start with a door that should have stayed shut, and I will have opened half of them.

**11. What is scarce.** A dry room with a door that holds. Shelter is the fight. Timber is poor, stone is far, and good hinges cost more than a cow. People will kill for a roof and a bar across the door.

**12. Buried.** Sealed doors. Stone ones, set into hillsides, with no handles and nothing written on them. Put them all over. I will find out what is behind each one.

**13. Other peoples.** Travelers from far away, few in number, and they brought the lock. Before them nobody here barred anything. Now every village has a locksmith and I do not like him.

**14. How gods recognize each other.** A pull at any distance, direction only, no name and no face. I will know where you are. You will not know it is me until I am already in the doorway.

**15. Sworn words between gods.** Worth exactly as much as the god who gave it. No force. I will keep mine anyway. That is my business and not the world's.

**16. The mortal worth following.** A deserter who turns around. One who walked out the door of the battle, got clear, and then went back through it knowing what was on the other side. I will wash her armor myself.

**17. What a god can discover.** A door that opens onto wherever we fell from. Let there be exactly one, and let it be hard to find, and let nobody know what it costs to go through.

**18. One wish not yet dared.** That someone opens a door for me. Once. Instead of the other way.

## Out of character note

Presence 98 against Voice 5 was the hardest pair: a god that hides but speaks like a hammer. I resolved it by making her rare and loud, visible only in doorframes and blunt when cornered. Order 98 (freedom) with a domain of doors was the second knot, resolved as a god who refuses locks rather than one who guards passage. Appetite 18 (content) against Flaw envy needed care: she wants nothing except to be seen. The survey suits her well, but questions 2, 3 and 11 assume a god with a homeland and a settled economy, which a hidden, wandering threshold god barely has. Question 13 blurs two different asks (non-human peoples versus foreign travelers) and should be split.
