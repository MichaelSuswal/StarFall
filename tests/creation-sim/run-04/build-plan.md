## Shared specifications

- **No approval gates (58).** In a campaign, no step waits on the owner. A ruling the files do not settle is made by the Keeper (or the checker, at the lock) by the judgment order in `keeper.md` (fairness, above all to the player's original intent; fit with the power as first written; balance), applied, and logged in the keeper log under "Rulings for review"; the agent never stops for it. The lock never pauses for a ruling: a flag only an owner could clear is ruled by the checker, recorded in the lock flags for review, and the lock goes on. Image approvals come from `state/images.md`, whose play lines are written when the campaign starts. The player, not Michael, gives the go that starts a campaign; `CLAUDE.md`'s run exception covers the whole campaign once started, play days and nights included. The overnight scheduled task runs with automatic approval (a note in `engine/night/scheduled-prompt.md` and the README for whoever enables it). Changes to the engine or design, and spending beyond `images.md`, still need the owner. Test runs keep the same rule: a test run's rulings are logged for review and never paused for, except a ruling Michael has asked to make himself.
- **Luck and DM rolls (66).** The DM rolls in the open as now, with stated odds. The Keeper, resolving the year, may overrule an outcome by the sealed factors, as a hidden stat would, and logs it in the sealed log only. Nothing public names it. The sealed file `SPOILERS_do_not_open/keeper/sealed-factors.md` says how and how often.
- **The unsaid answer, private (13, 30, 32).** On Day 2 (and any day the DM asks something sealed) the DM asks the unsaid question last and tells the player in (oog) to answer it in a separate reply headed `SEALED:`. The orchestrator (or, in a live chat, the DM itself) writes that reply straight to `S/player/unsaid.md` and leaves only "(One more answer was given privately, for the sealed record.)" in the transcript. When a sealed answer qualifies an open conduct line, the open line carries the plain mark "check with the player before acting", with no reason.
- **What the people know (46).** A public, DM-readable file `campaigns/<name>/civs/<people>/knowledge.md`: what the player's people know of their own land and its neighbors, by the names they use, in plain prose, with grid cells and no map ids, written by the Keeper on Night 3a from the map and the people's record, and updated by the Keeper each year in pass two. It is not the god's map knowledge: the god learns a name in play only when a mortal says it. The leak scan treats names in this file as told to the people, not to the god (they may appear in this file and in the DM's mouth for mortals).
- **NPC landing releases (56).** At Year 1 setup the night turn gives each NPC god its landing release (`S/releases/000/<label>-landing.md`, written on Night 3b) as part of its first release.
- **Underground (68, 67, 9).** Night 1 decides whether the world has an underground (a 1.5 checkbox, from Q12 and the pool). If it has none, there is no underground sheet. If it has one, Night 3a writes it into `map.json` as a layer (id `under-N`, depth, features `uNN`, optional regions) before any painting, and the request asks for the layer as it is in the data: solid rock with the hollows named as the only features when it is mostly solid. The Keeper checks the painting against the data; where they differ, the data rules and the player's sheet shows only known hollows. `tools/map.py validate` fails a world whose history or placement plan gives an underground that `map.json` lacks.
- **Mirror mode (run 03 item 94).** At the start of Day 1, before the descent, the DM asks in (oog): "Would you like to play mirror-mode, or in standard mode?", with one plain sentence on each: in mirror mode, a few of the god's smaller gifts are drawn from the player as a person; in standard mode, nothing is. The answer is recorded in `player/survey-day1.md`. Mirror mode never sets the god's form, core, name or the powers the player chooses. In mirror mode: the DM, which alone can reach the player's chat history and memory, reads what it can (memory files and a chat search, never health, money, relationships or anything sensitive) and writes a short sealed `S/player/mirror.md`: the person's small skills, habits and knacks it found, each with its source, and nothing sensitive. If there is too little, the DM asks the mirror survey at the end of Day 1 (about ten questions about the person, not the god: what you are good at without trying, what you put off, how you solve a problem, what your hands like to do, a small fear, a habit you would not admit, and the like), answered privately as a `SEALED:` reply. On Night 2 the Keeper draws the god's two hidden powers and two or three small innate knacks from `mirror.md` (in standard mode, from the world and the card as now), all sealed, found only in play. On Day 2 the player still chooses abilities: minor ones as now, greater ones priced. Test runs give the stand-in a short fictional person file (`tests/creation-sim/run-NN/person.md`) to be mirrored, read by the DM as if it were the player's history.
- **Stand-in brief (1, 37).** The next run's stand-in brief names a different kind of player (a builder, a conqueror, or a trader), keeping Michael's style only in how it plays the table. One NPC god may be given a forced lean that invites refusal, by the test override.
