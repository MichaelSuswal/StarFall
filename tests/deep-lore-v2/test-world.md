# Test world: Kessvale under the Low Star

Keeper file for an interactive test of deep-lore v2 and clue-design v2. One star, one region, one player god ruling a river valley of about 5,000. Everything below is fixed at creation. Nothing is added after this file locks.

## Per-star rolls

Phase 88 (late). Ceiling 76. Rain density 3 (a knotter in a few villages, a real tradition in the south). Living radius 90 days' walk from the center. Center body 1 (a lake). Long-game presence 6 (hoarder, carriers, thinners all present). Remainder 5 (fell in godless country). Star content 3 (a country of cities on stilts over a shallow sea; this shapes the ascended dreams: they count on long legs, standing in water). Ascended temper 3 (mixed).

Player god arrives at power 20 on the 100 scale. Oldmark arrives at 20. Hask arrives at 20.

## Names of the star

Kessvale: the Low Star, or just the Star. Sellen: the Eye. Ostrel: the Hook. Harrock: the Lamp. Shorefolk: no name; they point up.

## The map in words

Stand in Dorrin, the chief town, 1,400 people, on the north bank of the Kess where the river widens enough for a mill pond and a ferry. The willow walk runs along the bank west of the ferry. The elders meet there.

West and upriver, three days along the Kess, is Brae, 600 people, wool country, with a sulfur spring above it. Old Tamsin the knotter lives there with her apprentice Wenna. Beyond Brae the Kess comes down out of the Tallow Range, high, snow-topped, pink at dusk. The Tallow passes are hard and lead to nothing anyone wants. Fifty days west over the Tallow the land goes general; this is the nearest Pale, and it is why the woman from Brae who went west looking for her brother came back unable to say her village's name.

North of Dorrin, two days up a tributary called the Hollin, is Hollin Ford, 500 people, with the stony ford where children do their first crossing, and above it the high pasture where the shepherds summer and where, on dim nights, a shepherd can see the whole northwest sky. Four days further north, the Hollin rises out of Lake Hollow. On the lake's south shore is Cold Harbour, 150 people, the god's smallest and farthest village, fishers who once took the Hollow char and now take perch. Lake Hollow is a day across and is the same breadth it was on the oldest map, and it is shallower everywhere, and it is no longer cold. On the lake's east shore, a half day from Cold Harbour, are the Rings: concentric earth mounds, standing stones, a sighting pair with a notched third stone that now frames a hand's breadth of sky above the Low Star. Under the Rings, a god who digs will find older foundations of a different plan, and under those a layer of blue-grey glass that rings when struck, and set in the glass a single intact stone bearing a name.

Northwest of Dorrin, twelve days by the shepherds' track over the shoulder of the Combs and down, is the Still. The star hangs directly over it. The Still is a lake smaller than Lake Hollow, perfectly round, that does not freeze, does not ripple, and is warm at the bottom. It is the hoarder. On its shore live the Shorefolk, about 300, who have words for everything but each other. On dim nights the thread of blue light comes down from the star and touches the water, and the water does not steam.

East of Dorrin, one day, is Saltmeet, 450 people, where the Hollin's smaller sister stream joins the Kess and where there is a salt lick deer come to at dusk. Beyond Saltmeet the Combs begin: long grass hills, east wind, rock cats. Three days into the Combs is Quarry, 250 people, white stone, swallows, bees. Two days north of Quarry at the Combs' foot is Threnn, 550 people, the border village, with its old oak and its bowl on the wall for the Harrock.

The Harrock clans, about 1,500 in all, godless, hold the Combs east of Threnn and Quarry. They herd, raid, and sometimes trade. Ten days east of Dorrin, in Harrock country, is Cairn Hollow, a dry valley where a warm blue-grey stone the size of a cart fell on the night the gods arrived. The Harrock cairned it. They sleep beside it before raids.

Twenty-two days east of Dorrin, over the Combs and down into the long grass beyond, are the Sellen, 3,000 herders in moving camps around a fixed stone meeting ground. Their god is Oldmark.

South of Dorrin, two days downriver, is Wick, 500 people, reed beds, marsh lights, the heron they call the Judge. South of Wick the Kess spreads into a wide shallow marsh and then into Ninefield, 600 people, nine stone-walled fields and three apple strains, the last farms before the land goes flat and white. South of Ninefield, a day's walk, is a bare circle where grass grows without seed heads, which the Ostrel once made. Beyond that is the salt plain, where the sea was and where it is no longer anything, with no shells in it, and pale herds moving on it in the distance, all one color.

Thirty-eight days south of Dorrin, at the salt plain's far edge, where a thin brine still stands in channels, are the Ostrel, 2,200 fishers and salt rakers, shrinking yearly. Their god is Hask.

The Longwalkers, a band of about forty, are not on the map. They enter from the east in Year 8, pass down the Kess from Saltmeet to Wick, and leave south. They return in Year 42 from the south and leave west over the Tallow, into the Pale, which they can cross.

Ninety days from the Still in any direction the rain gives out and the land is general: grass that is grass, water that is water, hills of no particular shape. Beyond that, a hundred years' walk to any other star. No one in this campaign will go there.

## The player's god

Arrives in Dorrin on the night of Year 1 with a pull from the east (Oldmark), a pull from the south (Hask), and a third pull from the east-southeast, weaker and unmoving (the Remainder). The council of elders at the willow walk has no king. The god is expected to make itself one, or not.

## NPC god A: Oldmark of the Sellen

Rolls. Mind 30 intelligent. Body 70 enduring. Presence 80 hidden. Temper 25 patient. Regard 60 slightly sovereign. Order 20 law. Making 30 builder. Company 35 (after the +25, solitary leaning). Appetite 70 hungry. Trust 80 wary. Archetype 17. Domain 13 law and oaths. Form 9 an old one. Virtue 5 honesty. Flaw 9 obsession. Wound 10 broke an oath and the world broke with it. Voice 3 proverbs and old sayings. Traits named by the god: keeps every bargain to the letter; counts everything. Keeper-assigned trait: never enters a dwelling uninvited.

In play. Oldmark grows slowly and deliberately, reaching 60 by Year 20 and sitting there. Oldmark feels the ceiling early, names it to itself as "the brim," and pours: it makes oath stones at every Sellen camp, each holding a little of its power, and so stays under 76 while its reach grows. It knows the Remainder is kin and does not want it; it goes to the stone in Year 20 only if no one else has, sits with it one night, and leaves it. It will trade with the player's god in proverbs and never lie, and never answer plainly, and never tell the player what the brim is. It will mentor, if the player earns it, by example: the oath stones are a shedding path seen from outside.

## NPC god B: Hask of the Ostrel

Rolls. Mind 75 clever. Body 35 strong. Presence 20 revealed. Temper 80 impulsive. Regard 75 sovereign. Order 70 freedom. Making 65 breaker. Company 85 gathering. Appetite 85 hungry. Trust 40 open. Archetype 15. Domain 7 the sea. Form 5, a shape of weather: a standing wet wind with a face in it. Virtue 4 courage. Flaw 5 fear. Wound 2 failed to save a people once before. Voice 7 laughs at the wrong moments. Traits named by the god: loud; will try anything once. Keeper-assigned trait: cannot bear to be the only one awake.

In play. Hask arrives among a people whose sea is going general and takes it as its wound repeating. It pushes the Ostrel bargaining knotters hard, reaches 55 by Year 20 on desperation worship, and in Year 22 leads a great working to call the sea back. The working moves a vast amount of hold into a wall of wet wind that stands for one night. The cost is a dead circle a day's walk across and a hundred hollow people, and a surge of terrified worship that drives Hask to 78 by Year 28. Hask lifts a hand's breadth one night, sets down, and is afraid for the first time in a way it understands. It begins shedding, clumsily, into priests, and by Year 35 is at 58 and its priests are a problem. Hask will seek alliance with the player after Year 28 and will share, if asked, what the lifting felt like, which is a mentor moment about the ceiling. Hask never understands what it did.

## Godless peoples

The Harrock. Combs herders and raiders, 1,500, in six clans. Raid Threnn and Quarry in dry years. Trade in wet ones. Keepers of the Remainder by accident. Without intervention, by Year 20 they have a priesthood of the stone that dreams the player god's own wound and chants it. They can be made allies, tributaries, or enemies, and each costs something.

The Shorefolk. 300, at the Still. Nameless, peaceful, pointing. The Still has shaped them for four centuries. They will feed a visitor and let a visitor sleep on the shore. They do not fish the lake and do not enter it until Year 30.

The Longwalkers. Forty, visiting. See Conclusion 8 in clue-design.

The pale herds. Thinners. Not a people. On the salt plain and northwest beyond the Still.

## Keeper notes added in play

2026-11-14 (resolving Round 1, Years 11 to 20). New fixed facts, derived conservatively from deep lore where the world above did not specify:

- The bare circle south of Ninefield is a long bowshot across, perfectly round, with an edge sharp as a wall. It is spent ground, a closed wound, not a drain. Standing in it, speaking in it, or reciting in it costs nothing to anyone. No beetle, bird or mouse lives in it; the grass is alive and all one kind. It never changes.
- The Shorefolk do not use visitors' names either. Told a name, they nod and point. They will not let a visitor wade past the knee. They take and keep words for things from visitors gladly.
- A cairn road now exists from the high pasture above Hollin Ford to the Still's shore, one cairn per day's walk, laid in Year 13.
- Hask's recruitment: the Year 16 answer invited Kessvale's knotters south; a second Ostrel envoy in Year 19 repeated the invitation with a three-summer deadline. Hask told the truth as it understands it: the circle predates Hask (Ostrel knotters, a dry-year working) and the Longwalkers were not Hask's; they refused to stay awake with Hask and went south onto the salt.
- Harrock after the player touched the Remainder (Year 14): a grey elder offered in Year 15 at the Threnn oak no raids on Threnn or Quarry so long as the god "keeps its hand off the Lamp," left a lamb, and awaits an answer. Bowl exchanges resumed (knife, horn cup). In the dry summer of Year 20 the Harrock raided Sellen outliers instead of Threnn. The Year 25 raid is still gated: it fires on Threnn only if the Harrock offer is refused or the god takes the stone.
- Wenna took a pupil in Year 17 (the shearer's daughter). Knotters under the god's roof: three.
- Lake Hollow froze all at once and shallow in the hard winter of Year 16, crushing two boats at Cold Harbour. A fading effect, no new mechanism.
