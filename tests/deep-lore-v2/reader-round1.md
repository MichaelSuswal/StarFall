# Reader Round 1: Years 1 to 10 at Kessvale

Written as the god of the Kess, having read only block1.md.

## 1. THEORIES

### T1. Power in this world is paid for with distinctness, and the star is the ledger. Confidence 60.
The strongest pattern in ten years is a set of losses that all look the same. The Brae woman came back over the Tallow able to speak and recognize her husband but unable to say the names of Brae, her mother, or herself. Wenna, the healer's apprentice, keeps a list of things she used to know and the list grows. The third apple at Ninefield has gone bland: it is apple and nothing more, its particular quality gone. The bare circle beyond Ninefield grows grass where every blade is the same and nothing heads to seed, and the animals will not graze it. These are not deaths or blights. They are things being made generic. The specific is being sanded off.

Set against those losses is Tamsin's healing: a man who should have died lived, a light the color of the star showed in the cord, frost formed on the water jar in a warm room, and the dog did not know its master for an hour. Something was spent to save him. Heat was pulled from the room and identity was pulled, briefly, from the man. Wenna works beside Tamsin and is losing what she knows. I read this as the same transaction seen from the practitioner's side: the healer or her helper pays down over years what the patient pays in a moment.

The star connects to this because the light in the cord was star colored, because the shepherds see a thread of blue let down from the star during its dim hours, and because the children are told not to stare at it and one says it stares back. My working guess: the star is not a star. It is a thing above the world that takes or gives along threads of blue, and working with it is what the Ostrel did at the bare circle, what Tamsin does with cords, and what the wife met west of the Tallow.

Rival A (confidence 25): these are unrelated. Brae wife had a head injury or stroke in the mountains. Wenna has early dementia. The apple is an old graft running out. The bare circle is old salt working by the Ostrel, which fits "salt people" and dead ground well. Tamsin's story is a wool woman embroidering a story about a man who lived. The pattern is in my head, not the valley.

Rival B (confidence 15): the losses are not a cost of power but a disease or influence that spreads from the east and south, from the other presences, and Tamsin's healing is a separate and benign thing.

### T2. The star is the source of the gods, or the vehicle by which we arrived, and it is getting brighter. Confidence 50.
The Threnn trader said the stone fell into Cairn Hollow "the night the gods came," and it was warm all that first season. The Harrock built a cairn round it and leave it alone. I came down on the willow walk the same night and sensed three other weights in the country. The lantern walk is done by starlight alone and the young say it is enough to read by and the old say it was not when they were young. Both are right. The star has been brightening for at least a human lifetime, which means the change began before I arrived and I am part of something already in motion, not its start.

Rival (confidence 30): the star is simply a very bright fixed light, a moon or a comet this world has, and the gods came some other way. The falling stone is a meteorite that happened on a memorable night and folk memory has fused the two events. Brightening over one lifetime is a real thing an old man can misremember.

### T3. The three weights I felt in Year 1 are three other gods, and I can now name two of them. Confidence 70.
South and restless: Hask, lord of the Ostrel, "a wind with a face in it," who says the sea will return within his lifetime, which is forever. That is a god speaking, and the envoy's arrival in Year 10 matches the direction. East and far, "like someone standing behind a door": the one who leads the Sellen past the Harrock, who speaks in sayings and sets a cut stone at each camp. Nobody has seen this person, only the stones. Standing behind a door fits a god who acts through markers rather than appearing.

The third weight, east and a little south, smaller, that does not move and "does not feel like it is standing": I think this is the stone in Cairn Hollow. Either a god that fell and did not get up, or a god that arrived in the form of a stone and stays that way, or the remains of one. It was warm one season and then was not. The Harrock went quiet after it fell. Confidence that the third weight is the Cairn Hollow stone: 55.

Rival (confidence 20): the third weight is a fourth god seated somewhere in the Combs or beyond Threnn, and the Cairn Hollow stone is just a stone that the Harrock, a frightened people, sanctified.

### T4. The forty singers are a mapping or a harvesting operation, probably Hask's or the Sellen god's. Confidence 45.
Forty people carrying nothing walk in from the Combs road, ask every village name, sing them back exactly in the local saying, add names of places nobody knows and names that are no places, and leave south. Everyone who heard cannot get the lists out of their head. This looks like a god using people as a recording and broadcasting medium. The exact repetition of Saltmeet "in her own saying of it" is the unsettling detail: they were not learning names, they were taking them. The lists stuck in heads look like a seed planted. They went south, toward the Ostrel and Hask. The names of no places could be places that have already been made generic, or places yet to be.

Rival A (confidence 30): they are a pilgrim or mendicant sect with a memory tradition, harmless, and the sticky songs are what good songs do.

Rival B (confidence 20): they belong to the Sellen leader who "speaks in sayings," and the cut stones at the Sellen camps are the same kind of fixing of names in place. They came from the east road, after all, and only left south.

### T5. The valley is slowly warming and this is mostly ordinary. Confidence 55.
The char have not run since before I came, the lake is not cold the way it was, the ford froze a month but the pond froze three, the star is brighter, Hask promises the sea will return. These could be a climate shift this world is having, with Hask simply promising what is already coming. I do not think I am the cause. I do not rule out that the star is.

Rival (confidence 25): the warming is a side effect of the blue thread to the northwest pulling heat, in the way Tamsin's healing frosted the jar in reverse. If power pulls cold into the place it is spent, then where the thread lands something is being cooled and the rest of the world warms.

### T6. The standing stones on the Cold Harbour east shore were an alignment to the star or to the thread, and the thing they pointed at has moved. Confidence 40.
Two stones and a notched one, set up to look at something, and they do not point at anything now. Something that used to be in the sky or on the horizon is not there. If the star has moved, or the thread's landing place has moved, the Rings would record the old position.

Rival (confidence 45): they point at a sunrise or a star that precessed, as standing stones everywhere do, and the fisher is right to tell it the way he tells the perch.

## 2. WHICH OBSERVATIONS MATTER

### Meaningful, I believe
- The three weights felt on arrival, and their directions.
- The stone that fell into Cairn Hollow the night the gods came, warm one season, then cairned.
- Hask: a wind with a face, the sea returning, forever.
- The Sellen leader who speaks in sayings and sets cut stones.
- Tamsin's healing: star colored light in the cord, frost in a warm room, dog not knowing its master.
- Wenna's growing list of things she used to know, and that she chose to tell me there is a list.
- The Brae wife who came back from west of the Tallow without names.
- The third apple gone bland, with the young unable to taste the difference.
- The bare circle beyond Ninefield: uniform grass, no seed, animals refuse it, the Ostrel did it.
- The forty singers, the lists, the exact repetition, going south.
- The blue thread from the star to the northwest during the star's dim hours.
- Children told not to stare at the star, and "because it stares back."
- The star being bright enough to read by now and not a lifetime ago.
- The Rings at Cold Harbour not pointing at anything anymore.
- The char gone and the lake no longer cold.
- The Harrock raid that took forty sheep and left the bowl on the wall untouched. Something there is being respected or avoided. Low confidence this matters but I note it.

### Just the valley being the valley, I believe
- Blue cranes passing in a line and never landing. Migratory birds. The color is a coincidence I am choosing not to chase.
- The Judge heron at Wick. A village pet omen.
- The Harrock's breath, a dry east wind.
- Marsh lights at Wick in dry years. The Wick people are right.
- The ferryman and the miller. The ferryman's death. The miller weeping. This is grief and a long friendship disguised as a feud, and it is the best thing in ten years, and it is not a clue.
- Pig weighing, eel week, the glass eel in the bucket, the honey that tastes of stone, the wool going grey from a low spring, the swallow gap left in the wall, the rock cat tally, Quarry's silence day, the river stones for children, the wrist rope marriages, the naming at the third spring, the Threnn oak with the arrowhead, Ninefield's wall dispute, the eleven elders and their seating.
- The fox with black legs in the Dorrin yards. Probably a fox. I am watching it only because I have been told everything here is unexplained, and that is exactly the trap, so I set it aside.
- The Saltmeet deer that no one hunts. A custom. The thinness in Year 6 is the dry year.
- Snow on the Tallow, pink at dusk. Fog like milk. The Kess green in autumn.
- The ferry rope being new, said twice. A man proud of a rope.

I will confess the places I am tempted. The glass eels "clear as water" and the uniform grass both rhyme with the idea of things being drained of particularity, and I nearly listed the eels as meaningful. I did not, because the whole valley eats them every spring and has for generations.

## 3. ACTIONS, Years 11 to 20

1. Go to Brae and sit with Old Tamsin and Wenna through a healing, and ask Wenna to read me her sister's list. Reason: this is the one place in the valley where the blue light has been seen in a human hand. I want to watch what happens to the room, the healer, the patient, and the apprentice at the moment the cord is tied, and I want to know whether the things Wenna has lost are names, like the Brae wife, or skills, or everything. This tests T1 directly: if Wenna loses something specific during or after a healing I witness, the cost theory holds.

2. Send two shepherds from the high pasture above Hollin Ford northwest along the line of the blue thread, with instructions to walk only in daylight, to mark their route with stones, and to turn back the moment either of them cannot say the other's name. Reason: the Brae wife went west over the Tallow and came back nameless, and the thread lands northwest. I want to know whether the thread's landing place is the same place she went, and whether the loss begins at a boundary. The turn back rule is the test: if names fail at a line, T1 holds and I have found its edge.

3. Walk myself to Cairn Hollow, east of Threnn, and put my hand on the fallen stone. Reason: the third weight I felt on arrival does not move and does not stand, and that is where a warm stone fell the night the gods came. If the weight is the stone, I will know it on contact, and I will learn whether what is there is dead, sleeping, or something that was never a person. I go myself because the Harrock went quiet after it fell and I do not want to send a mortal to find out why.

4. Send the Saltmeet headwoman, who recites every tally since she was twelve without error, to the bare circle beyond Ninefield with a Ninefield elder who can name the old apples, and have her stand at the edge, then step in, then step out, and recite the tally each time. Reason: she is the most precise memory in the valley and the bare circle is the largest piece of ground that has been made uniform. If her recitation slips inside the circle and returns outside it, the ground itself carries the effect, and the Ostrel made it. This tests T1 and T4 together, since the singers went south toward the Ostrel. She does not go alone and she does not stay inside more than a hundred heartbeats.

5. Send a Dorrin elder with the Wick envoy's return party to the Ostrel with salt to trade and one question for Hask, delivered as a question between gods: what did you make at the circle beyond Ninefield, and did the singers come from you. Reason: Hask is loud and proud and calls himself forever. Proud things answer questions. I want to know whether he claims the circle and the singers, and I want to see how the envoy carries a message between gods, because the Sellen leader's silence makes Hask my only talkative neighbor.

6. Ask the Cold Harbour fisher to take me by boat to the Rings on the east shore at the star's dim hour, and sight along the notched stone. Reason: if the stones once pointed at the star or at the thread, then where they point now, compared to where the thread hangs, tells me how far the thing has moved and in what direction since the stones were set. This is cheap, it costs no one anything, and it tests T6 against its rival. If the stones point at an ordinary sunrise, I drop T6.

## 4. MOOD

I felt pulled. Not in Years 1 to 3, which plodded in the right way: the tally, the pig, the rope, the eels. The pull started at Year 4 with the Rings and the fallen stone said in the same breath as the perch, and it tightened at Year 5 with Tamsin's frost. From Year 7 on I was reading every line twice, which is partly good and partly a sign that by Year 8 I had a theory and was feeding it. The singers and Wenna's message in Year 9 are the two moments I most wanted to act in the same year rather than wait.

Did anything read like the narrator pointing? Yes, two places. Wenna's message is almost a hand on my shoulder: she asks nothing and tells me there is a list. That is a character pointing, which is legitimate, but it is pointing. And the Dorrin child's "because it stares back" with the teacher not laughing is the kind of line a writer puts in because they know the answer. The old man and the girl both being right about the starlight is a third, smaller one. Everything else felt like the valley talking in its own voice, and the ferryman's death in the same year as the loud envoy laughing at it was the most honest thing in the block: the world does not line up its griefs with its mysteries, and this one did not.

What I most want and did not get: anyone from the valley who has been to the northwest and come back whole, and any description of what the Sellen leader's cut stones have on them.
