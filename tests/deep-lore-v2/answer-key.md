# Answer key: what the player actually had, block by block

Blocks: 1 (Years 1 to 10), 2 (Years 11 to 20), 3 (Years 21 to 30), 4 (Years 31 to 40). "Had" means the clue appeared in the player's packet on the branches the player actually took. "Reachable" is the earliest block in which a careful player, holding only what was in the packets, could have reached the conclusion at 80 or better by the standard in mystery-standard.md (acting on a theory and seeing the result). Where the player never got the decisive clue, the key says why, in terms of the branch not taken.

## Conclusion 1. The star is sinking, and the rate is quickening

Had by end of block 1: Low Star name; the Rings' stones "point at nothing" (Year 4); the lantern walk brighter than a lifetime ago (Year 10).
Block 2: the god sighted through the notched frame and saw the star a hand below it (Year 11); the fisher's wrong sunset cause; more cold hours and the thread on each (Year 20).
Block 3: the lantern walk line repeated with the old man dead (Year 20 had it; Year 30 walk restated); nothing new. No marks were ever set, so Years 15, 22, 34 readings never existed on this branch.
Block 4: the star bright enough to read a stone by (Year 40). Nothing more.
Reachable: never at 80 on this branch. The player dropped T6 in Round 2 after reading the frame as a shifted sunset, accepting the fisher's explanation. The player had the single SEEN clue (star under the frame) in block 2 and could have reached 50 to 60 then, and 80 only by setting marks in Year 11 and reading them in block 3 (Year 18 plus) and again in block 4. Lost to a wrong reading of an honest clue, not to the packet.

## Conclusion 2. The fading is a loss of kinds, patchy, oldest places first

Had by end of block 1: char not run, lake not cold (Year 3); the Brae woman nameless from the west (Year 7); third apple bland (Year 8); the bare circle (Year 10).
Block 2: salt plain with no shells (Year 11); pale beast with general meat (Year 13); one apple (Year 15); mounds thickest at the shallows, weed gone (Year 11); lake froze all at once and shallow (Year 16); the weed's name gone (Year 19).
Block 3: the four tally sticks showing a steady rate regardless of the god's wanting (Years 23, 25); perch all one size and the Hollin bend softening (Year 24); verses, pool names, honey kinds, sayings lost; the Hollin a marsh (Year 30).
Block 4: the Hollin general from lake to ford (Year 40); the char line on the stone that no one can gloss (Year 40); the brae woman who crossed the Tallow and came back whole (Year 39, showing the edge is far).
Reachable: block 3. The player reached the loss-of-kinds reading at 60 by block 3 (T8) and had the world's clearest negative (the sticks) to anchor it. The player did not reach "oldest places first" or "the valley can be kept," because it never acted on the keeping branch (grafting the apples, seeking the char, teaching songs) and so never saw the Year 48 yes. The player's own experiment (stop wanting) was honest and informative and was the wrong lever; it proved the god's small acts are not the spend and nothing else. The tally stone was a record, not a kind. At Year 40 the player sits near 60 on the fading's nature and near 20 on its remedy, which is below target on the second half.

## Conclusion 3. Magic moves particularity; the mage pays with herself and the land pays too

Had by end of block 1: Tamsin without her name (Year 2); the frost, the blue, the dog (Year 5); Wenna's list exists (Year 9); the bare circle (Year 10).
Block 2: the god watched a working: the room leaned, the tub lost its smell, the shearer was well, Wenna lost a line that night (Year 12); the god recognized the motion as its own (Year 12); the circle costs nothing to stand on (Year 15); the Ostrel brine knotter not knowing her child (Year 16); Hesk begins (Year 17).
Block 3: the cold byre: stone pays nothing, the boy nameless a day, Wenna's sister's name gone for good (Year 22); Hask's working and its circle and hundred hollow (Year 22); Oldmark's "the one who carries it pays, with the hand" (Year 26).
Block 4: the hollow can be trained to answer and never know (Year 35); the Wick girl's first knot and the willow dropping its leaves (Year 38).
Reachable: block 3, and the player reached it, at 90 by Round 3. Earned by two actions (watching, then the byre test) and confirmed by Hask's working at scale. The one piece the player holds wrongly: it believes the god's wanting is the same transaction and spends the valley, then the sticks acquitted it and it concluded the cost of the god's wanting lands nowhere it can see. The truth (the god spends itself) was shown in Year 12 as "you do not know yet what it costs" and never resolved, because the player never tested a large want. Fine at 40 to 60 on that sub-question.

## Conclusion 4. The Still is alive, fed from above, and spreads namelessness

Had by end of block 1: the thread to the northwest (Year 9); the Dorrin child's "it stares back" (Year 9).
Block 2: shepherds' report: round lake, star overhead, no ripple, not cold, nameless pointing people, the thread touching the water, the counting dream (Year 13); the Hollin Ford man's second walk (Year 17).
Block 3: names land on huts, the named look at the water, the dream every night (Year 23); Shorefolk walking into the water, the Cold Harbour fisher's folded coat (Year 30).
Block 4: huts a third then half empty (Years 32, 36); the counting answer by sign (Year 32); no Kessvale taking after the road closed (Year 36 silence).
Reachable: block 3 for "alive and spreads namelessness"; the player reached roughly 65 and reads the lake as a drain people choose, which is wrong about the agency (the Still arranged the choosing) but right about the effect. "Fed from above" was in the packet from block 2 (the thread lands on it every time) and the player holds it inside T2 as the star and lake being one mouth. Not reached: that the lake is a being with a wish (the fall). The decisive clue for that is the god's own night on the shore and the elder's "the water did not want them," which were gated to the god in person; the god sent proxies three times and never went. Closing the road was a correct protective choice and cost nothing it did not expect.

## Conclusion 5. Gods grow by being held in mind; there is a ceiling; past it a god lightens

Had by end of block 1: nothing direct. (Power after harvests and winters is SEEN by the god's body and was not written into block 1 as an item; a gap in the packet, noted.)
Block 2: Quarry's silence, the step down with carts still coming, the step back (Years 14, 15); Oldmark's one-line stone and "a full cup is carried slowly" (Year 18); the south weight heavy and pacing (Year 20).
Block 3: Hask stood heavy after the working (Year 22); Hask lifted a hand's breadth and came down and began pouring, the south weight lifting and then lightening monthly (Year 28); Oldmark's oath stones in use and the Sellen woman's partly wrong gloss (Year 26); Hask's quiet request (Year 29).
Block 4: Hask's own account of the warnings, the dreams, the lift, the choice (Year 31); Dorrin's year of silence and the elders' "it has done as well" (Years 33, 34); "I keep my cup by pouring" (Year 35).
Reachable: block 3 at 60 to 65 (the player got there: T2 at 65), block 4 at 80 (the player has everything: growth by minds, a threshold, lightening, two ways to stay under, one god who did each). The player has not yet named the ceiling as a number or as a thing belonging to it; it sits well under and has never felt its own shadow pale. That is the design: a god under 60 may never hear the voices, and this one has not. The player holds Rival A (the star rewards) at 20, which is the intended residual.

## Conclusion 6. The Night Stone is kin to the gods; the gods are the remainder of a far fall

Had by end of block 1: the third pull, unmoving, "does not feel like it is standing" (Year 1); the stone that fell the night the gods came, warm a season (Year 4).
Block 2: the Harrock dream of the kneeling crowd and the river with roads (Year 12); the god's hand on the stone, the dream, the river known from its own fog (Year 14); the Harrock's terms (Year 15).
Block 3: the dream has no name, only the word a follower says to the followed, which the god has heard said to itself (Year 26); the whole clan dreaming that night; Oldmark's "a stone set down is still counted" (Year 26).
Block 4: the weight unchanged through a year of one clan's absence (Year 34); the grey man's "it is the dead who sleep there, not the stone" (Year 33).
Reachable: block 2 at 60 (the response table's stated payoff for touching it) and block 3 at 80 for "kin to the gods" and "we were followed somewhere else and came from there." The player is at 80 and has the content right in its own words: cups shaped by following, carried there, set down here. Not reached and not reachable in this campaign: whose. The player never asked Oldmark about the stone directly, so "the stone and I came in the same cart" was never given; it would have added kinship with Oldmark and nothing more. The player's remaining wrong piece: it reads the Lamp as a god that emptied itself all the way. It is dross that never compressed. The difference is not discoverable below the star.

## Conclusion 7. The star answers; the voices are many and they want the fall

Had by end of block 1: dim nights and the thread (Year 9).
Block 2: the shepherds' counting dream on long legs in water (Year 13).
Block 3: the Hollin Ford man's dream (Year 23); the Shorefolk dream every night (Year 23); the sea "taken back by something that did not want it" (Year 22, a wrong cause from refugees).
Block 4: Hask's account: they counted him, his number was nearly theirs, they offered the sea for good if he came up (Year 31); the Wick knotter's recognition of the dream water as a shallow salt sea (Year 32); "nothing in your sleep but sleep" on four cold hours (Year 38).
Reachable: block 4 at 50 at most. The player has, secondhand, that the dreams are of counters who make offers and that the offer is to come up. It has no first-hand voice, because it has never passed 60 and never slept a dim night on the shore. It cannot know the voices are many or that they want the fall; no mortal has said so and no mortal could. This is working as designed: Conclusion 7 is gated by the player's own power or by one night at the center, and the player chose neither. The player's T9 (Hask learned something at a hand's breadth) was correct and was paid in full in Year 31.

## Conclusion 8. Stored names survive the Pale and a fall; the fall renews what is kept and ends what is not

Had by end of block 1: the Longwalkers' visit, the lists, Saltmeet sung back exactly, names of no places (Year 8).
Block 2: Hask: the singers sang his name "in a way he did not like," would not stay awake with him, went onto the salt and did not come back (Year 16).
Block 3: nothing new. (Oldmark's "a stone set down is still counted" is adjacent, not about this.)
Block 4: the tally in stone; the char line legible and the thing gone (Year 40); the headwoman's granddaughter holding the whole tally by ear (Year 37).
Reachable: not before block 5 (Year 42 return) on any branch, and on this branch the preconditions are thin. The player did not speak to the Longwalkers in Year 8 and so never heard the two sentences about carrying; it holds them as the star's reckoning (T4 at 55), which is a clean miss built from honest evidence. The player did not order the dig under the Rings by Year 16, so the glass and the named stone in it were never found; that was the one clue linking the Longwalkers to survival across a fall, and it is still available to a late dig. The Year 42 visit is intact (no detention). The player's instinct in Round 3 (cut the tally in stone, keep what mouths cannot) is pointed at the right question and the wrong medium, and the Year 40 char line is the world's honest answer about stone. If the player gives names in Year 42, the Year 50 trader is the payoff. The granddaughter who has the tally by ear is, unnoticed, the valley's one true carrier.

## Keeper's notes on the test

Three things the packets did well: the negatives (shepherds found no boundary, headwoman recited perfectly, the sticks matched) were trusted more than the positives, as intended. The two cleanest mentor moments (Year 12 recognition, Year 31 Hask) landed as the player's own and not as delivery. The plain world carried the griefs (the ferryman, the miller, the Judge, Tamsin) and the player correctly refused to read them as clues every round.

Three things to fix in the next version: block 1 omitted the god's own felt weight rising after the first harvest and falling after the hard winter (clue-design Conclusion 5, Year 3), which left Conclusion 5 with no block 1 seed; the lantern walk and "it stares back" were twice flagged by the player as the narrator's hand and should be spaced wider or voiced differently; and no packet ever pushed the god toward setting marks or digging, which is correct by the standard (the decisive clue is earned) but means a player who misreads the Rings in Year 11 is locked out of Conclusions 1 and 8's hard evidence for the whole campaign. Consider a second, independent public body for the star's descent that surfaces by Year 25 whether or not marks were set, such as a Tallow pass whose old cairn sightline no longer clears the star.
