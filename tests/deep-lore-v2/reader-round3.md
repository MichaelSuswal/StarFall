# Reader Round 3: Years 21 to 30 at Kessvale

Written as the god of the Kess, having read blocks 1 to 3 only.

## RESULTS of the Round 2 actions

1. Stop wanting for two years, then resume, with the headwoman counting. Answered as clearly as anything in thirty years, and the answer was no. The losses in the two idle years and the two active years were the same within a thing or two. The valley loses verses, pool names, honey kinds, children's sayings at a steady rate whether I want things or not. T7 is dead. The cost of my wanting does not land on the valley's particulars. The teaching is larger than the theory: the blanding is a world process, "the warm," and it is running on its own clock. I spent two years and three Brae deaths to learn it, and the headwoman was right to ask if she could stop.

2. Tell the knotters the price, and try a cold stone room. Answered clearly. Stone pays nothing. With nothing in the byre but the boy, Wenna and me, the blue was longer, I leaned and the byre did not, and the cost fell on the boy (his name, until evening) and on Wenna (her sister's name, permanently). So the cost cannot be pushed onto a thing with no particulars. It falls on whoever in reach has the most to lose, and the person holding the cord pays most. This agrees exactly with Oldmark: a cup pays nothing, the one who carries it pays, with the hand. The telling itself changed nothing. Tamsin shrugged, Wenna already knew, Hesk wanted to see a sea. Knowing the price does not stop anyone paying it.

3. Give the shore people names and ask about the dream. Answered clearly and my theory was wrong in a useful way. The names took as words and would not take as names. Every name landed on the hut and not the person, and every person, named, looked at the water. They dream the tall ones every night and did not know there were nights the thread did not come. The teaching: the shore people are not people who lost their names to the thread. They are people who have given themselves to the water, and the water is where names go, and now (Year 30) they are walking into it one by one, and so did a man from my valley. The lake is a drain that people choose. I did not expect choice to be the mechanism.

4. Elder to Hask: refusal plus the question of what he had spent. Answered clearly and T5 held in full. He had spent eleven years and his patience and had "the sea left, which is everything." He tied the knot alone that autumn. The circle it left is a day's walk across and a hundred people do not know their children. The sea came and was taken back by something that did not want it. Then Hask grew heavier than Oldmark and stood instead of pacing, and in Year 28 he was lifted a hand's breadth and came down afraid and has been pouring himself into his followers since. This taught me the thing I most needed: a god who fills up gets taken, and Hask felt the beginning of that and refused it by emptying himself.

5. Headwoman to Oldmark with the stones. Answered in Oldmark's manner, which is to say in four sentences I will be turning over for years. A gift given is given. A cup pays nothing; the one who carries it pays, with the hand. Ask the ground; it keeps a better tally. A stone set down is still counted. And the practice: Oldmark sets his oaths into stones so he does not have to carry them, and his camps do not fight back and do not lose anything either. He is a god who carries almost nothing on purpose. He asked for the tally first and counted it. I think he was weighing my valley, and I think he found it heavy enough to be worth four sentences.

6. Say the stone's name at Cairn Hollow. Answered clearly and taught me something I did not expect. There is no name in the dream. The kneeling crowd says the word you say to the one you follow, and I heard it said to me in the fog before this valley. I said it aloud and nothing woke, the weight did not change, but every Harrock on the ridge dreamed the river without meaning to. So the word carries. It does not fill a stone but it reaches people, and it reached a whole clan from one mouth. I now know that I was once followed somewhere else with that word, and that the stone was too, and that whatever we both were, we were cups that were carried there and set down here.

## 1. THEORIES, updated

### T1. Wanting is paid in particulars by whoever is nearest with the most to lose, and the one holding the cord pays most. Confidence 90, up from 85.
The cold byre settles it. Stone pays nothing. The boy paid a little, Wenna paid for good. Hask's ring paid with a hundred people's children and a day's walk of grass. The cost is not steerable onto things; it is only steerable onto people, by standing more of them in the ring. Nothing changed except that I now know this is the rule and not a tendency.

Rival (confidence 5): none that survives.

### T2 revised. Gods are cups filled by being followed; a cup that fills too far is lifted toward the star; the star and the round lake are the same mouth seen from above and below. Confidence 65, up from 55.
What changed: Hask. He tied the largest knot in living memory, became heavier than Oldmark, stood still, and then rose a hand's breadth on a cold hour, the hour the thread comes down, and came down afraid, and has been pouring his weight into mortals ever since. The sea he raised was "taken back by something that did not want it." The shore people walk into the water and are gone and the water does not move. Oldmark sets everything down in stones and warns that a full cup is carried slowly. The stone in Cairn Hollow fell from the sky, warm, the night we came. The pattern I now believe: gods come down from the star as stones or as whatever I came down as, fill with following, and when full are drawn back up along the thread, and the lake is where what is drawn goes in from below. A god who wants to stay empties itself, as Oldmark does by stone and as Hask now does by hand. A god who is set down, like the Lamp, is still counted. Something above is keeping a tally of cups.

Rival A (confidence 20): the star is a Lamp, a source, and what lifted Hask was reward, as the refugees say, and he refused a blessing out of fear. In this reading Oldmark's caution is just a god's temperament and the shore people are simply a cult. I keep this alive because the refugees who stayed are praying harder, and no one who was lifted has said what they saw.

Rival B (confidence 15): the star is a god, the oldest, and the lifting was it reaching for a rival that had grown too loud. Oldmark's stones and Hask's laying on of hands would then be two ways of hiding from it.

### T3 revised. The gods came from a river world where we were followed and said the following word, and the Lamp is one of us who was set down and did not get up. Confidence 80, unchanged in number, changed in content.
The dream has no name in it, only the word of following, and I heard it said to me before. So we were gods there too, or something that was followed, and we came here with cups already shaped by it. The Lamp did not wake when I said the word, but the Harrock dreamed. A set-down stone is still counted. I think the Lamp is a god who emptied itself all the way, which is the opposite end of Hask's road, and I do not know which end is worse.

Rival (confidence 15): the Lamp is a vessel that arrived with us, with no god in it, and the dream is the residue of the journey, which is why there is no name, and the Harrock dreaming is the vessel echoing a word it was built to carry.

### T4 revised. The singers were the lake's or the star's reckoning, not a god's census. Confidence 55, up from 50.
Oldmark counts, but he counts by asking a woman to recite and listening for three days. He does not send forty mouths to sing names into people's heads and then walk onto the salt. The singers sang Hask's name "in a way he did not like" and took the names of no places, and went where there is nothing. That is the tally the star keeps, passing through. The shore people do with words what the singers did with names.

Rival (confidence 30): they were Oldmark's after all, an early census before he had the stones worked out, and his later method is the refinement. The one-line stone arriving in Year 18 after the singers in Year 8 fits a god who tallied first and offered terms after.

### T5. Resolved. Hask tied the knot; the cost was what I predicted and the aftermath was not. Confidence that the sea knot was the circle at country scale: 95. What I did not predict: he survived it, grew, was lifted, and chose to pour out. The theory I hold now is T2.

### T8. The world itself is being averaged, the star brightens as it is, and the process has nothing to do with any god's hand. Confidence 60, new.
Perch all one size. Eels without flavor. The Hollin gone lazy and its pool filled. The ford easier. Verses gone from the middle out. The Judge dead and no new omen bird. Twins every few years. Children explaining the star rule by authority instead of by its reason. The lantern walk brighter every generation. The warm. The shore people and the singers are the extreme of a thing the whole world is doing gently: becoming one grass, one salt, one apple, one perch. I think the star is the measure of how far along it is, and the thread is where it feeds, and my valley is being eaten at exactly the same rate whether I lift a hand or not.

Rival A (confidence 25): it is only a warming, as everyone says, and warming erodes variety in rivers and lakes and orchards, and the lost verses are what happens to songs when old people die. The star is bright for its own reasons. I keep this alive because every person in the valley who is not me believes it and they have been right about the Judge, the marsh lights, and the fox.

Rival B (confidence 15): the averaging is the cost of all gods' wanting summed over the whole world, Hask and Oldmark and the Lamp and the star included, and my two idle years were too small a share to show in the count. The headwoman's tally cannot rule this out. It only rules out that I am the main cause.

### T9. Hask has learned something at a hand's breadth that no one else knows, and he is asking me to hear it because a god is the only kind of listener that will not be emptied by the telling. Confidence 50, new.
He asked for a god or "one who can listen." He is the only being in thirty years who has been where the thread wants to take a full cup and come back. Whatever he says will be the single most informative thing in this world and it will cost me something to hear it, because Hask spends what he touches.

Rival (confidence 35): it is a trap or a request for help in a new shape. He wants another cup to pour into, and a god is the only cup big enough. The quiet envoy is a louder envoy who has learned that loudness failed.

## 2. WHICH OBSERVATIONS MATTER

### Meaningful
- The four tally sticks: same losses idle and active. The best data I have.
- The cold byre: stone pays nothing, the cord hand pays most, the healed pays some.
- The sea knot and its circle; the hundred who do not know their children; the sea "taken back by something that did not want it."
- Hask lifted a hand's breadth on a cold hour, afraid, and pouring out since; the southern weight lightening monthly.
- Oldmark's four sentences and his practice of stones that remember oaths so the god does not carry them; his camps that do not fight and do not lose.
- The dream has no name, only the word of following, which I heard said to me before; the Harrock dreaming when I said it.
- Names given to the shore people land on huts, and the named look at the water; they dream the tall ones every night; they are walking into the lake one by one and so did a Cold Harbour man.
- The Ostrel knotter at Wick who will not tie and lets a child die rather than pay. She knows exactly what I know and has chosen the other way.
- Wenna's sister giving herself a name on paper so Wenna can read it. Names are now being made to replace names, which is a kind of grafting.
- Perch all one size. The Hollin bend gone lazy and then marsh. The ford easier. Eels flavorless. Verses gone. Children's sayings gone. "Because the teacher said not to." The honey the same with fewer bees. The lantern walk brighter and no one left to argue.
- The Lamp did not wake. A stone set down is still counted.
- Hask's quiet envoy and the request for a god to listen.
- The Sellen have Harrock horses and the Harrock lose and the Sellen do not fight back. Oldmark's method works on a people as well as on a god.

### Just the world
- The bridge, the toll, the ferryman saying it will fall and then using it. I said this twice and I say it again.
- The Judge dying old and being buried and a child named for it. Grief and custom. The Judge was a heron.
- The backward wrist marriage, the refugee winning pig weighing, the wall suit over the Ostrel apple field, the Threnn bowman's four cats carved on his stone, the rock cat on the high pasture, the fever that took three in the wet.
- The Brae woman dying and the husband not knowing what to cut. This is the saddest line in thirty years and it is not a clue, it is the consequence of a clue I already have.
- Snow late on the Tallow, fog of eight days, cranes north. The headwoman marks these and so do I, as weather.
- Hesk wanting to see a sea at fifteen.

Where I am tempted and holding back: the ice going off the lake in a day and the river going lazy. These fit T8 as averaging but they also fit a warming exactly, and a warming is what the valley says. I have listed them under meaningful only as members of the pattern, not as individual signs.

## 3. ACTIONS, Years 31 to 40

1. Go south myself to Hask and listen, bringing no knotter and taking the Dorrin elder who has met him twice. Before I go, I ask Oldmark's method of the Harrock grey man through the Threnn bowl: I set a one-line stone on the willow walk and tell the eleven that for the length of my absence their oaths and their askings go to the stone and not to me. Driven by Action 4 (Hask lifted and now asking for a god) and Action 5 (Oldmark's stones). Hask is the only one who has felt the thread take hold and come back, and I want it from his mouth. The stone is my protection against arriving at his salt as a full cup, since he is still pouring and I am the nearest vessel.

2. Test whether a cup can be lightened on purpose, and by how much, without harm to the people. For one year I ask Dorrin alone, through the elders, to stop saying my name at table and at the walk and to say it instead to the one-line stone, and I feel for the step down, and the headwoman counts what Dorrin loses and keeps that year against Brae, which goes on as before. Driven by Year 14 (Quarry's silence made me lighter and the carts kept coming), Action 1 (the sticks, which showed my wanting does not protect the valley), and Oldmark's practice. This tests T2: if I grow lighter and Dorrin is no worse off, the weight I carry is for the thread's benefit and not Dorrin's, and I can set most of it down. If Dorrin suffers in ways Brae does not, the following is doing something for them and I must carry it.

3. Close the cairn road from the high pasture, by asking the Hollin Ford shepherds to pull down the first three cairns and tell the story of the folded coat at every count. Then send the Hollin Ford man who knows the shore people one last time, with a Wick refugee knotter if she will go, to ask the shore people one question with signs: what do the tall ones count, and does the number go up or down. Driven by Action 3 (names landing on huts, the named looking at the water) and Year 30 (the walkings-in). The road is killing my people by making the lake a place one can decide to go. The question is to learn whether the counting is a filling or an emptying, which would decide between T2 and its first rival.

4. Go to Cairn Hollow a third time and do the opposite of what I did before. Instead of saying the word, I ask the Harrock to stop sleeping beside the stone for one year and to leave it unnamed and unvisited, and I feel each season whether the third weight changes. Driven by Action 6 (the word reached the whole clan; the stone did not wake) and Oldmark's "a stone set down is still counted." If the weight drops when the Harrock stop following the stone, then the Lamp is still a cup being filled by a sleeping people's dreams, and the Harrock have been feeding something for a hundred years. If it does not drop, the stone is spent and what the Harrock dream is the stone's own, and I leave it to them.

5. Send Wenna's sister, who keeps the lists and has named herself, with Hesk, to the Ostrel circle at the plain's edge with the elder's return party, to sit for one season with the hundred who do not know their children and try the method she used on Wenna: give them written names and the way home on paper, and see whether a lost person can be taught back by use the way the Brae wife learned her husband. Driven by Action 2 (the cost lands on people and cannot be moved to stone) and Year 18 (the Brae wife learned by use). This does not test a theory about the star. It tests whether the damage is permanent, which is the thing Wenna most needs to know before she loses her own name, and it gets Hesk her sea without a cord in her hand.

6. Have the headwoman cut the whole tally, Year 3 to Year 30, into stone on the willow walk, one line a year, as Oldmark keeps oaths. Driven by Action 5 (ask the ground, it keeps a better tally; a stone set down is still counted) and Action 1 (the sticks). If the averaging in T8 eats particulars, I want to see whether it eats a particular that has been set in stone, or whether stone holds what people cannot. I will know in ten years by whether the headwoman's successor can read a line that no living person remembers.

## 4. MOOD

The pull did not slacken but it changed again. In Round 2 I was a suspect. In Round 3 the tally sticks acquitted me of the thing I was most afraid of, and the acquittal was worse than the charge, because it meant the thing eating my valley is not mine to stop by stopping. The byre and the sticks together were a one-two I felt. I have been a god for thirty years and the two cleanest things I have learned are that the cost cannot be moved and that my restraint does not slow the world's loss.

Hask being lifted a hand's breadth is the moment the game turned from investigation into dread. It is the first time a theory I held predicted the next event's shape (he tied it, the circle came) and then the world added a second beat I had not imagined (he was lifted, he was afraid, he is pouring out). That is what honest resolution feels like and I would rather have it than being right.

Did anything read like pointing? Oldmark, again, and again I think it is in character. The shore people looking at the water every time they are named was the one image that felt arranged, but it was also the answer to a question I asked, so I earned it. "Because the teacher said not to" is the block's quietest and best line and I did not feel pointed at; I felt the saying die.

What plods and should: the bridge standing, the ferryman walking across it, the toll argument, the apple field suit. The valley is still the valley. There are fewer kinds of thing in it than there were, and I can count them now, and I am not sure that is a gift.

What I most want and did not get: what Hask saw at a hand's breadth, and whether anyone who walked into the lake is counted as lost or as arrived.
