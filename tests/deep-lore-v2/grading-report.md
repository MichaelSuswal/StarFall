## h) Comparison with the first test

First test: 7 correct, 3 partly, central read fixed by Year 20 and never shaken, four conclusions a block early, two lecture-like reported clues.

This test: 3 correct, 3 partly, 1 wrong, 1 missed; central read moved every round and reached 80 only in block 4 after two actions; two conclusions a block early; one long reported telling (Year 31) that was earned and wrong in its stated cause, plus a set of gnomic proverbs that the reader read as a character's voice rather than lectures.

Better on: confidence pacing (the standard's rule 5 is met for three of four central questions), the action loop (three theories killed by the player's own tests, two restraint tests with visible responses), early-conclusion count (two instead of four), lecturing (one borderline instead of two), and signal discipline (the plain griefs stayed plain for forty years).

Worse on: coverage. Two conclusions are at 5 and 55-wrong rather than partly. One of those (Conclusion 1) was lost to the combination of a single public body and a wrong gloss in the same scene, which the standard's rules permit but which left no second road. The other (Conclusion 8) is not yet reachable, so it is a pacing fact rather than a failure, but the player's wrong reading went untested for thirty years.

Mixed on: end-state confidence. Three correct conclusions at 80 or above is the standard's target. But two wrong clauses sit inside those 80s and 85s and nothing on the branch could have shaken them.

Plainly: v2 is better. It does what the standard was rewritten to do, at the price of two conclusions that the first test would have delivered and this one withheld. The withholding is mostly correct by the rules; the single-body problem on Conclusion 1 is the one place the price was too high.

## i) Recommended changes

1. Give Conclusion 1 a second public body that surfaces by the middle of the campaign whether or not the player took the earning action in Round 1, so one wrong reading of one honest sighting cannot lock a conclusion for forty years. (The Keeper's notes already propose this.)
2. When a character offers a wrong cause in the same scene as a correct sighting, let the world put a second, different wrong cause beside it within a block. Two wrong glosses that disagree keep the sighting open; one wrong gloss closes it, as it did here.
3. Give the Conclusion 8 line one surfacing in block 3, even a thin one, so the player's reading can be tested against something before the scheduled payoff, instead of being carried at 55 with nothing to push on.
4. Keep the register of late plain-world items flat. Two plain items in Years 38 and 39 were written with a weight the ordinary items around them did not carry, and the reader recruited both into a pattern. Also space the Year 9 cluster across two years and vary the voice of the two repeated refrains.

2. At the Rings in Year 11, give the fisher's sunset gloss a competitor in the same block, such as a Hollin Ford shepherd who says the stones were for the thread and are wrong because the thread moved. Two wrong causes that disagree keep T6 alive at 40; one closed it.
3. One Longwalker surfacing in block 3: a trader who heard a list sung far south, or a Sellen camp where Oldmark's stone bears a name from the Year 8 song. Either gives T4 something to push against before Year 42.
4. Flatten Years 38 and 39 (the namesakes, the crane girl), or cut them; spread the Year 9 chord over Years 9 and 10; and write the god's own felt weight after the first harvest and the hard winter into block 1 (the key's own fix for Conclusion 5's missing seed).
