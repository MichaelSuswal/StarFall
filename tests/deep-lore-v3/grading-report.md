Graded against the 13 rules of the mystery standard (v2) and the 8 lenses of the evaluation standard (v3). Sources: the world files (mysteries, test-world, npc-gods, deep-history, baseline block), and for each lane the four blocks, four reader rounds, the Keeper log and the answer key. ## PART 1. SPOILER-FREE (for the player)

### The five failure modes, one line each

1. A perfect solve rate with no favorite moments: not present; solve rates were middling by design and every round in every lane lists eight to thirteen best moments.
2. A secret no one could have found: one instance (Lane C, Year 11, the god's own sensation), acknowledged and corrected; three further items were unreachable by test structure, not by Keeper choice.
3. A wrong conclusion caused by narration or tone: one at end of game (Lane C's sky-event pattern), traceable to a count reported off its fixed rate; one in the baseline, corrected by a reader's own measurement.

5. NPC gods inconsistent across lanes or irrelevant to the Epic and Long mysteries: not present; all three held their sheets across three lanes and forty years, and two of the three carried their pieces of the Epic into every lane.

Worse: short-mystery resolution by design, and cross-lane consistency, which v2 could not fail.

### Recommended changes (standards or process, not clues)

1. Process. Do not run a no-action baseline block that spans the short mysteries' clocks. Either make the first block three years, or start every short clock after the first action round, or let the reader submit a small set of standing instructions for the baseline years. In v3 four of five shorts were decided before any reader could act, and a one-night Epic chance fell inside the baseline in every lane.

2. Process. Keep one shared ruling ledger, consulted and updated before each lane's block is written, with a required cross-lane check for any trigger that another lane has already ruled on. v3 produced three divergences on the same fixed events, including the world's single clearest Year 40 signal.

3. Standard, rule 7 or the Keeper page. Extend "tone marks nothing" to frequencies: a recurring plain phenomenon is reported at its true fixed rate every year, or not counted at all; and the Keeper never changes a reporting rate or density in response to a reader's out-of-game comments. The baseline's selective count misled all three readers, and one lane's correction of it misled a fourth time.

4. Standard, rule 4. Define "decisive" to include a measured change in the Epic's central body, and require that such a measurement arrive only by an earned action (alliance, a sent counter, the god's own instrument), never stated in a fixed-schedule visitor's letter. In v3 the central body's change reached every lane in Year 25 unearned, and the god's own instrument then confirmed rather than discovered it.

5. Standard, rules 8 and 10, and the Keeper page. Require that the god's own bodily sensations be reported whenever they would occur (the one held-back item was a sensation); and for any standing question that outlasts a round (an envoy's question, a letter, an offer in a dream), require the Keeper to ask the reader out of game before writing the next block rather than infer an answer or supply an intent. Mark every inference in the packet's Keeper notes as "acted for the player."
