# Evaluation standard for blind lore tests (v3)

1. Grade each tier on its own expectations. By Year 40: short mysteries mostly resolved, long ones well advanced, the epic partly understood with execution still ahead.
2. "Missed" is not failure. Measure findability: what actions, focus or cleverness would have found it. A secret no one found is fine. A secret no one could have found was held back, and that is the defect.
3. Run more than one player style on the same sealed world: a focused investigator, a builder who mostly ignores mysteries, a bold risk-taker. The investigator should find more and sooner. The builder should still feel the world's pressure arrive.
4. Separate fair wrong conclusions (a reasonable reading of honest facts) from unfair ones (misleading narration, or a tone that marked something it should not have). Only the second is a defect.
5. Audit the Keeper for neutrality: any resolution shaped by the reader's theories rather than its actions is a defect (rescuing a good guess, punishing a bad one, nudging).
6. Grade the world, not just the mysteries: how much deep history surfaced, whether places and people were specific and alive, whether the reader enjoyed things unconnected to any mystery.
7. Ask about the experience: each reader keeps a list of best moments and of moments it felt bored or pushed. A perfect solve rate with no favorite moments is a failure.
8. Check the NPC gods: did each act by its rolls and traits, stay consistent across forty years, and matter to the epic and long mysteries.
