# Reader Round 2. Lane B, the Builder. Years 11 to 20

Ledger first. Households 1,094 at Hemma's death, 1,117 now. Sheep 4,340 to 4,580. Goats 610 to 840. Cattle 270 to 310. Horses 0 to 23. Two granaries full to the tenth. One bridge. One gate. One revetment. One road to the fen edge. One caravan ground. One census on clay. One bad drought eaten through without hunger at the two granary villages. One good man dead at the piers, one quarryman dead with a wedge, one Rook dead at the scar, one shepherd dead in a fold gate. I have twice the weight I had in Year 10. That is a decade I would sign.

## 1. RESULTS

**Granaries at Tollan and Weir.** Answered clearly and in full. The elders agreed in four days, which told me they had wanted one for years. Tollan roofed early and stayed dry through the Year 12 flood. Weir, unroofed, lost a course and a tenth of its barley, and was rebuilt three courses higher. Then Year 17 drought: both granaries opened, nobody at Tollan or Weir hungry, Lissom and Ferrow Ford fed and resentful. Year 18 Tollan asked for a fifth instead of a tenth. The world taught me three things: roof first, build for the worst flood not the average, and a granary makes politics. The villages that eat from it resent the ones that hold it. That is the next problem and it is a good problem.

**Bridge at Ferrow Ford.** Answered clearly and at a cost. The Year 12 flood took the timber frame and Dall of Greyholt. The footing held. Wat finished it, four stone piers, deck a span above the Year 5 mark, and it stood over a dry bed in Year 17 and through the Year 19 shake. The elders kept the first ford in the water below the bridge, which is right and I did not have to say so. Sheep will not cross planking. Two side effects I did not order: Berit's grandson says the piers back the flood into the Lissom mouth and have soured the lower orchard, and the ferryman bought a second boat. The first I believe and will fix. The second is just a man adapting.

**Vell.** Answered, and the answer was better than my question. No channel is possible; the ground climbs and falls four times. But nine cisterns of sweet cold water in a stone yard, four days up a goat path, is a summer pasture that does not fail. Year 17, three hundred goats went up and three hundred came down fat. The Greyholt elders have stopped saying unlucky. The stone is Scarrow stone and saves nothing. The capped cistern bears the same pattern as the Greyholt headman's hearth stone, and the Greyholt men did not like that. I noted it and left it. A cost I did not price: six Greyholt men were with me on the hill when forty Harl took Greyholt's goats and burned two clamps. Greyholt said so more than once. Lesson: do not strip a border village of men in raiding season.

**Harl gate at Greyholt.** Answered clearly and honestly. Ket is speaker and is called worse things by both sides, which is what a speaker is for. Gorm Longhand set the rate with his knife on the gatepost and has kept it through a drought. The gate did good trade in the dry year and Greyholt now eats moor mutton. But Gorm told me plainly he does not speak for Brude or the deep moor, and he was right: forty sheep in Year 17, ninety sheep and a dead shepherd in Year 20. The gate works for the clan that comes to the gate. It does not work for the ones that do not. And now Gorm's clan has stopped coming, out of shame or guilt. The gate taught me its limit in exactly the words Ket used: it cannot stop a clan that does not come to it.

**Written count.** Answered fully and it outlived its maker. Hemma did it her way, tags hung on cords so she could still feel them, and died with the Tollan cord in her hand two months later. 1,094 tags. The three children are grown. Ede now cuts a year mark on each tag and is building a peg wall so a household can be followed year to year. In Year 14 I got my first real numbers: 1,101 households, 4,340 sheep, 610 goats, 270 cattle. The elders disputed the sheep for a day, found the tags and their cords agreed, and were unhappy about having nothing to argue over. That is the single most satisfying line in twenty years. One gap: the Rooks would not be counted by Tollan children. Scarrow is still half off the books.

**Fire Hollow.** Answered, and taught me the one thing I least expected: Isca does not bargain. She would not take eel for horses. She took the eel as a gift and gave twenty horses and twenty riders as a gift and opened the Reed Crossing on my word, and said it was not for the honey. I came for a trade deal and got a patron. That is both better and worse than what I asked for. The horses themselves are nearly useless on my ground: too steep, too wet, a horse eats what three sheep eat. Two boys can ride. One rider stayed and married into Weir. The real yield was the road east, the forty cattle in Year 19, and the standing of having been called. Also: I saw what a strong god looks like. Eight thousand people singing names a half-beat behind a boy. I do not have that and I now know I want a version of it.

**Ossary revetment.** Answered cleanly and completely. Forty boatloads of Scarrow stone by Greelmen paid in barley, footing through bone the whole length, channel across the neck. Bank held Year 15 and every spring since. The feast has a name, the Dry Bank, and the Fenners come to it. Some 260 skulls in a stone chamber on the hill with an eel on the door. Fourteen tags, three needles, beads, a child's bracelet in a box in the count house. Nobody can read the marks, and the salt men would not hold one. Fine. They are in a box. That is where they belong for now.

**Road and caravan ground.** The spare action, and it paid. Tollan to Weir a half-day, Tollan to the fen edge a day and a half in any weather. Caravan ground with a well and no wall and no threshold, and the salt men slept on it and said nothing, which from them is praise. The pebble cairn moved itself to the edge of the ground and a child counted it: 1,106 against my 1,108. Someone else's census is within two households of mine.

## 2. THEORIES

I still hold most of these loosely, but two of them are now pressing on my people and I will say so.

- **My weight is attendance at the Row, and it compounds.** Confirmed twice over. Year 13 full festival, heaviest ever. Year 20, twice the weight of Year 10. Confidence 95. This is now the spine of the capital plan.
- **The west and east edges erase names, and names held by others survive it.** This has gone from story to pattern in ten years. Ebb lost his wife's name past the last well. Isca's eastern bands came back not knowing their faces and still singing the Song. Three Skelder hunters came back from past the Fell not knowing their bands; their dogs knew them. Eleven Lotfolk of two hundred came back from the west carrying stones with their own names on them and not knowing them. Isca sang my eight villages into the Long Song and offered to sing my dead and households for nothing. The Mother of Dogs names every person at every fire. The Lotfolk god is building a wall of every living name. Three gods are doing the same thing and I am not. Confidence 80 that this is one mechanism and the other gods know it. Confidence 70 that it is why Isca does not bargain: a name given freely is held; a name traded is not. This is pressing. Not because the edge is coming to me, but because a god who does not keep his people's names is behind the other three, and I can see exactly how to catch up with clay and pegs. Ede is already doing it.
- **The north-west thing is being fed by the Lamp and is now being worshipped by people who can reach my folds.** The weak presence that does not move. Col's blue hair. The warm stone at Fallow Dip with the kneeling dream. Brude's clan has built a hut there and feeds two men to tell the dream to raiders. The deep-moor clan said the dream aloud over my fold before they killed Col's nephew. Isca's Voice slept seven nights at the cairn and came back singing a song about a crowd on its knees in a place of many roofs beside a wide slow river with boats, which is not the Olm. Confidence 70 that the stone, the presence, and the dim nights are one thing. Confidence 60 that it is a god that fell, or part of one, and that sleeping near it hands you its memory. This is now pressing, and not as a mystery. It is a cult on my border with a raiding clan behind it, and it killed a shepherd in Year 20. Dim nights have gone from one a year to five.
- **The Lotfolk lake families walked into the north-west and the Lotfolk god sent them there deliberately.** Forty carts in Year 11 to "a lake our god drew for us." Year 16, "gone quiet," ten more sent. The one-color beasts walk to the big water north-west and turn back at the heather. Confidence 65 that the lake is at or near the north-west thing, and that the stone god is spending people to learn what it is, the same way it sent two hundred west by lot. Confidence 50 that the ones who went quiet are alive and nameless. Relevance to me: medium. Someone else is running the experiment. I do not need to.
- **Vell is a lordly town, emptied of its dead, and Greyholt's headman descends from it.** Hearth-stone pattern on the capped cistern. Flat grave with the border cut and nothing inside. No bones anywhere. The Rooks found their dead laid in stone under Lord's Hill with names. Someone took Vell's dead out of it. Confidence 55. Relevance: a goat pasture with a history. Low, unless the Rooks ever hear about the hearth stone, in which case it becomes a second torc.
- **The Rooks' claim is real as politics and cannot be settled by stone contracts.** The scraped black board, eleven torcs, the children in straw torcs at the Driving. Two quarries, one stone at the Row, back to back. Three dead over it. Confidence 85 that this gets worse before it gets better. I have been buying from the nephew and letting Weir buy from the Rooks, which is a way of paying both halves not to fight and it is not working. Pressing.
- **The one-color beasts.** Still two peoples refusing to eat them, now with more herds than ever, walking toward the big water and turning back at the heather like a thing on a lead. Confidence 40 that they are made things. Confidence 15 that I care this decade. They stay in the back of the book.
- **The Brann Cut bell is fading because the cut silted.** The world closed that thread for me without my asking. Confidence 60 that it is a drowned settlement and that the Ossary bones are from the same event. Relevance: nil.
- **The spotted eel are gone because there were two pike.** Or because the tarn changed. Confidence 50 either way. I did not do anything and the spotted eel stayed gone. Low, but it is a lost tithe and I want it noted.

## 3. WHICH OBSERVATIONS MATTER

### Matter to my plans

- Weight compounds with attendance. Build the Row up, not out.
- Three other gods keep names and I do not. Ede's peg wall is already half a Long Song. Finish it.
- Fallow Dip is now a shrine with a raiding clan behind it, and the deep moor is bypassing my gate. Ninety sheep and a dead shepherd in Year 20. The Backs' north end is exposed.
- Gorm's clan has stopped coming to the yard. If I lose Gorm I lose the only Harl who kept a treaty through a drought.
- Granary politics. Tollan wants a fifth. Weir asks who carries it. Lissom and Ferrow Ford resent eating from it. The answer is more granaries, not a bigger tithe into two.
- The piers have backed the flood into the Lissom mouth and the lower orchard is sour. Lissom cut forty-one trees. That is my bridge's debt.
- The Year 19 shake cracked a millstone and nothing else. The piers and granary walls are sound. One shake in twenty years. Note it, keep walking the piers.
- The Rooks will not be counted and will not run at the Driving. Half of Scarrow is off the tags and off the festival in spirit, and I am heavier when every village is at the Row.
- Isca's cattle. 310 head and a murrain across the Greel in Year 16 that did not cross. Dun Mere is selling tarn water in jars. The valley's stock is sound and the lowland's is not, which means the valley can sell cattle east if the road holds.
- The road. Tollan to the fen edge. It needs to go to the Reed Crossing and it needs to go up to Ferrow Ford.
- Horses eat what three sheep eat and cannot work the Backs. Twenty-three horses are a prestige herd. Put them on the road where the ground is flat.

### Just the world

- The fox under the granary. Fed against orders. Leave it.
- The twins settled seniority by a fight and the elder lost. The Dun Mere man and his old dog in the fog. Reyn singing her child's name at the river and the Weir women copying her. The grandson who admitted his grandmother was right about bland. All of it good and none of it an input.
- The wolf-dog that died with its head to the north. I noticed. I am not going to do anything about a dead dog.
- Oss died and the new Lotfolk trader charges more. Traders die.
- The Skelder theology about whether the three hunters are holy or punished. Their problem.
- The Ruun song about a crowd on its knees in a city of many roofs. Beautiful and from somewhere else.
- Whether Vell's dead were taken out. Not a plan item until it is.

## 4. ACTIONS. Years 21 to 30

1. **Build a stone count house at the Stone Row, one room with a peg wall for every household tag in the valley, and have Ede's children read the names of the dead and the newborn aloud at every harvest festival after the Driving is told.** Reason: the three gods I have met all keep their people's names and the two edges of the world eat names. I have clay tags and I have a festival that every village already attends and that makes me heavier. Put the names into the thing that already feeds me. Do it in stone from the nephew's cuts and let the Rooks' children carry the tags for Scarrow if they will not be counted by Tollan's. Pay for it from the Tollan granary's surplus.
2. **Raise a third and fourth granary, at Lissom and at Ferrow Ford, and keep the tithe at a tenth in each village instead of a fifth into two.** Reason: the villages that ate from Tollan in Year 17 resent it and Tollan wants more of their grain. Four granaries at a tenth each hold more than two at a fifth and nobody carries barley down the valley to someone else's shed. Roof them before the walls are dry. Build Ferrow Ford's on the bridge bank where the carts already come.
3. **Fold the Backs' north end into a walled summer steading with a stone fold and a watch post, and station Col's people and six of the horses there in raiding season.** Reason: ninety sheep and a dead shepherd. The deep moor comes to the folds, not the gate. The horses can at least carry word to Greyholt and Ferrow Ford faster than a running boy. Have Ket carry an offer to Gorm: if his clan summers its sheep inside the new fold alongside mine, they are inside my wall when the deep moor comes, and the yard at Greyholt reopens with no questions asked about Year 20. I want Gorm back more than I want to be right.
4. **Cut a relief channel at the Lissom mouth below the piers, lined with Scarrow stone, and pay Lissom in blood plum stock and the first year's tithe forgiveness for the forty-one trees.** Reason: my bridge soured their orchard. A god who builds owns what the building breaks. Have Wat do it; he knows the river better than anyone and he owes nothing but I want him on every water job in the valley for the rest of his life.
5. **Push the road from the fen edge to the Reed Crossing landing, and from Tollan up to the bridge at Ferrow Ford, and set a toll in kind at the caravan ground: one skin of salt in forty for the use of the well and ground.** Reason: Isca's road is open on my word, the lowland lost its cattle and mine are sound, and a cart road from the Backs to the Greel means valley cattle and eel go east on wheels. The salt men will pay a well toll where they would not pay a gate toll. Put the horses on the road as a cart team; it is the one ground in the valley they are good for.
6. **Send Ket and the Greyholt headman, with iron and no soldiers, to Brude's clan at Fallow Dip to buy the hut out: iron for the two dream-tellers to come and live at the Greyholt yard and tell the dream there to anyone who asks, where I can listen to it.** Reason: I cannot stop the deep moor from sleeping on a warm stone, but the men who turn the dream into a war song live in a hut, and a hut can be bought. If Brude will not sell, I have at least heard the price, and I want to hear the dream told in full once, because a crowd kneeling beside a wide slow river with boats is a city, and a city near my border that nobody has seen is a thing I should know about before Isca's Voice tells her first. I will not go to the stone myself and I will not send anyone to sleep by it.
7. **Settle Scarrow by splitting it on the ledger: two tags' worth of village, two stones at the Row, and one quarry contract each, the nephew's cuts for Tollan and the Row works, the Rooks' cuts for the road metal where worse stone is good enough.** Reason: Scarrow is already two villages. Pretending otherwise has killed three men. Give the Rooks their own stone at the Row to stand on and their own count, and the price of it is that their children are on the tags and their elder tells the Driving with everyone else. If they want to stand by the fire in straw torcs, they can do it on their own stone. Confidence this works: 50. Confidence the current arrangement works: 10.

If I get a free hand beyond those seven: ask Isca, through Reyn, in the form of a gift and not a trade, for a copy of what the Voice sang after Fallow Dip. And send one goat-herder up to Vell each summer with instructions to count the cisterns and nothing else.

## 5. BEST MOMENTS and BORED OR PUSHED

### Best moments

- The elders finding the tags and the cords agreed and having nothing to argue about, and being unhappy. The whole point of a census in one sentence.
- Hemma dying with the Tollan cord in her hand two months after she gave up the method she loved. She did it anyway. That is what a good administrator does.
- Three hundred goats up to Vell and three hundred down fat, and the Greyholt elders saying nothing about unlucky hills since. A seven-year-old's story paid off in goats.
- Isca refusing to bargain and then giving me twenty horses. I went in as a trader and came out having learned a rule about this world that no trade would have taught me.
- The salt men sleeping on the caravan ground and saying nothing.
- The pebble cairn counting 1,106 against my 1,108. Someone else's ledger agreeing with mine to within two.
- Wat going into the flood after Dall and bringing him out dead and not stopping work. Then setting the last pier stone himself. Then whitewashing the children's charcoal off the piers in the drought.
- The Rooks' children standing by the fire in straw torcs while everyone else's ran shrieking into the dark, and the silence, and the Driving going on. That is a political crisis delivered as a children's game, and it is the best thing in the block.
- Gorm Longhand's joke about his fold gate pointing at nothing for five grandfathers. I do not know what it means and for once I enjoyed not knowing.

### Bored or pushed

- The name-loss pattern arrived four times in ten years from four directions: Ebb, Isca's bands, the Skelder hunters, the Lotfolk eleven. By the third I had it. By the fourth I felt lectured. It is a good mechanic and it did not need the fourth witness.
- Dim nights: four, four, five, four, five, four, five. The count is being kept at me and it has flattened. I am no longer worried and I am no longer curious. If it is going to matter, let it do something.
- The one-color beasts got a third and fourth mention with nothing new except "more than ever." Either the beasts do something or stop telling me about them.
- The Voice's song about the kneeling crowd and the river came to me twice, from the Harl and from the Ruun riders. I would have preferred to hear it once in full than twice in summary.
- The Lotfolk lake families "gone quiet" is the narrator holding a door open. I will not walk through it. Somebody else sent them.
- Nothing bored me on the building side. Every action I took got a real answer with a real cost, and the costs were ones I should have foreseen and did not: the Greyholt men away during the raid, the Lissom orchard, the granary resentment. That is the right kind of punishment.

## 6. MOOD

The world gave me work and it gave me pressure, and the pressure came from my own buildings, which is the mood I want. The granary made a tithe quarrel. The bridge made a sour orchard and a ferryman with two boats. The gate made a treaty with one clan and a target for another. The count made a village that will not be counted. None of that was dragged in from outside. I did it and now I own it.

Where I felt dragged: toward the north-west. The Fallow Dip hut, the Voice's seven nights, the Lotfolk lake, the dim nights, the one-color beasts walking toward the big water. Five threads all with their hands pointing the same direction. I am going to deal with the hut because it killed a shepherd, and I am going to buy the dream-tellers because that is cheaper than a war. I am not going to the stone. If the world wants me there it will have to come and get me, and so far it has only sent a cult and a count of dim nights.

Where I felt the narrator was fair: Isca. She was built as a peer with a different rule set and she played it straight, and the consequence of her rule is now a design problem for my capital, not a mystery to be solved. That is how I like lore to arrive: as a constraint on the next thing I build.

Overall: busy, heavier, slightly resentful of being counted at, and with a count house to build. I know what the next ten years look like. Most of it is stone.
