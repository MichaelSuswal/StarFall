# Block 3. Years 21 to 30

## Year 21. The 332nd year after the Driving

- The count house went up on the river meadow at the end of the Stone Row, one room of the nephew's grey stone with a door toward the stones and a peg wall inside it floor to roof, eleven hundred pegs and room for more. The Tollan elders agreed to the building and then argued for a month about the door, which they said should not face the stones, because nothing on the meadow faces the stones but the people. It faces the stones. The old count house in the village keeps the ell and the eel tithe and the box from Ossary; the tags moved to the meadow in the ninth month, Ede carrying the Tollan cord herself.
- Ket and the Greyholt headman went up the moor road in the fifth month with two ponies of iron and no spears, nine days to Cold Hand, where they camped, and a half-day on to Fallow Dip by daylight, as you said, and did not sleep there. Brude is old now and sat with them in front of the hut. He would not sell. He said the two men in the hut are not sheep and the dream is not his to sell, that it is the Harl's dream, dreamed on Harl ground, and that the Low One's god may come and lie down on the stone like any man if it wants the dream, and that the price for that is the same iron the eastern riders paid. He took the iron the headman had brought as a gift and gave them a sheep. The two tellers, for a knife each, told the dream to Ket in full outside the hut by day, which is what they are fed for. Ket says it goes so: a crowd kneeling, more people than he has words for, on wide stone steps that go down to a river wider and slower than the Greel, with flat boats on it and roofs beyond it without end; they are kneeling toward a place at the top of the steps and waiting for someone to come and stand in it; roads full of people walking behind something; and at the end a hand that was lifted up turns and there is a pain in the side, and the dream ends there every time. The tellers say it is the Low One's own dream, that the Low One sleeps and the stone is its pillow, and that whoever sleeps on the pillow is let into the dream, and that the pain is the Low One's grudge against the men who hung it up. Ket says the deep-moor men like the ending best.
- Granary work began at Lissom in the fourth month, roofed before the walls were dry as you said, on the rise above the orchards. Ferrow Ford's was begun on the bridge bank where the carts come in, on a footing of five courses.
- Wat cut the relief channel at the Lissom mouth in the low water of the first month, a stone-lined cut below the piers through the gravel bar that the Olm throws up there, and the spring flood went through it and the Lissom mouth did not back up. Lissom took the blood plum stock and the year's tithe forgiveness and planted the stumps and the lower orchard, and will say in two years whether the channel has done anything, since a plum does not bear in one.
- The Skelder came to the spring market and said the Mother of Dogs went north herself two years ago with forty of her people and came back this winter with twenty-eight, all of them like the three, able to speak and not knowing their own names, and with fewer dogs than she went out with, and that she has not said why she went and has not stopped looking north. The Skelder are frightened of her for the first time and love her no less.
- The Lotfolk trader at the autumn market says his god's wall is finished around the city, a ring of cut stone a man high with every living Lotfolk name on it, six thousand and more, and that the whole city stands at the wall at dusk and says the names, and that his own is on the east side near a gate. He says a stone name is a name the lot cannot take. He paid the well toll with a cast of his stones and said the stones had been generous.
- Dim nights: four.

## Year 22

- The first reading of names. At the harvest festival, after the Driving was told on the third night, Ede's three, who are grown with children of their own, stood before the count house door with the year's tags in baskets and read out the dead of every village, then the newborn, by village from Greyholt down to Ossary and then the Fenners who were there, a hundred and sixty names, which took the length of a long meal. People wept at their own dead and laughed at some of the baby names. Reyn of Weir sang each Weir name after it was read, as the Ruun do, and by the Ossary names half the meadow was doing it. You were heavier after than after any festival before it, and you felt the difference as weight and as nothing else.
- Isca's answer to the asking you sent through Reyn came in the sixth month: nine singers and a herald, who spoke for most of an hour to the sense that the fire of the Olm had asked for a thing and asked nothing for it, and that Isca gives what is asked in that way and keeps nothing back. The nine sang the Voice's song in full at the Row that night, standing in a ring. It is not a list. It is the dream Ket brought down from the moor, sung: the steps, the river, the kneeling, the roads, the hand and the pain in the side, with much in it besides that the tellers did not have, about being wanted, about a name said by every mouth at once. The herald said, with care, that this is the Voice's song and not the Song, that Isca does not sing it, and that it is given because it was asked and for no other reason. The singers would not sing it a second time and left in the morning.
- Gorm Longhand came back to the yard in the fifth month when Ket carried your offer up the ridge, and sat in Ket's house and heard it out. He said his clan will not summer its sheep inside a wall on the Olm side of the hills, that a Harl inside a wall is a captive whatever the gate is called, and that his fold is on the ridge where his five grandfathers' is, and he will not leave it for a stone one. But he said the yard is open again and the rate stands, and he gave Ket this: when the deep moor moves south through the ridge toward the Backs, his boys will see it, and one of them will run to Greyholt ahead of it, for iron, and no questions asked by either side about the twentieth year.
- The summer steading on the Backs' north end was finished: a stone fold for eight hundred sheep, a wall a man high around a yard with huts in it, a watch post on the knoll above it that sees the moor edge and the Hagg Ridge saddle. Col's people are in it from the fourth month to the ninth with six horses and two of the boys who can ride. Col is old and sits on the wall.
- The Skelder at the spring market had a question they asked at every stall, as if told to: whether any god has gone far and come back. The Olm folk said theirs went to the Fire Hollow and came back with horses, and the Skelder wrote nothing down and asked it again at the next stall.
- The lower orchard at Lissom bore well, the blood plum stock took on the stumps, and Lissom says the channel cured the sour. The Lissom granary was filled to the tenth.
- Dim nights: five.

## Year 23

- A great storm in the ninth month, out of the east, two days of wind and a night of rain on top of it. The fen flooded to the Backs' foot and two Fenner steadings on the south side went under with their people, eleven dead. The Ossary bank held and the channel ran full and Ossary took in the Fenners who got out. The road along the fen's dry edge lost a half-day of its length to the water and was found under a hand of silt when the fen went down. The Ferrow Ford granary, roofed in the spring, stood in water to its third course for a day and lost nothing, the barley being above it. The Tollan granary's fox was drowned in the dry course, and the granary children buried it with a stone.
- Deep-moor Harl came for the Backs' folds in the eighth month, before the storm. Gorm's boy ran into Greyholt a day ahead of them and the horses carried the word to the steading by dusk. The sheep were inside the stone fold and the wall was manned, and the Harl, who were sixty, took twenty-two sheep from an outlying fold that had not come in and went back up the ridge without a fight. Ket says they had a hut man with them and that he said the dream on the saddle before they came down, and that it did them no good against a wall.
- The road toward the Reed Crossing: the stretch from the fen edge to the Olm mouth was raised and graveled before the storm and survived it, and the first mile along the Greel's west bank was begun, which is wet ground and takes four times the stone. A valley cart with two of the horses went to the Reed Crossing and back with salt in the seventh month, nine days each way, and the carters came home saying the horses were good for it and the Greelmen did not like it.
- Twelve cattle went east with Greelmen from the Olm mouth in the sixth month and sold at the Reed Crossing for bronze and salt at a price the valley has not seen, the lowland herds being still thin from the murrain. Dun Mere sent tarn water with them in jars and the Greelmen drank it on the way.
- Aldo Rook came to the Row at the count with his half of Scarrow behind him and his children carrying Scarrow's tags, forty-one households, which Ede hung on the pegs under a mark the Rooks cut themselves, a bird. The Tollan elders would not let anyone stand on the eighth stone, which no one has stood on since the Driving, and said so for most of a day. The ninth stone came down from the Rooks' own cuts in the tenth month on a sledge and was set at the Row's end past the eighth, standing a little apart, and Aldo stood on it. He told you before the Row that his family will stand and be counted and will carry its tags and that it will tell the Driving when the Driving is told true, and not before, and that his children may stand by the fire if they like. The nephew's half kept its stone and its contract. The tags say Scarrow and Scarrow Hill. No one has died over stone this year.
- Dim nights: four.

## Year 24

- In the third month a Skelder hunter came down the moor road alone with a dog at heel and forty pelts on a sledge and a grey-white pup in a basket on top of them, and came through the Greyholt gate and down the valley to the count house, where he set the pelts down, set the pup on them, and asked, in these words and no others, with his hands at his sides: "Does your god remember the sky?" He will say nothing else. Asked anything, he says he is to carry back one answer, and he has gone to Ket's house at the Greyholt gate to wait for it until the thaw. The pup eats, from Ket's hand.
- Garran the stone master died in the first month on his wall and Scarrow and Scarrow Hill both kept the silent day, on the same day, which is the first thing they have done together in twelve years.
- The Ruun riders who came with Isca's greeting in the sixth month were fewer, seven, and their herald spoke of the Gathering in a form that took less time than usual. They said that the Voice has left the Gathering with eleven bands and holds his own fire at the Reed Crossing of the Greel, where the Kneeling Song is sung every night and the Long Song is not, and that Isca has not yet gone to him. They sang the Long Song's Olm names at the Row and did not sing the Voice's song, and when a Weir child asked for it they looked at the herald and he said nothing.
- The road's end. The valley carters coming back from the Reed Crossing in the ninth month say the Crossing is a camp now, eleven bands of Ruun with their herds on the east bank and tents on the west where the caravans ford, a fire in the middle that is kept the height of a man, and a young man who sings every night, and that everyone who fords is asked to kneel at the fire and most do because it is quicker. The salt caravans forded and knelt and said nothing. The carters did not kneel and were let through.
- The lower orchard at Lissom bore poorly in a wet year and Berit's grandson says the channel took the water away too fast. The blood plum on the stumps bore well. The Lissom granary and the Ferrow Ford granary were both filled to the tenth, and Tollan and Weir held theirs, and for the first time no barley went down the valley on anyone's back.
- The second count of names at the festival, a hundred and seventy-one, and the whole meadow singing them after the Weir women now. The Fenners asked for their dead to be read and brought a cord of their own, knotted, which Ede has hung on a peg.
- Dim nights: five.

## Year 25

- In the fifth month three men came up the road from the south in grey robes with a bag of stones each and a sheet of lead rolled in a cloth, and asked at the caravan ground for the house of the Olm's god and were brought to the count house. They are Lotfolk counters from the city of Ennow and they speak in a form you have not heard, precise, old, and a little small, as if reciting manners learned from a book. The lead sheet is cut in the same form. The sense of it: the god of the Lots greets the god of the Olm and proposes a count of gods, each to tell the others what it has measured; that the Olm valley holds, at the last autumn, one thousand one hundred and forty-two households, which is the number on your pegs to the one; and that in exchange for what the god of the Olm has measured, the god of the Lots will give "the height of the Coin in thirteen years" and "what the stones carried back." The three counters say they will wait for the answer at the caravan ground as long as it takes and have begun, with permission or without it, to count the carts.
- In the eighth month fire at the Reed Crossing. The valley carters at the Olm mouth saw it from five days off as a light on the eastern sky for two nights. The Greelmen say Isca came to the Crossing as fire in the night with two thousand riders and the camp burned, tents and herds and people, and that the Voice is dead and two hundred with him, and that the eleven bands that lived are scattered down the Greel, and that the ford is open and no one kneels there now. The salt caravans came to Tollan late and did not speak of it. Isca's riders did not come to the valley this year. The Greelmen say that at the Gathering the Voice's name was sung into the Long Song by Isca herself and that her voice broke on it and she sang on.
- The Skelder hunter left Ket's house at the thaw, in the fourth month, and went up the moor road alone with his dog, carrying whatever you had given him to carry. The pup stayed, with Ket.
- Col died on the steading wall in the seventh month in his sleep, in the sun, and was buried on the Backs where he could see the folds. Pim's man leads the summer people now. Pim is forty-one and counts the Vell cisterns every summer with the goats as you asked: nine cold, one fallen, one capped, and this year nothing new.
- The road along the Greel's west bank reached the Reed Crossing landing in the seventh month, a month before the fire, five days from the Olm mouth, raised through the wet in stone. The toll at the caravan ground, a skin in forty for the well, has been paid every autumn by every caravan without a word, and this year came to thirty-one skins.
- The count: 1,142 households, as the lead sheet said. Sheep 4,900. Goats 1,010. Cattle 290 after the sales east. Horses 26.
- Dim nights: four.

## Year 26

- The thaw came late and with rain on snow and the Olm rose higher than anyone living has seen it, higher than the Year 3 flood by a man's height, and stayed up nine days. The Ferrow Ford bridge: the piers stood and the deck went, the whole span of planking taken off in the first night and found in pieces at Weir. Wat was on the bank. The Ferrow Ford granary stood in water to its roof course and lost a quarter of its barley and all of its floor. The Lissom channel ran like a river and the Lissom mouth did not back up. The Tollan and Weir granaries were dry. At Ossary the stone face held the whole length of the village and the channel across the neck ran full and over, and the river, finding no way through the stone, went around it: it took the bank above the village, upstream of the revetment's end, where no one had faced it because no one lived there, and the bank came away for two hundred paces in one night and the hill behind it opened, and there were graves in it, a hundred and more, in rows, with their bones and their clay tags and their beads, and most went into the river and down into the fen. Ossary spent the spring gathering what came ashore. The headman says the dead were buried there to be near the river and the river has taken them home, which he says to the children.
- In the sixth month Isca came through the valley. Two thousand Ruun on horses came up your road from the Reed Crossing in a column a day long, with the fire among them by night and the tall woman in the iron band by day, and camped on the caravan ground and the river meadow and ate for three days what the valley brought them, and gave for it horses and bronze and cloth beyond what was eaten. She stood on the meadow before the Row, not on a stone, and spoke to you and the valley for the length of an hour. The sense of it was that the Harl and their stone had made the Voice into what he became and that she was going to the moor to unmake what had made him, and that the fire of the Olm, who kept its own hearth and had kept faith, was asked to ride with her if it chose and honored if it did not. You did not go. She said it was well, with great ceremony, and the column went up the valley, over the bridge Wat had redecked in the spring, through the Greyholt gate, which was taken down to let the horses through two abreast and set back up, and over Hagg Ridge onto the moor. Gorm's boy had run ahead of it, as his father promised, and Gorm's clan was gone north off the ridge with its sheep before the first rider reached the fold, and the Ruun slept a night inside the fold and left it as they found it.
- Eleven days later the pull in the north-west moved. You have felt it since the night you came, the weak one that did not move and did not answer. In the seventh month it moved, and came south, and came down the moor road with the column, and crossed the ridge, and came through Greyholt's gate, and down the valley, and camped on the meadow a night. Twenty Ruun carried a litter among them with a stone on it the size of a sheep's body, blue-grey, under hides. Isca said before the Row that the Harl cairn was burned and the two tellers and six men of Brude's clan were dead, and that she had tried to break the stone as fire and it would not break, and that she was taking it home. The twenty who carried it had the look of men who had not slept. That night half of Tollan dreamed the kneeling crowd and the steps and the river, the whole meadow's worth of people, and said so in the morning, and the column went down the road east and the pull went with it, and is east now, far, and does not move.
- The Harl. Ket came down from the yard in the eighth month and said the moor is saying that the Olm opened its gate to the fire, which it did. Gorm's clan is back on the ridge and comes to the yard and is called the Olm's dogs by the deep moor. Brude is dead, not of the fire but of age, that winter. The hut at Fallow Dip is ash and the cairn is scattered and the Dip is a hollow with nothing in it.
- Pim counted the cisterns in the eighth month and the fallen one had water in it for the first time, from the melt, and the nine were cold.
- Dim nights: five.

## Year 27

- Harl came down on Greyholt in the fourth month, a hundred of them from four clans, in the dark, and burned the yard, Ket's house, the well cover and the charcoal clamps above the village, and threw down the yard wall, and took every goat on the hill, and went. Ket got out with the pup. No one of Greyholt was killed; two Harl were, on the wall. Gorm's clan was not among them and was raided itself on the ridge a month later by the same clans and lost sheep. The gate stands. The yard is a burned flat. Ket lives in Greyholt proper now and says the moor will not come to a yard for a long time and will come to the folds instead, and that the Backs steading had better be manned.
- Isca's riders came in the sixth month, twelve, and the herald spoke in full form again, and they brought thirty horses and sang at the Row, the Long Song's Olm names and, for the first time, a Harl list: the names of the men killed at the cairn, which Isca has put in the Song because she cannot not. The Weir child who asked for the Voice's song two years ago is older and did not ask.
- The one-color beasts crossed the moor again, Gorm's people said at the yard's ash, and walked over the empty Dip.
- The Skelder at the spring market hum now when they work, and when asked what, say it is the names of their camps and their own, which the Mother has them sing at every fire every night in a tune the traders say came from the singers who crossed the Fell twenty years ago. They say she is making ready for something and feeds everyone and smiles less.
- The Ferrow Ford granary was rebuilt on a footing of nine courses, and Ferrow Ford moved it back from the bank twenty paces to do it, which the carters dislike. Wat redecked the bridge in oak a span higher than before and said that if the river wants it again it can have it again.
- The names read at the festival: two hundred and four, the Fenner storm dead among them for the third year because the Fenners ask it. You were heavier.
- Dim nights: four.

## Year 28

- Locusts on the Tallgrass, the Greelmen say, the grass eaten to the root across the plateau, and the Ruun have brought their herds down off it into the wet lowland along the Greel's east bank, forty bands' worth of cattle, and the lowland is trampled and the Ruun are hungry. Isca's riders came in the ninth month asking, in full form, for grain, and the four granaries sent a tenth of their tenth east by cart on your road, and Isca's herald said the fire of the Olm would be remembered for it, and the riders left ten horses the valley had not asked for and cannot feed.
- The Lotfolk trader says his god's priesthood has doubled and that every Lotfolk house now says the count at night, the wall names, the pins above the Bowl, and the lost, and that the city is very loud at dusk. He says the three counters are still at Tollan because the lot has not told them to leave. They are. They have counted the carts, the horses, the pegs, the stones in the Row, and the swifts at Scarrow, and they pay for their bread.
- The Rooks have laid a footing of their own stone on Lord's Hill inside the fallen wall, where the hall was, and Scarrow Hill says it is a sheep shelter and Scarrow says it is a hall. Nothing stands on it yet but the footing.
- Wat's son made his first ford, in the water, below the bridge, with the village on the bridge.
- The Dun Mere eel fair had no spotted eel for the eighteenth year and the fishers have stopped saying pike.
- Dim nights: five.

## Year 29

- A quiet flood and a good barley. The four granaries full to the tenth, with the Ferrow Ford one dry on its nine courses.
- The Skelder came to the spring market and said the Mother asked every trader again whether any god had gone far and come back, and that word has come to the wood from the south that something is wrong at the big water past the Fell, which they would not say more about, and that the Mother heard it and was quiet for a day and then began counting dogs.
- Ket, with Gorm's leave, took the Skelder pup, which is a hound now and grey-white and long in the leg and eats, up to the Backs steading, where it sits on the watch knoll all summer looking north and will not work sheep.
- Reyn of Weir's child, fifteen, sings the Weir names at the festival now in her mother's place, and the names read this year were one hundred and ninety-eight, and the count house peg wall is full and Ede has begun a second wall on the back of the first.
- The black-legged fox is in Tollan's lanes again, a young one, and the children have named it after the old one.
- Pim counted the cisterns: nine cold, one with a hand of water in it, one capped.
- Dim nights: four.

## Year 30. The 341st year after the Driving

- The count: 1,173 households. Sheep 5,100. Goats 920 after the Greyholt raid. Cattle 340. Horses 61, which is too many, and the Backs steading, the road, and three villages have them. Barley in four granaries to the tenth. Salt toll this year thirty-four skins. Ede is fifty and has named the Lissom tag-keeper's daughter to follow her.
- The Lotfolk counters at the caravan ground have been there five years and have outlived one trader and seen another take his place at the cairn, which is still built at the boundary stone each autumn and which they count and compare with the pegs and write on lead. They have asked again, in their small old form, whether the god of the Olm has an answer to carry south.
- At Greyholt the yard has been rebuilt as a plain wall with no roof and no house, and Gorm's people come to it in daylight only. Ket says the deep moor has been quiet two summers and that quiet from the moor is the thing he likes least.
- Isca's riders came with the Song in the sixth month and sang the Olm names and the Harl names and the Voice's name, which they sing differently from the rest, slower, and the herald spoke of a Gathering that now takes two nights.
- Lissom pressed in the tenth month and the blood plum on the old grey stumps is the best in the orchard, and Berit's grandson, who is old, says the grey was never any good anyway and his grandmother was wrong about everything.
- The harvest festival filled the meadow past the ninth stone and the names, two hundred and nine, were read in full with the whole valley singing them, and the Driving was told as always, and the Rooks' elder, who is Aldo's son now, Aldo being dead this spring, stood on the ninth stone and did not tell it and did not stop it. You are heavier than at any time since you came, and you feel the weight as weight.
- Dim nights: five.
