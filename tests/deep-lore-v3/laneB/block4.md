# Block 4. Years 31 to 40

## Year 31. The 342nd year after the Driving

- A hard winter. The ford froze in the eleventh month of last year and did not open until the third month of this. Weir's pond froze to the bottom and the carp were dug out alive again. Four old people died in the steadings above Ferrow Ford and two children at Dun Mere. The four granaries fed the valley through it and Greyholt, which had lost its goats, ate from Lissom's.
- The three Lotfolk counters came to the count house in the second month when you sent for them, and Ede and her three read them the tags, every year's from the twelfth, which took eleven days, and the counters cut it into lead with small knives, three sheets a day, and asked the spelling of nothing because they have no letters, only marks for numbers and a mark for each village that they made up on the first day and kept. When it was done the eldest of them, Harl by his face though Lotfolk by his robe, unrolled a sheet of their own and read it in their small old manner. The height of the Coin: in the second year of the god of the Lots, two bronze pins were set in the cliff above the Bowl and a third to look through, sighted on the Coin; in the ninth year the Coin stood lower in the third pin's eye by less than the breadth of the eye; in the twenty-second year it stood lower again, and the second lowering was greater than the first, "by the eye of a pin in thirteen years." What the stones carried back: in the sixteenth year two hundred Lotfolk went west by lot with their names cut on stones, and in the nineteenth year eleven came back carrying eleven stones, and the stones had their names and the people had not, and the names were these, and he read eleven names. Then, unasked, a third sheet: the thirty families sent to the lake in the eleventh year and the ten in the fourteenth, by name, forty households; in the fourteenth year their children pointed instead of calling; in the twenty-fifth year the traders found they had no names for each other and fished the lake's edge and not its middle like the people already there; in the thirtieth year, this past summer, nine of them walked into the water in their sleep on three nights and were not found, and the Tally House has written their names with the lost. He said the god of the Lots grieves each by name and has sent no more. He said that the god of the Lots accepts the count of the Olm as a gift and will carry it south, and asks in return for one child of the valley to come to Ennow and learn to cut marks, and that one of the three will stay at Tollan to count, which he has been doing for six years anyway. Ede's niece, Nell, who is ten and can already read the pegs, went south with the two in the fifth month with a Lotfolk trader, by lot among six children who were willing, the counters would have it no other way.
- Ket took thirty moor sheep up Hagg Ridge in the fifth month, as a gift and not a rate, and Gorm Longhand, who is old and walks with a stick, took them into his fold and said that a gift between folds is a thing the moor understands and the valley had not done before, and gave Ket a ewe back so that it should not be a debt. His clan still comes to the bare wall at Greyholt by daylight.
- Thirty-one horses went east in the sixth month by your road to the Reed Crossing with the carters and Reyn's daughter to speak Ruun, and were sold to the bands in the lowland for bronze and salt and forty head of cattle, the Ruun being short of horses since the locusts and the Crossing fire. Reyn's daughter said to Isca's herald, in as much of the full form as she could manage, that the fire of the Olm has horses enough for its ground and would hold any other gift the dearer. The herald heard it out and said nothing and took it east.
- The Ossary bank. The Greelmen hauled stone all summer, the nephew's cuts working for nothing else, and the facing went in above the village for two hundred paces by the count, through the scar the river made, which was bone and tags the whole way down and was gathered as before. A second stone chamber on the hill beside the first, with an eel on the door. The headman will not read the tags because no one can; he has counted them, two hundred and twelve from the scar and the shore, and says he will say the number at the Row and that the number is the names.
- Wat drew the arches on the count house floor in charcoal in the winter, four spans on four piers, and Garran's nephew, who is old, said the valley has never turned an arch and that a corbel is honest and an arch is a trick, and Wat said the river has had two honest decks. Stone from the nephew's cuts began coming down in the tenth month.
- In the ninth month six Skelder with dogs came down the moor road a month before their season and asked at Greyholt the way to the Row, and stood at the back of the meadow at the harvest festival while the names were read and sung, two hundred and fourteen, and watched, and did not sing, and said when asked that the Mother had sent them "to see the stone house," and went home the next day.
- Dim nights: five.

## Year 32

- The pull moved again in the fifth month. It had sat far east and unmoving for six years. It came west and nearer along the line of the Greel over a few days and stopped, and it is nine days off now, where the road ends, and it does not answer. The carters coming home from the Reed Crossing in the sixth month said that Ruun had come to the Crossing with a litter under hides and a flat boat and had rowed out into the Greel where the Voice's fire had been and tipped the litter's load into the river, a stone the size of a sheep's body, and gone back east singing, and that the boat Greelmen say the water is deep there and the mud deeper.
- The first arch was turned at Ferrow Ford over the winter's low water on a timber centering, the nephew's stone cut to Wat's drawing, and when the centering was struck in the third month the arch stood for the length of a breath and came down into the river. No one was under it. Wat sat on the bank for a day and then drew it again, thicker at the haunch, and the second try, in the eleventh month, stood when the centering was struck and is standing.
- A lead sheet came north with the Lotfolk trader in the autumn, cut in the small old form, from the god of the Lots. The sense of it, as the Tollan counter read it: that the count of the Olm is laid in the Tally House beside the count of the Lotfolk; that the god of the Lots has heard how the Olm counts gods, by what each keeps and does not lose, and has made the two columns for itself and for the gods it knows of, and finds the second column the longer in every case, its own not least; that it is nevertheless building; that Nell of Lissom cuts a clean mark and is well; and that the lot has lately begun to fall as the god of the Lots would have it fall, which it records without yet knowing what to make of it.
- The Greyholt hill steading was finished: a stone fold for the goats on the shoulder above the village with a watch post that sees the gate and the moor road's first mile, manned from the fourth month. Ket's house stands again inside Greyholt's wall, of stone this time, and Ket is headman's speaker for life with a skin of salt in ten from the toll, which Greyholt calls a great deal and Ket calls late.
- The Ossary facing went on, two hundred paces more above last year's, into bank that had never given and had no bone in it, and was done by the count. Four hundred paces of stone above the village and the whole village faced below. Ossary's headman said the number of the chamber dead at the Row, two hundred and twelve, before the valley's names were read, and the meadow was quiet for it.
- Scarrow Hill took your offer in part. Aldo's son said a hall of their own stone on their own footing is theirs to build and they will build it, and that they will keep their tags in it under the bird and he will read them at the Row, and that the nephew's half may bring its tags there and be welcome, and that the Driving will be told in the hall as it happened. The nephew's half will not put its tags in a Rook hall under a Rook bird and said so at the Row, and keeps them in the count house on the meadow where they have always been. Walls went up on the footing in the summer, of the cheap stone, a man high by the count.
- Twelve Skelder came to the harvest festival this year and stood at the back and this time sang the names after the valley did, badly, and went home.
- Dim nights: four.

## Year 33

- In the seventh month a Greyholt charcoal burner, Ord, who was cutting on Hagg Ridge below Gorm's fold as the burners do in summer, got up from his fire in the night and walked into the tarn in the saddle there, and Gorm's boys found him at dawn standing in it to the chest, awake, and brought him out. He can speak and work and knows no one's name and not his own. Gorm says the moor's tarns do that to lowland men who sleep beside them and that the Harl never do. Ord's brother brought him down to Greyholt and he burns charcoal and is pleasant to everyone, like Ebb, who is old now, and the two of them sit together at the burn and do not know they have anything in common.
- Two arches stand at Ferrow Ford. The third was turned in the eleventh month and stood.
- The Tollan counter had word from the trader that the lot sent six counters north to the Skelder wood this spring to count the Mother's people and that the Mother sent them away with food on their backs and iron in her voice and would not let them count a single fire, and that the Tally House has recorded the refusal and the food.
- Twenty-two Skelder at the harvest festival, with a hunter who spoke for them, who said the Mother would hear how the Olm keeps its names and had sent them to learn the manner of it, and asked nothing else, and they stayed three nights, and the Weir women taught them the singing.
- The Rooks' hall stood to its eaves by the count and was roofed in turf. Scarrow Hill's forty-six tags hang in it under the bird. At the count Aldo's son read them at the Row from the ninth stone. Scarrow Hill held a feast in the hall in the tenth month and told the Driving in it: that the lords of the hill were not driven but were made to forget their names by Tollan's witch-woman, and walked out onto the moor not knowing why, and that their board was scraped by her hand, and that the Rooks alone kept their name because Ysolt Thrane had left the hall before the witch came. Tollan has heard it and says it is a Rook story and did not go.
- Wat's son married a Scarrow Hill girl and walked the valley tied at the wrist with her and arrived, and both halves of Scarrow came to the Row for it.
- Dim nights: five.

## Year 34

- The fourth arch stood when the centering was struck in the third month, and the deck was laid in flat stone over the four, and Wat's son set the last stone of the parapet in the fifth month with his father watching from the bank and the valley on both banks. The Ferrow Ford bridge is stone from footing to rail. Wat says the river may try. The first ford is still made in the water below it.
- Isca's riders came in the sixth month, twenty, with cattle and no horses and a herald who spoke for an hour, and took the tags Ket had cut for the Harl dead of the cairn, eight names in Ket's rendering of the Harl manner, and the herald said that names given to the Song are the gift Isca holds above every other and that they would be sung that autumn, and gave sixty cattle, which is not what was meant by cattle. The riders sang the Olm names and the Harl names already in the Song and the Voice's name, slow.
- The Lotfolk trader at the autumn market, who is a careful man, said that in the spring his god was seen by the Tally House to rise a hand's breadth above the Bowl in the night, and set down again shaken, and that the god of the Lots has since ordered the wall of names made complete to the last child within the year, and that the city is uneasy and the lots are falling very neatly. The Tollan counter heard this and cut it in lead and said nothing.
- The harvest festival. Thirty Skelder, who sang the names with the valley now in the Weir manner, and who said before they went that the Mother will go north at midsummer in two years with all who will follow her and that they will sing the whole way.
- The spotted eel was not taken at Dun Mere for the twenty-fourth year. Dun Mere no longer holds an eel fair; it holds a fair.
- Pim is fifty and counted the cisterns: nine cold, one with melt, one capped.
- Dim nights: four.

## Year 35

- A heavy flood, the first since the great one, and the stone bridge took it without a mark, the water a span under the arches at the height of it. The Ossary facing held for four hundred paces above the village and the river took nothing. The Lissom channel ran. The Ferrow Ford granary on its nine courses stayed dry. Nothing in the valley that was built in stone moved.
- The one-color beasts crossed the moor, Gorm's people said at the bare wall, and more of them than any year, and walked north-west over the empty Dip and turned back at the heather's edge as they always do.
- Nell of Lissom's second lead letter came north: she is fourteen and cuts marks for the Tally House and eats well and the city stands at the Wall every dusk and says the names and the spring of Ennow, which has run for a thousand years, the priests say, runs slower this year, and the god of the Lots has bound itself to the city and the Wall "to hold its weight," which she does not understand and has copied from the Tally House stone as it is cut there.
- Wat died in the ninth month in his bed, forty-two, of a sickness in the chest he had had since the Year 26 flood, and was buried above the ford, and the whole valley came, and his son stood on the parapet and said nothing. The bridge is called Wat's.
- The names at the festival, two hundred and thirty, with Wat's read first by Ede, against the order, and nobody said so.
- Dim nights: five.

## Year 36

- A second Greyholt burner, Ord's cousin, walked into the same tarn on Hagg Ridge in the eighth month and was found standing in it at dawn by Gorm's boys and is plain. Greyholt will not cut on the ridge now and buys its ridge charcoal from the Harl, who cut there and sleep there and have never walked into anything. Ket says the Harl say the tarn has always taken lowlanders and that this is not true, because it never did before.
- The Skelder came to the spring market in numbers, sixty, and said the Mother goes north at midsummer with everyone who will follow, fourteen hundred and their dogs, to find a country with a high star, and that twelve hundred will not go and will stay in the wood, and that the Mother has had every one of the fourteen hundred singing their own name and their camp's and hers every night for ten years and will have them sing it every night of the walk, and that she learned the singing from the Greel singers and the manner of it from the Olm's stone house, which they said to you directly. They sold every pelt they had and bought iron and salt and did not come to the harvest festival.
- The Rooks' hall is finished, a long room of poor stone on the old footing with a hearth that Scarrow Hill does not let go out, and the Scarrow Hill Driving is told in it every autumn. The nephew's half comes to the Row. Both read their tags at the Row, from the two stones. No one has died over Scarrow in thirteen years.
- Berit's grandson died at Lissom at eighty-one and was buried in the lower orchard among the blood plums, and the Lissom pools were full and cold at the pressing, all nine, and the stream came down the orchards as it does.
- The Tollan counter had a letter in the small old form that was not from Pell but from the Tally House: that the Bowl's rim has gone smooth as wax, that the stones in the Bowl do not sound as they did, and that the people of Ennow remember the god of the Lots and find they cannot say what it looked like, which the Tally House records and does not explain. Nell is well and has been made a cutter.
- Dim nights: four.

## Year 37

- In the fifth month twelve Ruun singers came up the road with no riders, and a herald, who spoke before the Row for an hour: that Isca has concluded, from her eastern bands and from a stone that would not break and from the Voice, that what is sung stays and what is not sung goes, and that she has sent singers to every people within her reach to ask for the names of their places and their dead, to put them in the Song, where nothing is lost, and asks the fire of the Olm, who keeps names in a stone house, for the names in the house. Ede looked at you and then opened the count house, and the singers sat on the meadow for three nights while Ede and her three read the wall to them, village by village, the nine pools of the Lissom, the Crown Fold, the folds and steadings, and the dead of fifteen years from the pegs, eleven hundred and ninety households and three thousand dead, and the singers sang each back and did not sleep. They went in the morning of the fourth day east and said the Song would hold the Olm entire from the Gathering of this autumn and that the Gathering would now take three nights.
- The Longwalkers did not come this year. The Fenner grandmother who keeps the Fenner cord says it is not their year.
- A Lotfolk trader, a new one, said at the autumn market that the god of the Lots has "gone into the Coin to count it": that on a dim night in the first month, which was a dim night here too, the people of Ennow saw a scattering of pale lights go up from the Bowl toward the Coin and that nothing has spoken from the Bowl since, and that the Tally House casts the lots in the god's name and says the lot still speaks, and that the spring of Ennow is dry. The Tollan counter heard this at the stall and went to the count house and sat on the floor and did not cut anything for two days, and then cut it.
- The pull to the south and west, the one you have felt since the night you came, stopped in the first month, on the dim night, and has not come back. The other two are where they were, the northern one fainter than it was.
- Ket is sixty and has a boy learning the speaking.
- The names at the festival, two hundred and forty-one. The valley singing them has become a thing travelers come to hear. The salt caravans stand at the edge of the meadow for it and will not step onto the grass.
- Dim nights: five.

## Year 38

- The Skelder came to the spring market, forty, from the twelve hundred who stayed, and said that nothing has come back out of the north and that the Mother will come back because she said she would, and that they sing the names at every fire as she taught, and that the pale beasts crossed the Fell last autumn and no one touched them.
- Nell of Lissom came home in the ninth month, seventeen, with the Lotfolk trader, with a roll of lead sheets the Tally House let her cut copies of: the pins and both readings with their dates and the breadth of the lowering; the two hundred and the eleven by name; the forty shore households and what became of each. She says what she saw: that in the god's last years the lots fell as it wished them to and it was more curious than she had ever seen it; that it was seen a hand above the Bowl and set down shaken and ordered the Wall finished; that it bound itself to the city and the spring slowed and the cisterns held; that the rim went smooth and the people forgot its face; and that on a dim night the lights went up from the Bowl in a scattering and were a long time rising and the Coin did not change. She says the Tally House says the god went up to count the Coin and will send the count down, and that she does not think so, and will not say what she thinks. The lead is in the count house.
- Isca's riders came with the Song in the sixth month and sang for three nights, and among the Olm names now are the nine pools and the Crown Fold and Hemma and Col and Garran and Wat and Dall, and the valley stood and listened to its dead sung by strangers in order and wept, and the herald said the fire of the Olm had given more than any people and that Isca holds the Olm as she holds her own, which she then said at length.
- The spotted eel was not taken. The Lissom pools were full and cold.
- Ord and his cousin and Ebb sit at the burn above Greyholt and are good at charcoal.
- Dim nights: four.

## Year 39

- In early summer forty strangers came up the Olm from the Greel, dressed in nothing particular, carrying nothing, and came to the Stone Row at dusk, and sang until dawn. Their songs are lists. They asked the name of every village and sang each back, Scarrow Hill among them for the first time, and the ninth stone, and went on to names no one knows and some that the valley half knows now from the Ruun singing: a long list that had the Olm's villages in it and then "Ulmarrow" and "Hesk" and "Teller" and "Thrane" and "Vell" among forty others. They would not take fish, took bread, and left at first light upstream toward the moor road without asking anything of anyone. The Fenner grandmother who keeps the Fenner cord says her own grandmother saw them and that they will come again when the children are old.
- The Skelder came to the spring market thin and quiet and said this: that in the autumn a pack of nine grey-white hounds came out of the north onto the Fell with a hundred and forty Skelder and their dogs, and that the hundred and forty know their own names and their camps' and the Mother's, because they sang them every night of the two years, and know nothing else, not their mothers, not the wood, and that the hounds are the Mother and speak when spoken to and say only that there is no high star in the north, and that the rest, twelve hundred and more, did not come back and will not. The twelve hundred who stayed feed the hundred and forty first. The Skelder say the Mother is the smallest she has ever been and that she counts the camps at night in a low voice and does not say the names of the lost. One of them said to Ket, who told you, that the hounds said one more thing, to the twelve hundred, once: that the singing was right and the walking was wrong.
- The Tollan counter died in the first month, old, at the count house, and the Lotfolk trader cast the stones over him and buried him on the hill above Ossary by the chambers, which the Fenners thought right and Ossary did not. No counter came to replace him. The cairn was built at the boundary stone after the market as always, by the trader, and Nell counted it against the pegs: 1,214 to 1,216.
- Pim, who is fifty-five, counted the cisterns with her grandson: nine cold, one with melt, one capped.
- The Harl at the bare wall say Gorm Longhand died on the ridge in the spring and his son holds the fold and keeps the rate and the runner.
- Dim nights: five.

## Year 40. The 351st year after the Driving

- A spotted eel was taken at Dun Mere in the fifth month, one, by a boy, in the plain-eel strings. It was sold singly at the fair at ten times the price and eaten by the elders in front of everyone. The fishers say the pike's line has died out at last. The old ones say the tarn has turned over. Dun Mere has gone back to calling it the eel fair.
- The Lissom came down through the orchards this year as it did in the first, nine pools cold and deep and each with its name, the Kettle full, and the yellow and the blood plum pressed in the tenth month, and the whole village smelled of it. Lissom says the channel and the grafts did it and that Berit's grandson was right about the water after all.
- The count: 1,216 households. Sheep 5,400. Goats 1,100. Cattle 410. Horses 30. Four granaries at a tenth, a stone bridge, four hundred paces of stone above Ossary, a road to the Greel in stone through the wet, two steadings manned, a count house with two walls of pegs and a third begun, and two hundred and fifty-two names read and sung this autumn by every village and the Fenners and the salt men at the edge of the grass. Ede is sixty and Nell reads beside her.
- Greyholt still says "thee" to an elder and nowhere else does. Greyholt's hill steading has been raided once in eight years and lost nine goats. The bare wall has Harl at it by day, Gorm's son's people, and the deep moor has not come down since the hall burned at Fallow Dip, which Ket says is not peace but waiting, and that he is too old to see the end of it and his boy is not.
- Scarrow Hill told its Driving in its hall and Tollan told the Driving at the Row and both read their tags from their two stones, and the eighth stone stood empty as it has for three hundred and fifty-one years, and no one in the valley can say what is cut on it.
- You are heavier than you have ever been. The pull that does not answer is nine days east under the Greel. Of the other three you feel two, the eastern one as strong as ever and the northern one faint, and the south-western one not since the first month of the thirty-seventh year. You feel the weight as weight. Dim nights: four.
