# Lane B (the Builder). Answer key, Years 1 to 40

Branch summary. No action in Years 1 to 10. From Year 11 a pure building program with four foreign contacts made for building reasons: the Harl gate, the Gathering as a trade trip, the Lotfolk count given freely, the Harl dream bought as intelligence. Never went to Fallow Dip, Hush Water, Vell's sealed cistern, the Notch or the eighth stone. Never set marks. Never spoke to the Longwalkers. Kept names yearly from Year 22, which is the one act in the whole epic's execution list that this player did on purpose, for the wrong reason (god food), and did completely.

"Received" means the clue reached the player's packet. "Could conclude" is what a careful player holding only those packets could reasonably hold at 60 or better. "Findable" lists what sat in reach and the action that would have reached it.

## EPIC. The Account

### Received, by block

Block 1 (Years 1 to 10, baseline): E1 the fourth pull; E2 the Lamp's names (partly, "the Lamp" only); E3 Col's blue hair; E6 not received (no Greelman talk shown); E7 the spotted eel going (Years 3 and 10); E8 the grey plum going bland (Year 8); E9 Ebb plain; E10 the singers (not spoken to, so the "carry names" line was never said); E12 the pale herds (Year 9); E13 the Warm Stone (Ket, Year 4); E16 the god's weight (Year 9); L1-1 the Mother of Dogs' customs and bans (Year 9).

Block 2 (Years 11 to 20): E4 the Notch, as Gorm's joke (Year 12); E7 the pike hunt and the eel still gone; E8 the grey plum cut (Year 20) and blamed on the piers; E12 herd years (14, 20); E13 Haddo's seven nights (Year 15) and the hut (Year 19); E17 Isca's eastern bands, in her own boast (Year 13); E18 partial: the Lotfolk two hundred and the eleven and the Wall (Year 20); L1-5 the three hunters (Year 17); L2-3 the shore families passing (Year 11); L2-4 "gone quiet" (Year 16); the Vell cisterns and the Scald (Year 11) by action; the Ossary tags by action.

Block 3 (Years 21 to 30): the Remainder's dream in full, twice, by action (Ket, Year 21) and by gift (Isca's singers, Year 22); L1-6 the twenty-eight (Year 21); L1-7 the envoy and the question (Year 24); L2-6 the Named Wall (Year 21); L2-7 Pell's letter with the household number to the one and its two offers (Year 25); E13 and E1 together: the stone carried through the valley and the pull moving with it (Year 26), half of Tollan dreaming; Isca's Year 25 and 26 acts; the Caddonese graves at Ossary (Year 26); L1-8 the Skelder singing (Year 27).

Block 4 (Years 31 to 40): E18 in full by alliance: the two readings and their dates and the quickening (Year 31); the eleven stones' names; the shore families' fate through Year 30 including nine walked into the water; the stone dropped at the Reed Crossing and the pull stopping there (Year 32); the two Hagg Ridge sleepers (Years 33 and 36); E20 the lift, the anchor, the softening and the rising (Years 34 to 38), with Nell's eyewitness account and lead copies; the Year 37 pull vanishing; Isca's conclusion "what is sung stays" and the Olm valley sung entire (Years 37, 38); L1-10 the 140 who came back knowing their names and the hounds' one sentence (Year 39); E10 the Longwalkers passing without asking, with the Ulm names (Year 39); the Lissom holding and the spotted eel returning (Year 40).

Never received: E5 (no marks set); E11 (never asked the caravaners about the flats); E14 and E15 (never went to Hush Water); E19 (never near the ceiling, never slept at the shore); L1-9 (never asked Maudre a question); the Notch lain behind; the "carry names" line from the Longwalkers.

### Could conclude, by block

End of Block 1: nothing on the epic beyond the player's own lines, which were in fact fair: the god feeds on attention; something north-west draws the Lamp; the fourth presence is a thing not a god. The last is right by accident and for the wrong reason.

End of Block 2: that the edges of the world strip names from people and not from stones or songs (Ebb, the eastern bands, the three hunters, the eleven stones). This player reached that at 80 by Year 20 and called it "one mechanism the other gods know." That is the correct reading of Truth 3 and Truth 4's shared surface, arrived at by a builder from trade gossip. The fourth pull as a stone rather than a place was at 55.

End of Block 3: that the stone carries a god's dream and gives it to sleepers (85, correct); that the pull was the stone (90, correct); that the dream is of a far place with a wide slow river and roofs without end (70, correct; it is the gods' own origin, which no one has told him); that Pell's two offers are about the sky and the edge (65, correct). That names said aloud feed a god (95, correct in effect). Still missing and not inferable from what he had: that the Lamp is lowering; that fading is the valley's own clock; that his count house is storage against a fall.

End of Block 4: with Pell's two readings in lead on his floor, a careful player could hold at 70 or better that the Lamp is lower and lowering faster; with the shore records and the sleepers on his own ridge, that the big water north-west takes names and then takes people, and that its reach has touched his border; with Pell's lift and rising, that a god can grow too heavy for the ground; with Isca's eastern bands, the eleven stones and the 140, that what is sung or cut survives what is not; with Maudre's "the singing was right and the walking was wrong," that there is no way out across the empty country. A careful player would not yet hold that the lowering is a fall to come, nor that the valley itself is spending, nor that his own works are the thing that saved the Lissom. This player, by his own round 3, holds the names-mechanism at 95, the stone at 85, the pull at 90, Pell's honesty at 60, and has filed the Lamp under "dim nights are a metronome." He has not connected the readings to the dim nights or to anything.

### Findable but unfound, and what would have found it

- E5 the god's own marks: two sightline stones in Year 1 or any later year; a reading seven years on. The only decisive clue in the epic, and the cheapest work this builder never did.
- E4 lain behind: walking two days to Gorm's fold and lying down behind the gate at night. The player was told of the gate in Year 12 and enjoyed not knowing.
- E14 Hush Water and E15 the glass: twenty-five days north-west, or a dig at the shore. The Lotfolk families walked through his valley to it in Year 11.
- E11 no shells: one conversation with a caravaner about the flats at any autumn market in forty years.
- E6 the fat Greel and the drowned jetty: one conversation with a Greelman at Weir; the player's own road runs past the jetty from Year 23.
- The Longwalkers' "carry names" line: speaking to them in Year 7. Had he done so, in Year 39 they would have asked, and he had the most sung valley in the region ready.
- L1-9 Maudre's memory: from Year 34 one honest answer to one question was his for the asking; twenty years of watching would have reached Year 44, beyond the test.
- Pell's computation of the fall: never shared by Pell; findable from the readings by a god who thought to extend the line.
- The connection between the dim nights, the blue hair and Hush Water: Col told him in Year 2 where the hair comes down. Twenty-five days' walk.

### How the pressure reached this player's people

Through his own works, exactly as the response table forecast, and through the alliances he made for trade. The spotted eel went in Year 10 and was blamed on a pike he then hunted. The grey plum was cut in Year 20 and blamed on his piers, which he then fixed. The gate he built for trade carried Isca's two thousand to the moor and brought the Harl's revenge down on Greyholt. The road he built for salt ended where the Voice built his fire and where Isca dropped the stone. The revetment he built sent the river into a cemetery of people who kept clay tags like his. The census he built for himself was read to the one by a god from a pile of pebbles. Two of his charcoal burners walked into a tarn on his border and came back plain. Three gods he traded with were, by Year 40, one risen, one diminished to nine hounds, and one holding his valley's every name. And the clearest pressure of all is one he cannot see: his valley kept its second river and got its eel back because he made a stone house for the dead and read them aloud every autumn for god food, and nothing in the world will ever tell him so.

### What execution would still require

The player has, without knowing it, done two of the six things on the epic's execution list: spent less (no workings, no knotters, festivals the same size, names and breeds kept) and stored (the valley entire in the Long Song from Year 37, the count house, the Fenner cord, the Weir singing). He has done the fourth by accident: the stone left his border in Isca's hands and lies under the Greel, which is as far from a priesthood as it can be got. He has done the third in part: Pell's records are on his floor, Isca is his ally and holds his names, and Maudre has watched him for nine years and owes him one answer. He has done the fifth without being tested: he is at about 52 and has refused nothing because nothing has been offered.

What remains. To understand that the readings mean a fall and that the fall is the end of everything he has built unless it is stored, which no one has told him and which the lead on his floor says if read as a line and not as two numbers. To decide what the Longwalkers are and reach them in a future generation, since he missed Year 7 and Year 39. To ask Maudre his one question before her ten years lapse, and to be the god she can show her memory to by Year 44. To keep his power under a ceiling he does not know exists, which he is on course to approach by about Year 60 if the name engine keeps compounding, and which will then speak to him in dreams. To buy or copy the rest of the Tally House from priests who will sell it for iron and explain it wrong. To decide what the Still is before it reaches past Hagg Ridge, which on its clock it will. And to do all of this for a century with one god gone, one broken, and one who will burn anything, including things he wants kept.

## LONG 1. The Hounds' Road

Received: L1-1 (Year 9), L1-3 (Year 9), L1-4 Ashen as a camp name only (Year 13), L1-5 (Year 17), L1-6 (Year 21), L1-7 the envoy exactly (Year 24), L1-8 (Year 27), the watchers (Years 31 to 34), the announced crossing (Year 36), L1-10 in the Lane B form: 140 and nine hounds and the hounds' sentence (Year 39). Not received: L1-2 (never asked the Harl about Skelder routes), L1-9 the memory, the lost hound (dead in Weir since Year 8; the player noticed its death and declined to act).

Could conclude: by Year 20 that the Mother's people lose their names past the Fell and their dogs do not lose them (fair, and the player had it). By Year 30 that she is preparing to go where gods do not come back whole and is asking how it is done (75, correct). By Year 40 that there is no high star north, that unsung flesh does not cross, that sung names cross a little, and that the Mother knew something and chose wrong. The player holds the first and files the rest as the Skelder's business.

Findable: Maudre's one honest answer from Year 34 (ask the hunter at any festival a question for his god); L1-2 from any Harl at his own yard; the memory by Year 44 if the watching continues. What would have turned her further: showing her watchers Pell's eleven names and the shore records in Year 31, which were on his floor as they stood in his meadow. Nobody connected them.

Pressure on his people: the north trade stopped for two years; the pup on his knoll; a god's attention he did not ask for. Lane B's one act toward her, the Year 24 answer, was given as courtesy and did more than any other single act in this lane: it saved about 130 Skelder and kept their god on the ground.

## LONG 2. The Small King's Count

Received: S5 whole (Years 3 to 8 baseline, Year 12 Oss's death, the cairn moving with the boundary stone, the cairn counted against the tags in Years 16 and 39); L2-2 (Year 8); L2-3 (Year 11); L2-4 (Year 16); L2-5 (Year 20); L2-6 (Year 21); L2-7 (Year 25); L2-9 in the shared part (Year 31) and in Nell's copies (Year 38); E20 whole (Years 34 to 38); Pell's letter in its own voice (Year 32); the first offer's effect noticed by Pell (Year 32), the anchoring (Year 35), the softening (Year 36), the rising (Year 37).

Could conclude: by Year 25 that Pell measures and does not lie (the household number). By Year 31 that the Lamp is lower by Pell's pins, and lowering faster. By Year 38 that a god can grow too heavy, try to hold itself to its city, and go up, and that its spring died with it. The player holds Pell honest at 60 and has the readings in lead and has not read them as a line.

Findable: Pell's own computation (never shared; derivable). The rest of the Tally House (the dreams in Pell's phrasing) for iron from the priests after Year 37, which Nell could have asked for and was not told to. This lane got nearly everything Long 2 holds, because the player answered a letter honestly after five years of leaving three men on his caravan ground.

Pressure: the counters on his ground; his census read from outside; a child of his valley at Ennow for seven years; a counter buried on his hill; the one god who measured gone before he read what it measured.

## SHORT 1. The Sweet Cisterns (Years 2 to 8)

Received: S1-1 Pim (Year 3); S1-2 the hearth-stone (seen by the party, Year 11); S1-3 the Scald (Year 11); S1-4 the blank grave (Year 11); S1-5 the sealed cistern and its match to the hearth-stone (Year 11, recognized by the Greyholt men). Not received: S1-6 (Marrit dead unfound). Resolved for its practical half in Year 11, four years after the clock closed: the water found, the goats saved in Year 17, the ban lifted. Could conclude: a lordly town emptied of its dead, the Greyholt headman's line from it (55, half right: Sele's line, the town emptied by hollowing not by removal). Findable but unfound: the sealed cistern, four men and a day; inside, two hundred tags that would read the eighth stone and the Ossary tags, and the roll that names what every knotter gave. The player counted the capped lid every summer for thirty years and called it "a drawer the narrator keeps opening." It was his to open. Cost of ignoring: Greyholt's goats in Year 4; the tags unread; nothing else.

## SHORT 2. The Rooks' Torc (Years 5 to 12)

Received: S2-1 (Year 5); S2-2 the Driving every year; S2-3 the eighth stone seen every year, never read; S2-5 the crypt as the Rooks told it (Year 12), the scraped board and their reading. Not received: S2-4 the walked men (never asked the Harl about old times, though Harl sat at his yard for twenty-eight years). The player managed the claim as politics only, by his own statement, and did it well: a quarry contract (did not prevent the Year 12 killing and dig), a ledger split and a ninth stone (Year 23, no deaths after), a hall as count house (Year 36, half accepted). Could conclude: that the Rooks' claim is real as blood and the board is a scraped roll (fair); nothing about why the names were scraped. The Rooks' own telling (a witch made them forget) is wrong about the cause and right that there was no battle, which is nearer the truth than Tollan's. Findable: the eighth stone read with the Vell tags (Short 1's sealed cistern) or a Harl elder with a few signs (at his own gate); either would have given "We drove no one. They were already gone," and shamed both tellings. Cost of ignoring: three dead, a generation's feud, two villages, a hall.

## SHORT 3. The Fen Bell (Years 1 to 6)

Received: S3-1 (Year 6 as the abandoned steadings); the silting (Year 18). Not received: S3-2 (no Greelman talk), S3-3, S3-4. Ignored entirely by the player's choice ("relevance nil"). The cut silted and the plate lies in it. Cost: the fattest eels in the fen, which the Fenners count a mercy. Findable in any autumn fog by one boat and a hook; a caravaner would then have read "Aud" and "tide," which with E11 is Truth 3's clearest small body. The player reads the Fen Bell and the Ossary bones as one drowned town; they are two peoples five hundred years apart.

## SHORT 4. The Lamb Taker (Years 3 to 9)

Received: S4-1 (Year 5), S4-2 the grass knot (Year 5). Not received: S4-3 (never asked the boy), S4-4. Marrit died in the Year 9 winter unfound and unmentioned. The player noticed the lambs stopped and recorded Pim's correct observation that it stopped before the gate, and did not ask why. Cost: the last reader of the signs, the last knotter, and the one mortal who could have shown him what a working costs. Findable: tracking, not hunting, on the Backs in Years 5 to 8.

## SHORT 5. The Boundary Pebbles (Years 3 to 12)

Received whole, and used. The player counted the cairn against his own tags twice and made the right inference (someone else's census) and the right practical response (build his own). Oss's "so the god knows how many to pray for" was received in Year 8 and correctly disbelieved.

## Treasures, secrets and histories never found (rule 11)

Unfound in Lane B: the Thrane silver (found by the Rooks, held by the Rooks, never seen by the god); Aud's plates under the fourth step; the Cord House roll and tags in the sealed cistern, four days above Greyholt, inspected from outside thirty times; the second sightline stone under the lake; the Teller stone in the glass; the eighth stone's line, stood beside every autumn for forty years and described to him in Year 1; Marrit's name, in the ground on the Backs with her tag; the Ulm measure in the ell (the player's "one bronze rod" in his first ledger line); Imre's name; Gorm Longhand's fold and what its gate was, told to him as a joke; the Whale Walk; Haddo's Kneeling Song, which this player heard sung in full, once, by nine singers who would not sing it twice, and filed as "beautiful and from somewhere else."

What this player found that the others may not have: the Remainder's dream in full without touching the stone; Pell's whole measured record by honest answer; a sung valley made by hand for the wrong reason; Maudre turned part way by a courteous answer he gave because "a question deserves an answer."
