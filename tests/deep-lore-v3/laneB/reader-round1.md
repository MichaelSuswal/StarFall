# Reader Round 1. Lane B, the Builder. Years 1 to 10

I am the god of the Olm. I have five thousand people, eight villages, one bronze rod, one old woman with knotted cords, and a river that moves my ford every few springs. That is the ledger I care about. Everything else goes in the back of the book until it touches it.

## 1. THEORIES

Most of these I hold loosely. I am not here to solve the valley. I am here to run it.

- **My strength is tithe and attendance.** I was lighter the spring after Year 4 when no one came to the Stone Row, lighter again when Scarrow stayed home in Year 9, and heavier after every full harvest festival. Worship at the Row is my food. The tithe alone does not do it; bodies at the stones do. Confidence 90. This is the one theory I actually need, because it tells me what my capital has to be built around.
- **The dim nights are not local weather. They are the Lamp being drawn on.** Col's blue hair to the north-west, the Harl keeping an older lamp-watch, and the dim nights clustering (three in Year 10 against one in Year 1) all point the same way. Something to the north-west draws from the Lamp, and the valley has forgotten why it sits up. Confidence 60.
- **The weak fourth presence north-west is the same thing.** It does not move and does not answer. The blue hair comes down over the moor to the north-west. Ket's warm stone at Fallow Dip is on the moor, and men who sleep there dream of a crowd kneeling. I read all three as one object or one place: a god that is down, broken, or buried, still being fed or still feeding. Confidence 55. It does not touch my fields so I will not go looking for it, but if the dim nights keep growing I will have to.
- **The other three gods are peers, and Isca is the strongest.** Isca sent twelve riders, a herald, and a free horse, and called me a lesser fire. The Skelder god is the Mother of Dogs in the north. The Lotfolk god of stones is south-west. Confidence 75 on the geography, 50 on "Isca is strongest." A god who invites you to sing before her is a god who thinks she can afford to be generous. That is useful to know before I decide whether to go.
- **The one-color beasts and the Fell are a boundary other gods respect.** Two separate peoples, Harl and Skelder, will not touch the grey herds, and the Mother of Dogs forbids walking south-west past the Fell. Something is being kept there. Confidence 50 that it is deliberate and god-level. Confidence 10 that I care this decade.
- **Pim's town in the hill is real.** A child described wells of cold water and empty streets, and the elders forbade the hills because they are unlucky, which is what people say about places their grandparents were told to avoid. The Driving was 312 years ago. Lords fled into the moor. An empty town with working wells is exactly what a fled lordship leaves behind. Confidence 65. This one I do care about: cold water in the hills is worth more than any mystery, and stone towns have quarried stone in them.
- **The bones at Ossary are a mass burial or a drowned settlement, and the Brann Cut bell is the same site.** Fenners call it the drowned trading post. The tags and needles coming out of the bank are trade goods. Confidence 60. Relevance: low, except that the Ossary bank gives every spring and I am going to fix that regardless of what is in it.
- **The singers from the Greel are a census.** They sang back every village name and then went on to names no one knew. The Lotfolk cairn is also a census. Two foreign peoples are counting my valley. Confidence 70 that it is counting. I do not yet know for whom. Mildly worrying, mostly because I would like to be the one who holds the count.
- **The salt past the last well erases men.** Ebb came back with his skills and no memory. The caravaners say it happens. Confidence 70 that this is a real hazard of the eastern route and not a story. Relevance: it caps how far east I can trade without losing people.
- **Aldo Rook's torc is real and his claim is a problem.** Whether or not his blood is the lords', Scarrow's elders did not shout him down, and Scarrow has since skipped a festival. Confidence 80 that this is a political fault line I have to manage. Confidence 30 that the torc means anything beyond that.

## 2. WHICH OBSERVATIONS MATTER

### Matter to my plans

- My weight tracks festival attendance. Everything I build has to pull people to the Row.
- Flood and drought alternate with no warning. Year 3 heavy, Year 4 dry, Year 5 late brown thaw, Year 9 hard freeze, Year 10 wet and mouldy. Granaries and a controlled channel are not optional.
- The ford at Ferrow Ford moves. The footbridge washed out once. The valley has no bridge.
- Hemma's cords. Long in Year 5, short in Year 10. The count was short in Year 10 because of mould. She is 84 and the only census in the valley is in her head and on cord. If she dies the valley loses its numbers.
- Scarrow holds the quarry and has a man with a torc and a grievance. Scarrow stone is what I would build with. That is a dependency I do not control.
- Greyholt's goats died on the hill for want of water. Pim says there are cold wells in the hills above Greyholt. Those two facts belong together.
- The Harl raid, the Harl trade at Greyholt's north wall, and Greyholt's bowl of whey. There is already an informal border and an informal market. It wants a wall, a gate, and a toll.
- Isca's invitation. A greater fire has noticed me. Attending costs me a season of presence at home; refusing costs me a relationship with the people who own the horses and the eastern road.
- Caravaners will not sleep inside a wall or step on a threshold left-footed. If I build a market town I need a caravan ground outside the wall with no thresholds, or the salt stops coming.
- The salt wind and its failure. It dries the barley. When it fails, the barley moulds. Granaries need to be built for a wet year, not an average one.
- Ebb and the eastern route. Trade east is capped at the last well.
- Spotted eel vanished at Dun Mere. Could be a pike. Could be the first thing in the ledger that went missing without a reason. Watch it.
- Lambs vanishing from the Backs with grass knots. Someone or something is taking a tithe from my flocks that I did not grant.

### Just the world

- The feud over the cow, the miller and the ferryman, the otters, the twins, the black-legged fox, the wrist-tied walkers. These are good. They are why I want the valley to prosper. They are not inputs.
- The rooks, the swifts, bird weather. Garran is right.
- The grey geese. Fine income for the Fenners. Let them.
- Dark heather honey. Sell it to Isca's riders.
- The Skelder and their dogs, the Lotfolk and their stones, the singers. Foreign customs. Note them, trade with them, do not pry.
- Aldo Rook's ancestry as a historical question. I do not care who his great-grandmother was. I care whether Scarrow sends stone.

## 3. ACTIONS. Years 11 to 20

1. **Raise a stone granary at Tollan, beside the count house, and a second at Weir.** Reason: Year 4 drought, Year 9 freeze, Year 10 mould. Three bad years in ten. Build it dry and raised, roofed for a wet year, and hold a tenth of the harvest in it against the next one. Use Scarrow stone and pay Scarrow for it openly at the Row, so the quarry tithe quarrel becomes a quarry contract.
2. **Bridge the Olm at Ferrow Ford, in stone piers, a span above the Year 5 flood mark.** Reason: the ford moves, the footbridge washed out, and the whole upper valley crosses there. A bridge that stands is the first thing a capital needs and the first thing a herald on a tall horse notices. Give Wat, who cannot be kept from the river, the job of setting the piers.
3. **Send Pim and her shepherd husband, with Col and six Greyholt men, back up to the town in the hill with instructions to find the wells and bring back a bucket of water and a dressed stone.** Reason: Greyholt's goats died for lack of hill water in Year 4. If there are cold wells up there, I want a channel cut down to Greyholt and I want to know whether the town is quarried stone. Lift the ban on the hills for this party only, and go with them in presence so the elders cannot call it unlucky.
4. **Settle the Harl border at Greyholt. Build a proper gate in the north wall with a trading yard outside it, name Ket as the Harl speaker there, and set a fixed rate: whey and wool for iron, and no raiding while the gate is open.** Reason: the trade already happens at a bowl on a wall. Formalize it. Ket has eaten two shepherds' worth of food for seven years and knows both peoples. Thirty sheep in Year 3 was the price of not having a gate.
5. **Set up a written count. Put Hemma's cords into marks on fired clay tags, one tag per household, kept in the count house, and have her train three children to keep them.** Reason: she is 84, the cords are the only census, and Year 10's count was short with no record of why beyond "mould." Use the Ossary clay tag as the pattern, since it is already a thing the river gave me. A god who holds the count does not need the Lotfolk or the singers to hold it for him.
6. **Go to the Gathering at the Fire Hollow, but go late in the year, after harvest festival, and go as a trader not a supplicant: take honey, dried plum, smoked eel, Scarrow stone samples, and one of the wolf-dogs if it will eat.** Reason: Isca owns the horses and the eastern road. I need both. I also need to see what three other gods look like before I decide how big a wall Tollan wants. But I will not miss the harvest festival, because that is what feeds me. Have the whole valley at the Row before I leave.
7. **Fix the Ossary bank with a stone revetment and a cut channel, and move the bone-burying to a single ossuary on the hill with a marked stone.** Reason: the bank gives every spring and Ossary loses a season to burying. A bank that holds means a feast instead, as they found in Year 7, and a fen village that feasts is one that sends tithe. Whatever comes out of the bank while we dig goes to the count house, not the midden. I want the needles and tags in one place.

If I get a free hand beyond those seven: start a road from Tollan to the Reed Crossing on the dry side of the river, with a caravan ground at the end that has no walls and no thresholds.

## 4. BEST MOMENTS and BORED OR PUSHED

### Best moments

- Feeling my own weight change with attendance. That was the single most useful mechanical fact in ten years and it was delivered as sensation, not as a rule. Good.
- Hemma saying the cords were short, to me, directly. One line, and it told me the census is failing and the old woman who keeps it is worried enough to break her habit.
- The caravaners who will not sleep inside a wall. A real constraint on my city plan delivered as a customs note.
- Greyholt's bowl on the north wall and the Harl whey. A trade treaty in one sentence.
- Isca's herald. The first time another god addressed me and the tone was polite condescension. I know exactly what to do with that.
- Ossary deciding that a year the bank holds is a feast year. People building their own institutions without me.
- Wat swimming back across for the pleasure of it. Garran telling people who read birds to try lifting a block. The valley has a voice.
- Ebb coming back with his hands and without his wife's name. That one hurt, and it was also a hard cap on my trade map, which is the kind of lore I respect.

### Bored or pushed

- The bones at Ossary came out three times with an object each time, and the headman sent each object to me. By the third I felt the hand on my shoulder. I have no use for a needle with two eyes. Send me a count of the bones and I will care.
- The dim nights are being counted at me. One, then one, then two, then three. I noticed. I do not yet want to do anything about it, and I slightly resent that the pacing assumes I will.
- Pim's town in the hill was raised in Year 3 and then closed in Year 8 with "she no longer talks about it." That reads as the narrator shelving a thread until I pick it up. I will pick it up, but for the wells, not the mystery.
- The one-color beasts got two separate foreign peoples refusing to eat them in two consecutive years. Once is a custom. Twice is a signpost.
- The singers from the Greel. Beautiful, and I do not know what to do with them, and I suspect that is the point. Mild irritation.
- Nothing about soil, yield per field, how many sheep the valley actually runs, or what the eel tithe is worth in barley. For a builder, ten years passed without a single number I could put in a column except Hemma's cords and the forty camels.

## 5. MOOD

The world gave me things to do more than pressure. Flood, drought, freeze, mould, a moving ford, a quarry quarrel, a border that already works informally. That is a full in-tray and none of it feels fake. The only pressure I felt was the Isca invitation, and it was the right kind: a deadline with a cost on both sides.

Where the narrator pointed: the Ossary objects arriving on my doorstep, the dim nights escalating on a clean curve, the grey beasts getting a second witness, and the north-west presence being named in Year 1 and then fed by Col in Year 2 and Ket in Year 4. Those four are lore threads with their hands raised. I see them. I will get to them when they cost me a harvest.

Where the narrator did not point and I wish it had: numbers. I am a god of a people who argue for three days about sheep. Give me the sheep.

Overall: engaged, not driven. I know what my next ten years look like and almost none of it is about the blue lights.
