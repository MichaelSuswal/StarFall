# Reader Round 3. Lane B, the Builder. Years 21 to 30

Ledger. Households 1,117 to 1,173. Sheep 4,580 to 5,100. Goats 840 to 920, after losing every goat on Greyholt's hill once. Cattle 310 to 340 after selling east. Horses 23 to 61, which is a problem and not a boast. Four granaries at a tenth, no barley carried down the valley on anyone's back since Year 24. Salt toll 31 skins, then 34. Nine stones at the Row where there were eight. A road to the Reed Crossing, five days from the Olm mouth, in stone through the wet. A count house with a full peg wall and a second begun. Two hundred and nine names read and sung by the whole valley. Heaviest I have ever been.

Losses. Eleven Fenners in the Year 23 storm. The Ferrow Ford deck twice. A quarter of the Ferrow Ford barley once. Two hundred paces of unfaced bank above Ossary and a hundred graves into the fen. Greyholt's yard, Ket's house, and every goat on the hill. Col. Garran. Aldo Rook. Hemma's fox.

## 1. RESULTS

**Count house and the reading of names.** Answered in full and it is the best thing I have built. Door faces the stones, the elders argued a month and lost. First reading Year 22, a hundred and sixty names, people wept and laughed, Reyn sang the Weir names after and by Ossary half the meadow was doing it. Heaviest I had ever been, and heavier each year since. By Year 24 the Fenners brought their own cord to be hung. By Year 29 the wall was full and a second begun. By Year 30 the whole valley sings two hundred and nine names. The world taught me that I was right about the mechanism: names kept and said aloud at the Row is the thing that feeds me, more than tithe and more than attendance alone. It also taught me a cost I did not expect: the Lotfolk god read my peg count to the one from a cairn of pebbles. A census that works can be read by anyone who can count.

**Granaries at Lissom and Ferrow Ford.** Answered. Roofed before the walls were dry as ordered. Lissom filled Year 22. Ferrow Ford stood in water to its third course in the Year 23 storm and lost nothing, then to its roof course in the Year 26 flood and lost a quarter and its floor. Rebuilt on nine courses and twenty paces back from the bank. By Year 24 four granaries at a tenth and nobody carrying barley to anyone else's shed, which is what I wanted and the resentment went with it. Year 28 I sent a tenth of the tenth east to Isca's hungry bands and the valley did not go short. Lesson: the bridge bank is where the carts come and where the river comes. I chose carts. The river corrected me. Nine courses and twenty paces is the right answer and should have been the first.

**Backs steading.** Answered clearly and the first test was clean. Fold for eight hundred, wall a man high, watch post on the knoll. Year 23: Gorm's boy ran a day ahead, horses carried word by dusk, sheep inside, wall manned, sixty Harl took twenty-two from an outlying fold and went home without a fight. The dream said on the saddle did them no good against a wall. Col died on that wall in the sun in Year 25. The horses finally earned their feed as runners. Lesson: a wall that is manned and warned works. Everything in that sentence costs something every summer.

**Gorm.** Answered and better than I deserved. He would not summer inside my wall, and said why plainly: a Harl inside a wall is a captive whatever the gate is called. But the yard reopened, the rate stood, and he gave me the runner for iron with no questions about Year 20. His boy ran twice, once ahead of the deep moor and once ahead of Isca, and both times it saved stock. Then four clans burned the yard in Year 27 and raided Gorm for being the Olm's dogs. The yard is a bare wall now and he comes in daylight only. Lesson: an ally on the far side of a border pays for the alliance in his own stock. I owe Gorm and I have not yet paid it.

**Lissom relief channel.** Answered honestly. The flood went through, the mouth did not back up, the blood plum took, Lissom said the channel cured the sour. Then in a wet year the grandson said the channel took the water away too fast. Then in Year 30 the blood plum on the old grey stumps was the best in the orchard and he said his grandmother was wrong about everything. Lesson: the channel works. The grandson is a man who will have an opinion about water whatever the water does.

**Road and toll.** Answered in full. Fen edge to Olm mouth before the storm and it survived. Greel west bank in stone through the wet, four times the stone, done Year 25, a month before the fire. Cattle sold east at a price the valley had never seen. Toll paid every autumn by every caravan without a word. Horses good as a cart team at last. The road also carried two thousand Ruun up the valley to the moor and a stone back down it, which I did not price when I laid it, and which is the lesson: a road runs both ways and anyone with wheels or hooves can use it.

**Fallow Dip, buying the hut.** Refused, and the refusal was the answer. Brude would not sell. The dream is Harl, dreamed on Harl ground, and the price for the Olm's god to have it is to lie down on the stone like any man. I did not. The tellers told it to Ket by day for a knife each: steps down to a wide slow river, flat boats, roofs without end, a crowd kneeling toward an empty place at the top of the steps, roads full of people walking behind something, a hand lifted that turns, a pain in the side, and the dream ends there. The deep moor likes the ending best. I then asked Isca for the Voice's song as a gift and got it, sung once in full by nine who would not sing it twice. It is the same dream with "being wanted" and "a name said by every mouth at once" added. Then the Voice took eleven bands and made a kneeling fire at my road's end, and Isca burned it with him in it, and came up my road and burned the cairn and could not break the stone and carried it east past my door, and half of Tollan dreamed it for a night. The pull I have felt since Year 1 is east now and does not move. The hut problem is solved and I did not solve it. Lesson: I correctly judged the hut as the dangerous part. I wrongly judged it as a thing that could be bought. And the actual cost landed on Greyholt in Year 27 for having opened the gate.

**Scarrow split.** Answered and it held. Aldo came to the Row with his half and his tags, forty-one households, under a bird mark the Rooks cut themselves. The elders would not let anyone stand on the eighth stone and said so for a day, so the Rooks brought a ninth stone from their own cuts and set it a little apart. Aldo said his family will stand and be counted and tell the Driving when it is told true. No one has died over stone since. Garran's death gave both halves the same silent day for the first time in twelve years. Now the Rooks have a footing on Lord's Hill that one half calls a sheep shelter and the other calls a hall, and Aldo's son stood on the ninth stone at the Driving and did not tell it and did not stop it. Lesson: a ledger split works for stone and tags. It does not settle a story. The footing on the hill is the next move and I have to decide whether to let it stand.

**The two asks I did not plan for.** A Skelder hunter walked to my count house with forty pelts and a pup and asked "Does your god remember the sky?" and waited at Ket's house until the thaw for one answer. The Keeper does not tell me what I sent. For the record, this is what I sent: that I remember a light and a fall and a meadow, and nothing before the meadow, and that I keep my people's names in a stone house so that they will not be forgotten, and that if the Mother of Dogs has gone far and come back she is welcome at my Row to say what she saw. The pup stayed, eats, and sits on the watch knoll looking north. The Lotfolk counters have waited five years at my caravan ground for an answer to a count of gods, offering "the height of the Coin in thirteen years" and "what the stones carried back." I have not answered them. That is a decision I now have to make and it is in the actions below.

## 2. THEORIES

The pressing ones first, and I will be honest that there are more of them than I would like.

- **Names said aloud are a god's food and a god's shield, and all four of us now run on it.** Confirmed by my own weight every year since Year 22. Isca's Long Song, the Mother's fire naming, the Lotfolk wall, my count house. The Lotfolk trader said it as a rule: a stone name is a name the lot cannot take. Confidence 95. Pressing in the good sense: this is the capital's engine and I know how to feed it.
- **The stone was a god's pillow and the dream was its memory, and that memory makes men kneel toward an empty place.** The tellers said the Low One sleeps and the stone is its pillow. The Voice slept there and built a fire where people kneel. Isca could not break it as fire and carried it home. Half of Tollan dreamed it for one night of its passing. Confidence 85 that the stone carries a god's dream and gives it to sleepers. Confidence 70 that the dream is from somewhere far away: a river wider than the Greel, roofs without end, stone steps. That is not my valley and not the moor. Pressing: no longer on my border, because Isca took it. Pressing in a new way: Isca has it, Isca's people carried it and dreamed it, and a god who does not bargain is now sleeping next to a pillow that makes men kneel. If the Voice came out of seven nights, I want to know what two thousand riders and a god come out of in ten years.
- **The weak presence I felt since Year 1 was the stone itself, not a place.** It moved when the stone moved. It is east now with Isca and does not move. Confidence 90. This closes the north-west for me. Whatever drew the blue hair from the Lamp is either the stone, now gone, or something else up there that is still drawing, because the dim nights did not change: four, five, four, five, four, five, four, five, four, five, through all of it. Confidence 60 that the dim nights are not the stone and never were.
- **Something is wrong at the big water past the Fell, and the Mother of Dogs is making ready to go there.** She went north and came back with twenty-eight of forty, nameless. She asks every trader whether any god has gone far and come back. She hummed the names into a tune the Greel singers brought. She heard word from the south about the big water and was quiet a day and began counting dogs. Her hunter asked me if I remember the sky. Confidence 75 that she is about to go somewhere a god does not come back from whole and is asking the other gods how it is done. Pressing on my people: not directly. Pressing on my plans: the Skelder are the north trade and the pup on my knoll is theirs. If she goes and does not come back, forty traders with dogs stop coming to my spring market and the north has no god keeping its names.
- **The Lotfolk god has measured the world and is offering to trade measurements, and its two offers are warnings.** "The height of the Coin in thirteen years" is a prediction about the Lamp or something in the sky. "What the stones carried back" is what the eleven survivors of the west learned. Confidence 65 that the stone god knows more about the edges than anyone and 60 that it is honest about what it will trade. Confidence 50 that "the count of gods" is also the stone god counting us the way it counted my households. Pressing: three men have sat on my caravan ground for five years. It is rude and it is also the only offer of information about the sky that anyone has made me, and the Lotfolk run on lots, which means they do not bargain either, in their own way. I have to answer.
- **The one-color beasts walk a road that does not care about any of us.** They walked over the empty Dip after the stone was gone. Confidence 40 that they are made and 30 that they are going to the big water for a reason I will care about. Not pressing. Back of the book, where they have been for thirty years.
- **The Ossary graves were a town's cemetery with the same tags I keep, and the river took them because I made it.** The revetment held and the river went around it and took the unfaced bank and a hundred graves in rows with clay tags. The headman tells the children the river took them home. Confidence 80 that those tags are a census like mine, three hundred years older, from whoever lived there before the lords. Confidence 70 that if I had faced two hundred more paces they would still be in the hill. Not a theory so much as a debt.
- **Scarrow Hill means to put a hall on Lord's Hill and tell the Driving the other way round.** Confidence 85. Pressing, and the one I can actually do something about with stone and a ledger.
- **Vell.** Nine cold, one fallen now with melt in it, one capped. Nothing new in six summers of counting. Confidence 50 that the capped one matters. Confidence 5 that I care before Year 40.

## 3. WHICH OBSERVATIONS MATTER

### Matter to my plans

- Weight follows names read and sung. The second peg wall is already begun. Build for it.
- Horses: 61 and rising, ten more arriving unasked every time Isca is grateful. A horse eats what three sheep eat. That is a hundred and eighty sheep of grass. Either they work or they go east as trade.
- Greyholt's yard is a bare wall in daylight only and Ket says quiet from the moor is what he likes least. The gate did its job and is now a target. The steading did its job and is now the only defense.
- The Rooks' footing on Lord's Hill.
- The Lotfolk counters, five years waiting. I either answer or I ask them to leave, and asking the Lotfolk to leave a place their lot sent them to may not be a thing they can do.
- Isca owes me for grain and knows it, and has taken ten horses off her hands onto mine. She has the stone. Her Gathering now takes two nights. She sings the Voice's name slower. She is not the god she was in Year 13.
- The road to the Reed Crossing is complete, the ford is open, nobody kneels there, and the Ruun are down in the lowland with trampled grass and hungry herds. That is my best customer in a bad year on my doorstep.
- The Ferrow Ford bridge deck has gone twice. Wat has said the river can have it again. He is not young.
- Two hundred paces of unfaced bank above Ossary are now a fresh scar with graves in it.
- The Mother of Dogs is counting dogs and asking about going far. If the Skelder stop coming, the north trade is gone.
- Dim nights unchanged through the stone's arrival, departure, and everything else. Four and five, every year, for twenty years.
- Ede is fifty and has named a successor. Good. The three readers are grown with children. The institution outlives its founders, twice now.

### Just the world

- The young fox named after the old one. Wat's son's first ford under the bridge. The grandson deciding his grandmother was wrong about everything. Reyn's daughter singing in her mother's place. Garran dying on his wall and both Scarrows keeping the silent day.
- The pup on the knoll looking north and refusing to work sheep. I like it and it is not an input.
- The spotted eel, eighteen years gone and the fishers have stopped saying pike. A lost tithe line I have stopped expecting back.
- The Skelder humming. Their god's business.
- Whether Isca's voice broke on the Voice's name. I am sorry for her. It changes nothing I build.

## 4. ACTIONS. Years 31 to 40

1. **Answer the Lotfolk counters. Give them the count, every year's tags read aloud to them at the count house and cut into lead by their own hand, and take "the height of the Coin in thirteen years" and "what the stones carried back" in return. Then ask them to carry my count south as a gift, and to tell their god that the Olm counts gods as it counts households: by what each one keeps and does not lose.** Reason: they already have my number to the one. I lose nothing by giving what they have. I gain the only information about the sky anyone has offered, from the one god who measures rather than sings, and I gain a standing relationship with the second god that does not bargain. Five years on my caravan ground is long enough.
2. **Face the bank above Ossary for the two hundred paces that went, and two hundred more above that, in stone, and build a second chamber on the hill for whatever came ashore. Put an eel on its door too, and have the Ossary dead read at the festival by the headman, from the tags that were recovered, as the first names each year.** Reason: my revetment sent the river around itself and into a cemetery. The dead who went into the fen had tags like mine. If names kept are the engine, then a hundred names I let the river take are a debt against it, and the only way I know to pay it is to read them. The stone is just stone. Do it anyway.
3. **Rebuild the bridge deck in stone arches on the four piers, and make it the last time.** Reason: two decks gone in twenty years. The piers have held through everything, including a shake. Wat says the river can have the deck again and he will not be there to redeck it the third time. Stone arches from the nephew's cuts, with the carters' tolls and a year of the Tollan granary surplus paying for it. Wat designs it. Wat's son sets the last stone.
4. **Turn the horse herd into a cart and courier line, and sell the rest east. Keep thirty: six at the Backs steading, six at Greyholt, six at Tollan, twelve on the road as cart teams. Everything above thirty goes to the Reed Crossing for bronze while the Ruun herds are still thin.** Reason: sixty-one horses eat the grass of a hundred and eighty sheep and I have no plateau. Thirty working horses carry word and salt. Thirty-one idle ones are a gift I cannot afford to keep receiving. Tell Isca's herald, in full form, that the fire of the Olm has horses enough and asks that future gifts be cattle or cloth. Say it as a gift of information and not as a refusal.
5. **Put a second summer steading on the Greyholt hill above the village, stone fold and watch post, for the goats, and man it from the fourth month to the ninth with Greyholt men and six horses. Rebuild Ket's house inside Greyholt's wall and make him headman's speaker for life with a stipend from the salt toll.** Reason: Greyholt has lost every goat on its hill twice. The yard is finished as a trading post and Ket says the moor will come to the folds. The Backs steading proved the design. Ket has paid for thirty years of carrying messages between people who call him worse things from both sides, and lost his house for it.
6. **Settle Lord's Hill by contract before it is settled by a fire. Offer Scarrow Hill this: the footing may become a hall, of their stone, if it also becomes the count house for Scarrow and Scarrow Hill both, with their tags kept there under the bird and read at the Row every autumn by Aldo's son. The Driving is told at the Row by Tollan as always. On the hill, in their hall, they may tell it however they like.** Reason: I cannot stop a hall going up on a footing that is already laid, and three men died the last time I tried to control what Scarrow does with stone. I can make the hall a count house, which puts the Rooks' names inside the engine that feeds me and gives them a thing to be proud of that is not a grievance. The eighth stone stays empty. The ninth stone stays where it is. If they refuse, I know what the hall is for and I will plan accordingly.
7. **Pay Gorm. Send thirty sheep up Hagg Ridge with Ket, hardy moor stock bought back from his own people over the years, as a gift and not a rate, for the raid he took for running his boy ahead of Isca.** Reason: twice his runner saved my stock and once it cost him his. A treaty with a man who keeps it through a drought and a war is worth more than a yard. If the deep moor calls his clan the Olm's dogs, the Olm should at least feed its dogs.

If I get a free hand beyond those seven: send a cart east with Isca's riders carrying the names of the men of Brude's clan who died at the cairn, cut on clay tags in the Harl manner as best Ket can render them, as a gift to the Song. Not for the Harl. For Isca, because she is the god sleeping next to the pillow, and I want her to be reminded, every night, of the thing it cost to take it.

## 5. BEST MOMENTS and BORED OR PUSHED

### Best moments

- The elders arguing a month about the count house door and losing. Nothing on the meadow faces the stones but the people. Now the names do too.
- The first reading. People weeping at their own dead and laughing at the baby names. Reyn singing after each Weir name until half the meadow was doing it.
- Gorm: a Harl inside a wall is a captive whatever the gate is called. The best line anyone has said to me about my own building. He was right and he kept the treaty anyway.
- The Rooks bringing their own ninth stone when the elders would not yield the eighth. The ledger split producing a physical object nobody planned.
- The Lotfolk lead sheet saying 1,142 and my pegs saying 1,142. The world confirming my census from the outside. And the three counters still there five years later counting the swifts and paying for their bread.
- Isca unable to break the stone as fire and carrying it home on a litter under hides, and twenty men who had not slept, and half of Tollan dreaming it for one night as it passed. That was the one piece of lore in thirty years that came and went through my valley without asking me to do anything, and it was all the better for it.
- Wat redecking in oak a span higher and saying the river can have it again. Then his son's first ford, in the water, below the bridge, with the village on the bridge.
- Garran dying on his wall and both halves of Scarrow keeping the silent day on the same day.
- The Skelder hunter with his hands at his sides saying "Does your god remember the sky?" and nothing else, and waiting a winter for one answer. I did not know what to do with that and I was glad to be asked.
- The grandson, old now, saying his grandmother was wrong about everything, because the plum on the stumps is the best in the orchard. Forty years of one family's opinions about water and fruit, resolved by fruit.

### Bored or pushed

- Dim nights: four, five, four, five, four, five, four, five, four, five. Thirty years. The stone came and went. The count did not twitch. At this point the line reads as a metronome and I have stopped hearing it. If it is a clock I would like to see its face.
- The Mother of Dogs is being built up for a departure through four years of Skelder reports, and I am being asked about the sky. I can feel the thread being handed to me and I am not sure it is mine to hold. I answered the hunter because a question deserves an answer. I will not go north.
- The one-color beasts, a sixth and seventh mention. They walked over the empty Dip. Noted.
- Vell counted every summer with nothing new, which is honest, but a capped cistern that never gets uncapped is a drawer the narrator keeps opening to show me it is still shut.
- The Ossary graves. I understand why the river went around the revetment, and it is a good consequence, but a hundred graves with tags just like mine is the narrator making very sure I noticed the parallel. I noticed.
- Nothing bored me on the building side. Every stone I set got a real test and most of them passed. The ones that did not told me exactly why.

## 6. MOOD

This decade the world gave me work, pressure, and one war that walked through my valley on my own road without asking. The pressure was real and most of it I had built: the gate drew the burning, the road carried the column, the revetment turned the river into the graves, the census was read by a foreign god from a pile of pebbles. I own all of that and I am glad of it. That is the mood of a builder in a world that pushes back.

Where I felt dragged: north, by the Mother's questions and the pup on the knoll, and east, by the stone leaving with Isca and the pull going with it. Both were handled with some grace. The stone went past me rather than at me. The Skelder hunter asked and waited and went. Nobody made me go anywhere, and the one place I was invited to go, the moor with Isca, I declined and was honored for it. The narrator pointed, but the narrator also let me say no, and the saying no had a cost at Greyholt that was fair.

Where the narrator was most fair: the Lotfolk counters. Five years of three men paying for their bread is the world holding a decision open without forcing it, and I appreciated that more each year I did not answer. I am answering now because I want what they offer, not because they pushed.

The thing pressing on me most is not any of the mysteries. It is sixty-one horses, a bare wall at Greyholt, a footing on Lord's Hill, and an old man who has redecked a bridge twice. I know what the next ten years look like. Stone arches, stone bank, stone fold, one answer carried south, and thirty sheep up a ridge to a man who kept his word.
