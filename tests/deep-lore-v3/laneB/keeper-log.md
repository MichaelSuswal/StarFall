## Neutrality self-check

- The player said in round 1 that he resented objects being sent to him and dim nights being counted at him. I was tempted to answer that by thinning both. I did not thin the world to please him: Ossary's grave goods came out because he ordered a trench through the bank and asked that they go to the count house, and they were shown once, in his own box, with his own count. - I was tempted to let the written count slow the fading clock enough to save the grey plum, since the response table rewards "records the count." I did not. One kept thing begun in Year 12 does not move a Year 20 mark for a plum that was already not worth drying in Year 8. I recorded it as partial toward the later marks, which is where it honestly bears.
- Isca's question about the weak pull and her remark that it lay nearer the Olm. I cut a clause in which she observed that no one had gone, because it would have read as the world nudging a player who has chosen not to go. Her question itself stands because it is her opening goal and she asks openly.
- Gorm's "points at nothing" is E4 reaching the player through regular Harl contact the player built. I placed it as a joke among several things Gorm said about the gate and did not return to it.
- The player theorizes that the fourth pull, the blue hair and the warm stone are one object. Nothing in the block confirms or denies it. Harl reports about Fallow Dip arrived because the player made a Harl trading yard, and they report what the Harl saw, no more.
- The player theorizes that the Lotfolk cairn and the singers are a census aimed at his valley. I showed the cairn count beside the tag count because a child at a caravan ground would count it, and said nothing about why they agree.
- I resolved the Gathering with the god naming his eight villages because a trader names his home, and stopped there. I did not have him give his dead or households, which the player did not say and which would have been a large gift made for him. The standing offer is Isca's, in her words, and is not pressed.
- The player's theory that his strength is attendance at the Row is at 90 and is roughly right. I neither rewarded nor punished it: the weight line in Year 20 states what he feels and nothing else.
- Costs fell where the actions put them: a hauler drowned in a fixed flood on a frame the player ordered built; six men were absent for a fixed raid because the player sent them; Scarrow's split, which the player chose to manage as a contract rather than a claim, did what the table says it does.
