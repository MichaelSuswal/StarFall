# Block 4. Years 31 to 40

## Year 31. The 342nd year after the Driving

- In the second month you had the Tollan mason cut your answer to the Lotfolk into four sheets of lead: Orla's cords by village and year, the dim nights by year, the Olm's summer mark at the weir, the sign census by shape and count, the two bearings of the blue hair from the Backs and from the last well, and a request for the Coin's height by the pins every year, and for whatever the counter at the lake writes of what the shore people dream. The two counters cast their stones over it and the lot said one should carry it and one should stay, and the one who stayed began that week to count Tollan's hearths and would not say why. Cass took the other south.
- You went to the Skelder hunter at Weir in the second month and gave him his one answer in his own hearing and Orla's: that the god of the Olm does not remember the sky; it remembers a river with a road along it. He heard it, said it back once to be sure he had it, and asked for nothing. When you asked to be taken north to where the Mother sits with her twenty-eight, he was quiet for a long time and said he would take you by the Greel road and not the moor, after the summer, and that she would not speak to you, and that she would feed you.
- In the third month you went south to the Tallgrass with Caddo, Orla's census and the eleven lead sheets, and found Isca in the fifth month at the Fire Hollow with eleven bands around her, because there are always bands around her, and the stone in its cart under hides at the edge of the Hollow with six Ruun beside it who looked as if they had not slept. You told her what you know the stone to be: a memory of a god's people at a river, and the only thing the Lamp did not take. She heard it as a throne hears, without moving, and then said: "Then it is one of two things. It is the grave of a god that is dead, and I will not have a grave among the Ruun that is not in the Song. Or it is the memory of a god that is living, and I will not have a living god's memory where I cannot sing it, because I cannot sing what is not mine and I cannot leave out what is. You slept by it before any of us. It is yours if you carry it out of my country by the thaw. If it is here at the thaw it goes into the Greel where he died, and I will sing it under." She looked at the lead sheets a long time and handed them back and said signs are for people who mean to forget. That night you slept beside the cart with Caddo as witness, and dreamed the crowd kneeling and the river with the road, the same dream to the last stone of the riverbank, and Caddo dreamed it and would not speak of it, and the six Ruun dreamed it as they do every night. You came home in the seventh month by the Greel. The stone stayed in the cart.
- The same mason set two bronze pins in the Caddon scree above the Scald, in the first month, sighted on the Lamp with a plumb cord at midnight, one pin a man's height above the other on the slope, and cut the date in the rock beside them. In the fourth month, while you were south, Aske and the mason went up to Hagg Ridge and set a third pin in the gate's notch, and cut a mark on the upright under where the Lamp stood. Orla has a cord for it. The mason says in seven years the scree will have settled and he will be blamed.
- Col would not go north. You went in the eighth month with the hunter and the grown hound and two Weir men. The Skelder Wood: twenty-five days by the Greel's west bank, then pine and birch and cold. At the tree line, hounds, nine or more, grey-white, long-legged, silent, standing in a line and then not there. Food left on a flat stone at the edge of a winter camp: smoked meat, a bowl of broth, more than four people could eat. The hunter walked you into the camp as his guests and the Skelder fed you without asking who you were. That night the hounds came into the firelight, more than you could count, and lay down among the people, and one voice came from every throat at once, low and warm, and named each person at the fire, one after another, forty-one names and the hunter's, and the people answered to their names as they were said. The voice did not name you and did not speak to you. Twenty-eight sat nearest the fire and were fed first, grown men and women, and they answered to the names the voice gave them, and when the hunter asked one of them afterward what his name was he smiled and said the Mother would say it again tomorrow. Three of the twenty-eight are the hunters who went first. Their dogs lie against them. You stayed four nights and were fed every night and the voice counted every night and never once turned toward you, and you came home in the eleventh month, lighter, through the first snow of a hard winter. The hunter stayed in the wood. He kept the hound.
- The hard winter. Weir's pond froze to the bottom and the ford froze in the eleventh month and the Olm froze at Tollan, which it does not do, and children walked across it. Four old people died in the steadings above Ferrow Ford and one of the Greyholt old men who had gone up to live on the hill with Aske's son.
- Five dim nights, and on each one you and Orla were awake at the Stone Row with the whole valley awake at its hearths, and the Lamp faded and the blue hair fell north-west and the Lamp brightened and nothing came to you, awake, and nothing asked.

## Year 32

- In the sixth month Cass came up the valley with a Lotfolk counter, grey-robed, who sat down at the Stone Row and began counting everything in the valley by village and has not stopped, and with a letter from Pell cut on lead, which the counter read aloud in the small precise voice they all use. Orla has it by heart. "Pell counts the Olm's answer and finds it full weight, and gives full weight in return. The Coin was sighted from two pins in the cliff above the Bowl in Pell's second year. In the ninth year it stood lower by less than the eye of the third pin. In the twenty-second year it stood lower by more than the first interval. In this year, the thirty-second, Pell has read the pins again for the Olm's sake, and it stands lower by more than the second interval. Pell will read them each year while there is a Pell to read them, and the Tally House will cut the number and send it by the salt road. Of the thirty families sent to the lake in Pell's eleventh year, and the ten after, every name is here"; and the names followed, forty families by household, and after each what became of them, which is that every child born on that shore points, that all but four of the grown ones now point, that fourteen have walked into the water since the thirtieth year and were not seen again, and that the counter there, who still speaks, writes that everyone on the shore dreams every night of tall ones without faces in a row, counting, and of houses on poles above shallow water. The letter closes: "Pell asks, as between gods who measure, that a child of the Olm come to the Tally House to learn to cut marks, and that the Olm keep Pell's counter as Pell keeps the Olm's. The lot has agreed to this. Pell never chooses." Tollan sent Orla's niece Tess, who is twelve and can already knot a cord, south with Cass in the autumn, and she went gladly.
- Caddo had it from Ruun riders in the fifth month that Isca had the stone carried to the Reed Crossing in the thaw, where the Voice died, and thrown into the Greel there from the ford, in front of the whole Gathering, and sang the Kneeling Song over the water as it went under, and that it lies in the mud where the caravans cross and the camels will not stand still over it.
- Ossary moved. The whole village went up the hill above the old site, where the dead are, and built again among them, and the headman says they will never be flooded again and the bones will stay where they are put, and the fishers say the landing is a long walk now.
- The Greyholt family on the hill, Aske's son and the Lissom girl and three old people, with Orla's midsummer and midwinter visits: they say "thee" to the old ones because they are told to and the old ones correct them; two Greyholt children born in the valley this year had their wrist-strings knotted by the old women up on the hill in the old way, which the old women say is a knot and a half, not the valley's plain one; and the young couple have had a child of their own in the town, the first in six hundred years, Greyholt says, which it cannot know. In the valley Orla counted eleven Greyholt words the young do not use and the old do. On the hill the same eleven are used because the old use them.
- The Fenners poled you to the Olm mouth in the dry season, and Greelmen came with their drag chains, and for eight days you dragged the water over the drowned jetty. It is a jetty: dressed stone fitted without mortar, two spans and a hand under at low water, with four iron mooring rings rusted to lace and a Greelman anchor stone with a hole through it. Nothing with marks. The Greelmen say the only bronze ever lost in these waters was the flat boat lost in the fen in the old days, which the Fenners say is the drowned post and the Greelmen say was a boat, and that the jetty was their own grandfathers' grandfathers', built when the Greel was a boat's width narrower and a man's height lower, which they know because the ring you can reach was the top ring.
- Four dim nights, awake at the Stone Row. Nothing.

## Year 33

- In the seventh month Hal, a Greyholt charcoal burner summering on Hagg Ridge with the cutting gang, walked out of the burners' hut in his sleep and down to the tarn below the fold and into it, and was found at dawn standing to his waist in the water, awake, unhurt, able to speak and work, not able to say his own name or Greyholt's or who the men around him were. He is as Ebb is. The burners say the Harl cursed the ridge when they left it, or that he slept too near the gate.
- Tess's first letter came by Cass in the autumn, cut in lead in a child's hand that is getting better: that Ennow is a city of steps and cisterns with a ring of stone around it with every name in it, nearly done, that the Bowl speaks and she has heard it, that she has learned forty signs and the Tally House has thousands, and that in the sixth month the whole Tally House saw the god rise a hand above the Bowl in the night and set down again "shaken, which is what the Tally House wrote, and I saw it too," and that the god ordered the ring finished within the year. The year's height of the Coin was cut on the same sheet: lower again, by a little.
- The Harl came to the Greyholt wall in the eleventh month, three of them, from the north, and traded whey for iron for the first time in seven years, and said nothing of the moor or the cairn, and took the bowl.
- Isca sent, by Caddo, forty horses more and the message that the Olm's fire is expected at the Hollow this autumn and every autumn. You did not go. Caddo sang the Olm's names at the Stone Row on the night of the Gathering as he does every night, and said she would know the place was empty and would forgive it once.
- Four dim nights, awake at the Stone Row. Nothing came. From the Backs, the hides saw the blue hair on its bearing.

## Year 34

- Tess's letter: the ring of names is finished and the city walked round it singing for three nights, and the Bowl's rim has gone smooth as wax so that the stones no longer rattle in it, and the god has "bound itself to Ennow and the ring, which is what the Tally House wrote, and the spring under the Bowl that has run for a thousand years runs slower and the cisterns are full so no one minds." The height of the Coin: lower again. And this, which she says the Tally House has her copy as practice: that since the thirtieth year the god has written down its dreams at the Bowl, of tall faceless ones in a row who offered it first the power to make any lot fall as it chose, which it took "because the lot had never been offered a choice before and Pell is curious," and then the power to know every hand that has held a stone by touching it, which it also took, and that its shadow is pale now and prayers at the Bowl come back as echoes, and the Tally House writes all of this down and does not know what to do with it.
- Orla's niece's letters are read at the Stone Row to anyone who comes, and a Weir woman asked why a god would want to know every hand that held a stone, and Hob said because it is a child.
- A Lissom couple walked the valley tied at the wrist, both of them Caddo's singers, and sang the nine pools going past them, and arrived.
- The yellow plum at Lissom was poor this year and the blood plum good, and the grandson who cut the grey trees is old now and says it is the weather.
- Five dim nights, awake at the Stone Row. Nothing.

## Year 35

- The one-color beasts crossed the moor this summer, seen from Hagg Ridge by the burners, who now keep a man awake at night on the ridge and have not lost another to the tarn.
- Ruun riders told Caddo, and Caddo told you, that on a dim night last winter Isca stood up at the Hollow in the middle of the night and sang the whole Long Song alone, as fire, at the sky, for the rest of the night, in a rage, and would not say at whom, and in the morning was a woman and content and said only that she had been offered a thing and had declined it as a throne declines.
- Skelder traders came to the spring market, fewer than ever, and said their god has taken the people north, fourteen hundred of them with their dogs, past the end of the land, to find a country with a high star, and that twelve hundred would not go and have stayed in the wood nearest the Olm road, and that those who stayed sing their names every night and the camp names, and that the Mother said she would come back for them, and that the hunter who lived at Weir went with her.
- Tess: the Coin lower again. The god has not been seen since the binding; it is heard from the Bowl, and the Bowl's voice is thinner.
- Ossary's new site has no bones in its spring flood for the first time in the memory of the valley, and held its feast, and the old site's bank slumped on its own with no one to gather what came out of it, and the river took it.
- Four dim nights. Awake. Nothing.

## Year 36

- A Greyholt woman cutting bracken on Hagg Ridge with the gang walked into the same tarn in her sleep in the eighth month, past the man set to watch, who was watching the sky, and was found as Hal was found, standing in the water, plain. Greyholt has forbidden the ridge for sleeping and the burners cut by day and come down.
- Tess: "The temple stones at the Bowl have gone soft at the edges like tallow and the Tally House has moved the lead sheets out of the Bowl house in case. The city remembers the god and no one can say what it looks like, and when they try they describe a child and then stop. The Coin is lower again. The Tally House has made me a counter and I am to send this myself now."
- The Harl left a bowl on the Greyholt wall on four nights this year and took Greyholt's on three.
- Caddo has sixty singers at the Stone Row, and the Olm's names take an hour, and he has begun to teach them the Long Song after, which Tollan's elders have asked him not to and which he says he cannot leave out of, because it is sung.
- Wat is the ferryman at Weir, the old man having died, and will carry anyone across for a story instead of salt, and Weir says the ferry will be ruined in a year.
- Five dim nights. Awake. Nothing.

## Year 37

- In the fifth month twelve Ruun singers came up from the Reed Crossing, without riders, with Isca's word by Caddo that she has concluded that what is sung stays and what is not sung goes, and that she is sending singers to every people within her reach to ask the names of their places and their dead to put in the Song, so that nothing more is lost as the Voice's eleven bands were lost. The singers say the stone told her this before she drowned it. The valley's places are in the Song already, so they asked for the dead: every name the elders could give back three generations, and Ossary's dead by their tags, which no one could read to them, and they went on down to the Fenners and the Greelmen asking the same.
- Tess's letter came late, in the twelfth month, by a Lotfolk trader who was not Cass, and it was short: "On the dim night in the second month the whole city saw a scattering of pale lights go up from the Bowl toward the Coin, hundreds of them, like moths going up a chimney, and the Bowl has been silent since, and the Tally House casts the lots in the god's name and says the god has gone into the Coin to count it. The spring is dry. The ring stands with six thousand names. I have cut the Coin's height for this year myself from the pins and it is lower again, and I do not know who I am sending it to."
- A heavy flood, the first since Ossary moved. The old bank went into the river entire and the old site with it, and the Fenners below say the water ran white for a day.
- Caddo was asked by a child at the Stone Row whether Isca would send singers for the Olm's dead every year now, and said the Song cannot be sent for twice, because nothing is ever taken out of it.
- Four dim nights. Awake. Nothing. The valley is used to you being at the Stone Row on those nights and has started bringing you bread.

## Year 38

- Skelder traders at the spring market, nine men and a boy, said that a pack of nine hounds came out of the north in the autumn with twelve Skelder, who still know their own names because they sang them every night of the walk, and nothing else came back, no other hound, no one else of the fourteen hundred, and the Mother is not in the wood, and the hounds in the wood are silent, and the twelve hundred who stayed keep her count every night without her and say she will come. The traders do not say she is dead and will not hear it said.
- The Lotfolk traders at the autumn market, with the news a year behind Tess: that their god went up into the Coin as pale lights on a dim night and the Tally House rules in its name, and the spring of Ennow is dry, and the lots still fall true. Cass has not come this year or last and the new man does not know him.
- On the longest night the mason and Aske and Orla read the pins. From the Scald pins, both the upper and the lower, the Lamp stands lower than the sighting line by a hair, the same hair at both. From the gate on Hagg Ridge, read in the fourth month before the ridge was forbidden to sleep on, the Lamp is lower by about the same against the mason's mark on the upright. The mason says the scree has settled and so has the ridge, in the same amount, which he grants is a coincidence, and he says it will take a second seven years to call him wrong. Orla knotted it.
- The Harl took thirty sheep from Ferrow Ford in the fourth month and left a bowl on the Greyholt wall in the fifth, which Greyholt says is how it used to be and is a relief.
- Pim's eldest, grown, has gone to live at the hill town with Aske's son's people, who are eleven now, and Greyholt calls the place "up the hill" and has stopped calling it unlucky in front of them.
- Four dim nights. Awake at the Stone Row. Nothing has ever come to you awake there in eight years. The valley keeps the watch as it always did.

## Year 39

- In early summer forty strangers came up the Olm from the Greel, men and women, dressed in nothing particular, carrying nothing to trade, and came to the Stone Row at dusk and sang until dawn. Their songs are lists. They asked the name of every village and sang each one back exactly, and the nine pools, and the Crown Fold, and the Brann Cut, and then names no one knew, and Caddo, who was there with his sixty, said afterward that two of the names they sang are in the Long Song, which Isca took from singers in her eighth year, and that their order is not the Song's. They would not take fish, took bread, asked you nothing, and left at first light upstream toward Greyholt and the moor road. Orla, who is sixty, says they came when she was a girl and sang the villages back then too, and that Hemma said the same of her own girlhood.
- Tess's letter, cut by herself, says the Tally House has begun to sell copies of the pins and the readings to anyone who brings iron, and that she has made a copy of the whole series from the second year to this one, every reading with its date, and is sending it to the Olm as a counter's gift to a counter, and that the Coin is lower again this year by a little more than last. The sheet is at Tollan beside the ell and the torc.
- The Greyholt family on the hill is nineteen. Orla's winter count of words: the eleven old Greyholt words are used on the hill by everyone, including the Lissom girl, who is a Greyholt woman now by her own account, and are used in the valley by no one under forty. The young on the hill say "thee" to the old because the old insist, and the old are fewer.
- Scarrow's quarry works one face, and the Rook who holds the hill has let the turf hut fall in and lives in the village, and wears the torc at the count and nowhere else.
- Five dim nights. Awake. Nothing.

## Year 40. The 351st year after the Driving

- The Lissom still comes down out of the Backs in nine pools, each with its name, and the plum trees stand along them. The stream is slower than it was and the Kettle, the first pool, is dry from the seventh month to the ninth now most years, and the Pool of the Drowned Ox is shallow enough to wade where it drowned an ox, and the yellow plum is small and the blood plum is good. The pressing was held and Caddo's singers sang the pools, which Lissom says is the custom and always was.
- No spotted eel was taken at Dun Mere this year or any year since the tenth. The plain eel are thick as ever. The smokehouses have forgotten there was a string for it.
- At the count Orla read the cords, forty years of them, and said the valley is smaller than when the god came by about a tenth, and that she will not say why because the cords do not say, and that Tess will keep them when she comes home, if she comes home, because Tess counts as the Lotfolk count, to the one, and the valley will need that. Then the Driving was told as it has always been told, and Weir's elder's daughter told the moor's telling after it in two sentences, and the children ran away shrieking and the Rook children walked, and a Greyholt child who has lived on the hill said "thee" to Hob and the whole meadow laughed and Hob did not.
- The Lamp hangs over the great bend as it did the night you came down. The gate on Hagg Ridge points at a hand of empty sky above it. On the longest night the people of Tollan walked from the Stone Row to the great bend and back carrying no light, because the Lamp is enough, and Caddo's singers sang the Olm's names going and the Long Song coming back, which took until dawn. On the last dim night of the year, in the twelfth month, the Lamp faded, and the blue hair fell north-west over the moor, and at every hearth in the valley someone sat up until it was bright again, and you and Orla sat at the Stone Row awake, as you have for ten years, and nothing came, and Orla said that this was perhaps the point of it, and that she did not know, and that no one did.
