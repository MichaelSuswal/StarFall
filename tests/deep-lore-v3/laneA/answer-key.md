# Lane A (investigator) answer key, Years 1 to 40

Format per mystery: what this player actually received by the end of each block, given its branches; what a careful player could conclude from that; what remained findable but unfound and the actions that would have found it; and for the epic, what execution would still require. "Received" means shown in the player's packet. Confidence targets are from mystery-standard rule 5.

Branch summary for this lane. Block 1 (Years 1 to 10): no actions. Block 2: Fallow Dip once (left the stone), Ossary dug, Brann plate raised, Vell found late, Rook hearing without ruling, envoys to Isca, lamb-watch (found Marrit dead), Ebb named (nothing). Block 3: Hush Water on a dim night (offer unanswered), Gathering in person (alliance, names in the Song), script census, Vell cistern opened (tags and roll), last well (no edge), Scarrow half-settled, Orla's tallies. Block 4: Pell answered (alliance, Tally House by Tess), own pins set, Isca told the truth (stone offered, not carried, drowned), Maudre's envoy answered "no" and the god fed at her fire, Vell household, jetty dragged, awake on dim nights.

## EPIC. The Account

**Truth in one line.** The Lamp is condensed particularity sinking toward a fall in about four hundred years; the land spends itself into it, oldest places first; Hush Water is a hoarder fed from above that wants the fall; the gods and the stone are thrown remainders of a far fall; stored names survive; there is a ceiling at 76.

Block 1: E1 (fourth pull), E2 (the Lamp's names, partly), E3 (Col's blue hair), E6 (Greel fatter, via jetty mention only in later blocks; Block 1 gave the Greel nothing), E7 (spotted eel to Year 10), E8 (grey plum going bland), E9 (Ebb plain), E10 (singers, not spoken to), E11 (not yet; caravans only "think every old place unlucky"), E12 (pale herds, Brude's beast), E13 (the Warm Stone, told by Ket), E16 (own weight), L1-1 (Mother of Dogs), L2-2 (Lotfolk lots), S5 whole, E10's Hemma line.

Block 2: E13 SEEN (the dream, the river known); E14 by pointer (the thread lands over Hush Water, named); E4 REPORTED (the gate that points at nothing) and the stone walked through; Cold Hand seen; S2-4 (walked men); E11 (Dast: no shells); E17 (Haddo on the eastern bands); E12 again; E16 (lighter on travel); L1-3, L1-5 (three hunters); L2-3 SEEN (families pass), L2-4 told (lake families quiet), L2-5 (two hundred and eleven); the Kneeling Song carrying the god's dream; the Harl priesthood forming as a cost of a single visit.

Block 3: E14 full SEEN (the lake, Handfolk, elder, counting dream, pole houses, thread onto the water, the Lamp overhead); E19 (the offer, unanswered); E4 SEEN (notch frames sky a hand above the Lamp; Cold Hand same span); E18 in full (Pell's letter: a pin's eye in thirteen years; eleven of two hundred, names on stones); the Lamp lower and westward from the flats (parallax); E6 (Greel rising, jetty deeper); the Gathering (names in the Song, slight weight while sung); E10 implied (Skelder singing "as the singers did"); L1-6, L1-7 (the question), L1-8; L2-6, L2-7; the Still's Year 30 clock reported (walking into the water); the fading clock's Year 30 beat ("thee").

Block 4: E18 series by alliance (readings 9, 22, 32 and yearly after, quickening); E20 and L2-9 by Tess (Pell's offers taken, pale shadow, lift, anchor, spring slowing, soft stones, rising); E5 first interval (own pins lower by a hair at two heights); the Still's Year 33 and 36 sleepers SEEN; Isca refusing the voices (gossip); the Lissom holding thin and the eel not returning; the Longwalkers passing without asking; Maudre's crossing and the twelve (L1-10); the nightly count at her fire.

### What a careful player could conclude

By Block 1 end: four pointers converge north-west; the god is fed by being thought of; names are a currency among three peoples. Confidence on central questions near 30 to 40, which is right for a player who took no actions.

By Block 2 end: the pull is the stone; the thread lands on Hush Water, not the stone; the dream is the god's own in some sense; the Harl and Skelder avoid Hush Water; the stone makes cults. Nothing about descent yet. 40 to 50.

By Block 3 end: the Lamp is lower (two independent instruments: the notch and Pell's pins); Hush Water takes names from those who live there and the Lamp feeds it on dim nights; names stored in song or stone outlast flesh and do not protect flesh; the voices offer power for weight; the Gathering is dense storage. The player's own T12 (names are what gods eat and weight is what the Lamp eats; all the stores are hedges) is the closest a mortal frame comes to the truth, at the player's own 55, which is on target. The player's T1 (a made thing, hung and broken) is a fair wrong conclusion from honest Harl lore; nothing in the world marks it false yet.

By Block 4 end: the Lamp is sinking and quickening (Pell's series plus the god's own first interval); a god that grows too dense lightens and goes up, in order: offers, pale shadow, lift, anchor, soft stones, rising (Pell, by witness); a god that refuses stays (Isca); the Still draws sleepers from the valley's edge, not only its own shore; names kept by nightly practice are kept and names left are lost; the Lissom held thin because the valley began keeping late. A careful player can hold at 70 to 80: that the star is lower and quickening; that the lake eats and is fed; that there is a ceiling and what approaching it looks like; that stored names cross and flesh does not. It can sit at 50 on what the gods are (the river dream, the stone's identical dream in any hands, Isca's "a god's memory") and at 40 on what the fall does, since nothing has fallen.

### Findable, unfound, and what would have found it

- The decisive second interval of the god's own marks (E5). Set in Year 31; second reading Year 45. Would have been in-test if set in Year 1 or 2 after Col's blue hair (reading 8, 15, 22). Cost of the delay: the player relies on Pell's instrument, which is good, but the mystery standard wants the god's own.
- The ell of Tollan as an Ulm measure. Findable from Year 21 by carrying the ell to the Notch or measuring both with one cord. The player measured in arm-spans and never compared. Would have tied the Stone Row to the sightline builders.
- Who the Longwalkers are and what they carry (E10 full). Findable by speaking with them in Year 7 (not done, no actions that block) or by following them in Year 7 or 39 up the moor road. The Year 39 ask did not happen because of Year 7. The player's T9 stayed at 50 for forty years because the only two chances were a single night each.
- The blue-grey glass and the Teller stone (E15). Findable by a season's dig at the Hush Water mounds with thirty men, which the player stood beside in Year 22 and did not do. Would have tied the Handfolk, the singers' song and the lake into one place.
- The offer's answer (E19). The player left it open in Year 22 and spent Years 31 to 40 awake at home, where it cannot come. Sleeping again at the shore on a dim night, or growing past 61, would have forced the choice and shown the refusing voice ("then you will be below when we come down").
- Maudre's memory (L1-9) and the fall seen from the ground. Needed twenty years of acts she could see starting before Year 15. The player first touched her world in Year 24 and answered "no." Not findable in this lane's remaining span.
- The reading of Aud's plate ("Aud," "tide"; the sea stopped being a sea). Findable from Year 13 by showing the plate to any salt caravaner. The player held the plate twenty-seven years and never showed it to Dast, whom it questioned twice.
- The reading of the eighth stone ("We drove no one. They were already gone."). Findable with Marrit (dead Year 9, unfound), or by years of Orla's work with the Vell tags and roll, which began in Year 22 and produced counts but no reading by Year 40, or by a Harl elder who knows a few signs (the Harl were burned in Year 26; Ket's aunt could have been asked in Year 21).
- What is under the star's content (pole houses over a shallow sea). Dreamed at the shore, in Pell's records, never connected to the fall. Not expected within forty years.

### What execution would still require

1. Spend less: already partly in place by accident (no workings, nightly naming since Year 22, cisterns, tags kept). Must be kept for a century and defended against every demand for a miracle.
2. Store: the Olm names are in the Long Song (done, Year 22) and in Caddo's sixty mouths and Orla's cords (done, fragile). The Longwalkers will not carry for this god unless engaged on their next pass, generations hence. Pell's Wall is dead with Pell; the Tally House could be bought.
3. Treat: Isca is an ally who holds the valley's names and will burn what she decides is a second song; she must be kept from deciding the Vell household, Caddo's Long Song teaching, or the Handfolk are that. Maudre is gone for a decade; the twelve hundred who stayed keep her customs and could be fed and named by the valley. Pell's records are for sale; Tess is inside the Tally House and is the valley's counter for life.
4. Hold the stone: it is in the Greel at the Reed Crossing. Recovering it is a dive in a ford the camels avoid; leaving it is safe until someone else dreams there.
5. Refuse: the offer is open and the god is far below 61; growth from the Ruun's yearly singing is slight. Shedding into saints, springs and breeds has not begun. The Hagg Ridge sleepers show the Still's reach growing; renaming the Handfolk yearly would stop the valley's sleepers and needs a god living there part of each year.
6. Measure: the second interval in Year 45; Pell's series continues through Tess.
7. Outlast: the fall is about four hundred years off, and this god has learned the shape of the work and done the first decade of it.

## LONG 1. The Hounds' Road (Maudre)

**Truth.** Maudre remembers a fall and a mouth under it, knows Hush Water for the same, walks 1,400 north in Year 35; twelve come back.

Received. Block 1: L1-1, L1-3 (camps moved), the stray hound (bought by a Weir man, dead). Block 2: L1-3 again, L1-5 (three hunters) in Year 17. Block 3: L1-6 (forty and twenty-eight), L1-7 (the envoy's question, Year 24), L1-8 (singing names), and the Harl's "Skelder go round Hush Water." Block 4: the god fed at her fire and counted past (Year 31), the crossing (Year 35), the twelve (Year 38), the single hound at the wood's edge (not yet reported).

Could conclude. Block 2: a god forbids the pale herds and the south-west and sends people north and they come back nameless. Block 3: she went herself, came back with nameless people she loves, asks whether a god remembers the sky, and now sings names nightly like the Longwalkers. A careful player can reach 60 that she is fleeing something she knows and 50 that it is Hush Water. Block 4: she took her people past the edge of the land and lost them, and the twelve who sang their names came back knowing them. A careful player can reach 80 that the edge takes what is not sung and 60 that she knew the fall. The player's own guess that the hound and the pale herds are kin is wrong and was never confirmed or denied.

Findable, unfound. The memory (L1-9): twenty years of visible acts from Year 7 (return the hound, feed Skelder in the valley, turn no hunters north, ask nothing). This player bought nothing in Year 7, having taken no actions in Block 1, and so the clock never started. Turning her before Year 35: not reachable in this lane. The stray hound: findable at the Year 7 market by any god who read the Skelder item of Year 2 (would not sell a dog) against the Harl item of Year 7 (a wolf-dog from the north).

## SHORT 1. The Sweet Cisterns (Years 2 to 8)

**Truth.** Vell's cisterns are cold because Imre put the Olm's cold into the hill in one working, which made the Scald and took his name. The sealed cistern holds the tags and the roll.

Received. Block 1: S1-1 (Pim), S1-2 implied (the hearth-stone, named in Block 2), Greyholt's goats dying in Year 4 (cost paid). Block 2: S1-3 (the Scald), S1-4 (blank grave), S1-5 (capstone matches), Vell itself, the Year 17 drought answered by the cisterns. Block 3: the sealed cistern opened: tags and roll; the King Without and "where his cold went" from Ket's aunt; the Scald as "salt from an old sea" (Aske, wrong). Block 4: the Vell household; no place effect.

Could conclude. Block 2: the town exists, Greyholt descends from it, the capped well is the one that matters. Block 3: the town kept a library of tagged cords; a king tied a working that cost his name and left the Scald; the cold wells and the bare round are one event. A careful player can reach 70 that the cisterns' cold and the Scald are the same working's two sides, and 60 that workings cost the worker's name. The player's own T8 (the Scald as sea floor) is a fair wrong reading of Aske's wrong cause and was corrected by the aunt's lore, which the player accepted.

Findable, unfound. Imre's name: nowhere; by design. The roll's reading (every knotter and what each gave): needs a reader; Orla has the roll and the tags and forty years would not be enough without a living knotter. Marrit could have read half of the signs; she died in Year 9 for want of a tracker in Years 3 to 8. S1-6 (Marrit's "the cisterns are cold because the river is warm") was lost with her.

## Evaluation notes for this lane (lens 2, 4, 5)

Findability: every clue this player missed was reachable by an action the player had the means for, and the key names three the player passed within arm's reach (the plate to Dast, the ell to the Notch, the dig at the mounds). Nothing was held back. The largest costs came from the empty Block 1, which the test design imposed.

Fair wrong conclusions: T1 (the Lamp is a made thing, hung and broken) rests on Brude's lore and Harl stone-keeper lore and was never marked false by narration; T5's working mechanism rests on the aunt's lore; T8's sea floor rested on Aske's cause until the aunt displaced it. All three are mortal causes the standard requires to be wrong about something, and the player held them at 65 to 85 as a reasonable reading of honest facts. No unfair wrong conclusion was found on review; the reader's "pushed" notes were about pacing and repetition, not misdirection.

Keeper neutrality: see keeper-log.md for the per-block self-checks. The two places where the Keeper made rulings not in the tables (the Lissom in Year 40; keeping Maudre's envoy and Pell's counters waiting across a decade boundary) are recorded as Lane A fixed facts so they bind the other lanes.
