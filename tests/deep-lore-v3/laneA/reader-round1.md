# Reader Round 1. Years 1 to 10. Lane A, investigator style

## 1. THEORIES

### T1. The Lamp is not a sun or a god. It is a made thing, and it is failing or being drained.
Confidence: 65
Evidence: the lamp-watch is kept at every hearth and nobody knows why, which reads like a procedure that outlived its reason. Dim nights are getting more frequent: one in Year 1, one in Year 2, two in Year 5, three in Year 10. Col's "blue hair" coming down out of the Lamp to the north-west over the moor four times in his life; he reads it as the Lamp drinking from a river, I read it as a tether, a discharge, or a feed line between the Lamp and whatever sits to the north-west. The Harl keep a lamp-watch too and claim theirs is older, so the watch predates Tollan's culture and is not local superstition.
Rival A (25): the Lamp is a natural celestial body with an irregular cycle, and the dim-night clustering in Year 10 is noise in a ten-year sample. Three hundred years of watch would then just be a very old habit around an eclipse-like event.
Rival B (10): the dimming is tied to me. I arrived, and the frequency went up. I cannot rule out that my presence, or the four of us arriving together, is costing the Lamp something.

### T2. The weak fourth presence to the north-west is the same thing Col's blue line goes to, and it is either a dead or dormant god, or an artifact.
Confidence: 60
Evidence: Direction match. The fourth presence is north-west, nearer, weaker, does not move, does not answer. Col's blue hair is north-west over the moor. Ket's fallen stone at Fallow Dip is on the moor, warm for a season, gives dreams of a crowd kneeling, and the Harl call it a piece of "the Low One." A crowd kneeling is worship seen from the receiving end. The Skelder's Mother of Dogs forbids walking south-west past the Fell's end and will not say why; from the far north, south-west of the Fell could easily be the same moor region. So three independent cultures (Ferrow Ford shepherd, Harl, Skelder) point at one patch of moor and none of them want to go there.
Rival A (25): the fourth presence is a god like me that is simply asleep or wounded, and Fallow Dip, the blue line, and the Skelder prohibition are three unrelated moor things that I am stitching together because they share a compass bearing.
Rival B (15): the fourth presence is not a god at all but the Lamp's anchor or counterweight on the ground, and "does not answer" because it is a mechanism.

### T3. The Lamp and the gods feed on attention. I am literally heavier when worshipped. The Lamp's dimming may be the same hunger at a larger scale.
Confidence: 70 for the first sentence, 35 for the Lamp link
Evidence: Year 9 is explicit. I was lighter when Scarrow stayed away, lighter in the spring of Year 4 when nobody came, heavier after every harvest festival. That is not metaphor; "you feel it as you feel your own weight." Oss the Lotfolk builds a cairn counting households "so his god knows how many of us to pray for," which is a second culture treating headcount as something a god consumes. The Ruun singers and Isca's riders sing lists of names; the Mother of Dogs speaks every person's name every night "so that no one is forgotten." Three traditions treating names as a currency between gods and people. If the Lamp works on the same rule, the dimming nights mean it is being under-fed or something is drawing it off.
Rival (30): weight tracks worship for gods, but the Lamp is a different kind of thing and the name-singing is just how pastoral peoples keep memory. Three cultures counting people is not surprising on its own.

### T4. The Ossary bones are not a graveyard. Something large and old is buried in the bank, and the river is slowly opening it.
Confidence: 55
Evidence: bones every spring for three hundred years, so many that reburial is a civic ritual. The objects are wrong for a village cemetery: a bronze needle a hand long with an eye at each end (not a sewing needle; a double-eyed tool, perhaps netting, perhaps surgical, perhaps a bodkin for lacing something heavy), and a fired clay tag with scratched marks on a cord, which is a label or a record. Labelled bodies plus tools is a disaster deposit or a battlefield or a flooded settlement, not a cemetery. The Fenners also have a "drowned trading post" ringing a bell under fog at the Brann Cut on the north fen, which is a second drowned-place tradition in the same lower valley.
Rival A (30): it is an ordinary old burial ground on a bend that erodes, and villages everywhere turn up grave goods. The tag is a name tag on a body, which is what cemeteries do.
Rival B (15): the bones and the Driving are the same event. "The lords fled into the moor and never came back" may be the polite version. Three hundred years is roughly right for bones to still be whole in waterlogged clay.

### T5. The Driving story is at least partly false, and Lord's Hill is where the real evidence is.
Confidence: 50
Evidence: the eighth stone is never stood on, and nobody says why, which is the same pattern as the lamp-watch: a prohibition with the reason gone. Mother Tollan's arm is a bronze rod, a physical standard that someone brought, not something a peasant uprising invents. Aldo Rook holds a gold torc as thick as a child's wrist and claims lordly descent, and Scarrow's elders did not shout him down. A gold torc that size is a surviving piece of the pre-Driving regime that the official story says fled to the moor and vanished. Pim, a nine-year-old, found a town in the hill with wells of cold water and nobody in it, and was called a liar and forbidden the hills; the elders did not go look, they suppressed. Then she married and stopped talking.
Rival A (35): the Driving happened essentially as told. Aldo's torc is a stolen or inherited valuable and his claim is opportunism triggered by my arrival. Pim found an abandoned Harl summer camp or an old quarry.
Rival B (15): the lords did not flee into the moor; they went into the hill, and the empty town with cold wells is where they went, and the hills are "unlucky" because the valley sealed it.

### T6. The Lissom grey plum, the spotted eel, the heather honey, and the lambs are one phenomenon: something is thinning out the valley's distinctive living things, and it is subtle and slow.
Confidence: 40
Evidence: Berit says the grey plum went bland first, then small, over decades. The spotted eel vanished in Year 10 after being rarer and pricier for years. Lambs taken with no blood and no drag mark, two years running, with a knotted-grass sign no herder ties (Col blames Harl; the Harl are raiders who take thirty sheep at once with blood everywhere, not one lamb a month quietly). The one-color beasts with no smell and no findable heart cross the moor in some years, and two cultures refuse to eat them. If something is producing or consuming life in a way that strips out variation, the one-color herd is what the end state looks like.
Rival A (45): plums decline from bad grafting, a pike ate the eels, a lynx or a human thief takes the lambs, and the one-color beasts are an unfamiliar herd animal the Harl have folklore about. Four unrelated things.
Rival B (15): the lambs are being taken by Pim's hill people or by whatever lives at Fallow Dip. The grass knot is a signature, and I should be collecting them.

### T7. Isca's Gathering at the Fire Hollow is a trap or a tribute system, not a courtesy.
Confidence: 45
Evidence: "a lesser fire is invited to a greater." The riders gave a horse unasked and refused payment, which among gift-cultures establishes obligation. The Ruun are something you sing before, which in this world (see T3) means feeding. The herald spoke in a manner nobody had heard, and nobody in the valley can judge what was actually said. Isca is one of the three strong presences I felt; east and a little south matches the Reed Crossing direction. A stronger god inviting a weaker one to a feeding ground is a reason to be careful.
Rival (55): it is a real diplomatic convention among the gods who came through, and refusing it is the mistake. The Ruun could be the oldest god or the thing that let us all in. I would learn more in one night at the Fire Hollow than in ten years of valley gossip.

### T8. Ebb's "salt" memory loss is contact with something past the last well, and the caravaners know more than they say.
Confidence: 45
Evidence: the caravaners think every old place is unlucky, will not sleep inside a wall, will not step on a threshold with the left foot. That is a culture with heavy taboo around built places, which is what you develop if built places have hurt you. Ebb came back functional but with his names gone. Names are the currency in this world (T3). Something out east took his names. Nan asked me to give him back; if I can, that tells me what I am.
Rival (55): heatstroke, dehydration, or a blow to the head on a desert crossing, folk-attributed to salt. Caravaners have taboos because all traders do.

### T9. The Ruun singers and Isca's riders are doing the same thing, and the singers who came in Year 7 are not human.
Confidence: 35
Evidence: forty people, "dressed in nothing particular," carrying nothing, no trade, sing lists of names all night, ask every village name and sing it back exactly, then go on to names no one knew, refuse fish, take bread, leave upstream toward the moor. Hemma says they came when she was a child and "sang the villages back to us." That phrasing suggests the valley had lost something and the singers restored it. Isca's riders also sing lists of their bands and camps and dead. Two groups keeping a registry of names by song.
Rival (65): a wandering religious order or a bardic caste from downriver, fully human, with a long memory, who make a circuit every few generations.

## 2. WHICH OBSERVATIONS MATTER

### Meaningful, I am fairly sure
- Lamp-watch kept without reason; Harl have an older one; dim nights increasing (Y1: 1, Y2: 1, Y5: 2, Y10: 3).
- Col's blue hair out of the Lamp to the north-west. Four sightings in a lifetime, always dim nights.
- The fourth presence: north-west, near, weak, does not move, does not answer.
- Fallow Dip: stone fell the night I came, warm a season, cairn, dreams of a kneeling crowd, "piece of the Low One."
- My own weight tracking attendance at the Stone Row. This is the only hard mechanical datum I have about myself.
- The eighth stone never stood on.
- Ossary: the double-eyed bronze needle and the scratched clay tag on a cord.
- Pim's town in the hill with cold wells, and the elders' refusal to look.
- Aldo Rook's gold torc and the fact Scarrow did not shout him down.
- The knotted grass at the lamb fold.
- One-color beasts: no smell, no findable heart, two cultures refuse to eat them, the Mother of Dogs forbids both hunting them and walking south-west past the Fell's end.
- Isca's invitation and the "lesser fire" framing. Unpaid gift of a horse.
- The Year 7 singers who knew names the valley did not.
- Oss's household-count cairn "so his god knows how many to pray for."
- Hemma's short cords in Year 10, said to me directly. She is telling me the population curve has turned down and she wants me to know it.
- Ebb's lost names, and the caravaners' threshold and wall taboos.
- The Brann Cut bell under fog and the drowned trading post. Second drowned-place story in the lower valley.
- The spotted eel vanishing.

### Probably just the world, and I like them for it
- The miller and ferryman's twenty-year cow feud, its ending, the remarriage.
- Wat who cannot be kept from the river, and his first ford.
- The Lissom wrist-tied walks, the one that succeeded and the one that untied at Weir.
- Dun Mere twins, the eel fair date quarrel, the way a spotted eel looks at you.
- Garran's fingers, his retirement, the nephew on Scarrow's stone.
- Otters in the mill race, the black-legged fox in Tollan's lanes, rooks and swifts as weather signs.
- Gorse coming back thicker, dark honey, salt wind, fog, geese.
- The Harl bowl of whey on Greyholt's north wall. This one is lovely and I think it is just two peoples quietly trading despite raids. I note it because it is also a channel I can use.
- Greyholt's goats dying, Ferrow Ford old people dying in hard winters, Ossary's feast custom.

### Unsure which side
- The Skelder refusing to sell dogs at any price, and the Harl "wolf-dog" that will not eat or look at anyone. Probably color, but a silent grey-white long-legged animal from the moor with no appetite sits oddly close to the one-color beasts. Keep an eye on it.
- The salt caravaners' taboo on old places. Could be flavor, could be the most informed people in the valley.
- The Fenners counting a marriage out as a loss. Suggests the Fenners keep their own headcount, which suggests they have a reason to.

## 3. ACTIONS, Years 11 to 20

1. **Go north-west onto the moor in person, with Col as guide and Ket the Harl as interpreter, to Fallow Dip, in the dim-night season.** Reason: tests T1 and T2 together. I want to stand at the cairn on a dim night and see whether the blue line comes down there or beyond it, and whether the fourth presence gets louder as I approach or stays mute. I will sleep by the stone and record the dream. If the presence answers at close range, T2 Rival A rises. If the blue line lands on the cairn, T2 goes to 85. Cost: a season of the god absent from the Stone Row; I accept being lighter for it.

2. **Dig the Ossary bank deliberately, in the dry season, before the flood does it for me.** Reason: tests T4. Put the Ossary headman in charge of a controlled cut above the normal slump line, and have every object kept, not reburied, with its depth noted. I want to know whether there is a floor, a wall, a layer of ash, or ordered rows of bodies. Ask the Fenners to pole me into the Brann Cut on a foggy autumn day to hear the bell and sound the bottom with a weighted line.

3. **Take Pim to Greyholt and go up the Caddon Hills with her, with two Greyholt hunters who know the deer paths.** Reason: tests T5. She stopped talking because she was called a liar for six years, not because she forgot. A fifteen-year-old who still remembers the goat path can find the town. I want to see the wells, count the houses, and look for the torc's siblings. If it exists, I lift the elders' ban on the hills myself.

4. **Summon Aldo Rook to Tollan alone and examine the torc, and then set Hemma's cords beside Scarrow's genealogy.** Reason: tests T5 from the other side. I want his family story in his own words, and I want to know if the torc has marks that match the Ossary clay tag or the eighth stone. I do not grant his claim. I buy his cooperation with the hearing.

5. **Send a small deliberate embassy to the Fire Hollow instead of going myself: Hemma's chosen successor at the cords, one Tollan elder, and Garran, who is old and skeptical and will not be impressed by singing.** Reason: tests T7 without committing my person. Their task is to listen, count the Ruun's attendants, learn what "singing before the Ruun" physically does to the one who sings, and come back. I attend only after I have their report. If Isca's herald is insulted by a proxy, that itself is information about what "lesser fire" means.

6. **Set a standing lamb-watch on the Backs with Col: a herder in a hide at every fold on dim nights, and collect every grass knot untouched into a box at Tollan.** Reason: tests T6 Rival B against the mundane readings. If the lambs go only on dim nights, that ties the taking to the Lamp. If a herder sees who ties the knot, most of T6 collapses one way or the other. Cheap, and it keeps Ferrow Ford from launching a Harl raid on a guess.

7. **Attempt to give Ebb his names back, formally, at the Stone Row, with Nan present and the whole of Greyholt there to say his name to him.** Reason: tests T3 and T8. If names are what gods trade in, a full village speaking a man's name to him in front of his god is the most direct experiment I can run on my own nature. If it works, I know what I am and I know what took him. If it does nothing, I question the caravan master about the last well on the next salt market, and I keep the eighth stone unstood until I understand it.

## 4. BEST MOMENTS and BORED OR PUSHED

### Best moments
- Year 9: feeling my own weight change with attendance. The first piece of hard mechanics about myself, delivered as sensation rather than rule.
- Year 2: Col mentioning the blue hair only when asked about pasture, and not caring for it. The casualness is what made it land.
- Year 8: Oss building the household cairn so his god knows how many to pray for. One line that reframes every head count in the packet.
- Year 10: Hemma saying the cords were short, to me directly, for the first time. An old woman changing her protocol is louder than any omen.
- Year 4: Ket's dream of a crowd kneeling. Worship from the receiving end.
- Year 3: the double-eyed needle, brought to me because nobody knew what it was for. Perfect investigator bait that does not explain itself.
- Year 6: the ferryman's cow dying and the two men speaking. Nothing to do with anything and it made the valley real.
- Year 9: Brude cutting open a one-color beast and finding no heart.

### Bored or pushed
- The flood, fog, geese, salt wind, swift, and rook entries repeat with small variation nearly every year. By Year 7 I was skimming them. One or two a year would carry the same texture.
- Year 10's Isca embassy arrived fully formed with a named god, a named gathering, a named audience, and a gift horse, in a single bullet. After nine years of oblique hints it felt like a door being opened for me rather than one I found. Mild push.
- The Year 7 singers and the Year 10 riders both "sing lists of names," and the Skelder Mother of Dogs "speaks every name every night." Three name-litany cultures in four years starts to feel like the theme being underlined.
- Hemma aging one year per entry (79, 81, 84) is a nice running clock but the repetition of her age reads as the author making sure I noticed her.
- Pim's story closing in Year 8 ("no longer talks about the town") was good restraint, but I had nothing in Years 3 to 8 to pull on; the elders' ban was stated and never tested by anyone. I would have liked one villager to sneak up and come back with something, or not come back.

## 5. MOOD

Pulled to act, strongly, and in a specific direction: north-west. The packet does a good job of making the moor the gravitational center without anyone ever saying "go to the moor." Four separate pointers (fourth presence, blue line, Fallow Dip, Skelder prohibition) converge on it from four cultures, and I only noticed the convergence on a second read, which is how it should feel.

Not plodding. The valley-life bullets give texture rather than padding most of the time, though the weather entries tipped into padding by mid-block.

Narrator pointing: a little. The Year 9 weight paragraph is the one place the narrator steps forward and explains a rule outright ("you feel it as you feel your own weight"), which I welcomed because it was mechanics rather than plot. The Year 10 Isca entry is the place where it felt most like a hand on my back. Hemma speaking "to you directly, which she has not done before" is a flag rather than a hint; I noticed the flag. The name-litany repetition across three cultures is the thematic underline I most felt. Everything else, including the needle, the tag, the knot, the torc, and the eighth stone, was planted and left alone, which is exactly what keeps me digging.

One thing I would put money on and have no evidence for: the eighth stone is for the fourth presence.
