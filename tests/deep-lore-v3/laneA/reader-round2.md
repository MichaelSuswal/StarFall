# Reader Round 2. Years 11 to 20. Lane A, investigator style

## 1. RESULTS

### Action 1. Go north-west to Fallow Dip with Col and Ket, in dim-night season.
The world answered clearly and in more directions than I asked. What I got:
- The pull is the stone. It grew plainer every day and sat under my hand at the cairn, and it still did not answer. So the fourth presence is not a sleeping god of my kind; it is an object that registers to me as a presence.
- The blue line does NOT come down on the cairn. It comes down fifteen days further on, over Hush Water, "where the pointing folk are." T2 as stated was wrong in its specific claim and right in its gist: the north-west convergence is real, but there are two things out there, not one. The stone is the near one. Hush Water is the far one, and it is where the Lamp's line lands and where the Skelder god and the one-color beasts both refuse to go.
- The dream came. A crowd kneeling and a wide slow river with a road along it, and I knew the river "the way you know where your wound is." That is the single most important sentence of the block: the stone's dream is my memory, or the memory of whatever I am a piece of.
- Brude's account: the Low One was hung up as a lamp by the first men, and broke when the gods came through it. So the Lamp is a made thing, it is the door we came through, and we broke it coming in. T1 moves hard upward.
- Two square-notched stones on the way: Gorm Longhand's fold gate, "the gate that points at nothing," and Cold Hand. Both notched, both ancient, one of them laid flat as a boundary. Something was built across the moor in a line, and the Harl have inherited the pieces as folklore.
- The walked men. Lowlanders who came up out of the valley three hundred years ago "plain as sheep" having forgotten who they were, taken in by the Harl. That is Ebb's condition, three hundred years ago, applied to the losers of the Driving. The Driving was not a torch mob. Something was done to the lords that stripped their names, and the Harl have never forgiven the valley for it.
- Cost was real: I came back lighter, as in Year 4. The weight mechanic holds under travel, not just under attendance.
- Side effect I did not intend: a god sleeping at the cairn made the Harl treat the stone as chosen. By Year 20 there are six keepers and no raid leaves the moor without consulting the dream. I made a cult by investigating.

### Action 2. Dig the Ossary bank; sound the Brann Cut.
Clear answer, theory wrong in the interesting way. It is graves, in rows, heads upstream, at two depths with clay between. No floor, wall, or ash. So T4 ("something large and old is buried, not a cemetery") was wrong; it is a cemetery. But the details cut deeper than the theory: the deep row has tags on every third neck and blue glass beads and the double-eyed needles; the shallow row has almost no tags. Two populations, or one population that lost the habit of tagging between the two layers. The headman's reading (the lords' people from the Driving) fits the shallow row but not the deep one under it, which predates whatever the shallow row is.
The Brann Cut gave a bronze plate with banded marks and a shorter line "in a hand no one in the valley has seen." Second script. Rang the bell's note when the lead struck it, and the reeds have not rung since. So the bell was the plate, and the plate was being struck by something or ringing with something; it stopped when removed. I now hold two inscribed objects from the lower valley and a box of fourteen tags, plus the witch's tag with four signs.

### Action 3. Take Pim up the Caddon Hills.
Clear answer, theory right. The town exists: three tiers cut into grey rock, more house-cuts than Greyholt and Lissom together, eleven wells of cold sweet water, one capped with a carved stone whose pattern is Greyholt's hearth-stone snake. A flat grave stone with a border and nothing inside it. No gold, no bodies. On the way: the Scald, a half-day round of bare salt with no seed or bird, which Aske calls the salt of an old sea. So the eastern salt flats that took Ebb's names and the Scald above Greyholt are the same stratum. The town is above the old sea line; the valley is below it.
T5 Rival B (the lords went into the hill) is not supported: the town is empty of people and of anything lordly, and its one carved motif belongs to Greyholt, a village of goat herders. The town predates the lords. Lifting the ban cost me nothing and gained the valley a drought well in Year 17, which is the only time in twenty years a thing I did fed people.

### Action 4. Summon Aldo Rook; compare torc, tag, cords, eighth stone.
Clear answer and a real find. The torc is unmarked. Aldo's line is thirteen named generations from Ysolt the hall-daughter, and the cords cannot check it past seventy-two years. But one of the three signs on the Ossary tag is cut on the eighth stone, fourth sign along. So the eighth stone carries the tag script, and the eighth stone is the one nobody stands on. The Driving tradition preserves a stone of the lords' writing and forbids touching it, which is the same move as the lamp-watch: an inherited rule with the reason scraped off.
Cost: Aldo told Scarrow the god heard him. Rab Rook killed Tam over the tithe in Year 12. Scarrow split, broke the hill, brought up eleven torcs, wave-stamped coins, a dish, and the oak board that has been scraped and recut and scraped again. Aldo wears two torcs on Lord's Hill with a tenth of half a quarry. I did not grant his claim and I did not stop him, and the valley reads my inaction as both. This is the costliest thing I did and I would do it again for the board.

### Action 5. Send an embassy to the Fire Hollow instead of going.
Clear answer. T7 (trap or tribute) came back roughly half right and half wrong. Isca did not harm them. She was insulted precisely, and she said it precisely, and she kept a place for the Olm that will stand empty "until the Olm fills it or the Song outlasts the asking." She burns people who will not sing for her. The Song keeps the dead "where the Ember can hear them." The Voice sings every name of the Ruun and nothing visible happens to him. Hob was told a valley's names can only be given by its god. So the Gathering is a registry of names, which the god holds, and a god cannot send proxies because the god IS the thing being registered. Garran's verdict is the right one: "Go if you like." The embassy was correct as an investigator move and wrong as a diplomatic one, and I now owe a visit I cannot delegate.
Isca's riders then came through twice, to the stone, and carried my dream east as a song. Whatever the dream is, Isca now has it.

### Action 6. Lamb-watch on the Backs; box for knots.
Clear answer and T6 Rival B collapsed in the best way: the box stayed empty because the lambs stopped in the hard winter of Year 9, and Dunn, keeping the hide anyway, found the witch's hut. Old woman, tag with four signs at her neck (one on the eighth stone), dozens of knotted cords no one at Ferrow Ford can tie or untie, lamb bones for years. The lambs were being eaten by a tag-wearer who kept knotted records, alone on the Backs, who died in the Year 9 winter. Col calls her a Harl witch. I think she was the last person in the valley who still wore a tag and still knew the knots, living out of sight. Hemma's cords are knotted; the valley keeps records in knots; she kept dozens. Two record systems, one dying with her. The hide-watch also gave me a reliable dim-night count and confirmed the blue line is on every dim night, not some.

### Action 7. Give Ebb his name back at the Stone Row.
Clear answer, theory wrong. Nothing left me and nothing arrived. Ebb kept the name as a gift and still calls Nan "the woman who weeps." So I cannot restore names by willing, and attention flowing toward a person does not do what attention flowing toward me does. T3's first clause (I am fed by attention) stands; its extension (names are a currency I can spend) is dead. Dast's testimony is the bigger prize: eleven days past the Reed Crossing is the last well; past it one white plain to the edge of seeing; the salt climbs a man from the feet; ten days beyond stand "the dead city's steps," which his uncle reached and came back emptied; no shells, no wrack, "the sea took everything with it when it went away angry." He will guide a god there for a named price and will not go down the steps.
Cross-reference by Year 20: Ruun bands grazing the far east come home nameless but can still sing the Song; Skelder hunters sent north past the land's end came back nameless, their dogs knew them; Lotfolk sent west into bare country came back nameless with their names still sharp on the stones in their hands. Four directions, four cultures, one disease: go past the edge of the inhabited land and your names are taken, but names stored outside you (song, stone, cord, tag) survive. Every god with a people is now building a name store: the Song, the Mother's nightly roll call, the Lotfolk ring of stone. The tags at Ossary were a name store too. That is what the deep row was doing.

## 2. THEORIES, updated

### T1. The Lamp is a made thing, the door we came through, and we broke it. The dimming is the breakage worsening.
Confidence: 80 (was 65)
Why: Brude's account is explicit and is the Harl's oldest lore. The dim-night count went 1, 1, 2, 3 in block 1 and 4, 5, 4, 4, 5, 4, 5, 4, 4, 5 in block 2. Flat at four or five a year since Year 11, which is interesting: it jumped when we came and then plateaued. Not a steady decline; a step change.
Rival A (12): the Lamp is a natural body with a cycle that happened to shift. The Harl story is a story.
Rival B (8): the step change is us four gods drawing on it continuously, and it has found a new equilibrium. If one of us dies or leaves, the count should change.

### T2 revised. There are two things north-west. The stone at Fallow Dip is a broken piece of the Lamp that carries a memory. Hush Water, fifteen days beyond, is where the Lamp's line grounds, where "the pointing folk" are, and where the notched stones point.
Confidence: 75 for the two-things structure, 50 for the notched-stone line
Why: the blue line lands at Hush Water, not the cairn, and every culture avoids Hush Water. Gorm Longhand's gate "points at nothing," which is what you call a sightline when the target is gone or invisible. Cold Hand is a notched stone laid flat. Notches are for sighting or for seating a beam. "Pointing folk" at the landing point of the line is too neat to ignore.
Rival A (25): the notches are quarry marks or Harl ritual and the "pointing" is a folk name. Hush Water is just a bog people drown in.
Rival B (15): the line is not grounding at Hush Water; it is coming FROM there. The Lamp is being fed from the ground, and the one-color beasts turning back a day short every year are animals that know where the fence is.

### T3 revised. Gods are fed by their people's attention and lose weight when apart from them. Gods cannot give names back. The name loss at the world's edges is a separate mechanism that every god is building defenses against.
Confidence: 85 for the first sentence, 70 for the second, 65 for the third
Why: weight loss confirmed by travel. Ebb experiment failed cleanly. Four cultures' edge-walkers nameless; four gods building name registries. The Fire Hollow herdsman: the Song keeps the dead where the Ember can hear them. Oss's cairn count, Cass's cairn count short by nine in Year 19.
Rival (20): the registries are ordinary pastoral memory practice and the nameless travelers are heatstroke, cold, and privation in four flavors.

### T4 revised. Ossary is a two-period cemetery. The deep row is a tag-wearing people with blue glass and double-eyed needles, older than the Driving. The shallow row is the Driving dead or their descendants, who had mostly stopped tagging.
Confidence: 70
Why: the dig. Also the witch on the Backs, who was still tagging and knotting into my own time, which means the deep-row culture did not die; it went into hiding or dwindled to one.
Rival A (20): both rows are the lords' people, and the tag difference is rank.
Rival B (10): the shallow row is the valley's own dead and the headman's story is upside down.

### T5 revised. The Driving was a name-stripping, not just a killing. The lords' roll was scraped off the oak board and recut shallower, repeatedly. Survivors walked onto the moor "plain as sheep" and the Harl took them in. The eighth stone and the Ossary tags are the lords' script. The valley has preserved both while forbidding both.
Confidence: 70 (was 50)
Why: the board, the walked men, the eighth stone sign match, Aldo's thirteen unbroken generations, Nan's husband as a living demonstration of the condition. The hill town is NOT where the lords went; it predates them and belongs to whatever people carved Greyholt's snake.
Rival A (20): the Driving was a torch mob as told, the board was scraped for reuse, and the walked men are Harl flattery of their own hospitality.
Rival B (10): the valley folk did not strip the lords' names. Something at the moor's edge did, when they fled onto it, and the valley has been taking credit.

### T6 revised. The valley's distinctive living things are thinning for mundane reasons, and I was pattern-matching.
Confidence: 55 for mundane
Why: lambs resolved to an old woman. Grey plum resolved to grafting, probably, though "the yellow is not what it was either" is a bell I heard. Spotted eel unresolved. One-color beasts: they turn back a day short of Hush Water every year, more each year, and more of the Harl dream the kneeling each year the god has slept there. The beasts remain the thing I cannot fit.
Rival (45): the one-color beasts are made things from Hush Water, the same as the Lamp is a made thing, and the thinning of distinctive varieties is real and slow and will show in the cords before it shows in the fruit.

### T7 revised. The Gathering is a name registry. Isca holds the names of her people and their dead, and a god must present itself because the god is the registry's key. The empty place at the Hollow is both an honor and a hook.
Confidence: 70
Why: Isca's words, the herdsman's line about the dead, the refusal to accept Hob's naming of the villages. The nameless Ruun who can still sing is her proof of concept.
Rival (30): Isca is building a confederation by fear and ceremony and the "names" talk is her theology, not mechanism. Garran's reading.

### T8 revised. Past the last well is a dead city on the floor of a sea that "went away angry." Whatever is there takes names from the living. The Scald above Greyholt is the same sea's floor. The hill town stood on its shore.
Confidence: 65 (was 45)
Why: Dast, the Scald, the uncle at the steps, Ebb. Three edges of the world now behave the same way (east, north, west), which is less like a place than like a boundary condition.
Rival (35): it is one desert with one disease and the other edges are unrelated privations.

### T9 revised. The singers of Year 7 are a registry-keeping people, probably human, probably Ruun or Ruun-adjacent, and "sang the villages back" because the valley's names had slipped once before, in Hemma's childhood.
Confidence: 45
Why: the Voice does exactly what they did. Hemma's phrasing. The Year 7 singers went up toward the moor, as Haddo did.
Rival (55): a wandering order with good memories, and the phrasing is Hemma being Hemma.

### New T10. My dream at the stone is not the Low One's dream of the first men. It is my own memory. The crowd is my people before I came through, and the wide slow river with a road along it is where I was. The stone carries it because it was torn off the Lamp as I passed through.
Confidence: 50
Why: "You know the river. You have never seen it and you know it the way you know where your wound is." Brude's and Haddo's reading (the first men who hung the Lamp) requires the hangers to have knelt to it in a crowd, which is possible, but the wound language points at me. And it fell the night I came.
Rival (50): Brude is right and the dream is the Lamp's. A god would of course feel kinship with a memory of being worshipped.

### New T11. Hemma and Orla's shortening cords are the real clock. The valley's population has been falling since Year 10 and nobody but the cord-keepers is counting.
Confidence: 70
Why: short in Year 10, short in 19, short in 20, Cass's cairn short by nine, Greyholt's drought goats dying twice, the hard winters, Scarrow's split, the Fenners counting a bride as a loss. Hemma told Orla to tell me directly. That is a succession instruction about me.
Rival (30): normal fluctuation, and two old women trained to see decline.

## 3. WHICH OBSERVATIONS MATTER

### Meaningful
- The dream and the wound sentence.
- Brude: the Lamp was hung by the first men, broke when the gods came through.
- The blue line grounds at Hush Water, not the cairn. "Pointing folk." Every people avoids it; the beasts turn back a day short.
- Two notched stones: the gate that points at nothing, and Cold Hand laid flat.
- The walked men in the Harl barrows, and the Harl grudge.
- Eighth stone bears tag script. Witch's tag bears it too.
- Ossary at two depths; deep row tagged, shallow row not. Blue glass. Double-eyed needles twice, by women's hands.
- Brann Cut plate: two scripts, the second unknown to anyone. Bell stopped when removed.
- The oak board, scraped and recut and scraped.
- Capstone on the eleventh well bears Greyholt's hearth snake. The wells did not drop a hand in drought.
- The Scald. Salt of an old sea above Greyholt. Dast's sea that went away angry.
- Four cultures, four directions, one name sickness; names stored outside the body survive it. Every god now building a registry. The Lotfolk ring. The Lotfolk's names sharp on the stones.
- Isca's exact words. The empty place. "A valley's names are given by its god or not at all."
- Haddo carrying my dream east as a song. Harl humming it. Six keepers at the cairn consulting it before raids.
- The Greel wider by a boat's length; the Olm jetty two spans under. The sea left angry; the water is coming back from the north.
- Cords short three years running; said to me directly, by instruction.
- Dim nights stepped up to four or five a year and held.
- Ket stayed, and is a keeper now. My interpreter is inside the cult.

### Just the world, and welcome
- Wat taken on by the ferryman. The otters changing landlords. The miller's claim on them.
- The Lissom walk done in six days by a man who did not look at anything.
- Garran dying on the wall mid-criticism, and both halves of Scarrow keeping silence without agreeing to.
- The Dun Mere twins settled by ford and unsettled by mother.
- Greyholt's whey bowl after a raid that speared a man. This is the most durable relationship in the valley and nobody in authority is party to it.
- The Rook girl and Tollan boy living at Weir.
- The swifts to both halves of the quarry.

### Unsure
- The grey plum and the oldest woman's "the yellow is not what it was either." Probably nothing. Noted.
- The murrain across the Greel that stopped at the river. Weir says I spared them. I did not. Something did, or the river did.
- The ground shaking once in Year 19 and the Ossary bank slumping dry. Could be a world event, could be a hinge.
- The Rook child who would not run from the torches. The Driving story is losing its grip on a quarter of the valley.
- Pim's second child named with a knot in the wrist-string. Greyholt is quietly adopting the witch's practice. Or the valley always did this and I had not been told.

## 4. ACTIONS, Years 21 to 30

1. **Go to Hush Water, by way of Fallow Dip, with Ket and Brude's leave, on a route that passes Cold Hand, Gorm Longhand's gate, and any other notched stone the Harl know, and have Col pace and sight the line between them.** Driven by: Result 1 (the blue line grounds beyond the cairn; the notched stones). Tests T2 and T2 Rival B. I want to stand where the line comes down on a dim night and see whether it falls into something, out of something, or onto the pointing folk. I will stop a day short where the beasts stop and watch what they are afraid of before I take the last day. Cost: another season lighter, and the Harl may treat a second god-walk as the Low One sending me. I accept both.

2. **Go to the Fire Hollow myself, in autumn, with Orla and the box of tags, and when Isca asks me to sing the valley's names, sing them, and watch what it does to me and to the valley's cords the following spring.** Driven by: Result 5 (the empty place; a valley's names are given by its god or not at all) and Result 7 (names stored outside the body survive). Tests T3 and T7. If singing the names before the Ruun makes me heavier, the registry is a feeding mechanism and the Song is how Isca holds forty bands. If it costs me, Garran was right. Either answer is worth the trip, and I now owe it, and Haddo has my dream. I want to hear what Isca makes of it.

3. **Have the two scripts set side by side: the eighth stone, all fifteen tags, the witch's four signs, the oak board, the ledge inscriptions in the hill chamber, and the Brann Cut plate. Put Orla on it with the tag box for a year and have her count signs the way she counts knots. Then take the witch's cord bundles to the Harl at the Greyholt wall and ask if any living Harl of the walked men's blood can read a knot.** Driven by: Results 2, 4, 6 (one sign shared across tag, stone, and witch). Tests T5. If the board's shallower recuts match the tag script and the deep recut matches the second hand on the plate, the valley has two literate layers and the Driving scraped the upper one.

4. **Lever the capstone off the eleventh well at the hill town with four Greyholt men, in the dry season when the other wells are visibly full.** Driven by: Result 3 (ten wells sweet and unmoving in drought; one sealed with Greyholt's own hearth snake). Tests T8 and the question of who built the town. I want to know what Greyholt's ancestors capped and why they carried the mark down the hill but not the knowledge. If the covered well is dry, salt, bones, or a shaft, each tells me a different thing about the old sea.

5. **Pay Dast's price and go east to the dead city's steps, but not down them: go only as far as the last well, then send Dast's camel men one more day with a Lotfolk-style name stone cut for each, and have them come back, and see whether they come back with their names.** Driven by: Result 7 (Dast's testimony, the Lotfolk stones). Tests T8 directly and cheaply before I spend my own person. If the stones protect, I go on to the steps the following year with my own name cut on stone and Orla's cords left behind at Tollan as my valley's backup.

6. **Put the Scarrow split to the eighth stone: bring Aldo and Tollan's elders to the Stone Row together, tell them what the fourth sign is and where else it appears, and make the price of the hill a public reading of the Driving in which the lords walk onto the moor and forget rather than run shrieking.** Driven by: Result 4 (my inaction read as both verdicts; the board; the walked men). This is ruling, which is second, but the split is bleeding the quarry and I can trade a true story for a settled tithe. I do not make Aldo lord. I let the valley hear that the lords were made into sheep and that the Harl remember it.

7. **Keep the hide-watch, but change its task: on every dim night one hide watches the Lamp, one watches the blue line's landing bearing, and Orla tallies dim nights against the next spring's cords, against goat and lamb deaths, and against the Greel's level at Weir.** Driven by: Result 6 (the hides gave me the only clean count I have) and T11. Tests T1 Rival B and T11 together. If the dim-night count moves when I travel, I am part of the Lamp's load. If the cords shorten in step with the Greel's rise, the valley's loss is water, not names, and I have been reading the wrong clock.

## 5. BEST MOMENTS and BORED OR PUSHED

### Best moments
- "You know the river. You have never seen it and you know it the way you know where your wound is." I would read another twenty years for a line like that.
- The blue line coming down somewhere else. The world refused my tidy theory and gave me a better one.
- The walked men. Three hundred years of Harl grudge delivered in a sentence over a barrow row.
- The tag box empty of knots and the witch found anyway, because a herder kept the watch I was about to end and followed a lynx.
- Isca's speech, especially "if you drop a word of it on the road, I will know which." A god who threatens by remembering.
- Garran: "someone had to go who could not be sung at." And his report in full.
- The Brann Cut bell being the plate, and the reeds falling silent when it came up. A mystery that closed and opened at once.
- Ebb saying it was a good name and he would keep it. Worst outcome, perfectly written.
- The Lotfolk eleven, with their names sharp on the stones in their hands.
- The Rook child who would not run.

### Bored or pushed
- The four-cultures-four-edges name sickness arrived in Years 14, 17, and 20 in the same shape each time (travelers go out, come back nameless, a god builds a registry). By the Lotfolk telling I could see the next one coming. One fewer would have been louder.
- "Four dim nights" or "five dim nights" as a closing line every year became wallpaper. I understand it is the clock, but the Year 11 and Year 12 hide-reports already established the pattern; the later entries could have varied what the watchers saw.
- Isca's embassy result was long. Good, but long, and the Voice's song and the herdsman's line and Hob's question all stacked into one bullet. I would have liked the herdsman's line to arrive later from someone else.
- The hill town delivered everything at once: tiers, eleven wells, capstone, snake, grave stone, salt round. Rich, but I had nothing left to find there afterward except the one well, which is fine, but it felt like a reward room rather than a place.
- Greyholt reconciling with Pim "carefully" and the goats at the wells are good consequences, but Pim herself vanished again after saying nothing. I would have liked one line from her at the town.

## 6. MOOD

Pulled, hard, in two directions now, and that is the correct problem to have. West to Hush Water, where the line lands and nobody goes; east to the Fire Hollow, where I owe a debt and a rival god holds my dream. The block converted every one of my seven actions into an answer plus a new door, and the answers mostly contradicted my specific theories while confirming my general ones, which is the feel of a real world rather than a script agreeing with me.

Plodding: not at all in the first three years, which were dense with my own consequences. Years 14 to 18 slowed, mostly because I had used all my actions and was watching the world digest them. That is fair. The Harl cult growing at the cairn without my touching it was the best of the slow years.

Narrator pointing: the wound sentence is the narrator stepping forward, and I am glad of it. Hemma's instruction to Orla to speak to me directly is a flag I noticed and accept as a flag; it reads as the valley, not the author. The one place I felt a hand was the name-sickness series: Ruun, Skelder, Lotfolk in that order with mounting detail, each from a trader's mouth. The repetition is the theme being made unmissable. The three edges ARE the discovery, so I forgive it, but by the Lotfolk telling I knew what the next trader would say before he said it.

The thing I would now put money on without evidence: Hush Water's "pointing folk" are not folk, and the one-color beasts are theirs.
