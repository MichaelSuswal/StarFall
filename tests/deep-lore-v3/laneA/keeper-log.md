## Neutrality self-check

- The player's T2 (the fourth presence and the blue line go to the same place) is wrong in its second half. I was tempted to soften the dim night at Fallow Dip, or to leave the thread's landing place vague, so the player would keep two live readings. I did neither: the thread landed where it lands, over Hush Water, fifteen days on, and Ket named the place because a Harl guide standing next to a god on a dim night would. The player's T2 first half (the pull is at Fallow Dip) is right and was shown as the sensation it is. Facts fell.
- T4 (Ossary is a disaster deposit) is wrong. I was tempted to give the cut one ambiguous feature (a burnt layer, a wall stub) to keep the theory breathing. There is none in the deep history, so there is none in the ground. Rows, tags, beads, needle, no ash. Costs the player a season of labor and teaches that the bones are a cemetery, which is what the action was for.
- T5 (the Driving is partly false) is right, and the world's fixed schedule happened to hand the player the crypt in Year 12 through the Rooks' own dig, plus the walked men through Ket. I checked whether I was rewarding a good guess: the Year 12 dig fires for any player who does nothing about the Rooks, and the walked men story is a placed Harl clue the god reached by spending three weeks on the moor with a Harl talker. Neither was moved. I did not have the board read for the player, and Aldo's reading of it is wrong, as a mortal's must be.
- T6 Rival B (the lambs tie to Fallow Dip) is wrong; the lambs were Marrit. The watch found her body because a standing watch over every fold on the Backs for years would. I considered leaving the hut unfound to spare the player an answer they did not ask for; that would have been holding back a placed body. I also considered having a herder read the knots as magic; no herder would. Col's wrong cause stands.
- T1 (dim nights increasing) rested on a sampling artifact in Block 1's selective mentions. Once the player set herders to watch every dim night, the honest count is four or five a year, and I reported the real numbers. This corrects nothing by narration; it is what the action measures.
- Ebb: the player hoped names given by a god would restore a man. I was tempted to give the god a flicker of sensation to reward a clever experiment. The deep lore gives a god no such power and the Handfolk rule says a given name holds only while given. Nothing moved. Greyholt's hope is a mortal's and is wrong.
- The Isca refusal: I gave her the full courtly speech because that is her voice and the envoys would carry it back whole. I did not have her hint at names or storage. Hob's question about the names was Hob's; her answer is her sheet (names come from a god in person).
- The Vell search: I made the path cost twelve days rather than letting Pim walk straight to it, because the fixed cost says the path was forgotten in Year 8. I did not make it fail outright, because Vell is findable by anyone searching the right hills with hunters, and rule 8 forbids hiding it from a god who goes. The sealed cistern stayed sealed because three people cannot lift it; the player can bring four.
- The Brann Cut: I let the Fenners hook the plate up on the god's word rather than stopping at the sounding, because the god, the Fenners and the hook were all in the boat and the intent was plain. I did not supply the reading of "Aud"; that needs a caravaner shown the plate.
- Haddo's route through the valley is my ruling on an unspecified route, taken because the valley sits on the only named road from the Reed Crossing to the Harl moor. It gives the player two sightings of Isca's Voice and, in Year 18, the god's own dream in a mortal's song. I checked that this is the world acting, not the Keeper feeding: Haddo goes west in Year 14 and returns in Year 18 for every lane, and a band of thirty Ruun riders does not cross the Olm valley unseen.
- Tone: I cut two places where I had lingered on the capstone pattern and on the thread. The well test: the three things a bored farmer would repeat from Year 12 are the Rook dig and its gold, Hemma's death, and the heavy flood. The thread's landing place is in the god's own item, not the farmer's. Acceptable.
