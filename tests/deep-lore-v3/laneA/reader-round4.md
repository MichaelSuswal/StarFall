# Reader Round 4. Years 31 to 40, and the whole game. Lane A, investigator style

## 1. RESULTS

### Action 1. Answer the Lotfolk letter in kind.
Answered in full and the world paid back more than I sent. Pell's letter gave me the Coin's height at three intervals, each drop larger than the last: the Lamp is sinking and accelerating. Then forty families' names with their fates, written as data: every child born on the shore points, fourteen walked into the water. Then the exchange of children, and Tess went south and became the best instrument I have. By Year 39 I hold the whole series from Pell's second year, beside the ell and the torc. The only god in the world who measured instead of sang is now gone into the Coin, and the Tally House sells copies of the pins for iron. The measurement outlived the measurer. This was the right action and the best-paying one of the forty years.

### Action 2. Set pins above the Scald and in the gate notch.
Answered honestly and slowly. Seven years: both Scald pins and the gate mark show the Lamp lower by the same hair. The mason says scree and ridge settled the same amount and grants it is a coincidence and wants another seven years. Three sightlines at three heights on two hills agreeing settles T1 Rival B (ground rising) for me, though not for the mason, and I like that he will not be hurried. The gate was the first instrument and the last check.

### Action 3. Go to Isca with the truth about the stone, and sleep beside it.
Answered, and I lost the stone. Isca's logic was exact: a dead god's grave cannot be among the Ruun unsung, a living god's memory cannot be where she cannot sing it. Mine if I carried it out by the thaw; otherwise into the Greel where the Voice died. The dream was the same to the last stone of the riverbank, with Caddo as witness. I did not carry it out. I had no way to carry a stone heavier than it should be across the Tallgrass with a herald and a knot-keeper, and I told myself that; the truer reason is that I was afraid of what carrying it would do to my weight, and I had Hush Water's offer still unanswered. She drowned it at the Reed Crossing and the camels will not stand still over it. T10 is now unfalsifiable: the dream did not change with the keepers dead and the Ruun around it, which favors the memory being fixed in the stone and not in me, but I will never dream it again to check. The one thing she said that I keep: "signs are for people who mean to forget." She was wrong, and Tess proved it, but it is the clearest statement of the Song's theology I have.

### Action 4. Answer the Skelder hunter and go north to the Mother.
Fully answered and the strangest four nights of the game. The Mother is the hounds: one voice from every throat, naming every person at the fire, and the people answer to the names she gives them. The twenty-eight nameless are fed first and smile and say she will say it again tomorrow. She never named me and never turned toward me. So a god that has seen the edge, lost twenty-eight names to it, and solved the problem by becoming the register herself, nightly, in every mouth she has. The hunter stayed. Then in Year 35 she took fourteen hundred north past the end of the land "to find a country with a high star," and in Year 38 nine hounds and twelve people came back, those twelve with their names because they sang them every night of the walk. The Mother is not in the wood. The hounds there are silent. She was offered the thing I was offered; the Lotfolk god took it in two installments and went up into the Coin as pale lights; Isca was offered it and declined "as a throne declines." The Mother went to look for a high star, which I read as another Lamp, and did not come back. Four gods, four answers. T12 confirmed in the only way it could be: by watching three of them choose.

### Action 5. Greyholt's hill colony and the word count.
Clean and the result I did not want. Eleven old Greyholt words: used by everyone on the hill including the Lissom girl, used by no one under forty in the valley. The hill knot is a knot and a half. The first child in six hundred years, Greyholt says, which it cannot know. By Year 39 the colony is nineteen and Pim's eldest has moved up and Greyholt calls it "up the hill" instead of unlucky. So T6 Rival (erosion is a function of place) wins over marrying-down: the same people keep their words at the old name store and lose them in the valley. The hill town was built by people who knew that. I knew it was a test and I did not expect it to pass.

### Action 6. Drag the Olm mouth for the second script.
Clear negative and useful. It is a jetty: dressed stone, four iron rings, Greelman anchor stone, no marks. The Greelmen say their grandfathers' grandfathers built it when the Greel was a boat's width narrower and a man's height lower, and they know because the ring I could reach was the top ring. So the fen plate's script has no second text and probably came off the "flat boat lost in the fen," a trade object from somewhere downriver. The water is rising a man's height in five generations and that is now measured too, by a mooring ring.

### Action 7. Stay awake at the Stone Row every dim night.
Ten years, forty-five dim nights, nothing. Nothing came awake, nothing asked. Meanwhile Hal and a Greyholt woman sleeping on Hagg Ridge near the gate walked into the tarn in their sleep and came out as Ebb is. The offer reaches sleepers, not watchers. The lamp-watch is exactly what I said it was last round without evidence: the valley staying awake on the nights the question comes, kept by people who have forgotten what they are refusing. By Year 37 the valley brings me bread. Orla's last line: "that this was perhaps the point of it, and that she did not know, and that no one did." I think she is right and I think not knowing is part of how it works, because a people who knew would have a king who said yes.

## 2. FINAL THEORIES

### T1. The Lamp is a made thing, the door the gods came through, broken by the crossing, sinking and accelerating.
Confidence: 92
Why: Harl lore, Pell's three intervals each larger than the last, Tess's yearly readings "lower again by a little more," three pins on two hills in the valley agreeing, the gate pointing at a hand of empty sky in Year 40 exactly as it did in Year 21.
Rival A (6): natural body in a decaying orbit, with the first-men story a story. Functionally identical for anything I can do about it.
Rival B (2): the ground is rising. The mason wants seven more years.

### T2. Hush Water is where the Lamp's line grounds and where it takes names, from children first, and the faceless row that counts is the Lamp's side of the bargain. The gate on Hagg Ridge is near enough to the line that sleepers there fall into the same water.
Confidence: 80
Why: direct observation; Pell's forty families; Hal and the bracken woman at the tarn below the gate; the shore people walking in. The tarn is the valley's small Hush Water: still water near the sightline.
Rival A (15): Hush Water is a separate thing that the line happens to fall on, and the tarn sleepwalkers are a Harl curse or coincidence of a cold tarn and tired men.
Rival B (5): the faceless row are the other gods, or what gods become, and the Lamp is only the wire.

### T3. Gods are fed by attention in the moment and lose weight apart from their people. Gods cannot return names. Weight is the price the Lamp asks.
Confidence: 90
Why: forty years of my own weight; the Hollow; Pell's "shadow is pale now" after two payments, then pale lights going up into the Coin; the Mother's hounds feeding and naming rather than being fed.
Rival (10): the weight is morale and the offers were dreams gods happened to share.

### T4. Tags, hill chamber, witch's cords, wrist-string, and the Ossary deep row are one culture's name storage decaying over three hundred years into vestige. Greyholt is that culture's heir and does not know it.
Confidence: 85
Why: Orla's census, the capstone snake, the hill colony keeping its words and its knot-and-a-half.
Rival (15): rank markers and a debt tally and a cemetery, and the hill colony keeps its words because old people are strict.

### T5. The Driving stripped the lords' names by the King Without's working, and the lords walked onto the moor as sheep. The valley preserves the tools and forbids them.
Confidence: 60
Why: the board, the walked men, the eighth stone's formula line, the aunt's story, Tollan's refusal and Tollan's torc by the ell.
Rival A (30): the lords fled onto the moor and the moor took their names near the line, as it took Hal's, and the valley has been taking credit for three hundred years. This rival has grown: Hagg Ridge is on the moor road, and it takes names from sleepers.
Rival B (10): inversion; the lords were the tag people.

### T6. Distinctiveness erodes as a function of place and of not being sung. What is kept at a name store or in a nightly song survives; what is left to ordinary memory goes without anyone noticing when it stopped.
Confidence: 70 (was 40 for mundane in the other direction)
Why: the hill word count; the Lissom pools kept by Caddo's song and declared "always the custom"; the spotted eel's string forgotten; the grey plum gone and the yellow going; Greyholt's "thee"; the one-color beasts still unexplained and I now leave them unexplained.
Rival (30): plums, eels and dialects go in every world, and the hill colony is a small sample run by stubborn old women.

### T7. Isca's Song is her body. What is sung stays and what is not goes, and she has now concluded this openly and is singing the whole world's dead into herself. She declined the Lamp's offer because a throne does not take gifts.
Confidence: 85
Why: everything, plus Year 37's singers for the dead and "the Song cannot be sent for twice." She has become the thing the hill town was, at the scale of a continent, by fire.
Rival (15): warlord with a theology, and the declining was pride, not understanding.

### T8. The world's edges, Hush Water, and the Ridge tarn are places where the Lamp reaches the ground, and names fail there. Stones preserve names but do not protect their bearers. Nightly singing protects, because the twelve Skelder who sang every night came back whole.
Confidence: 75
Why: Lotfolk eleven versus Skelder twelve is the cleanest controlled comparison the world gave me: stones no, song yes.
Rival (25): the twelve survived because they turned back sooner.

### T9. The forty singers who come every generation are a registry people older than the Song, keeping an order that is not Isca's, and two of their names are in the Song because she took them from such singers in her eighth year.
Confidence: 70 (was 50)
Why: they came again in Year 39 and sang my new places too (the Crown Fold, the Brann Cut). Caddo checked their order against the Song. They asked me nothing. They predate Hemma, Orla, and probably the Driving.
Rival (30): a bardic circuit with a very long memory.

### T10. The stone's dream was a god's memory of its people at a river, torn off the Lamp as the gods came through. Whose god, I will never know. I believe it was mine.
Confidence: 50, and no longer testable
Rival (50): the first men's worship of the Lamp, as Brude said.

### T11. The cords measure blows. The valley is a tenth smaller than when I came and the cords do not say why.
Confidence: 90 for Orla's reading, which is the only honest one.

### T12. Names are what people have that gods and the Lamp consume; weight is what gods have that the Lamp consumes. Every registry in the world (tags, knots, Song, hounds, ring, cords, lamp-watch) is a way of holding names where the Lamp cannot reach them, and every god that has been offered the Lamp's bargain has either refused (Isca, the Olm by sleeping through it and then by staying awake), taken it and gone up as light (Pell), or gone looking for a better Lamp (the Mother).
Confidence: 70 (was 55)
Why: three gods' choices observed, with outcomes. Pell's temple stones softening like tallow and the spring running dry as the god bound itself: a god that gave its weight to a place and then to the Coin.
Rival (30): I have built one theory out of five funerary customs and three gods' misfortunes, and the simpler reading is that the world is hard and gods are not wise.

## 3. WHICH OBSERVATIONS MATTER

### Meaningful
- Pell's three intervals, each larger. Tess's "lower again by a little more than last." Three valley pins agreeing after seven years.
- The Mother is the hounds: one voice from every throat, naming; never turned toward me.
- The twenty-eight fed first, smiling, "the Mother would say it again tomorrow."
- Fourteen hundred north for a high star; nine hounds and twelve people back, the twelve with their names because they sang nightly. The Lotfolk eleven with names on stones and not in them. Song protects, stone preserves.
- Pell's dream record: two offers taken, shadow pale, prayers as echoes, then pale lights up the chimney into the Coin. The spring dry. The temple stones soft as tallow. The city describes a child and stops.
- Isca singing the Long Song at the sky in a rage on a dim night and declining "as a throne declines."
- Hal and the bracken woman walking into the tarn below the gate, asleep, past a watcher who was watching the sky. The ridge forbidden for sleeping.
- Nothing came to me awake in ten years. The valley bringing bread.
- The hill colony: eleven words kept, a knot and a half, nineteen people.
- "Signs are for people who mean to forget." And Tess, who proved otherwise.
- Isca's Year 37 conclusion: what is sung stays. Singers for the dead. "The Song cannot be sent for twice."
- The Year 39 singers again, with my new place names and an order that is not the Song's.
- The Greel a man's height higher in five generations, by the mooring ring.
- The stone drowned where the Voice died, and camels will not stand over it.
- The Harl bowl back on the wall in Year 33, and a raid plus a bowl in Year 38, "how it used to be and is a relief."

### Just the world
- Wat as ferryman taking stories for fare, and Weir predicting ruin.
- The Lissom pair singing the pools as they passed them.
- The Rook who holds the hill letting the hut fall in and wearing the torc only at the count.
- Ossary's long walk to the landing.
- The mason insisting on seven more years.
- The grandson who cut the grey trees now old and blaming the weather, which is what Berit's grandson said of Berit.
- The Greyholt child saying "thee" to Hob and the meadow laughing and Hob not.

### Unsure
- The one-color beasts. Forty years, still crossing, still turning back a day short, no Harl to see them, now seen by burners. I never fit them and I am leaving them unfit. I think they are the water's, and I have no evidence.
- The Lotfolk counter at Tollan who began counting hearths "and would not say why" the week the letter went south. Pell was already counting my valley for the lot before it went into the Coin. Whether the Tally House still is, I do not know.
- Caddo teaching the Long Song to sixty Tollan children after the elders asked him not to, "because it is sung." My valley's children now sing a foreign god's body every night. Protective or a hook. Probably both.

## 4. WHAT I WOULD DO NEXT, Years 41 to 50

1. **Send Orla's best knot-pupil and two Greyholt hill people to Ennow with Tess's copy of the pins, to learn to cut the forty signs onto lead and to read what the Tally House has of Pell's dream record in full, and bring back the Coin's height yearly.** The measurer is gone; the measure must not be. Tests T1 (is the acceleration steady, and does the rate change now that Pell is in the Coin).

2. **Go to Hagg Ridge myself and sleep inside Gorm Longhand's fold, alone, with Col's son in a hide a mile back and Caddo's singers told to sing my valley's names all that night at the Stone Row.** The two tarn sleepers were unsung. I have been offered the bargain once and woke before answering. I want to be asked again, in a place I can measure, while my names are being held elsewhere, and I want to know whether the row counts me or passes over me as the Mother did. Tests T2, T8 and T12 at once. This is the action my style demands and the one my people would forbid if they knew.

3. **Ask the twelve Skelder who came back what they sang and how, every night of the walk, and bring two of them to the Stone Row to teach Caddo's sixty the Skelder way beside the Ruun way, and write both in Orla's knots.** Tests T8 directly: if nightly self-naming is what saved the twelve, the valley should hold its names in its own mouths, not only in Isca's.

4. **Go to the Reed Crossing on a dim night and sit at the ford over the drowned stone, awake, with the caravans stopped for the night, and see whether anyone there dreams the river, and whether the camels still will not stand.** The stone is the one object in forty years that the Lamp did not take and Isca could not break, and it is in a ford that every salt caravan crosses. I want to know if it is still dreaming under the water. Tests T10's only remaining edge.

5. **Walk the moor road from the gate to Cold Hand with the mason and Aske and look for every other notched stone, standing or fallen, and put a pin in each, so the whole old sightline is an instrument again.** Someone built a line of Lamp-sights across the moor before the Harl. If the Lamp was hung, it was hung from somewhere, and the line should end where it was hung or where it was meant to fall. Tests T2 and the geometry of the whole thing.

6. **Tell the valley at the count what the lamp-watch is for, in two sentences, after the Driving, the way Weir's elder tells the moor's telling: that the Lamp asks a question of sleepers on those nights, and the valley has answered no by staying awake for three hundred and fifty years without knowing it.** This is ruling and it is also the one experiment I have not run: does the watch still work if the people know why. Orla thinks not knowing is the point. I think she may be right, and I will ask her first, and if she says no I will not.

7. **Visit the hill colony every midsummer with Orla and take the Ossary tag box and the lead sheets up there to stay, in the eleventh chamber with its two hundred coils, and have the old women there tag a cord for each living person in Greyholt.** The hill is the one place in the valley where names are measurably not eroding. The archive should be where the archive works. Tests T4 and T6 by making the vestige a practice again and counting what happens to the valley's Greyholt words over ten years.

## 5. BEST MOMENTS and BORED OR PUSHED, Years 31 to 40

### Best
- One voice from every hound's throat, naming forty-one people, and never turning toward me.
- "The Mother would say it again tomorrow."
- Pell's dream record: a god that took the offer "because the lot had never been offered a choice before and Pell is curious." Hob: because it is a child.
- Pale lights going up from the Bowl like moths up a chimney, and Tess not knowing who she is sending the number to.
- "Signs are for people who mean to forget."
- Isca singing the whole Long Song at the sky as fire, in a rage, and declining as a throne declines.
- The mason granting the coincidence and asking for seven more years.
- The Greelman ring you can reach being the top ring.
- Nine hounds and twelve Skelder out of fourteen hundred, with their names because they sang.
- The valley bringing me bread on dim nights.
- Orla at the end: "perhaps the point of it."

### Bored or pushed
- Tess's letters were the best device of the decade and also the most mechanical: each one delivered a Coin reading plus one escalation of Pell's dissolution. By Year 36 I knew the sheet's shape before it was read.
- "Four dim nights. Awake. Nothing." ten times. I set this up myself and the world was right to make me sit through it, but the line could have varied what the hides saw or who brought the bread.
- Isca's Year 37 conclusion ("what is sung stays and what is not sung goes") stated her theology outright after thirty years of showing it. I had it already; the children at the Stone Row had it. The line was for me and I felt it.
- The Mother's departure and the return of twelve was reported through traders twice over two years. I wanted to have been there, and my own Year 31 trip put me there and then I came home. That is on me.
- Hal and the bracken woman were the one truly new mechanic of the decade (the line takes sleepers near the sightline) and both arrived as one-bullet accidents with the burners blaming the Harl. Good restraint, but a third would have made the pattern undeniable and I was left at two.

## 6. OVERALL, forty years

### Discovering or being led
Discovering, with the lead showing at the hinges. The first block planted everything and explained nothing, and I found the north-west convergence on a second read, which is the feel I want. Every block after that answered my actions with results that contradicted my specific theories and confirmed my general ones, which is how a real world behaves. The places I felt a hand: the Isca invitation in Year 10, the three-cultures name-sickness series in Years 14 to 20, the offer at Hush Water, Pell's letter arriving the year I found the first instrument, Isca stating her theology in Year 37. Each of those was a hinge the block needed and I accept them as such. None of them told me what to think; they told me where to look.

### Too subtle, too obvious, or right
Right, erring toward obvious in the thematic repetitions and toward subtle in the mechanics. The name-sickness and registry theme was underlined three or four times more than it needed. The mechanics (weight, sleepers near the line, song protects while stone preserves, the Lamp sinking) were each delivered once or twice and left for me to connect, and that is exactly correct. The one-color beasts were the right amount of unexplained. The eighth stone's line, which Orla would not read, was the right amount of unread.

### Did focus and cleverness pay, and did the world answer wrong actions
Yes on both, and the second more than the first. My best results came from systematic, boring moves: hides, a box, a sign census, pins, a letter answered in kind, a word count. My cleverest theory (one thing in the north-west) was wrong and the world corrected it with a better structure. My wrongest action (summoning Aldo and comparing the tag to the stone) cost a man his life, split a village for fourteen years, and gave me the board and the eighth stone's script, and the world kept the cost running for the rest of the game. My most cowardly action (not carrying the stone out by the thaw) cost me the stone permanently and the world did not soften it. The Ebb experiment failed cleanly and the failure was more informative than success would have been. The world never punished me for being wrong; it charged me for it and gave me the receipt.

### Did the other gods feel like real beings
Isca, completely. She has one principle, applies it to everything including the man she loved making, and her reasoning about the stone in Year 31 is the most coherent thing any character said in forty years. Her escalation was fast but every step followed from the one before. The Mother of Dogs is a different kind of real: never speaks, never turns toward me, solves the problem of forgetting by becoming the remembering, and then goes looking for a better sky and does not come back. Pell is the one I liked most and knew least: a child who measures, takes a gift because it was curious, writes down its own dissolution, and goes up as moths. The fourth presence turned out not to be a being, and that was the right answer. I never met anyone's god face to face but Isca, and that also felt right; gods in this world are mostly known by what their people do at night.

### What I enjoyed that was just the world
The miller and the ferryman and the cow. Wat, from four rescues to ferryman who takes stories. The Lissom walks tied at the wrist, which turned out to be the one custom that absorbed every change (a Lotfolk counter, two Ruun singers, a Rook and a Tollan boy). Garran telling his nephew what he was doing wrong until he died doing it. Greyholt's whey bowl surviving raids, a god's cavalry, and seven years of absence. The Dun Mere twins. The mason. Hob. Orla's refusals. These were the ballast of the game and they also did quiet structural work: the wrist-walk and the bowl showed me what a durable relationship looks like in a valley that loses a tenth of itself, and the wrist-string on the babies turned out to be a vestige of the whole plot. The weather entries were the only pure padding and they faded in the later blocks.

### Five best moments of the whole game
1. "You know the river. You have never seen it and you know it the way you know where your wound is." Year 11.
2. Lying behind the gate and finding the notch frames sky a hand above the Lamp. Year 21.
3. The old man by the fire: "the water did not want them," as a man says the wind is from the east. Year 22.
4. One voice from every hound, naming everyone but me. Year 31.
5. Ebb saying it was a good name and he would keep it. Year 11.

### Worst moment
Not carrying the stone out of the Tallgrass by the thaw, and knowing as I read it that the world had given me the choice plainly and I had chosen comfort. Second: Ket dying at the cairn with the five keepers because I slept there once. The world let me make a cult by investigating and then let a stronger god burn it to glass, and never said it was my fault, which is worse.

### Confusing or unmotivated
- The fen plate. A second script with one object and no second text, which the Greelmen then attributed to a lost boat. Either it is a seed for a later game or it was a red herring that cost me a dig. I do not mind, but it is the one thread that went nowhere.
- The one-color beasts. Forty years, never explained, never connected to anything but a bearing. I am content to leave them, but if they were meant to matter, they needed one more fact.
- Why Hush Water takes children first and the shore adults over generations, while the tarn below the gate takes a sleeping adult in one night. Possibly distance from the line, possibly a rule I was not given. I would have liked one more case.
- The Year 7 and Year 39 singers. Clearly important, twice, and completely opaque. That may be the point, but two visits forty years apart with no handle is the most "wait for the sequel" the game ever felt.
- Isca's "forty horses" twice. A small thing. Gifts that large from a god that precise should mean something specific and I never learned what.
- The lamp-watch's purpose is my strongest unverified theory and the game ended with Orla saying "perhaps." I would have liked one dim night where a valley hearth did not sit up and something happened to the sleeper, so that the watch was shown to work rather than inferred to.
