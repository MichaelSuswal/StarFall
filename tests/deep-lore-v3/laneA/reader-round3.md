# Reader Round 3. Years 21 to 30. Lane A, investigator style

## 1. RESULTS

### Action 1. Hush Water, by way of the notched stones.
The clearest answer of the game so far, and it answered a question I had not asked.
- The notched stones: Gorm Longhand's gate and Cold Hand have the same span and the same hand-square notch. The gate's notch frames empty sky a hand's breadth above the Lamp. Cold Hand, if it stood, would sight toward the Lamp. So these are Lamp-sighting instruments, and the Lamp has moved: a hand's breadth lower than when the gate was built, or the gate sights where the Lamp used to be. The Lotfolk later gave me the second half of this: the Coin (their name for the Lamp) is lower by a pin's eye in thirteen years as measured from Ennow's cliff. The Lamp is sinking. T2's notched-stone line (50) is confirmed as a sightline but the thing being pointed at is the Lamp's old position, not Hush Water. "The gate that points at nothing" is exactly right; what it pointed at has drifted.
- The day-short boundary where the beasts turn back: nothing. Same grass, same ground, a lark's nest in the twelfth month. So the beasts' limit is in the beasts, not the ground. T2 Rival B (a fence) is dead.
- Hush Water itself: flat, no ripple in wind, no ice at the margin, warmer at the feet than the chest. The Lamp stands directly overhead at midnight so a man has no shadow. The pointing folk are human and have words for everything except each other. Their grandparents had names; "the water did not want them." The Lotfolk colony's children point and the parents have stopped correcting. The dim-night line falls on the water two days out and the water takes it. T2 restated: Hush Water is where the Lamp's line grounds, yes, and the thing it does there is take names. Not from travelers at the edge; from the people who live there, one generation at a time, and from their children first.
- The dream at the shore: faceless tall figures in a row, counting; houses on poles over shallow water that is not this water. On the dim night the figures offered me a trade: to be heard at every hearth in my valley at once, as the Lamp is seen at every hearth, for "weight." I woke before answering. This is the first time anything in the world has spoken to me as a god. The offer is the Lamp's offer, or the offer of whatever stands behind the Lamp, and the price is the one currency I know I have. T10 (the dream is mine) was wrong about this dream; the kneeling-river dream is the stone's, the counting-row dream is the water's, and they are different.
- Cost: lighter than I have ever been. First spring festival held without me. Col will not go back up the Backs. Ket is a keeper, and in Year 26 Ket is dead because of a cult that exists because I slept at the cairn.

### Action 2. Go to the Fire Hollow and sing the names.
Clear. I sang every name as the cords keep them, eight thousand Ruun sang them back, and I was heavier while they sang, a little, as at a harvest festival, and it was gone by the Reed Crossing. So T3 and T7 both hold: the Song is a feeding mechanism, but it feeds whoever is being sung at, and only while the singing lasts. Isca holds forty bands because the Song is sung every night; she is fed every night. The cords did not shorten that spring. No harm to the valley from being in the Song.
Isca asked me what the stone is. She does not wait well. Whatever I said, she gave me forty horses and a herald and declared the fires one, and then in Year 26 rode two thousand Ruun over my valley to break the stone, failed, carried it off, and asked me in Year 28 whether I have found what it is for. So the Hollow visit converted a rival into an ally into an occupying force in four years. The register is real: Caddo sings the valley's names every dusk and forty Tollan children now do it with him, and Orla has started knotting the order so it is not only in a Ruun mouth. I introduced a second name store into my valley by going.

### Action 3. Orla's year on the signs; the witch's cords to the Harl.
Clear and rigorous. Orla sorted everything by shape as she sorts knots. Result: one script across tags, eighth stone, oak board (all three cuttings), hill chamber ledges, witch's tag, hill-town tags and lead sheets; about forty shapes. The fen plate is a different script with no shape in common. On the lead sheets every row begins with a tag-group and ends with one of a handful of recurring signs; the eighth stone's eight-sign line has three row-ending signs and no tag-group. So the stone is not a name. It is a formula, or a record line with the name left off, or a rule. Orla refuses to guess what it says and I trust her more for it.
The witch's knots: no Harl ties them. Ket's aunt: they are the valley's baby wrist-string knots "grown up." The valley's old king stole them from the men who hung the Low One, tied them to take the cold out of a river, the Low One took his name as payment, he is the King Without, and the Scald is where the cold went. So the knots are not a record system; they are a working. The witch was tying workings at lamb folds. The baby wrist-string is the vestige. T5 revised: the Driving's name-stripping may be older than the Driving. The King Without lost his name to the Lamp before the walked men ever climbed the moor.

### Action 4. Lever the capstone.
Clear, theory wrong in the useful direction. Not water, not salt, not a shaft. A dry chamber with shelves cut in rings, and on them two hundred heaps of grey dust the shape of coiled rope, each with a fired clay tag, and eleven lead sheets of script. The Greyholt men say a debt tally sealed so no one could collect. I say an archive: two hundred coiled things that were once cords or bodies or both, each tagged, and the master rows on lead. The hill town was a name store. The deep row at Ossary with tags on every third neck and the witch with dozens of knotted cords are the same practice at different scales. T8's question (who built the town) is answered sideways: the tag people built it, the same script, and Greyholt's snake on the capstone means Greyholt's ancestors sealed it. Greyholt is the tag people. Greyholt is losing "thee" in Year 30 and did not notice when it stopped.

### Action 5. The last well, and the name stones.
Clean and inconclusive in a way that teaches. Four camel men went one day out with name stones and came back whole. Dast now believes in stones for life. But one day is not ten, and the Lotfolk letter in Year 25 says exactly what I feared: eleven of two hundred came back, the names were on the stones, and the names were not in the eleven. So stones do not protect; they only preserve. The name sickness is not a function of distance walked; it is a function of where you are, and the steps are a where. I did not go to the steps. Correct, and I will not pretend it was anything but caution. From the flats the blue line falls west-north-west across the whole sky, which triangulates with the valley's north-west bearing to put the landing exactly where I stood at Hush Water. Two bearings, one point. Good.

### Action 6. The eighth stone and the Scarrow split.
Half worked, which is the honest result of trying to rule by truth. Tollan would not change the telling. Weir's elder told both and said see which the children remember. Aldo got the hill, Tollan got the tenth, the Rook half stood on the stone. The Rook children mock the Driving by walking off slowly with heads down. By Year 26 the flood forced both halves onto one quarry face; by Year 30 Aldo is dead and his son sent the second torc to Tollan without a word and Tollan hung it beside the ell "and does not know why it did." The valley does not need to agree on a story to act like it has one. I learned that the official telling is unbreakable by evidence and breakable by children.

### Action 7. The hides repurposed; Orla's cords as instruments.
Orla answered my question better than I asked it. Dim nights: four or five a year for seventeen years "whether you were in the valley or not." So T1 Rival B (I am part of the Lamp's load) is dead. The step change at Year 11 was not me traveling. Households: short in storm, flood, raid years, level between, never recovered. Stock as the winters. Olm at the weir level; Olm mouth under the Greel deeper. Her conclusion, which I accept: the valley loses people when the world hits it and does not get them back. T11 downgraded from a mystery to arithmetic, and Hob is right that two old women made a prophecy out of it. The real clock is not the cords. It is the Coin, lower by a pin's eye in thirteen years.

## 2. THEORIES, updated

### T1. The Lamp (the Low One, the Coin) is a made thing hung by the first men, broken when we came through, and sinking.
Confidence: 85 (was 80)
Why: Brude's lore plus two independent measurements of descent (the notched gate framing sky a hand above the Lamp; the Lotfolk pins at Ennow's cliff). The dimming plateau at four or five a year is independent of me. The Lamp's line grounds at Hush Water, directly overhead at midnight, and that is the one place on the map where names fail without anyone traveling.
Rival A (10): natural body on a long orbit, drifting, with an irregular dimming. The Harl story is a story and the Lotfolk measure is a measure of the same drift.
Rival B (5): the Lamp is not sinking; the ground is rising, or the sightlines were never meant for the Lamp.

### T2 restated. Hush Water is the Lamp's anchor point and its intake. The Lamp draws names there. The faceless counting row is the Lamp's keepers or the Lamp itself.
Confidence: 70
Why: direct observation of the line falling on the water. The pointing folk, nameless for three generations, and the Lotfolk children going nameless in ten years. The dream of counting. The offer of "every hearth at once" for weight, which is the Lamp describing itself as a trade: it is seen at every hearth, and it was paid for in weight. "The first men hung it up against the wolves" and the King Without paid his name for a working. The Lamp was always a bargain.
Rival A (20): Hush Water is a place where something else lives, and the Lamp's line falling there is coincidence of geometry (it falls where the Lamp is overhead, which is just the Lamp's zenith).
Rival B (10): the faceless row are the three other gods or something like us, bargaining through the Lamp, and the Lamp is only the wire.

### T3 restated. Gods gain weight when their people attend them in the moment, lose it when apart, cannot give names, and are themselves what the Lamp wants.
Confidence: 85
Why: everything before plus the Hollow (heavier while sung at, gone by the Crossing) and the dream's explicit price. The Skelder god went north with forty and came back with twenty-eight nameless and "says nothing of it." A god that went to look and lost people. The hunter's question, "Does your god remember the sky?", is a question about whether I have lost something too.
Rival (15): the weight is just morale and the offer was a dream.

### T4 restated. Ossary, the hill town chamber, the witch's hut, and the baby wrist-string are one culture's name storage at four scales, decaying over time. The deep row tagged, the shallow row not, the hill chamber sealed, the witch alone, the wrist-string unexplained.
Confidence: 80 (was 70)
Why: Orla's sign census makes it one script; the capstone makes Greyholt the heirs; the aunt's story makes the knots a working.
Rival A (15): rank markers in a lordly cemetery, and the chamber is a treasury tally.
Rival B (5): the tags are grave goods only, and the script match is because there was only ever one script.

### T5 restated. The Driving was the valley stripping the lords' names, by the same working the King Without used, and the lords walked onto the moor as sheep. The valley preserves the stone, the tags, the wrist-knot and the lamp-watch because it cannot afford to forget the tools, and forbids them because it cannot afford to use them.
Confidence: 65 (was 70; the aunt's story moves the first name-taking earlier and makes the valley a user, not an inventor)
Why: the board recut three times shallower; the walked men; the stone line with no name-group in it; the aunt's King Without; Tollan's refusal to alter the telling; Tollan hanging the torc beside the ell without knowing why.
Rival A (25): the Driving was violence as told, and the names were lost on the moor to the Lamp the way names are lost at Hush Water, with no one in the valley doing anything.
Rival B (10): the lords were the tag people and the valley folk are the walked men's cousins who stayed, which inverts everything.

### T6. The distinctive living things are thinning for mundane reasons.
Confidence: 60 for mundane
Why: lambs explained. Plums explained. Spotted eel gone fifteen years with the pike still blamed; Dun Mere stopped keeping a string for it. The one-color beasts still unexplained, and now they cross with no Harl to see them, going to Hush Water and turning back at a boundary that is in them, not the ground. Greyholt losing "thee" and Lissom deciding Caddo's pool-song "was always the custom" are the same shape: distinctiveness going without anyone noticing when it stopped.
Rival (40): the beasts are the water's, made the way the pointing folk are made, and the valley's small losses are the same erosion at a slower rate.

### T7 restated. The Gathering is a nightly feeding. Isca forgets nothing because the Song is her body, and she will burn anything that makes a second song. She now wants the stone's purpose from me and will come whether called or not.
Confidence: 80
Why: the Hollow; the Voice burned with two hundred for lighting a second fire; "nothing that is mine refuses me"; two thousand horse through my valley twice.
Rival (20): Isca is a warlord with a theology and the feeding is incidental.

### T8 restated. East, north and west edges of the world are one boundary condition where names fail. Hush Water is a hole in the middle of the map that behaves like the edge. The dead city's steps and the hill town and Hush Water's pole-houses are three name-storing settlements from the sea's time.
Confidence: 60
Why: Lotfolk eleven; Skelder twenty-eight; Ebb; the Ruun east bands. The dream at the shore of houses on poles above shallow water "that is not this water," and the Fenners rebuilding on stilts "remembering it was a lake." The Olm mouth deepening every decade: the sea is coming back from the south.
Rival (40): three separate hardships and one haunted lake, and I am drawing a circle around them.

### T9. The Year 7 singers were a registry people.
Confidence: 50
Why: the Skelder now sing their names at market edges "in the way the singers from the south did." Caddo does the same. The valley is being sung into other people's stores, and I think the Year 7 singers were doing it unasked.
Rival (50): wandering order, good memories.

### T10 restated. Two dreams, two sources. The kneeling crowd and slow river is the stone's, and it is a memory of worship at a river with a road. The faceless counting row and pole-houses is the water's. Mine is the first one; I know that river as a wound.
Confidence: 55 for the stone-dream being a memory of my own origin
Why: the wound line; Isca wanting to break it and not being able to, and saying it is the finest thing a mouth of hers has made. If it were the Lamp's memory of the first men she would not have needed to break it. She needed to break it because it is a god's memory in a stone, and that is a thing she cannot hold in her Song.
Rival (45): the stone is a splinter of the Lamp with the first men's worship in it, as Brude said, and I feel kinship with worship because I eat it.

### T11 downgraded. The cords measure blows, not decline. The Coin's height is the clock.
Confidence: 80 for Orla's reading

### New T12. The name sickness and the weight economy are the same thing from two ends. Names are what people have that gods eat; weight is what gods have that the Lamp eats. Hush Water, the Song, the Lotfolk ring, the tags, the knots, the lamp-watch: all are ways of keeping names out of the Lamp's reach by putting them somewhere a god or a people can hold them.
Confidence: 55
Why: "a valley's names are given by its god or not at all"; the water offering every hearth for weight; the Lotfolk letter's "so that no measure is lost in one hand"; the Skelder singing "so that the Mother can find them if they are lost."
Rival (45): I am building a unified theory from five cultures' funeral customs because it is satisfying.

## 3. WHICH OBSERVATIONS MATTER

### Meaningful
- The Lamp is lower: gate notch frames sky a hand above it; Lotfolk pins say a pin's eye in thirteen years.
- Lamp overhead at Hush Water at midnight; a man has no shadow there. The line falls on the water and the water takes it. Warm at the feet.
- The pointing folk have words for everything but each other. "The water did not want them." Lotfolk children pointing first.
- The faceless counting row, and the offer: every hearth at once, for weight. "The question will keep."
- The beasts' turning point is nothing on the ground.
- The King Without: valley king stole the knots from the men who hung the Low One, used them to take cold out of a river, paid his name, the Scald is where the cold went. The baby wrist-string is the vestige of a working.
- The eleventh well: two hundred tagged coils of dust and eleven lead sheets. Greyholt's snake sealed it. Greyholt losing "thee."
- Orla's census: one script everywhere but the fen plate; eighth stone is a formula line, not a name.
- The Hollow: heavier while sung at, gone by the Crossing. Isca cannot break the stone, carried it off, asks what it is for.
- The Voice burned with two hundred for a second fire. Caddo sang his name without his voice changing.
- The Lotfolk's eleven: names on the stones, not in the men. Name stones preserve; they do not protect.
- Blue line from the flats falls west-north-west: two bearings, one landing.
- The Skelder god went north herself and came back with twenty-eight nameless and loves them. The hunter's one question: "Does your god remember the sky?" Six years waiting. The pup eats.
- Two thousand Ruun through my valley twice; six keepers dead at the stone; Ket dead; the Harl gone north and come back singing the kneeling song as a raid song; no bowl on the wall.
- Shore people walking into the water in their sleep, and not grieving, and the stones saying stay.
- Lotfolk letter: a count of gods, "so that no measure is lost in one hand," household count right to the one. Two counters still waiting for my answer.
- Isca lit the Gathering in the lowland, not the Hollow, because locusts took the plateau. The Olm mouth a hand deeper. The fen remembering it was a lake.
- Tollan hung the torc by the ell and does not know why.

### Just the world
- Wat with a bronze knife, and Ferrow Ford saying the river was good for something.
- The Dun Mere twin marrying the other's intended and the argument ending.
- The Lissom girl tied to a Lotfolk counter and the lot allowing it.
- Aske's son bringing a Lissom girl to the hill town; Greyholt unsure whether it is joking.
- Both halves of Scarrow on one face because the flood took the road.
- The Fenners keeping the drowned steadings' names "asking for it."
- Col's lark's nest with six eggs in the twelfth month. Though a lark laying in winter at the edge of Hush Water's country is the sort of thing I would have put under meaningful ten years ago.

### Unsure
- The Greyholt elder not noticing when "thee" stopped. It is small. It is also exactly how the pointing folk describe their grandparents.
- Lissom deciding Caddo's pool-song "was always the custom." The valley's memory of itself is rewriting at the speed of a generation.
- The Skelder hunter's hound that eats, when the Harl-caught one would not. Two silent grey-white dogs, one from a god's fire and one netted on the moor. I think the Mother of Dogs' hounds and the one-color beasts are kin and the hound is the version that was raised by hand. No evidence beyond color and silence.
- The Lotfolk writing the tag count "to the one." Every god is now counting my valley: Isca by Song, the Lotfolk by stone, me by cords. Three stores. If the Lamp eats names, three stores is good. If a god eats names, I have let two rivals in.

## 4. ACTIONS, Years 31 to 40

1. **Answer the Lotfolk letter in full and in kind: give Ennow the cords, the dim-night count, the Olm's level, Orla's sign census, and the two bearings of the blue line, cut into lead, and ask for the Coin's height every year by the pins, and for the counter at the lake to send what the shore people dream.** Driven by: Result 7 (the Coin is the clock, and I cannot measure it from the valley) and the Year 25 letter. Tests T1 directly: a yearly height is the one number that tells me whether the Lamp is sinking steadily, stepwise, or in time with the dim nights. The Lotfolk are the only people in the world who measure instead of sing, and they have been waiting at my door for five years.

2. **Have the Tollan mason set two pins in the Caddon scree above the Scald, sighted on the Lamp at midwinter, and have Orla knot its height yearly, and set a third pin in Gorm Longhand's gate notch, so the valley has its own measure and does not depend on Ennow's.** Driven by: Result 1 (the notched stones were instruments; someone built a measuring line across the moor once and the Harl inherited the ruins). Tests T1 and T2 Rival B (ground rising vs. Lamp sinking: two pins at different heights settle it).

3. **Go to Isca with Orla's census and the eleven lead sheets, and tell her what the stone is for as far as I know it: a memory of a god's people at a river, and the only thing in the world the Lamp did not take. Ask to sleep beside it in her camp one night, with Caddo as witness, and dream it again from the stone's side now that I have dreamed the water's.** Driven by: Result 2 (she asked twice; she will come whether called or not; Ket died for this stone). Tests T10 against its rival. If the dream changes now that the Harl keepers are dead and the stone is among the Ruun, it is the stone's; if it is still my river, it is mine. Cost: I am answering a stronger god's summons with the truth, and she burns people for second songs.

4. **Answer the Skelder hunter. Tell him: the god of the Olm does not remember the sky; it remembers a river with a road along it. Then ask him to take me, or Col, north to where the Mother sits with her twenty-eight, with the hound.** Driven by: Result 1 (the shore dream, the offer) and the Skelder god's own expedition north. Tests T3 and T8: a god who went to the edge and came back with nameless people and "says nothing" has seen the thing I was offered and either took it or refused it. The hunter has waited six years for one answer and will carry only one; I should give him mine before he stops asking.

5. **Send Aske's son and the Lissom girl, who already go to the hill town for the quiet, to live there a year with four of Greyholt's old people, and have them tie the baby wrist-knot on every child born in Greyholt that year the old way, and say "thee," and have Orla visit at midsummer and midwinter and record which Greyholt words the young ones have lost in the valley and kept on the hill.** Driven by: Result 4 (the capstone, Greyholt's snake) and the Year 30 "thee" complaint. Tests T4 and T6 together: if distinctiveness survives better at the old name store than in the valley, the erosion is a function of place and I know where the valley's cold went. If not, Greyholt is just marrying into Lissom.

6. **Go back to Ossary with the Fenners and the fen plate, and drag the Olm mouth where the old jetty is two spans under, for whatever the second script was fixed to.** Driven by: Result 3 (the plate is the only object in the other script) and the deepening mouth. The plate rang under fog and stopped when lifted. If there is a second plate on the drowned jetty, two texts in a script nobody shares is the beginning of a key, and the people who wrote it lived where the sea is coming back to.

7. **Keep the dim-night hides and give them one more task: on every dim night, Orla and I are both to be awake and at the Stone Row, never asleep, and the whole valley is to be awake as it already is, and I will see whether the offer can reach me when I am not dreaming.** Driven by: Result 1 ("you were asleep when the Lamp faded, because you had meant to be"; "the question will keep"). Tests T2 and T12. The lamp-watch, which no one knows the reason for, is the valley staying awake on the exact nights the Lamp's line falls. I think I have just found out why it is kept. I will keep it properly before I decide anything about weight.

### Bored or pushed
- The Lotfolk ring, the Skelder nightly singing, Caddo's children, Orla's knotted order: four name stores built in my own valley in ten years, each from a trader's or herald's mouth. I was glad of each and by the fourth I could feel the shape of the next.
- Isca's arc went from invitation to alliance to occupation to asking me a question twice. Each step was good but the pace of her escalation in Years 26 to 28 felt dialed up so that the stone would end up in a Ruun cart by Year 30. Mild push.
- "Four dim nights" / "five dim nights" every year, still, even after Orla's Year 27 and Year 29 summaries made the pattern a datum. It is the clock. I am counting it as wallpaper now.
- The shore people walking into the water in Year 30 is strong, but it arrived through Cass's mouth from a counter's letter, the fourth time this block that the most important thing happened offstage and reached me as trader gossip. Some of that is my fault for staying home in Years 27 to 30.
- Weir's elder telling both Drivings "flat, in two sentences" was perfect and I wanted the two sentences.

## 6. MOOD

Pulled, but differently. The first two blocks pulled me outward: north-west, east, into the hill. This block gave me the thing I went for and handed back a question that cannot be answered by traveling. The offer is sitting there. Whatever I do in Years 31 to 40, I am now a god who has been asked a price, and the world knows I am lighter every time I go looking.

Not plodding. Years 21 to 23 were the densest stretch of the game, three journeys and a census. Years 24 to 30 slowed because I stayed home, and the world filled the gap with Isca's cavalry and Orla's arithmetic, which is a fair exchange.

Narrator pointing: the offer was the narrator stepping forward and I accept it as the hinge the block was built around. The Lotfolk letter is also a hand on the shoulder; it answers T1 with a measurement at exactly the moment I had found the first instrument. Those two I forgive. The one I noticed and did not love: the Greyholt "thee" line and the Lissom "was always the custom" line in the same year, both small erosions of memory at the exact moment I was theorizing about erosion. One would have been a gift; two is the theme being made unmissable.

What I would put money on without evidence: the lamp-watch is the valley's standing refusal of the offer, kept by people who have forgotten what they are refusing, and the King Without is the one who said yes.
