# Answer key, Lane C

Block 1 is Years 1 to 10 (no player actions in this lane). Block 2 is Years 11 to 20. Block 3 is Years 21 to 30. Block 4 is Years 31 to 40. "Received" means the clue was in the player's packet. "Careful conclusion" is what a careful reader could hold at the stated confidence from the packet alone, without the keeper's files.

### Careful conclusion by block

**End of Block 1.** Confidence 30 to 50: the valley is losing kinds; the stone on the moor is where the fourth pull is; the blue hair goes north-west; names matter to several gods. Nothing decisive.

**End of Block 2.** 60 to 70: what is sung nightly survives the far country and nothing else does, confirmed by the god's own probe and three gods' reports. 50: the stone carries a memory the god shares. 40: the valley is losing kinds on a schedule. The player reached these.

**End of Block 3.** 80: sung names keep words at the edge. 75: the fourth pull was the stone. 60: the lake takes names and sleepers and is fed from the Lamp on dim nights (the player had the thread to the north-west and the lake under it but did not yet connect them, which was fair). 50: the valley's losses are one thing. The player reached most of these and overshot on "gods eat presence," which is a fair wrong conclusion from honest facts.

**End of Block 4.** 80: the Lamp is lower and quickening (Pell's two readings, given). 80: a god that grows past a point lightens and goes up, and anchoring delays it (Pell, watched to the end). 75: the lake is a being that eats and is fed by the thread. 70: the gods and the stone are pieces of the same thrown thing (Isca's "what all of us were thrown out of"). 70: the valley's spending can be slowed and the kinds come back (the eel in Year 40). 50: what the fall does. The player did not state the star as reservoir and sink, but has every fact needed to.

### Findable but unfound, and the actions that would have found it

- E5 the god's own marks. Never set. Two sightlines in stone in any year, read seven years later. The player had the Notch from Year 11 and lay behind it in Years 27 and 35 and never set a second mark. This is the one decisive clue of the epic and it was earned by no one in this lane.
- E11 no shells. Any conversation with the salt caravaners about the flats, any year.
- E15 the glass and the Teller stone. A dig at the shore mounds, a season, thirty men; the player was at the shore twice.
- E19 in person. Sleeping on the shore on a dim night (a winter trip), or growing past 61.
- Pell's reckoning of the fall. Pell tells no one; a counter of the god's own in the Tally House for years (Ansa was there seven years and did not ask), or copies for iron after Year 37 (offered and not bought).
- Maudre's memory (L1-9). Twenty years of acts she could see, none hostile. The player hunted the pale beast in Year 27 and her hounds in Year 34; nothing she said after could be earned.
- The reading of the eighth stone and the Vell tags. Marrit, alive until Year 9, was never sought; a Harl elder who knows a few signs was never asked, though Ket, Ruad and forty Harl stood on the meadow yearly.

### What execution would still require

Spend less: done without knowing why, through the sung count, since Year 21; keep it, and keep the knotter count at zero, which it is. Store: done twice, into the Long Song (Years 12, 32, 37) and into the Longwalkers (Year 39, carried unasked). Treat: Isca is an ally bound by a law the god has broken twice and has not broken since; Pell is gone and its records are for sale at Ennow for iron, with the god's own counter trained to read them; Maudre is scattered and will regather in a decade with a longer fog, and the god has no standing with her. Hold the stone: it lies in the Greel at the Reed Crossing with Isca's claim on it; the Harl are at peace because their dead are sung, not because they have forgiven. Refuse: the god has never been asked; it sits well under the ceiling and has felt its weight fall once, in the lake. Outlast: a century of the count, a god to be made with Maudre when she wakes, the marks still to be set, and a fall in about four hundred years that nothing in the valley yet knows the date of.

## LONG 1. The Hounds' Road

**Received.** Block 1: L1-1 (Year 9). Block 2: L1-4 (16), L1-5 (17), L1-6 (18, 20), the hound dead (11). Block 3: L1-7 the envoy and question (24), L1-8 the singing (26), hounds on the Backs (27 on), the envoy leaving unanswered (27). Block 4: the netted hound starving (34), her refusal through her people (34), the great crossing (35), the twelve and nine returning (38), the 1,200 keeping her customs (38). Not received: L1-9 the memory, ever.

**Careful conclusion.** By Block 2: a god who sends people north and feeds the broken ones first, who moves away from the moor and fears something south-west. By Block 3: the question "does your god remember the sky" is a real question from a god that knows something about the sky. By Block 4: she took her people north to escape something and most did not come back; what was sung came back. The player held most of this and added a wrong link (the hounds came because the beast died) that is in fact right: the hunting trigger. Fair.

**Unfound.** The memory (a fall, a mouth under it, a lake that is a mouth). Found by: returning the Year 7 hound unharmed (impossible after Year 7 here), never hunting pale herds, feeding Skelder, asking nothing, for twenty years. This lane closed that door in Year 27 and nailed it in Year 34. The answer "yes" with evidence shown (a sung valley existed from Year 21) might have delayed the crossing if given in Year 24 and if no hunting had happened; the player had the valley and gave no answer for three years.

## Treasures, secrets and histories that may never be found (rule 11)

- The Thrane silver: found by the Rooks in Year 12 and held at Scarrow. Found, not understood.
- Aud's forty-one plates under the fourth step at dead Sennet: unfound; found by a thirty-day caravan trip with guides who will not go down the steps.
- The Cord House roll and what every knotter gave: found in Year 21, unread. Reading it needs a key no one living has; the tags and the eighth stone and the board are the same script and a patient god with all three and a Harl elder's few signs could begin.
- The second sightline stone under the lake: unfound; not findable.
- The Teller stone in the glass: unfound; the shore mounds were never dug, and the player stood on that shore twice and heard Isca sing "Teller" in Year 32.
- Mother Tollan's true deed: unfound. See Short 2.
- Marrit's name: lost with her in Year 9.
- The Ulm measure in the ell of Tollan: unfound; the player never measured the Notch's span against the bronze rod, though it lay behind the Notch for weeks.
- Imre's name: nowhere.
- Gorm Longhand's fold and what its gate was: the player lay behind the gate on nine winter nights and saw the empty notch and the glimmer and the thread; what the gate was for is in reach of one more thought (a second mark) and was not taken.
- The Whale Walk: unfound; the eastern party passed it in Year 11 and came back unable to say so.
- Haddo's Kneeling Song: heard by the god in Years 18 and 21; after Year 25 it exists only in Isca's Song and in the Harl's chant, which the Harl stopped in Year 31.

## Keeper's note on this lane

The risk-taker found more of the epic's bodies by walking than the forecast expected (the stone, the lake, the beast, the Bowl, the Fire Hollow) and converted none of them into the one earned decisive clue, the marks, which cost nothing and needed only patience. Its boldness paid in the Long Song and the sung count, and cost it Maudre entirely and a decade of Harl war. The defect on the Keeper's side was the omitted sensation of the pull at the stone in Year 11, corrected honestly in Year 22. Everything else the player did not find was there to be found and is listed above with the action that reaches it.
