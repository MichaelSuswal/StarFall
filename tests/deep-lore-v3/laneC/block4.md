# Block 4. Years 31 to 40. Lane C

## Year 31

- A hard winter, the hardest since the ninth year. The ford froze in the tenth month and stayed frozen into the third. Weir's pond froze to the bottom and the carp were lost entire this time; the miller has put in young ones from Dun Mere. Four old people died above Ferrow Ford and a Lissom child.
- In the fourth month you walked up to Hagg Ridge with the Greyholt girl, Enna, now eighteen, and no spear among you, and stood under the Longhand gate, and the Harl came down the far slope with the Greyholt boy they had held six years, who is a Harl boy now in everything but birth. Brude's son, Ruad, made the trade across the gate-stone with his own hands, and the girl went across one way and the boy the other, and Ruad said the valley owes his clan a stone and eleven dead and that this was the smaller half. You asked him to bring his dead's names to the count. He said the Harl do not sing to the Lamp's people. In the autumn forty Harl stood at the north edge of the meadow with their spears grounded, Ruad among them, and gave Hild the names of the eleven burned and the four stone-men, and Hild's singers sang them, and the Harl listened and did not sing and left before dawn. No Harl has raided the valley since. Greyholt's burners went up to the ridge for the first time in eight years. Ruad says the Low One will be quiet now that its dead are counted somewhere, which is why the raids are done.
- You sent a Tollan rider east to Isca with word that you were going to the stone-child's bowl to trade measures, before you went. He came back with her answer, given before the whole Gathering as everything is: "Go. It will give you nothing it has not weighed and take nothing it has not weighed, and it will be courteous. Tell me what it counts you at. I would know the number." The rider says she laughed, which no one of the valley had seen.
- In the fifth month you went south-west with Hild, twelve riders, the lords' board, and Hild's cords, six weeks: down the Greel's west bank by the salt road, then into broken dry hills where the water is in cisterns and the villages are cut into the steps of dead rivers, farmed from the top down. Every village cast stones before it would sell you bread. Then Ennow at the head of the Terraces: a city of cut steps around a stone basin as wide as the Stone Row is long, and around the whole of it two rings of cut stone to a man's height, every block with names on it, thousands, and a third ring begun. At dusk the whole city stands at the walls and says the names through, which took until the middle of the night.
- The Bowl. Across its floor hundreds of small white stones, knucklebones, and pale moths that move together, and when you spoke they gathered into the shape of a child of about eight and stood for the time it takes to say a long name and then fell apart again across the stone, and a voice came out of the stones, small and exact, with the manners of a court: "The god of the Olm is counted and welcome. Pell counts everything. Pell never chooses; the lot chooses, and lately the lot has been agreeable. You come to trade measures. Good. Pell will give you what it offered, exactly, and take what you offer, exactly, and weigh both." The counters brought out lead and stone. Two bronze pins set in the cliff above the Bowl in the second year of the gods, sighted on the Coin, and a third to look through; in the ninth year the Coin stood lower than the pins by less than the eye of the third pin; in the twenty-second it stood lower again, and the second lowering was near twice the first. Pell said it has reckoned from the two when the Coin reaches the ground, and that the lot fell against telling the number to anyone, and that the lot is the law. Eleven stones the size of a loaf with a name cut on each, which came back from the bare country in the hands of eleven people who did not know the names; the counters have the two hundred names in lead and the eleven who returned marked. Lead sheets with the names of the forty lake families, each name with what became of it: "points," "points," "walked into the water in the thirtieth year," "walked into the water," and so on down the sheet. And a sheet in a different hand, which a counter read to you because Pell would not: "In the thirtieth year at the Bowl in the night came figures without faces who counted me. They offered that the lot should fall as I say, for weight. Pell has never chosen. Pell took it. Pell is lighter by the weight of a child's hand and has had it measured." Pell asked for your measures and Hild gave the count, every household, and the counters cut it in lead as she sang it. Pell asked for the board and had every remaining mark on it counted and copied and could not read one. Then: "Pell asks, by the lot, for one child of the Olm to come to the Bowl and learn to cut marks and count, and Pell will keep a counter at your Row as long as you keep one here. The lot has fallen so. Pell is curious what an Olm child counts." You were home in the ninth month. Isca's rider went east with the number.
- The count was eight nights. On the fourth, Dagg Rook stood on the lords' old seat and spoke eleven names "as the Rooks have always said them," and Hild's singers sang them, and Tollan's elders would not sing that part and stood with their mouths shut, and on the fifth night a line was sung for "the hundred who were lost at Ossary," with Ossary's basket of tags counted out on the meadow one by one as the tally, and the kneelers came up from the bend and knelt on the grass for it and wept, in front of everyone, for the first time inside the count. You were heavier for the count than for any before it, and could not tell the kneelers' part from the rest.
- Dim nights in the first month, the second, and the twelfth.

## Year 32

- At the Reed Crossing in the eighth month, on the way east with the board, a Vell lead sheet, an Ossary tag, and the bronze plate, the pull you have felt east and a little south since the twenty-sixth year was under the ford, in the river, under the Greelmen's boats. The Greelmen say the Ember's riders came in the fifth month and threw a stone off the east bank into the deep channel where the Voice's camp burned, and that the eels have left that reach.
- At the Gathering you laid the four things before the fire and asked whether the Song holds the marks. Isca came out of the fire to look at them as a woman and touched none of them. "The Song holds no mark. A mark is a thing kept outside a mouth, and what is kept outside a mouth I do not keep. The forty strangers sang me forty names of a people I never saw, and I hold them; if one of those names is cut here I would not know it, and nor would they." She sang the forty, there, out of order with the rest of the Song, which the Ruun say she has never done: Ulmarrow, Hesk, Teller, and the others, and a river. You asked whose piece the stone is. "It dreamed my river before my Voice did, and yours before mine, if you are honest, and I have been told it dreamed the Harl's before any of us. It is a piece of what all of us were thrown out of, or it is a piece of me, and I do not know which, and I have put it under the water where Haddo died, where it can dream to the fish. Take your stones home. The Song does not want them. But give me your new fields, which I hear you sing now." You gave the fields and she sang them in. On the third night, the Song took the whole night and most of a fourth. She sang Haddo last among the dead.
- Pell's counter at the Row, Ammet, received a lead sheet by trader in the autumn and read it to you as he is instructed to: "The pins show the Coin lower again. In the thirty-second year the figures came a second time and offered that Pell should see, by touching any stone, every hand that has held it, for weight. Pell took it. Pell has touched the eleven stones and seen the eleven, and their mothers. Pell's shadow is paler and Pell has had it measured against a counter's of the same height, at noon, and it is paler by a third. Prayers at the Bowl come back as echoes. Pell has had the echoes counted."
- The Tollan child who went to Ennow, Hild's apprentice Ansa, writes in cut lead that she is fed and taught and that the Lotfolk children are kind and count her teeth.
- The Lissom plum pressing, two plums; the nine pools full; the Kettle has frogs again. A Greyholt girl made the Vell walk and back and was given a stone.
- Dim nights in the first month and the eleventh.

## Year 33

- In the fifth month you went north-west again with twenty of Hild's singers and the Skelder hound, now a grown dog, grey-white and long-legged, which would come with you to the moor's edge and then had to be carried onto the moor and walked at your heel with its head down the whole way. At Fallow Dip, the black pit. At the lake the Lotfolk huts were half empty and the people in them pointed, all of them now, grown and children; the reed-hut people on the mounds' shore the same. The old man was dead. On the second night, one of your singers, Rafe of Lissom, walked into the water asleep and was not found, and the hound howled all that night and would not stop, and the people on the shore watched it and did not come near. The dream came as before: tall figures in rows, counting, and houses on poles over bright shallow water. On the third night you waded out from the mound shore and swam and dived. The water is warm a man's height down and warmer below that, and the bottom is soft and warm and gives like a body under the hand, and the water there is thick. You came up lighter. As light as the spring of the fourth year when no one came to the Row, lighter than you have been in thirty years, and you stayed so through the walk home, and the weight came back over the autumn and winter as the count was sung and has all come back. Nothing came up with you. Nothing in the lake moved. The hound would not look at you for three days.
- A Greyholt charcoal burner, Pol, summering with the burners on Hagg Ridge, walked out of his hut in his sleep in the seventh month and into the tarn below the Longhand fold, and was found in the morning standing to his chest in it, alive, and does not know Greyholt or his name, and works well. The burners say the Harl put something in the whey.
- Ammet read Pell's sheet at the autumn market: "In the thirty-third year, on a night without a lamp, Pell rose a hand's breadth above the floor of the Bowl and set down. The Tally House saw it and has recorded the height. Pell has ordered the Wall finished within the year and has not asked the lot."
- Lotfolk traders say counters their god sent north to the wood of the Dog-Mother were met at the trees by hounds and a woman's voice from all the hounds at once, were fed, were given iron, and were told to count their own, and were not let in.
- Isca's rider came in the tenth month with a horse for Ansa's mother "because the child's name is in the Song and the mother's is too, and the mother should ride."
- Dim nights in the second month, the eleventh, and the twelfth.

## Year 34

- In the third month you went up the Backs by night with fifty men, nets, and the Skelder hound to the Crown Fold. The hounds were on the skyline as always, silent, eleven of them. The Skelder hound ran to them before anyone could hold it and lay down in the middle of them, and they did not move, and when you called it came back. The nets took one, at the fold wall, a bitch, grey-white, which did not fight the net and looked at you the whole way down the hill. The others stood on the skyline until dawn and then were gone, and by the next night were back, ten. The netted hound would not eat or drink, would not look at anyone but you, made no sound, and was thinner each week, and died in the ninth month in the count house, having never eaten, and weighed at the end like a bundle of sticks. The Skelder hound lay by it every day and ate as always.
- You sent Cade of Tollan north with the words "Yes. Tell her to ask me what I saw." He was twenty-five days going by the Greel way. At the wood's edge there were hounds at the tree line and food left on a stone, and after two days a Skelder band came out and fed him and took the words and went in, and came out the next morning and said the Mother had heard them and said: "It has one of mine in a net. It has shown me what it saw." They gave him pelts and would not let him past the first trees.
- Ammet's reading, autumn: "In the thirty-fourth year Pell has bound itself to Ennow and to the Wall, that they may hold the weight with it. The spring of Ennow, which has run for a thousand years, runs slower and is measured daily. The cisterns hold. Pell is heavier and does not rise. Pell builds."
- Ruun traders say that at the Gathering this autumn the Ember went into the fire on the first night and shouted the Long Song at the sky until dawn, all of it, alone, with the Ruun silent, and in the morning said only that something had asked her for a thing and she had answered it, and that the Song is hers.
- A Weir girl walked the valley with a Scarrow quarryman tied at the wrist and they arrived, and the quarry has a Weir wife in it for the first time.
- Dim nights in the first month and the twelfth.

## Year 35

- The Skelder came to the spring market with few pelts and said the Mother has gone north for good with fourteen hundred of them, all the bands of the Fell side, their dogs, and every hound she has, and that twelve hundred would not go and have stayed in the wood by the Olm road, and that the ones who went sang their names every step of the way to the Fell's end and were still singing when they went out of hearing. The hounds on the Backs were gone from the skyline from the second month, all at once, and the Ferrow Ford shepherds have gone back up to the Crown Fold and found the walls grown over.
- The one-color beasts crossed the moor, Harl traders say, and this year they came no nearer than the old line, and the Harl let them pass and did not watch them.
- Hild is sixty and has given the leading of the count to Osk of Weir, the best of the twenty-four, and sits on a stool at the eighth stone and corrects him.
- Ossary's bank held for the third year and Ossary feasted, and the headman says the river has moved its mind.
- In the eleventh month you went up to Hagg Ridge with four men and furs and lay behind the Longhand gate night after night for a dim night. The nights were still and moonless and very cold, and after the first week, lying long enough on your back, you could see what you had not seen in thirty-five years of lying on your back less long: a fine falling glimmer in the air, like frost drifting down with no cloud to drop it, thin, everywhere, and thicker toward the north-west than the south-east. The men saw it too, once shown. On the ninth night of the twelfth month the Lamp faded. The notch held empty sky as it always does and the sky in it stayed empty. Below the notch the Lamp faded for an hour, and out of it, far to the north-west over the moor, a blue hair came down slow and went below the edge of the land, and the glimmer did not stop while the Lamp was dim. Then the Lamp brightened. One of the four lost two toes.
- Dim nights in the first month, the second, the eleventh, and the twelfth.

## Year 36

- A second Greyholt burner, Pol's brother, walked into the same tarn in his sleep in the eighth month and was found in it at dawn, alive and plain. Greyholt has moved the burners' summer huts down off the ridge and says the tarn is bad and always was.
- Ammet's reading, autumn, which he read twice because he did not believe it: "In the thirty-sixth year the stones of the Tally House go soft at their edges and the rim of the Bowl is smooth as wax. Pell's people remember Pell and cannot picture it, and Pell has had them try, and has counted the failures: all. Pell has finished the Wall. Pell is heavier than the Wall can carry and Ennow's spring has stopped. The lot has not been cast this year. Pell says to the god of the Olm, in courtesy: Pell has measured one thing no other god has measured, which is the weight at which Pell was first asked, in the thirtieth year. Pell sends it in Pell's own measure, which is the count of its people reciting nightly, so that the god of the Olm may set its own beside it: six thousand and forty, every night, for two years."
- Ansa, nineteen, writes that the counters at Ennow stand at the Wall every night and say the names and that she stands with them and that the Bowl has been quiet.
- The Harl left whey at the Greyholt wall every week this summer and Ruad came to the count again and stood and listened and did not sing, and brought three names.
- Dim nights in the first month, the eleventh, and the twelfth.

## Year 37

- In the sixth month twenty Ruun came up the valley, not riders, singers, old and young, with no herald, and went village to village asking at every hearth the names of its places and its dead, and the Ember's instruction, which they recited: "What is sung stays and what is not sung goes. Give me what you have and I will keep it." The valley gave them the whole count, eight nights of it, which Osk sang and they learned, and they went on to the Fenners and the Greelmen, who gave them little.
- Ammet received no sheet at the autumn market. Rell's successor, Tovin, said only that the Bowl has been dark since a dim night in the fourth month and that the counters run the lots.
- The spring flood was ordinary and the ford moved back to where it was in the second year, which the oldest at Ferrow Ford say they always knew it would.
- Dun Mere's eel fair: plain eel. The smoker says the spotted eel was a story.
- Dim nights in the first month, the fourth, the eleventh, and the twelfth.

## Year 38

- Skelder traders came to the spring market, few and old, and said that a pack of nine hounds came out of the north in the autumn with twelve Skelder who know their names because they sang them every night, and nothing else came back, no dogs but the nine, no Mother, and that the hounds in the wood have gone silent and will not speak with any throat, and that the twelve hundred who stayed say she will come back, and count themselves every night in her way, and sing the names she taught, and keep the ban on the one-color beasts.
- Lotfolk traders at the autumn market said it plainly and then cast a stone to see if they should have: on a dim night in the fourth month of last year the Tally House saw a scattering of pale lights go up from the Bowl toward the Coin, hundreds of them, slow, and the Bowl has been empty stone since. The Tally House says Pell has gone up into the Coin to count it, and casts the lots in Pell's name, and the lots fall as lots do. Ennow's spring is dry. Ansa has written that she will come home when a Lotfolk child comes to the Row in her place, by the terms, and that the counters hold forty years of lead and will copy anything for iron.
- Isca's riders came to the autumn market and said the Song took four nights this year and holds, among much else, the Ruun, the forty strangers' names, the Olm entire, the Harl the Ember killed, Haddo, and the places and dead of nine peoples besides.
- The honey from the Backs was the darkest in memory, with the folds grown over and the heather thick, and sold at the count by the pot as it did in the sixth year.
- Dim nights in the first month, the second, and the twelfth.

## Year 39

- In early summer forty strangers came up the Olm from the Greel, men and women, dressed in nothing particular, carrying nothing to trade, and came to the Stone Row at dusk. Hemma is thirty years dead and no one living had seen them but the oldest Fenners. They asked the name of every village and Osk sang them the count instead, all eight nights of it in one night, fast, and they listened without moving, and at dawn they sang the whole of it back, every household, pool, field and fold, in order, without a fault, which they had never done before, and then went on to names no one knows, and then left at first light upstream toward Greyholt and the moor. They asked you nothing and took bread.
- Ruad of the Harl came to the count with eleven names of Harl dead this year, old and young, and stood and listened and did not sing, and a Harl girl with him did.
- The Lissom pools were all nine at midsummer and the Pool of the Drowned Ox had an ox in it, which lived.
- Ansa came home, twenty-two, cutting marks in lead and counting in the Lotfolk way, and a Lotfolk boy of nine sits at the Row in her place and counts the singers.
- Dim nights in the first month and the twelfth.

## Year 40. The 351st year after the Driving

- At Dun Mere in the eighth month a boy took a spotted eel in the tarn, the first since the tenth year, sweet, and the smoker who said it was a story cut it himself and wept, and three more have been taken since, and Dun Mere says the pike has died at last.
- The Lissom runs in its nine pools through the orchards as it did, the Kettle, the Long Pool, the Pool of the Drowned Ox and the rest, each with its name, two plums, and the pressing in the tenth month smelled through the village.
- Greyholt says "thee" to its elders and to no one else, and to you, and the rest of the valley laughs at it, as it always has.
- The sung count was eight nights, Osk leading, Hild on her stool, Ammet's successor counting the singers, Ruad standing at the north edge with twenty Harl who gave their year's dead and did not sing, and the kneelers, who are children's children now and kneel for the hundred of Ossary and do not weep. You were heavier than in any year, and in the spring after the hard winter of the fourth year you were lightest, and you have felt every year between as you feel your own weight.
- Greelmen say the Greel is wider again below the Olm mouth and the drowned jetty is three spans under now, and that it is the northern rain.
- Dim nights in the first month, the eleventh, and the twelfth.
