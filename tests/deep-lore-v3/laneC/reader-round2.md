# Reader Round 2. Lane C, bold risk-taker. Years 11 to 20

## RESULTS

**1. Fallow Dip with Ket, open the cairn, sleep by the stone.** The world answered clearly. The stone is real, blue-grey, too heavy, cold now. I dreamed the kneeling crowd and a broad brown river that I know from before the meadow and cannot place. Ket got the crowd and not the river. So the river is mine: a memory of where I came from, or of what I am. My T1 (siphon line to a dead god) was not confirmed: no blue hair fell, I felt no closer to the fourth presence, and nothing drained me. What it taught instead: the stone is a transmitter of memory, it gives to whoever sleeps by it, and I primed the pump. Opening the cairn started a chain I did not foresee: the Harl rebuilt it higher, posted sleepers, Haddo slept there and made a song of it, and now that song is a war chant and the Harl are raiding harder than at any time since the Driving. My boldness fed something. Theory half right (the stone matters, it is a god-fragment or god-memory), half wrong (not a siphon, not the fourth presence).

**2. The Fire Hollow with two hundred and Aldo Rook.** Answered very clearly. Isca is a woman by day and a fire by night, she keeps a Voice, she holds every name in a Song, and her stated law is total reciprocity of knowledge: a friend keeps nothing from her and that is the one thing she will not forgive. I was heavier while they sang the valley back and it did not all go. T2 confirmed and sharpened: weight is attendance, and being sung by a large crowd also feeds, and the weight persists. The cost: Mab drowned, an old man died of fever, the harvest was short, the count was held without me, Hemma died and her long cords died with her. I also handed Isca the valley's names and my own, and she sang Aldo in. She now has a claim on me and on my people's memory, which is the exact asset this world fights over. Bold move, big payout, bigger exposure than I understood at the time.

**3. Vell with Pim and the Greyholt elders.** Answered. Vell exists: a roofless rock town, eleven cisterns, nine sweet and cold, one collapsed, one sealed with a capstone carved with the Greyholt hearth snake. A grave stone with an empty border. No knots, no life. T3 (bigger civilization before the Driving) strengthened. T5 (lamb-taker lives up there) not supported. The payoff came in Year 17 when Greyholt drove its flocks to Vell in the drought and saved them, and cut a path. That is the best thing I have done: breaking a taboo in person made the hills usable. The sealed cistern with the snake is unopened and the Greyholt headman recognized the mark on his own hearth. That is a door with a family's name on it.

**4. Buy the grey dog and walk it south-west.** The world answered with a shrug: the dog died four years before I asked, never having eaten. Taught me that I waited too long on T4 and that these grey things do not survive captivity. The south-west test never happened. Theory untested. I regret not sending someone the same year I noticed the dog.

**5. Brann Cut in the fog, lead boat.** Answered. No drowned post, no walls, no timber. One bronze plate a span across with rows of marks and a script no one reads, lying edge-up and humming in the wind. A caravaner read two words, "Aud" and "tide," and called it the sea's anger written down. T3 strengthened (literate predecessors, a trading or recording culture). T6 not touched. The Fenners went straight back to fishing the cut, which I like: I removed a fear for them for the price of a morning.

**6. Stand on the eighth stone, cut Scarrow's tithe for Scarrow's bodies.** Answered in two directions. Standing on the stone silenced the elders, revealed a line of marks under my feet I cannot read, and Hemma said Mother Tollan stood on it last. The Ossary elder would not stand again that count. So T8 confirmed in substance: the eighth stone is the god's seat, and a human stood on it last. The tithe trade: Scarrow comes in a body, so my attendance theory was correctly applied. But I left the hill "where it lay" when Aldo asked in front of everyone, and that was the mistake of the decade. He dug it himself in Year 12, found eleven torcs, foreign coin, silver, the named bones, and a scraped tally-board, and now holds a rival festival that draws half of Scarrow on the longest night and stands on Scarrow's stone with three torcs. I built a rival hearth by refusing to answer a direct question. Not in my style, and it cost me.

**7. Six men east past the last well with Hemma's name-cords, Ebb among them.** Answered brutally and precisely. Four came back knowing only the four names they sang nightly to each other and nothing else; the cords came back intact and meaningless to them. Alun died of thirst. Ebb walked on east and did not return. T6 confirmed hard: past the last well, memory is stripped, and only what is sung every night survives. Objects do not hold memory; voices do. Cross-confirmed from three other peoples in the same decade: Isca's band that lost its camp but kept every verse; the Skelder Mother's hunters sent north and returned nameless; the Lotfolk's two hundred sent west with name-stones, eleven back blank, stones intact. Four gods are now independently probing the same edge and getting the same result. Dace's wife teaches him his children's names every morning and he loses them by evening. I did that to him and I know it.

## THEORIES (updated)

**T2, now the core law. Gods eat presence and being-named. The valley and all its neighbors live inside a shrinking ring beyond which names are erased.** Confidence 90. Evidence: my weight at the Stone Row and at the Fire Hollow; four gods' probes (mine east, Skelder north, Lotfolk west, Isca's band east on the plateau) all returning blank survivors; every neighboring god is a name-keeper (Isca's Song, the Mother of Dogs speaking every name every night, the Lotfolk wall of names, the Greel singers singing the villages back). Rival (10): the salt and the bare country are simply lethal to memory as a geographic fact, unrelated to gods.

**T10, new. The ring is tightening and the valley is losing texture from the edges in.** Confidence 65. The spotted eel gone and now called a myth within seven years; the grey plum gone, cut and burned, and children do not know there were three; the Skelder Mother moving camps away from the moor every year; the Lotfolk lake families gone quiet and the stone says do not speak of it; a round of bare grass with no seed heads and no bird south of the Vell path; the bare country north and west and east. The pattern of small losses is the same pattern as the big one: a thing goes bland, then small, then gone, then was never there. Rival (30): drought, pike, grafting, and three separate peoples' misfortunes, no shared cause.

**T11, new. The Fallow Dip stone is a piece of a god, and it transmits one memory: the kneeling crowd and the brown river, which is MY river.** Confidence 75 that it is a god-fragment. Confidence 60 that the river is from my own origin and the crowd was my worshippers or someone's. The Harl say it is the Low One's; Haddo says it is the Ember's oldest song from before the Ruun. Both claiming it means neither knows. Rival (30): it is a fragment of the fourth presence, and the dream is the fourth presence's last memory, which would explain why it is cold and does not answer and why Isca "does not love what does not answer." Rival (10): the stone is a piece of me shed on arrival, and the dream is mine, which is why the river was familiar.

**T12, new and urgent. The Kneeling Song is a weapon or a contagion.** Confidence 60. Haddo took it from the stone to the plateau; a dozen fires sing it; Isca went into the fire all night and burned nothing and spoke to no one when she heard it, and sang Haddo's name next morning in a voice nobody knew. The Harl chant it before raids now and raid harder than in three hundred years. A song about a crowd kneeling makes crowds kneel, or makes gods want crowds to kneel. The murrain across the Greel started two years after Haddo carried it home. Rival (35): the song is merely sacred and Isca was moved, the Harl were already drifting toward war after the whey-bowl truce broke in Year 11, and the murrain is a murrain.

**T1 revised. The fourth presence north-west is a god that has stopped answering, and the blue hair is a thread between it and the Lamp.** Confidence 50, down from 60, because nothing on the moor confirmed it. What keeps it alive: the Lotfolk families went north-west toward a lake their god "drew for them" and have gone silent; Isca is actively hunting the thing in the west; the beasts cross the moor and now come closer. Rival (35): the fourth presence is not a god but the ring's edge itself, a place where naming fails, and it "pulls" because it is a hole.

**T4, grey beasts.** Confidence unchanged at 60 that they are not animals. New: the dog never ate and died; the herd came within sight of Hagg Ridge in Year 20, closer than ever. Pairs with T10: whatever is at the edge is moving in, and the beasts are its outriders or its leavings. Rival (30): migratory herd shifting range in dry years.

**T9, Aldo Rook.** Now a live political threat, not a question. Confidence 80 that he will claim the hill as a lord and split the valley's attendance. The scraped tally-board and named bones in the chamber mean the lords were literate and memory-keeping, and Mother Tollan's people erased them deliberately. Confidence 55 that the Driving was a memory war, not a peasants' revolt: the winners scraped the losers' names. This fits the whole world's logic. Rival (35): plain revolt, plain vandalism.

**T3, old civilization.** Confidence 85 now. Vell, the bronze plate with a script, the Lord's Hill chamber, the Ossary bones in slabs, the notched old gate on Hagg Ridge that points at nothing, the fen that "was a lake once." Much more here than one valley's history.

**T5, lamb-taker.** Dropped to 30 and parked. Nothing in ten years.

**T13, new. Isca is a friend who will become an enemy the moment she learns I kept something from her.** Confidence 70. She said it in her own mouth. I did not tell her the river in my dream was mine. If Haddo's song is "the Ember's own oldest song" and I recognized the river in it before he did, she will feel that as a theft or a lie when she learns it.

## WHICH OBSERVATIONS MATTER

Matter most:
- The four independent memory probes and their identical results (east, north, west, plateau east). Objects keep nothing; nightly singing keeps names.
- The Kneeling Song's spread and Isca's night in the fire.
- The Harl chanting the dream before raids, the stone-men, and raids larger than any since the Driving.
- The brown river in my dream that Ket did not get.
- The Lord's Hill chamber: scraped tally-board, named bones, foreign coin, eleven torcs.
- The sealed cistern at Vell with the Greyholt hearth snake on it.
- The bronze plate: "Aud," "tide," the sea's anger written down. Plus the Fenner elder saying the fen was a lake and will be again. Plus the ground shaking in Year 19.
- The round of bare grass with no seed and no bird on the Vell path.
- The beasts crossing within sight of Hagg Ridge.
- The Lotfolk lake families gone silent, and the Lotfolk god building a wall of names.
- The Skelder Mother moving camps toward the Fell, then going north herself and coming back with twenty-eight blanks.
- Hemma's death and the loss of what the long cords meant. Hild starting over. The valley's memory just reset at the top.
- The old gate on Hagg Ridge that points at nothing and that no Harl will move.
- Dim nights steady at two to three a year, not climbing fast but not stopping.

Just the world:
- Otters, Wat's free ferry, the Greelman wintering at Weir, the wrist-walk marriages, red calves and thin milk, the rooks being wrong, the lantern walk birth, the Lissom couple on the water. The Dun Mere pike story is world too, though the forgetting of the spotted eel is not.

## ACTIONS, Years 21 to 30

1. **Open the sealed cistern at Vell with the Greyholt headman's own hands on the capstone.** Driven by Result 3. His hearth-snake is cut on it. Whatever his family sealed, his family opens, with me standing there. Tests T3 and the memory-war version of T9: I expect names or bones kept deliberately away from the valley.

2. **Confront Aldo Rook on Lord's Hill on the longest night, in front of his half of Scarrow, and put the tally-board question to him: read me the names or I will.** Driven by Result 6, my worst mistake. I will not fight him for the hill; I will out-lord him. Take the scraped board, bring it to the Stone Row, and have the valley sing whatever letters remain. If the Driving was a memory war, then giving the lords their names back under my roof ends the Rooks' monopoly on the past and folds their festival into mine. Risky: it may give him exactly the legitimacy he wants. I would rather that than two hearths.

3. **Go to the Fire Hollow a second time, alone with twenty riders, and tell Isca the river is mine.** Driven by Result 2 and T13. Her law is that a friend keeps nothing. I kept something. I would rather confess it at her fire before Haddo's song teaches it to her than have her find it out. While there, hear the Kneeling Song from her own fire and watch what she does. Tests T12 and T13 directly, and it is the most dangerous room I know of.

4. **Walk north-west past the Lotfolk's lake with the four blank men from the east (Dace, Hob, Sim, Jory) and a company of thirty, every one of us singing the whole valley every night, not just our own names.** Driven by Result 7. The four already proved that what is sung survives; now test whether singing the valley keeps the valley in us past the edge, and whether men who have already been emptied can be emptied again. Go toward the fourth presence and the vanished Lotfolk. Tests T1 and T2 at the hardest edge. I lead the singing.

5. **Ride to Hagg Ridge when the beasts cross and kill one myself, in daylight, under the old gate that points at nothing, and bring the carcass back to the Stone Row.** Driven by Result 4, the dog I was too slow for. Tests T4. If there is no heart, I want the whole valley to see it. If the Harl stone-men object, I want to meet them with a dead grey beast between us.

6. **Go to Fallow Dip again and take the stone.** Driven by Result 1 and T12. The Harl have made it an altar and a war-drum. Carry it to the Stone Row and set it under the eighth stone, then sleep on it myself every count night. Either the dream is mine and I reclaim it, or the stone is a god-fragment and it belongs beside a god, not under raiders. This will start a war with the Harl, which the stone-men have already started anyway.

7. **Recruit Hild and three young singers from each village and make the count a sung count: every household name, every pool, every field, sung at the Stone Row every year, with a second singing at Vell and a third at Ossary.** Driven by Results 2 and 7 and Hemma's death. This is the one non-reckless action and I take it because the world has told me five times how memory survives here. It also makes the count the biggest attendance of the year, which feeds me.

## BEST MOMENTS and BORED OR PUSHED

Best moments:
- Ket dreaming the crowd and not the river. One line that told me the river is mine.
- The four men at the last well, sitting, singing four names in turn. And the cords came back with every knot in them.
- Isca: "I do not bargain. I give. And I keep nothing from a friend, and a friend keeps nothing from me, for that is the one thing I will not forgive." A real god with a real law I am already breaking.
- Isca going into the fire all night when she heard the Kneeling Song and singing Haddo's name in a voice no one knew.
- Greyholt driving its goats to Vell in the drought because I broke the taboo four years earlier, and still saying the hills are unlucky, and cutting a path anyway.
- The Lotfolk trader casting a stone to see whether to say more about the lake, and the stone saying no.
- The Rooks digging the hall floor and finding the scraped tally-board, scraped and recut and scraped, the last cuts shaky.
- Dace saying his children's names back every morning and losing them by evening.
- The Harl chanting the dream on the hill above the folds before the raid.
- Lissom's children not knowing there were three plums.

Bored or pushed:
- The journey to and from the Fire Hollow was given one paragraph each way with two deaths in a clause. For a five-week expedition with two hundred people, I wanted one real incident on the road.
- The Weir dog being already dead was a fair consequence of my slowness but the result was a null in a ten-year block. Something could have been under the willow.
- The Brann Cut plate was good but the follow-up (a caravaner reading two words) was the only development in seven years. The plate hummed and then nothing.
- Otters again. Rooks again. Wrist-walks again. The texture repeats are starting to read as a rotation.
- Slightly pushed: Isca asking me about "the fourth thing that pulls from the west and does not answer" in almost my own words from the opening packet. And the Lotfolk Year 20 report lining up so exactly with my own east expedition (stones intact, names gone) felt like the narrator making sure I saw the parallel. I saw it anyway. The Fenner elder's "the fen was a lake once and will be a lake again" also read as a flag planted for later.
- The lamb-taker thread is still dead. Either kill it or pay it off.

## MOOD

Pulled, hard. The world punished and rewarded my boldness in proportion, which is what I want from a god game. The Fallow Dip cairn and the east expedition both came back with consequences I own: a war chant on the moor and a man who forgets his children daily. The Fire Hollow gave me the single best scene of the game so far and a standing debt to a god who does not forgive secrets. I have one regret in my own style, which is that I let Aldo's question sit, and that one hesitation built a rival hearth. I will not hesitate again.

The narrator pointed a few times (Isca's phrasing, the Lotfolk parallel, the lake prophecy) but the big revelations arrived through the consequences of my own moves, which is the right way. The three-god consensus on memory at the edge was earned rather than told. The pacing of this block was better than the first: nearly every year had a result or a cost that came from something I did.
