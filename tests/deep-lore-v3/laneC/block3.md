# Block 3. Years 21 to 30. Lane C

## Year 21

- In the sixth month you went up to Vell by Greyholt's cut path with the headman, Orm, and had him set his own hands to the carved capstone. It took him and four others a day to lever it. Under it, a chamber the size of a byre, dry, with stone shelves along three walls. On the shelves, rows of grey dust, each row the shape of a coiled cord, and lying on each row a fired clay tag the size of a thumb with marks scratched in it, two hundred or so, the same kind of tag the Ossary flood gave up in the eighth year. In a stone box at the back, a stack of thin lead sheets scratched edge to edge with the same sort of marks, in lines, like a tally. Orm knows none of it except his snake. He says the chamber was the lords' treasury, emptied before they fled, and the tags are counters for what was taken, and that his family's snake is on it because his family were the lords' stewards, which no one in Greyholt had ever said before and everyone in Greyholt now says.
- Hild has gathered three young singers from every village, twenty-four of them, and they spent the summer going house to house with her cords, learning every household's names and every pool and field and fold, and the count this autumn was sung, not argued, for the first time: three nights, every name in order, Hild leading and the whole meadow answering. It was the biggest crowd the Stone Row has held. You were heavier for it than for any harvest festival, and the elders complained that the sheep were not argued over properly and under-counted themselves out of habit.
- You rode to the Fire Hollow in the seventh month with twenty riders on Isca's horses. At the Reed Crossing the Greelmen would not ferry horses for less than iron, and a Tollan rider, Cade, swam his across and won the price down for all of you, and the Greelmen have told the story against themselves since. On the plateau a Ruun band, Mera's, rode with you three days for the company and sang the valley's names to you as they ride them every autumn, pools and all, and asked which pool the Drowned Ox was drowned in.
- At the Gathering Haddo sang the Kneeling Song from the fire's edge on the first night, and Isca walked into the fire and the fire said nothing until dawn. On the second night you stood before the Ruun and told her the river in that song is a river you dreamed at the stone on the moor before Haddo did, and knew, and kept from her. The fire rose to twice its height and the Ruun nearest it ran. It said: "Nine years you have carried this to my fire. I do not forgive a thing kept. But you have brought it before every band of the Ruun with your own mouth, and so it was not kept; it was slow, and I will call it slow." Then: "The river is not yours. I have dreamed it since the forty strangers sang it on this grass in the eighth year, and it is in the Song where I put it. Now it is in my Voice's mouth, and in yours, and I like it less every time it is spoken by anyone but me." She asked where the stone lies and you told her. She said she would come to it when the Song allowed. She gave nothing this year and took nothing, and sang the valley's names on the third night as always, and Cade's.
- Lotfolk traders at the autumn market say their god's wall around its city is finished to a man's height and every living name of their people is cut on it, and that the whole city stands at the wall at night and says the names through, which takes until the middle of the night. They say it is so the god can find anyone it wants. The Skelder, at the spring market, said the Mother came back out of the north with twenty-eight of the forty and fewer hounds, and the twenty-eight are like the three: they speak and hunt and do not know their names, and she names them every night anyway.
- Harl raiders came down twice, on Lissom's upper orchards in the fifth month and Ferrow Ford's folds in the ninth, with the chant above them both times. Lissom lost its upper wall and forty trees.
- Dim nights in the first month, the eleventh, and the twelfth.

## Year 22

- In the fifth month you walked north-west with thirty and the four who came back from the east, singing the whole of the valley every night, you leading, three hours of names before sleep. Nine days to Fallow Dip, with Harl on the skyline the whole way. Four men live by the cairn now in a turf hut and did not come out while you passed, and chanted. Past the hollow, the pull you have felt from the north-west for twenty-one years was behind you, in the hollow, and stayed behind you, and in front of you there was nothing pulling at all.
- Sixteen days more of moor, then a flat grey water two days across with no ripple on it in a stiff wind. On its eastern shore, grass mounds the people there will not walk on, and reed huts. A half-day north of those, the Lotfolk families, forty huts, with their god's marked stones in a bowl at the door of each. The grown ones still have names and use them less than they use their hands; their children, born here or brought small, point. They fish the edge and not the middle because the stones said so. The reed-hut people on the mounds' shore point at each other and have words for everything else, for the fish, the weather, the god. An old man there, after you had stayed a night, said his grandparents had names and the water did not want them. Everyone who slept on that shore dreamed the same: tall figures with no faces, standing in rows, counting, and houses on poles above a shallow bright water. A Weir man swam out and came back saying the bottom is warm. Nothing came out of the lake. Nothing happened in the lake.
- You went on past it, west and north, twenty days, to see. The heather gave out into a grass of one kind, streams you could not tell from one another, birds all the one brown, and no track of any people. The men sang the valley each night and sang it well. When the food ran to ten days you turned. Two of the thirty came home having lost small things: Tam of Dun Mere knows his wife's name and not her face until she speaks, and a Lissom man has lost every song he knew except the one you taught them. The four from the east are as they were, and they can sing the whole valley now without a fault, and Dace sang his own children's names in it on the first night home and did not know they were his.
- At Fallow Dip on the way back, in the ninth month, you took the stone. The four stone-men stood in front of the cairn and would not move, and your men killed two of them and the other two ran, and Bran of Tollan was killed in it with a Harl spear through him. Eight men carried the stone on a litter of ash poles. Every one of the eight dreamed the kneeling crowd and the river every night of the road, and asked to be relieved, and were, and the next eight dreamed it. The Harl came at you on Hagg Ridge in the dark at the Longhand fold and two more of yours were cut before they were driven off. You came down into Greyholt on the fourth day of the tenth month, and the thing you have leaned toward from the meadow for twenty-one years was in the litter beside you.
- You set the stone in a pit cut at the foot of the eighth stone and lay on it on the night of the sung count, and dreamed the crowd and the river, and nothing else, as before.
- The sung count, second year, four nights now because Hild has added the fields by name. The salt caravan after it brought word that the Lotfolk god's people cast lots for a month this summer and then started cutting a second ring of stone outside the first.
- Dim nights in the second month and the twelfth.

## Year 23

- Harl war. Raiders came down in the third month, the fifth, the seventh, and the tenth, on Greyholt, Lissom, Ferrow Ford, and Greyholt again, forty to a hundred men each time, chanting the dream above the folds before they came. Greyholt has lost eleven people and most of its goats and has a wall now instead of a hedge, and its charcoal burners do not go up to Hagg Ridge. The Harl who were caught say the stone is the Low One's and the valley has stolen it and the Low One will not let them rest until it is back.
- At Tollan the people who sleep nearest the Stone Row have begun to dream, since the thaw, of a crowd kneeling and a river, and wake weeping and cannot say why. By the autumn it is a quarter of the village. They say the sorrow came down from the moor with the stone and belongs to someone else.
- On the longest night you went up Lord's Hill with forty of Tollan and stood in the hall's footprint in front of the Rooks' half of Scarrow and told Aldo Rook to read you the names on his board or give it to you. He read nothing, because he cannot, and he gave it, and said as he gave it, loudly, that the god of the valley now carries the lords' board to the lords' old seat at the Row, and that this is the first justice his line has had in three hundred years, and his half of Scarrow cheered him for losing it. The board is at the Stone Row now, propped against the eighth stone. The marks that remain on it are the same kind as the line under your feet, and as the Vell tags, and as the signs cut over the lords' bones in the chamber. No one in the valley reads any of them. At the sung count Hild's singers sang a line for "the lords of the hill, whose names are scraped," since there were no names to sing, and Tollan's elders said nothing while it was sung and a great deal afterward. About a third of the Rooks' following came down to the meadow this longest night. Aldo kept the hill, the silver, the torcs, and the rest.
- In the ninth month a storm came up the Greel such as no one remembers, three days of wind from the south-east, and the fen rose and took two Fenner steadings whole with eleven people, and the Brann Cut is a mere now, and the geese did not come.
- The Skelder came to the spring market and asked, at every hearth they traded at, whether any god has gone far and come back. The valley told them its god walked past the great lake in the north-west and twenty days beyond and came back, and the Skelder went quiet and bought nothing more that day and were gone in the morning.
- Dim nights in the first month, the eleventh, and the twelfth.

## Year 24

- In the fourth month a single Skelder hunter came down the moor road alone with a pack of forty pelts and, in a basket on his back, a grey-white hound pup with its eyes just open. He came to the Stone Row, set the pelts and the pup down before you, and said these words and no others: "Does your god remember the sky?" He says he will carry back one answer and will wait for it as long as it takes, and he has pitched a hide tent on the meadow and feeds the pup from his own mouth.
- The kneelers. By midsummer forty or fifty of Tollan go down to the great bend at dusk, kneel in the grass facing the Row, and weep, and get up and go home. They do not pray and do not speak your name while they do it. They say they kneel for the ones who were lost, and do not know who. The elders have asked you to forbid it and the kneelers have asked you to join it. You are no heavier for them.
- Ruun horse traders at the autumn market say the Voice, Haddo, walked out of the Gathering this autumn with eleven bands behind him and has made his own fire at the Reed Crossing on the east bank, and sings the Kneeling Song there every night, and that the Ember sang all three nights at the Hollow as if nothing had happened and sang the eleven bands' names in their places.
- Harl raids in the fourth and eighth months. Ferrow Ford has moved its folds down off the Backs into the valley floor and the sheep are poorer for it.
- Greyholt says "thee" to the Skelder hunter, who is old, and he does not know what to make of it.
- Wat of Ferrow Ford drowned in the spring flood, pulling a child out, and the child lived, and Ferrow Ford gives its river stones from his hut now.
- Dim nights in the second month and the twelfth.

## Year 25

- In the sixth month three Lotfolk came up the valley road in grey, counters, with a letter cut in a sheet of lead in a hand so small it took Hild a day to make it out, phrased as from one court to another. The god of the Lots proposes to the god of the Olm a count of gods: each to tell the others what it has measured. It states that the Olm valley holds one thousand three hundred and nine households at this year's count, which is Hild's number exactly, and offers in exchange for the Olm god's measures "the height of the Coin in thirteen years" and "what the stones carried back." The counters will wait a season for the answer and have asked to watch the sung count, and cast lots about where to sleep.
- Greelmen came up to Weir in the eighth month white-faced and said that the Ember came to the Reed Crossing as a fire taller than the willows and the east-bank camp burned in a night, tents, cattle, and people, and that two hundred died in it and the Voice among them, and that she stood in the ash till morning and then went back east. Ruun traders at the autumn market, fewer than any year since the tenth, say she sang Haddo's name into the Song at the Gathering in its place among the dead and that the Ruun heard her voice break on it and have never heard that before.
- The kneelers at the bend are more, and have begun to kneel on the longest night too, after the lantern walk, in the dark.
- The Lotfolk counters, waiting, counted everything: hearths, sheep, the standing stones, the knots in Hild's cords, the pools of the Lissom, the kneelers. They say their god's lake families are "ours no longer and point like the others," and cast no stone about it.
- Harl raids in the fifth month and the ninth. A Greyholt girl was taken and a Harl boy was taken by Greyholt the month after, and both are held.
- Dim nights in the first month, the eleventh, and the twelfth.

## Year 26

- The flood in the third month was the greatest anyone living or dead in the valley has seen. Five days at Tollan's meadow under water to the stones' knees. At Ossary the whole bank from the landing to the hill went into the river in one night, and a hundred graves opened at once, old ones, laid in rows with bone on bone, and among the bone a hundred clay tags on rotted cords, bronze needles with an eye at each end, beads of blue glass. Ossary spent the spring burying and sent you a basket of the tags. Ossary's elder says these are the dead of the Driving, buried in a hurry, and the tags are the badges the lords made their people wear.
- In the sixth month Isca came. Two thousand Ruun riders down the Greel's west bank and up the valley road, and the valley stood in its doorways. She came to the meadow as a woman and spoke before the Row: "I was told at my own fire, by a trader, that the stone lies under your eighth stone, and not by you. I called your slowness slow once. I will not call it so twice. I have come for the stone that made my Voice sing against me, and I will have it, and I will not burn one roof of the Olm for it, because the Olm is in the Song and nothing leaves the Song while I stand. But the next thing I learn from a trader and not from you, I will learn as fire." At dusk she walked onto the stone as fire and stood on it until dawn. The grass burned in a ring the width of the meadow. The eighth stone is black on its river side. The stone under it was not marked. In the morning her riders dug it out and lifted it on poles, and before they were out of the valley the carriers were asking to be relieved. She sent a thousand riders up through Greyholt onto the moor, and the Harl traders who came to the wall in the autumn say the cairn at Fallow Dip is a black pit, the turf hut ash, and the two stone-men who were left and nine others with them are dead, and that the Harl say the valley god called the fire down on them.
- The stone went east with her down the Greel. The pull you had under your feet for four years went with it, east and a little south, and from the meadow you feel it that way still, faint and not answering.
- The dreams at Tollan stopped within the month. The kneelers kneel at the bend still, fewer every month, and say they kneel for the ones who were lost, and have begun to argue among themselves about who.
- The Skelder, at the spring market, said the Mother now has every camp sing its name and every person his own, nightly, in a tune the traders hummed over their pelts without meaning to, and that she learned it from the strangers who sang across the Fell.
- The Skelder hunter is still on the meadow with his tent and the pup, which is a hound now, and says he will wait for his answer. Dim nights in the first month, the second, and the twelfth.

## Year 27

- The one-color beasts crossed in the seventh month. You had word from Greyholt's watchers and rode to Hagg Ridge with twelve, through the Longhand fold, which was empty this year; the Harl do not summer where Isca's riders burned. You camped four nights behind the old gate waiting; by day the notch frames the moor's edge, by night a piece of empty sky with the Lamp a hand below it. On the fifth morning the herd came by below the ridge, some hundreds, all the one grey, in silence, and you rode down and killed one yourself in the open with a spear, in daylight, and the herd neither ran nor stopped. It was carried to the Stone Row in two days. All of Tollan saw it opened. Meat, grey through, and bone, and nothing else a butcher could name: no heart he could find, no liver, no gut you could call a gut, no fat, no smell, and the dogs would not go near it. It did not stink on the third day. Dun Mere's smoker says it is a beast that has been dead a long time and does not know it. It was buried below the bend by your order and the ground over it has grown plain grass.
- From the ninth month, lambs have been going from the Ferrow Ford folds and the Backs steadings with blood and drag marks and the prints of long-legged dogs, and at dusk grey-white hounds are seen on the skyline of the Backs, never fewer than nine, silent. Old Col, who is past seventy, says the lambs went like this once before, in the years before the hard winter of the ninth year, but with no blood and no mark, and stopped that winter and never started again, and that this is different and worse.
- The Skelder hunter on the meadow struck his tent on the day the hounds were first seen on the Backs, and left northward without the pelts and without the hound, which he tied to your stone, and did not ask again.
- The sung count was six nights, with the Lotfolk counters, who have come back, counting the singers. The elders have stopped complaining and have taken to correcting the singers' order.
- Dim nights in the eleventh and twelfth months.

## Year 28

- Locusts on the Tallgrass, the Ruun traders say, grass eaten to the root across the plateau, and the Ruun have driven their cattle down into the Greel lowland, where the murrain was, and are camped along the river in their thousands. The Greelmen are selling them grain at any price. The caravans came to the autumn market by the north way for fear of the cattle.
- Hounds on the Backs all year. Ferrow Ford has lost sixty lambs and a dog, and the shepherds will not stay up at the Crown Fold overnight. A hunt went up with nets and spears in the fifth month and saw hounds at every skyline and caught nothing and lost two more dogs.
- Lotfolk traders say their god has doubled its counters and every house of the Lotfolk now recites the count every night, the names on the wall, the pins on the cliff, the ones who were lost. The traders say this as if it were the weather.
- Harl at the Greyholt wall again, trading, for the first time since the burning. They say the Longhand clan went north to Brude's folds and that their cairn is a pit with nothing in it and that their dead are not avenged, and they left whey, and Greyholt took it.
- The Lissom pools are all nine still, each with its name, and the plum pressing in the tenth month smelled through the village as it does, two plums.
- Dim nights in the first month, the eleventh, and the twelfth.

## Year 29

- A Greyholt girl of twelve made the walk to Vell alone with a goat and back and was given a river stone for it at Ferrow Ford, though it is not a ford, and the elders could not agree whether that is allowed.
- The Rooks' longest night drew less than a quarter of Scarrow, and Aldo Rook, who is sixty, has given the hill to Dagg and sits on Scarrow's stone and corrects the singers in Scarrow's order with the other elders.
- Hounds on the Backs. Dun Mere's steadings on the Backs' southern end have lost sheep now too, and the Dun Mere people say the hounds came because the valley killed the grey beast, and the Ferrow Ford people say they came because the valley stole the Harl's stone and the Harl sent them.
- A Greelman boat was lost in the Brann mere in the autumn fog, two men, and the Greelmen say the fen has been wrong since the storm.
- Ruun traders say the Ember has made the Gathering three nights now, the Song is so long, and that she sings the eleven bands who followed the Voice in their old places as if they had never left and sings the Voice last among the dead, every year, and the Ruun go quiet for it.
- The Lotfolk counters have asked again for the Olm god's answer to the letter, and cast lots about whether to wait another year, and are waiting.
- Dim nights in the second month and the twelfth.

## Year 30. The 341st year after the Driving

- Lotfolk traders say the lake people in the north-west, their own families among them, have begun walking into the lake in their sleep, one or two a month, and are not found, and that their god has cast lots about it and the lots said leave them, and the traders say the lake is a god and takes its tithe.
- The sung count was seven nights. A Greyholt child, correcting an elder's order in the singing, said "thee" to him before the whole meadow, and the meadow laughed, because only Greyholt says it.
- Hounds on the Backs for a fourth year. The high folds are empty and the Backs' heather has grown over the fold walls and the bees are thick in it and the honey is dark.
- Harl traders say Brude is dead and his son leads, and that the Harl have no cairn and no stone-men and sleep badly, and that they want the Greyholt girl back for the Harl boy, and Greyholt is thinking about it.
- The Lotfolk traders, asked after their god, said it has been casting lots at the Bowl every night this year and that the stones have begun to fall as it says they will, which has never happened, and that the counters are frightened and the god is not.
- The kneelers at the bend are a dozen old people now, on the longest night only, and the children call it the weeping, and do not know what it is for.
- Dim nights in the first month, the eleventh, and the twelfth.
