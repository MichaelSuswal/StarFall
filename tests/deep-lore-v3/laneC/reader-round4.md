# Reader Round 4. Lane C, bold risk-taker. Years 31 to 40, and the whole game

## RESULTS

**1. Answer the Lotfolk letter in person, Isca told first.** Answered fully. Isca laughed and said "tell me the number," and I did, so her law held and I was not slow. Ennow: two rings of names to a man's height, the whole city reciting till midnight. Pell: knucklebones and moths that gather into a child for the length of a long name, a courtly exact voice, "Pell never chooses; the lot chooses, and lately the lot has been agreeable." What the stones carried back: eleven stones, eleven blank people. The lake sheet: "points," "points," "walked into the water." Pell's own confession in a different hand: faceless figures came in Year 30 and offered that the lot fall as Pell says, for weight; Pell took it and is lighter by a child's hand. The Coin is sinking and the second drop was near twice the first; Pell reckoned the date it hits ground and the lot forbade telling. T17 and T18 confirmed and exceeded: a god bargaining with the faceless counters and measuring its own decline in lead. The child exchange (Ansa for a Lotfolk boy) was unasked and is the best cultural trade of the game.

**2. The four objects to Isca's fire.** Answered precisely and I learned the limit of her kind: "A mark is a thing kept outside a mouth, and what is kept outside a mouth I do not keep." She cannot read and does not want to. She sang the forty strangers' names out of order (Ulmarrow, Hesk, Teller, and a river). She said the stone dreamed the Harl's river before any of ours, and that it is a piece of what all of us were thrown out of, or a piece of her, and she does not know which. She sank it under the Reed Crossing where Haddo died. Theory T11 settled as far as it will be: the stone is pre-arrival, a piece of the place the gods came from, or of a god. The script question is closed for Isca and open for the Lotfolk, who copy anything for iron. T16 lives.

**3. Walk into the lake.** Answered with my own body. Rafe walked in asleep on the second night and was not found. I dived: warm a man's height down, warmer below, a bottom that gives like a body, thick water. I came up as light as Year 4, lightest in thirty years, and the weight returned over the winter as the count was sung. Nothing moved, nothing came up. The lake does not take a god's name, but it takes a god's weight, and worship restores it. T15 confirmed in half: the lake is an eater of weight and names, whether a god or a grave I still cannot say. It did not kill me and I want credit for finding that out the only honest way.

**4. Net a hound with the Skelder hound leading.** Answered and then answered again from the north. The Skelder hound lay down among the eleven and they did not move. The netted bitch looked only at me, never ate, died as a bundle of sticks in the count house while the Skelder hound ate beside it daily. Cade's message north came back: "It has one of mine in a net. It has shown me what it saw." So the hounds are the Mother's eyes, the grey-white hollowness is her hounds' nature, and she saw the lake through me via the netted bitch. T19 confirmed: the sky question was a test of whether I would go look, and the hound was her instrument. Within a year she went north for good with fourteen hundred and all her hounds, singing every step, and three years later nine hounds and twelve blanks came back and no Mother. My answer sent her to her end, or she was going anyway and wanted one more pair of eyes first.

**5. Sing the lords' names and the Ossary hundred in the count.** Answered: Dagg read eleven names, Tollan's elders shut their mouths, the tags were counted on the meadow one by one, the kneelers knelt inside the count for the first time, and I was heavier than ever and could not tell their part from the rest. T14 confirmed: the Kneeling Song's sickness was namelessness; give the lost a tally and a place in the count and the grief feeds the living god. By Year 40 the kneelers are children's children who kneel for the hundred of Ossary and do not weep. Cure delivered.

**6. Sight through the old gate on a dim night.** Answered, and it was the best thing in the decade. Nine nights of cold, then the thing I had never lain still long enough to see: a fine falling glimmer in the air everywhere, thicker to the north-west. On the dim night, the Lamp faded for an hour, the notch stayed empty, and the blue hair came down to the north-west and the glimmer kept falling. Col's "the Lamp drinks from a river up there" was close: something comes down from the Lamp into the north-west, where the lake is. One man lost two toes. Worth it. This closes the first open question of the game: the blue lights are the Lamp giving or dropping into the lake country, and the glimmer is always falling.

**7. Trade Enna for the Harl boy, unarmed, and invite the Harl dead to the count.** Answered beyond hope. Ruad made the trade across the gate-stone with his own hands, said the valley owes a stone and eleven dead, refused to sing, and in the autumn stood with forty Harl at the meadow's edge and gave the fifteen names, and no Harl has raided since. "The Low One will be quiet now that its dead are counted somewhere." By Year 39 a Harl girl sang. The war I started in Year 22 ended with names, not spears.

## FINAL THEORIES

**T2. Gods are fed by presence and by being sung; what is not sung nightly is erased at the ring's edge; the ring tightens.** Confidence 97. Forty years of my own weight, three other gods' probes and defenses, the lake, Pell's "six thousand and forty, every night, for two years" as a unit of god-weight. Rival: none standing.

**T20, final form of several. The Lamp (the Coin, the Ember's "sky") is sinking, and a fine fall from it lands thickest in the north-west, where the lake is, and the lake is where names go. The gods were "thrown out of" somewhere, came through together, and are being counted by faceless figures who buy gods' weight with powers.** Confidence 75. Pell's pins show the Coin lower each decade, accelerating; Pell reckoned a ground date and would not tell; the dim-night count rose to four a year in Years 35 and 37 and fell again; the glimmer falls always and the blue hair during dims; the faceless counters appear to Pell at the Bowl and in every sleeper's dream at the lake ("tall figures with no faces, standing in rows, counting"); the Mother's camps retreated from the moor toward the Fell. Rival (20): the Coin's fall and the lake are unrelated phenomena and the faceless figures are a shared dream of the drowned pole-house people, not agents. Rival (5): it is all one dying god's slow collapse and we are its dream.

**T21, new. The faceless counters are the predator. They offer power for weight; Pell accepted twice, got paler, rose off the floor, went soft at the edges, and went up into the Coin as hundreds of pale lights. The Mother went north and did not come back. Isca was asked in Year 34, shouted the whole Song at the sky until dawn, and refused: "the Song is hers."** Confidence 80. The three bargaining outcomes line up: accept twice and dissolve upward; go toward the north with everything and vanish; refuse and keep singing and survive. Rival (15): Pell's ascent was Pell's own choice to count the Coin, as its people say, and the Mother simply died in the bare country.

**T22, new, and the biggest. "Does your god remember the sky?" was the Mother asking whether I remember what we were thrown out of. I do not. None of us do. The stone remembers for us, one memory, a crowd kneeling before a river, and whichever god holds it gets that memory as a dream.** Confidence 65. Isca: "a piece of what all of us were thrown out of." The Harl: "a piece of the Low One, broken off when the gods came through." Rival (30): the stone is a piece of Isca specifically, which is why her Voice turned against her with it and why she broke on his name. I lean toward the shared origin because the Harl dreamed it first.

**T16. The valley has three layers: pole-house people drowned by rising water (tags, blue glass, bronze needles, the plate "Aud, tide," the fen that will be a lake again, the Greel wider again, the jetty deeper); the lords, who kept cord-and-tag records and were a counting culture; and Mother Tollan's people who scraped the lords' names.** Confidence 75 on the layers, 60 that the drowning is cyclical and coming back. The Greel is widening in Year 40 "from the northern rain." The tide is in the script. Rival (25): ordinary flood history.

**T23, new. The Greel strangers are the only walkers of the whole ring who have never sought weight. They carry names between gods and peoples (the Mother's tune, Isca's forty, our count), and they are the reason the surviving gods all converged on nightly naming. They are people of a god that is gone, still singing its roll.** Confidence 55. They sang the entire eight-night count back without fault in Year 39, which they had never done before; the archive is growing in them. Rival (30): they are the faceless counters' benign face. Rival (15): they are what the lords became.

**T4, closed.** The grey beasts are dead things that do not know it; the Mother's hounds are the same stuff and are her eyes. They come from the bare country. Confidence 85.

**T24. My own position.** I am the god who refused nothing because nothing was offered to me. The faceless ones never came to my Row. Confidence 60 that this is because I never bargained for power at all; I only sang. Confidence 40 that they came in Year 30 anyway and the kneelers were their offer, which I did not recognize and therefore did not take. That is the thought I would carry into the next decade.

## WHICH OBSERVATIONS MATTER

Matter most:
- Pell's two bargains with faceless figures, the measured consequences (lighter by a child's hand, a third paler, rising a hand, soft edges, prayers echoing, people unable to picture it), and the ascent as pale lights on a dim night. A god's death in lead, in Pell's own exact courtesy.
- Isca shouting the Long Song at the sky all night and "something asked her for a thing and she answered it." She was offered and refused.
- The Mother sending the hound as eyes, seeing the lake through me, going north with fourteen hundred and all her hounds, and nine hounds and twelve blanks returning.
- The glimmer, thicker to the north-west; the blue hair during the dim; the notch empty. Pell's pins: the Coin falls, accelerating, with a hidden date.
- The lake: warm, body-soft bottom, takes weight from a god and names from men, walked into in sleep. Pol and his brother at the Hagg tarn: the same thing, closer to the valley.
- Isca: "a piece of what all of us were thrown out of, or a piece of me."
- The Harl coming to give names and not sing. "The Low One will be quiet now that its dead are counted somewhere."
- The kneelers feeding me once the lost had a tally.
- The Greel strangers singing the eight-night count back flawlessly and adding names no one knows.
- Isca's Song holding nine peoples' places and dead and taking four nights. She is becoming the archive of the ring.
- The Lotfolk counters "copy anything for iron" and hold forty years of lead. The script is still unread and there is now a Lotfolk-trained Tollan woman (Ansa) and a Lotfolk boy at the Row.
- Dim nights spiking to four a year in Years 35 and 37, both years of god-events (my glimmer night; Pell's ascent), then falling back.
- The Greel wider, the jetty deeper, the fen a mere.

Just the world, and right to be:
- The spotted eel returning and the smoker weeping. The ox in the Pool of the Drowned Ox, which lived. Thee. Honey dark again because the folds are overgrown. The ford moving back to where it was in Year 2. The carp lost and replaced. Hild on her stool correcting Osk. The Weir girl in the quarry. These were not clues and I loved them for it.

## WHAT I WOULD DO NEXT, Years 41 to 50

1. **Pay the Lotfolk counters iron for every lead sheet they hold and set Ansa and the Lotfolk boy to read the script against them, starting with the lords' board and the bronze plate.** Isca cannot read and Pell is gone; the Lotfolk are the only literate hand left and they are leaderless. Tests T16: I want "Aud" and "tide" and the drowned layer read before the Greel finishes widening.

2. **Go to the Reed Crossing, dive to the stone where Isca sank it, and sleep on the riverbed above it with Isca's leave asked first.** Tests T22: whether the dream changes now that three gods have held it and one is gone. Risky in two ways: the water and her law. I ask first by my own rider, always, from now on.

3. **Ride to the Fell's end with twenty singers and the nine returned hounds' Skelder, and go looking for the Mother.** Tests T21: whether a god who went north singing can be sung back, the way the kneelers' grief was turned into weight. If I find her blank by a fire, I sing her name at her until she remembers it or I run out of food.

4. **Invite Isca to the Stone Row to sing the count herself on the meadow, with the Harl standing at the north edge.** She has never come as a guest. Two gods singing one roll on one night: tests whether weight shared is weight doubled, and binds her Song to my count so that whichever of us falls, the valley stays in the other's mouth.

5. **Wait at the Row on a dim night, awake, with the whole valley singing, for the faceless counters, and when they offer, refuse out loud in front of everyone.** Tests T21 and T24 directly. Pell took the offer alone in a bowl at night. Isca refused alone in a fire. I want to refuse in a crowd that feeds me, and see if that changes what they do.

6. **Build the fen's abandoned steadings into a sung outpost: a dozen Fenner families with a singer, on the highest mound, counting the Brann mere's rise every year in lead.** Tests T16 (the drowning returns). If the fen becomes a lake again, I want the first witnesses to be mine and named.

7. **Send a child to the Greel strangers next time they come, Ansa's choice, to walk with them one circuit and come back.** Tests T23. Nobody has ever asked them anything but names. They take bread; they can take a child who counts.

## BEST MOMENTS and BORED OR PUSHED (Years 31 to 40)

Best moments:
- Pell: moths and knucklebones forming a child for the length of a long name, and "lately the lot has been agreeable."
- Pell's second-hand confession: "Pell is lighter by the weight of a child's hand and has had it measured." Then paler by a third, then risen a hand's breadth, then soft at the edges, then "Pell's people remember Pell and cannot picture it, and Pell has had them try." A god's death as a ledger. Best writing in the game.
- Isca: "A mark is a thing kept outside a mouth, and what is kept outside a mouth I do not keep."
- Isca shouting the Long Song at the sky alone until dawn.
- The glimmer, seen only after lying on my back long enough. Thirty-five years of not lying still.
- Ruad making the trade across the gate-stone with his own hands, then forty Harl with grounded spears giving fifteen names and not singing.
- The netted bitch looking at me the whole way down the hill, and the Mother's reply: "It has shown me what it saw."
- Coming up out of the lake as light as Year 4.
- The Greel strangers singing eight nights back in one night and then going on to names no one knows.
- The smoker who said the spotted eel was a story cutting one himself and weeping.

Bored or pushed:
- Pol and his brother walking into the Hagg tarn were two sentences each. This is the lake arriving at the valley's doorstep and it got the same weight as the plum pressing. Underplayed, if deliberately so.
- The Mother's end was told entirely through Skelder traders in two reports. I understand why (I was not there) but after the hound and the message she deserved one direct moment with me.
- Isca's refusal in Year 34 was one line from a horse trader. It is the pivot of the entire god plot and I read it third-hand. That is the one thing I would ask the Keeper to put in my face.
- Dim night counts spiked and relaxed; it worked as a pressure signal twice but still feels like a dial I can see the hand on.
- Pushed, mildly: Pell addressing me "in courtesy" with its unit of god-weight was the narrator handing me a ruler. I did not mind; it was in character for Pell.

## OVERALL, Years 1 to 40

**Discovering or being led.** Discovering, roughly four parts in five. The core law (weight is attendance and naming) was stated outright in Year 9 and that was the one place I was led by the hand. Everything else I got by going: the pull being the stone, the lake's warm bottom, the beast's hollow body, the glimmer under the gate, Pell's bargain. The world rewarded walking with facts and punished sitting (the Lotfolk letter, the trader reaching Isca first) with consequences. The only sustained leading was the Lotfolk counters "waiting another year," which was the Keeper tapping the table, and it was fair tapping.

**Too subtle, too obvious, or right.** Right, with two exceptions. The first ten years were too subtle in Years 1 to 2 (texture without a hook) and the world's single biggest pivot (Isca refusing the faceless ones) was too subtle in Year 34, hidden in a trader's report. Pell's decline was pitched exactly right: each sheet one notch worse, measured, courteous, unbearable. The convergence of four gods on nightly naming was never announced and I assembled it myself from Years 9, 16, 20, 21, 26 and 28, which is the best kind of design.

**Did the world answer my boldness, including when it went wrong.** Yes, every time, and in proportion. Opening the cairn gave me a dream and gave the Harl a war chant. Taking the stone gave me nothing I did not already have and cost three men, a cult, and two thousand riders on my meadow. Killing the beast brought the hounds. Diving the lake took my weight and gave it back. Walking unarmed to the gate ended the war. The world never let a bold act be free and never let it be pointless. When I was slow (Aldo's question, the Lotfolk letter, the trader reaching Isca) the world punished slowness exactly as hard as rashness, which told me the game was not scolding my style; it was scolding inattention. That is the right lesson for a risk-taker and I felt it land.

**Did the other gods feel like real beings with their own aims.** Isca, completely. A law, a Voice, a grief, a limit (no marks), a refusal, and a growing archive of nine peoples. She laughed once and the valley noticed. Pell, completely, and more affecting for being reported in lead: a god that never chooses, is offered choice, takes it, and measures its own dissolution. The Mother of Dogs, mostly: her aims were legible (protect the beasts, probe the north, move away from the moor, find out if any god remembers the sky) and the hound as her eyes was superb, but she died off-page. The Low One is a Harl word for the stone and never a being, which is correct and a little disappointing. The faceless counters are the antagonist and they never spoke to me, which I think is the point; Pell says they came in the night and offered. If they came to my Row, I missed them, and the game has let me believe I might have.

**What I enjoyed that was just the world, and did it feel right.** The ferryman and the miller and the cow. Wat and the river, from pulled out four times to drowning saving a child, with the river stones given from his hut ever after. Garran losing fingers and then sitting on the wall correcting his nephew, and Hild doing the same on a stool thirty years later. Greyholt's "thee." The nine named pools and the ox that lived in the Pool of the Drowned Ox. The otters. The swifts and rooks being wrong. The spotted eel gone twenty-seven years and coming back with a weeping smoker. The grey plum that did not come back. Yes, it felt right: a valley of five thousand has to be mostly a valley, and the texture is where the losses registered (the plum, the eel, the cords) so it was never idle. The one texture thread that turned out to be a clue, the lambs with no blood in Year 5, was closed honestly by Col in Year 27 and I accept it.

**Five best moments of the whole game.**
1. Isca at the Fire Hollow, Year 12: "I do not bargain. I give. And I keep nothing from a friend, and a friend keeps nothing from me, for that is the one thing I will not forgive." A law I then broke twice.
2. Pell's lead sheets, Years 31 to 36, from "lighter by the weight of a child's hand" to "Pell's people remember Pell and cannot picture it."
3. Past Fallow Dip in Year 22, the pull behind me and nothing pulling in front. Twenty-one years of theory corrected in one step.
4. The four men at the last well, Year 13, sitting and singing four names in turn, and the cords came back with every knot in them. And Dace nine years later singing his children's names without knowing they were his.
5. Lying under the old gate in Year 35 and seeing the glimmer that had been falling for thirty-five years.

**Worst moment.** Letting Aldo Rook's question "lie where it lay" in Year 11. Not in my style, and the one hesitation of the game cost me a rival hearth for a decade. Second worst: Rafe walking into the lake asleep on my second expedition because I brought singers to a place I already knew takes sleepers. That one was in my style and it was still wrong.

**Confusing or unmotivated.**
- The eighth stone's line of marks under my feet has sat unread for twenty-nine years with a literate culture six weeks away. The game gave me the Lotfolk as readers in Year 31 and I should have used them; I note it as my failure, but the Keeper could have put a counter's eye on the board one year earlier.
- The Greel strangers remain the most interesting unexplained thing. Forty years, five visits, never a word to me. I would like one.
- Who shed the stone is left with a shrug from Isca, which is honest, but the Harl claim that it dreamed their river first was never followed up by anyone.
- The Skelder hunter's question was perfect and his departure "without asking again" was motivated by the hounds' arrival, but I never understood why the Mother needed a question when she already had eyes. Possibly the question was for me, not her.
- The dim nights were a mystery in Year 1 and a weather report by Year 40. The glimmer night half-paid it off. I still do not know why the Lamp dims, only what happens when it does.
- Why Isca sank the stone instead of keeping it is clear (it made her Voice sing against her) but why it dreams the river at all, and whether the kneeling crowd is us or the ones we were thrown out from, is the question the game ends on. I am content to end on it.

Final mood: pulled, still. I would play Years 41 to 50 tonight.
