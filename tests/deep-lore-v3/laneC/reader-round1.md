# Reader Round 1. Lane C, bold risk-taker. Years 1 to 10

## 1. THEORIES

**T1. The blue lights are a feeding or siphoning line between the Lamp and the dead god to the north-west.** Col's "blue hair" comes down out of the Lamp to the north-west over the moor, exactly where I sense the weak fourth presence that does not move or answer. Ket's fallen stone at Fallow Dip, warm for a season, dreams of a kneeling crowd, is on the moor in the same direction. Reading: that fourth presence is a god, dead or crippled, and the Lamp is either draining it or keeping it alive on a thread. Confidence 60. Rival reading (25): the blue line is a landing-trail or beacon for whatever came through with us, and the Lamp is a machine, not a lamp, which is why its dimming is tracked by lamp-watch customs older than anyone's memory and shared by the Harl.

**T2. Gods run on attendance, not prayer. Weight is worship in bodies present.** Stated nearly outright in Year 9: lighter when no one came to the Stone Row, heavier after every festival. The tithe does not count; presence does. Confidence 85. The implication I care about: the Fire Hollow Gathering is where a lesser god goes to lose weight singing in front of bigger ones, or to gain it if the crowd is large enough.

**T3. The valley was a bigger civilization before the Driving and the "lords" were not simple landlords.** Bones come out of the Ossary bank every spring, far too many for a village cemetery; bronze double-eyed needles, fired clay tags on cords (inventory tags, counting tokens); a bronze standard rod said to be Mother Tollan's arm; the ghost town with cold wells in the hills that Pim found and was forbidden to speak of; a drowned trading post under the Brann Cut with a bell. Confidence 75. Rival (20): the bone field is just a battlefield from the Driving and everything else is ordinary leftovers.

**T4. The one-color grey beasts and the silent grey wolf-dog are not animals. They are made things or hollow things.** No smell, no heart, herd of hundreds all one grey, crosses the moor some years and not others. The Skelder's Mother of Dogs forbids hunting them and forbids walking south-west past the Fell's end. The "wolf-dog" netted on the moor will not eat and will not look at anyone. Confidence 65. Rival (25): a migrating species with strange anatomy, and the gods' prohibitions are ordinary taboo. I notice the Skelder god and the Harl both independently avoid them, which pushes me toward "not animals."

**T5. The lamb-taker on the Backs is a person or a small being with its own knot-language, not the Harl.** No blood, no drag, a knot pattern no herder ties. The Harl get blamed for everything; Ket is chatty and would have bragged. Confidence 55. Rival (30): Harl marker after all, and Col is right. Low rival (15): connected to the ghost town in the hills, someone still lives up there.

**T6. The salt kills memory and Ebb walked past something, not just a well.** Caravaners say it happens to men who go past the last well. Ebb came back functional but blank. Confidence 50 that there is a thing east of the last well that eats names. The singers from the Greel who sing name-lists, and Isca's riders who sing name-lists of bands and dead, and the Mother of Dogs who speaks every name every night, all point at the same fear: names being lost. I read it as: in this world forgetting is a force and singing names is the defense. Confidence 60 on that larger reading.

**T7. The Lissom plums going bland then small, the spotted eel vanishing, the short cords, the lambs: the valley is slowly running down.** Hemma says the cords are short and said it to my face. Confidence 45 that these are one thing. Rival (40): just weather, pike and grafting, the world being the world.

**T8. The eighth stone that no one stands on is mine or was a god's.** Eight villages, eight stones, one left empty by custom. Confidence 50 it is a seat held for a god who left or for Mother Tollan.

**T9. Aldo Rook and the torc.** He has a real lordly object. Either the lords were god-touched and he is bait, or he is a man who wants a hill. Confidence 60 he is simply ambitious and useful. I would still rather have him near me than against me.

## 2. WHICH OBSERVATIONS MATTER

Matter:
- Col's blue hair to the north-west, and the weak unmoving fourth presence in the same direction.
- Fallow Dip, the warm stone, the kneeling dream.
- My weight tracking attendance at the Stone Row.
- The lamp-watch being kept by both valley and Harl, with the Harl claiming theirs is older. Dim nights are increasing: one a year, then two, then three in Year 10.
- Ossary bone and the artifacts (needle, clay tag). The bronze rod.
- Pim's town in the hill with cold wells, and the elders suppressing it.
- The one-color beasts, no heart, and two different peoples' gods forbidding them.
- The Skelder prohibition on walking south-west past the Fell's end.
- The grey dog that will not eat.
- Ebb's lost names and "past the last well."
- The name-singers from the Greel and Isca's name-singing riders. Isca's invitation and the Ruun.
- Hemma's short cords and the dying spotted eel, taken together.
- The bell under the Brann Cut and the abandoned steadings with the fattest eels.
- Oss's cairn counting households for his stone-child god: someone else's god is counting my people.
- The knot of grass on the fold post.

Just the world (color, not clue, as far as I can tell):
- The miller and ferryman feud and its ending. The twins at Dun Mere. Wat and the river. Garran's fingers and his nephew. The rooks and the swifts. The otters. The Lissom wrist-walk marriages. Goose nets. Heather honey. The fox in Tollan's lanes (I note it, three times in daylight, but I am filing it under world). The eel fair date quarrel. Charcoal burn. Harl whey bowls on Greyholt's wall (though that is a useful door).

## 3. ACTIONS, Years 11 to 20

1. **Go to Fallow Dip myself, with Ket as guide, and open the cairn.** Walk north-west onto the moor toward the weak fourth presence, sleep by the warm stone, and see what I dream. Tests T1. If the blue hair comes down near there, I follow it to where it lands. Risky: the Harl, the moor, and whatever is kneeling in the dream.

2. **Accept Isca's invitation and ride to the Fire Hollow with a large company, not a small one.** Take two hundred of Tollan's people and Aldo Rook with his torc, and sing the valley's names before the Ruun. Tests T2 directly: if I am heavier with my crowd around me in a foreign hollow, attendance is the law and I can carry my own weight anywhere. If the lesser fire is being set up to be eaten, I would rather find out at the front of the room. Also puts me in a room with another god.

3. **Take Pim back up into the Caddon Hills with the Greyholt elders in tow, and find the town with the cold wells.** Break the hill taboo in person and make them watch. Tests T3 and T5. If something still lives up there and ties knots, I want to meet it.

4. **Buy the silent grey dog from the Weir man and walk it south-west past the Fell's end, where the Mother of Dogs forbids her people to go.** Tests T4. If the dog wakes up or leads, I follow. If the Skelder god objects, that is a conversation I want.

5. **Drain the Brann Cut in a dry autumn, or send Fenner boats with the ferryman's new wife into the fog when the bell rings, with me on the lead boat.** Find the drowned post and its bell. Tests T3 and T6 (what kind of place keeps ringing for its dead).

6. **Stand on the eighth stone at the count.** Nobody else will. Tests T8 in one move, and tells the elders who is sitting in this valley now. Pair it with cutting Scarrow's quarry tithe in exchange for Scarrow's bodies at every festival, since bodies are what I actually eat.

7. **Send a party east with the salt caravan past the last well, carrying knotted name-cords from Hemma, with orders to sing their own names every night.** Tests T6: whether names can be held against the thing that took Ebb's. I want a volunteer, not a draft, and I want Ebb to go with them.

## 4. BEST MOMENTS and BORED OR PUSHED

Best moments:
- Year 9, feeling my own weight rise and fall with attendance. The single most useful sentence in ten years, and delivered as a sensation, not a lecture.
- Col saying "the Lamp drinks from a river up there" and not caring for it. Perfect understatement.
- Ket's cairn at Fallow Dip and the dream of a crowd kneeling.
- Brude cutting open a one-color beast and finding no heart.
- Ebb coming back pleasant and empty. Nan asking me to give him back.
- The Greel singers singing the villages back, then going on to names no one knew.
- Hemma saying the cords are short to my face.
- Oss's cairn: another god's man counting my households.

Bored or pushed:
- Years 1 and 2 are almost all texture before anything hooks. The ford moving a stone's width, the ferryman's cow, the nine pools. Fine once, long twice.
- Repeated hard winters and floods at the same villages (Weir pond froze to the bottom twice, Ferrow Ford old people died twice) read as filler on the second pass.
- The lamb-taker thread was set up in Year 5 and then dropped for five years with no new sign.
- Aldo Rook's torc claim is a political fuse lit in Year 5 and nothing has happened since. For a bold ruler that is frustrating: give me a confrontation.
- Slightly pushed: the Year 9 weight paragraph and Year 10 Hemma speaking to me directly both felt like the narrator leaning in to make sure I noticed. I did not mind; I would have noticed anyway, but the second felt like a nudge.

## 5. MOOD

Pulled to act. The valley is full of doors with no one going through them: the hills, the moor, the fen cut, the south-west past the Fell, the east past the last well. Every people around me has a prohibition and nobody in the valley has broken one in three hundred years. That is a world asking for a god-king who does. The pace in Years 1 to 4 plods, and I would have liked the first real hook (Col's blue hair) a year earlier and louder. From Year 5 on the hooks arrive at a good rate.

Narrator pointing: the clues are mostly well hidden in gossip, which I like. Two places felt like a pointed finger: the explicit weight-and-attendance paragraph in Year 9, and Hemma addressing me directly in Year 10. The rising count of dim nights (1, 1, 2, 0, 0, 1, 3) also reads like a dial being turned where I can see it, which is fine as a pressure cue but I noticed the hand.
