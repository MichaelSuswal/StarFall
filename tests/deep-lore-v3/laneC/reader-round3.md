# Reader Round 3. Lane C, bold risk-taker. Years 21 to 30

## RESULTS

**1. Open the sealed cistern at Vell with Orm's hands.** Answered. A dry chamber with shelves of grey dust in coiled-cord shapes, each row with a clay tag, two hundred or so, the same tags the Ossary bank gives up; a stone box of lead sheets scratched like a tally. Orm's family were the lords' stewards, which no one said before and everyone says now. Theory (T3, T9 memory war) confirmed in shape: the predecessors kept records in cords with tags, the same way Hemma did, and someone stored the cords here deliberately. The cords rotted; the tags and lead survived and nobody reads them. The world is rubbing my face in it: objects survive, meaning does not. Clear answer, and it put a steward's line in Greyholt that did not exist a year before.

**2. Confront Aldo Rook on Lord's Hill for the board.** Answered, and better than I deserved. He cannot read it, gave it, and spun losing it as justice. The board sits against the eighth stone; its marks match the line under my feet, the Vell tags, and the chamber signs. Hild sang a line for the scraped lords, Tollan's elders choked on it, a third of the Rooks' following came down to the meadow, and by Year 29 the Rooks' night draws less than a quarter and Aldo sits on Scarrow's stone correcting singers. Theory right: out-lord him by taking the past into my hearth. The rival festival is dying of neglect. Best political result of the game.

**3. Confess the river to Isca.** Answered with a fire twice its height and a word: slow, not kept. Then she told me the river is not mine: she has dreamed it since the forty strangers sang it in Year 8, and it is in the Song where she put it. T13 (she becomes an enemy over a secret) was right in law and I survived it by being first with my own mouth. But I got it wrong in Year 26: a trader told her the stone was under my eighth stone before I did, and she came with two thousand riders, stood on my stone as fire all night, took the stone, burned Fallow Dip and eleven Harl, and promised the next thing she learns from a trader she will learn as fire. The lesson was stated exactly: a bold confession once bought me nothing permanent; her law is continuous, not a one-time toll. I am now bound to report to her, and I have been slow twice.

**4. North-west past the lake with the four blank men, singing the valley nightly.** Answered richly. The pull I had felt for twenty-one years was Fallow Dip, not beyond it; past the hollow there was nothing pulling at all. So T1 was wrong about a fourth god in the north-west: the fourth presence was the stone, all along. The lake: flat grey water, warm bottom, no ripple in wind; mound-dwellers who point instead of naming and whose grandparents had names "the water did not want"; the Lotfolk children pointing; a shared dream of faceless tall figures in rows, counting, and houses on poles over bright shallow water. Beyond: one grass, one brown bird, no track. The singing worked: only two men lost small things, the four blanks lost nothing more and now sing the valley flawlessly, including Dace singing his own children's names without knowing they are his. T2 confirmed at the hardest edge, with a bitter corollary: singing preserves the words, not the knowing.

**5. Kill a grey beast under the old gate.** Answered, and the valley watched. Meat grey through, no heart, no liver, no gut, no fat, no smell, no rot. "A beast that has been dead a long time and does not know it." T4 confirmed: not animals. Consequence: the grey-white hounds appeared on the Backs within two months and have not left in four years. The Skelder hunter left the moment they appeared, leaving the hound tied to my stone and his question unanswered. Dun Mere blames the killing, Ferrow Ford blames the stone theft. I think the Skelder knew the hounds would come, and the pup was meant to be my answer or my ward.

**6. Take the stone from Fallow Dip.** Answered with war. Two stone-men killed, Bran and two more of mine dead, Harl raids four times a year for three years, Greyholt walled, Ferrow Ford's folds moved off the Backs. The stone under my eighth stone spread the dream to a quarter of Tollan and made the kneelers, for whom I was no heavier. Then Isca took it and the dream stopped within a month. Theory T11/T12: the stone is a god-piece that broadcasts one memory and that memory is grief; confirmed. T11's rival (the stone IS the fourth presence) confirmed: the pull moved with the litter and now sits east and a little south with her. My boldest move was also my worst-costed: it bought me a dream I already had, a war, a cult, and a reason for Isca to stand on my stone.

**7. The sung count.** Answered, and it is the thing I will be remembered for. Hild and twenty-four singers; three nights, then four, six, seven. The biggest crowd the Row has ever held and I am heavier than any harvest festival. Elders who complained now correct the order. A Greyholt child said "thee" to an elder before the whole meadow. The Lotfolk letter quoted Hild's household number exactly. This is attendance and naming at once, which the whole world says is the law, and I built it from what the east expedition cost.

## THEORIES (updated)

**T2. Gods are fed by presence and naming; the world's edge erases what is not sung nightly.** Confidence 95. Only the mechanics left to learn. Rival (5): none worth stating.

**T11 revised. The Fallow Dip stone was the fourth presence: a shed piece of a god, cold, broadcasting one grief-memory to sleepers, and it was never mine.** Confidence 85. The pull moved with it. Isca says the river was in her Song from Year 8 via the Greel strangers. Whose god shed it? The Harl say the Low One. Isca now says, by acting, that it is hers to hold. Rival (35): it belongs to no living god; it is a piece of whatever was Driven or drowned, and both Isca and the Harl are claiming an orphan. Rival (20): it is Isca's own shed piece from the arrival, which is why it made her Voice sing against her and why she broke on his name.

**T14, new. The Kneeling Song is a god-sickness. It makes its singers leave their god.** Confidence 70. Haddo took it, walked out with eleven bands, and was burned with two hundred. Tollan's kneelers knelt "for the ones who were lost" and gave me no weight. It is a song of a worship that has no god left to receive it, and it pulls worship away from living gods into the void. Isca understood this before I did; that is why she stood in the fire all night and then killed her own Voice. Rival (30): Haddo's schism was ordinary ambition and the kneelers were grief contagion from a magic stone, nothing structural.

**T15, new. The lake in the north-west is a god or a god's grave that erases names and takes sleepers; the grey beasts and the hounds come from that country.** Confidence 65. Warm bottom, no ripple, shared dream of faceless counters and houses on poles, people walking into it in sleep, children who point. The bare country beyond has one grass and one bird: the same "one-color" quality as the beasts. Rival (25): the lake is simply inside the erasing edge and the dream is residue of the pole-house people who drowned there; the beasts are unrelated. Rival (10): the lake is the Low One.

**T16, new. The pre-Driving people, the lords, were record-keepers using cords and tags; the Driving was Mother Tollan's people destroying that memory; and the predecessors before them (the pole-house people, the bronze plate "Aud, tide" culture, the Ossary mass graves) were drowned by a rising sea or fen.** Confidence 60 on the first half (lords as keepers; stewards in Greyholt; scraped board). Confidence 50 on the drowning: the fen "was a lake and will be again," the Brann Cut is now a mere after the storm, the Year 19 shake, the greatest flood in Year 26 giving up a hundred graves in rows with blue glass and tags, the pole-house dream. Rival (30): separate layers, no single catastrophe.

**T17, new. The gods are all converging on the same defense (nightly naming) and the one who did it without a god is me.** Confidence 75. Isca's Song, the Mother's nightly tune (learned from the Greel strangers), the Lotfolk double wall and nightly recitation, my sung count. The Greel strangers seeded all of it. What they are, I still do not know. Confidence 40 that they are the remnant of a dead god's people walking the ring and reseeding memory.

**T18, new. The Lotfolk god's stones have started falling as it says, which means it is beginning to control chance, or chance is ending.** Confidence 40 that this matters hugely and 60 that it is the next thing that breaks. Rival: it is a frightened people's rumor.

**T1, dead.** The fourth presence was the stone. Buried.

**T9, Aldo.** Resolved. Confidence 85 the Rook line folds into the sung count within a decade. Dagg holds the hill and the silver and is the one who killed Tobin; watch him, do not fear him.

**T19, the Skelder question. "Does your god remember the sky?"** Confidence 60 that this asks whether I remember where I came from before the meadow, and that the Mother does not, and that a god who remembers the sky can do something the others cannot. The hound tied to my stone is a gift or a watch. The hunter left when the hounds appeared on the Backs, so the hounds are the Mother's, or she knew they would come when the grey beast died. Confidence 55 on that link.

## WHICH OBSERVATIONS MATTER

Matter most:
- The pull was the stone. Beyond it, nothing pulls. The map of gods I sensed in Year 1 had only three gods and one fragment.
- Isca's river claim from Year 8, the Greel strangers as its source, and her Voice singing against her. Her broken voice on Haddo's name.
- Isca's second law, stated plainly: next thing from a trader is fire. The Lotfolk counters are in my valley, counting everything, and report to a god who corresponds with gods by letter. Everything I do reaches her through them.
- The lake: warm bottom, pointing people, the shared dream of faceless counters and pole-houses, sleepwalkers.
- Dace singing his children's names without knowing they are his. Singing keeps words, not knowing.
- The grey beast's body and the hounds arriving two months later, never fewer than nine, silent.
- "Does your god remember the sky?" and the hound tied to my stone.
- The Lotfolk letter: a god proposing a count of gods, offering "the height of the Coin in thirteen years" and "what the stones carried back." I have ignored it for five years. That is slowness again.
- Lotfolk stones falling as the god says. Lake people walking into the lake asleep.
- Vell chamber: cord-dust and tags, Greyholt as stewards. Ossary's hundred graves with tags and blue glass. The board's marks matching the eighth-stone line. One script, four places, no reader.
- The Brann Cut became a mere. The fen "will be a lake again." The ground shook in Year 19.
- The sung count's weight: more than any harvest. Kneelers gave nothing.
- The old gate frames the Lamp a hand below empty sky at night. Nobody said why.
- Locusts on the Tallgrass and two thousand Ruun camped on the Greel, hungry, between me and Isca.

Just the world:
- Cade swimming the horse (good, but world). The Greyholt girl's river stone. Wat's death (world, but earned). Thee. Honey dark again. Plums. Otters not mentioned, thankfully.

## ACTIONS, Years 31 to 40

1. **Answer the Lotfolk letter in person: ride to the Bowl with Hild and the board, and trade my full measures for "what the stones carried back" and the height of the Coin.** Driven by Result 3 and the five ignored years. Also tell Isca first, by my own rider, before I go. Tests T17 and T18: I want to see a god that lives in a bowl and whose stones have started obeying it, and I want to know what its two hundred stones carried back from the bare country. Risk: giving a counting god every number I have.

2. **Carry the lords' board, a Vell lead sheet, an Ossary tag, and the bronze plate to Isca's fire and ask the Song whether it holds the script, and ask her whose piece the stone is.** Driven by Results 1, 2, 3. She holds the oldest names in the world; if anyone can sing the marks, her Song can. This fulfills her law (I bring her everything, unasked) and tests T11 and T16. Risk: she keeps the objects as she kept the stone. I accept that.

3. **Walk into the lake.** Driven by Result 4 and T15. Go back to the north-west with the hound from my stone and twenty singers, sleep on the mound shore, and when the dream comes, wade in to the warm bottom and dive. I am the one who is supposed to remember the sky. If the lake takes names, I will find out whether it can take a god's. Tests T15 and T19 at once. This is the most reckless thing I have proposed and it is the right test.

4. **Hunt the hounds with the Skelder's hound leading, up the Backs to the Crown Fold at night, with fifty men and nets, and take one alive.** Driven by Result 5. If it is a grey hollow thing it will die without eating like the Weir dog; if it is the Mother's, the Skelder pup will tell me by how it behaves. Tests T19 and T15's rival. Then send the Skelder hunter's answer north with a hunter of mine: "Yes. Tell her to ask me what I saw."

5. **Make the sung count sing the lords' names from the chamber bones, with Dagg Rook reading them from the stones over each lord, and sing the pole-house dead as "the ones who were lost" with their counted tags as the tally.** Driven by Result 2 and Result 7. Give the kneelers something to kneel to that is under my roof, and fold the Driving's victims into the count. Tests T14: if the kneelers' grief feeds me once it has names, the Kneeling Song's sickness was namelessness and I have the cure.

6. **Go to Hagg Ridge on a dim night and sight through the old gate's notch at the Lamp.** Driven by the Year 27 observation and the twenty-year lamp-watch mystery. The notch frames the Lamp a hand below empty sky; on a dim night I want to see what is in the notch when the Lamp fades. Low cost, high ceiling.

7. **Trade the Greyholt girl for the Harl boy myself at the Longhand gate, and invite Brude's son to the sung count to have his dead sung by name.** Driven by Result 6. I made the war; I end it by giving the Harl what they lost that matters in this world: their dead's names in a song. Their cairn is a pit and they sleep badly. Bold in the other direction: walk unarmed to the people I robbed.

## BEST MOMENTS and BORED OR PUSHED

Best moments:
- Past Fallow Dip, the pull behind me and nothing pulling in front. Twenty-one years of theory corrected by walking.
- Isca's fire twice its height and "it was not kept; it was slow, and I will call it slow." Then "I will not call it so twice." Two years later, two thousand riders. A god with a law that holds.
- Dace singing his own children's names in the valley song and not knowing they were his.
- The lake people pointing. "His grandparents had names and the water did not want them."
- Isca's voice breaking on Haddo's name in the Song, which the Ruun had never heard.
- The grey beast opened before all of Tollan. "A beast that has been dead a long time and does not know it."
- The Skelder hunter: "Does your god remember the sky?" Then leaving the hound tied to my stone the day the hounds came.
- Aldo cheered by his own half for losing the board.
- A child saying "thee" to an elder before the meadow.
- The Lotfolk god's stones beginning to fall as it says, and the counters frightened and the god not.

Bored or pushed:
- Harl raids ran five years as a list of months and villages. Three raids with faces would have beaten fifteen without.
- The hounds have been on the Backs four years with no event except lamb counts. Give me a hound at a window.
- The Lotfolk letter sat unanswered because the packet never gave me a moment that forced it, which is my fault as a player, but the counters "waiting another year" three times reads as the narrator tapping the table.
- The fen rising and the Brann Cut becoming a mere are planted and then left. The drowning theory is building in the background and I want it to surface.
- The dim night count has flattened at two to three and no longer feels like a dial. Either turn it or drop it.
- Slightly pushed: Isca's exact line about the trader was the narrator making sure I knew what I had failed to do. Fair, because I did fail. The Lotfolk letter quoting Hild's number exactly was pointed, but effective.
- The lamb-taker thread from Year 5 was finally closed by Col (it stopped the hard winter of Year 9 and never returned). Late but honest. I withdraw the complaint.

## MOOD

Pulled, and for the first time, chastened. This decade answered every bold move with a real consequence: the stone bought a war and a cult, the beast bought the hounds, the confession bought a lesson I then failed again. The two moves that paid cleanly were the two that gave rather than took: the sung count and taking the board into my hearth. The world is teaching me that in a place where memory is the only currency, the bold act is to sing, not to seize. I do not fully believe it yet, which is why Action 3 is walking into the lake.

Narrator pointing: Isca's trader line and the Lotfolk waiting were hands on the table. The lake sequence, the beast's body, and the pull moving east were earned by walking and were the best writing of the three blocks. The theories are converging and the world has stopped offering rival readings for the core law, which is right at Year 30. What is left open is whose stone it was, what the script says, what the lake is, who the Greel strangers are, and what the sky question means. That is the right amount left.
