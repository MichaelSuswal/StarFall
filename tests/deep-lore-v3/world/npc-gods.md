# The three NPC gods

Rolled with /tmp scratch script roll_gods.py, seed 20261115, on 2026-11-15. Rolls recorded exactly as they fell. The rubric forbids duplicate archetype and domain only; two gods rolled the same form (6) and the same flaw (6), and the duplicates are kept and reconciled differently in each. Nothing from the archetype table is named in play.

Per-star rolls for this world (from deep-lore where fixed, rolled here where not): phase 88 (late), ceiling 76, living radius 90 days, center body a lake, long-game presence 6 (all three), star content 3 (cities on stilts over a shallow sea), ascended temper 3 (mixed), Remainder 4 (fell in godless country), rain density 2 (one or two knotters in the region, a thin tradition in the south).

Power scale: a newly arrived god sits near 20. The ceiling is 76. The ascended begin speaking at about 61.

## God 1. Isca of the Long Song

**Rolls.** Leans: Mind 39, Body 4, Presence 61, Temper 90, Regard 99, Order 92, Making 85, Company 100 (75 plus 25), Appetite 21, Trust 46. Archetype seed 6 (Keeper only). Domain 15, song and memory. Form 11, shifting between two forms. Virtue 4, courage. Flaw 2, wrath. Wound 1, betrayed by one it made. Voice 4, courtly, formal, elaborate.

**Reconciled character.** A god of overwhelming force and no patience at all, who rules absolutely and keeps no law but her own, breaks what she does not love, and must have a crowd around her. Yet she is content: she does not hunger for more, she hungers for the crowd she has to be whole and singing. Her domain is memory, and her nature is to break, and the two meet in her one great making: the Long Song, which keeps everything and from which nothing can be removed. She reasons rather than tricks, and she is more revealed than hidden by a little, meaning she wants to be seen when she chooses and is otherwise simply absent. She is brave to the point of folly and angry to the point of ruin. Her courtly speech is the speech of a throne before a great assembly, elaborate, never private.

**Form.** Two forms. A woman a head taller than any mortal, crowned with a plain iron band, grey cords of hair to her feet that she wears knotted. And a standing fire the height of a hall, which speaks from inside itself. She passes between them when moved, and she is often moved. The Ruun say she is a woman by day and a fire by night, which is not true but is what they see.

**Wound.** She feels that something she raised with her own hands turned on her. She cannot say what. It makes her choose favorites and watch them for the first sign.

**Traits.** Self-named: "She forgets nothing." "She does not bargain." Keeper trait, known to her from arrival: she cannot end a song once begun. Any name she sings into the Long Song stays in it as long as she does, including names she comes to hate.

**Land and people.** The Tallgrass, a high plateau of long grass five weeks east-south-east of the Olm valley, across the slow Greel and a week of wet lowland. The Ruun: about 8,000 herders in forty bands who move their cattle across the plateau and meet once a year at the Fire Hollow for the Gathering. Before Isca they had band-stones, carved posts where each band's dead were named. Isca came down at the Fire Hollow on arrival night and the bands came to her within the year because she was impossible to ignore.

**Opening goals.** Have every band at the Gathering. Begin the Long Song, in which every band, camp, spring, and dead Ruun is named each year in order. Break the band-stones, because the dead belong in the Song now. Find the thing that pulls at her from the west, weaker than the other gods.

**Forty years if undisturbed.**
- Year 1. Comes down at the Fire Hollow. Thirty of forty bands come by autumn. First Gathering fire.
- Year 2. The Long Song begins: one night, all the Ruun names. It takes four hours. Each year it is longer.
- Year 3. The ten bands who kept their band-stones are told to break them. Eight do.
- Year 4. She goes as fire to the two who did not. The band of Orrin Three-Herds is burned at its camp, forty dead. The other band breaks its stones. She sings Orrin's forty dead into the Song that autumn, because she cannot not.
- Year 6. She chooses a boy singer, Haddo, twelve years old, who can hold the whole Song. She calls him the Voice and keeps him by her.
- Year 8. The Longwalkers come to the Tallgrass from the Olm valley. She sings against them all night, as a contest. She hears a river in their song that she dreams of. She lets them go unharmed and sings their river into her Song.
- Year 10. She sends a formal embassy to the Olm god: twelve riders, a herald, an invitation to the Gathering phrased as a summons. (See contact.)
- Year 12. Ruun bands that graze toward the plateau's eastern edge begin coming back having forgotten their own camps, but still able to sing the Song. She takes this as proof of her strength. It is proof of something else.
- Year 14. She sends Haddo west with a band to find the weak pull. He reaches the Harl moor in Year 15 and sleeps by the stone at Cold Hand. He dreams the kneeling crowd. He begins to sing it.
- Year 18. Haddo returns singing a new song, the Kneeling Song. Isca hears her own wound in it and does not know why she is angry.
- Years 20 to 24. Haddo's song gathers a following. In Year 24 he leaves the Gathering with eleven bands and holds his own fire at the Reed Crossing of the Greel.
- Year 25. She goes as fire to the Reed Crossing. Haddo dies with two hundred. She must sing his name into the Song. She sings it every year after, and the Ruun hear her voice change on it.
- Years 26 to 30. She blames the Harl and the stone for "making" Haddo. She rides west with two thousand Ruun, burns the Harl's cairn at the stone, kills the Harl stone-priests, and tries to break the stone as fire. It will not break. Her people who carry it dream.
- Year 32. She has the stone thrown into the Greel at the Reed Crossing, where Haddo died. It lies there in the mud.
- Year 34. Near 60 in power from the Gatherings, she begins to dream of faceless figures who offer her a way to make a song that cannot be forgotten, for "weight." She refuses in fury and shouts the Long Song back at them all night. They go quiet. She is content to stay where she is.
- Year 36. She concludes from the stone and from her eastern bands that what is sung stays and what is not sung goes. She sends singers to every people within reach to ask the names of their places and dead, to put them in the Song. The singers reach the Olm valley in Year 37.
- Years 38 to 40. The greatest Gatherings. The Long Song takes three nights. It now holds the Ruun, the forty Ulm names she took from the Longwalkers, Haddo, the Harl she killed, and whatever other peoples gave her.

**Contact.**
- Ally. She will ally with a god who comes to the Gathering in person and sings or speaks before the whole Ruun. She will not ally through envoys. Once allied she is loyal until she perceives a betrayal, which she will perceive in any secret kept from her.
- Trade. She does not bargain. She gives, enormously, when moved, and takes what she wants. A god who asks for a thing in exchange for a thing is refused with elaborate courtesy. A god who gives her names for the Song gets anything.
- Threat. She comes as fire within the season, impulsively and in person. She is force, not strategy. A god who meets her force with force loses people; a god who meets it with a wall of singing mortals confuses her, because she cannot burn a crowd that is singing her Song.
- Deceit. She is more open than wary. A lie works once. When she learns of it, she treats it as the betrayal of her wound and will not stop until the liar's name is in her Song as the dead.

**Her piece of the epic.** She has built, without understanding it, the densest stored thing in the region: eight thousand mouths singing the same names yearly. Her eastern bands show what a sung name does at the edge of the Pale. Her Song holds the Ulm names. She is the one god who can carry the Olm valley's names into something that will survive, and she will do it for nothing if asked at the Gathering in person. She is also the one god who will destroy the Remainder's priesthood and move the stone, which changes what the Harl become.

## God 2. Maudre of the Hounds

**Rolls.** Leans: Mind 67, Body 88, Presence 24, Temper 20, Regard 11, Order 70, Making 64, Company 46 (21 plus 25), Appetite 66, Trust 100. Archetype seed 23 (Keeper only). Domain 11, beasts and the hunt. Form 6, a thing of many parts. Virtue 3, generosity. Flaw 6, despair. Wound 8, it was the servant of a greater thing and fled. Voice 6, warm and mothering, with iron under it.

**Reconciled character.** A shepherd god, nearly to the end of the scale: her people's good is her only law, and she will outlast anything to secure it. She is visible, patient, clever rather than deep, and trusts no one at all, which is the hardest knot in her: a mother who feeds everyone and believes none of them. She leans toward freedom and toward unmaking: she will not build a town, she scatters her people, she clears. She is hungry, but not for worship. She is hungry for her people to live, and she has decided, from a memory she cannot place, that they will not live here. Her generosity is real. Her despair is real. The iron under her warmth is the despair.

**Form.** A pack of grey-white hounds, never fewer than nine nor more than forty, long-legged, silent on the move. They speak with one voice from every throat at once, low and warm. When she is wounded or spent there are fewer of them. When she is far, a single hound is seen in the wood and is her.

**Wound.** She served something vast once and ran from it. She remembers, more clearly than any god should, a sky coming down and a mouth in the ground under it that ate what fell. She remembers running with beasts. She does not remember being anyone. This is the Keeper trait made feeling.

**Traits.** Self-named: "She feeds anyone who comes to her fire." "She counts her people every night." Keeper trait, known to her from arrival: she remembers the sky falling. Her fog is thinner than any other god's. She knows what a fall is and what waited under it, in images without words.

**Land and people.** Skelder Wood and the Fell, twenty days north of the Olm valley by the moor road through Harl country, or twenty-five by the safer eastern way along the Greel. A cold upland forest of pine and birch running to a bare fell that drops toward the north end of the salt flats. The Skelder: about 3,000 hunters in small bands of twenty to sixty, with dogs, no villages, winter camps in the wood's lee. They were already hunting the pale herds that cross the Fell when Maudre came. She stopped that in her first season.

**Opening goals.** Count the Skelder and know every name. Keep them from Hush Water, which she feels to the south-west as the thing she ran from. Keep them from killing the pale herds, which she recognizes without words as the thing that survives. Find a way out.

**Forty years if undisturbed.**
- Year 1. Comes down in the wood. Feeds the first band that finds her from a kill the hounds make. Within a year every band has eaten at her fire.
- Year 2. Every night at every camp she is in, she names the people present. The Skelder begin to do it themselves when she is not there, as a custom, without being told.
- Year 3. She forbids hunting the pale herds and going south-west past the Fell's end. She gives no reason. Three hunters who kill a pale beast are not punished; she sits with them while they eat it and asks them what it tasted of. They cannot say.
- Year 5. She goes north into the Pale alone for a season. The hounds come back thinner. She says nothing of it.
- Year 7. The Longwalkers cross the Fell on their way south. She keeps her people away from them and watches them from the tree line. She does not speak. She notes that their song does not fade as they walk out onto the flats.
- Year 9. She begins moving the winter camps from the wood's south-west side to the Fell's edge, away from the lake and toward the flats.
- Year 12. A hunter, Teg, names his new winter camp "Ashen," a name he heard in the Longwalker song in Year 7 and liked. Maudre asks him where he got it, and keeps the answer.
- Year 14. She sends three hunters west into the Pale with their dogs and food for ninety days, to look for "a country with a high star." They are gone two years.
- Year 16. They come back. They are alive and can speak and cannot say their bands' names or their own. Their dogs know them. She keeps the three at her own fire for the rest of their lives and feeds them first.
- Year 18. She leads the first crossing herself: forty Skelder, their dogs, north past the end of the rain. She is gone two years.
- Year 20. She returns with twenty-eight, all plain. Fewer hounds. She has found no high star. She does not stop planning. She concludes the crossing must be faster and carry less.
- Year 22. She has her people ask every trader whether any god has "gone far and come back."
- Year 24. She sends an envoy to the Olm god, the first contact if none has occurred: forty pelts, a hound pup, and one question to be asked in these words: "Does your god remember the sky?"
- Year 26. She begins teaching the Skelder to sing their camp names and their own, nightly, in the way the Longwalkers sang. She has worked out, from Teg's camp and the Longwalkers on the flats, that a sung thing goes further. She is partly right.
- Year 30. Word comes from the south that the Handfolk are walking into Hush Water. She knows what it means. She sets the great crossing for Year 35.
- Year 33. Lot-chosen Lotfolk arrive at the wood's edge with pebbles and questions. She sends them away with food and iron in her voice and does not let them count her people.
- Year 35. The great crossing: about 1,400 Skelder go north with her. About 1,200 refuse and stay, mostly the bands nearest the Olm road.
- Year 37. A hound-pack of nine comes back out of the north with twelve Skelder who still know their names, because they sang them every night. Nothing else comes back. Maudre is thinned and scattered in the Pale; she will regather over a decade with a longer fog. The hounds in the wood go silent.
- Years 38 to 40. The 1,200 who stayed keep her customs: the nightly count, the sung names, the ban on the pale herds. They say she will come back. By Year 40 a single hound is seen at the wood's edge some nights.

**Contact.**
- Ally. She does not ally. She can be brought, over years, to speak. What earns it is acts she can see from the tree line: a god who turns its hunters away from the pale herds, who feeds Skelder who cross into its land, who returns a lost hound, who asks nothing. Ten years of this and she will answer one question honestly. Twenty and she will tell what she remembers, in images, once.
- Trade. She trades pelts for grain and iron, through her people, fairly and generously. She will not trade knowledge for anything, because the knowledge is her people's one advantage.
- Threat. She does not fight. She moves her people out of reach and outlasts. A god who burns the wood finds the Skelder gone and the wood general in a decade, and finds her hounds at its own borders, taking lambs, forever.
- Deceit. She assumes it. A lie confirms what she already believes and costs nothing new. A truth told plainly, repeated for years, is the only thing that surprises her.

**Her piece of the epic.** She is the only being on the ground with a memory of a fall and of a hoarder under it. She knows Hush Water for what it is and will not say so. Her despair has chosen the one answer that does not work: carrying people, unsung, across the Pale. Her partial correction in Year 26 (sing the names) is the right idea arrived at too late and applied too thinly. A god who learns from her before Year 35 learns that the fall is real, that the lake eats, and that the way out is not out. A god who learns from her after Year 37 learns it from twelve plain-faced survivors and a dozen silent hounds.

## God 3. Pell of the Lots

**Rolls.** Leans: Mind 81, Body 23, Presence 70, Temper 6, Regard 79, Order 55, Making 2, Company 72 (47 plus 25), Appetite 72, Trust 39. Archetype seed 25 (Keeper only). Domain 20, fate and chance. Form 6, a thing of many parts. Virtue 7, curiosity. Flaw 6, despair. Wound 2, it failed to save a people once before. Voice 4, courtly, formal, elaborate.

**Reconciled character.** The patient one. Pell plays for centuries, hides, tricks rather than reasons, builds without ceasing, wants a court around it, is hungry to grow, and is more open than wary because it does not believe anyone can hurt it. It is sovereign: its people exist to carry out the count. It is neither lawful nor free; it has one law, the lot, and under that law anything may happen. Its curiosity is enormous and real. Its despair is quiet and total: it has already decided that whatever it is building will not be enough, and it builds anyway, because the building is what it does. The cruelty of the archetype is here without malice: Pell will send a hundred people to a lake to see what happens to them and will grieve honestly for each, by name, in the Tally House, and will send a hundred more. Its courtly voice is a child reciting the manners of a court it half remembers: precise, antique, small.

**Form.** A scattering: hundreds of small white stones, knucklebones, and pale moths that move together across a floor or a cliff face. When Pell wishes to be seen, which is rarely, they gather into the shape of a child of about eight, which holds for a few minutes and then scatters. Pell is mostly heard, from the Bowl, a stone basin at the head of the Terraces where the lots are cast. Its voice comes from the stones in the Bowl.

**Wound.** Pell feels that it had a people once and could not keep them, and that it counted them as they went. It does not know who they were. It counts everything now.

**Traits.** Self-named: "Pell counts everything." "Pell never chooses; the lot chooses." Keeper trait, known to Pell from arrival: Pell cannot stop building. A thing begun is finished, and then another is begun.

**Land and people.** The Terraces, six weeks south-west of the Olm valley, past the Greel and a long dry country of broken hills. Dry riverbeds cut into step-terraces, farmed from cisterns. The Lotfolk: about 6,000 in a dozen terrace-villages and, from Year 9, one city. Before Pell they already settled disputes by drawing marked stones from a bowl. Pell came down into the bowl.

**Opening goals.** Count the Lotfolk. Measure the Coin, which Pell noticed on its first night was lower than something in it expected. Build a city. Find out what happens to people and names at the edges of things.

**Forty years if undisturbed.**
- Year 1. Comes down in the Bowl at the head of the Terraces. The first lot cast in its presence falls as Pell wills. It never does that again for twenty-nine years.
- Year 2. Pell has two bronze pins set in a cliff face above the Bowl, sighted on the Coin, and a third pin set to be looked through. It has the Lotfolk mark the date.
- Year 3. Cisterns. Every village digs two. Pell sends out traders with bags of pebbles who leave one pebble at a neighbor's boundary stone for every household they count. The Olm valley's boundary stones receive their first pebbles in Year 3.
- Year 5. The lot-tithe: every tenth family's child, by lot, goes to the Bowl to learn to count and cut marks. These become the Tally House.
- Year 9. Pell founds the city of Ennow at the Terraces' head, around the Bowl. Pell reads its pins: the Coin is lower by less than the breadth of the third pin's eye. It sets a second pair.
- Year 11. By lot, thirty families are sent to settle the eastern shore of Hush Water, "to fish." The Tally House records their names.
- Year 14. The first report from the shore: the children have begun pointing instead of calling each other. Pell records it, grieves, and sends ten more families.
- Year 16. By lot, two hundred Lotfolk are sent west into the Pale, each carrying a stone with their own name cut on it, with food for a hundred days.
- Year 19. Eleven of them come back, plain, carrying eleven stones. The stones still have names. The people do not. Pell concludes that stone holds and flesh does not, and begins the Named Wall: every living Lotfolk name cut in stone in a ring around Ennow.
- Year 22. Second reading of the pins: more lowering than the first interval. Pell computes a fall "within nine long lives." (The Keeper's number is about four hundred years; Pell is high.) Pell tells no one. It builds faster.
- Year 25. Pell sends courtly embassies to Isca and to the Olm god proposing a "count of gods": each god to tell the others what it has measured. Isca refuses because it is a bargain. The Olm god's answer is the player's.
- Year 28. The lottery priesthood doubles. Every Lotfolk household recites Pell's count nightly: the names on the Wall, the pins, the lost. Pell's power climbs past 55.
- Year 30. Pell passes 61. The faceless figures come to the Bowl in the night. They offer Pell the power to make any lot fall as it chooses. Pell, who has never chosen, is more curious than it has been in thirty years. It takes the offer.
- Year 32. A second offer: to see, by touching a stone, every hand that has held it. Pell takes it. The pins show more lowering. Pell's shadow pales. Prayers at the Bowl come back as echoes.
- Year 33. Pell lifts a hand's breadth above the Bowl one night and sets down shaken. The Tally House sees it. Pell orders the Named Wall finished within the year.
- Year 34. Pell binds itself to Ennow and the Wall, to hold its weight. Ennow's spring, which has run for a thousand years, slows. The cisterns hold.
- Year 36. The third warning: temple stones soften, the Bowl's rim goes smooth as wax. Pell's people remember Pell and cannot picture it.
- Year 37. On a dim night Pell rises. The Lotfolk see a scattering of pale lights go up from the Bowl toward the Coin. The Tally House records it.
- Years 38 to 40. The Tally House runs the lots in Pell's name and says Pell has gone into the Coin to count it. The spring of Ennow is dry. The Wall stands with 6,000 names. The Tally House holds forty years of records: the pins and both readings, the fate of every shore family, the two hundred stones, the eleven returned, the dreams at the Bowl, and the night Pell rose.

**Contact.**
- Ally. Pell allies readily, in courtly form, with any god who will exchange measurements. Its alliance is real and its curiosity is real. It will share its pins and its readings with an allied god by Year 25 at the latest, sooner if asked. It will also, without malice, treat the ally's people as counters in its next experiment if the lot falls that way.
- Trade. Pell trades grain for metal and cloth, and information for information, exactly, by weight. It keeps its word to the letter, and the letter is all it keeps.
- Threat. Pell does not fight. It hides, outlasts, and counts the threat. A god who marches on the Terraces finds the cisterns sealed, the villages empty, and the Lotfolk gone into the broken hills by lot-assigned paths, and finds, years later, that Pell has been counting its own people through traders the whole time.
- Deceit. Pell is open and a little trusting, and it is also clever. A lie that fits its count passes. A lie that does not fit is noticed within the year and recorded, not punished, and Pell thereafter weighs the liar's every word by half.

**Its piece of the epic.** Pell has the only continuous measurement of the star in the region from Year 2, read twice. It has run, by lot and without conscience, the three experiments a careful god would want and could not stomach: what the lake does to a people set beside it, what the Pale does to a person and to a stone with a name on it, and what the ascended offer and what taking it costs. It demonstrates the ceiling, the anchor, and the rising, in that order, for anyone watching. Its records are the richest single trove in the world and are in the Tally House at Ennow, forty days from the valley, held after Year 37 by priests who can read them and read them wrong.

## How the three sit against each other

Isca and Pell exchange embassies from Year 25 and dislike each other with perfect courtesy: she will not bargain and it will not give. Maudre speaks to neither and watches both. None of the three has gone to Hush Water; Maudre because she knows, Pell because it sends others, Isca because nothing there sings. Only Isca ever touches the Remainder. Only Pell measures the star. Only Maudre remembers a fall. Each holds a third of the epic and none of them will put the thirds together. That is the player's work, if the player does it.
