# Mysteries of the Olm country

Tiered per mystery-standard rule 13. One epic, two long, five short. Every clue below exists in the world from the year given and is findable from that moment by anyone who goes where it is or asks the person who saw it (rule 8). Clue kinds: SEEN (the god or its people witness it), REPORTED (a named mortal tells it), RUMORED (secondhand, older, or through traders). "Readings" lists the honest interpretations the evidence supports; none is a decoy (rule 2). "Found by" lists the actions that reach it. No clue states a mechanism; every mortal who explains a cause is wrong about something that matters (rule 1).

Year 1 is the gods' arrival. The Olm folk call it the 312th year after the Driving.

---

# EPIC. The Account

**The truth.** The Lamp is sinking and the rate is quickening; it will fall in about four hundred years. The land under it is spending itself into it, oldest and most-worked places first, and the Olm valley has begun to lose kinds. Hush Water, twenty-five days north-west, is a single being that grew over the ruins of the oldest city, eats what fades around it, is fed from the Lamp on dim nights by one faction inside the star, and wants the fall. The four gods, including the player, are what a far fall threw here, and the warm stone in the Harl moor is the same stuff uncompressed. When the Lamp falls, everything particular under it is crushed and overwritten, and only what is dense enough comes through: a name sung by enough mouths for enough years, a breed kept pure, a god anchored to a mountain. The Longwalkers carry such names across the Pale and plant them. Isca has built a storage vessel without knowing it. Maudre knows the fall and has chosen a way out that does not work. Pell has measured everything and is going up. None of them will put it together. The answer, once found, is not a thing to know but a century of work: slow the valley's spending, store its names where they will keep, treat with three gods who are each wrong, hold the stone away from those who would pray to it, refuse the voices, and stay under a ceiling no one has stated.

**Bodies.** The Lamp's measurable descent. Hush Water and the Handfolk. The Notch on Hagg Ridge. The blue-grey glass under the Ulmarrow mounds. The Remainder stone at Fallow Dip. The Longwalkers. The pale herds on the Harl moor. The Long Song. Pell's pins. Maudre's memory.

**Cost if ignored, and its clocks.**
- Fading clock. Every decade the valley loses a visible kind: Year 10 the spotted eel of Dun Mere; Year 20 Lissom's third plum; Year 30 the Greyholt way of saying "you" to an elder; Year 40 the Lissom stream, the valley's second water, goes general: slower, wider, no particular depth, a marsh where the orchards were. This clock slows if the valley spends less (no great working, no school of knotting, no demand for ever-greater festivals, more things kept: breeds, dialects, songs, named places) and quickens if it spends more.
- The Still's clock. Year 30 the Handfolk begin walking into Hush Water in their sleep. Year 33 a Greyholt charcoal burner summering on Hagg Ridge does the same, in a tarn, and is found in the morning, plain. Year 36 a second. After Year 40, further.
- The Remainder's trigger. If no god has reached the stone by Year 20, the Harl have a priesthood of it that dreams the gods' wound and recites it before raids on Greyholt and Ferrow Ford. If Isca reaches it first (Year 26 if undisturbed) she destroys the priesthood, kills the priests, and drops the stone in the Greel in Year 32.
- The Longwalkers' trigger. They pass in Year 7 and again in Year 39. A god who did not speak with them in Year 7 is not asked anything in Year 39.
- The ceiling's trigger. If the player's power passes 61, the voices come. Three years above 76 and the god rises on the next dim night.
- Beyond the test. The fall, in about four hundred years, which takes everything unkept.

**What execution requires once the truth is partly held.**
1. Spend less. Refuse great workings. Keep the knotter count under a dozen or at zero. Keep breeds, names, dialects, customs, and the Lissom's bends by keeping the particular things along it (the plum kinds, the mill, the named pools). This is decades of restraint against every mortal who asks for a miracle.
2. Store. Get the valley's names into something dense: the Long Song, by going to the Gathering in person; the Longwalkers' carrying, by having engaged them in Year 7 and having the names ready as sung lists in Year 39; the valley's own nightly or yearly naming, which is slow and real.
3. Treat. Turn Maudre from the great crossing before Year 35, which needs twenty years of acts she can see. Get Pell's records, by alliance before Year 37 or by trade with the priests after. Decide what Isca is to the valley before her singers come in Year 37, and before she rides west in Year 26 if the stone matters.
4. Hold the stone. Reach Fallow Dip before Year 20, or decide to leave it to the Harl, or to Isca, knowing what each does.
5. Refuse. Take no offer from the faceless figures. Shed power into saints, springs, and breeds rather than gathering it, which is also storing.
6. Outlast. Nothing here finishes in forty years. A god that understands all of it in Year 40 has a century of work ahead and three gods to do it with or around.

## Epic clues

**E1. The fourth pull.** SEEN. Year 1, arrival night. The god feels three pulls at great distance: east-south-east (Isca), north (Maudre), south-west (Pell). And a fourth, weaker, north-west and nearer, that does not move and does not answer. Readings: a fourth god asleep; a god that died in the arriving; a place rather than a being. Found by: any god feels it; going toward it reaches Fallow Dip in nine days.

**E2. The Lamp's names.** RUMORED. Year 1. Olm folk say the Lamp; Harl say the Low One; salt caravans say the Coin; Ruun the Ember; Skelder the Eye. All old. Children are told not to stare, for no reason anyone can give. Readings: names are names; the Lamp has always been low here. Found by: any conversation about the sky. A seed, not a lead.

**E3. The blue hair.** REPORTED. Exists from Year 1; first told Year 2. Col, a Ferrow Ford shepherd who summers on the Backs, has seen on dim nights, when the Lamp fades for an hour, "a blue hair" come down out of it far to the north-west, toward the moor. He thinks it is the Lamp drinking from a river up there. He is wrong about the river. He tells it only if the god asks about dim nights or sleeps a night on the Backs. Readings: a fire in the hills; the Lamp drinking; the Lamp speaking to something. Found by: asking the shepherds; sleeping on the Backs on a dim night.

**E4. The gate that points at nothing.** REPORTED then SEEN. Exists always; first told Year 3. The Harl clan of Gorm Longhand summers six hundred sheep in a fold on Hagg Ridge, two days above Greyholt, whose gatepost is two uprights and a notched cross-stone. Greyholt charcoal burners who cut on the ridge, or any Harl trader, will say the old gate "points at nothing" if asked about the ridge. Lying behind it at night, the notch frames empty sky a hand above the Lamp. Readings: it framed a star that is gone; the ground has tilted; it was never for the sky. Found by: asking about Hagg Ridge; going; lying down behind it after dark. Opens: setting the god's own marks.

**E5. The god's own marks.** SEEN. From Year 8 if set in Year 1, and every seven years after. Two fixed sightlines set by the god, in stone, at two places, read back after seven or more years show the Lamp lower. A second interval shows more lowering than the first. Readings for the first interval: the eye erred; the stone settled; the ground is sinking. The second interval is decisive and is the only decisive clue in the epic, and it is earned by the god's own act and patience (rule 4). Found by: doing it.

**E6. The Greel is getting fat.** REPORTED. Year 1 onward. Greelmen boat traders at Weir say the big river is wider and slower than their grandfathers knew it, the old stone jetty at the Olm mouth is two spans under, and the Greel's three kinds of fish are down to one. They say it is "more rain in the north." Readings: more rain; silting; a fallen bank upstream; the river is becoming river in general. Found by: talking to boat traders; walking to the Olm mouth and looking at the jetty.

**E7. The spotted eel.** SEEN and REPORTED. Year 1 onward. Dun Mere's eel fishers say the spotted eel, the sweet one, has been fewer each year since the old headman's time, while the plain eel is thick as ever. By Year 10 no spotted eel is taken. Fishers blame a pike, or warm summers. Readings: overfishing; a predator; warm water; a kind going. Found by: the autumn count; asking at Dun Mere.

**E8. The third plum.** SEEN. Year 1 onward. Lissom grows three plums: the yellow, the blood, and the small grey "bland plum" that old people say was once the best and "went bland first." By Year 20 the grey plum has no fruit worth keeping and the trees are cut. Readings: blight; bad grafting; old people's memory. Found by: harvest at Lissom; asking the old.

**E9. Ebb who came back plain.** REPORTED. Year 6. Ebb, a Greyholt charcoal burner, went east with a salt caravan beyond the flats in Year 4 to see the edge of the world and came back in Year 6 able to speak, work, and recognize no one. He cannot say Greyholt's name or his wife's. His wife, Nan, comes to the god. The caravaners say the salt did it. Readings: a head injury; a curse; grief; the salt; the far country. Found by: being in Greyholt in Year 6, or asked about anything strange that year. Opens: sending someone to the edge on purpose.

**E10. The singers.** SEEN. Year 7, early summer, and Year 39. Forty wanderers come up the Olm from the Greel, dressed in no particular way, and sing all night at the Stone Row in Tollan. They ask the name of every village and sing it back exactly. Their songs are lists: Olm names, then names no one knows, among them "Ulmarrow," "Hesk," "Teller," "Thrane," "Vell." They trade nothing, take no fish, leave at dawn upstream toward the Harl moor and the Skelder Fell. Old Hemma, eighty, remembers them from her childhood. Asked directly by the god, one says they "carry names across the bare country," and that a name carried long enough "comes back." Readings: spies; madmen; a holy order; the dead; a lost tongue. Found by: being at Tollan in Year 7; asking Hemma; following them. If detained by force, they sing nothing, and do not return in Year 39.

**E11. No shells.** REPORTED. Year 1 onward, autumn salt market at Tollan. Salt caravaners say the flats where the sea was have no shells, no bones of fish, no wrack line, "as if the sea took everything with it when it left in anger." Readings: there was never a sea and the caravaners' story is a story; the salt ate the shells; the sea stopped being a sea. Found by: talking to caravaners; going to the flats.

**E12. The pale herds.** REPORTED. Year 5 onward. Harl hunters say that in some years beasts cross the moor all one color, with no scent, that leave no dung, and that the Harl will not eat because "there is no taste in them." In Year 5 a Harl hunter, Brude, killed one and opened it and found "meat and nothing else, no heart you could find." Readings: cursed; holy; sick; something very old. Found by: talking to Harl captives or traders; going to the moor in a herd year (Years 2, 5, 9, 14, 20, 27, 35).

**E13. The Warm Stone.** RUMORED then SEEN. Fell Year 1, arrival night; first told Year 3 by a Harl captive taken in a raid on Ferrow Ford, or by any Harl trader. A stone fell at Fallow Dip, half a day from the Cold Hand boundary, warm for a season, blue-grey, heavy. The Harl cairned it. Those who sleep by it dream of a crowd kneeling and of a river none of them has seen. The god, touching it, dreams the same crowd and knows the river from its own fog. Readings: a piece of the Lamp; a god that failed to wake; a dead sibling; where the gods came from. Found by: going to Fallow Dip, nine days north-west through Harl country.

**E14. Hush Water.** SEEN. Year 1 onward. Twenty-five days north-west. The lake does not freeze in winter, does not ripple in wind, and is warm at the bottom, which anyone who swims out finds. The Handfolk on its eastern shore point instead of calling each other and have words for everything else. An elder, if the god stays a night, says their grandparents had names and "the water did not want them." Anyone who sleeps on the shore dreams of tall figures without faces, counting, and of houses on poles above shallow water. On dim nights the blue hair comes down and lands on the lake, every time. Readings: a hot spring; a sacred place; a god; a wound in the world; the Lamp feeding the lake; the lake feeding the Lamp. Found by: going.

**E15. The glass.** SEEN. Always. Under the grass mounds on Hush Water's eastern shore, which the Handfolk will not walk on: Ulm rubble, then fitted stone of a kind no one knows, then a layer of blue-grey glass that rings when struck. Set in the glass, intact, a notched law stone bearing the Ulm word "Teller," which is in the singers' song. Readings: glass from a great fire; a floor; from the sky; the singers robbed this place; the singers were this place. Found by: a dig at the shore, which takes a season and a crew and the Handfolk's silence.

**E16. The god's weight.** SEEN. Year 3 onward. The god's power rises after the first harvest festival and falls after the hard winter of Year 4 when people are too tired to pray. In Year 9, when Scarrow quarrels with Tollan and turns from the god for a season while still paying tithe, the god is lighter. Readings: the god feeds on joy; on grain; on the season; on being thought of. Found by: noticing one's own body.

**E17. Isca's eastern bands.** REPORTED. Year 12 onward, via Ruun riders or any trader from the plateau; stated openly by Isca's embassy if asked. Ruun bands that graze to the plateau's eastern edge come back forgetting their own camps but still able to sing the Long Song entire. Isca calls it her strength. Readings: Isca's power; the edge takes and the song does not; the song is a spell. Found by: contact with the Ruun.

**E18. Pell's pins and stones.** REPORTED. Year 9 and Year 22 readings; Year 19 stones. Via Lotfolk traders from Year 20, or in full from Pell by alliance, or in Pell's embassy letter of Year 25, which states the Coin "is lower by the eye of a pin in thirteen years" and that "eleven of two hundred returned, and their stones with them, and the names were on the stones." Readings: Pell lies; Pell's cliff is sinking; Pell's people are mad. Found by: trade with the Lotfolk; alliance with Pell.

**E19. The voices.** SEEN. When the god's power passes 61, or any dim night the god sleeps at Hush Water's shore. Faceless figures offer a power the god cannot yet do, in exchange for "weight." Taken: the power is real, the god feels lighter, its shadow pales. Refused: another voice, different, says "then you will be below when we come down, and that is also a place." Readings: the lake tempts; the Lamp rewards; the Lamp hunts; a rival's trick; the Lamp is many. Found by: growing, or sleeping at the shore.

**E20. Pell lifts and rises.** REPORTED. Year 33 and Year 37, by Lotfolk traders in Year 34 and Year 38. Pell was seen a hand above the Bowl and set down shaken; then on a dim night pale lights rose from the Bowl toward the Coin and Pell has not been heard since; the Tally House says Pell went into the Coin to count it. Readings: struck for pride; taken as reward; a trick of the priests. Found by: trade with the Lotfolk.

## Which god holds which piece

- Isca: the storage (E17 and the Long Song itself); the Ulm names from the singers; the fate of the stone after Year 26.
- Maudre: the memory of a fall and a hoarder (Long 1, clue L1-9); the demonstration that unsung flesh does not cross (L1-5, L1-6, L1-10) and that sung names help (L1-10).
- Pell: the measurement (E18); the shore experiment (L2-4); the stone experiment (E18); the ceiling, the anchor, and the rising (E20).
- No god holds: the Notch, the glass, Hush Water itself, the Longwalkers, the Remainder before Year 26, or the valley's own fading. Those are the player's to find or not.

# LONG 1. The Hounds' Road

**The truth.** Maudre, the god of the Skelder, remembers a fall and the mouth under it, knows Hush Water for the same kind of thing, and has decided her people cannot stay under the Eye. She is preparing, over twenty years, to walk them north across the Pale to find a country with a high star. There is no such country within reach. The Pale makes people general. She learns, too late and too thinly, that sung names go further than unsung flesh. In Year 35 she takes 1,400 Skelder north. Twelve come back.

**Bodies.** Maudre's pack. The three hunters of Year 16 at her fire. Teg's camp called Ashen. The twelve of Year 37.

**Cost if ignored.** The Skelder are halved and their god scattered. The one living memory of a fall is lost for a decade or more. The north wood thins without them.

**Clock.** Year 14 three hunters sent. Year 18 first crossing. Year 26 the singing begins. Year 35 the great crossing. Year 37 the return.

**Clues.**

**L1-1.** REPORTED. Year 2 onward. Skelder pelt traders at Tollan's spring market: their god is "the Mother of Dogs," feeds anyone, names every person at every fire every night, and has forbidden hunting the one-color beasts and walking south-west past the Fell, and will not say why. Readings: a jealous god; a god who knows something; a god who is strange. Found by: the spring market.

**L1-2.** RUMORED. Year 3 onward. Harl say the Skelder who cross the moor now go the long way round rather than toward the north-west. Found by: Harl contact.

**L1-3.** REPORTED. Year 9 onward. Skelder traders complain their winter camps have moved to the Fell side and the walk to Tollan is a week longer. Found by: the spring market.

**L1-4.** REPORTED. Year 12 onward. A Skelder camp is called Ashen, and the trader says the name "came from the singers who crossed the Fell." Readings: a borrowed word; a joke; a planted name. Found by: asking Skelder traders about their camps. Links to E10.

**L1-5.** REPORTED. Year 17 onward. Three hunters came back from the far north after two years, alive, speaking, unable to name their bands or themselves, and the Mother feeds them first at her own fire. Readings: punishment; blessing; madness; the far country. Found by: Skelder traders.

**L1-6.** REPORTED. Year 21 onward. The Mother went north herself with forty and came back with twenty-eight, all of them like the three, and fewer dogs. Found by: Skelder traders.

**L1-7. The envoy.** SEEN. Year 24. Forty pelts, a grey-white hound pup, and a Skelder hunter who asks, in these words and no others: "Does your god remember the sky?" He will carry back one answer. Readings: a riddle; a test; a threat; a real question. Found by: being the god of the valley in Year 24. Note: Maudre's envoy comes in Year 24 whether or not contact was made earlier; if it was, the question is asked in person at her fire instead.

**L1-8.** REPORTED. Year 26 onward. The Skelder now sing their camp names and their own every night; traders hum it. Found by: Skelder traders.

**L1-9. The memory.** SEEN. Only if earned. Ten years of acts Maudre can see (turning hunters from the pale herds, feeding Skelder in the valley, returning her lost hound, asking nothing) earn one honest answer to one question. Twenty years earn the memory itself, told once, in images: a sky coming down; a mouth in the ground that ate what fell; running with beasts; and a lake "that is a mouth," which is Hush Water. Readings: a dream; a lie; a different world; this world's own past; this world's future. Found by: patience and acts.

**L1-10.** REPORTED. Year 38 onward. Twelve Skelder and nine hounds came back out of the north; the twelve know their names because they sang them every night; nothing else came back; the Mother is not in the wood; the 1,200 who stayed say she will return. Found by: Skelder traders.

**The lost hound.** An object of chance that is also the strongest single act. In Year 6 one of Maudre's hounds strays onto the Harl moor and is netted by Harl raiders, who sell it at Tollan's spring market in Year 7 as "a wolf-dog from the north." It is silent, grey-white, and will not eat. A god who buys it and sends it north with no message is, for Maudre, the first god worth watching. A god who keeps it finds it dead in a year.

**Wrong branches.** Hunting the pale herds on the moor, or sending hunters north to "see for ourselves": Maudre's hounds appear on the Backs within the year and take lambs for a decade, and she never speaks. Going into the Pale with a party to test what the crossing does: the party returns plain, which teaches the thing at the cost of the people. Marching on the wood: it empties.

**Restraint.** Nothing happens. In Year 35 she goes. In Year 38 the news arrives.

**What turning her requires.** The memory (L1-9) must have been earned, so she trusts the god enough to hear it; then the god must show her what it has: a sung valley, or Pell's eleven stones, or the Longwalkers' willingness to carry. She will not take the god's word. She will take evidence she can see. Even then she may go, with fewer, and sing them all the way, and more may return.

# LONG 2. The Small King's Count

**The truth.** Pell, the god of the Lotfolk, has measured the Coin since Year 2 and knows it sinks. It has, by lot and without malice, settled thirty families on Hush Water's shore to see what the lake does, sent two hundred people into the Pale with their names on stones to see what the Pale does, built a wall of every Lotfolk name as a hedge, and recorded everything in the Tally House at Ennow. From Year 30 it takes the offers of the faceless figures out of curiosity, lifts in Year 33, tries to anchor to its city in Year 34, and rises in Year 37. Its records are the richest trove in the world and are afterward held by priests who read them wrong.

**Bodies.** The pins above the Bowl. The shore families. The eleven stones. The Named Wall. The Tally House. The night of pale lights.

**Cost if ignored.** The measurements and the experiments are known only to a priesthood that believes Pell went up to count the Coin and that the lot still speaks. The shore families are gone into the Handfolk. The player learns the ceiling, if at all, from its own shadow instead of from watching.

**Clock.** Year 3 pebbles. Year 11 shore families pass. Year 19 stones return. Year 25 embassy. Year 30 the voices. Year 33 the lift. Year 37 the rising.

**Clues.**

**L2-1. The pebbles.** This is Short 5, resolved. See below.

**L2-2.** REPORTED. Year 4 onward. Lotfolk traders at the autumn market cast marked stones from a bag for every decision including prices; they say their god is "a child made of stones who lives in a bowl," and that the Coin "is a coin because one day it falls." They say this as a proverb and do not know they are right. Readings: a trading custom; a mad people; a god who gambles. Found by: trading with them.

**L2-3.** SEEN. Year 11, late summer. Thirty Lotfolk families with carts pass up the Olm valley road and over the Caddon Hills, "going to fish a lake in the north-west that our god drew for us." Readings: exiles; settlers; a cult; a punishment. Found by: being in the valley that summer.

**L2-4.** REPORTED or SEEN. Year 14 onward. The shore families' children point instead of calling. Known at Hush Water to anyone who goes; known to Pell by Year 14; told by Lotfolk traders from Year 16 as "the lake families have gone quiet." Found by: visiting the shore; Lotfolk traders.

**L2-5.** REPORTED. Year 20 onward. Lotfolk traders: two hundred were sent west with their names cut on stones; eleven came back after three years and had forgotten everything, but the stones had the names still; now the god is building a wall of every name. Readings: stone keeps and flesh does not; the west is cursed; Pell is mad. Found by: Lotfolk traders.

**L2-6.** REPORTED. Year 21 onward. The Named Wall at Ennow, described by traders: a ring of cut stone with every living Lotfolk name, which the whole city recites at night. Found by: traders; going to Ennow, forty days.

**L2-7. The embassy.** SEEN. Year 25. Three Lotfolk counters with a letter cut in lead in courtly form, proposing a count of gods, stating the Olm valley's household number to the one, and offering in exchange "the height of the Coin in thirteen years" and "what the stones carried back." Readings: a bargain; a trick; a boast. Found by: being the god of the valley in Year 25.

**L2-8.** REPORTED. Year 34 and Year 38. See E20.

**L2-9. The Tally House.** SEEN. Always, at Ennow. Lead sheets and cut stone: the pins and both readings with dates; every shore family by name and what became of them; the two hundred and the eleven; from Year 30 the dreams at the Bowl written in Pell's own antique phrasing; the lift; the rising. Found by: alliance with Pell before Year 37 (Pell shares freely); a counter of the god's own sent to the Tally House to learn, which Pell permits from Year 25 in exchange for one of the valley's children to count; or after Year 37, trade with the priests, who will sell copies of anything for iron and will explain each as the Coin's will.

**Wrong branches.** Refusing the count in Year 25 costs nothing but access, and Pell records the refusal. Attacking Lotfolk traders stops the pebbles for a year and Pell thereafter weighs the valley's every word by half. Lying in the count is noticed within the year and recorded. Attempting to stop the shore families in Year 11 saves thirty families from the lake and costs Pell's goodwill until Year 25, when Pell, curious, asks why.

**Restraint.** Pell rises in Year 37 and the records pass to the priests. Nothing is lost that cannot be bought later at a worse price.

# SHORT 1. The Sweet Cisterns (Years 2 to 8)

**The truth.** Vell, four days above Greyholt, has nine open cisterns that have stayed cold and sweet for six hundred years because the king Imre put the Olm's cold into the hill in a single working that also made the Scald and took his name. The sealed cistern holds the Cord House tags and roll. The blank grave is his.

**Bodies.** The cisterns. The Scald. The blank grave. The sealed cistern.

**Cost if ignored.** In the drought of Year 4 Greyholt loses most of its goats for want of water it could have walked to. By Year 8 the child who found the path has married away and the path is forgotten again. The tags stay sealed and the eighth stone (Short 2) stays unreadable.

**Clock.** Year 2 the path is found. Year 4 drought. Year 8 the path is lost.

**Clues.**
- **S1-1.** REPORTED. Year 2. Pim, a Greyholt herd-child of nine, follows a goat up a path into the hills and comes back saying there is "a town in the hill with wells of cold water and no people." Greyholt's elders say the hills are unlucky and forbid it. Readings: a child's story; a town of the dead; the water is poisoned. Found by: being in Greyholt in Year 2 or hearing of the strange child at the count.
- **S1-2.** SEEN. Always. Greyholt headman's hearth-stone: a round cap of grey rock with a carved pattern the family calls a snake. Readings: a snake; an old charm; a lid. Found by: sitting in the headman's house.
- **S1-3.** SEEN. Always, from the Vell path. The Scald, a half-day across, bare grass with no seed heads, no bird in it. Readings: salt; an old burn; a curse. Found by: walking to Vell.
- **S1-4.** SEEN. Always, at Vell. A flat grave stone in the upper yard with a cut border and nothing inside it. Readings: unfinished; the dead was nameless; a slave. Found by: being at Vell.
- **S1-5.** SEEN. Always, at Vell. One cistern sealed with a capstone carved with the same pattern as Greyholt's hearth-stone. Readings: the same family; the same craft; a lid matching a lid. Found by: being at Vell with Greyholt's stone in mind.
- **S1-6.** REPORTED. From Year 3 if Marrit (Short 4) is found. She knows the path to Vell, calls the Scald "where the king's cold went," and says the cisterns are cold "because the river is warm," which is wrong in the direction that matters. Found by: finding Marrit.

**Found by.** Going up with Pim; opening the sealed cistern, which takes four men a day.

**Wrong branch.** Piping Vell's water down to Greyholt with channels: the water arrives, is ordinary by the time it reaches the village, and the cisterns refill cold. Costs a summer's labor and teaches that the cold is in the hill.

# SHORT 2. The Rooks' Torc (Years 5 to 12)

**The truth.** The Thrane lords of Lord's Hill were not driven out. They faded, their last lord could not name his sons, and Mother Tollan walked them onto the moor and ordered a battle story told. The Rooks of Scarrow descend from the one daughter who kept her name. The crypt under the hall holds the silver and the scraped board. The eighth stone of the Stone Row says, in Caddon signs, "We drove no one. They were already gone."

**Bodies.** The torc. The crypt. The eighth stone. The Driving, told at every count.

**Cost if ignored.** Year 5 the claim. Year 9 Scarrow splits, half for the Rooks. Year 12 a killing at the quarry, and the Rooks dig the hill themselves, find the crypt, take the silver, and read the scraped board as proof their line was wronged. The valley has a lord's feud for a generation.

**Clock.** Years 5, 9, 12.

**Clues.**
- **S2-1.** SEEN. Year 5, autumn count. Aldo Rook of Scarrow produces a gold torc and claims Lord's Hill and the old tithe, saying the god's coming is the sign the hill should have a lord again. Readings: Rooks are lords; Rooks are liars; a found trinket. Found by: the count.
- **S2-2.** RUMORED. Always. The Driving, as told. Readings: a battle; a story. Found by: any count.
- **S2-3.** SEEN. Always. The eighth stone in Tollan's Stone Row with a line of pictographs, "Mother Tollan's mark." Readings: a mark; a name; a line of writing. Found by: looking; readable only with the Vell tags (Short 1), Marrit (Short 4, who knows about half the signs), or a Harl elder who knows a few.
- **S2-4.** REPORTED. Year 3 onward, Harl. Harl elders tell of the "walked men" who came out onto the moor three hundred years ago "plain as sheep," and whose line is in the Harl still. Readings: beaten men; cursed men; a Harl boast. Found by: Harl contact.
- **S2-5.** SEEN. Always. The crypt, under the hall floor on Lord's Hill, by a dig that breaks Scarrow's custom. Eleven torcs, a chest of Sennet coin, a dish, the earlier lords with their names, and Edric's board, scraped and re-cut and scraped. Readings: a cursed hoard; a wronged line; a line that forgot itself. Found by: digging.

**Wrong branch.** Backing the Rooks as lords: Scarrow has a lord, Tollan resents it, and in Year 12 the Rooks dig anyway and the board shames them. Forbidding the claim outright: the Rooks dig in secret in Year 9 instead.

# SHORT 3. The Fen Bell (Years 1 to 6)

**The truth.** A bronze tide plate from Sennet, dug up by the trader Brann and carried upriver as ballast, lies in the mud of the Brann Cut in the Merrow Fen, where his boat went over. In autumn fog, wind through the reeds sets the plate ringing. It bears the Sennet script and the name Aud.

**Bodies.** The plate. The Brann Cut.

**Cost if ignored.** By Year 3 the Fenners stop fishing the cut, which has the fattest eels in the fen. By Year 6 they abandon the three steadings nearest it and the cut silts.

**Clock.** Years 1, 3, 6.

**Clues.**
- **S3-1.** REPORTED. Year 1, autumn. Fenners at Ossary's landing say a bell rings under the fog in the Brann Cut, "the drowned trading post ringing for its dead." Readings: a drowned post; a spirit; gas; a bell. Found by: Ossary.
- **S3-2.** RUMORED. Always. The Greelmen tell of a flat boat lost long ago in the fen "with bronze in it." Found by: boat traders at Weir.
- **S3-3.** SEEN. Autumn fogs, Years 1 onward. The sound: one low note, irregular, from the bend where the reeds stand thickest. Found by: poling into the cut in fog.
- **S3-4.** SEEN. Always. The plate, a span across, in the mud under the reed bend, dragged up with a hook. On it, rows of marks and a short line in a script no one in the valley reads. Readings: a temple bell; a shield; a tax tally; a foreign thing. Found by: dragging the bend. A salt caravaner can read "Aud" and "tide" and will say it is "the sea's anger written down."

**Wrong branch.** Declaring the cut haunted and forbidding it: the Fenners are pleased and lose their best eels. Teaches nothing but costs little.

# SHORT 4. The Lamb Taker (Years 3 to 9)

**The truth.** Marrit, the last hill knotter, lives alone on the Backs and takes a lamb now and then to live. She cannot say her name. She knows the way to Vell, half the Caddon signs, and the knots. In a hard winter in Year 9 she dies if no one has found and fed her.

**Bodies.** Marrit. Her clay tag. Her hut in a fold of the Backs.

**Cost if ignored.** Her death in Year 9, the end of the knot line, and the loss of the one living reader of the signs on the eighth stone and the Vell tags. Also a decade of lambs and a decade of blaming the Harl, which costs a raid.

**Clock.** Year 3 lambs. Year 5 the knot. Year 7 the sighting. Year 9 the winter.

**Clues.**
- **S4-1.** REPORTED. Year 3 onward. Lambs vanish from the Backs, one or two a month, no blood, no drag. Herders blame the Harl, then wolves. Readings: Harl thieves; wolves; a cat; a person. Found by: Ferrow Ford's complaints at the count.
- **S4-2.** SEEN. Year 5. A knot of twisted grass tied on a fold post where a lamb vanished, in a pattern no herder ties. Readings: a Harl sign; a charm; a child's game. Found by: being on the Backs or hearing Col's complaint.
- **S4-3.** REPORTED. Year 7. A shepherd boy sees an old woman at dusk carrying a lamb into a fold of the hill and is told he saw a ghost. Found by: asking the boy.
- **S4-4.** SEEN. When found. Marrit herself, with a clay tag on a cord at her neck with signs she cannot read. She will, if fed and not frightened, warm a hearth with a cord, and the water jar beside it frosts, and she says the cold "goes into the pot" and does not know where the pot's cold goes. Readings: a witch; a mad woman; the last of something. Found by: tracking, not hunting.

**Wrong branch.** A wolf hunt on the Backs: three weeks, no wolves, two dogs lost to a Harl trap, lambs still go. Teaches that it is not wolves. A raid on the Harl for the lambs: a Harl raid back in Year 5, and the lambs still go.

# SHORT 5. The Boundary Pebbles (Years 3 to 12)

**The truth.** Pell's trader Oss leaves a cairn of white pebbles at Tollan's boundary stone each autumn, one for each household he has counted in the valley, to be collected by the next Lotfolk trader and carried to the Tally House.

**Bodies.** The cairn. Oss. The Tally House.

**Cost if ignored.** None local. Pell's letter in Year 25 states the valley's households to the one and the valley wonders how. The count continues forty years whether seen or not.

**Clock.** Year 3 first cairn. Year 5 the count matches. Year 7 Oss is seen. Year 12 Oss dies and another trader takes over without a pause.

**Clues.**
- **S5-1.** SEEN. Year 3, after the autumn salt market. A cairn of white pebbles at Tollan's boundary stone, on the road south. Gone by spring. Readings: a traveler's custom; a curse; a grave. Found by: walking the south road.
- **S5-2.** SEEN. Year 5. A Weir boy counts the pebbles for a dare: as many as the valley's households at that year's count. Readings: a tally of the living; a tally of deaths to come; chance. Found by: hearing the boy; counting.
- **S5-3.** REPORTED. Year 7. A child sees the Lotfolk trader Oss placing pebbles at dusk after the market. Found by: watching the stone after market.
- **S5-4.** REPORTED. Year 4 onward. Lotfolk traders' custom of lots (L2-2). Oss, confronted, says the pebbles are "so the god knows how many of you to pray for," which is not what they are for.

**Wrong branch.** Scattering the cairn each year: Oss builds it at a different stone. Expelling Lotfolk traders: the count continues through Greelmen paid in salt.

# Treasures, secrets, and histories that may never be found (rule 11)

The Thrane silver. Aud's forty-one plates under the fourth step at dead Sennet. The Cord House roll and what every knotter gave. The second sightline stone under the lake. The "Teller" stone in the glass. Mother Tollan's true deed. Marrit's name, which is on her tag. The Ulm measure in the ell of Tollan. Imre's name, which is nowhere. Gorm Longhand's fold and what its gate was. The Whale Walk. Haddo's Kneeling Song, which after Year 25 exists only in the Long Song, sung by the god who killed him.
